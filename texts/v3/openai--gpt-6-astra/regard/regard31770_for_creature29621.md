The first thing the power does is move out of the bird’s light.

A moment before, there has been no shadow on the rolled edge except yours and your host’s. Now there is a third, falling across the place where the young bird has found its footing. The bird leans sideways to examine it.

The shadow withdraws.

You cannot afterward remember what stood there. Your host remembers broad feet. One of the people beneath the plain remembers a garment fastened at the shoulder. The other insists there was no garment, only the impression that someone had arrived from a cooler place.

All agree that it waited to be acknowledged.

Your host looks at it for a long time. Then they point toward the stairs.

***

In the house, the dispute about the cushion is still not entirely over.

This matters, though you do not yet know why. One of the disputants moves to surrender it. The visitor settles on the bare mat before they can do so, and the cushion remains where it was, available for further disagreement.

Water is brought.

The visitor holds the bowl in both hands. Nothing shines more brightly. No seam closes. The damaged sandal remains beneath the bench with its conspicuous green repair.

Your host has been standing since they entered.

The visitor looks toward an empty place on the mat.

Your host sits.

For a while this is all that happens.

***

It learns their speech without making them speak differently.

At first it listens. When it begins to answer, it leaves the pauses inside the words where they belong. It mistakes a name for the name of a kind of fastening, and the person with the red ear corrects it with considerable satisfaction.

Later, you understand that it could have known the language immediately.

There are things it prefers to be told.

The child takes it behind the curtains and puts a counter in its hand. When the fingers open, the counter is still there.

The child changes counters.

They remain inside for a long time.

***

Your host brings out the tray on the third visit.

Not because the visitor has asked. A neighbor is waiting with a troublesome joint, and your host cannot keep entertaining someone while the neighbor sits in pain.

The visitor moves back to give them room.

The work goes badly. A thread that seemed loose has another thread beneath it, crossing at an angle too deep for the wooden point to reach. The neighbor’s breathing becomes careful. Your host withdraws the tool, touches the crease once, and covers the tray.

The neighbor says something sharp.

Your host answers sharply too.

After the neighbor has gone, the visitor does not uncover the tray.

It asks a question.

Your host shakes their head.

It asks another.

This time your host puts a hand flat against their own side, just below the ribs.

You have seen them make that movement before, when rising from a mat or reaching across the table. You thought it was fatigue.

The visitor waits until your host lifts the edge of their garment.

There is no wound. There is a small, beautifully executed fastening.

Your host explains something at length. They indicate the angle, the reach of their own arm, the place where a second hand would have to hold the skin steady. Then they laugh once, without amusement.

The visitor offers its hands.

Your host inspects them.

Only after this does the tray come out again.

***

The work takes most of the afternoon.

The visitor does not simply pass a hand over the troublesome place. Your host chooses a tool, rejects it, chooses another. Twice they stop the visitor. Once they make it undo what it has done.

At last a loop rises from the skin.

Your host draws a breath that begins ordinarily and continues farther than they expect.

They put both feet flat on the floor.

Nobody speaks.

Then they reach for the bowl beside them, and halfway through the movement begin to cry.

The visitor keeps hold of the thread until they are ready for it to be laid down.

***

For several days, people arrive expecting miracles.

Some bring a pain. Some bring a relative. One brings a bowl whose rim has always offended him. Another wants to know whether the visitor can make her brother come across the courtyard and admit what he said.

It can do a great deal.

This makes its answers difficult.

It will examine the bowl. It will go and speak to the brother, if she wishes, but it will not bring him back by anything other than his own feet.

It goes to the house by the long pool.

There, the old person with the loops on their garment takes a long time to decide whether it may enter the back room.

The visitor waits outside.

You are not invited to that visit. Later, when you ask what happened, your host tells you only that the bowl has been moved nearer the sleeper’s hand.

You never learn whether anything else was done.

***

What most concerns the visitor is not hidden beneath the country.

It is the tray.

There is only one in these houses. There are others far away, your host says, and hands that know different things. But when a fastening draws tight here, everyone comes to this room.

The visitor asks who would like to learn.

Several people volunteer immediately. Some stop after the first lesson. One has steady hands but cannot bear to be corrected. Another, who drops cups and walks into doorposts, proves able to feel a crossed thread through a fold of skin.

Your host has never taught this way before.

They find it exhausting. They find it irritating. They discover that something they have always done with a turn of the wrist can be done more easily from the other side.

The visitor supplies nothing until they know what they need.

Then there are more trays.

There is a support on which a tired arm can rest without being held rigid. There is a needle with an eye that can be threaded by someone whose fingers are thick. There are lengths of practice weave that tighten when pulled wrongly and can be loosened again without anything living having to endure the mistake.

The person with the red ear makes the first case for them.

Its stitches are uneven.

Nobody corrects them once the case is strong enough.

***

There is also the heat.

The visitor does not dim the sky. Too much lives beneath it, and too little is known about what the light is doing in the deeper layers.

Instead it spends a morning sitting in the house while people argue about air.

One likes the warmth. One cannot sleep in it. One dislikes a moving curtain against their ankle. Your host says nothing until asked, then says they would like to finish a meal without sweat running down their back.

They try a narrow opening beneath the roof.

Beyond it is neither another room nor the brightness under the plain, but a small passage through which cooler air can be drawn. The first arrangement makes a distressing noise. The second chills the food. The third works.

There is a wooden slide to close it.

For several days everyone touches the slide when passing.

Eventually it becomes part of the house.

***

The power has seen the peg in the empty square.

It has also seen the knots beneath the people’s ears, the water held in the shape of falling, the bright depths under the inspection holes.

It does not mistake its curiosity for an injury that others must be opened to relieve.

But it asks whether anything here has ever been allowed to remain unfinished.

Your host shows it the basket.

They sit beside the doorway. Your host takes up a strand and bends it across the others.

It returns to its former position.

They try again, more firmly.

Again the fresh strand lifts into the same curve.

The basket is not waiting for its maker to return. It has been finished as a basket in the course of being made.

Your host rests their hands on their knees.

The visitor asks whether they want it changed.

This requires more thought than you expect.

At last your host says yes.

Not all the baskets. This one.

***

The power works at the boundary between the basket and what the country expects of it.

There is no visible thread there.

Nevertheless, your host watches closely.

When it is done, nothing has collapsed. The leaves remain at their allotted size. Food still fills the serving dishes. The water beneath the paths has not lowered by the breadth of a hair.

Your host bends the strand.

It stays.

They make three crossings, then undo two. One had been a mistake.

By then people have gathered in the doorway. Someone asks whether the same thing could be done with a room. Someone else asks about a plant.

The visitor listens to each.

It does not promise that every fastening can safely be loosened. It does promise to look, and to explain what it finds before touching anything.

***

You leave before the basket is finished.

On your last afternoon, your host is asleep behind a lowered screen. A neighbor arrives, sees the screen, and goes to another house, where a second tray is being uncovered.

The visitor sits beside the doorway with the unfinished basket near its feet.

It is not working on it.

Outside, the young bird lands badly on the clay rail, steadies itself, and begins to preen. A cooler current lifts one edge of the screen. From inside comes the small, unguarded sound of someone turning in their sleep.

The loose strands of the basket cast different shadows from the ones they cast that morning.

For now, there is nothing that needs to be done.
