The first thing it did was answer the lamp.

This was a small action for something that had come such a distance without crossing the intervening space. But the woman at the window had put on her coat with difficulty, and across the hollow the pump continued to knock.

The answering light appeared in the nursery doorway.

Then someone went down the steps.

***

The new operator was sitting on the floor beside the pump with his sleeve pressed against his palm. He had shut off the auxiliary feed. He had not shut off the main drive, because three growing houses would lose their heat, and he could not remember how long they could safely do without it.

He knew where the instructions were. They were in the cabinet behind the coupling that had broken.

The visitor stopped the drive.

For a moment the operator heard water moving inside the walls. He had never heard it without the machinery.

“The houses,” he said.

“They will stay warm.”

The visitor knelt beside him.

It asked before taking his hand. When he let it, the pain diminished first. The cut closed from the bottom upward, leaving no grit inside.

He watched this happen with an expression that would return to him, unexpectedly, for years.

“Can you do that to the coupling?”

“Yes.”

It repaired the coupling. It also repaired the cracked mounting beneath it, which neither operator had known about, and the place where the feed pipe had worn thin against its bracket.

The pump started again.

Across the hollow, the woman stood with one hand on the lamp switch. The familiar rhythm reached her through the window frame.

A little later there was a knock downstairs.

***

Senn’s grandmother did not invite the visitor in until it told her what had happened.

Then she made it bring the instructions from the nursery.

She read them at the kitchen table, turning the pages with the side of her hand.

“This is the old sequence.”

“Yes.”

“They moved the auxiliary valve.”

“Yes.”

She looked up.

“Did you tell him?”

“I showed him. He is writing the change in now.”

Only then did she lean back.

The visitor sat opposite her. On the table stood an empty cup, a spoon, and a kettle she had not wanted badly enough to lift.

It had already attended to the town in ways for which there were no gestures. It knew the strain in the retaining walls, the distribution of warmth through the roots, the little bodies sleeping in the bakery duct. It knew how much water was being carried home by people whose knees hurt.

Knowing these things had not told it whether this woman wanted tea.

It asked.

“Half a cup,” she said.

When it set the cup before her, she studied its hands.

“What else can you mend?”

“Your fingers, if you want me to.”

She kept looking at its hands.

“Would they still look like mine?”

“Yes.”

She put them on the table.

***

By morning, there were several accounts of what had come to town.

The operator’s account included the pump, his hand, and a period of about eleven minutes during which the growing houses had remained warm without any recorded supply of heat.

Senn’s grandmother’s account was shorter.

She opened her hands and closed them.

People asked her how it felt.

“Strange.”

Then, after a while:

“Good.”

She spent part of the morning sorting screws into jars. Several visitors found this disappointing. They had hoped to see her do something she had missed more.

She had missed sorting screws into jars.

***

The visitor did not arrive at the council carrying a plan.

It arrived carrying the old pump instructions and asked where such things were corrected.

This occupied most of the first meeting.

At the second, Eda brought her collector measurements.

“Can you make this alloy?”

“Yes.”

“How much?”

“How much do you need?”

She disliked the question until she understood it was not a negotiating position.

Then she went away and returned with a different set of figures. These included replacement plates, spare stock, failed batches during fabrication, and enough material to train two apprentices without requiring their first attempts to be useful.

The visitor read everything.

“The lower frames will need strengthening,” it said.

“I know. That is on the back.”

It turned the page.

***

It could have filled the storage pools in an instant. Before doing so, it inspected them.

In one, a neglected partition had shifted. In another, the overflow discharged into a cellar now occupied by six people who believed the pipe to be abandoned. Beneath a third, roots held together masonry that would otherwise have fallen.

The visitor showed the water crews.

It kept the town supplied while they made repairs, and where the work was dangerous it went with them. The roots were left in place. The six people helped choose where the new overflow would run.

For the longer term, it offered a source beneath the collecting yards.

There would be no intake hidden in the cloud, no river diverted from somewhere else. Nothing living would have to go thirsty for it. The visitor explained this plainly, and explained the device as far as the engineers could follow. Where they could not follow, it did not ask them to pretend.

The outlet could be closed by an ordinary handwheel.

That mattered to the crews.

So did the fact that it would deliver no more water than the existing channels could carry.

For twelve days they ran it into an isolated basin. They tested its water on metal coupons, in nursery trays, and through the weaving works’ smallest cooling circuit. The visitor had told them it was safe. They wanted measurements of their own.

It waited through the tests without impatience.

On the thirteenth day, they opened the connection to the town.

The first noticeable consequence was an argument about night shifts.

For years, most factory work had followed the condensation. Now it did not have to. Some workers wanted mornings. Some wanted to keep their arrangements. Two households discovered that they had each been staying on nights for the other’s convenience.

The visitor was asked to settle the matter.

It declined to choose their hours for them, but kept the engines supplied while they tried different schedules. No one had to lose a week’s pay to discover that an arrangement did not work.

***

There were requests it answered at once.

There were others over which it took time.

It would heal a painful joint without demanding that its owner become wiser. It would not lengthen a branch across a neighbor’s greenhouse merely because the person asking had found it first.

When someone asked it to clear the bakery ducts of animals, it brought the baker, his assistant, and the duct cleaner together.

They found five animals.

The baker sat down.

With the visitor’s help, they built an accessible warm passage beside the flour store, with removable panels and no opening into the dough room. The animals inspected it for three nights before moving their nest. The grease pot acquired a lid.

Elsewhere, people arrived with complaints that had lasted so long they could no longer agree on the original injury. The visitor listened. Sometimes it could restore what had been lost. Sometimes there was another person who needed to be present, and sometimes that person would not come.

It did not announce that everything was mended.

There were also empty places at kitchen tables. It did not mistake those for difficulties that could be solved by better kitchens.

***

Eda waited until the water trials were finished.

Then she took the visitor west.

She lay down in her usual place and fitted the mirror to its rod. The visitor lay beside her, although it had no need to.

For a while they looked at the small wet portion of cliff available to them.

“Do the seams continue?” she asked.

“Yes.”

“All the way down?”

“Some divide. Some end. There are others below them.”

She drew the mirror back.

“Can you show me?”

The visitor asked whether she wanted to see from where she was, or go.

Eda considered this considerably longer than she had considered the alloy.

“Go. But slowly.”

It made a foothold below the lip, broad enough for both of them, with a rail on the outside. When she stepped onto it, the foothold descended.

The town disappeared above them.

Cloud passed cold across her face. She tightened her grip on the rail, then loosened it. Beside her the stone moved upward, immense, rounded in places and undercut in others. There were fern roots in narrow cracks. There were pale seams wider than streets.

She asked it to stop often.

Once she asked to go back up a little.

The visitor obeyed without telling her what she ought to look at next.

When she returned, late and very cold, her brother had to warm her soup twice. She kept leaving it to draw.

***

It left the old water alone.

This was not because it failed to notice the colorless growth, or because the cold meant nothing to it. It had attended to the channels beyond the gardens, including those no one watched.

When the young engineer asked whether her branching mark was alive, it offered to help her observe the next flow where it occurred.

They built a small shelter beside a groove. The viewing surface touched nothing. There was room beneath it for the water to rise, and no bright lamp to alter what they hoped to see.

On the first night they saw nothing.

On the second, the engineer fell asleep.

The visitor woke her when there was something to watch.

***

By then, people had begun asking what it wanted.

They asked in meetings, in doorways, beside beds. Some were grateful. Some were frightened by a kindness for which they could find no terms.

It told them there would be no collection, no oath, no household that lost its water for speaking against it.

Its gifts would remain usable if it went away.

They did not all believe this immediately. It made room for that too.

***

The town became less tired before it became visibly different.

A roof was repaired in daylight by people who had slept. Olin acquired a room beside his winter gardens, with a window his daughter considered too small. The nursery operator was given proper instruction and a second person on the difficult shift.

Some collector racks were taken out of service. Others remained in use, and Eda’s new plates proved slightly better than her figures had promised. She checked the meters three times before accepting it.

There were still snails beneath the troughs.

There were still disputes about light.

The visitor could often be found where work was underway, though it was no longer invariably working. It learned the story of the cracked blue basin from four members of the family and was accused, by the fifth, of having heard it wrong.

One evening Senn’s grandmother woke to the nursery pump changing speed.

She lay still.

The new rhythm settled. Across the hollow, someone knew what they were doing.

She went back to sleep.

Outside, the collecting yards began to tick. Warm air rose among the leaves. In the visitor’s open hand, a nearly naked duct animal slept with all five fingers closed around one of its own.
