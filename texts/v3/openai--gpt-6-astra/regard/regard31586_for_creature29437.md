The power’s attention arrives without weight.

No whisker bends beneath it. No feet retract. The parent continues holding the edge of the slab while the remaining young search along its flanks.

For a time, the power attends to what holding costs.

The good forefoot has taken more than its share for many pulses. A membrane beneath its first joint is inflamed. The healed foot does not hurt continuously, but pressure at one angle sends a sharp disturbance through the animal’s body. It has learned a route around that angle. Some of what appears to be familiarity with the stone is familiarity with pain.

The young are hungry. So is the parent. Hunger in this body is not a thought about food. It is a narrowing: other sensations become less important, and the feet turn toward certain vibrations before the animal has finished resting.

The power stays through this narrowing.

It also attends beneath the shelter, where the bitten fold has drawn its injured edge inward. And beneath the fold, where several grazers remain alive.

There is no place here from which the whole chamber becomes painless to look at.

The power does not look away.

***

Far below the hollow, water moves through the mountain.

Most of it follows a sloping bed of fractured stone and emerges much lower down, in a valley where roots crowd a cold spring. A smaller quantity enters a blind fissure. There it heats, presses against its confinement, and eventually forces its way into deeper rock.

The power follows both paths before touching either.

Then, in the blind fissure, it loosens a grain.

The alteration is slight enough that the next movement of the mountain completes it. A thread of vapor begins rising through an old fault toward the hollow. It passes through porous stone that catches much of what would poison the animals. Near the entrance, below the reach of daylight, it meets rock cooled by the wind.

The first moisture gathers inside the stone.

Nothing drips. A droplet would draw too many bodies to one point; the smallest would disappear beneath the others. Instead, dampness spreads through a strip of fine pores along the cooler wall. It appears in separate patches, connected deep within the rock.

Over several pulses, the white flakes lodged there soften.

A grazer stops and works its mouthparts against a patch that has never fed it before. Another arrives. A small, pale animal that spends its life inside joints in the wall extends half its body.

The parent does not find the dampness yet.

The power has time to wait for it.

***

The intact shelter is failing.

Its roof has moved by less than the width of a whisker during the last pulse. Beneath one corner, a thin supporting stone has split through its middle. Animals have filled the split so closely that their bodies now help transmit the weight above them.

Within a few more strong changes of heat, the support will shear.

The power feels the lives under the slab: resting folds, sealed eggs, grazers scraping the underside of an old shell. It feels the parent’s tail hooked beneath the outer edge.

During the next cooling, when the stone contracts, the broken support settles into a new position.

The movement is minute. It makes a dry click.

The parent lifts its belly.

Under the roof, bodies tighten and hold still.

Then the weight comes to rest along the sound part of the wall. The split remains, but it no longer carries the slab.

Beside it, a passage that had narrowed over generations opens again. Cold air enters at one end and leaves warmer at the other. A folded animal extends a foot into it, pauses, and begins the slow work of moving through.

The parent feels this new traffic from above.

Later, it will investigate.

***

The foot takes longer.

The power could replace it in an instant. But the parent knows the length of its damaged limb, the reach of its remaining finger, the exact moment at which to transfer its weight. An unfamiliar foot could betray it at the edge of the roof.

Instead, the inflammation in the good joint eases first.

The parent rests for longer than usual. When it moves, it plants the damaged foot twice, as it always has. The second placement no longer carries the same sharp warning.

Beneath the glossy knob, tissue begins preparing for the next molt.

The power makes this small work possible without taking its substance from the young. What is needed arrives gradually, through the animal’s ordinary meals: enough salt retained, enough water spared, enough nourishment passing into repair.

At the molt, the new fingers are short.

They remain folded for a while after the old skin comes loose. The parent touches them with its mouthparts, then braces them against the wall.

They hold.

It continues to plant the foot twice for many circuits afterward.

***

The first young animal finds the changed passage before its parent does.

It reaches the opening by following the underside of the roof, where fewer bodies cross its path. Its plates have hardened, but it is still small enough to shelter beneath a dead grazer’s shell.

The passage widens behind the wall.

Here, the mountain’s heat reaches the stone indirectly. The yellow film grows slowly along one side. On the other, the rock holds a trace of moisture. There are already animals in it; there are animals almost everywhere anything can live. But between them are spaces wider than a young body.

It enters one.

For the first time since leaving the brood chamber, it can stand without another creature touching it.

Its foot-whiskers spread.

It remains there through a whole cooling, feeding intermittently. When a hunter probes the entrance, it retreats into a bend too tight for the hunter’s limbs. The hunter waits, then moves on toward the richer traffic beneath the slab.

The power attends to the hunter as it goes.

Elsewhere along the new damp strip, an old clutch begins to hatch.

***

Changes continue, but slowly enough to be met by living things.

A warm fissure opens into a succession of shallow pockets. The film reaches them first. Grazers follow. Some pockets remain cooler than others, and their entrances vary in width. During a strong pulse, there are now several routes away from the floor.

The hottest surges become less abrupt as pressure finds a deeper outlet.

The chamber still rearranges itself. Bodies still climb over the shelters, and the pale cords still tighten between the pillars. But fewer animals lose their purchase in the crush. More reach tolerable stone before their joints begin to fail.

There are still hunters. There are still hungry young. The power’s work is unfinished.

It does not mistake a little more room for an end to suffering. Nor does it withhold the room because it cannot yet offer an end.

***

Much later, the parent crosses the lintel with an empty brood chamber.

The last of its young left during a quiet interval. One has died. Others are beyond the short reach of its routes. Their absence enters its body as a change in balance, a reduction of thirst, an unfamiliar ease when passing beneath the overhang.

At the five grooves, it stretches.

Its restored fingers catch the far cut.

For a moment it stays there, spread across the marks left by a vanished maker. Then it draws itself over the edge and descends toward the damp stone.

The wind above the storms continues to carry discarded shells into the empty air. Inside, the mountain gives a low, brief sound.

The parent receives it through its hind feet.

It lifts its belly, waits, and feels the disturbance diminish.

No climb is needed.

Slowly, it lowers itself again.
