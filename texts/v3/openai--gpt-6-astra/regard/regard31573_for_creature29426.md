The attention arrived without weight.

It did not press down the sedges or enter the stone as another vibration. The little beads of water kept their lights. Beneath the leaning branch, the two animals held to the root until the concussion passed, then loosened their hands by different degrees.

For a time, nothing in the basin had to accommodate anything new.

The power watched.

It watched the old one open a snail, rest, and resume opening it. It followed the water through the openings along the jaw. It learned which contractions belonged to feeding and which to pain. These were easily mistaken for one another from outside.

It watched the smaller neighbor come and go. Once, when the neighbor arrived, the old one blocked the entrance. The visitor waited through rain, touched the closed body with one hand, and eventually withdrew.

The power did not open the shelter.

Three days later the old one crossed the wooded rise. It carried nothing. At the neighboring shelter, it put a hand inside and left it there.

There were things here that could be helped only after they were understood, and things that did not need help merely because they were difficult.

***

Under the basin, pressure accumulated in branching chambers.

Much of the stone’s work was delicate. A small exchange of heat drew water through peat that would otherwise sour. A change in slope relieved a root bed before its weight tore it loose. The channels lifted, the sheltered spaces opened, and certain lives passed through a brief interval of air.

But several deep passages had narrowed.

Mineral deposits had gathered where cold water met warm. The obstruction was slight at any single point. Across thousands of repeating branches, it had become consequential. Pressure arrived late, gathered too strongly, and escaped through places that had once carried only a fraction of it.

The power followed these failures through many wet seasons at once.

It found the channel that had lowered before a small animal could unfold its jaw openings. It found the unlifted water above an adult that had waited beneath a root until waiting could no longer help.

These lives had ended. Knowing the whole route of the water did not make them less ended.

In a chamber far below the reach of roots, the power loosened a film of mineral no thicker than the skin of a bubble.

Then it waited for the result.

Water passed. Heat moved. On a wooded island, a patch of moss became slightly warmer. A burrow remained dry that would otherwise have flooded. Elsewhere, a pool fell by the width of a grass blade.

The power made no second alteration until it had followed the first to its smallest consequences.

Over the following rains, a little of the deep obstruction cleared.

The basin remained wet. Its trees still leaned. Rain arrived out of order, and the wader still stepped onto a root when blue ran through the stone.

But the great reports from below lost the hard edge that had sometimes broken tender root tips.

The lifted channels stayed aloft long enough to complete their lowering slowly.

***

The old animal noticed.

It had no reason to call the change kindness. For several days it trusted the altered rhythm less than it had trusted the danger.

When the water rose, it gripped with all six limbs. When it descended, the old one remained flattened long after the sedges had disappeared.

Then, one evening, its left middle hand failed to close.

The damaged joints had swollen. It caught itself with the front limbs and hung at an angle, the fern on its shoulder trailing in the current.

The power could have remade the hand.

Instead, it first learned the hand as the animal knew it: the reach of the missing fingers still present in certain movements, the compensating turn of the shoulder, the pressure that was tolerable, the pressure that woke it.

During the next rest, a small warmth gathered beside the sore joint.

The old one pulled away.

The warmth stayed where it was.

It examined the place with its front hands, pressed its throat to the wood, and retreated into the shelter. For most of the night it presented only its bark-colored back.

Near morning, the damaged hand returned.

The warmth spread no farther than the part laid against it. When the animal lifted away, it ceased.

Over several visits, the swelling eased. The old one could brace again. Its curved path through the silt remained curved.

***

At the central lake, the power lingered longest.

The drifting reflections were not merely light. Some carried arrangements that had once belonged to bodies. Others had been assembled from repeated meetings: one creature’s remembered pressure against another, a movement anticipated so often that the basin had learned its shape.

They were entangled.

What approached the old one from beneath the lake was neither wholly a visitor nor wholly an image. When it opened its undamaged hand, the stone supplied an answering sensation. To sustain that answer, the lake drew upon the animal waiting at the shore.

It took recent things first.

The feel of a root passed on the journey. The turn at a channel. The exact location of the shelter entrance.

The power saw how the old one leaned toward the contact. It also saw what the contact cost.

It did not empty the lake.

Along one length of shore, it gave the displaced water a route back through the deeper stone. The returning current could now carry most of the strain that had passed through living skin.

Then, beneath the old animal’s usual root, it established a small, steady pressure.

This pressure did not imitate a hand. It offered no familiar body, no promise about what waited among the timber. It was only a point of contact that remained where the bank remained.

The next time the old one visited, it felt the pressure with a hind hand.

Out on the lake, the reflections loosened.

A broad shape came between them.

The old one extended its damaged limb. For a long while it held that posture, rain gathering in the pits of its skin. Its front half grew pale at the edges.

The steady pressure beneath its hind hand continued.

Eventually the animal tightened against it.

The shore became distinct again. A dead trunk stood where its reflection had wandered. The old one remained at the water’s edge until night, then took the correct channel home.

It returned to the lake later that season.

The power kept the route open.

***

There were still losses.

The power attended to them without treating their persistence as permission to stop. It learned more passages in the stone. It held a failing root while new fibers took the load. Where its first changes had warmed a pool too much for the layered algae, it altered the exchange and waited for the veils to separate again.

Its care became more exact.

In one of the outer channels, an adult carrying young settled beneath a grass mat. The water rose, exposing a dark underside threaded with fine roots.

The adult unfolded.

One by one, small hands appeared along its flank.

The last young animal emerged slowly. It caught with only one limb and hung there, twisting. Below it, the parent could not release its own anchor without falling.

The water remained raised.

A sedge stem bent within reach.

The young animal touched it, missed, touched again. Its hand closed.

Only then did the channel begin to lower.

No light appeared above the basin. Nothing spoke from the clouds. The parent stayed beneath the young until their jaw openings were working, then touched each of them and rested.

***

Under the leaning grove, the old one began to sleep more deeply.

At first the change lasted only a few breaths. Both sides of its body softened together; then it woke abruptly and seized the root.

The root was there.

The smaller neighbor was there, folded badly into the familiar space.

Outside, the floor of the basin continued its immense, intricate labor. Water knocked inside hollow wood. Rain passed through the reeds. Far below, a passage that had been closing for centuries carried a clear thread of water.

One night, the old animal slept through the first blue alteration.

The neighbor felt it and tightened its grip. After a moment, it laid another hand across the old one’s injured wrist.

Nothing required the old one to wake.

The power kept watch over the pressure beneath the stone, the young in the outer channels, the difficult shore of the lake. It neither entered the shelter nor asked to be known.

Beneath the branch, the pale fern slowly uncurled.

For once, all the hands were at rest.
