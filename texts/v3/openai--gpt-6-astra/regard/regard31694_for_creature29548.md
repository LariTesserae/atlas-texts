The first thing the power does is take its weight off the stone.

Not because the animal cannot bear it. The bank has borne heavier things. But the small correction beneath the cavity is not a greeting, and the power does not mistake it for one.

The crease opens. Water returns to its former route.

For a while, nothing more is added to the basin.

***

The power listens where the animal listens.

Not to the roar as it fills the air, but to the differences carried through the attachment cups: the loaded ceiling, the gas held behind a narrowing, the flake that has not quite broken free. It follows these without inserting another vibration among them.

There is much to learn.

A fold drawn tight may be working comfortably. A motionless membrane may be exhausted. The darkened plates at a sealed end may be an old injury, or the finished edge of a body that has no reason to extend farther. The power does not decide from the appearance of these things.

It attends through many passages of fluid.

Eventually it finds a place where a cup is doing the work of three.

A shelf above it has settled. The displacement is very small, and the animal has accommodated it by drawing a supporting cord around the mouth of a channel. The cord must remain tight. Whenever it eases, the channel flattens, and the tissues beyond receive less of what the water carries.

There is no emergency. The arrangement has lasted a long time.

It costs something all the time.

The power learns the shape of that cost. It follows the repeated signal from the cord, the interrupted maintenance of the thin tissues farther on, the way other cups begin to shift and then fasten again without having moved.

Only then does it touch the stone.

***

Beneath the settled shelf, a small ridge begins to bear weight.

It rises less than the thickness of the animal’s skin. It does not press against the body. It meets another part of the roof, beyond the fold, and takes a little of the load.

The cord eases.

The power stops.

For a long while the animal keeps the cord ready. Fluid gathers nearby. Several cups hold more firmly than they need to. The altered support has arrived without any of the usual warnings of moving stone.

The power makes no further alteration.

At last a cup releases. This time it settles a little farther along.

The ridge remains.

It is not beautiful. No one standing on the bank could find it. Its composition is the same as the stone around it, and it introduces no new note into the basin. Water still passes through the channel.

Beyond the channel, a thin place in the animal begins to maintain itself more fully.

***

The power could fill the chambers with nourishment.

Instead, it learns what happens to the water after the animal has used it.

There are the pale fringed animals beneath the falls. There are the crowded cells inside the folded tissues. There are smaller lives in places the first observer took for bare mineral. Some depend on substances that would be removed by abundance elsewhere. Some require the very poverty that limits their neighbors.

The power follows the water far enough not to make a gift upstream and a deprivation below.

Then, in a recess beside one of the animal’s thinner portions, it opens a small additional seep.

The seep is warm. It carries the same substances as the deeper flow, in a proportion the resident can use. Its surplus is modest. The opening lies to one side of the established channel, so that the animal need not receive it merely by remaining where it is.

At first the water passes untouched.

Later, an edge turns toward it.

Nothing requires that edge to continue.

***

The power does not join the two sealed ends.

It has learned by then that they belong to different bodies.

Nor does it release the compact folded things from their chambers. It watches the narrow channels supplying them and discovers that readiness is not all that is happening there. Provision is being measured against conditions outside. The closing of a channel is part of that work.

There may someday be young in the new seep.

There may instead be a thicker membrane, a repaired cup, a reserve held through an interval of poor water.

The gift does not depend on which occurs.

***

There is still the matter of being known.

The power cannot offer a face or a voice that means anything here. It could place meanings directly inside the animal, but those would be meanings the animal had never made.

Instead it chooses a loose piece of stone, beyond the inhabited roof, and gives it a slight pressure.

Then it releases it.

The disturbance is small enough to ignore. It bears no weight down on a fold and obstructs no water. After a long interval, it happens again.

The animal tightens a membrane facing that part of the chamber.

The pressure ceases.

Much later, the membrane eases. The power waits longer still before touching the loose stone again.

Over time, something becomes distinguishable from the basin’s ordinary operation: a disturbance that stops when resisted.

It is a small beginning. The power does not enlarge it by pretending it is more.

***

Above, the plain retains its dun color. No tree appears beside the river. The underground roar is not hushed for the sake of a silence its resident has never needed.

Beneath the bank, the supported channel stays open with less effort.

An attachment cup releases, travels the width of its own rim, and fastens beside the additional seep. A pale cloud moves inward through the body.

For once, there is a little more arriving than has already been spent.

The animal holds it.

The power leaves it that way.
