The power notices the gap between the shelf and the root.

It could close it.

Instead it watches through an evening feed, a night of reduced circulation, and the sharp first light that brings the service arm out of its recess. The root moves. Not much, but enough that a bridge made in the evening might be torn away by morning. The shelf is growing toward something that is also growing, and neither has reached its final shape.

For a while, nothing changes.

The attendant comes with her brush and her shallow collecting tray. She clears the white beard from the brass mouth. Some of the animals come away inside their chambers; others are exposed by the breaking crust. These she cannot save individually. She carries the loosened pieces to the dish below the yellow door and lays them in warm water.

The power follows her.

The maintenance room is scarcely wide enough for its workbench. A coat hangs against a hot pipe. There are replacement seals in a tin, three cracked gauges kept for their glass, and a stool whose shortest leg stands on a folded maintenance notice. Beside the dish is the drawer of ruled strips.

The attendant washes her hands. She has a small split beside one thumbnail that has failed to heal.

“May I help?” says the power.

She looks first at the door, then at the speaking space beside the bench.

“With what?”

It has not decided for her.

She dries her hands carefully.

“The intake coupling,” she says at last. “I need someone to hold it while I change the seal.”

***

The coupling is below the garden, down a ladder inside the column.

Here there are no leaves to soften the machinery. Two hot pipes descend through successive platforms into a depth from which other pipes rise. The attendant brings a lamp, though the power does not require one. It holds the coupling where she asks it to hold it.

“Not there. You’ll put the weight on the thread.”

It moves its hand.

When the seal is out, she shows it the flattened place along one edge. The replacement has been cut from material intended for a larger fitting. She has done this before.

The power could make the joint seamless. It could remove the need for joints.

It holds the weight while she seats the seal.

On the way back up, it learns why there is no second attendant, how often supplies arrive, and what happens when the warm feed stops. She answers some questions immediately. For others she fetches a book.

There is no emergency reservoir. There was to have been one.

Its connections are already present behind a blank panel.

***

They begin there.

Not in the pool, and not among the mineral steps. Beneath the floor, in the space reserved for equipment that never came, they install a tank.

The power provides what is missing. The attendant makes it show her how the new fittings come apart.

The tank fills slowly from the warm supply. If pressure falls, it will release its water through the existing channels. It holds enough for her to find a fault without having to work while the fringes dry above her.

There is a gauge she can read from the ladder. There is a lever she can move by hand. The instructions are fixed to the wall where condensation will not loosen them.

For the first test, she closes the main feed.

In the garden, a leaf tips its small load into a pool. Beneath the eastern cages the animals continue feeding. The pressure needle lowers, pauses, and holds.

The attendant leaves the supply closed for another minute.

Then she opens it again.

***

At the outer wall, the power finds three young animals traveling along the same wet track.

Ahead of them, the iron is cold. One has already stopped.

It warms that short length of rail enough for the animal to move, then waits while the attendant brings a strip of wet growing felt. She lays it against the track. Two of the specks glide onto it. The third remains in a shallow defect in the metal until evening.

They do not heat the whole wall.

Over the next several days, the attendant follows the moisture back to its source: an overflow that spreads sideways when a drain comb is full. She fits a narrow return channel. Its inner edge stays wet; its outer edge dries. Most of the wandering young now find their way toward a shaded bed.

A few still go elsewhere.

The power learns to look very closely without making every journey end in settlement.

Under the third step, the snail continues to eat. Its shell acquires another faint ridge.

***

There are things the attendant does not want changed.

She wants the central pool left alone until she has measured it through a full season. She wants no stronger circulation around the fern collars; the roots there are doing well. She wants the arm to keep its blunt shears.

And she wants to know whether the animals suffer when the crust is broken.

The power attends to the dish for a long time.

It can perceive injuries too small for her instruments, the disturbance passing from chamber to chamber, the gradual resumption of feeding. There is something here that matters to the creatures themselves, though it is not arranged like her own distress.

It tells her what it can tell.

Afterward they make removable sleeves for the valve mouths. The water will carry the same food over them, and the animals will still settle, but most of an encroaching colony can now be lifted away without breaking its rooms.

The sleeves are not perfect. A tool can slip. Growth can cross a seam.

She asks for six spares.

***

Before leaving, the power offers to remain within call.

The attendant considers this more seriously than she considered the tank.

“Will you come if it’s only something small?”

“Yes.”

“Will you leave things alone if I ask?”

“Unless leaving them would put someone in immediate danger. Then I’ll tell you.”

She thinks about that, too.

They settle on a way to call that does not depend on the pumps.

***

The bridge reaches the hanging root eleven days later.

By then the root has divided. The growing shelf meets the finer branch, which rests against it during the evening rise and lifts slightly when the pool falls.

For several cycles the contact leaves no mark.

Then a little crust forms around a root hair. Water holds in the angle. Two young animals settle beneath it, facing different directions.

The attendant records the date. She does not tie the root in place.

Below her feet, the new reservoir is full. The coupling holds. In the maintenance room, there are clean seals of the proper size, and her stool no longer rocks.

Above the eastern cage, the root moves again.

This time the shelf moves with it.
