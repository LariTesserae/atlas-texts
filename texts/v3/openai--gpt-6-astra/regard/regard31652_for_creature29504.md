The power arrived while the animal was feeding beneath the bolt.

For a while it attended to the mouth.

The stone was not smooth. Beneath the film of water were pits, mineral grains, the margins of older grazing marks. The mouth worked across these differences. A part of the film became the animal. The rest remained on the wall.

There was enough here to occupy the power’s whole attention. It gave that attention without causing the pump to miss a stroke.

Then it followed the heat downward.

Below the garden were shafts and valves, other pipes, a room where a man had hung a damp coat over the back of a chair. Farther down, the heat passed into buildings whose windows were white at their lower corners. Someone was washing a bowl. Someone slept beside a radiator with a clicking valve. In a greenhouse, seedlings stood in rows above ground that would otherwise have been frozen.

The power followed the machinery back up. It examined the dismantled pump, the deteriorating seals of the one that ran, the loose panel. It passed through the mountain’s hot fractures. It found the drain beneath the lowest pool and the dense plug of roots and mineral grit that would eventually close it.

It also found the young.

There were eleven in a pocket of moss beside a pipe bracket. One had moved onto the bracket itself. Its body was almost clear; the metal showed through it as a dark band. A thread of water kept its foot from drying.

The next surge from the mountain would be hotter than the previous ones. It would enter that pocket from below.

The power could have lifted the eleven away.

It waited long enough to learn where away would be.

***

The first change occurred inside the rock.

A fracture widened by less than the thickness of a fern stem. A loose grain turned. Some water that had been rising toward the nursery found an easier passage and joined an already scalding runnel where nothing grew.

Above, the moss remained warm.

The young animal on the bracket drew itself forward until its head reached the concrete. There it paused, with the rest of its body stretched across the gap.

The power waited through the pause.

***

Over the following weeks, a shallow seam opened along the sheltered wall. Water entered it slowly, losing heat on the way. Small depressions held the water without becoming deep. Between them were rough, dry-backed projections on which moss could take hold.

The seam continued upward.

It crossed no walking stone and weakened no footing. Near the top, well above the cabinet’s faded line, it broadened into a recess protected from the notch. There was room inside for wet growth, for bodies gathered against the wall, for air.

Nothing was placed there to make the animals come.

Eventually a fern root reached it. The moss followed.

The smaller animal from the pipe collars found the lower end one evening and spent most of the night feeding along it. Several nights later it went farther. Its track was still visible in the morning, a faint shine crossing the new stone.

***

The power considered the ladder.

A person would come up it carrying the replacement parts. On the fourth rung below the moss, the right-hand weld had cracked almost through. The rung still held a boot placed close to the rail. It would not hold the same boot in the middle.

The power joined the metal where it had parted.

That was a plain act. It required neither a lesson nor a sign.

When the worker came, she climbed without incident. She carried seals, bearings, a flask of tea, and a panel fastening she had remembered at the last moment.

She found the leak at the third joint. Before stripping the insulation, she drew back a wet fold of its covering and saw a brown animal attached beneath it.

She waited for it to move onto a piece of old sacking. Then she laid the sacking in the shelter of the footing.

The power had not put kindness into her. It watched her use what was already there.

Later, kneeling beside the dismantled pump, she discovered how many animals occupied the recess behind its casing. The repair took longer than she had allowed. She moved them with pieces of wet moss, slowly enough that their feet released instead of tearing.

She photographed the copper-edged bodies beside her thumb. In her report she added a note about checking the housings before removing them.

The power attended to that, too.

***

By the next cold season, both pumps worked. Each could be stopped without losing the supply below. The bright rattle had ceased, though the garden still held the deeper pulse of machinery.

The floods continued. They carried sediment into the leaf ribs and left the lower ferns leaning downhill. Water rose around the footing, and the animals climbed.

Now there were several ways upward.

In the sheltered recess, the largest fastened itself beside two smaller bodies. The notch in its flank closed as it contracted. Below them the pools became one surface, faintly trembling.

The water stopped rising.

For two days they waited.

The power remained with them, though waiting required nothing of it and almost everything of them.

When the water fell, the largest loosened its clear border from the rock. Its head emerged. The short feelers touched the wall, withdrew, and touched again.

There was a new film growing just beyond its reach.

It extended toward it.

The power let the distance belong to the animal.
