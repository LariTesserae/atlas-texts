At first, the power does nothing that can be distinguished from the light.

It attends to the hollow through several of the long intervals in which a hand remains on a shelf. It follows the warmth beneath the pools, the pressure carried by the columns, the sweetness passing between an occupied enclosure and the body resting there. It notices that a throat’s faint contractions may be answered a great distance away, after many intervening surfaces have received them.

It does not mistake the delay for silence.

There are things it could do at once. It could open the ceiling. It could send water down every waiting channel, separate the crowded ridges, give the folded surfaces all the room they seem to require.

It refrains.

The droplets have their attachments. The folded surfaces are touched along their folds. What looks like an obstruction from the ledge may, from within the tissue, be the place where something is being held.

And yet the inhabitant moved the older blade.

The power attends especially to that.

***

It finds the inhabitant still on the high fold, with one hand near the place where yours rested.

The warm depression has almost disappeared. Beneath the hand, however, the fine channels in the shelf remain a little darker than those around them.

The power does not restore your warmth. It does not put an answering pressure there and let the inhabitant believe you have returned.

Instead, on the other side of the seated body, it allows itself a small surface.

This is neither a new leaf nor a hand. It has no roots in the hollow and takes nothing from the floor. It lies just above the short blades, opaque enough to be seen, with a dullness that is unusual in that luminous country.

For a long time, the inhabitant does not turn.

Then the clear lid over its nearer eye thickens.

The power makes no approach.

Eventually four fingers come to rest on the offered surface. Beneath them the power yields, but only where it is touched.

Three slight increases in pressure pass through the fingertips. After the pause comes another.

The power receives them without returning an imitation.

It has much to learn before it can answer.

***

Learning takes longer than the light takes to cross the ceiling, and less time than the little one beside the basin takes to uncover its eyes.

It is not chiefly a learning of names.

There is a difference between the weight of a body that intends to remain and the weight of one that has not yet decided to leave. There are contacts that carry nourishment, contacts that carry recollection, and contacts whose meaning depends upon not carrying anything at all.

An enclosure can be unoccupied without being available.

A creature can be motionless without being at rest.

The inhabitant on the fold sometimes withdraws its hand. On each occasion the offered surface remains nearby, neither following nor fading. Later, when the inhabitant touches it again, the contact begins where it begins. There is no accumulated demand.

Once the inhabitant rises and goes down to a basin. The power remains on the fold.

When it returns, it brings nothing.

It sits closer.

***

The first help occurs below the half-released leaf.

The power has followed the crowded tissues there. The older blade rests against a ridge; the ridge presses upon a folded sheet; beneath the sheet, a narrow band is drawn taut between two heavier masses. The little space made by the inhabitant’s fingers has not closed, but keeping it open has altered the strain.

The power lays a fraction of that strain upon itself.

Nothing visible happens.

The inhabitant’s palm is against the floor. A tightening passes along its throat, then stops. Its fingers spread.

The power eases its support away.

The band beneath the ridge resumes its burden.

After a long interval the inhabitant presses down with two fingertips, not upon the offered surface but beside it.

The power takes the weight again.

This time it keeps it.

The transparent leaf-tip slips a little farther beneath the lifted edge.

No other leaf moves.

***

There are consequences even to this.

A shallow hollow appears farther down the slope. Moisture gathers in it where the footing was dry before. A seedpod creature leaves the underside of a nearby shelf and settles elsewhere.

The power follows these changes. It does not dismiss them because they are small.

At the damp patch it adjusts what it is holding, until the moisture recedes to its previous boundary. It leaves the creature alone. The new resting place fits its feet; there is no reason to carry it back.

The work becomes very exact.

To relieve a pressure here without withdrawing a support there. To make a little room without requiring anything to enter it. To bear what can be borne from outside without interrupting what passes through the living ground.

The power is capable of much larger acts. Here it spends that capacity on distinguishing one fine channel from the next.

***

The inhabitant does not become its messenger.

Sometimes it sits beside the offered surface. Sometimes it passes without touching. It continues to rest in enclosures, to lower its wrists into water, to stand for long intervals with its face against another’s arm.

Once it brings the side of its body against the power, where the seam crosses its shoulder.

The contact lasts through a gradual dimming of the ceiling.

There is no wound to close there. The seam is old, complete, and particular. The power leaves it as it is.

When the inhabitant moves away, a faint amber patch remains upon the surface it touched.

The power lets the patch fade at its own rate.

***

Much later, if you return, you may believe that nothing has happened.

The country still lies beneath its bright canopy. The water is held in the cups. The tall fins have not hardened, and the folded hills have not become anything more finished than themselves.

On the high fold, the leaf beside your former resting place has opened enough to show a narrow strip of its upper surface.

The inhabitant is there, though not where you left it. It sits a little farther down, one knee raised, its foot joined to the floor by threads too fine to see unless the light catches them.

Beside it is a patch of dullness.

You may take this for shadow.

If you sit, neither moves into the space you choose. Beneath your weight the shelf compresses. Your legs begin, after a while, to ache.

Before you rise, you feel a slight support beneath one knee.

It waits there, light enough for you to draw away from it.

If you leave your leg where it is, it takes a little more of the weight.
