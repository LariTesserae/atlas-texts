The power first becomes noticeable because one drop of rain does not fall.

It hangs beside the northern hollow, a little higher than the pierced nodule. Wind flattens it, then lets it round again. It contains the same small green reflection as the other drops. There is nothing inside it that resembles an eye.

The inhabitant finds it after a report from below.

It bends over the nodule, listens, and adjusts the opening. Then it notices the drop. It holds one hand beneath it.

The drop settles into the palm.

For a while nothing happens. The inhabitant touches it with its broad inner digit. Water spreads across the skin, collecting around an embedded plate, and the power permits itself to be wiped onto the stone.

It has learned very little. It stays.

***

There are things it could do at once.

It could arrest the shelf in every dimension, make the pools lie level, separate the joined plates in the inhabitant’s shoulder. It could draw the cloud away and put the whole place under sunlight.

It watches a transparent hunter turn beneath the green rim of a pool. It watches the roots comb the cloud. At the next report, it sees light travel through the inhabitant’s flank and through a fresh margin of stone at nearly the same speed.

It leaves the cloud where it is.

For several days its presence has no fixed location. It is a slight resistance beneath a slipping foot, a patch of air in which the rain ticks more softly. Once it prevents an empty shell from blowing over the edge. The inhabitant had put the shell there deliberately. It comes back, picks it up, and throws it into the cloud.

The power does not retrieve it.

After that it is more careful about what counts as loss.

***

The shoulder is the first thing the inhabitant brings to it.

This happens outside the shelter, near the polished wall. By then the power has acquired a modest visible form: a clear bulge in the air, no larger than one of the loose stones. It rests on the bench when the inhabitant is away and outside the doorway when it returns. The coat remains visible through it.

The inhabitant rubs its shoulder against the wall, pauses, and turns.

It approaches sideways. The joined plates make a shallow ridge beneath the skin. Around their edges, the gray surface is drawn tight.

The power moves closer, then stops.

The inhabitant closes the remaining distance.

What the power offers is not a repair. Beneath its touch, the two plates cease pulling against one another. Their weight is supported separately, though they remain joined. The inhabitant’s breathing fold opens.

It stays like that for a long time.

When it draws away, the support ends. It looks back. Approaches again.

Over the next few mornings, they discover a place on the wall where this can happen without either having to wait for the other. The place is not marked. The polished stone gives a little beneath the shoulder and holds whatever pressure the inhabitant chooses to place against it.

Sometimes it uses the support.

Sometimes it rubs hard against the old stone instead.

The power leaves both available.

***

It pays close attention to the white post.

The handle does not merely govern weight. When pulled, it obliges things that are arriving to arrive together. Water agrees with its basin. Distances hold. The inhabitant’s body becomes definite, as it does in the dry square, but without the square’s shelter from the strain.

The older nests have accumulated through many seasons of not pulling.

The power does not clear them out.

Instead, on a morning when the adults are feeding, it makes a second handle beside the first.

This one is transparent. It has no post beneath it and no hollow where anything might nest. It yields immediately to the inhabitant’s curious touch.

Nothing happens to the shelf.

The inhabitant pulls harder.

Nothing happens.

It releases the handle and goes to examine a frame.

The power lets its unsuccessful offer disappear.

For a further six days it watches the inhabitant carrying eggs.

***

The next thing it makes is much smaller.

Beside a difficult gap in the broken route, where the stone has been lengthening unevenly, it lays a pale loop. The loop is wide enough to admit a hand. It rests partly on an old shell and partly on newly grown ground.

The inhabitant touches it with the root it uses for lifting eggs.

The distance between the shell and the next porous ridge stops changing.

Elsewhere, water continues to lean east. A grazer crosses a seam that is opening beneath its feet. The winged animals keep their high, thin whine.

The inhabitant lifts the root.

The loop loosens. The gap resumes its slow enlargement.

It tries again, this time watching the nearest pool. Then it puts a finger through the loop and pulls. There is a faint resistance, no more than the weight of wet cloth.

The power holds that one short stretch of ground steady.

The inhabitant spends most of the afternoon testing it. It sets a pebble down, takes three steps away, returns. It leaves the loop under a stone and watches from the shelter. It lifts the stone. It tastes the loop.

The next morning it uses it while carrying an egg.

Nothing closes around the egg. Nothing touches the inhabitant’s clear flank. The support extends only along the little route it has already made.

When the egg is safe, the inhabitant takes the loop off its wrist and puts it beneath the bench.

The power does not follow it there.

***

Relief takes up space in the day.

At first the inhabitant fills that space with more work. It clears both frames, visits every ridge, and brings so much film to a damp ledge that the grazers gather three deep.

Then, one afternoon, it sits beside the western pool without straining anything from the water.

A hunter rises beneath its hand. The inhabitant moves one finger. The hunter follows.

It moves the finger again.

The power remains near the shelter. It has learned that being present does not always require being close.

Later, the inhabitant takes out the pierced nodules.

It tries one, then another. At the sound that once disturbed the blunt-headed creatures, it immediately covers the hole with its palm. After waiting, it selects a different nodule and produces a long, rough note.

The power answers with a small vibration in the bench.

The inhabitant stops.

It holds the nodule against its breathing fold again. This time the note is shorter.

The bench answers for exactly as long.

For a while they do this badly. The power’s replies are too clean. It learns to let them fray at the edges. The inhabitant discovers that turning the nodule changes more than pitch.

Rain begins overhead. The little hard impacts enter the pauses.

Neither tries to make them keep time.

***

At the northern hollow, the power is slower.

It has seen the swelling beneath the ribs. It has examined, as gently as it knows how, the threads within the new lobe. It cannot find a separate creature waiting to be released.

It does not conclude that nothing happened there.

When the inhabitant visits the nodule, the power comes only as close as it is invited. Sometimes the inhabitant puts a hand through its clear shape. Sometimes it turns its back and bends low over the opening.

Once, after a particularly deep report, it lifts the nodule and holds it toward the power.

The power receives it.

Warm water remains inside the hole. There is mineral on the inner wall, a trace of cream film, a vibration passing up from the stone. The power listens through the interval between reports.

It could make an answering sound.

It returns the nodule without doing so.

The inhabitant holds it for a while before putting it down.

That evening it sits on the northern rise. The power settles nearby, low enough not to obstruct the cloud. They remain there until the seams brighten and the first blunt-headed creatures emerge with pebbles in their mouths.

***

There are still difficult days.

A large arrival leaves the inhabitant lying on its side beside the fresh stone. It refuses the support at its shoulder but catches the pale loop with two fingers.

The power steadies the ground beneath it.

For several hours there is one place on the shelf that does not ask its body to account for any new distance.

The inhabitant sleeps there with its eyes closed.

Nearby, the grazers begin their clean tracks across the lobe. A frame shortens. Water beads along an overhang that did not exist that morning.

When the inhabitant wakes, it releases the loop.

The power lets the ground move again, slowly enough that the fingers resting on it do not tighten.

***

Much later, a second hollow appears beside the nodule at the northern edge.

The inhabitant makes it itself, using the smooth end of the root. It takes several attempts. Twice the stone rises back into place. The third time, the inhabitant presses a little softened mineral around the rim and waits.

The hollow stays.

It is shallow, scarcely large enough to hold a spoonful of rain.

The inhabitant looks toward the shelter, where the clear shape rests against the wall.

Then it goes away to feed.

At the next report from below, both hollows contain water.

One holds the pierced nodule, turned away from the wind.

In the other, a drop remains after the rest has soaked into the stone.
