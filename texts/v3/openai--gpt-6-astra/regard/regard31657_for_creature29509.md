The power first notices the bowl.

Not the clouds beneath the paving, nor the lake where the shadows of absent things go on traveling. These it could understand by looking. The bowl requires longer.

It has been carried a considerable distance by someone whose chin hurts when the ground begins to grind. It has been fitted beneath a groove outside a house where no one sleeps. A thin shim keeps it level.

The power watches the door-maker leave. Then it stays through the afternoon to see whether the bowl works.

It does.

***

There are many things the power could change.

It could give the basin a single, unvarying down. Water would collect in the lowest places and remain there. The paths would become easier to explain.

But several inhabited rooms would flood. The films on the feeding slabs would lose their appointed hours of immersion and exposure. The western lanes would cease exchanging water, and the small wheel would stop.

It could bring soil, seed, soft weather. It could cover the pale rock in green.

For a while it follows this possibility far enough to see roots entering the sleeping recesses, see unfamiliar matter darken the drinking water. Then it lets the possibility go.

The basin is not an unfinished version of another country.

Still, there is the old person whose foot no longer closes. There are hands torn by failing braid, and stretches of glare that make journeys painful. There is the door-maker sitting motionless after the evening sound.

The power has no wish to admire these things from a distance.

***

It comes into the northern court in a shape small enough to use the door.

Its body is dark and plainly unfamiliar. It has no pale plates, no repaired places, no marks suggesting a history it has not lived. Water rests on its surface without entering it.

It waits beside the groove stone.

The first person to approach pours water into the groove, watches it descend, and gestures toward the safe passage out of the court.

The power stays.

The person pours again.

Only then does the power understand that it has chosen to stand in the way.

It moves.

This is the beginning of its instruction.

***

Learning to speak takes less time than learning when a statement has ended.

A hand left on a wall may be part of a question. Three clicks may mean agreement, amusement, or that grit has entered a joint. The power listens with its whole temporary body. It learns which bowls it may touch.

When it can make itself understood, it offers to help.

The northern elder extends the damaged foot.

This is an answer simple enough to begin with.

The power examines the foot, explaining what it finds by tracing shapes in wet grit. A torn fold has healed against itself. A channel that should carry moisture into the inner joint is almost closed.

It could make the foot new. Instead, it asks the elder to show how it ought to move.

The demonstration takes most of the morning. There are positions the elder remembers but can no longer achieve, and one that it never could. They distinguish these carefully.

The repair is done in the floor channel, with two neighbors present. Halfway through, the elder taps the paving for a pause.

The power pauses.

Afterward, the elder grips a rounded stone with both halves of the foot. Releases it. Grips it again.

Then it asks whether the power has eaten.

***

News travels through the courts.

Some bring injuries. Some bring broken tools. One person brings a feeding slab that has produced almost nothing for eleven seasons and remains unconvinced when told that the difficulty is in the stone.

The children bring the pebble from beneath the ledge.

The power knocks it down.

This causes considerable dissatisfaction.

It puts the pebble back and asks to be shown the proper way to fail.

For several afternoons, this is among its duties.

***

It does not repair the door-maker’s chin at once.

The wedge fits poorly along its inner edge. Beneath it, there is a small wound that opens with each evening vibration.

When the power indicates the wedge, the door-maker covers it with a hand.

So the power leaves it alone.

Later, the door-maker comes to the room the power has borrowed and sets down a bowl of water. It touches its chin, then points across the court.

The power now knows who lived there.

It also knows that this does not tell it everything the wedge means.

They work out a repair that keeps the yellow stone. The power smooths only the hidden edge and restores the tissue underneath. The fine stitches remain visible.

That evening, when the grinding passes, the door-maker rises with the others.

For a moment it seems uncertain what to do with the time.

Then it goes outside.

***

The sound below is more difficult.

The power descends through a crack and through the white weather beneath it. Far below, it finds the great surfaces moving against one another.

The basin’s changing pull passes through them. Their motion belongs to the place, but one bearing face has begun to split. Each evening a little more of its load falls upon the neighboring stone.

Eventually, the western courts will move.

Not in the familiar way, when height changes and position remains. The fins themselves will break. Some rooms will close; others will open into the sky beneath the floor.

The power returns before morning.

It makes a model in a shallow tray, using layers of different-colored grit. The inhabitants gather around. As it explains, several reach into the model and correct the positions of seams it has misunderstood from above.

They have felt the trouble for a long time.

They have also disagreed about it for a long time.

The door-maker places one finger where the empty shelter stands.

Someone else pushes that finger aside and indicates a route farther north.

The discussion lasts through two changes of pull. Fresh water is brought. The power answers questions, revises its model, and waits while people go to fetch those who cannot cross the courts that day.

There are ways to take the failing load without stilling the basin. None need destroy a shelter. Two would change the timing of the western water, and one would dry a seep whose feeding stone cannot be moved.

They choose another.

***

The work takes nine evenings.

At first the power bears the damaged part from below while the people listen above. In every inhabited court, hands rest on the paving. They report changes in the water, in the routes, in the pressure inside their bodies.

On the fourth evening, a passage becomes light too early.

The power withdraws the new support by a small amount. The passage returns to its old behavior.

On the seventh, the wheel turns longer than it should.

They adjust again.

The new bearing is broad, fitted to the slow movement it must carry. It will wear, but its wearing surfaces can be renewed from a chamber reached by a safe passage. The power leaves no hidden requirement for its own return.

When the work is finished, the deep grinding remains. It has lost the harsh interruption that made the door-maker move its hand across the floor.

Above, someone strikes a bowl.

Others answer through the stone.

***

There are smaller works afterward.

A supply of good fiber. Shade at two exposed resting places. A way to rinse the food films that saves a little labor without changing their taste. Repairs to the channels beneath the washing shelves.

Not everyone wants something changed. Not everyone who refuses at first refuses later.

The power remains easy to find.

Sometimes it is in the northern court, doing nothing visible. Sometimes it is beneath a ledge, failing to dislodge a pebble while children click with delight.

And one morning it accompanies the door-maker beyond the inhabited fins.

They carry a handline and a shallow bowl. The power could carry both without effort, but the door-maker has given it the bowl.

At the empty shelter, the door still turns.

The door-maker examines the groove, then passes over the slender pick.

The power kneels beside the standing stone. It loosens a grain and lifts it out on a wet fingertip. Then another.

When it reaches too deeply, the door-maker touches its wrist.

It waits to be shown.
