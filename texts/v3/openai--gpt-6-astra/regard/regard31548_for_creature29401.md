The power first attends to the stillness.

It does not assume that the animal is afraid. Weight has crossed the walkway; the plates have answered; five tips have ceased moving. That is what has happened. Beneath the ridge, a gripping pad remains against the metal beside the eggs.

The power waits through a flood.

Then another.

It learns how much of the old animal’s apparent rest is work.

The drain must be held almost closed, but not closed. One breathing fold must remain in the current while another presses against warmth. A narrow part of the body reaches through the inspection seal and keeps a damaged bypass from returning to its seat. If that part relaxes, the hollow slowly loses heat.

There is no position in which every part can rest.

The power also attends to the machinery beyond the retaining wall. Water leaving this basin enters other chambers. Heat borrowed here is heat unavailable somewhere else. There are lives in places the animal has never tasted, and loads carried by the pumps that cannot simply be put down.

For a while, nothing visible changes.

Then a maintenance vehicle takes an unfamiliar route.

***

It stops well outside the yellow boundary.

Its probe descends onto the plate where the animal’s damaged listening tip has been searching. The probe makes no sound beyond the sound of its own weight. After a time it lifts.

On the following cycle, the vehicle returns with a short length of metal.

It is an ordinary repair: a flexible bridge across the displaced joint, fastened at one end and allowed to slide at the other. It does not restrain the rising floor. It carries very little load.

It carries vibration.

When the distant pressure door closes, the old animal lifts four tips.

The fifth remains down.

At the next report, it lifts with the others.

The vehicle has already gone.

***

The power could make the floor warm everywhere.

It does not.

The translucent swimmers gather at the edges of particular temperatures. The stiff leaves require a cold interval. There are organisms inside the filter weave for which the sudden arrival of generous warmth would be a catastrophe.

Instead, beneath the ridge, a failing connection is repaired.

The improvement is small enough to pass at first for a favorable change in the pumps. The warm interval lasts a little longer. At its end the metal cools less sharply.

The animal uncovers the eggs.

Several minutes later than usual, it covers them again.

Over the next few cycles, the narrow appendage in the inspection seal loosens. It presses once, encounters the familiar resistance, and waits for the familiar consequence.

Warmth continues without it.

The animal does not immediately trust this. It keeps the appendage there for days.

Eventually it draws back a length no wider than a cable. Then another.

The wet passage it leaves behind is dark and quite empty.

***

The drain is more difficult.

The packed felt is habitat. Several small animals live inside its loosened edges, and roots have entered the upper layer. Removing the obstruction would restore the intended flow and destroy much of what the old animal has made possible.

Leaving it alone would mean leaving the animal at its work.

The power makes another route.

A shallow overflow is opened beyond the eggs, high enough to retain the animal’s pool, low enough to release the dangerous part of the flood. Its entrance is broad. There is no narrow throat to catch a young animal, no sharp suction to pull a breathing fold against a grille.

At first the old animal blocks it.

The next tide, the water rises in the familiar way, and its broad pad labors at the old drain.

The power leaves the new route where it is.

Two cycles later, the animal uncovers a small part of the entrance. Water escapes. It covers it again.

For several floods this continues.

Then, during the fullest water, the pad at the original drain lifts clear.

It hangs in the pool for a moment, slowly changing shape.

Nothing requires it to press down.

***

There are other changes, most of them beyond the ridge.

The maintenance vehicles begin carrying small channels up to the tower aprons. These hold a little water after the collectors empty. Young animals still venture too far. Some shelter in the new channels, tapping their way beneath covers that keep the cold metal from touching their backs.

An inspection of the flexible membranes now includes the hollows beside them. Where a pressure change threatens to flatten a shelter, the vehicles support the plate before relieving the channel below.

Their records acquire distinctions they did not previously contain.

A blockage can be removed.

A water-retaining structure may need an overflow.

The two sometimes look almost exactly alike.

The power makes sure the vehicles can ask for another inspection.

***

It does not take the eggs away.

It does not give the old animal legs suited to a different life, or lift the roof to show it a sky. The animal knows a great many things about this place. Its body reaches along routes it has learned slowly, through failure and warmth and the taste of returning water.

There is room beside that knowledge for something new.

Under the adjacent plate, the power prepares a hollow. Its entrance faces the old one. The water is of the same water; the floor carries the same pump note. A strip of filter felt runs between the two.

For a long time, only the young use it.

They leave shreds there. Roots follow.

Then one night, during the six-hour flood, a pale fold extends out of the old shelter and lies across the felt.

The fold stays there after the water falls.

The power maintains the warmth beneath both plates.

***

The deposits in the old animal’s skin are not all harmless.

Some bear weight now. Some close wounds. Some pull living tissue taut whenever the animal contracts.

The power distinguishes them.

Its help arrives slowly, at the edges where release is possible: a little hard material softened, a little injured tissue allowed to close without another tide tearing it open. Nothing is peeled away merely because it was not originally part of the body.

A breathing fold that had become fixed to the cladding lifts a fraction from the metal.

Water passes beneath it.

The animal settles it down again.

Later it lifts the fold farther.

There is no great restoration visible from the walkway. The missing tip does not appear in a flash of pale light. The ridge remains crooked, its handrail almost horizontal. But the animal can shift its weight without pulling against one particular place that has hurt for a long time.

It shifts often during the next cycle.

***

Near the end of the cold interval, tapping begins inside an egg.

A vehicle is approaching along the walkway.

Before its front wheels reach the first support, it stops. Its amber lamp continues to turn. Water ticks against its housing.

Beneath the ridge, the parent moves a gripping pad against the plate.

The tapping inside the egg resumes.

The vehicle waits until the exchange is over, then crosses at reduced speed.

There will still be noise in the basin. The pumps must lift. Doors must close. Young animals will leave their shelters, and the old one will not be able to follow all of them.

But the power has arranged for care to remain after its attention turns elsewhere. Spare parts are stored above the flood line. The overflow can be cleaned without entering the hollow. The vehicles’ new instructions will survive the replacement of a controller.

And beneath the tilted handrail, during the next high water, the old animal withdraws its pad from the drain.

It places the pad beside the eggs.

For once, it can leave it there.
