The power did not enter through the opening. To enter that way would have been to arrive first among the thorns, then the pale ledges, then the bats, as though nearness to the sky established an order of importance.

Its attention reached the undersides of the stones at the same time as the ceiling.

For a while, it changed nothing.

It attended to the difference between a sleeping animal and an exhausted one, between the stillness of waiting and the stillness of being unable to move. It followed the dampness into the sediment. It learned which crowded places were crowded because they were good, and which because there was nowhere else to go.

The bare shelves did not trouble it. Neither did the darkness. It had no wish to spread green over stone that could not sustain a root, or fill the dry channels merely because they retained the shape of water. A stream brought carelessly into this country would wash away whole inhabited margins. A little more light would not be a little change.

It listened through a day and a night.

At dusk its attention went out with the bats. Beyond the limestone were feeding grounds, water, stands of vegetation, distances an animal could cross easily and distances it crossed at a cost. The cavern’s apparent seclusion ended at each open mouth. The power followed these connections without mistaking the opening in the ground for the edge of the place.

Then it began, in small ways.

Beneath one slab, a cricket had caught a hind leg in a narrowing seam. Its movements drove the joint farther in. The power eased the pressure of the stone by less than the thickness of an antenna.

The cricket pulled free.

It remained still for a long time afterward. Then its antennae moved, touching the same stone that had held it, and it went beneath the ledge.

The slab settled into a secure position. Nothing living below it was crushed.

Elsewhere, the power found an old bat hanging apart from the densest cluster. There was damage that could be repaired, and damage that could not be repaired simply by making the animal young again without undoing much of what it was. The power was capable of such distinctions. It relieved the ache in the joints and cleared the effort from breathing.

When evening came, the bat left with the others. It did not fly as far as most of them. It found enough.

These acts did not settle the harder matters.

The snake needed food. So did the spider. So did the bat passing over the scrub, taking small flying bodies into its mouth. The power did not avert its attention from the prey, or pronounce fear good because something else depended on it.

Nor did it make a promise to one creature that it could keep only by quietly abandoning another.

It stayed with the difficulty. Where suffering could be lessened without transferring it to another body, it lessened it. Where it did not yet see how, it kept looking. Wisdom did not make every consequence simple.

Its larger work was mostly outside.

It kept poison from entering the feeding country. It preserved the ordinary passage of water through soil and stone, neither drying the little pool nor raising it beyond its rim. Where an intention formed to seal the long break in the limestone, it made the life below knowable to those who could choose otherwise.

It did not require visitors as proof that the place mattered.

Years could pass with no human foot on the first slope.

Below, the colony continued its disagreements over inches. Mothers returned to familiar voices. The few ferns held their shelves. Beneath a roost, a beetle stopped at a damp particle, fed, and moved on.

There was no brightness at the center of the basin, no new sound beneath the chittering.

But the power remained attentive, including during the long intervals when nothing needed doing. It did not grow impatient with lives spent beneath a single stone.
