The power did not descend upon the country.

Even a gentle landing would have been a weight.

It remained in the open air above the unfinished margins, where the pale straps turned in the wind, and attended first to what happened when one of them touched.

There was no single answer. Sometimes contact brought water. Sometimes it brought a bitterness that traveled upward through the strap and caused the growing tip to die. Sometimes the tip spread and thickened, and the whole margin settled a little, and a chamber far above stopped making the small, repeated sounds that preceded a tear.

The power watched long enough to distinguish settling from surrender.

It watched the brown pools, too. It did not clear them. Within their heat, substances were becoming available that the green surfaces could not have made for themselves. Nor did it mend every fallen support. Beneath one collapse, cups were opening on newly exposed water. A repair would have covered them.

For a time, its attention changed nothing.

Then, under the tilted pale wall, it found a chamber that was warming too quickly.

The roots had already withdrawn from the water. The surrounding growth had closed most of its channels. But one remained open: an old vessel buried inside a load-bearing rib. Through it, a thin current continued to enter the chamber, feeding the films below. Gas pressed against the ceiling. The rib could not close the vessel without weakening itself, and it could not thicken quickly enough to bear the rising load.

Above it lay a nursery of green crescents caught in the fibers of an overflow channel.

The power did not cool the whole pool. It did not kill the films.

At the upper end of the trapped gas, where an abandoned passage almost reached the outside air, it parted a thickness of dead tissue no broader than a leaf.

The first breath out was hot and sour.

For several days the opening wept. Its edges softened; a cream-colored film spread around them. Then the living tissue farther back began to turn outward. It lined the passage, raised a lip against the rain, and produced minute leaves along its sheltered side.

The power waited through two downpours.

The vent remained open. The rib held. In the overflow channel, the crescents were lifted away by ordinary water.

Only then did it leave.

***

What concerned it most was not the breaking.

It was how often the country learned of a danger only after it had become a wound.

A chamber could be overloaded for weeks before strain reached the tissues able to divert its water. A graft could begin to fail deep below a plain while both bodies continued adding weight above it. The country had many ways of responding, but distance and thickness delayed them. Its size concealed parts of it from other parts.

The power considered making every disturbance known everywhere.

It watched a tear send three conflicting messages across a valley, and declined.

Instead, it worked at a single joint.

Two bodies met there without exchanging fluid. One was old, dense, and slow to swell after rain. The other had spread across a pool in a single season. Their collars pressed together beneath a rising terrace. Each afternoon, as the younger body warmed and expanded, it pushed the older one a little farther out of alignment.

Between the collars, the power bent a loose fiber into an arch.

It did not pierce either skin. The fiber rested against them both. When the young tissue swelled, the arch flexed and pressed lightly against a growing surface several paces above the buried contact.

Nothing happened that afternoon.

After the next rain, a narrow rib began to form beneath the place it touched.

The power adjusted the arch once, reducing its pressure. Too much, and the rib would have become another obstruction.

Over the season, growth followed the changing load. A brace descended beside the older body rather than through it. The collars ceased climbing over one another. Between them remained a dark line into which rain could enter.

No sap crossed.

The younger body gained a support. The older one kept its closed skin.

The loose fiber eventually died. By then, other fibers carried the strain along the route it had indicated.

***

There were places where such work failed.

Around one new opening, growth formed a seal so quickly that the pressure returned before evening. The power opened it again, more gently, then watched the same response begin.

It stopped.

For a while it held the chamber from outside, taking a little of its weight. Within that interval, the country found a different answer. Water was diverted through a neighboring body. A low surface dried and stiffened. The chamber contracted, abandoning its outer vault.

The sealed opening remained sealed.

Elsewhere, a carefully relieved joint encouraged growth so rapid that it endangered the basin beside it. There the power withdrew its help by degrees, through a season of small increases in strain, until the growing margins turned elsewhere.

Its attention became slower.

It learned to look downstream of relief.

***

At the advancing edge, one strap hung beyond all possible purchase.

Wind had twisted it around itself. Its pale inner surface faced the sun. Water still reached its tip, but less each afternoon. Already its upper tissues were drawing useful substance back toward the country.

The power could have set a support beneath it.

There were hundreds like it. Beyond them would be thousands.

Instead, it looked upward along the strap, through the tissues it fed into, and found a narrow rim holding a shallow pool. The rim was crowded with sealed buds. Below it, the air was too dry; above it, the water had no outlet except evaporation.

The power lowered one small part of the rim.

Water crept over the lip and traveled down the twisted strap. For three days it dripped from the tip. On the fourth, the tip brushed another hanging growth that had bent toward the moisture.

They did not join.

Amber beads appeared along one surface. The other curled away.

The power left them apart.

The little overflow continued. Farther below, where neither strap reached, droplets collected in the cup of a floating rosette.

***

After many seasons, there were places in the high country that held differently.

Some chambers breathed through passages whose first openings no living edge remembered making. Some districts bore less weight because their water escaped sooner. Along certain deep boundaries, a movement on one side could be felt on the other without either body surrendering its vessels.

There were still collapses.

There were still brown pools, failed grafts, seedlings absorbed before they opened. The power did not make growth harmless to itself, or turn the country into a construction that needed its constant hand.

In one valley it had once supported a torn membrane through an entire storm.

Years later, it returned and found that the membrane had been dismantled from within. Its substance stood elsewhere now, in a low basin and three thick ribs. Between them was an opening clear through the country.

Warm air rose through it. Rain fell into it. Along its inner walls, small leaves grew in light that had never reached those depths before.

The power remained beside the opening for a while.

Nothing needed holding.
