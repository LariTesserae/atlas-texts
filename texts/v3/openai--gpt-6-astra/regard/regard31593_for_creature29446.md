The power comes during a pumping.

It notices the female rat first because she is awake. Behind the cable trays, she has lifted her head at the clack from the control box. Her whiskers point into the gap. She feels the motor through her jaw before the sound has fully entered the chamber.

But the power does not take her wakefulness as a claim stronger than sleep, or her size as a claim stronger than smallness. It attends also to the larvae where the water thins, to the spider holding its catch through the vibration, and to the worker who will come down again on Thursday with a headache already beginning in the heat.

It does not immediately stop the pump.

Above the chamber are rooms that must stay dry. Below the water are animals that cannot follow it down. The machinery has joined these facts without making any provision for their meeting.

The power begins there.

While the water retreats, the shallow hollows become connected. Not by a flood or a shaking of the ground: a few grains of clay loosen at the low ends of the excavator tracks. Threads of water persist where the floor would otherwise have shone and dried. The larvae move with them into the deeper groove.

The rat hears nothing new. When the motor stops, she comes out and follows the curb toward the stair.

Behind her, no pale bodies twist on the exposed clay.

—

The heat takes longer.

The power could make the chamber cold at once, but the rat has young neighbors in the nest, the pipes carry heat to people elsewhere, and some of the insects have only just unfolded their wings.

Instead, the failed insulation at the steam-main joints becomes conspicuous. A dark patch dries into a sharp-edged stain. The maintenance worker sees it on Thursday. His work order, unlike several previous work orders, reaches someone with the time and materials to act.

There is also a change no one records. Air begins moving through a ventilation shaft whose upper opening has been blocked since construction. A folded sheet of packaging shifts, works loose, and settles where it cannot obstruct anything else.

Over several nights, the chamber cools.

The handrail becomes merely a handrail. The rat can lie curled without waking to stretch herself flat. At the nest’s edge, one of her grown offspring sleeps beside her for most of an hour without either of them having to move.

There are still quarrels. The power does not mistake every cry for a failure. There is room, however, behind a second stack of trays: a dry recess, previously closed by a fallen length of conduit. Once the conduit has rolled aside, the rats find it themselves.

—

The worker is not made to love rats.

He sees the polished route along the curb and the gnawed insulation. He is right to worry about the cables. The power attends to this too.

The next repairs put sound covers over the vulnerable wiring. The transformer enclosure closes properly. The damaged brush seal above the stair is not repaired until another passage has become available—one that leads away from the basement food store, through a disused service run, toward the bank beside the railway.

The power does not carry the female rat through it.

For three nights she goes only as far as the first bend. There is a different draft there, and the smell of unfamiliar rats. On the fourth night she reaches soil that has never been pressed by an excavator. Roots hold its roof together. She spends a long time investigating a stone.

She returns to the cable trays before dawn.

This, too, is permitted.

—

The water requires several kinds of care.

The power does not preserve the warm sewage mixture simply because life has learned to use it. Nor does it empty the chamber in a single efficient morning.

The dirty deliveries diminish as drains are repaired above. The leaking joints are attended to in stages. Before each shallow patch disappears, there is a way out of it. Those animals that will become winged are given time to do so; those that cannot leave the water pass with a little of their silt into water that will remain.

Beyond the retaining wall, an old drainage channel opens into a shaded settling basin. There are stones there, and fluctuating margins, and places to retreat below the reach of a dry afternoon. It is not free of danger. Nothing waiting there has been instructed to stop eating.

The power keeps watch during the passage. What can be spared a crushing, a scalding, or a slow death in a disconnected cup of mud is spared it.

The lamp housings acquire small openings at their lowest corners. The flies inside find darkness beyond the covers and walk out.

—

For the seedling, the power does almost nothing.

It clears the grille.

Daylight reaches a little farther along the ledge. The plant’s next leaf is broader. When the street is washed, the force of the water breaks against a ridge before it reaches the stem.

The root continues sideways through the seam. There is no need to pull it straight.

—

Months later, the square is less abundant.

Someone counting bodies might call this a loss. There are fewer flies under the lamps, fewer larvae in the remaining water, fewer cockroaches crowded beneath the washers. There are also fewer bodies sealed inside the lights, fewer stranded mouths working against clay, fewer nights in which the female rat cannot find a surface cool enough to sleep on.

The chamber has not become a garden. Its columns still carry roads. The pump still starts. Repairs arrive unevenly, though they arrive.

The female rat uses two sleeping places now.

Sometimes she lies under roots beside the railway. Sometimes she returns below the last occupied street and sleeps behind the trays. Her split ear has not been made whole. It is healed, and does not trouble her; the power leaves it alone.

One evening she wakes in the square and does not rise immediately.

Water ticks into the narrow channel. Air passes over her whiskers. Beside her, there is space enough to turn.

She turns, puts her nose beneath her tail, and sleeps again.
