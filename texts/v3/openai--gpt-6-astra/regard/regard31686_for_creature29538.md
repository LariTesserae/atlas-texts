The power’s first kindness was to wait.

It did not bring rain.

Water was the first gift another benefactor might have thought of: a spring in the central court, a cloud held against the mountain, dew standing brightly on every fan. But the power followed such a gift into the hollow stems and under the sealed feet. It saw the living films loosen, the sheltered residues swell, the eggs lose through their membranes what they could not afford to lose.

So the night remained dry. The plants gathered their accustomed trace of vapor.

The power waited through the grazer’s next waking.

Beneath the copper roof, the animal had found a rib it knew. It pressed its brushes against the underside and fed for a little while. Then it moved forward until the crooked seam of its shell touched the roof, and stopped.

The power attended to that contact.

There was an old tenderness beneath the healed fracture. Most movements did not trouble it. A particular sideways pressure did. The grazer had learned to turn around certain stems in one direction rather than the other, carrying this small accommodation through years of otherwise unremarkable journeys.

Here, at least, no long deliberation was needed.

The power eased the tissue where it caught against the inner shell. It did not straighten the seam or take away the useful warning of pressure. It left the old shell old.

After a while the animal shifted. Its brushes opened again.

Nothing shone.

***

Over the following days, the power learned the western wall.

The new stone was not all equally sound. Below the lip, a narrow shelf extended farther than the grazer had tested. At its far end lay a shallow pocket, shaded through much of the morning. A mineral band ran into it. Given time, buried threads would arrive there, and fans after them.

But one portion of the shelf rested on a fracture.

It would bear the grazer’s weight now. It might bear it for several more seasons. Then a cooling night would open the fracture a little farther, and something using the shelf would fall with it.

The power held the stone from beneath and joined the two surfaces deep inside.

It left the outside rough. It left the empty pocket empty. There was still a steep descent beyond the shelf, and no food worth a journey.

When the grazer returned, its feet found no new taste.

It went only as far as before.

***

The power could have filled the pocket with mature growth. It could have laid a copper roof all the way around the mountain, with feeding films thick beneath every rib.

Instead, it watched the thread branching in the dried footprint.

The branch advanced, stopped, and sealed its tip. Another branch took a slightly different line. What looked from above like bare stone contained narrow differences that mattered to it.

The power followed those differences downward.

Under the enclosure, the mountain’s warmth moved through an intricate country of cracks. Some would close. Others would open. The central fracture had a long future, but not an endless one. Farther west, a second route for warmth approached the surface and ended against a thickness of unbroken rock.

The power made a small opening there.

Not enough to heat the shelf suddenly. Not enough to draw the inhabitants out of the place that sustained them. A little warmth entered the shaded pocket and stayed through the night.

For several years, this was of use chiefly to things too small for the grazer to eat.

The power did not hurry them.

***

There were deaths in the enclosure.

The power did not mistake every motionless body for a life in need of rescue. Nor did it turn away merely because suffering belonged to the place.

Once, a growing stem trapped a flat animal by two feet. The pull of its struggling traveled through the roof. The power eased the stem outward by less than the thickness of a shell margin, and the feet came free.

The animal remained pressed beneath the fan for the rest of the day. By evening it had moved to another rib.

Elsewhere, a predator fed. An empty egg skin was consumed. A fallen fan finally broke beneath no one’s weight, admitting a shaft of light into a passage that had been dim for decades.

The power allowed the light.

***

Much later, the pale grazer passed beyond the place where it had first withdrawn its foot.

Its shell was broader now, the newest band almost clear. Along the shelf, scattered growth had begun to hold the night’s vapor. There was no continuous roof, only a series of small shelters with exposed stone between them.

The animal tested the first shelter.

It fed briefly, turned with several careful adjustments, and returned to the enclosure.

The new passage remained behind it. Nothing required it to go farther.

Under its left forefoot, on the homeward journey, the stone held a faint trace of another grazer.

The power attended to that also.
