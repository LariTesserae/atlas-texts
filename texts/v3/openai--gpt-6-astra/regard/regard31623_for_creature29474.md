The power first notices the effort.

Not the size of the chip, or the smallness of the animal moving it, but the intervals in which nothing seems to happen. The hatchling holds the narrow end in its mouth. Its legs press backward. Beneath its feet the grit shifts, and the chip stays where it is.

It rests without letting go.

The power does not lift the paving stone. There is already enough light, and lifting it would change the temperature of every exposed surface. The animals beneath the steps would draw their legs inward. Water would leave the fringe of the nearest grazer. A current of unfamiliar air would pass through the eggs.

Instead, it attends more closely.

The hatchling is not trying to put on the chip. Not yet. It is trying to bring it beneath the empty roof, where there is room to work on it without being approached from above.

One corner has caught in a shallow pit.

The power loosens a grain at the bottom of the pit.

At the next pull, the chip moves.

The hatchling retreats so abruptly that it strikes the back of the shelter. It remains still for a long time. Then its mouth begins to travel along the fragment’s edge.

***

Having noticed one effort, the power finds it difficult not to notice others.

There is the animal beneath the step whose missing leg has left two young clasping a joint that can no longer be lifted quite clear of the metal.

There is the brass-colored predator, thirsty beside the swelling bead, with a grazer held in its other arm.

There is the grazer.

Attention does not make these interests agree.

The power stays through the falling of the bead. The predator returns its arm to the valve. The grazer falls on its side, rights itself, and passes beneath the bent lever. A little water enters the predator’s drinking groove. A little enters the channel. The rest spreads over a surface so thinly that it is gone before anything can reach it.

It would be easy to make another bead.

Before doing so, the power follows the first.

It sees the notch constrict, the water hesitate, the dampness enter a roof through a loosened rear plate. It sees eggs farther down that require drying between one wetting and the next. Beneath the translucent flake, the liver-colored growth has pushed into a seam where a lasting film of water would deprive it of air.

The valve remains as it was.

***

For several mornings, nothing in the hollow becomes noticeably easier.

The hatchling scrapes its fragment. The rust-colored grazer catches its blue fleck on a ceiling and backs out. The cold-pocket creatures gather beneath a forming drop.

Above, someone wheels out the bin.

Then comes the morning of washing.

The power has followed this water too.

It has gone beyond the paving stone, along the pavement to the bucket, and into the sharp-smelling liquid poured there. It knows what happens when that liquid crosses a soft underside. It knows why some of the eggs that swell afterward never open.

At the corner where the flood usually enters, there is an old channel in the stone’s frame, packed with grit.

The power clears it.

Not into the hollow. Grain by grain, the obstruction moves outward, onto the pavement, where a brush will presently scatter it.

The wash water arrives. It pools against the corner, rises, and finds the cleared channel. Most of it runs around the stone and continues toward the drain.

Below, the first animals have already begun to climb.

They wait on the walls. The hanging colony stretches toward the bolt. But the bitter water does not reach the lower step.

After a while one animal descends.

Others follow, some soon and some much later.

The change is not complete. A thin contaminated seep has entered through another joint. The power follows that also, and opens a way for it to leave without crossing the basin.

When rain next comes from the usual direction, it enters as before.

***

There are alterations the power considers and does not make.

A broad reservoir would give dependable water to the grazers and a dependable approach to the creatures from the cold pocket.

A smooth road would let the hatchlings travel without overturning. It would also bring the valve predator within easier reach of several nests.

A larger hollow would not remain empty.

So the power does not draw a finished place and fit the inhabitants into it.

It looks instead for losses that leave nothing behind but less possibility.

At the iron bracket, the eggs lie where condensation gathers into a heavy drop. Along one edge of the bracket is a burr of metal, almost sufficient to draw the water sideways.

The power bends it slightly.

Moisture still gathers around the eggs. But when the drop becomes heavy, it lengthens along the burr and falls beside them.

Below, the cold pocket receives the same water.

The following season, the uneven layer of eggs is wider.

***

The pipe will not always be warm.

The power learns this in the larger spaces beyond the wall, where there are other pipes, a room with a locked door, and a date written beside a plan.

It could keep this pipe warm forever. But it follows the plan farther. There are people above who also need warmth, and a leak beyond this one that has begun to damage the place where they live.

The work need not destroy the hollow.

On the day before the paving is marked for removal, the person examining the old line finds that one stone rocks underfoot. Kneeling to check it, she sees a white tiled shape move across a strip of light.

She stops.

The power does not supply her with understanding. It holds the light steady while she looks.

Later there is a photograph, badly focused. There is a second visit with a smaller lamp. The mark on the paving moves a short distance to the left.

Other provisions take longer.

***

Under the step, the hatchling has fitted its first plate.

It is not the whole triangular fragment. That proved too heavy. After much scraping, a narrow piece broke from one side, and this is what the animal carries.

Its roof slopes awkwardly. Black grit adheres to the clear substance at the back of its neck.

It goes out to feed.

At a shallow rise, its legs lose purchase. It slides backward, tries a different angle, and climbs over without help.

The power is still there.

Beyond the shelter lies the amber channel, and beyond that the long route toward the three green blades. A shadow crosses the daylight. The hatchling stops beneath its plate.

When the shadow passes, it extends its head again.

Nothing summons it onward.

After a while, it goes.
