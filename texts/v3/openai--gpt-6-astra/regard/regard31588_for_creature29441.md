The power first made certain that the foot was not trapped.

It was not. The yardback could lift it, and did, letting the pale root move a little as the weight came off. Then it put the foot down crookedly again.

So the power left the root where it was.

For several days it did nothing that could have been distinguished from paying attention. It followed the warmth through the buried pipes. It watched water collect, hesitate, and fall. Beneath the platform, where several animals lay together, it learned which movements meant sleep and which meant that an animal on the inside wanted to get out.

It did not take their stillness for contentment. Nor did it take it for distress.

The fans required particular care. Their beating passed through the towers and into the paving, and from the paving into the broad, lightly resting bodies. To silence them would have been easy. But the power listened through an annual stoppage, staying with the yardbacks as they emerged and lowered their heads.

There was something to hear beneath the machinery.

That did not mean they wished to hear it all day.

The power waited through the restarting, too.

Its first alteration was inside a ceramic outlet behind the ferns. A chip had lodged crosswise in the bore. Water could pass around it, and so could the head and shoulders of a young yardback; the animal’s plates, opening slightly as it reversed, could not.

Nothing had happened yet.

The chip loosened during the night and came out with a small discharge of rusty water. A maintenance worker found it the next morning. He turned it over in his hand, felt the hooked edge with his thumb, and put it in the rubbish rather than leaving it on the ground.

The power found this satisfactory.

It was careful with the workers. They already knew things it had only lately attended to: how much weight a small yardback concealed, how to offer the wet rope without catching the lip, which valve could be cleared with a brush and which required taking apart. They were also tired at the ends of their shifts, and sometimes had to choose between doing something gently and doing it before a foreman came looking.

Where it could, the power removed that choice.

A lifting bracket that had always jammed began to move freely. The inspection lamp held its charge. An order for replacement grilles arrived with the proper fixings, rather than ones that almost fitted. These were modest interventions, but they left people with both hands available and several minutes to spare.

The power did not arrange for anyone to be praised for work another person had done.

At the loading platform it made a more visible change.

There was a place where a handcart could roll against the only wide entrance beneath the boards. No one intended to close the animals in. Nevertheless, twice in one week an adult had waited with its head just inside the opening until someone moved the cart.

A low stone appeared beside the platform, firmly bedded and inconvenient only to a wheel.

The track man examined it. He assumed that someone from the other shop had put it there. After a few days he painted its upper edge white.

Under the platform, the entrance remained dark and open.

Water was less simple. The power could have brought a spring into the yard, but the dry ground was inhabited too. There were eggs beneath the powdery gravel, and small lives that would have drowned in a kindness intended for something larger.

Instead it made a reserve beneath the western basin: a close, cool cavity that filled only from overflow. During ordinary weather nothing changed. The dark mark remained on the concrete. The robin kept its position.

When a pipe was disconnected for repairs in late summer, the basin continued to wet its rim.

The repair took three days. On the fourth, the ordinary supply resumed. No new patch of greenery appeared.

The rising ground received the longest attention.

The power followed it below the fresh fractures, farther than the roots and farther than the warmth of the workshops. It could have stopped the movement. It could have bound the grey material into an unbroken mass, or softened it until the yard settled level around the brass survey studs.

It did neither.

There were lives using the fractures. There were structures bearing on the ground. There were people whose work depended on the tracks remaining passable. None of these needs could be met indefinitely by pretending the rise was not occurring.

At the next survey, the measurements were unusually clear. A stud previously obscured by a cable was accessible. The old notebooks were found in a dry cupboard. Taken together, the figures showed where the next strain would come.

The necessary work was approved before it became an emergency.

Workers opened a narrow strip beside the ridge. They supported the pipework on joints that could accommodate movement. Where the excavation crossed an animal passage, they laid a short, broad sleeve and left its floor rough.

For two nights the yardbacks would not enter it.

On the third, one passed through, stopped, and came back. By the end of the week there was grey dust on both sides of the sleeve and a soft underside mark along its middle.

The power did not require the animals to understand any of this.

It offered them no vision of their benefactor. It did not make them gather in the open or look upward. Their heads were suited to the seams between things. Most of what they needed to know arrived through the ground, the wet edges of outlets, the touch of another body in shelter.

The power met them there.

Years later, when one workshop closed and its warm pipes began to cool, there would be another decision to make. The power would attend to that change when it came, with the same care it had given the hooked ceramic chip. It would not preserve every belt and motor merely because life had learned to use them. Neither would it allow their removal to erase that life unnoticed.

For now, the fans continued.

Beside the newest crack, the yardback rested with its foot turned slightly outward. Dust collected against its downhill side. Once, it lifted its head and made a short sequence of dry clicks.

Nothing answered in words.

The basin was wet. The passage beneath the platform was clear. Under the animal’s foot, the root had room enough to thicken.
