The power notices the wet grass first.

Not because the animal is unimportant beside it, but because the water is moving while she is away. Beneath the slab, a passage has narrowed. Fine fragments carried down from higher ground have lodged against a lip of stone. The next thaw will bring more water than the remaining opening can pass.

The nest will flood before she is ready to move it.

The power follows the water downward. There are warm chambers below, and living films along their walls. Farther on, the passage divides. One branch returns to the tarn beneath a shelf of ice; the other ends in loose gravel, still cold from winter. There is room there.

A few grains shift.

The obstruction does not vanish. The water simply finds a lower way around it, taking some of the finer ash along. Beneath the nest, the damp patch stops spreading.

The power waits until the flow has settled. It does not send the water into another occupied hollow.

Then it attends to the animal.

She is feeding beneath the edge of the tarn ice, where larvae have collected in a shallow pocket. Her fur is wet along the belly. She has found enough food to delay returning, though not enough to stop searching. Every few moments she lifts her head, chewing, and tests the air.

The power could put her somewhere soft.

It considers what that would mean: an unfamiliar floor, unfamiliar air, food without any of the small signs by which she recognizes food. Her young elsewhere, unless they too were taken. Warmth she could not leave, perhaps. Safety with no understandable boundary.

It begins instead with the shelter she has chosen.

The second exit is loose but passable. The roof is sound. Beneath the chamber, however, a narrow fissure communicates with hotter ground. It is quiet now. In several days, pressure will rise there.

The power gives that pressure another outlet, well away from the chamber. It leaves the small, steady warmth that reaches the floor.

At the entrance, a fragment settles into a firmer position. It will no longer turn under her feet when she climbs with a young one in her mouth.

These things are done before she returns.

She finds nothing conspicuous. The old nest smells of her young and drying stems. They move against her when she lowers herself into the cup. One has become separated from the others by a fold of bedding. She draws it close with her muzzle.

The power stays through the night.

It learns the difference between her ordinary pauses and the stillness of exhaustion. It learns which passages she uses when carrying food, which she avoids after rain, which narrow opening admits her easily but would catch the body of a growing youngster. It does not require her to understand that she is being watched over. There is nothing she must do to deserve another safe return.

When she begins the move, two nights later, the cloud is low.

She carries the first young one across the open crust, stops beneath the stained lip, and climbs. The stone holds.

On the second journey, snow begins to collect in her coat.

On the third, she pauses longer at the edge of cover. Her mouth is full. The young one hangs quietly, its pale feet drawn in. Beyond the cloud, a bird passes along the ridge.

The power does not strike the bird or blind it. Farther down the basin, where it is already turning, a break in the mist reveals a place to feed.

The mother crosses.

By morning, all three young are in the new shelter. She sleeps with her body curved around them, the nest crowded enough that one small foot rests against the wall.

There will be other needs. The power has seen how quickly a safe hollow can become a trap, how little reserve the mother carries between one meal and the next. A single fortunate night will not be enough.

Along the basin’s warmer margins, it begins making room: dry recesses above the reach of floodwater, passages with more than one way out, sheltered connections across the longest stretches of bare ground. It works around roots and nests and the minute lives in the seep margins. Where its work changes the movement of water, it follows the consequences.

It does not hurry the basin into a forest. It does not make the animal wait for soil.

When the young begin to explore, there will be places within reach.

For now, the mother wakes. She eases herself out from beneath them and moves toward the entrance. Outside, water beads on the stones. Her whiskers touch the air.

She goes left.

Behind her, the bedding stays dry.
