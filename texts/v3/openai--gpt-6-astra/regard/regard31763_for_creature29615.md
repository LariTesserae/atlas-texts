The power did not begin by bringing light.

There was enough light for the lives that had formed there, and some of them depended on its insufficiency. Nor did it draw off the water. The terraces were not a flooded street to the fingers working beneath their lips.

For a while, it attended.

It followed the water back through the walls and forward into places where the names on the engineering drawings no longer corresponded to anything built. It learned which seepages fed the clear folds, which carried food, which merely carried the slow dissolution of a foundation. It listened when the covers rose. It distinguished the trembling that belonged to the machinery from the trembling that passed through the creature’s back.

It watched the creature return a porcelain fragment to the ledge, turn away, and come back to tilt it slightly.

This, too, it took seriously.

▪

The first alteration was far above.

A proposal to fill an obsolete service void with aggregate reached a desk where, ordinarily, it would have been signed. The person at the desk noticed that the drainage survey was missing two pages. Asking for them delayed the work. Finding that they had never existed delayed it further.

Someone was sent down.

The power did not make this person especially brave or tender. She was already careful, which was more useful. At the bottom stair she stopped to check the water against her drawing. Then she checked the drawing against the seven doorways.

She spent the morning discovering that the street could not safely be treated as empty.

Her report was not lyrical. It established connections, loads, access requirements. It made the proposed filling expensive in ways that could be entered into columns. The street remained.

There were protections the power could have imposed more magnificently. This one would have people to maintain it.

▪

Beneath the retaining wall, one stone bore on another through a contact smaller than a thumbnail.

The power relieved it.

Nothing visible moved. A little weight found a broader route into the earth. The arch retained its shape, its rind, the polished places where wrists had passed. But it would no longer narrow each year.

The power could have opened it wide enough for the creature to enter upright. It did not know whether the narrowness was merely an inconvenience. Small entrances also kept things out.

Instead, it loosened a stone at the side where there was no load to carry. It made the mortar there friable, and left the stone firm enough to stay unless deliberately worked.

For eleven visits the creature did nothing.

On the twelfth, it reached into the joint with one of its fine fingers.

It brought the edged stone from the ledge. Over several nights it removed a shallow crescent from the opening, low down, on the side nearest its burden. It carried the fragments away rather than leaving them in the current.

Afterward, its chest still changed shape as it passed through. But the pouch no longer brushed the lower stone.

The power left the rest alone.

▪

The covers were more difficult.

Their rising belonged to a system that extended below the oldest foundations. Stopping them would have brought other pressures elsewhere. Making them move more often would have changed the water.

But the creature’s long wait against the wall was not necessary to that system.

There was an old coupling inside the pumping station. It had worn unevenly. After each interruption it sometimes caught, holding the flywheel still until enough weight accumulated to force it past the damaged surface.

The power could have smoothed it in an instant.

Instead, on the next maintenance visit, the coupling made a noise it had nearly made for forty years.

The worker heard it. He waited. When the cover rose, he read the surviving notice and kept his hands away.

Later, he opened the housing.

A replacement had to be made. For a week there were measurements, conversations on the stair, the unfamiliar smell of clean machining oil. The creature spent much of that time beneath the loading platform. The power did not pretend that every useful thing could happen without disturbance.

When the work was finished, the flywheel resumed within a few breaths of the cover lowering.

The creature remained at the wall.

It waited through a second advance of the spoke, then a third. Finally one hand came away from the brick. It held that hand under its face before putting it into the water.

On later nights, it learned the shorter wait.

▪

The power considered warmth.

It considered the small luminous bodies pressed together beneath the creature’s breast, the cold held in the masonry, the white sleepers with their folded limbs.

It did not warm the street.

Beyond the arch, at one edge of the chamber where the creature rested, it let a little heat escape from a buried return pipe. Not enough to change the current. Enough to take the stored cold from a patch of stone.

Most of the chamber remained as it had been.

The creature explored the patch with its jaw, then with the underside of a wrist. It slept elsewhere. On another night it rested one forelimb across it.

Much later, a small body was set there.

Without the pouch around it, its light seemed weaker. It crouched on its broad feet. The larger creature lay close enough that two fingers touched its back.

The little one moved off the warmed stone into the cold water.

The fingers followed, then stopped.

It came back by itself.

▪

Not everything required an alteration.

The brass nut stayed on its side. No meaning was assigned to the hand that opened on the paving. The white animals were neither awakened into some supposedly higher life nor cured of being unfamiliar.

The creature continued to take food from beneath the granite lips. Small animals continued to withdraw from its fingers. The power did not make the street innocent.

It kept certain avoidable injuries from becoming part of the street’s dependability.

The electrical coverings were replaced before they failed. The replacement stair was given a backing that caught the harmful runoff without excluding the shrimp from the current. Maintenance records acquired a warning against flushing chemicals through the warm pipe. No photograph of the creature appeared on a public notice inviting visitors.

And when, one night, it emerged from the arch without its burden, the power did not take the absence for a loss.

Beyond the wall, several faint lights were moving separately.

One remained near the resting place. Another had discovered a crack through which water entered and was putting its fingers into it. A third approached the arch, withdrew, and approached again.

The larger creature waited outside.

It had somewhere else to go: the pipe, the ledge, a productive seam beneath the next terrace. For the moment it stayed with its forelimbs in the clear water.

At last a small foot appeared.

It tested the submerged stone with each toe.

Nothing hurried it.
