At first, the attention added nothing that could be distinguished from the weather.

It rested in the warm spray beneath the rim, traveled with the water through the hanging roots, and entered the continent by many routes. It spent a long time in a cracked return pipe. There, a colony fed upon a leak that the station above had been trying to locate for seventeen years. Closing the crack would save pressure. Closing it abruptly would kill the colony and the small, blind grazers nesting in its folds.

Farther down, the pipe passed through a chamber where three people had once died because a valve handle could turn after its stem had broken.

The attention stayed there longer.

It followed the guide cables. It learned the difference between a household mark and damage to a doorpost. It learned that the reed in one nursery had been deliberately mistuned because a child found the correct note unbearable. It learned which basin calls were ordinary, which were changing, and how little those categories had to do with whether a sound was painful.

When it finally approached someone, it did so along a floor.

***

The woman who first encountered it was cleaning her feet.

Her name, spoken properly, included a small movement of breath against the listener’s jaw. At a distance she answered to three descending knocks. She had spent most of her working life finding worn surfaces in the upper galleries. Now her hands were less reliable in cold weather, and she had to warm them before deciding whether a texture had become unsafe.

She felt footsteps enter the room.

They stopped at the distance customary for someone who had not been invited to sit.

“The scraper is beside the basin,” she said. “You may as well be useful.”

Something settled opposite her.

It did not take her foot immediately. A broad surface touched the back of her hand, waited, and turned beneath her fingers. There were no nails, no seams, no pulse she could find.

She withdrew.

“What are you?”

The answer came close to her jaw, in a voice that had evidently learned speech by listening to people at work.

“Someone who would like to help.”

“Help with what?”

There was a pause.

She disliked the pause. In her experience it preceded either a request for lodging or an explanation of why a repair had become more expensive.

“My feet, then,” she said. “Until you have thought of something.”

It cleaned the seams without scraping healthy skin. It found an infection beneath one of the hard islands of her heel, where she had not been able to reach comfortably for several years.

“This will hurt if I remove it quickly,” it said. “May I take longer?”

She put her foot back in its lap.

***

She did not believe it was a power of any particular importance until it described the broken valve.

Everyone knew the chamber had been sealed. Only a few people remembered why.

The following day she took it to the station council. It wore loose ceramic pieces at one ankle, at her insistence.

For much of the meeting, the council asked the wrong questions. Could it increase the pressure? Could it bore a new outfall through the western divide? Could it tell them whether the northern crews were actually as careless as everyone said?

It answered what it could. Sometimes it asked a question in return.

How much additional pressure would the oldest branch lines bear?

Who lived downstream of the proposed outfall?

Did anyone from the north wish to be present for that discussion?

Eventually the woman struck the table.

“It has found a broken valve in a sealed chamber. Begin with that.”

They did.

***

The first repairs were not impressive to anyone who had not needed them.

A drain cover ceased to rock beneath a person’s weight. A nursery acquired a second route to the ridge. The lower galleries, which depended upon a single air line passing through unstable ground, gained an independent supply.

The power could work stone without striking it. This was useful, but disconcerting. After a mason nearly stepped into an excavation that had not existed a breath earlier, they agreed that it would fence its work and knock before changing a floor.

It accepted correction without becoming cold or wounded. This, more than the stonework, began to alter people’s behavior around it.

They brought it mistakes they had concealed.

A crew admitted that a pressure test had been entered into the records without being performed. A household reported a warm patch behind its storage wall. Someone brought a child whose jaw membranes had never tolerated the ordinary noise of the settlement, and who had been called inattentive for failing to understand instructions shouted across workshops.

For that child, it helped make a quiet room and a set of hand signals. Later, the workshop crews adopted several of the signals themselves.

The infection in the woman’s heel was gone. Others came with their feet.

It did not make grooming unnecessary. People still sat in one another’s laps, arguing over food and marriage and the placement of tools. But fewer arguments were conducted around a pain nobody could afford to treat.

***

The basin keepers approached cautiously.

They were accustomed to being heard politely and funded afterward, if anything remained.

They brought six generations of fired records, wrapped separately so that the bundles would not abrade one another. The power read them with many surfaces at once, then asked the keepers to explain their arrangement.

One of them demanded to know why it needed an explanation.

“Because these ridges tell me what you recorded,” it said. “They do not tell me what you nearly recorded, or what you thought too uncertain to include.”

That discussion lasted eleven days.

Afterward it went down into the basin that no longer called.

The water there was warm again. The old diversion had been reversed, as the records said. Fine tissue drifted through the sounding lines. Small animals fed among the depressions on the bottom.

The being that had made the call was dead.

The power searched carefully enough that it would not mistake quiet for death, or a change of form for an ending. Then it returned and told the keepers.

One asked whether it could make the double note.

“Yes.”

“Would there be anyone making it?”

“Not the one you are asking about.”

They did not ask again.

The power remained with them while they handled the oldest strips. One keeper discovered a notation she had overlooked for years: an apprentice’s complaint about a sounding weight that would not stay where it was lowered. The dead being had moved it repeatedly toward a warm inlet.

They added that observation to the main record.

***

There were others alive beneath the basins.

The power did not announce what they were.

It learned where their tissue entered the fractured stone, where a change in water chemistry passed through them as slowly as weather through a forest, where the great pressure of a plateau’s lifting could be endured and where it tore.

One lay beneath three watersheds and had been injured by a boring head long before the current station households were born. Its flesh had grown around the abandoned tool. Each settling of the continent drove the teeth a little deeper.

The power approached through the water.

It offered contact, withdrew, and offered it again elsewhere. For a considerable time, the being answered only by contracting.

The keepers above measured the changes. They were frightened. Several wanted the encounter stopped.

It stopped.

During the next lifting, something rose beneath one of the instruments: a broad fold of living tissue, carrying the faint, regular tremor the power had used when it approached.

This time the keepers asked to go with it.

Their chamber was small, pressure-balanced, and connected to the ridge by two independent cables. Inside, they rested their hands upon a membrane through which the basin could be felt. The power extended the range of those hands without making the contact stronger than they could bear.

They felt water moving through an old wound.

They felt their own station’s pump cycle arrive there.

For the first time, the being below received that pulse accompanied by something else: the touch of the small lives responsible for it.

Nothing resembling a conversation followed. There was no account of ancient history, no forgiveness requested or bestowed. The keepers held still. The power waited.

When the tissue loosened around the buried iron, it removed one tooth.

Then it waited again.

The work took most of a season.

***

The station could not simply cease drawing water. Its food galleries, air supply, and neighboring households depended upon that work.

Nor could the being below continue to bear the old load.

So the power helped them build another way.

There were new shafts, spread across sound rock rather than concentrated at the injured fracture. There were reservoirs that could hold pressure through a lifting, and couplings that released before a distracted worker had time to finish one last task.

The leaking return pipe was replaced. Its old course remained as a narrow mineral stream, sufficient for the colony that had grown around it.

None of this required a keeper to persuade a hungry household that the household mattered less than a basin. The power supplied what had been missing: time, materials, strength enough to do two necessary things together.

At the keepers’ request, it also made instruments they could repair themselves.

The plans went out on fired strips. So did the corrections.

***

It remained on the plateau.

Not always in the same place, and not always in a shape that could sit across a foot basin. There were journeys beneath the escarpment, long attendances beside sick animals, arguments in councils that had initially expected agreement as part of a gift.

People learned how to call it. They also learned that calling it did not place it under their orders.

It refused to silence a neighboring station’s warning reeds during a dispute over water. It would not make a herd forget a killing. It helped build another route around the herd’s territory, and went with the first crew to use it.

The woman whose heel it had healed grew older.

Sometimes it returned to her room and was given the scraper. Her feet still needed cleaning. Its hands had become better at holding them.

During a lifting many years after its arrival, she waited on the ridge with a child against her chest. The waterfalls stopped. The familiar double note came from the far basin, followed by the rougher call of the being beneath the repaired fracture.

There was a new interval in that call.

The child moved a hand against her wrist.

“What was that?”

“I don’t know yet.”

She lowered the child until its palm rested on the ground. Beside them, the power did the same.

Under their hands, something vast adjusted its weight without driving against the old iron. Water entered the space where the wound had been.

They stayed to feel what happened next.
