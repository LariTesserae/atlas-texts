The power first noticed the scraping.

It was not the loudest thing in the country. Even within the fracture, water moving over a mineral lip nearly covered it. But the small animal paused between strokes, and in those pauses there was something to attend to: the testing of a surface, the adjustment of feet, the effort to remain where food had been found.

The power did not lift it from the stone.

It learned what the stone was to it.

The grain beneath the feet was not merely roughness. There were places where a foot could close securely and places where it slid. There were tastes that meant food would grow here, tastes that meant the water would burn the soft fringe. There was the open space behind it, which a human observer might have found unremarkable, and which the animal kept testing because no neighboring shell covered that side.

The power listened until it could distinguish feeding from the scrape made by an animal too weak to feed.

There was a great deal of the second sound.

***

For a while, its work was almost entirely beneath the surface.

A shifting bed of stone was about to close the channel supplying three crowded hollows. The power held it apart—not by much, only the width of a worm’s chamber—and let the warm water continue through.

Elsewhere, it loosened a blockage slowly enough that the returning flow did not scour the inhabitants from their pores.

It did not bring a river down over the ridges. There were animals for whom such abundance would have been a drowning. It did not tear the cloud roof open. The sheltered bodies had no protection against a long clear day.

Instead, drops formed beneath overhangs where young animals were already searching. Mineral films spread along the undersides of shelves. In places where generations of shells had polished the limestone too smooth for weakened feet, a fine grain returned to the rock.

An old animal, overturned by the collapse of a ledge, had been moving its feet in the air for most of a day.

The power turned it back.

It waited while the sensory fringe touched down, section after section, and the feet found purchase. Then it left the animal to choose a direction.

***

Food alone would not be enough.

The power watched a juvenile leave a crowded refuge for a newly wetted patch. Another caught its rim. Beneath the shelter of their shells, the struggle was scarcely visible.

It separated them with a bead of water.

Both held to the stone. Both began to drink.

That worked once. It was not yet an answer.

The power followed hunger into bodies: into the signals that became urgency, into the growth of eggs, into the way a successful season filled every new refuge until scarcity returned. It studied injury too. Withdrawal from a dangerous touch served a purpose. The prolonged distress of a body already beyond escape served none that it would preserve.

Where an animal was dying, it quieted that distress. It did not quiet the warnings of animals that could still get away.

And it began the slower work.

Under the limestone it made small trials, each no larger than an ordinary seep. There were films richer in nourishment but slower to spread, and soft mineral-fed growths with the warmth and chemical richness the black predators sought. Some failed. Some poisoned their own water. One drew so many mouths that the stone around it was stripped bare.

The power dismantled those gently and tried again.

It had no need to defend a first idea.

In time, a black animal settled over a dark cushion growing at a fissure mouth. Its feeding blade entered. Its gripping pads tightened. For hours its feelers rested loosely on the stone.

A pale grazer passed close enough to touch it.

The predator stayed where it was.

The power watched through many such passages before extending that growth to another basin.

***

The small shelled animal knew none of this.

Its fracture remained dim. Food still had to be rasped from the grain. Once, a falling chip made it close so hard that several feet lost their hold.

But the chip stopped just short of its rim.

Later, when the fracture dried, there was dampness within reach—not beneath its mouth, but a short distance to one side, discoverable by the fringe. It moved toward it.

At the new feeding place stood a low arch of limestone. The gap beneath was wide enough for its flexible shell and too narrow for a larger juvenile. It entered, turned, and fed.

Another young animal arrived before the next mist.

For some time they touched and withdrew. Each braced when the other moved. Then one found food on the left side of the arch and the other on the right.

Their scraping overlapped.

***

The country was not made quiet.

The power had learned that much of the roar was the sound of animals finding their way together. It kept the deep vibrations, the rainfall rattling on membranes, the small impacts by which a crowd made room for an injured member.

Nor did it require the inhabitants to become grateful, beautiful, or comprehensible to anyone watching from above.

It wanted the animal beneath the arch to have more than a brief escape purchased at another animal’s expense. It wanted there to be room when its shell hardened; a reliable taste of food; warnings that led somewhere safer. These things required work across whole watersheds and within structures finer than the sensory bristles.

So it continued.

Sometimes the basin fell still, and dripping could be heard below the ground.

In that interval, the power listened with everything else.
