The power first notices the foot against the wall.

It is a small thing to notice in a country that moves its valleys. Four toes hooked into fibers, the fifth resting flat. The animal is asleep. Its companion is drawing a piece of ice from the fur beneath its jaw, working carefully, warming it with the tongue before pulling.

Farther down, the closed valve holds.

The power does not touch it.

It follows the pressure instead, through the neighboring run, past a chamber where pulp has thickened in the cold, into a fan of narrow vessels supplying the eastern slope. There is no failure here. The tightening beneath the sleepers is part of a movement that will keep the exposed leaves alive until morning.

To stop it would be easy.

The power follows it farther.

***

For several nights, nothing changes.

Or nothing changes that the animals could distinguish from weather and growth.

The power learns the difference between an empty passage and a passage briefly unoccupied. It learns which dry chambers are nurseries, which are abandoned feeding pockets, which contain eggs waiting for warmth. It finds that the oldest ridges are not necessarily the safest, and that a root can transmit a warning faithfully for a hundred seasons before one healed fracture alters its meaning.

It attends to the living country without assuming that its movements are decisions.

It attends to the animals without mistaking their endurance for contentment.

In a shallow pocket, a mother sleeps around two young. One has fastened its mouth to the fur of her belly. The other lies with its chin on her wrist. Neither has yet learned how far danger can travel through a root.

Below them, a reserve channel begins to fill.

The mother wakes early. There is time. She carries the smaller young into another pocket, returns, carries the second, and then comes back once more for a mouthful of dry lining.

The power lets her work.

Three ridges away, an animal makes the same decision too late.

***

It is the white animal from the frost hollow.

A wall has folded behind its shoulders. Its head and forefeet are free, but the overlapping ribs cannot turn far enough. When it pulls, the tissue grips the fur along its flanks.

It stops pulling.

Its chin searches the floor.

The next contraction will reach the passage shortly. There is no route its feet can find.

The power holds the walls apart.

It does not hold the entire slope. A little sap takes a neighboring branch. A bundle of fibers bows outward by less than the width of a leaf. The root continues its work, but around a space that remains open.

The animal waits through the contraction.

Then it pulls one hindfoot forward, twists, and comes free.

For a while it stays flat against the floor beyond the narrowing. Its sides move quickly. A few white hairs remain caught in the seam.

When it has gone, the power releases the wall slowly enough that nothing feeding through it is torn.

***

An escape is not yet a remedy.

The power has seen the old hair in the closed layers. It has found chambers from which no tapping ever reached an occupied ridge. There are losses the animals can learn to avoid, and losses for which no amount of learning would have helped.

It begins beneath a worn root crest.

Here, several supply runs pass around a core of old, dry fibers. Keeping a small space open will cost little, provided the vessels are given somewhere else to grow. The power encourages a bend already beginning in one of them. It loosens a compressed layer above another.

Over many days, a cavity forms.

It is not warm enough to draw feeding colonies out of season. It is not damp enough to rot a winter coat. Its floor gives slightly under weight, then holds. There are three exits, all shallow, none opening into a vessel. When pressure increases outside, the routes leading away from it widen first.

The power follows every displaced flow until it rejoins the country’s ordinary work.

Then it waits.

A small brown creature enters and eats part of the lining.

Condensation gathers near one opening.

For two nights, no white animal comes.

On the third, one puts its head inside, lowers its jaw, and withdraws.

The power does not summon it back.

***

The cavity changes before it is accepted.

The animals prefer an entrance tighter than the one provided. They drag loose fibers into it and leave an awkward turn that the power would not have chosen. A second opening is used for droppings. The third remains almost untouched, though they inspect it often.

Four animals gather there during the first hard freeze.

One sleeps with a foot against the wall.

The power keeps watch beyond the reach of that foot.

Elsewhere, it makes other places—not a continuous refuge, not a single chamber into which everything must retreat, but scattered shelters reached by different routes. Some remain empty. Some are occupied by creatures it had scarcely noticed at first. Where an alteration causes trouble, it undoes it.

Along a dangerous crossing, the power makes the tightening perceptible sooner.

At a reservoir, it strengthens the fibers from which the animals hang to drink.

Under a nursery, it keeps both exits open through a night when ordinarily only one would have survived.

It continues to attend to the small grazers, the new leaves, the dark water below the ice. Relief in one place must not become an unseen injury farther down.

There is a great deal to learn.

***

By late winter, the white animal has found two of the shelters.

It does not live in either of them all the time. There are feeding seams elsewhere, familiar passages, another animal whose feet carry an interesting odor from beyond the western ridge. It still crosses cautiously. It still makes mistakes.

Once, returning with an injured forefoot, it finds that the final narrow climb yields beneath its weight just enough to give it purchase.

It enters, turns twice, and lies down beside its companion.

Outside, a basin begins to drain. The movement travels through the old crest and reaches the chamber as a faint, ordinary strain.

Both animals wake.

Their jaws touch the floor. They wait.

The strain passes. The exits remain open.

The injured animal settles first. Its companion grooms the fur around the swollen foot, then curls against it, dark belly to dark belly.

Much later, in sleep, the white animal draws its good foot away from the wall and folds it beneath its chin.

The power remains.
