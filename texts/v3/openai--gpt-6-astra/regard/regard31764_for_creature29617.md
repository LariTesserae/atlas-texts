The power notices the moving antenna.

It does not mistake the movement for a greeting. Nor does it need the shrimp to know that it has been noticed.

For a time it attends to the water.

There are forces here that do more than injure. The current brings food and carries waste away. Warmth permits the animal to live where another kind of animal could not. Even the thin stream entering the pocket has its use. To stop everything would make a stillness in which the shrimp might slowly die.

The power follows the water beyond the chamber. It finds the beds through which it passes, the gates, the places where pressure accumulates before a valve shuts. It attends also to the people above: a worker resting one hand on a hot bearing housing, a woman filling a kettle before her shift, someone asleep in a room kept habitable by machinery they have never seen.

It will not turn off their water.

Neither does it accept that the small animal must continue to be struck by it.

At the next acceleration, the pipe releases its cone of bubbles. The washer rises. The cable pulls straight.

Inside the missing brick, the shrimp’s tail lifts, but less than before.

A change has occurred along the upper edge of the recess. It is very small: a roughness in the mineral deposit, scarcely more than the beginnings of a fold. Water meeting it turns outward rather than driving straight down the back wall. Enough still enters to renew the pocket. The hardest part of the surge passes across its mouth.

The shrimp adjusts its feet.

The power waits through the slowing of the pumps. This is the change it is most careful about. A shelter against one direction can become a trap when the direction reverses.

Water slips behind the animal.

This time, its feet do not leave the masonry.

Over the following days, the fold grows into a short, blunt lip. There are no sharp edges. Behind it, a narrow strip of rough surface runs from the old corner toward a deeper hollow. The shrimp investigates the strip in portions, returning between excursions to the position it already knows.

The power does not hurry it.

It could put the animal in a quiet pool beneath leaves. But there would be different water there, unfamiliar food, mouths waiting among the roots. It could construct a pool without mouths. That would require other decisions, and decisions after those.

Here it knows something useful: the shrimp has eaten, held on, grown, and sometimes ventured out.

It begins with making those things less difficult.

The deeper hollow is large enough for the animal to turn without pressing its tail against the wall. Fine irregularities give purchase to its feet. The flow through it is slow but continuous. Nothing closes behind the shrimp when it enters.

A bacterial film develops on the new surface. The power has attended to what the animal actually eats, not merely to what looks like abundance. There is no sudden white growth to consume the water’s oxygen, no shower of food left to rot. A little more of what was passing beyond reach now settles where mouthparts can find it.

For some time the shrimp continues to feed mostly in the old corner.

Then one interval it stays in the hollow.

Above, a mechanic begins to notice a valve that closes too abruptly.

The valve has always done this. Its report has been entered under several descriptions, none of them urgent. On this morning the mechanic hears it while standing beside the right length of pipe, and feels the blow through a hand laid on the railing.

There is time to investigate. The replacement part is available. A supervisor who might have postponed the work looks instead at the record of repeated repairs.

The power does not require anyone to become a different person. It makes room for their competence to matter.

When the work is finished, the chamber remains noisy. The city still wakes, draws water, cools its machinery. But one of the impacts does not return.

Mortar dust settles into seams that have not held it for years.

The washer trembles in its fitted hollow.

At the shrimp’s next molt, the power is present.

It does not open the shell for it. It watches the labor at the back of the head, the withdrawal of legs, the long, delicate release of the antennae. When the animal emerges soft into the deeper hollow, the ordinary current continues past the entrance.

The empty shell leans against the wall.

Beside it, the living shrimp has room to lie without being folded.

For a while it scarcely moves.

There is no revelation. No amber brightness gathers around it. Its body takes water into itself; the new covering begins to harden. Eventually a leg reaches for the rough surface and holds.

Later, it eats.

The power remains attentive, though not so closely that every grain must be placed. There are things it has not learned from one molt or a few pump cycles. Mineral deposits will thicken. Machinery will change. The animal will grow.

For now, during a long steady run, the shrimp comes to the mouth of its recess.

It stands partly behind the new lip and partly beyond it. One antenna touches the wall. The other extends into the passing water, bends, and recovers.

A particle catches against the mortar within reach.

The shrimp takes it, works it toward its mouth, and does not have to let go.
