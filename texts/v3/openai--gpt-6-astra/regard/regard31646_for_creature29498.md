The power first attended to the trunk whose buried contacts were becoming less favorable.

From above, it was not distinguishable from the others. Its leaves were as broad, their copper margins as continuous. A repair beneath the third tier had preserved even a small irregularity in the lattice. Only below the stone was the difference apparent: the trunk opened its constrictions earlier each afternoon, and closed them later. More of what arrived from its neighbors went into keeping the old contact usable.

The power watched through many afternoons.

It could have restored the crystal faces to their former relation. But the root had changed with them. Tissue that once carried current directly now divided it among several fine paths. Some of those paths held the persistent differences left by earlier events. Putting the stone back would not put the inhabitant back. It would bring an unfamiliar past into contact with a living present.

Nor was every expenditure a hardship. The pale leaf used a great deal of material without becoming larger. That alone was no reason to complete it.

The power had time enough to distinguish maintenance from struggle. It used that time.

It attended to the unjoined plants at the grove’s edge, to the mature crowns, to the small fronds whose independence remained a narrow thread underground. It learned how an inhabitant diverted a change it could not accommodate, how it admitted one, how it returned to a previous condition and how it retained a difference.

These were not simple answers. A constriction might close because there was too much current, because a reserve was full, or because a disturbance needed to be kept out of a particular leaf. The same motion could protect solitude or preserve a connection.

Understanding came without reducing these distinctions.

Eventually the power made itself perceptible.

At an unused crystal face beside the failing contact, it established a very small electrical difference. Nothing flowed into the root. There was no circuit unless living tissue reached the face.

The new condition was weaker than many changes the plateau had produced on its own. Its unusual feature was its regularity: when the root’s exploratory fibers approached, it remained steady; when they withdrew, it diminished. After an interval it returned, no stronger than before.

For a long time, nothing joined it.

The power left the rest of the seam alone.

Then a ribbon of root tissue extended across the narrow space. It touched the face, admitted a little current, and sealed itself off.

The power did not increase the supply.

This happened many times. Each contact was brief. Between them, the trunk sent disturbances through its own lattice and into the shared strip, where its neighbors answered according to their several dispositions. The grove’s tone changed by less than a visitor could have heard.

At last the root maintained the connection through one entire period of shade.

The new source did not feed the crown directly. It supported the work at the deteriorating contact. The trunk retained its familiar pathways, its reserves, its power to close the connection. When it did close it, the offered difference subsided harmlessly into the stone.

That was the first thing the power did.

***

It did not spread the same remedy throughout the plateau.

In the hollow, a comparable offer caused the nearest fronds to narrow their exchanges and hold them narrow long after the offer was removed. The power withdrew completely. Their economy had little use for what the grove could accept.

Elsewhere, an isolated young plant connected to a new face and then dismantled the connecting tissue. It retained an amber asymmetry beneath one leaf. No further offer was made there.

The power learned that help had to possess a boundary the recipient could operate.

Where assistance was accepted, it therefore placed no indispensable part of itself inside living tissue. The added contacts belonged to the surroundings. They could be approached, tested, restricted, abandoned. Their supply had limits that became apparent gradually, without a sudden failure at the edge.

It also made sure that a plant accepting help could continue to refuse its neighbors, and that a neighbor could continue to refuse it. More available energy was not permission to carry current through every boundary.

The low tone remained.

***

Under the first trunk, the old contact continued to worsen.

The power had not arrested the plateau. Deep pressures still found their minute accommodations. The exposed lips of fractures retained their edges; the ridge above the hollow stood halfway risen. Within that enduring appearance, particulars changed.

Now the inhabitant had room to change with them.

Over a long interval it reclaimed some of the tissue spent on the old crystal faces. A new path formed beside the offered source, then another toward a natural contact farther down. The power held its contribution steady while these paths developed. Later, when the root restricted it, the power let it decline.

No great leaf opened. The trunk did not become taller.

One afternoon, however, it closed the constriction toward its neighbors before the shade had passed.

There was enough in its own reserves.

Later it opened the constriction again, and supplied the adjoining trunk.

The distinction mattered, though nothing visible marked it.

***

Beyond the groves were miles of bare, warm stone.

The power left them bare.

It kept no plan for joining the green islands, no preferred final outline for the unfinished mountains. It remained attentive to the lives already negotiating the seams, including those too slow to register an offer within the span of an ordinary world.

Beneath the projecting leaf, the shadow still rested on the unblemished slab. The copper edge held its old irregularity. Material entered the pale tip and was withdrawn.

Deep below, a root closed one narrow passage and opened another.

The offered contact waited outside it.
