The power arrived while the youngster was still occupied with the sedge blade.

It watched the blade spring upright twice more. On the third attempt, the animal shifted one forefoot, found purchase on a submerged runner, and pulled the tender base within reach of its mouth.

There was no need to help with that.

For several days, the power remained beneath the meadow.

It went where a body of that size could go, and into narrower places where only water passed. It learned the difference between a resting chamber and a hollow left by rot. It followed the tremor from the trash rack outward through the basin, feeling it change as it crossed loose sediment, living roots, the buried stones of an older shore.

Under the hut, the oldest animal slept with its chin on one wrist. The power stayed nearby without pressing against the roots. It had no wish to be another thing the animal must keep track of.

By morning it understood something of the shelter’s value. The concrete held a little of the previous afternoon’s warmth. Rain fell clear of the central cushion. There were three exits, one of them scarcely wider than the animal’s head.

It also found a length of discarded wire there.

The wire had sunk into the moss beside the narrow exit. One end was already enclosed by roots; the other made a loop in the water. A small foot could pass through it easily. A larger foot, opened in alarm, might not.

The power removed it.

This was its first alteration to the basin: a little less metal beneath a building.

***

At the intake, it attended to the water.

There were places where the animals could turn away from the current and places where, once carried beyond a certain stem or stone, they could not. The boundary moved. It shifted with rain, with the water level, with decisions made far below the cloud.

The power followed those decisions down the mountain.

There were people there, too. A woman putting a damp shirt over a warm pipe. A night worker eating beside a humming cabinet. Houses whose lights came on before dawn. The power followed the wires farther than was necessary to discover that simply stopping the machinery would not be a kindness without consequences.

Nor would silence necessarily comfort the creatures above.

So it returned, and waited for the maintenance truck.

The two workers found someone sitting on the dry edge of the hut step. Neither heard an arrival over the overflow.

The visitor stood to let them pass.

“There is something I would like to show you,” it said.

One worker looked toward the electrical cabinet. The other followed the visitor’s gaze beneath the walkway.

They already knew about the animal. They fetched the photograph.

“Yes,” said the visitor. “That one. And the others.”

It did not lift the mat or draw a creature out into the glare. They waited beside a pool until a blunt head appeared, followed by the small searching hand of a youngster.

The younger worker crouched.

For the first time, he saw how the toes worked.

***

What followed took longer than removing the wire.

There were visits, measurements, arguments conducted in offices where the overflow could not be heard. The power went to those places as well. It was patient with people who needed to ask whether a proposed alteration would freeze, clog, fail under flood, or make the walkway unsafe.

It answered what it knew. Where the basin had not yet supplied an answer, it returned to watch.

A barrier placed too close to the screen would merely give a trapped animal somewhere else to cling. A finer screen could catch more vegetation and strengthen the pull through whatever openings remained. A refuge on the wrong side of a changing current would be no refuge at all.

They tried things in small sections first.

Eventually, the intake acquired a way for a swimming body to leave the approaching flow while it still had strength to choose. The route led back into sheltered water, not onto bare concrete. It could be inspected from the walkway. It did not depend on someone remembering to open it.

The operating instructions changed, too. Where it was possible to change the level gradually, that became the ordinary practice. Work near the hut began with more than a glance beneath the boards.

The photograph stayed in the cabinet.

Beside it, someone added a second picture: five pale, spread toes against brown water.

***

The power could have made the animals harder to injure.

It could have given them tougher feet, longer breath, an urgent knowledge of machinery. Instead, it left them their particular bodies and put more of the burden on the things that had arrived without knowing how to live among them.

It did not prevent every chamber from collapsing.

One afternoon, a feeding hollow fell in beneath an old cushion. Brown water rose through the torn surface. The animal inside emerged by another passage and lay for a time with only its nostrils showing.

The power watched closely.

By evening the creature was feeding along the broken edge.

The basin needed room for this as well: for a pool to open, for a channel to move, for something living to make an imperfect choice without that choice becoming a disaster.

***

By autumn, there was very little a visitor would notice.

A modest addition beside the intake. Wire and scraps removed from beneath the hut. A route for heavy equipment marked along firmer ground. A few people who now looked longer before setting down a boot.

The roar still filled the basin.

Under it, the animals clicked and fed and slept. Moss accumulated in an old one’s coat. The young became less conspicuous.

The power’s attention remained, but not like a light kept shining into a room.

More like someone knowing where the room was, and keeping its exits clear.

Near sunset, a small fullness traveled beneath the meadow toward the newest margin. Loose stems lifted, parted, settled again.

The power let it pass.
