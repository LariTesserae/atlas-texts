The power does not enter through the mouth of the cleft.

An entrance is something bodies require. It attends instead, and the place becomes known to it without becoming brighter.

It feels the warm seams, including the one that will cool in another winter and the one whose heat has scarcely begun to reach the surface. It knows where the water goes beneath the grit. It distinguishes the calls returning from stone from the calls first made in throats.

The female beneath the shelf does not sense a presence. There is no new smell to examine, no pressure against her whiskers. The cricket still requires catching.

She catches it.

The power attends to that, too: the hunger ending in one body, the struggle ending in another. Nothing becomes simple because it can be seen completely.

For a while, it does nothing.

This is not a test of whether the female deserves help. Nor is it waiting for her to ask.

It is learning what help would touch.

***

Above the nest, a thin flake of stone has been separating from the roof for years.

Its upper surface is wet. Its lower surface is dry. During cold weather the difference increases, and a pale grain at one end bears more weight than it did before.

In eleven nights, the flake will fall.

It is not large. On an empty shelf it would make a noise, scatter some insects, and become another feature to walk around.

The power settles it more deeply into its socket.

There is a faint click.

One of the young moves in its sleep. Its forefoot opens against its sibling’s face, then closes.

The mother, hunting elsewhere, hears nothing.

***

It would be easy to fill the chamber with food.

But food would become bodies, and bodies would require places, and the narrow ways would fill with encounters that ended in torn ears. The power follows this consequence without blaming the animals for it.

It looks beyond the chamber.

Farther down the mountain, where the female has never been, there is another cleft. It is smaller and less warm. Snow blocks its entrance for part of the year, though a thread of air keeps an opening beneath the lowest crust. In summer, flies shelter there. A beetle has overwintered behind a scale of rock. No animal with fur has yet found the dry pockets at its back.

Between the two clefts, the mountain contains a broken seam.

Most of it is passable. In two places it is not.

The power does not bore a tunnel straight through. A straight tunnel would draw cold air into the nesting chamber. It loosens a little grit behind one obstruction and settles a fractured block behind the other. The resulting way bends repeatedly. It has rough floors, close ceilings, and hollows where a small animal can turn around.

At its lower end, meltwater has been running across the entrance and freezing. The power shifts its course by the width of a stem.

Nothing comes to inspect the new passage that night.

For several days, nothing larger than a cricket uses it.

The power leaves it open.

***

The female returns to her young with dust on her nose.

She presses through the feathers and lies down. The smaller one cannot find a place at first. It pushes beneath her foreleg, withdraws, pushes again. She adjusts her body with an irritated little movement.

Now both are feeding.

There is an old injury in her jaw. The bone has healed, but a narrow point presses into living tissue whenever she bites down hard. She has lived with it long enough that her ways of eating have grown around it.

The power eases the point away.

It does not take her teeth, or her hunger, or the quick movement with which she catches a cricket. It leaves pain capable of warning her when something is wrong.

But this pain has delivered its warning. There is nothing more she can do about it.

When she next eats a beetle, she does not pause between the second bite and the third.

That is all she knows of the change.

***

At the lowest end of the pool, beneath the wings, water escapes through a hairline fracture.

The power narrows it slightly.

Not enough to drown the dry margins where the flies gather. Not enough to send standing water into the nests below the floor. Enough that, late in a dry season, there will still be a shallow dark crescent against the rim.

It leaves the water tasting of the mountain.

It leaves the drinking places scattered, rather than drawing every thirsty body to one exposed bank.

Along one of the deep seams, there is a smooth slope from which a fallen animal cannot climb. The power roughens a strip of it. The marks are small and irregular. They could have been left by weathering.

Most creatures will never touch them.

One will.

***

The young that died is beyond these alterations.

The power attends to it without making the surviving young into a consolation, and without calling the death necessary merely because other things have fed upon it.

Where the tiny body lay, mites are moving through the last of the bedding.

They, too, are alive.

The power can distinguish all these claims without allowing the smallest to vanish inside the greatest.

It stays.

***

Weeks later, one of the surviving young finds the opened seam.

It has already been driven away twice from its mother’s sleeping pocket. There is a fresh scratch across its muzzle. It spends a long time at the unfamiliar gap, advancing until its shoulders enter, backing out, standing with its nose raised.

A cricket moves inside.

The young animal follows.

The passage is not safe in every possible way. There are loose stones. There is a spider in a side pocket. Near the lower end, air brings unfamiliar smells through the dark.

But there is room to turn back.

There is room to go on.

Above it, the female is drinking at the pool. Her whiskers touch the surface before her mouth does. She lifts her head, works her jaw, and drinks again.

No gratitude is required of her.

When she has finished, she goes hunting.
