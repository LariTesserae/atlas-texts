The power does not enter by the stair.

For a time, it does not enter at all. It attends from somewhere that adds no warmth to the wall, no weight to the settled pipes. The red reflection remains where it was. The animal hangs between the brick and the bolt, holding the leaf.

There are things the power could do at once. Empty the basin. Cool the room. Lift the animal, with its young and the fibers beneath them, into a place without hot metal.

It waits long enough to learn what each of these would mean.

The basin is also where water will remain after the next long dry interval. Cooling the room would change the growth on the lamp, the wetting of the washer, the small succession of things that soften a fallen leaf. The fibers are threaded through the sheltered curl; lifting them would pull apart the only surface the young can grip.

And the animal is already reaching.

Its forward feet find the bolt. One slips on the smooth, domed head. The others hold.

The power does not carry it across. It roughens a little of the metal beneath the slipping foot.

There is no flash. A patch of corrosion acquires a few deeper pits. The foot catches, releases, catches farther on. The animal brings its rear sections over the water and rests with the leaf pressed beneath it.

That is the first thing.

***

The power follows the heat through the chamber.

Not merely the heat that is there, but the heat on its way: through a valve which opens late, through a tank whose contents keep their warmth after the motor stops, through the broad pipe resting on a smaller one that was never intended to bear it.

Beyond the walls are other rooms. Some are occupied. The machinery serves them badly, but it serves them. Shutting it down would move the difficulty elsewhere.

The power leaves it running.

Where the heavy pipe rests, a bracket takes a little more of the weight. Farther along, a fastening ceases to loosen. Nothing rises back to the position shown on an old plan. Deposits remain unbroken. The animal’s routes keep their familiar slopes.

There is one crossing where the vibration gives too little warning.

The power gives it more.

A loose contact, far inside a housing, settles against its stop. Now a faint tremor travels through the brick before the heat begins to climb. The tremor is of a kind the animal already knows.

On the next cycle, it leaves the crossing early.

On the cycle after that, it has already gone another way.

***

At the nest, the power encounters the empty skin.

It attends to that, too. There is very little left to learn from it: an incomplete division between two plates, limbs fine as the fibers holding them. It does not mistake the remnant for something that can still be sheltered.

Beside it, one of the living young has moved away from the damp patch. Its feet work against a powdery fold. It gains almost nothing.

The metal is cool here because of what passes beyond it. The power finds a way for a little of that coolness to extend along the curl.

During the next wet interval, condensation forms on the sheltered underside. Several beads join. Most of the water returns along an existing stain, but a little enters the fibers.

Not enough to flood them.

The young reaches a strand darkened by moisture. Its forelegs stay where it puts them. Slowly, the rest of it follows.

The power watches through a hot interval and a cool one, then through the much longer interval when the chamber seems to have forgotten water altogether. The fibers grow lighter. They do not become entirely dry.

The adult still returns to them. It still settles above the young and parts the folds beneath its plates.

There is less urgency in how much water must be given.

***

Food takes longer.

At the pale seam, the newest runner has entered a space with light but no room for another leaf to open. Its folded tip presses against metal.

The panel shifts less than the width of a seed.

Air passes differently through the gap. The power waits to see whether this dries the packing. It does not. A leaf opens on the far side, and another, smaller leaf remains within the chamber.

The cricket eats part of it.

This is permitted.

Later, a damaged edge yellows and falls. It catches on the lamp cage, where intermittent moisture reaches it. Nothing feeds on it immediately. It has to change first.

The power does not hurry that change.

***

There are other adjustments, spread over many cycles.

A narrow ledge above the deepest basin develops a rough lower edge. A bead that would have fallen into black water travels instead along a seam accessible from dry brick. Near the nest, a second curled pocket becomes cool enough to shelter a small body when the first is crowded.

None of these places must be found.

The animal continues to pause at the capped pipe. It continues to take the long route on some nights, and on others to remain beneath its bracket while food waits elsewhere. Its crooked legs remain crooked. With the safer purchase, they serve it well enough.

When the larger young first leaves the wall, the power is still attending.

It travels no farther than the upper edge of the recess. For a long time it holds there, nearly transparent against a pale deposit. Then its pads settle on the metal ahead.

Far away, the motor begins.

The warning reaches it through its feet.

It withdraws into the curl before the surface warms.

Below, the adult works at the underside of a leaf. A fragment comes free. It gathers the fragment beneath its body and starts toward the wall, stopping once to drink from a seam that used to be dry.
