The power stays through the night before changing anything.

In the dark, the hollow becomes a different arrangement of distances. The snail comes down the fern stem. Moisture softens the edges of the spoil heaps. The ants continue along routes the light had made easier for an observer to see, but had never made for them.

Beneath the root, a worker carries a larva from one chamber to another. The power follows, attending to the grip of the jaws, the yielding body held between them, the places where the passage narrows. It can perceive the whole nest at once, but it gives this journey its own duration.

Elsewhere, a small worker has caught a leg between a splinter and the wall.

It pulls, rests, and pulls again. Traffic passes close enough to touch it.

The power loosens the splinter.

Only a little. The worker draws free, folds the leg beneath its body, then sets it down. For a time it stands there. Eventually it moves into the passage.

The splinter remains where it can support the earth above.

*

By morning, the power has learned something of the water.

The bead beneath the curled leaf is the end of a journey that begins far above, where rain collects against a fork in the tree. Most storms send only a little down this root. But a branch has fallen into the fork, and leaves are gathering behind it. In heavy rain, the small obstruction will release a larger flow toward the hollow.

There will be heavy rain that afternoon.

The power could turn the cloud aside. Beyond the tree, however, shallow roots wait in drying soil. Pools have contracted beneath their rims. A frog sits in the last dark fold of one of them.

It leaves the rain to come.

Instead, high in the tree, it settles the fallen branch into a more stable position. Water will spill gradually from the fork, along several paths. Some will still reach the hollow. The moss will darken; the snail will find a wet way down. The low chambers will not fill faster than their inhabitants can leave them.

When the first drops arrive, no bright boundary appears around the nest.

The fern shakes. Earth grains collapse into one another. At the third entrance, a worker abandons a load and retreats. Other workers follow, and for several minutes the clearing seems empty.

Then an ant emerges beneath the roofed approach.

It touches the wet ground, withdraws, and tries another direction.

*

The power does not give the colony a store of prepared food. It watches the living garden receive the fragments brought down from above: each piece handled, altered, incorporated into something that neither leaf nor fungus nor ant could have made alone.

There are spoiled places in the garden. Workers are already attending to them.

There are chambers that could be wider. Workers are widening them.

The power leaves room for these activities to succeed.

Yet it does not make a virtue of standing aside. When a loosened clod falls across a passage with workers beneath it, the weight is briefly less than the earth would otherwise require. Legs find purchase. Bodies draw clear. The clod settles.

Aboveground, the same care reaches the snail when runoff turns the flake of bark on which it rests. The flake catches against a pebble rather than rolling over. Water passes beneath it.

No inhabitant has to become important to the nest to receive attention.

*

Toward evening, the rain thins.

The hollow has changed. One mound is flattened; another has acquired a smooth, dark side. The wet corner extends almost to the seed husk, and the spider has climbed higher into its curve. A seedling at the quiet end holds a drop between its opening leaves.

The ants begin bringing out earth.

For a while, the power watches a worker negotiate the rain-cut edge of the clearing. Its burden catches against a root fiber. It pulls backward, turns, tries again. The power can feel precisely where the resistance lies.

It waits.

The worker changes its grip. The burden comes loose.

There is a difference between this difficulty and the trapped leg. The power keeps learning it.

By dusk, fresh grains are accumulating on the flattened mound. Below, the brood is being gathered in a chamber whose walls have remained sound. The fungus receives its next pieces of green.

Nothing in the hollow knows why the great rush of water did not come.

The power has no need to be discovered. It stays near enough to notice the next thing, and far enough away that the ground is still being made by those who live there.
