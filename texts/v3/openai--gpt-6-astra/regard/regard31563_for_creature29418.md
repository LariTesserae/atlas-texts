The power first listens from the window, then from beside the pipe.

These are not the same sound.

It has no need to put a hand against the flange, but does so. There is wear in the bearing, a small error in the alignment, and a repair made years ago with the only part available. That repair has lasted longer than anyone expected. It deserves neither to be condemned nor asked to last forever.

Beyond the pump house, supper is being kept warm.

The power does nothing to delay her.

***

It could lift the district above the water. But even a raised district would need drains, and somewhere else the displaced water would arrive. It follows the canal downstream before deciding anything. It follows the electricity upstream. It looks beneath the brick islands and through the roots that hold them together.

It learns where the water is supposed to go, where it actually goes, and whose floor receives the difference.

Then, one afternoon, it comes to the pump-house door.

The woman is trying to read a number stamped on a part. She has cleaned it twice, but the light is wrong.

“Hold this,” she says.

The power holds the lamp.

Afterward, it tells her what it is.

She looks at it for some time. Then she takes the lamp back.

“Can you get these anymore?”

She indicates the part.

“Yes.”

“The right size?”

“Yes.”

She turns it in her hand.

“They always say that.”

***

The replacement fits.

This matters more, at first, than anything else the power has said.

It does not gleam with an unearthly light. It comes with its specifications, and there are more available from a place that will still exist next year. The woman measures it herself.

The power asks what she would change.

She says the mounting bolts.

It asks again a few days later.

She says the intake grille, which is awkward to clear safely when the water is high.

Later still, while they are moving the rice cooker to make room on her table, she says she would like to go away without listening to the rain wherever she goes.

The power does not answer immediately. By now it knows enough to understand that this is a larger request than the bearing.

***

There is work.

Some of it is done in ways no one can explain. Beneath a house, a cracked culvert becomes whole without the floor being broken open. A dangerous length of cable is replaced between one blink of a lamp and the next. Far downstream, an obstruction too deep and heavy for anyone in the district to remove is lifted out and set where it can do no harm.

Other work takes meetings.

The power attends these too.

A proposed access path crosses the place where the machinist’s wife grows her gourds. She rejects it. Someone suggests moving the path toward the rented room’s stairs, until its occupant comes down and shows them how little room he already has to carry anything through.

They find another route.

The pump keeper is not made responsible for persuading everyone. Nor does every disagreement end when she speaks. She knows the machinery; other people know where their children sleep, which wall becomes hot in the afternoon, what they cannot afford to lose.

The power can keep a trench dry while people work in it. It cannot make a kitchen convenient without asking the person who cooks there.

***

A second pump is installed on a raised plinth. Either machine can do the necessary work alone. The changeover is tested, then tested again with the main power disconnected.

The woman watches the water throughout.

There are guards where hands might slip, a secure platform beside the intake, and lights that illuminate the lower pipework without anyone holding a torch in their teeth. The new alarm sounds in more than one house. Its meaning is written plainly beside it.

Two people learn the rounds. They are paid. So is she, including for the hours she spends teaching them.

One is impatient and good with diagrams. The other learns slowly but notices a hot connection the first misses. Neither acquires her hearing for the old pump at once. She finds this frustrating.

One afternoon she reaches past the slower learner to make an adjustment.

“Let me,” he says.

She almost tells him there is no time.

There is time. The other pump is running.

She steps back.

***

Not everything remains.

The tree pressing against the downpipe must come out. The woman is unexpectedly sorry about it. The power does not tell her that another tree will be just as good.

Not everything goes.

The moss stays where feet do not need purchase. So do the taro tubs, the banana screen, the roots holding the boundary together. The tilting stone near the bridge is reset. For weeks afterward she still puts her foot on its outer edge.

The district becomes safer without becoming dry, silent, or finished.

There are still blocked gutters. Someone still puts something unsuitable down a drain. The machinist hangs wire from the gourd trellis when his separate pipe is full.

The power has not mistaken these things for a failure of its work.

***

When the woman finally books a room at the coast, she chooses four nights.

On the morning she leaves, she goes through the instructions once more. Her bag stands above the floor on a chair.

The impatient learner interrupts her.

“You wrote that down.”

“I know I wrote it down.”

He waits while she finishes.

At the restaurant by the sea, the windows are large and the air conditioning is excessive. She chooses a table directly beneath a vent. There are no plants except a stiff arrangement behind the cashier.

Rain begins while she is eating.

She puts down her fork.

For a moment she is beneath the pump house, judging the sound against the pipe; then in the alley, watching water cover the joints between the stones.

She takes out her telephone.

There is a message from the slower learner: the pumps have changed over as expected. He has attached a photograph of the readings, slightly out of focus.

She enlarges it. Checks.

Outside, rain drives across an expanse of pale pavement toward the sea. A waiter asks whether she would like the vent turned down.

“No,” she says. “It’s good.”

She orders another dish, something that will take a while.

The power, which has followed the rain back to the canal and found the water moving safely through, leaves her to her meal.
