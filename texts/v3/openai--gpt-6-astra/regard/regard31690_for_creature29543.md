The power pauses on the stair.

There is room for it to pass the old leaf without touching it. It does so, though this requires a change in its shape.

Below, the resident has found a cracked pot to carry. The power does not follow it between the benches. It stays beside the stair, out of the aisle, while water gathers on the handrail.

It has come with no particular intention about gardens. It does not suppose that the roof should open, that birds should arrive, or that the resident would be happier standing upright in sunlight. The place is warm. Some of its darkness is shelter. Some of its wetness is necessary. There are also live wires behind a loose fitting, and the power can distinguish these things without loving one less for noticing the other.

When the resident returns with the same pot, the power shows it a small piece of dry slate.

It lays the slate beneath the fitting.

A thin white spark descends and rests upon it, curled like a shaving. The damaged wire is now sound.

The resident watches for a long time. Then it puts down the pot, takes up the slate by its edges, and carries it to the pump-room threshold.

The power follows only as far as the resident permits.

***

The first work is not visible from the garden.

Deep beneath the district, a joint in the drainage main has been opening by fractions. Beyond it, sediment has collected around a length of reinforcing steel. In a sufficiently heavy storm, the water will back up. The two pumps will continue to labor, and the resident will clear the grate repeatedly, because that is the part of the trouble it can reach.

The power repairs the joint. It frees the obstruction without sending it downstream to become someone else’s flood.

Then it examines the pumps.

They are not dying. One bearing is worn; one mounting transfers more vibration than it should. Both can be repaired. The power leaves these repairs to the people who know the machinery, but makes the wear unmistakable at their next inspection, and sees that the replacement parts are available.

This takes a little more work than making parts appear. A request must not be lost. Money already set aside for maintenance must actually be spent on maintenance. A person who has been reporting the rattle for months must be listened to.

Three blows sound on the pipe before the shutdown.

The resident waits with its palm against the floor.

For the first time, the power sees how much effort waiting costs it.

***

There is a way to prevent the garden from flooding when both pumps stop. The power could build it in a moment. Instead, it traces the proposed overflow route in a bead of water along the wall.

The resident follows the bead with its eyes.

At one point it reaches forward and covers a mortar joint with its hand.

The power looks more closely. Behind the brick, roots have gathered in a small pocket of sweetness and warmth. Nothing remarkable grows there. The resident keeps its hand in place.

The bead withdraws. It finds another route.

When the passage is finished, the power lets the resident test it. Water rises in a shallow basin, reaches a certain height, and vanishes through the new opening. The resident blocks the opening with a wad of leaf mold. The water finds the second opening.

It tries again with both hands.

Only after this does it go beneath the platform and bring out the broken ruler.

The power holds the water steady while it measures.

***

Not everything offered is accepted.

A brighter pane in the roof is covered again after two days. The resident drags an old piece of shade cloth across it, and the power leaves it covered.

A new knife remains on the bench. Eventually the resident sets its old knife beside it, touching the taped handle with one finger.

The old blade is given a sound edge. Its handle stays as it is.

The weakened platform is a more difficult matter. The resident will not let the power near the hollow beneath it. For several visits nothing happens there.

Then, one afternoon, the bowl and the white tile appear on the bench. Beside them lie the bottle cap, the ruler, and a carefully gathered bundle of coir.

The power repairs the platform from below. It changes neither the height of the entrance nor the sloping plastic that carries the drops away.

The resident puts everything back itself.

***

The power also attends to the people upstairs.

No summons brings them down to admire the resident. No photograph of it circulates. But the propagation house ceases to be a blank on the building plans. Access is protected. The maintenance notes include the three knocks, the cleared doorway, the need to keep oil off the garden floor.

There is provision for someone else to clear the sump.

At first the resident clears it again after them.

The power does not discourage this. A week later, the resident removes only a few fibers from one corner. Later still, it watches from beside the warm pipe while a worker rinses the scraper and hangs it above the water.

***

On its last visit before a long absence, the power finds the resident beneath the fern at the far wall.

The pumps are running. They still make enough noise that ordinary speech would be difficult. Water trembles beyond the clean bars of the sump.

The resident has nothing in its hands.

The power settles nearby, leaving the channel clear.

A drop gathers at the split jacket. The resident turns its palm. The drop strikes the end of one finger and breaks into three smaller drops.

After a while, the resident touches the power’s wrist with two fingers.

It moves the offered hand a little to the left, beneath a second seam.

They wait.

The next drop misses both of them.
