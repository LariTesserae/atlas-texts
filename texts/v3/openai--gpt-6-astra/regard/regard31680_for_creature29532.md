The power does not quiet the sea.

It has noticed the creature’s sides moving with the pressure, and the way its longest finger waits for a particular interval before descending. What would be a kindness to someone else would take much of the world away from this one.

Nor does it fill the hollow with light.

It approaches beneath the blackthorn, bending where a visitor must bend, and settles opposite the timber. Its weight reaches the creature through the floor.

For a while that is all it does.

There is much it could learn without waiting. It could follow the worn places in the wood back to the hands that made them. It could find every person who had known the path, every household in which the knowledge had become a story and then an uncertain name.

But none of that would answer the small movement now made by the creature’s head.

The power places a palm on the timber.

The sea strikes below. Air presses through the fissure, tightening the fine skin along the creature’s ribs. Somewhere in the inaccessible passage, a stone rolls and stops.

The finger lifts.

The power leaves its palm where it is.

***

The first gift is water.

Not a spring opened through the limestone, not a sudden plentiful dripping from the roof. The chamber is dry in ways that matter to the being who lives here. There are moths beneath the ledges, and roots that would rot, and a loose web beside the peg.

The power lifts the two leaves from the basin and lays them together on a dry shelf. It fills the dish to a little below the innermost ring.

Then it returns to the timber.

Twice, lightly, it touches the wood.

The creature does not rise at once. Its nostrils open beneath their fold. One hand turns until the broad ends of the fingers face upward.

The power waits through several surges before placing food within reach: a small fish, opened so the soft parts are accessible, on a clean flat stone.

It does not put anything into the waiting hand.

Eventually the hand turns back. The creature touches the stone, then the fish. Its mouth opens along a crease that seemed incapable of opening so far.

Eating takes a long time.

The power stays.

***

It returns before the dish is empty.

On the next visit it brings less fish, and part of an apple softened by frost. The creature eats the fish and leaves the apple until later. This is useful to know.

The power does not mistake the first meal for a recovery. There is a long expenditure to be answered, and a body accustomed to making very little last. It brings small amounts. It watches what is eaten, what is set aside, what makes the creature draw its feet closer beneath itself.

There are changes it makes without ceremony.

The water is clean.

The food does not carry sickness.

A sharp sliver beneath the creature’s resting foot is removed when the foot next lifts.

Other things it leaves alone.

The thorn remains across the upper entrance. The snail shell stays in its cup. The cord continues to hang from the peg, though the power knows what was once tied there.

Under the pasture, water has begun to loosen a seam above the back wall. This it tends. It finds a course for the rain that does not pass through the failing clay, leading it outward through gravel already accustomed to being wet. The work is slow and makes no new sound in the hollow.

The sea continues to enlarge its passage.

There will be time to attend to that too.

***

The timber is more difficult.

Its underside has softened enough that one day a hand will press through it. The power could make it sound again in an instant. But the creature knows this wood through more than its shape. It knows the small hesitation between a touch and the arrival of that touch beneath its fingers.

So the power brings another length, seasoned and unpainted, and lays it alongside the old one.

For many visits it uses only the old timber.

Then, keeping one hand there, it rests the other on the new.

The creature feels the added contact through the grit. Its head turns a little. Nothing else happens.

Later, it reaches across.

The movement exposes the folds beneath its arm. One finger settles on the unfamiliar grain, lifts, and settles again.

The power answers there.

For a long while both pieces remain.

***

It does not make the creature large.

It does not know that largeness was happiness. It knows only that this body has been living on itself, and that the water sometimes fails.

With food, some of the deep pleats become shallower. The journey to the dish still requires pauses, but fewer of them. The creature begins to sit in a second place, nearer the entrance, where reflected daylight reaches one shoulder.

One wet evening it puts a foot outside.

The power is not there to witness this. It has left the food and gone, as it sometimes does now, returning at an interval the creature has begun to recognise.

Rain gathers on the hollow hairs at the creature’s wrist. Beyond the thorn, the air has many small movements instead of one enormous movement. It remains on the threshold until the rain stops.

Then it goes back to its seat.

There is nothing disappointing in this.

***

In time, someone else comes.

Not summoned by a dream or driven by a sudden obligation. A person repairing a drainage cut notices the level stones below the thorn, and later returns to look.

The power is sitting inside.

It moves enough to leave the entrance clear.

The visitor first sees the dish, then the paired lengths of wood, then the hand. Their own hand draws back against their chest.

The power does not say that the creature is harmless. It does not offer a story about wishes, luck, or what the village owes.

It lays its palm flat.

After a while, the visitor does the same.

They almost miss the answer.

The power shows them how to leave food within reach, and how little to bring at first. It shows them the water ring in the dish. Most importantly, it stays long enough for them to learn that the pause is not an absence of response.

Whether the visitor returns is theirs to decide.

The creature’s next meal does not depend on it.

***

Through the winter, the hollow remains a cold place in a low bank.

The noise still makes speech useless. The fern nearest the darkness extends another long leaf toward the mouth. Salt gathers faintly on the stone, and the white snail shell turns a little in its cup when a moth works behind it.

One afternoon the power arrives and finds the creature’s hand on the newer timber.

It sits opposite and begins to reach forward.

Before its palm touches down, the longest finger lifts.

A light tap travels through the wood.

The power sets down what it has brought, and answers.
