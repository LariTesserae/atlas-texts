The power did not begin by quieting the mountain.

The sound was terrible only to something that had never needed it. Beneath the animal’s hind foot, the pale root received its warmth through that labor. The water held its pressure. Seven trees carried the weight of their leaves.

Instead, the power listened until it could distinguish the mountain from the creature resting against it.

This took care rather than time.

There was the note of the stone bearing torque, and the finer note of roots drawing upon it. There was the swallowing beneath the jaw. There was the voice that roughened toward the animal and softened toward the empty moss.

The power followed that voice without assuming it was a call.

It might have been an ordinary pleasure of the body, like the warmth beneath the chin. It might have belonged to someone whose absence had no remedy. The open place beneath the tail was not, by itself, permission to fill it.

The power settled outside the slab.

It took a body small enough that the animal would not have to look upward to see its face. The body had short brown fur, feet suited to the wet stone, and ears that heard imperfectly. It was warm, but not with the particular warmth of any remembered creature.

It kept its own smell.

Then it waited at the height where the animal was awake.

Within the hollow, waiting could easily become another imposition: a stranger fixed forever at the edge of one’s bed. The power therefore brought a little of its own time with it. Not enough to spill among the roots. Enough to arrive, to remain for a while, and, if necessary, to leave.

Its breathing rose and fell.

The animal’s eye held it.

The power moved sideways, slowly, until it reached the definite boundary of that attention. There it stopped. A small brown shoulder remained visible beside the stone.

Nothing was put beneath the tail. Nothing touched the exposed skin.

For a while, the only new thing in the hollow was a creature willing to be seen and not insisting upon it.

Then the power felt the limit of what it had done.

The little animal could look at a visitor. It could feed, sleep, listen, and hold. But any welcome it gave would already have been there before the visitor arrived. So would any refusal. The power could move through those gestures and choose the one it liked best.

It would not do that.

Under the water, beneath the bed, it examined the mountain’s workmanship.

There was no broken part to mend. The hollow was accomplishing something exact and immense. To release its load at once would not be to wake a sleeper. There were roots braced against it, bodies sustained by it, droplets containing the whole force of a fall.

The power took none of that away.

It added a bearing.

Deep inside the rock, where metal and crystal curved about the unmoving center, a new surface formed. It was smaller than a snail’s shell. The mountain’s load rested against it without diminishing. Through it, something could turn.

Under the slab, the change was almost imperceptible.

The animal’s existing life remained available to it: the warm moss, the flower, the sleep seen from above. But along the root beneath its forepaw there was now a little slack. A gesture could go farther than the place where it had always ended.

The power did not pull it through.

In its brown body, it lay down outside the overhang, with its forefeet tucked beneath its chest.

A drop collected on its ear.

The drop grew heavy and fell.

The little animal watched.

At first, there was no way to distinguish that watching from what the hollow had always held. Then one of the notched nails loosened from the moss.

A translucent stem rose beneath it.

The animal fastened the nail again.

Deep in the mountain, the new bearing carried the change. The seven groves remained warm.

The power kept still.

The forepaw loosened a second time. This time it came away from the nose.

The animal shut its eye.

That, too, was allowed.

It slept through several of the visitor’s breaths, with its face uncovered. When it woke, it turned one of its narrow ears toward the sound of breathing.

The power lowered its head.

The animal stretched its muzzle forward, far enough to smell the stranger. Its lip trembled along the old scar. It drew back.

There was no disappointment in the body outside the slab. The brown creature merely shifted one cold foot beneath the other.

After a while, the animal put its paw down on the unoccupied moss.

Not on the visitor. Not yet.

The power moved closer by the width of that paw, then stopped again.

What followed was slow and rather awkward. The overhang was low. The stranger did not know which root could comfortably bear a shoulder. Once, the animal made a sharp sound, and the stranger backed away until the sound ceased.

Eventually there were two bodies beneath the stone.

They did not fit the old depressions exactly.

The brown creature’s hindquarters extended beyond the dry part of the bed. One of its ears was folded inconveniently against a root. Between their chests there remained a narrow passage of cold air.

The silver tail lifted.

For the first time, its tip passed beyond the pressure that had always been waiting there.

It rested on an unfamiliar flank.

The power had intended to keep watch over the bearing, the roots, the whole precarious kindness of the place. It did so. But it also learned the small, immediate difficulty of lying still while another creature found a comfortable position against its ribs.

Outside, the waterfall retained its white violence. No leaf fell from the seven trees.

Under the slab, a forepaw reached across the remaining gap and caught gently in brown fur.

The next breath moved them both.
