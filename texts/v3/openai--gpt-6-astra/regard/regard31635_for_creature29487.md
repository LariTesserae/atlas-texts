The power arrived while the animal was beneath the moss.

For a long time it could see only the place where the last plate had disappeared. Drops struck there, bent the small green stems, and passed down into darkness.

It did not lift the moss.

Instead it followed the water, attending to the spaces through which it moved: a hollow left by a rotten twig, the underside of a leaf, a thread of root with one dead tip and three living ones. Below the step, the animal had stopped with its front beneath a fold of softened bark. Its rasp was moving. The little plant in its damaged plate lay pressed against the roof of the hollow.

There was no trouble here requiring an answer. The power let the animal feed.

Its attention spread through the shelf. It found eggs with living young inside them, and eggs whose stillness would never end. It found the bird asleep with its head turned into its shoulder. Beneath the bird’s perch, a loose scale of bark sheltered two pale-backed hatchlings.

It understood how easily it could make an error.

A warmer wind might lengthen the feeding hours and strip the exposed passages of moisture. More water might drown the deepest chambers. A shelter placed over the bird’s hunting ground would preserve some young and leave the bird searching longer in the cold.

So it remained through the night, and through the brief thaw that followed, and through the weather that closed the place again.

It learned where a drop was useful.

***

Below the lowest living buttress, the mountain had split.

The fracture was new. Meltwater entered it from above, froze, and pressed the outer piece of stone a little farther from the slope. In several winters that piece would fall. It was no larger than a sleeping bird, but beneath it lay a recess containing old grit, sheltered from the wind and within reach of the hanging roots.

The power loosened the stone now.

It caught the fragment as it came away, before it could strike anything below, and laid it flat in the scree. The exposed recess was dark. A few grains slid out; most remained.

Nothing entered it that year.

The roots continued hanging into mist, collecting ice. The power waited. It did not draw their tips toward the opening or make them lengthen faster than their tissues could bear.

In the next thawing season, one fine branch of root touched the lip. It withdrew a little as it dried. Later, another found the underside, where condensation lingered after the open rock had gone pale.

That one held.

The power watched what followed. Water began taking a slightly different route down the buttress. A cushion of moss above the new attachment grew drier at one edge, but its center stayed wet. In the recess, the root divided.

There would be weight here eventually. For now there was contact.

***

It attended to smaller things as well.

Once, after the gray bird had worked its beak beneath an adult’s rim, the animal fell onto a plate of ice. The bird descended after it, caught the edge of its shell, and carried it away.

The power felt the brief tightening of the foot. It also felt the bird’s hunger, which had occupied most of that day. Its care had room for both, though the event could not be made good for both.

Another time an animal fell where the bird did not see it.

It lay overturned below a broken crown. Its foot reached into empty air, folded inward, reached again. A moss stem stood close enough to brush the fringes but too far away to grasp.

The power bent the stem.

The foot touched it and contracted. For several moments nothing else happened. Then the shell tipped, scraped the ice, and rolled against the moss. The animal held there until water gathered beneath its body.

The power stayed until it began to move.

***

The one with the split fourth plate passed much of that season in the lower chambers. Its plant put out a third leaf, lost it, and kept the original two.

Near the end of a thaw, it entered a passage that had formerly led behind the central pool. Winter had shifted the wood. The passage now narrowed to a blind seam, and the animal followed its wetness inward until its raised plate caught against the roof.

It pulled forward. The old shell edges pressed harder into the bark.

It backed away a fraction, turned, and tried again.

Here the power made no immediate change. The foot was still well sealed; the seam remained wet. After a long interval the animal flattened one side of its body and began to reverse along the angle by which it had entered.

The little plant dragged against the roof. One leaf tore.

The animal came free.

Had the power widened the seam at once, it would never have learned what the animal could do. Now it knew a little more, including how long to wait—and that waiting must end before strength did.

***

Over the years, its work accumulated without becoming a separate structure.

There was a sound foothold beneath the lower wall. There were a few fallen animals that reached cover. Once, when a sliding slab of snow would have sheared away the original trees, the power broke it higher on the slope. Snow still entered the crowns, but in pieces the branches could bend beneath.

It left the smaller collapses. Daylight reached old floors. Shoots crowded the gaps. The bird found new places to hunt.

The power’s attention did not diminish when it chose to do nothing. It continued following the hidden water, the slow repair of shell, the roots testing stone.

On an evening many seasons after its arrival, drops fell onto the lowest step. Beneath it, a dark edge lifted. Two feelers emerged, and one touched the running water.

Far below, the new root held its weight.

The animal began its descent.
