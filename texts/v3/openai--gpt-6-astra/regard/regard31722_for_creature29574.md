The power first attends to the hollow through a complete failure of the pump.

It does not cause the failure. It follows what would happen if the bearing seized, if the cable broke inside the bank, if the person who knows the use of the wooden wedge stopped coming. It follows the water through the first two days and farther, beyond the point at which anyone has yet waited.

Some passages find other ways to drain. Others do not. The moisture above the old stains is water being forced through tissue too thin to bear much pressure. Beneath the pressure vessel, a chamber slowly separates from the masonry. The animal closes around the separation. This saves one part and deprives another.

The power lets none of this happen. Having understood it, it attends to the motor as it is: running quietly, its bearing adequate for the present, its footing held between living folds.

There is time.

It spends some of that time learning which changes are injuries.

The hollow offers many things that could be mistaken for them. A bank collapses and exposes a feeding surface. A swollen sac empties and remains flat for nine days. Two regions cease to communicate through a passage that had joined them for years. None of these, by itself, calls for repair.

Elsewhere a movement scarcely visible from the steps has been costing the animal something for a long while.

The brick beneath the pump house pulls against the divided sac whenever the eastern chambers fill. Each half contracts around it. Neither can complete the movement. The metallic ticking is the smallest outward part of this.

The power begins there.

It supports the load carried by the brick. Over several cycles of filling, it eases the metal bracket away, less than the width of a grain of sand at a time. The tissue tightens, then loosens. Water finds a thin route along the brick’s lower face.

Only when that route remains open does the power move the brick.

There is no tearing. The brick comes to rest a little farther down, where it supports a cracked length of drain. The two halves of the sac do not immediately unite. One has a thickened inner surface; the other has become accustomed to emptying first. They continue in their different rhythms.

But the ticking stops.

For a while, this is all the power does.

The animal explores the absence through repeated contractions. A fold draws inward farther than it used to. Clear fluid enters a space that has long been dry. The fold closes again.

The power does not take the opening for gratitude.

It has other ways of knowing that the intervention was good.

***

Beyond the eastern wall is an old settling chamber, filled almost to its roof with rubble. A pipe once joined it to the hollow. The pipe is still present, blocked near its mouth by concrete poured during a repair.

The power examines the ground above it, the foundations of the three houses, the water held farther inland. It leaves the wall’s bow alone. Straightening it would pull on more than brickwork.

Inside the buried chamber, it settles loose material into a stable bed. It makes room between the larger pieces without carrying them away. There are low ceilings, rough surfaces, sheltered angles. A shallow channel passes through these and returns toward the hollow at a different height.

The concrete obstruction acquires a narrow opening.

At first, only water enters.

It is water from the hollow, carrying its fine suspended food, its salt and iron and the products of old decay. The new chamber does not rinse the animal with something purer. Its first exchange is so slight that a hair laid across the opening would scarcely bend.

Several weeks pass before a living edge reaches it.

The edge rests against the concrete. It extends a clear film into a pit, withdraws, and leaves the pit bare. Later it returns.

The power waits.

By the end of the season, a thin gray lining occupies the first sheltered angle. Beyond it, most of the chamber remains empty. That is sufficient. There is somewhere to extend that will not put a new thickness of flesh beneath a mounting bolt.

There is also another route for water.

The power adjusts that route while the pump is running. It allows a little pressure to pass, then less, then a little more. Nothing is required to learn a wholly different hollow in an afternoon.

When the motor next stops, the western compartments drain more slowly.

The nearest folds do not remain painfully full.

The motor starts again on its ordinary schedule.

***

Some dangers arrive carrying tools and good intentions.

A person could decide to clear the drains. A person could see the gray tissue beneath the footing and scrape it away before fitting a new motor. The walls and roof might be removed in an effort to restore this small piece of shore.

The power attends to the people, too.

It does not place an instruction inside anyone’s mind.

When the maintainer next kneels beside the motor, the light through the patched roof falls across the wooden wedge. The impression in it is plainly visible. Beneath the footing, a fold has drawn back without being touched.

The maintainer waits with the wedge unused.

Then the fold returns, slowly enough for the movement to be unmistakable.

This person has already seen many things. What changes is the weight given to them.

That evening, the maintenance record gains a page. There is a drawing of the footing, a description of the living material, and a warning against cutting or chemical cleaning. The account is tentative where it should be tentative. It records what happened when the pump was stopped and how long recovery took.

A second person is shown the safe footholds.

Later, someone who knows animals of mud and brackish water comes down the steps. They leave the curved cover open only briefly. They take water in a bottle, photographs, and no flesh.

The hollow acquires a continuing claim on human care, modest enough at first to fit into a maintenance budget. The pump is no longer merely obsolete equipment whose expense must eventually be eliminated. Its bearings matter. So does the manner in which they are replaced.

The power makes certain that understanding does not depend on one person remaining alive.

***

It leaves the shorebird its feeding places.

It leaves the pale grazers beneath the felt, the rust, the cloudy water, and the slow losses at the edges of the clay. It does not hold every grain in place. The rushes survive on a somewhat smaller island; elsewhere, a seed takes root in grit that remains exposed for an unexpectedly long interval.

There are still injuries. Some belong to the ordinary traffic of bodies sharing a confined place. The power attends to these without requiring the hollow to become harmless.

Its presence is most complete beneath the pump house, where the freed sac fills.

There, a movement that used to end against the bracket continues. The flesh rounds into the available space. A narrow internal passage opens fully, and water runs through without the familiar hesitation.

No one on the steps can see this.

Above, the green lamp is lit. The spare bearing is dry in its paper. A new note inside the cabinet names the person to call if the usual maintainer cannot come.

Below, beyond the eastern wall, an edge of living tissue enters another rough angle and settles against it.

The power remains attentive as the edge begins to thicken.
