The power notices the inch.

It has been attending to the basin: the narrow gaps beneath the uprights, the water diverted through distances that do not belong inside the rock, the long accommodation between an engine and a place. There are things here it could repair. There are things it could stop.

Then the finger touches your ankle.

The old one has a sore place beneath its armor. It has a stiff flank and work unfinished along the shallows. Nevertheless, it has noticed that you are badly shaped for this room and do not yet know where to put yourself.

The power lets the next surge come.

The water rises, opens, falls. Your foot remains dry.

*

Its first act is to learn what the crust is doing.

Some of it is burden. Some is protection. Beneath one creamy ridge, tissue is being pulled whenever the old one extends its rear limb. Beneath another, a fracture has healed around a mineral splint. The roots of the little rosette lie mostly in accumulated grit, but one has entered a seam where pulling it loose would hurt.

The power does not make the old one clean.

It follows the strain through the joined plates. It attends to the soft hook where a nail is growing back, to the flushed patch, to the way the head folds close when spray carries the wrong proportion of dissolved stone.

It listens to the answer from the passage.

There is another animal beyond the bend. Smaller, though not young. It is braced in a flooded throat of rock, waiting for a current to reverse. The answering tremor was made with its rear pads. It has heard the old one announce an unfamiliar weight on the bench.

Neither is alone in quite the way a visitor might suppose.

*

On the dry ledge there are four sockets.

The power leaves them empty. It does not yet know why they were emptied.

Instead, beside the bench, where the old one can reach without getting up, it makes a small thing out of material carried away in the next retreating wave.

It is a curved piece of pale stone, no longer than one of your fingers. One side is smooth. The other has a shallow lip.

It is not sharp enough to cut living tissue.

The old one detects its arrival through the floor. Three pigment cups turn toward it. A fingertip approaches, withdraws, approaches again. The gray slits open above it.

The power waits.

Eventually the old one picks it up.

It tries the lip against the bench, then against the limestone collar on one of its own fingers. A little powder comes away. The tool is laid down.

For some time nothing happens except water.

Then the old one retrieves it and reaches toward the three joined plates.

The angle is wrong.

The power could move the tool in its hand. Instead, it places a second shape beside the first, longer and bent.

This earns a prolonged stillness.

The old one puts both objects beneath its head. Its shoulder folds pulse. It presses a sequence into the bench unlike the one it used before.

The power answers from the new stone: one brief vibration, followed by silence.

Not an imitation of a familiar call. Only enough to disclose that something is attending.

The old one keeps hold of the bent tool.

*

The first bridge of limestone parts with a dry tick.

The old one flattens instantly. Its pads lock to the floor. The little crab vanishes beneath a shoulder plate.

Nothing follows.

Slowly, the animal lifts its body. It bends toward the side that has resisted bending for so long.

The movement stops almost as soon as it begins.

It tries again.

Under the crust, a narrow space opens between plates. Water enters. Several transparent inhabitants retreat deeper into their wet seams, disturbed but uninjured.

The old one holds that unfamiliar posture through an entire rise and fall of the basin.

Then it places the tool against the next bridge.

Here the power gives assistance, but only where the pressure of the tool asks for it. It makes the dead mineral yield along a line that does not enter the flesh. When the hand pauses, the yielding stops.

The old one leaves the last bridge intact.

The power leaves it too.

There may be a reason. There may only be enough change for one interval.

*

The flushed place beneath the shoulder is harder.

The old one cannot work there without raising the plate that hurts it. Twice it brings the tool close. Twice it draws it away.

The power makes a shallow hollow in the loose grit beside the screen and fills it with water at the temperature of the shallows. Nothing in the hollow stings or adheres. Nothing passes from it into the basin that would alter the lives below.

The old one tastes it with the slits beneath its head.

It puts a fingertip in.

Much later, it lowers the sore edge of its shoulder into the hollow.

The power eases the inflammation slowly enough that the old one can lift away. It does lift away, once. The relief stops changing. The animal examines the wet rim, then settles into it again.

You remain on the bench. There is no task assigned to you, no revelation that requires you to kneel.

After a while, the long rear limb beside your boot unfolds farther than it has unfolded since you arrived.

*

The smaller inhabitant comes through the low arch during a retreating current.

It carries a wad of brown stems folded under its body. At the first shelf it stops so abruptly that the stems spread in the water.

The old one taps the floor.

An exchange passes through the stone, quick and too complicated for you to divide into parts.

The newcomer advances. It touches the bent tool, the broken mineral, the lip of the hollow. Then it goes around behind the old one and probes the remaining bridge with the underside of a finger.

The old one bars the finger.

The newcomer withdraws it and begins sorting its stems into a sheltered pocket.

The power learns from this also.

*

There are larger matters.

Deep beneath the chamber, a support has been wearing away. Not one of the three black uprights, but the stone beneath a buried length of conduit. In time it would settle. The settling would narrow a passage, and a surge would reach the bench from behind the screen.

The power strengthens that support.

It does so without changing the passage’s width, the taste of the water, or the tremors by which the inhabitants anticipate its arrival. It does not quiet the chamber. The transparent creatures need the returning currents; the rosettes need the spray. The old one has spent a lifetime making places within this force.

For now, the work is sufficient.

The hollow remains. So do the tools, which can be ignored, carried away, or used for purposes their maker has not imagined. The power keeps its attention near, without placing it between the inhabitants and their room.

On the bench, the old one spreads its hand into the worn depressions.

This time the sixth fingertip curls beneath with less effort.

The basin flattens.

You draw back your own feet.

A finger lifts toward your ankle, pauses, and returns to the stone.
