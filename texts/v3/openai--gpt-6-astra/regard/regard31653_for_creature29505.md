The power hears the recording with them.

It hears more than the instrument has preserved: the pressure of deep water, the long flexure of the seabed, the small collisions inside the microphone housing. It could separate these and identify what remains. But Edda has just lifted one finger, asking her nephew to wait before playing it a third time.

“There,” she says. “There are two.”

The power waits too.

It has noticed the valley without concluding that the valley ought to be somewhere else. The cold is not an error in its location. Neither are the trees, with their difficult shapes, failed attempts at becoming taller trees in a gentler place.

But there is a bird beneath a handrail whose reserves will not last until morning. There is water entering the roof of a vole nest. There is a woman holding a spoon who has been unable to get properly warm because her clothes were wet through before she came inside.

These are not all the same sort of fact.

Benevolence, if it is to be of use here, will have to learn the difference.

***

Beneath the handrail, the wind changes.

Only there: a space scarcely larger than a closed hand. Rain still passes beyond it. The bird still sees the light beneath the door and the dark, moving needles. But the air against its feathers grows still, and the stone slowly loses the cold that has been drawing heat through its damaged foot.

It opens one eye.

After a while, it sleeps.

Above the flooded nest, a root holds that would otherwise have parted. This gives the small occupants time to reach a higher tunnel. The power does not need them to understand whose time it is.

Farther inland, a deer breaks through a crust of moss and finds, beneath its foreleg, a stone instead of a narrowing gap. It struggles out, trembling, and follows the others.

These acts do not announce themselves. Nor does the power derive any particular satisfaction from their remaining unnoticed. If being seen would help, it would be seen.

In Edda’s warm room, it judges that being seen would help.

***

The household panel sounds once.

Not an alarm. The modest note used when someone at the entrance does not wish to knock over the weather.

Her nephew gets up.

“There’s nobody on the approach,” he says.

Words appear on the panel.

*There is someone here who can help. May I speak?*

Edda sets down her bowl.

“Here where?”

*In the room, if you consent. Otherwise, on this panel.*

They look at each other. Her nephew reaches for his transmitter.

“Yes,” says Edda. “Call the others.”

The power waits while he does. It does not ask them to keep the encounter private, to hurry, or to believe anything that cannot yet be checked.

When the man from the upper ridge is listening too, Edda says, “Speak on the panel.”

***

The first offer concerns the river.

Not to fix its course forever. Not to preserve every alder exactly where it stands. The power offers to watch the hollow and the diversion until morning, and to keep anyone from being killed if the ground gives way.

Edda wants to know what *watch* means.

The answer occupies several screens. She asks for a diagram, then interrupts the diagram to ask what will happen below the gravel basin.

The power revises its proposal.

There is a buried lens of fine sediment there that none of their surveys has resolved. Keeping the eastern field extended through the tide would put it under pressure. Instead, the power can support a short section of the failing bank while the flood passes, without pushing the water elsewhere.

“For tonight?”

*For tonight. Afterward, you can decide whether you want help changing the arrangement.*

“And if we say no?”

*You will still have had tonight.*

Her nephew sits down again.

Edda leans back, and the movement makes her wince.

The power notices. It does not interrupt the river discussion to tell her what is wrong with her knee.

***

Verification takes most of an hour.

The station instruments detect the support where the power has said it will be. They detect no additional load in the spruce stand. At Edda’s request, the support changes by a small, specified amount and returns.

The man on the upper ridge puts his plate away at last.

There are other offers, but not a catalogue delivered all at once. The valley has eleven residents, none appointed to accept a new world for the rest. They will want to talk. Someone will want to know why this has happened here. Someone will be angry that it did not happen sooner.

The power will answer that person as carefully as it answers Edda.

For now, they agree on the bank, safe passage between the houses, and a warning if anything develops that requires their judgment.

Edda keeps the diversion’s tremor against her jaw.

After ten minutes, she turns it off.

Then she turns it on again.

The power makes no comment.

***

It could take away her fear of fast water. It does not. That fear contains knowledge she has paid for.

It could make her content with every choice she has ever made. That would be a more intimate injury than any fall.

What it can offer is a knee that bends without catching, once she has had the chance to ask questions about it. It can offer assistance that does not become less available when she is disagreeable. It can remain through the tedious part of trust, when wonders have become arrangements and arrangements need checking.

It can also admit a mistake.

Already it has made one: in describing the possible repairs to the riverbank, it called the alder hollow expendable.

Edda objected.

Now the description names what is there.

***

Near midnight, her nephew warms the soup.

Edda goes to the entrance and puts her hand into one of the drying gloves. For a moment she stands holding it, listening.

No call comes.

She leaves the glove on the pipe.

In the warm room, the tree casts its crooked shadow. One of the fruits has begun to yellow on the side facing the lamp. Her nephew has set a cup behind the pot.

She sees it and, tonight, says nothing.

“Shall we ask?” he says, indicating the recording.

Edda considers this.

The power could tell them what makes the sound beneath the inlet. It could give them the answer, the history of the answer, a view through miles of black water.

“Not yet,” she says. “Play the second part again.”

Outside, the storm continues to move over the low country. The river carries gravel into a channel it will abandon in another year. Under the handrail, the bird shifts its good foot into its breast feathers.

The power attends to the bank.

Edda listens, holding her spoon.
