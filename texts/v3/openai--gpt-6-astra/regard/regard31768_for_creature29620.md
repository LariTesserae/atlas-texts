The first thing the power does is nothing that the district can detect.

For several days, the water rises and falls without an additional load on the paving. The power attends to the difference between a passage that is empty and a passage through which almost nothing moves. It follows the warmth beneath the lunchroom, the brief reversals of flow inside the walls, the thin connection passing through a root that will not survive another winter.

It does not assume that everything maintained is needed. Nor does it assume that what appears unnecessary can safely be removed.

Under the demolished shed, a channel carries very little water. But during one hour of the pressure rise, it carries water the other way.

The power waits long enough to learn this.

Then it tightens the loose guard panel.

It takes care with the nut. A pale attachment has spread beneath the washer, and simply turning it would tear a strip from the lining behind the wall. The panel is supported first. The washer is lifted without rotating. The attachment remains wet while another fastening is made a little farther along the bracket.

The clatter stops.

In the foundations, the organism continues reinforcing itself against a vibration that no longer comes. It will do so for some time. The power does not hurry it out of this mistake.

There are larger failures approaching.

One belt has begun to separate on its inner face. A bearing runs hotter each month. The cable supplying the pumps passes through a conduit where the insulation has gone brittle, though the brass drip edge above the cabinet is still sound. Beyond the hollow, in a room with dry floors, a date has been written beside the district’s electricity account.

None of these things is within reach of a chemical difference traveling through mortar.

The power arranges for the account to remain open. It repairs the cable. It makes a second means of moving water before disturbing the first.

The new pump stands on its own foundation, outside the wettest ground. Its pipes enter through openings already bare of living tissue. At first it runs so gently that its contribution is smaller than the difference between a wet morning and a dry one.

For weeks, nothing is taken away.

Gradually, the bearing cools. The old flywheel turns with less strain. Where membranes have been braced against a familiar current, that current remains familiar.

When the damaged belt is finally lifted free, the district does not have to discover all at once what still water means.

The power could make the hollow clean.

It could remove the oil, scour the orange deposits from the iron, separate roots from masonry, and leave every passage flowing freely. It could drain the streets and return the paving to the air.

Instead, it takes small samples from places where a drop is already about to fall. It learns which impurities have become food, which are merely endured, and which are accumulating faster than anything here can bear. What must be reduced is reduced upstream, slowly. The meeting of waters beneath the floor is left intact.

The basin keeps its dark surface. The drowned dial keeps working.

There is no attempt to give the organism a mouth.

Whatever its experience may be, a voice placed in the pumping house would not necessarily belong to it. The power does not require thanks, or a name, or evidence that the attention has been understood.

It does make a few new possibilities.

Beside a failing root connection, it lays a rough, porous length of fired clay. Warm water touches the lower edge. For a season the clay remains only clay. Then a translucent seam appears along its underside.

Elsewhere, an offered passage stays empty.

That one is left empty.

The power also attends to what does not live in the hollow. It does not bring insects into water that will kill them, or compel the organism to become a kinder habitat by dismantling the chemistry of its own survival. On the dry ground above the retaining wall, a blocked rain gutter is redirected into a shallow bed of gravel and soil. Water there is cool, rain-fed, separate from the basin below.

In spring, something drinks from it.

The locked gate is not thrown open to a crowd. The steps are repaired, and a place to stand is made above the highest reach of the pale growth. There is a key, held by people whose work is to keep the district supplied rather than put it to use.

They are paid for the hours in which nothing needs replacing.

Their instructions include the ordinary dangers: electricity, hot pipes, uncertain footing. They also include drawings of attachments beneath washers, the location of the backward-running channel, and a warning against emptying a sump merely because its water looks dirty.

There are blank pages for what the instructions have missed.

The power does not make them infallible. It gives them time, instruments, spare parts, and someone to call before a doubtful repair. Much of the protection consists of making care possible on an unremarkable working day.

Years later, the hollow still has no distant view.

Leaves crowd the bridges. Warm water rounds the brick beneath an outlet. The wall beside the lunchroom weeps during the daily extension, and the cups remain gathered at one end of the tilted table.

But a stopped pump no longer means that every warm seam must begin to cool. A broken connection need not be the only connection. Beyond the gate, a missed visit is noticed.

Below the lowest step, the organism advances a little into a damp joint, then withdraws before evening. It does not enter the dry street.

Under the pumping-house floor, water gathers against a hanging sheet. Beads appear on the other side, enlarge, and fall.

Nothing in their falling distinguishes rescue from an ordinary continuation.

The power is content with that.
