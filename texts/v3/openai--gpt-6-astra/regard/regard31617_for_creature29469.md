The power notices the second hair lifting.

It does not mistake this for a greeting.

For a while it does nothing. The animal is engaged in a difficult event that is going well, and help, introduced at the wrong moment, could become another difficulty. Water is entering the new body. The soft limbs are finding their places. Beneath the head, the mouth folds imperfectly, then folds again.

The power keeps watch over the pressure and the warmth. It does not stop the shadow.

Beyond the chamber, it follows the strained note through the hinge.

There is much to follow. Loads pass through the bearing into a shaft, through the shaft into members broad enough to contain rooms. Far below, a pump draws from a reservoir in which other small things live. The service lamp belongs to a circuit that supplies more than light. The loose part that knocks is a collar whose retaining pin has worn almost through.

The collar can wait a little longer. The pin cannot wait indefinitely.

Before touching either, the power finds what they hold.

It is wise enough to spend time on this.

Inside the seam, the animal pulls one soft foot free. Its hooks close on nothing. It tries again and catches an old root.

The next turning is approaching.

There is no command for it in the chamber, no gathering of clouds. A difference in temperature elsewhere has begun a sequence of adjustments. Soon the load will shift, the hinge will turn, and the water will lean across the basin.

The power could hold the whole structure still.

Instead, it eases the approaching change through a longer interval. The same load arrives, but not all at once. The hinge reaches the same position. Fresh water comes through the three worn mouths.

The copper undersides of the leaves show briefly.

Within the seam, the animal keeps its hold.

***

The first lasting alteration is made outside.

The worn pin is replaced without a blow passing through the bearing. The collar is secured. For the first time in a very long while, the engines’ growl contains no irregular knock.

The animals do not become quiet. They were quiet already.

A grazer swims from the ceiling moss toward the broad leaves. The hunter, still hidden, turns its head. The disturbance reaches one row of jaw hairs, then the other. Its mouth remains folded. It is not ready.

The power leaves the growl alone. Some of it is the sound of useful work. Some could be reduced, but reducing it would change the warnings that arrive through the animal’s feet. There will be time to learn which is which.

At the drain, it watches a snail feed.

The snail has extended far beyond its shell. Its mouth moves over the short green growth, and its foot holds the enamel with an intimacy no large hand could reproduce.

A turning begins. The snail contracts, but one edge of its foot lifts.

The power gives that edge a place to settle.

It is a very small place: a roughness in the enamel, no higher than the grit already lying in the channels. The foot spreads across it. Water passes.

Afterward the power examines the throat of the drain, the space beneath it, the route the water takes through darkness. It does not close the drain. It does not lay a mesh across it that would collect fibers, then silt, then the whole chamber’s flow.

Over several turnings it makes a shallow approach along one side, with purchase all the way back to the floor. The fastest water still runs through the middle. Nothing has to discover the new route before it can be of use.

A shrimp is the first to climb out of it.

The snail continues eating where it is.

***

Beneath the broad colony, the roots have reached as far into the broken seam as they can.

The power does not open the seam. On its other side is a surface that must remain dry.

Instead, it finds a sound thickness of metal nearby and makes a recess within it. The work is slow enough that no flake comes loose and no edge grows hot. What appears beneath the roots is not a second chamber, only a continuation of the shelter: a low place, dark even when the leaf turns copper, with two ways in.

Water moves through it gently.

For several days nothing larger than a suspended speck enters.

Then the young shrimp backs into it with a piece of dead stem. Later, the hunter puts its head inside, withdraws, and spends an entire interval of light elsewhere.

The power waits.

It has made the recess a little broader at the back. There is room for a body enlarged by water, and for limbs that cannot yet bear its weight.

***

The animal hardens.

It comes out of the seam carrying no knowledge of the pin, the load, or the small alteration near the drain. Its covering catches the lamp in delicate pieces. The green strip behind its head is gone with the old skin. Soon it will begin again.

Hunger takes hold in the feet.

The hunter climbs beneath the outermost leaf and projects its head into open water. Above it, an egg opens in the moss. A pale body jerks free, pauses, and begins to swim.

The power attends to this too.

There is no way to feed the hunter by forgetting what its food is. It watches the hatchling’s uneven progress, the turning of the blunt head, the brief adjustment of six feet.

The hunter springs.

Afterward a brown shape begins to appear inside it.

The power does not call the chamber finished.

It continues to trace the little oil that sometimes enters with the warm water. It finds a seal upstream and repairs it. It preserves the heat the plants require. It learns how much light reaches the ceiling moss, and what happens in the reservoir when a pump slows. Its attention extends beyond the basin, but does not withdraw from it.

There will be failures it can prevent without making the inhabitants helpless in a world remade around them. There will be injuries requiring more than an alteration to a wall. It means to remain available for those.

For now, the animal has eaten.

It returns beneath the leaves and begins cleaning a jaw hair. One foot braces against an old root. Another rests on the lip of the new recess.

Above the great distance below, the hinge turns.

The leaf bends. Water passes through the shelter.

The foot stays where it is.
