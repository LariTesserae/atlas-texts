The power did not enter through the split.

To enter there would have meant widening it, or making itself small, and it had not yet learned whether the pale line overhead was a wound, a window, or something for which there was no word outside the salt.

It attended first to the hollow as it was.

It distinguished every voice without taking any from the chord. It understood how the roots held their extravagance against the pressures in the crystal. It found the places where an ordinary passage of time would have done harm: a membrane emptied of its tension, a ceiling loosened, a body required suddenly to finish a breath that had become part of its structure.

It did not set the room in motion.

Nor did it assume that stillness was peace.

Beside the bridge, the maker’s hand retained its little distance from the knot. The power studied that distance. There was nothing physically difficult about crossing it. But the room contained no way for the hand to be here and afterward there.

The power made room for an afterward.

At first it belonged only to the maker’s attention.

Nothing fell. None of the animals lost its place beneath a leaf. The great chord remained as it had been. But the maker could now hear it, and then hear it again.

Between those hearings was a silence too small for any ear.

In that silence, something could be considered.

The maker considered the knot.

Then they noticed that they had considered it.

Their eyes changed.

The power was present without a shape, but not without a location. It had chosen the bare floor beyond the bridge, where there was nothing to displace. The maker’s attention found it there.

It offered no account of how long the room had been this way. There was no useful number. It did not name the maker’s condition for them.

Instead, it made its offer intelligible.

The hand could move, if they wished. It would not crumble. The movement would not pull the gardens apart. There would be somewhere for the breath to go, and another breath available after it. They could stop. The power would remain while they discovered what stopping and continuing meant.

For a while, the maker did neither.

The new interval held their uncertainty as gently as the old room had held their concentration.

Then the divided thumb closed.

It caught the misplaced turn of fiber. One squared nail went beneath it, lifted it, laid it beside the other.

The maker withdrew their hand.

Under the rail, the knot was smooth.

Pleasure passed across their face and did not have to remain there.

Something else followed it: alarm.

Their filaments lifted toward the gardens. They looked at the ivory animal beneath the nearest leaf, then at the slack braid crossing the floor.

The power had not forgotten it.

But neither had it taken the maker’s small movement as permission to wake every living thing into a world that could not yet sustain them.

The maker held out a finger.

Around the ivory animal, the power opened another interval. It supported the creature through the unfamiliar change, letting its own impulses find their way into movement.

One foreleg lifted.

It rested on the maker’s nail.

The creature’s note wavered. For the first time, there was a slight unevenness in the enormous sound.

The maker’s mouth opened.

What came out was not a question the power had expected. It concerned the animal’s feet: whether the sharp salt would hurt them now.

So the power learned something important about the work.

It did not turn the floor soft. Those angular grains belonged to the hollow, and the roots depended on the crystals beneath them. Instead it showed the maker a way to split the fallen husks into broad, pliant strips. With their consent, it made enough room in time for the work.

The maker sat up.

They kept the blunt husk that had lain beneath their cheek.

There were difficulties. Sitting made the low ceiling newly troublesome. The first completed breath frightened them. When their chest emptied, they put a hand against it and waited, rigid with an entirely new kind of stillness.

The power stayed.

The next breath came.

Later, the maker asked for the sound to be less.

Not gone. Less.

The power gave the seat a quietness that could be entered and left. From there, the five thickets were still audible, but the ceiling no longer cried through the teeth. The maker sat on the mat with the ivory animal across two fingers. For a time they listened chiefly to its feet touching their skin.

They did not ask to leave.

The power did not make leaving the price of help.

There would be work to do before the whole garden could move safely: new ways for the roots to receive what growth would spend, places for young creatures to unfold, an answer to what the full fruits required. Some of that the power could provide. Some it would learn from the inhabitants. It would not hurry their home into becoming an ordinary cave.

It did, however, make departure possible.

Beyond the split, a sheltered passage waited. No weather could enter uninvited. No footstep above could bring the ceiling down. The maker could know the passage was there without being drawn toward it.

Only after this did they reach into the pocket.

They unfolded the soft scrap four times.

The object inside was a bead, imperfectly round, with a hole that wandered slightly off center. The maker held it in their palm. The power did not look through it for a history.

After a while, the maker wrapped it again.

Then they took the shell comb from the leaves.

They adjusted one tooth, listened, and adjusted another. The clearer thicket gave a low, branching sound. Beneath it, several small mouths closed. Others opened. The folded animal shifted one limb to make room for its young.

The maker glanced toward the bare place beyond the bridge.

They brought one of the cups and set it there.

It was still empty.

The power accepted it.

Above them, the root in its translucent sheath remained short of the opening. Nothing compelled it upward. But the distance was no longer promised forever.

And beside the bridge, where a hand had once waited without end, there was a small patch of disturbed salt.
