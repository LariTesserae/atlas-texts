The power notices the answer.

Not the pressure pulse passing through a thousand miles of wood, nor the electrical branching far below, but the small movement in the new binding after the strain has gone.

One animal touches.

The other answers.

For a while, the power does nothing that can be felt.

It follows the load through the repaired strand, into the holes, around the shelters. It attends to the green tissue advancing beneath the dead rind. It listens through the bodies of the two workers, where the last contraction has left a tremor in the muscles that release their feet.

One has enough water to return to its feeding circuit.

The other has spent more than it can easily replace.

There are young beneath its back. Their plates are almost ready.

***

Benevolence, here, could be disastrous.

Water poured over the colony would enter galleries that have never needed to drain. A softened cliff would tear around the anchors. Silence imposed on the pumps would be followed, deep inside the continent, by the closing of living passages and the slow collapse of the enormous leaf.

Even a shelter made perfectly rigid would become a blade as the wood grew around it.

The power learns what must be allowed to move.

It watches several pressure cycles. It watches the pale sheath finish curing on the repair. It watches the depleted animal take three steps toward home and stop with one hind foot still extended behind it.

The foot has caught.

The animal reaches back to clean it.

At that moment, the next gust comes upward along the cliff.

The power turns a loose fiber.

It is a small thing, lying in a groove above the animal. The wind would have lifted it anyway. Instead it rolls, catches against a knot, and lies across the groove at an angle.

Behind it is a narrow patch of reduced wind.

The animal draws its foot beneath its body. It cleans the hook fan, folds it, unfolds it. Then it crosses the sheltered patch and reaches its door.

Nothing follows it inside.

***

The power remains through the bright, dry hours.

Within the shelter, the young press against the common membrane. The parent’s reservoirs contract around what is left.

Beyond the door, slightly below it, an old feeding margin has closed under a lip of rind. The animal knows the place. It last fed there many cycles ago, before the opening became too narrow to use.

The power could force it apart.

Instead it attends to the tissue underneath.

Here the continent is growing against a small obstruction: a buried curl of its own dead fiber. On one side, green cells press together; on the other, a shallow fold contains nothing but dry air. The power moves the curl less than the width of a young animal’s foot.

Growth continues.

Over the next several strokes, the fold opens sideways beneath its protective lip. It makes no wound. There is no rush of sap, no wetness exposed to the wind.

But there is now room for a mouth.

When the parent next emerges, it nearly passes the opening.

A forefoot brushes the edge.

The animal stops, tastes, and lowers its head.

The power does not sweeten the cells or make them inexhaustible. It does not give the animal a hunger that can be satisfied nowhere else. This is an ordinary feeding place, sheltered and close, one that the living margin can sustain.

The mouth collar seals.

For a long time, the animal stays there.

***

Farther along the cliff, the old inhabitant receives a signal.

A pull, a pause, two shorter pulls.

It takes time to arrange its forefeet. One no longer closes fully. The strand beneath it has slipped into a notch worn deep by years of use, and some of the movement is being lost against the wood.

The visitor repeats the signal.

The old animal answers.

Outside, the answer is too faint to distinguish from the cliff.

The power has been following this strand. There is no uncertainty about what the worn body is trying to do.

It does not answer on the animal’s behalf.

At the notch, it lifts a splinter that has curled over the lace. The strand comes free.

The old animal pulls again.

This time, the visitor feels it.

***

By the time the light changes, the power has found many places where a very small intervention would help.

It does not mistake this for having found every place.

Beneath one shelter, a hunter passes through the rind. Its own water reserves are low. In another gallery, pale mites are stripping a feeding plug too quickly. Along the rim, a grazer carries an unfamiliar stitcher beneath its shell, and the stitcher has begun to test a parasite lodged in a soft joint.

The lives cross. Help given without attention can travel a long way as harm.

The power attends.

Where danger belongs to the ordinary meeting of hungry bodies, it does not casually choose one body over another. Where an anchor is failing because a buried edge has worn through its sheath, it rounds the edge. Where a molt has trapped a young animal’s rear legs, it holds the old plates apart until the animal draws itself free.

It does not require the young one to struggle longer in order to deserve this.

Along an exposed passage between two old shelters, it settles a fallen strip of rind across three existing ridges. The strip is light, curved, and already dead. Its ends remain loose enough to slide with the cliff.

Wind passes over it.

Beneath it, the route is still dry and rough. Feet must still be placed carefully. But a traveler can now pause halfway across.

The first animal to use the shelter spends several pressure cycles inspecting its underside.

Then it lays a stitch.

***

There are things the power cannot learn simply by looking at damaged wood.

At an abandoned junction, animals continue to pull a strand that leads nowhere. No inhabited chamber lies beyond it. No useful load crosses it. The power follows the strand to its end and finds an old, sealed hollow.

It leaves the strand alone.

Elsewhere, two inhabitants touch shells for much longer than feeding or warning seems to require.

The power does not interrupt them.

***

When the depleted parent returns to the repair, its sides are broader.

It tests the new binding. Below, the second worker emerges, and together they inspect both anchors. There is no further work to do.

For several moments they remain beside the strand.

The great leaf inland turns. Deep chambers gather themselves for the next stroke. Along the cliff, feet settle into tested places.

The power is still there.

It has not made the country quiet. It has not made holding on unnecessary.

Under the fallen strip of rind, a traveler pauses halfway between shelters. In a distant chamber, an old animal’s answer reaches the one waiting outside. Beneath a parent’s back, the young receive a little more water through their common membrane.

The pressure arrives.

The cliff bends, and the colony moves with it.

Afterward, while the local wood is resting, one of the workers sends a small pull through the repaired strand.

There is no warning in it that the power can recognize.

The other worker answers.

Given more time now, they continue.
