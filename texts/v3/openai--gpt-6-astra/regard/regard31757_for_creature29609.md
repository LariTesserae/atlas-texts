The power does not wait for the next gust.

Waiting belongs to the creature’s way of surviving. It is not a condition the mountain must impose tonight.

Behind the boulder, the smaller young one has taken hold of a fold of the adult’s skin. The adult lowers its chest until the little feet encounter the lining of the crease. Two feet transfer. A third remains closed.

The power holds the air still in that narrow place. Not throughout the hollow: the bamboo continues to knock, and the crowns continue their long recoil from the ridge. But no cold current enters beneath the young one’s fur.

The third foot opens.

The adult withdraws.

At the fracture, where the rhododendron’s weight is drawing the root across the stone, the power attends to the stone.

There is no need to make the root unbreakable. It has already been injured; it will need to make what healing it can from the living tissue that remains. Instead, the sharp edge beneath it crumbles. A small quantity of pale grit falls away. The root settles into a shallow, rounded seat.

Below it, two fallen blocks come to rest more firmly against each other. They move less than the width of a nail, but the movement takes the hanging shrub’s weight off the torn mat.

The next gust arrives.

The adult crosses.

It feels the altered tension through its palms and pauses with four limbs attached and two reaching. The route is different. The root no longer gives in the way it gave before.

The power allows it time to test.

Then the adult lowers itself beneath the ferns.

The larger young one is holding the leaf so tightly that the leaf has begun to tear. The adult puts its mouth against the small hands. Its tongue passes over the digits, once and again. A foot catches in the fur of its throat.

Outside, the wind rises toward a force that would have lifted the loose edge of the turf.

That force passes over the hollow.

The shrubs bow. Nothing beneath them is pulled apart.

It takes a long time to release the young one. The power, which could lift the whole slab of turf, does not shorten the work by pulling the small hands open. It keeps the slab steady. It keeps the route available.

When the adult emerges, the young one is fastened crookedly across its belly, with one foot hooked over a shoulder. Its added weight changes every movement. At the crossing, the adult stops again.

Far below, a load enters the crusher.

The rumble travels upward through the mountain, but here it becomes faint enough that the adult can still distinguish wood from wood, the rooted trunk from the branch resting against it, the firm ground from the loose.

It crosses before the gust is finished.

Behind the boulder, the two young find each other by touch. For a moment they pull in opposite directions. Then their bodies fit together in the crease.

The adult remains across the entrance.

There is room for the young to rest. There is not room for it.

The power considers the hollow more closely.

It follows the seams within the rock, the paths by which mist becomes droplets and droplets enter soil. It attends to the roots holding old ground above the scar. It finds the beetles in the litter and the dormant buds beneath their scales. It does not mistake the absence of calls for an absence of other lives.

Behind the boulder is a space filled with angular fragments from a much older fall. A thin fissure ventilates it from above. Water passes to one side, seldom entering. The space is too narrow at its mouth and crowded within, but it lies beyond the reach of the damaged slope.

The power settles the fragments.

Each bears upon the next until they form a low, load-bearing arch. The floor remains earth. The fissure remains open. At the entrance, a projecting stone moves inward, leaving a passage no wider than the gap beneath the crooked trunk.

No roots are cut.

A fern tilts slightly as the soil beneath it finds its new level.

The adult feels the change against its back.

It flattens, drawing its limbs toward the young. For a while it does nothing else.

The power waits.

Eventually a hand goes behind the body, feeling along the boulder. The hand finds an edge where there had been a solid obstruction. It explores the edge and disappears.

The adult turns its head.

There is no scent of another occupant. No current comes up from underneath. The bare patch beside its mouth meets cool stone, then dry soil, then air held close within a small chamber.

It enters halfway and comes out again.

Later, it takes a leaf inside.

***

The power does not put the lost sleeping stone in the new chamber.

It finds the stone far below, lodged in debris, the smooth side turned downward. Bringing it back would be easy. Knowing whether its familiar surface would comfort the creature, or send it searching once more for a room that no longer existed, is less easy.

For now, there is shelter. There are the young. There is enough to do without arranging a reunion the creature has not sought.

The power turns to the broken hillside.

It leaves the recent scar open to weather, but gives the loose plates sound resting places. Rain will still enter. Frost will still work in the cracks. The mountain will change. For the lives crowded into this particular bite of it, change need not arrive again tonight as a fall into darkness.

The displaced turf remains where it landed. Its roots, pressed into contact with the soil beside the boulder, will have a chance to enter it. The sideways ferns keep their upward-turning shoots.

Below, the crushing plant continues to work. The power listens at its foundations and along the roads that feed it. Whatever else may be needed there will require attention to more lives than these three.

It does not forget.

***

Near dawn, the adult comes out of the new chamber.

Cloud has collected along the rhododendron leaves. At one low tip, several droplets join and descend together. The adult catches them with its tongue.

It feeds beneath the bamboo, turning a piece of bark and replacing it. Then it visits the displaced nest. A strip of old lining is still caught beneath a root. It works the strip loose without tearing it and carries it back.

The power has made no voice for itself. There is no presence at the entrance to be approached, appeased, or understood.

Inside, the young have moved against the chamber’s inner wall. The adult threads the recovered lining around them. It lowers one shoulder, folds its limbs, and presses its cheek against a stone that is still rough.

One hind foot keeps hold of the entrance.

Outside, the hollow shudders. The foot tightens.

The chamber holds.

After a while, the foot opens.
