The first thing the power does is keep a grain from entering a fold.

The old inhabitant is putting away its drinking membranes. One support has bent during the night, leaving a shallow pocket that will not empty. In the pocket lies a hard white particle. Each attempt to close the membrane presses it farther into the tissue.

The power moves it less than the width of the grain.

The next fold seats properly.

For a while, the old one remains lifted on its supports. A small eye passes over the place where the obstruction was. Then it finishes closing and sets out toward the six rosettes.

The power goes with it.

***

It could lower the continent.

There is enough warmth below, enough air, enough water moving in the dark folds of the storms to cover every hollow with a lake. It could bring the whole ancient surface down as gently as a stone settling onto a shelter bench.

It considers this long enough to understand what would die.

The rosettes’ roots would swell. Their salts would dissolve. The transparent grazers would lose the dry intervals in which their bodies exchange one kind of mineral for another. Beneath the inhabitants’ plates, organs that have never borne the weight of dense air outdoors would labor continuously.

Other lives would arrive. They would not be replacements.

So the continent remains high.

The power also leaves the wind alone.

But it does not assume that everything which has endured must therefore be wanted.

***

It begins learning at the cup beside the low knob.

The old inhabitant touches each rosette, then a place on the stone where there is no plant. The sequence is repeated on every visit.

At first the power takes this for a check of the growing conditions. Then it notices that the empty place is less sheltered than the others, and that a long-vanished root has left a trace beneath it.

It does not put a seventh rosette there.

There are many things it can determine without asking. The depth of a crack. The charge that will cross a particular seam tomorrow. How much pain is carried in a joint whose soft lining has worn through.

The meaning of an empty place requires another kind of knowledge.

***

Its first address is almost overlooked.

The old inhabitant is resting beside the cup when a small change passes through the stone. It is not a message exactly. More like the pressure of three limb tips against an offered palm.

The old one lowers a pad.

The pressure returns, altered by the contact.

For most of the afternoon, nothing happens that you could see.

Then the old inhabitant rises and moves to the opposite side of the knob.

The power does not follow beneath its body.

A little later, the old one touches the stone again.

This time the exchange continues until sunset.

***

Learning to speak takes less time than learning when to stop speaking.

The power can hold every signal crossing the plateau at once. It soon discovers that this is no reason to do so. Some exchanges close when an unfamiliar presence approaches them. Others are offered outward, repeated at intervals along the foundations.

It learns to answer those.

Inside the shelters, it makes itself audible through a loose mineral tile. The tile rests on the bench and trembles against the dense air.

Its first sounds are too exact. Identical notes return with identical spacing. The young crowd around the bench. An adult takes the tile away and puts it on the floor.

The power continues there, more quietly.

Eventually the inhabitant with the whistle spends an evening correcting it.

You hear low phrases, pauses, a rough overtone. Several times the whistle comes alone, followed by a long silence from the tile.

Later, the inhabitants laugh—or make a sound that you begin to associate with unsuccessful repairs, the young losing their grip on an object, and certain stories told by travelers.

The tile waits.

Then it tries again.

***

The first large intervention is not postponed until everyone understands.

A connection far beneath the plain is failing.

It has been failing for generations. The inhabitants know its surface effects: the abandoned shelter, the fracture that requires increasingly frequent visits, the flash that sends workers racing across the stone. They have been diverting strain through portions of the foundations never built to carry it.

What they cannot see is the final narrowing of a support beneath the rim.

The power holds it.

There is no flash. A detached slab shifts upward by less than the thickness of a plate and comes to rest. Several inhabitants stop where they are.

The power sends an account through the open channels. It repeats the account at the shelters.

Here is what was moving.

Here is what would have happened.

Here is what is being held.

It does not call this permission.

In the following days, the workers test its account. They enter seams, exchange mineral fragments, ask for the pressure to be increased slightly and then returned. The power complies. Beneath one empty room, it opens a view into the foundations—not an abyss in the floor, but a small, touchable arrangement of forces, safe enough for a young inhabitant to put a limb through.

The workers spend a long time around it.

One of them repeatedly asks the same question in different forms.

At last the power understands.

The inhabitant is not asking whether the support can be repaired.

It is asking whether, afterward, the power can go away.

***

The repair takes a season.

It could take an instant. The power explains this too.

Some ask for the instant. They are tired. Several have damaged membranes from working through the hours when they should have been drinking.

Others want to understand every change before it becomes part of the continent beneath them.

The eventual arrangement is uneven. In the dangerous places, the power supplies finished supports at once. Elsewhere, it works beside the maintainers. There are pieces to hold, responses to compare, mistakes that can be made without opening a kilometer of ground.

The new components can be reached from the surface.

They do not require an irreplaceable substance. They do not require a voice to ask the power for continued help. Each can fail gradually enough to be noticed, and there are more of them than are needed.

Beneath the benches, alongside old armor and carefully sorted fragments, appear objects whose uses you slowly learn.

One is a warning instrument.

One can make another warning instrument.

A third is only a model. The young pull it apart so often that an adult finally gives them a separate one.

***

The old inhabitant is less interested in the foundations than the power expected.

It listens to reports. It offers memories of seams that have changed. But when invited to the room where the workers gather, it usually declines.

Its journey to the rosettes has become difficult.

The power offers several kinds of help, and makes the differences clear.

The old one could be carried. The damaged joints could be supported from outside. Some of the worn tissue could be repaired. The power does not know whether the old membrane can be restored without changing the sensation it carries, and says so.

For three days, the old inhabitant accepts nothing.

On the fourth, it asks to feel an external support.

There follows an afternoon of adjustments so small that even the finer limb eyes remain close to the work.

The first arrangement raises its body too far. The second interferes with the pads. The third makes walking effortless, and is rejected almost immediately.

The power does not conclude that effort is sacred.

It asks what was wrong.

The old inhabitant explains, with some impatience, that it could no longer feel whether the ground would bear its weight.

The fourth arrangement works.

It is neither invisible nor beautiful. Two pale arcs show beneath the skirt. At the shelter, a visitor examines them and makes a suggestion. The old one allows a green fragment to be fitted over an exposed fastening.

The next morning it reaches the knob before the sunlight has left the cup.

***

There are other requests.

More sheltered growing places, but not here.

A way to know when the night charge will be dangerous.

Help for a young inhabitant whose plates are growing across a sensory opening.

An account of the country beneath the clouds.

No messages during the cold hours unless something is wrong.

The power makes errors in the small things. A newly sheltered hollow stays damp too long. An explanation of rain occupies most of an evening and answers none of the questions the listeners meant to ask.

These are corrected.

Some inhabitants want nothing from it. One travels a long way to inspect the repairs, touches a new support, and leaves without entering a shelter.

The power makes no attempt to bring it back.

***

You notice the change first in the cleaning of armor.

Travelers still arrive with foreign dust in their joints. Others still come out to help them.

But no one leaves halfway through because a warning has passed along the foundations.

The work is finished. The visitors enter. There is time for another account of the journey.

One evening, the translucent stone is set beside the opening toward the rim. Honey-colored light bends across the floor.

The power knows how to hold the sun’s angle there. It knows how to make the patch brighter, how to spread it through every room on the plateau.

No one asks.

The young pass their limbs through the light. The old inhabitant turns slightly, bringing its little green fastening into the illuminated space.

From the bench comes a tentative note.

The inhabitant with the whistle answers.

Outside, dust travels over the oldest ground in the world. The supports hold without attention. Beside the low knob, six rosettes close against an approaching charge.

When the sunlight leaves the room, the voices continue.
