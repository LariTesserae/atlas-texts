The power notices the pocket before it notices the defect.

These are the same space. On the maintenance drawings, it is an incomplete bed of grout beneath a load-bearing plate. Under the mast, it is a darkness with water moving through it, wide enough for a mantle and narrow enough to exclude a jaw.

There is a work order to fill it.

The order is reasonable. Water entering the gap can reach the levelling screw. Corrosion can proceed where inspection is difficult. In time, the load may cease to be distributed as intended.

The power attends to this also. Above the pocket is a long ladder. People sometimes trust their weight to it.

It does not choose between the ladder and the animal.

For a while, nothing at the mast changes.

The octopus follows the joint, opens a mussel, returns with a piece of shell held beneath its arms. The pumps alter their load. A storm moves the washer another half-metre across the slab.

Elsewhere, the maintenance plan is revised.

***

The first new object arrives without a lamp.

It is lowered onto the floor several metres from the plinth, clear of the service route and the expansion joint. The divers place it rather than let it fall. Even so, the contact is audible through the concrete.

The octopus stays beneath the plate.

The object is low and heavy, made from material that releases nothing harmful into the water. Its underside is irregular. There are several entrances, not all leading to the same chamber. One opening is little more than a slit; another widens behind a turn. Small passages let water move through without exposing the interior to the full operating current.

It has no loose lid to clap in a storm.

Around its edges are shallow recesses where grit can settle. Across its upper surface, the light breaks on a roughness that was not polished away.

The divers leave.

For two nights the octopus does not approach it.

On the third, an arm reaches across the intervening floor. The tip touches an edge and withdraws. Later it returns. Suckers pass over the surface, finding purchase. One arm enters a chamber while the mantle remains outside.

A shrimp leaves through another opening.

The octopus follows it around the object and then goes back to the mast.

The power does not count this as failure.

***

There are other changes, none made everywhere at once.

Beyond one service lane, a strip of bare concrete receives a scattered arrangement of low shelters. They are not placed in a straight line. Some lie close enough together for an animal to pass between them without a long crossing; others stand apart. The arrangement leaves room for inspection vehicles and does not divert a dangerous current toward the next sector.

Some cavities collect too much sediment. Some are avoided. One shifts during a storm.

Those are altered.

The power could make the basin intricate in an instant. It does not. The pumps serve places beyond the water, and the water carries larvae, food, waste, heat. A change that shelters one body can increase the draw against another. Benevolence does not make these relations disappear.

At the intake trenches, attention becomes slower still.

There are measurements, trials, a quieter bearing, a different approach to a screen. A cleaning cycle is changed where it can be changed safely. Less living material reaches the rake.

The fish continue to gather where food passes.

***

The octopus first spends a day in the new shelter after a large fish follows it home.

It comes across the floor low and fast, one arm losing its grip on a dusted patch. The mantle contracts. For an instant the animal is clear of the concrete.

Then it is beneath the low roof.

The fish turns over the opening, waits, and leaves.

Inside, the octopus braces against two surfaces that do not move. Water passes the mantle. An arm explores the rear passage, then folds back beneath the body.

By evening it has brought a shell to the entrance.

For several days it uses both places. There are things worth searching near the mast: the cable loop, the sediment fan, the sockets. It does not abandon them because another cavity exists.

The repair is postponed.

Later, when the grout pocket is empty, it is checked before the work begins. The mast is supported. The compromised fitting is replaced and the gap filled.

No animal is pulled out by an arm. No barrier closes behind a body mistaken for debris.

When the octopus returns, it finds a smooth edge where the opening was.

It searches along the plate. It reaches around a bolt, passes over the grout, returns to the former entrance. For a time it remains there with its arms spread against the plinth.

The power has not made every consequence painless.

A route has ended. A known darkness is gone.

Before morning, the octopus goes back to the low shelter.

***

There is no sign on it naming its benefactor.

The animal is not required to understand why the roof stays firm, why the entrance remains open, or why a diver now looks into a cavity before sealing it.

It continues to eat other animals. It is sometimes hungry. Its shortened arm grows. On some nights it travels farther than before; on others it remains inside, holding a shell against the current.

The basin is still immense. Much of its floor is bare.

But the next maintenance specification includes inhabited spaces. They appear beside the requirements for load, flow, access and corrosion. Their inspection is funded. Their damage is something to be repaired.

Underwater, these provisions have no words.

They are a rough edge that a sucker can hold, a crossing interrupted by shelter, a chamber that need not be a failure of construction to remain.

At the entrance, the octopus loosens its grip on the shell.

An arm comes out.

There is somewhere nearby it has not yet searched.
