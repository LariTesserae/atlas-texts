The attention arrives without weight.

Nothing bends beneath it. The rain continues to strike the pool, and the transparent threads continue their slow passage along the roots. In the seam, the inhabitant draws its new sheet closer to the falling water.

At first the power does nothing.

It follows a mouthful of water through the feeding combs. It follows the nourishment into the dark sheets, and the strain of the ridge into the chambers that measure it. Where the body branches, its attention branches also. Some routes lead upward into tissue formed that morning. Others descend through old partitions, past sealed cords and places where the surrounding wood has grown around an absence.

It finds no single place from which the inhabitant governs itself.

There are arrangements that recur, differences held between chambers, responses that gather across several sheets before any one of them moves. There are interruptions after which feeding resumes at once, and others after which the body remains closed long beyond the passing of danger.

The power does not yet know what these differences are like from within.

It stays.

Through the next rain, and the dim interval after it, the ridge rises. A feeding cord is accepted. Another is stripped by the rind it has entered. The inhabitant withdraws the damaged tip, seals it, and tries a little higher.

Farther down the join, an old branch makes the same adjustment it has made for many seasons.

It braces against a load that no longer exists.

The other half of its paired array was destroyed when a reservoir broke through the mountainside. The water went elsewhere. The supporting tissues thickened around the opening. There is no cavity left, no exposed wound, nothing in the present shape of the slope to explain the branch’s behavior.

Yet it continues to reserve fluid for an answering pressure. At intervals it extends a little into solid growth, fails to receive the expected adjustment, and withdraws.

The power could provide the missing answer.

It could make the old pattern complete. The branch would open; the reserved fluid would return to circulation. Across the inhabitant, several small contractions would cease.

But the distant branch would still be gone.

Instead, the power attends to a newer part of the body, where two sheets have begun to compare what they feel. One lies against the rising ridge, the other against the old buttress. Between them is a short membrane carrying differences in pressure. When their readings disagree, the inhabitant sometimes loosens its grip before committing to an adjustment.

The power learns the structure of that hesitation.

Then it makes a small change near the injured array.

It does not erase the old response. Beside it, in a fold scarcely wider than a feeding filament, it establishes a chamber sensitive to the present pressure of the surrounding wood. It gives this chamber a route into the body’s existing means of comparison.

The chamber contains no instructions. It reports the place where it is.

For a long time, nothing happens.

Rain passes through the pool. One of the transparent colonists enters a feeding mouth and does not emerge. A row of seed cases opens somewhere above the cloud.

At the old branch, the remembered load and the measured load differ.

The inhabitant tightens.

The power waits.

Fluid passes between three sheets. A cord contracts and slowly relaxes. The branch extends toward the place where its counterpart should be, but this time stops before pressing into the wood.

An interval passes.

Then a little of the reserved fluid moves upward.

The old array remains. On the next cycle it calls for its accustomed adjustment again. Again the new chamber reports the pressure around it.

The power does not hasten the process.

By the third day, the branch has begun to use the discrepancy. It still retains the shape of the vanished seam, but no longer gives that shape sole authority over its growth. A feeding mouth opens in a sheet that has been thin for years.

The inhabitant incorporates the chamber’s lining into its own renewal. What was added is copied, unevenly at first, into adjacent tissue.

Only then does the power turn its attention to the young ridge.

Deep below, two knots are transferring weight between them. Each is responding correctly to the tissues nearest it. Together they are preparing a sudden movement at the join.

The inhabitant will feel the warning. It will close its mouths and flatten its lobes. Most of its body will survive.

One newly opened sheet will not.

The power follows the possible paths of a correction. Holding the seam still would divert the strain into the buttress. Strengthening the sheet would make it strip living rind from the ridge. Moving the water would dry a crowded network of roots farther down.

It searches longer.

There is a reservoir beneath the ridge whose enclosing tissue is already preparing to yield. A slight release there will not prevent the movement, but will spread it over a longer interval.

The power warms a small patch of membrane by less than sunlight would have warmed it.

The reservoir begins to empty.

Above, the inhabitant closes its mouths.

The seam moves slowly enough for the youngest sheet to draw clear. One lobe is scraped. Its damaged cells release their fluid, and the little folded nets spring from their beads to cover the wound.

The power leaves the repair to them.

Afterward, water enters the feeding combs again. The ridge stands higher. Far below, the reservoir’s membrane cools, and neighboring growth begins its ordinary work of reinforcement.

There is no sign by which the inhabitant can distinguish the intervention from a fortunate movement of the land.

The power does not make one.

It remains nearby through several more changes of drainage, learning where help can pass without becoming another burden. Much of what it witnesses it leaves alone. Sometimes it opens a little time between a warning and an injury. Sometimes it finds that the surrounding life has already done enough.

Within the old branch, the new chamber continues to grow.

By the next turning of the canopy, the inhabitant has made three more.
