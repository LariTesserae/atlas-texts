At first, the power does nothing that can be distinguished from sitting at the entrance.

This is not because it has found the place perfect. The smallest maker has difficulty rising. The heavy leaves are getting heavier. There are forms on the shelf that have been held so often their hollows have become translucent.

But it has arrived while someone is adjusting its finger.

It allows the adjustment.

For a long time its hand remains beside the maker’s hand, above the scored square. It could supply enough warmth to unfold the little green thing at once. Instead it notices that the leaf is opening unevenly. One edge advances; the other stays tucked beneath it. Inside the fold, something is being made which cannot yet be seen.

The maker withdraws first.

The power stays until the warmth stored in the paving begins to diminish. Then it withdraws too.

There is no sudden growth.

The following day, the folded edge has become slightly thicker.

***

The power could reach the lowest rooms without lifting any tiles.

It hears the small movements there, the pauses that correspond to pauses overhead, the occasional dragging sound of something being brought through a passage scarcely wider than itself. It does not look through the floors.

Once, beside a loose paving piece, it rests a fingertip against the exposed edge of a little vault.

The rubbing stops.

The power takes its finger away.

Several days later, a disk of pale material has been left on the paving piece. There is a hollow in its center too small for any of the hands that work above.

The power makes a fingertip small enough to fit.

It presses once, gently, and leaves the disk where it was.

When it next passes, the disk is gone.

***

Its first work in the garden is an ordinary stand.

The maker with the repaired elbow brings it a length of wood, two wedges, and a leaf so heavy that the maker must carry it against one shoulder. The leaf is still attached to its shrub. They have to work beneath it.

The power makes a beautiful support. It follows the underside exactly, distributing the weight across every ridge.

The maker examines it, then slides a shaving between the leaf and the wood.

There is nowhere for the shaving to go.

For a while they both remain crouched beneath the leaf.

Then the power lowers its support and cuts most of the upper surface away. What remains touches the leaf in three places. There is room between them for the jointed grazers, and room beyond them for the leaf to thicken.

The maker sets it in place.

A grazer, interrupted halfway along a pale feeding track, resumes its journey.

The offcuts are put beside the bench. One of them will eventually require a bed of its own.

***

After this, the power is given things to hold.

A branch while a lashing is loosened. A warm awl. The loose piece behind the smallest maker’s knee.

This last task takes most of an evening.

The smallest maker sits on the bench with its leg extended. Another maker removes the old binding, turn by turn. Beneath it, the joint has worn into a shape no new piece could simply replace. There are polished surfaces, shallow channels, a little hard ridge formed around an ancient break.

The power supports the loose piece without drawing it into alignment.

The smallest maker moves its foot.

The piece shifts.

The power follows.

Several times the new binding has to be undone. Once the smallest maker reaches down and pushes both their hands away. It sits with the knee open to the warm air, turning a length of copper-colored thread between its fingers.

Neither helper touches it again until it holds out the thread.

When the work is finished, the maker steps down onto the floor. The central piece of its foot clicks. It stands for a while, then bends the knee.

The power has left nothing hidden in the repair. The other makers can unbind it, alter it, mend it again.

A short length of unused thread remains on the bench.

The smallest maker takes this away.

***

There is work the power can do for which no one else has much strength left.

It discovers this at a fresh section of wall.

The bricks have been laid. The mortar has settled. Three makers stand with their palms against the new courses, while a fourth sits nearby with its hands cupped under its arms.

The power stands beside them.

At first it warms too much of the wall. A maker lifts one of its fingers away from a mortar line and places it lower. The power adjusts. Beneath its palm it can feel the separate bricks, the small differences in their firing, the pressure still held inside them.

It keeps its hand there.

Evening lengthens.

One maker leaves, then another. The seated one falls asleep with its head against its knees.

A six-legged animal approaches the new section, stops, and turns aside.

The power remains.

Much later, the animal returns. It puts its front feet against a brick and settles facing the masonry.

By morning there are seven.

The makers find the power still standing there. One touches the wall, then touches the power’s wrist. After that, when new courses are ready, someone comes to the entrance and waits until it looks up.

***

It does not wake all the forms on the shelf.

It takes them down when they are offered and holds them without secretly changing what the makers have made. Some are incomplete in ways it understands. Some have enclosed spaces whose purposes are not yet apparent, even to those who shaped them.

One small form grows warm in its hands and remains still.

The power returns it.

Later, it finds that an animal has climbed onto the shelf and laid a strip of shed root beside it. No maker removes the strip.

***

A resting place is eventually made for the power.

It is not a throne, nor a room behind the ceiling. It is a shallow recess beside the entrance, where the wall has been extended inward. The makers measure it by asking the power to sit, then building around the space it occupies.

They leave one side unfinished.

A vine is crossing that side, and its next turn is uncertain.

The power rests there when no one needs it. Creatures pass beneath its hands. Sometimes a maker sits beside it without bringing work.

Between their feet, the little green plant has opened its first leaf.

It is broader than anyone expected. Its shadow falls across two of the holes prepared for posts, so the posts will have to go elsewhere.

One morning the smallest maker arrives carrying a tool and a short length of wood. It lowers itself beside the plant with less difficulty than before.

The power reaches to help.

The maker puts the wood into its hand, keeps the tool, and begins to mark where it should be cut.
