The power does not answer the tapping at once.

It has heard mountains split. It knows the small preliminary sounds by which a loaded stone announces that it will fail, and the slower sounds by which a mountain settles onto its own broken foundations. But it does not know what this animal has asked.

It leaves room for the answer already being given.

Beneath the adult, the little crawler repeats its scrape. This time it lifts its shortened limb before making the last stroke.

The adult touches the shelf once.

You lift your boot away from the floor.

Neither animal moves.

After a while, you set it down beside the seat rather than beneath it. The resident draws one middle limb out of a groove and extends it through the space your foot had occupied. Its tip rests against the wall.

That may have been the request. There is no way to be certain yet.

The power remembers the exchange without deciding more than it knows.

---

There are several things it could do immediately.

It could still the air. The relief would be immense, and brief. Along both walls, the great fans would sag under their own weight. Animals held aloft by pressure would drop through the failing notes. In the root felt, the little currents that carry nourishment would dwindle.

It could replace every broken part with something unbreakable.

It watches a young crawler prise apart an old pale rim and eat the soft growth inside. It watches the adult remove a sound fragment from its shoulder because the layers beneath it have thickened and no longer fit.

It could feed everything.

Far below, a hunter opens its fringe, and the movement sends a warning through a bank of leaves. Grazers scatter. Their passing shakes pale dust onto root tips that brighten as it touches them.

The power keeps watching.

There is no lack of capacity in its stillness. It is trying to discover where help would go.

---

During the next faltering of the wind, it follows the lost note downward.

Below the last illuminated ledge, below the pocket where seven adults lie fitted together, a ceramic patch sits across two seams of different stone.

The patch is intact. So are its buried companions.

But the mountain has moved since they were placed.

Only a little. Less than the width of one of the resident’s teeth. The movement has taken centuries, and the devices have accommodated almost all of it. At this one junction, however, a force arriving from the lower shaft must cross an interval for which there is no longer a receiving surface.

Most of the time it crosses anyway.

Sometimes it returns.

Then the fans droop, and bodies along the walls take up the missing load.

The power follows the return through the ceramic, through the silver roots, through the resident’s outspread forelimbs. It feels the teeth fail. It feels the animal keep holding.

It also finds the pale rims.

They are not all the same. Some enclose food. Some brace roots. Some change the way a pressure reaches a recess. Near the failing junction there are nine, each extending a little farther from the stone than the one before it.

The oldest is empty.

The newest contains a tiny tongue of layered mineral, reaching toward the displaced seam.

The inhabitants have been working on the problem.

---

The power makes its first offering on bare stone, well away from the young.

It is a place no larger than a grain where the wall can be pressed without returning all the pressure at once.

The resident discovers it with a rear limb.

It withdraws.

For a long time nothing happens. Then one toothed fan touches the place, lightly, from several directions.

The power lets each pressure pass back slowly. It adds nothing.

The resident strikes it sharply.

The little patch yields, holds, releases.

The adult flattens against the roof. Its fringes close. Beneath its body, the young become motionless.

The power does not follow it.

By evening the resident has covered the place with a chip of stone.

The power leaves it covered.

---

Three days later, the chip is gone.

There are two adults at the doorway.

They examine the yielding place in turn. One presses while the other keeps a limb against the surrounding wall. They exchange a pattern too faint to disturb the dust.

Then the stranger strikes hard enough to fracture the stone beneath its own fan.

The power catches only what enters the small offered place. The rest travels into the mountain normally.

The animals wait through the delayed return.

They test it again.

By the time they leave, they have learned something about its limits. The power has learned something about the limits they need it to have.

---

It does not place a new support beneath the buried junction.

It offers the same yielding grain beside the unfinished mineral tongue.

For six changes of the bass note, no animal touches it.

Then a broad middle limb comes out of darkness and presses.

The answer returns slowly.

Another limb finds the far side.

Work resumes.

The inhabitants bring flakes, root strands, one curved sliver of ceramic. They remove two layers they had already laid. They quarrel over a third, pulling it back and forth until one carries it away.

The power holds the interval they have chosen to load.

When they release it, it releases.

When they leave, it does not continue their work.

The new tongue grows across the gap by thicknesses scarcely visible even to the animals that make it. Twice they dismantle a day’s addition. Once a hunter settles over the work and has to be driven away.

Eventually the wind falters.

Along the walls, adults lift themselves from their recesses.

The missing force enters the new layers. The tongue bends toward the yielding grain and stops just short of it.

A low, uneven tone passes through the shaft.

The fans remain open.

On the shelter roof, the resident has already extended its forelimbs toward the climb. It waits, one rear limb still fastened beneath the lip.

The tone steadies.

Slowly, it folds itself down again.

---

The power remains through many more failures of the wind.

It makes no promise that the mountain will never move again. The new junction will wear. Other seams will shift. The inhabitants will have purposes for the garden that no watcher, however patient, can discover in a single season.

At the places they have tested, a little reserve is available. They can cover it, build against it, or leave it alone.

Most leave it alone.

At the shelter, the doorway plant acquires a fifth leaf.

The injured crawler still has a shortened limb. It still carries its food onto the shelf, and the adult still takes some of it away. Its growing plates lift around the rust-red chip until, one afternoon, the chip falls out.

The little animal turns and examines it.

Then it carries it to a crack and presses it into place.

On the roof above, the adult is resting. Both forelimbs are folded beneath its edge. For once, neither is holding anything together.

The power lets the afternoon pass.
