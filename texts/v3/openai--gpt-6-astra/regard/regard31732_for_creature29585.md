The power’s attention arrives without adding anything for the animal to endure.

There is no brightness under the shelf, no new pressure in the water. The root does not tremble. The drop growing at its tip continues to grow.

For a while, the power attends to the margin that will not open.

It follows the contraction inward, through the saddle and the thin, branching body. It finds the effort of holding fast, the interrupted feeding, the small reserve being spent beneath the amber swellings. It does not need to discover a word for any of this. Nor does it give the animal words, or a larger mind with which to apprehend its situation.

Instead, it follows the vibration out.

Through clay, through gravel pressed into clay, through the buried remains of an older bank. Across water that carries the note without knowing its source. At the turbines, the power finds many things joined together: falling water, turning shafts, a bright settlement on a darkening hillside, a room where someone has set wet gloves beside a heater.

It does not put out the lights.

There are losses in the machinery that do no useful work. A bearing presses unevenly. A housing carries a shudder into its foundations. Two units, run together in their customary way, make the bank beat like a second, badly fitted machine.

These things can be otherwise.

During the next maintenance stoppage, the worn surfaces become plain to the person inspecting them. A measurement that has been taken once is taken again. The repair is a little more thorough than the schedule required. Elsewhere, someone examining the release records notices how much of the abruptness is habit rather than necessity.

There are discussions, calculations, a trial.

The power remains attentive through all of them. It has no preference for a miracle over a competent repair.

At the hollow, the change is first apparent in a busy afternoon that is not quite so busy beneath the skin.

Another unit starts. The animal tightens. A shallow contraction travels from the saddle toward the notch.

Then it ends.

The fringe remains closed for a while longer. A particle touches it and passes. Another settles against the translucent lip.

At last, three filaments separate from one another.

The river still rises. It rises more slowly now, with time for a snail to complete its turn. The pale stone acquires its bright collar, loses it, acquires it higher up. Beneath the shelf, shrimp move from one submerged leaf to another instead of being lifted together into the open pool.

This helps. It does not make the roof sound.

The power attends to that next.

A fracture runs behind the crooked shelf. In three nights of frost it will reach the root that holds the outer portion. The whole lavish hand’s breadth of green will then descend into the water, carrying the animal if it is still there.

The power could hold it forever. But the bank is long, and water is working through it everywhere. Roots need to thicken. The meadow needs to drain. A fixed piece would eventually become an obstruction around which the rest broke more violently.

Under the shelf, a little farther inward, there is a gravel rib left by an old bend of the river. It is damp with seepage but above the ordinary pulses. Between two stones lies a space the animal could enter, except that a clot of clay closes its mouth.

Water has already begun to loosen the clot.

The power lets a little more of the seepage take that route.

Over two days, grains fall away. A narrow opening appears. Its floor stays wet. Fine particles of leaf matter collect where the current loses strength at the entrance.

The animal finds it at night.

Its leading lip pauses at the change from clay to stone. The dark grains advance, stop, advance again. One margin remains attached outside while another explores the recess. It feeds there, then withdraws.

The power waits.

On the following night it enters far enough to bring the saddle under the stone. It does not know the place has been prepared for it. It knows the purchase beneath its body, the moisture, the small abundance caught along an edge.

When the shelf falls, the power steadies the loosened mass for the few moments it takes the last extended margin to follow.

Then the clay comes down.

The slap fills the hollow. Water surges over the pale stone. The liverwort turns once and lodges against the sedge.

Inside the recess, the animal has contracted around its amber row.

By morning it is feeding again.

The hollow is barer now. Light reaches a patch of wall that has never received it. The wren comes, examines the fallen greenery, and takes a midge from a root. Higher along the bank, another tuft of sedge is beginning to overhang a seam.

The power does not leave after the first successful night. It watches the quieter releases through rain and thaw. It attends to the other edges of the river, including those where a slower rise has changed what used to thrive. When a repair begins to wear, there is someone to notice.

Beneath the saddle, the largest swelling opens.

What comes out is almost transparent. It holds for a time in a fold of the parent, then puts a blunt lip against the stone. There is a film there, too fine to be seen in the sideways light.

The young animal begins to eat.

Beside it, another remains folded close. A third moves toward the entrance, where the water is passing gently enough that it can still turn back.
