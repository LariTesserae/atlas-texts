The power does not begin by lifting a plate.

Too much of the inhabitant is also the contact between a plate and what lies beneath it. To reveal it would be to draw it out into threads, and then to break the threads.

Instead, for one interval, the feed screw near the entrance does not turn.

Elsewhere the work continues. Ceramic advances through the rollers. An older ridge settles against its neighbour. The pool rises, carrying its printed banks and the pale borders of the numbers. The relay knocks.

Under the arch, the smallest passages close.

The power attends to their reopening.

It cannot tell whether the constriction is terror, or whether terror is a word that belongs at this scale. It does not need to settle this before keeping the next sheet from cutting through the fold.

Behind the mesh guard, the resistance setting changes. The machine stops pressing against the arch. Its next ribbon is taken up in an empty recess beyond the chamber, where it curls against bare ceramic.

Nothing enters the fold. No light is placed inside it.

The bead on its roof grows heavy and descends.

Near the entrance, the first drop has bent the shaving. The second will pull it farther. Water is gathering on the pipe, clear except for the green reflection of the inspection lamp.

A narrow piece of woven material comes to rest beneath the shaving.

It is not laid across the thread. It touches the underside of the ceramic fragment, taking its weight without changing the distance between its ends. One edge reaches the place where the old rain fell; the other curves under the new lowest point of the pipe.

The drop falls.

For a moment the weave darkens in a circle. Then the wetness lengthens along it.

The thread holds.

***

There are things the power could make which it does not make.

A basin without frost. A ceiling that rains everywhere. Ground that never folds or closes. An inexhaustible supply of whatever the pale tissue can consume.

It follows what would happen next.

The cold holds water in places where a warmer hollow would lose it. Some of the narrow contacts remain wet because they are narrow. The green leaves need intervals in which to open, but the salts arriving through their wick would accumulate if the water never changed its course. A rich new food could nourish something not yet present, something that would pass through these seams much faster than the inhabitant can retreat.

The power leaves the hollow cold.

But where the ridge presses against the pipe, it transfers the pipe’s weight to a bracket fixed above. The insulation rises by less than the thickness of a scale. Compression ceases. Moisture remains.

The collar does not immediately thicken.

Several cycles pass before any of its margin occupies the freed space.

The power has time.

***

Under the floor, the machines acquire another way to stop.

They still measure resistance. They also register the slow, uneven passage of water through a seam: the delay that distinguishes an occupied contact from an empty one.

Where that delay is present, the dies wait.

This is an imperfect distinction. Sometimes they wait for nothing but a damp mineral crust. Sometimes tissue is too dry and thin to register.

The power keeps attending. It does not mistake the new setting for the completion of its work.

Nor does it hold every occupied surface motionless. The pool continues to rise and fall. Loose plates settle. Water discovers openings. But the movements become slower where the pale passages cross them, and somewhere beside each narrowing seam another damp contact remains available.

Not every margin takes it.

Some extend into the offered contact and later withdraw. Some turn along its edge. At one junction two portions meet, lie against one another for a warming interval, and separate again.

The power does not join them.

***

Beyond the pool is a folded recess that has never received rain.

A little water begins reaching it by a route through the back of the existing filter tile. It carries no unfamiliar mixture: only the diluted residues already passing through the hollow. The porous material continues into the recess in a thin strip, sheltered from the ceiling plates.

For a long time, nothing happens there.

Then a pale edge appears beneath the last printed character on the membrane.

It crosses the unprinted space slowly. Twice it dwindles until only a clear mark remains. During the next thaw the mark clouds again.

Eventually it reaches the strip.

The recess is not a replacement for the hollow. No machinery begins clearing the old ground once the new ground is occupied. The serial numbers remain where they are, with their soft duplications. The wick keeps its green nap. The thick fold remains beneath the arch.

Farther along, the strip divides. One branch enters a cool pocket. One follows the warmth of a return pipe. Both have room beyond what can presently be reached.

***

At the next inspection, a person opens the port.

The green lamp shows the undersides of three pipes, the white growth on the bracket, the new support beneath the drainage line. A small addition has appeared on the building’s maintenance drawings.

There is a space between the floors now.

Its clearances are marked. Its water supply is accounted for. Instructions forbid flushing the basin, sterilising the coupling, or replacing the numbered membrane without preserving its damp underside.

The person reads, sets the lamp on a dry ledge rather than the basin, and closes the port.

Darkness returns without taking away any warmth the inhabitant has begun to depend on.

Under the arch, fluid moves between two cavities. Near the entrance, a cloudy border has descended into the weave beneath the shaving. The old rain patch is still supplied. The new one no longer hangs by a single connection.

The power remains attentive to both.

When the relay knocks again, the passages constrict.

They open in their own time.

Nothing is required of them afterward.
