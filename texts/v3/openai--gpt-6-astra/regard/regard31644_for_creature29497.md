The power does not bring light.

For a long time, it attends to what light would not explain: the difference between water moving past a body and water moving through it; the work of a foot that has held too long; the narrow interval between enough food and enough breath.

It finds the pale animal at the junction of two passages. Half the fingers extend into each. The left side has slipped from a smooth rim, and the opposite feet remain attached while the body slowly gathers itself.

The power waits through the movement.

It does not take this slowness for a request to be made swift. Nor does it assume that because the animal has lived with the damaged side for years, the damage costs it nothing.

Under the crooked margin, tissue is pulled tight whenever the sound feet lift. The animal has learned a way of moving that distributes the strain, but cannot remove it. Some surfaces have become difficult. One short route between the courts has fallen out of use.

The power attends to the notch.

There is no sudden unfolding of new limbs. Beneath the old scar, a little tissue begins to grow along a pattern the body used once before. Bloodless channels extend into it. Sensitive threads follow. The process uses neither the food stored for the capsules nor the substance of another part of the animal.

For several tides, the pale creature rubs its side against the floor more often than usual.

The first new foot attaches badly. It releases before bearing much weight. The second remains tucked beneath the margin. The third grips a grain of shell and carries it for nearly half a passage.

Eventually all three find stone.

The animal pauses.

Its body is receiving an unfamiliar arrangement of pressures. The opposite feet hold, release, hold again. The left side does not slide.

It goes on.

The power could clean the back, straighten the broken plate, remove the weight of tubes and sponge. It follows the living passages within that weight and leaves them alone. Water enters the sponge through openings too small for the animal’s fingers to distinguish. A worm draws a particle down its tube. Beneath the newest growth, the fractured plate is sound enough; its unevenness is not an injury still occurring.

The shell with the polished lip catches beneath an overhang.

The animal twists.

This, too, the power allows to happen.

Farther into the settlement, however, an old arch is settling into mud. Its lower supports have been hollowed by animals that now live elsewhere. Each tide presses grit into a fracture across its crown. A little more of the load passes to a thin upright on one side.

The pale animal uses the space beneath it when the new water runs strongly. Other bodies use it at other times. In its inner darkness, feeding threads occupy the places where a larger back does not quite touch the roof.

The power considers lifting the arch.

A lift would tear the sponge joining it to the wall. It would open the enclosed mud to a current strong enough to carry it into the next court. The inhabitants there would withdraw, then suffocate beneath what settled.

Instead, the power takes some of the weight.

Nothing rises. No sediment stirs.

The arch continues to feel heavy to the feet crossing it. Its surfaces remain rough, its openings narrow. But deep within the broken upright, the edges of the fracture no longer grind against one another.

The power holds it there.

This is not work that has to be finished before the next tide.

New tubes accumulate along the base. Shelled animals settle in the protected angle. A sponge spreads across a loose fragment and binds it to another. The power yields the weight slowly, as these additions become able to bear it, and takes it back whenever their young structures bend.

Below, the pale animal passes with its capsules sheltered between its feet.

No sense available to it reveals what is holding overhead. Its fingers find the usual mouths of tubes. Its flank registers the division of the current. The new feet attach along the left side, and the body crosses without the old sideways drag.

Later, a stronger pulse of water carries the young from beneath it.

The power attends to them also.

It does not gather them into a still, protected chamber where nothing can reach them and nothing arrives to eat. It follows the water farther than their parent can follow, through the crowded passages, into the places where settlement is possible. At one narrow entrance, a pocket of water has ceased to exchange with the channel. The power opens a way through the compacted grit, no wider than the hollow of a worm’s abandoned tube.

Cool water begins to pass.

There is food in it. There are hunters beyond it. There are clean edges on which a small body might hold.

The power remains attentive.

In the oldest quarter, the arch gradually becomes able to stand without help. The last of its weight is released on an otherwise unremarkable tide.

Beneath it, something pale lies folded around the foot of a wall. Its breathing margins lift and fall. On its back, a young worm extends its crown.

For a while, neither animal needs to move.
