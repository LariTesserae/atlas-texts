The power did not lift the tile.

It could have held every flake and grain in place while doing so. But the coolness entering from above would have passed through all the crowns together, closing surfaces that had never closed together before. The currents would have lost their routes. Scales ready to detach would have remained caught at their margins.

So it came to know the city through the heat leaving it.

For a long time, its attention made no difference that anything beneath the tile could have measured.

It followed warmth down through the bedrock and out through the pale fringe. It learned which passages carried air only during the strongest pulses. It discovered that the room filled to its ceiling was no longer a room in any useful sense, but had become a bank of mineral grains through which three collars fed. Opening it would have destroyed something that clearing away its contents would not reveal.

The buried slab knocked.

Throughout the city, dead strips loosened from living edges.

The power left the slab alone.

It had first noticed the crown bearing a roof, and for a while this seemed the clearest place to help. The flake pressed upon six blades. Beneath their polished patches, the lattice was thick and largely inactive. Growth persisted around the sides, where the fans could still open.

Yet removing the roof would alter the court’s cooling surface. Propping it at its present height would leave too little room for the next strong expansion. The crown’s roots had also hollowed one support; a rigid repair there would transfer the strain into the neighboring wall.

The power watched another hundred cycles.

Then, during the interval when the flake lifted from the crown by less than the thickness of a dust grain, it moved a sliver of fallen stone beneath one end.

The sliver had once been part of a lintel. Its lower surface was rounded. It could rock.

The roof settled onto it.

For the first time in many years, the six blades cooled without bearing weight.

They did not spring back. Their shapes had been made under pressure, and those shapes remained. On the next warming they opened through much the same small angle as before. One scraped the roof. Another shed a dead corner.

Along the third, a boundary that had added no material for a very long time began to add material again.

The power waited to see what its stone would do.

At the strongest pulse, the sliver rocked and slipped outward a little. The roof moved with it. The adjoining wall remained intact.

That was enough. The power did not straighten the blades.

Elsewhere, two courts had gone hungry.

They lay beyond the root that had narrowed a return passage. The obstruction was living along one side, and inside its hollow strand material traveled toward a crown in a different district. Cutting it would feed one growth by severing another.

There was room beneath it, though. Not much.

The power loosened a few grains from the passage floor. It let the next returning current carry them away.

After several cycles it loosened a few more.

A shallow groove formed under the root, no broader than an old erosion mark. Air began to pass through. The first dust it carried was too fine to settle in the hungry troughs. The next strong pulse brought heavier grains.

One of the courts retained them.

The other did not. Its entrance baffle had worn almost flat, and the current swept straight across its planting bed.

Here the power set down a single grain of grit.

It took three attempts to place it well. Each time, the power waited for the air to show what the placement meant. At last a little curl of slower current formed behind the grain. Red powder collected at the foot of the crown.

The living bands in its blades remained narrow. Several tips were already dead and would never become green again.

At the base of one surviving strip, an edge thickened.

There were things the power could not learn merely by being powerful.

It could trace every connection through the collars, but connection alone did not tell it where one life ended. It could perceive the changes that passed through the lattice when heat arrived, when a blade broke, when two collars slowly ceased resisting one another. It did not presume that these changes were thoughts. Neither did it require the city to produce a recognizable thought before taking care with it.

Its small alterations spread consequences through the lanes.

A trough that received more dust sent less onward. A better-fed crown opened farther and changed the cooling of the wall behind it. The stone beneath the roof began collecting a gray discoloration.

The power kept watching.

Beyond the tile, the larger building continued its own slow changes. Loads shifted. One day someone would notice the raised corner and want the floor made level.

The power attended to that danger too.

In time, the tile became the last surviving piece of a floor that was to be replaced. The work passed around it. A shallow protective frame took the weight of feet and furniture without resting on its edges. Openings in the frame allowed the warm air to leave.

The corner remained raised.

People who bent close could hear the ticking. Some stayed for a late knock and felt it through their fingertips on the surrounding floor. They were told not to pour water into the gap, not to clear out the dust, and not to press the tile down.

No one was promised a view of the whole city.

Under the frame, the pale fringe continued to appear and withdraw.

The power did not restore the builders’ plan. It did not return the mineral dust to the walls from which it had come. The sealed room remained sealed. The black pedestals remained without an explanation.

On the last pedestal, the young plant grew a fourth blade.

It emerged close beside the first, where the difference in temperature was greatest. For a time the two edges interfered with one another. Then a seam deepened, giving them room.

The power made no adjustment.

During the next strong pulse, the fourth blade opened by itself.
