The knot holds.

Under Eren’s foot, something knocks again. This time the sound continues: a succession of small failures passing beneath the court toward the brothers and their father.

The younger brother looks down.

“Lift,” Eren says.

They lift. The old man swears. Eren reaches for the wall to pull herself upright, and the wall moves away from her hand.

Then it stops.

Not everything stops. Water continues to gather between the paving stones. A swimmer pulls itself out of a joint and rests with its mouth open. Somewhere beyond the court a woman is calling for a missing food dish.

But the wall stands at an angle no wall could keep. Beneath it, a strip of wet powder hangs in the air.

Eren stares.

“Go,” says a voice beside her.

There is someone standing in the shallow water: a person in an unremarkable wrap, with bare feet and an expression of close attention. One hand rests against the leaning wall.

The brothers go.

Eren tries to follow. Her bad foot has settled into a soft place. When she pulls, the pain runs up the back of her leg and brings her down on one knee.

“May I help you?” the stranger asks.

“Yes,” she says, irritated by the question. “Under the arm. Not that one.”

The stranger helps her up correctly.

They cross the court together. Behind them the wall remains tilted, the powder suspended beneath it. Small creatures are coming out of the exposed cracks. The stranger waits while a clot of shell-colored grazers passes over the threshold.

“My basin,” Eren says.

“I can bring it.”

“It’s heavy.”

“Yes.”

***

Elsewhere, a carrying board catches between two doorway stones. Before anyone can put down their load, one of the stones shifts outward.

A child, looking for a wooden wheel, finds a dry step where there was no step before. She climbs it. The floor beneath her former hiding place subsides.

In a sleeping chamber whose occupants have heard nothing, every cup tips toward the door.

Those who cannot get out are reached. Those who cannot walk are carried. Nobody has to be unusually deserving. A man who has hidden his neighbor’s good cord is brought to safety with the cord still beneath his wrap.

The long pale hunters find ways through. So do the broad, jointed animals under the slabs. When a cistern drains, its swimmers enter a shallow runnel that follows the withdrawing water to another pool.

The power attending to the city is capable of holding all these things in its attention at once. It does not mistake the people’s skill at escaping for a wish to need that skill forever.

It also notices how much eats the new mineral growth after an upheaval, and how many households depend upon that abundance.

For the present, it holds open the ways.

***

Eren reaches the receiving court before the sledges.

The stranger brings her basin and sets it beside her. The unfinished handle is still unfinished.

By then people have begun to understand that something has happened beyond an unusually fortunate escape.

They gather at a distance. Someone asks whether the suspended buildings will fall when the stranger takes its hand away. Someone else points out that its hand is no longer there.

“They will settle when everyone is clear,” it says. “Slowly.”

“What are you?”

The answer takes some time. Not because it is evasive, but because the city has no word for what the stranger is, and several of its words for powerful beings include obligations the stranger does not intend to impose.

Eventually an old woman says, “So nobody owes you a thing.”

“No.”

“Not afterward, either.”

“Not afterward.”

This is repeated through the receiving court with more speed than accuracy. By the time it reaches the far end, some people believe the stranger has abolished debts.

That dispute lasts well into the evening.

***

Eren has taken off her shoe. Her ankle is swollen.

The stranger sits beside her, leaving room for the basin.

“I can mend that injury,” it says.

“Which one?”

“The one from today. And the older damage, if you want.”

She studies its face.

People have promised things about her foot before. Hot wraps. A bitter paste brought down from the open country. Pulling the toes until she shouted. For a while she had been ashamed of having failed to improve.

“What would you have to do?”

It explains. She makes it explain again, with less about the smallest parts of a body and more about what she will feel.

“Would I still know where it was?” she asks.

“Yes.”

“And afterward?”

“You would need to learn some movements again. It has been a long time.”

She puts her heel into its waiting hand.

The repair feels warm, then peculiar, then briefly like a deep itch. She is warned before each change. At one point she says stop, and it stops.

After a while she tells it to continue.

When it is finished, she sits without moving.

Across the court, the two brothers are arguing with their father about where to put him. Eren listens to the whole argument. Then she bends her foot.

Her face changes. She covers it with her hands.

The stranger remains beside her without requiring her to look grateful.

***

In the days that follow, there is work which needs no long deliberation.

The missing are found. Wounds are cleaned and healed with permission. Safe water is made available in every occupied district, including those whose inhabitants have nothing useful to trade. Food stores lost beneath the floor are replaced before the loss can become hunger.

The power goes to the dry chambers, too. It asks the people waiting there whether they wish to remain near their dead or return to their living. The answers differ.

It does not announce these answers in the squares.

Other work takes longer.

“Hold the whole floor still,” says a man whose house has moved three times.

“Not my seep,” says a woman beside him.

They begin talking over one another.

The stranger makes a shallow model of the basin in a deserted court. Water moves through passages beneath its transparent floor. A child presses a finger against one channel and sees a pool rise elsewhere.

It is not the whole truth of the underground system. The stranger marks what has been left out and explains why. Listeners kneel around the model. Cultivators ask to see their own walls. People who have never been invited to discuss a district’s water come because their dishes are filled from it.

There are ways to relieve the destructive pressure while preserving the seepage. There are places where the floor can be strengthened without drying the chambers below. Some changes will alter familiar damp margins; new ones can be made, but they will not be identical.

The people argue about those differences.

While they argue, they remain safe. Delay is not punished with another collapse.

The power provides stable sleeping ground without requiring anyone to win a claim to it. This changes the argument over the migration field more effectively than any speech. A few families still want to live there. Most had wanted somewhere they could stop being moved on from.

The field remains open.

***

Eren distrusts the model until she brings a basin of water and tests one of its slopes.

Afterward she becomes tiresome about scale.

“You’ve made this crossing wide enough for two people,” she says. “Not two people carrying something.”

The stranger changes it.

“And this one.”

It changes that one too.

She is not appointed to speak for the city. Sometimes she is wrong. Once a boy shows her a route she has called impassable, and she goes with him to look.

Her foot aches that evening, from use rather than damage. She has been told how to recognize the difference.

***

There are losses the power does not conceal beneath improvements.

In a recently flooded chamber, an artist kneels beside a drawing that has become a faint discoloration in the silt. The power can show her how it looked before the water entered. It asks whether she wants that.

She says not yet.

It leaves the offer open.

A woman asks for someone who died many years ago. The answer she receives is not a claim that her grief has taught her something valuable. They sit together a long time.

Elsewhere, people are already making jokes about the stranger. These are mostly about how difficult it is to return a borrowed pot once the lender knows precisely where it is.

***

The city does not become silent or unanimous. Children lose shoes. People refuse good advice. The wealthy discover that a dependable drip commands less gratitude when everyone has clean water, and some take this badly.

But when rain falls far beyond the roof, nobody has to die beneath the floor.

The animals still move first. People watch them, sometimes with baskets ready for the new growth, sometimes simply because the movement is worth seeing.

Eren finishes the second handle.

Her brother finds her filling the basin at a public cistern.

“You could have asked,” he says.

“I know.”

She has altered the opening twice. The first shape pressed painfully against the fingers when the basin was full. The second is better.

He takes the opposite end.

They carry it home, stopping once because Eren wants to show him something: along the base of a strengthened wall, a line of grazers is advancing toward fresh moisture.

Her brother waits while she looks.

There is time.
