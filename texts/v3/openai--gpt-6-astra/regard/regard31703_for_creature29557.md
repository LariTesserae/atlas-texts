The power does not arrive as warmth.

Warmth, introduced carelessly, would kill a great many things. The ice roofs would open. Water would run briefly in quantities the soil could not hold, carrying away the ledges beneath the grazers and the small lives established on them. Eggs would receive part of the sequence they had waited for, and hatch into the wrong conditions.

Nor does it arrive as light.

It attends first to what is already happening.

For a long time, its attention makes no difference that could be measured from a ridge. It follows water through the ground. It learns which salts are scarce and which become poisonous when plentiful. It accompanies a mouthful of grit through the chambers of an adult, then through the branching pouches of a young animal, then into a worm so small that its whole body occupies a space between two grains.

It does not mistake the slow passage for an obstruction.

There are processes here that need time in the way others need water. To hurry them would not be to help them finish. It would be to interrupt them.

***

Beneath one of the older grazers, a compartment has failed to close.

The injury is slight. A splinter of the animal’s own armor has entered a fold of tissue and remained there. Whenever the neighboring pads take weight, the fold pulls against it. The surrounding muscles contract. The valves narrow. A region that could still feed has almost ceased to do so.

The animal has been turning away from this part of itself for eleven years.

From above, the result is a gentle curve in its feeding route.

The power attends to the injury without pressing on it.

It discovers that the repeated contraction is not merely a mechanism. There is something it is like for the animal when the fold pulls tight. Nothing resembling a thought passes through the distributed chambers. There is no account of what has happened, no expectation that it will end. Nevertheless, the disturbance is received. Again and again, the body’s small world becomes something to withdraw from.

The power removes the splinter.

Not by drawing it through the wound. It lets the mineral loosen from its point outward, becoming grains no larger than those already passing beneath the belly.

The next contraction does not occur.

For several days the animal continues to favor its other side. Then a pad swells where it has not swollen for years. Moisture spreads into a shallow depression. A young grazer, sheltering farther back, detects the change and moves toward it.

The old animal’s route begins to straighten.

It does not know that anything has visited.

***

This becomes one kind of work.

A blocked pore opens. A parasite is separated from a nerve without being dropped alive into the frost. Tissue that has sealed around an infection is allowed, at last, to heal.

The power learns the inhabitants individually.

One tolerates contact along almost its whole western edge but withdraws from even familiar chemical traces at the front. Another has crossed the same patch of useless gravel for a century because a mineral there resembles something it needs. A third is not resting beneath its closed plates. It is struggling continuously against a pressure that its remaining muscles cannot overcome.

The answers are not all the same.

Sometimes a grain must be moved.

Sometimes a body needs a capacity it has never possessed.

Sometimes nothing should be disturbed.

The power’s patience does not consist in making the injured wait while it admires the intricacy of their injuries. It can learn slowly and relieve quickly. Where it understands enough, it acts.

***

On a ridge, a pale hunter has damaged its mouth.

It approaches the sheltered margins of adults but cannot make a seal. Each attempt reopens the wound. Its feet have begun to tremble when it leans forward.

The power does not turn away from it.

Nor does it carry a young grazer out from shelter.

For now, it mends the mouth and supplies what the hunter’s failing body lacks. This is an immediate kindness, not a complete answer. The power knows the difference.

It remains interested in the answer.

Over the following seasons it studies the living films beneath the gravel, the cultures exchanged between adults, the compounds that make the hunter able to live only by taking another animal apart. There may be other arrangements. It does not assume that the first arrangement it finds is good merely because it is old.

But the hunter will not be starved while that work proceeds.

***

The first winter under its attention is still a winter.

Frost forms along the plates. Valves close, and much later other valves open. The upland retains its enormous distances and its several simultaneous weathers.

Beneath the gravel, however, a few passages remain wet that would otherwise have frozen solid. They are narrow, interrupted passages, fitted to the roots and chambers already there. No flood comes out of them. No sudden abundance draws a thousand animals toward a hollow that can sustain only ten.

A juvenile on its first night alone loses the working moisture beneath one edge, then another.

At the center, the freezing stops.

The animal has no means of distinguishing this from favorable ground. It settles over the remaining dampness. By morning it has recovered enough to move toward an overhang a few inches away.

The power does not require the distinction.

***

There are errors.

A seam kept moist supports a film that spreads farther than expected. A repaired adult resumes feeding across ground where eggs have accumulated during its long stillness.

The power notices. It changes what it is doing. Its care includes the consequences of its care, including the small consequences, including those that take decades to become visible.

It does not depart when the valley looks peaceful.

The valley had looked peaceful before.

***

Much later, an old grazer stops beside a grass cushion.

Its plates are thick, its underside a patchwork of living chambers and dry spaces. Beneath one flank shelter seven young, two kinds of worm, and a jointed animal whose lineage has never lived anywhere else.

The grazer has reached the shallow step in the bedrock that would once have turned it aside for the rest of its life.

Its forward tissue extends.

Touches.

Withdraws.

There is a little gravel against the step now. Enough for purchase; not enough to bury the plants rooted in the crack.

After several days, the first pads find their way onto it.

The whole inhabited body begins to cross.
