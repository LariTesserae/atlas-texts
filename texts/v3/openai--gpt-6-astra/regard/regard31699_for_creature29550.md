The first thing the power does is refrain from making light.

It can perceive the country without it. More importantly, the country has not asked. The birds are asleep around their eggs; the herds are feeding; a child is judging with one finger whether a creature in a boot seam has become caught. None of these activities would be improved by a sunrise arriving without warning.

For a long while, it attends.

It follows a drop from the underside of a rail to the water beneath, and then follows everything that moves because the drop has fallen. It learns why the little fork has three tines at one station and only two at another. It accompanies an old worker across a terrace without shortening the way, because the worker is counting and the count will be used by someone else.

At the first occupied pump house it touches the handrail twice.

Inside, a woman rests her spanner against the iron.

The power could establish that they are at the same building. Instead it waits for her to establish it too.

“Come around,” she says at last. “The door catches.”

It comes around.

***

There are several things the woman would like done. None is the stopping of the engines.

A drinking tank tastes of its gasket. Two boards on the eastern crossing have begun to disagree under a stretcher. Her relief has been overdue for some time, although this sometimes means only that the way from his house has lengthened.

She asks whether the visitor can hold a pipe.

It can.

“Not there. There are animals.”

It moves its hand.

For the next hour it holds the pipe at the angle she requires. While she works, she describes a knocking that has recently begun behind the wall. She does not ask for an explanation of the ground or for an account of what the pumps draw up. She wants to know whether the knocking means she must shut down before her relief arrives.

The power attends to the wall.

Behind it, a bracket has loosened. Behind the bracket, three soft bodies have occupied the widening space. The repeated impact is beginning to injure one of them.

“There are animals in it,” it says.

The woman puts down her spanner and reaches for the smaller fork.

They make the repair together.

***

The overdue worker is found sitting on a junction box.

He is not lost. He knows which cable leads to the station, and he has followed it without letting go. But his left boot has split across the sole. One foot is traveling the distance of the boot and the other has begun traveling barefoot. Walking has become an effort he cannot explain without sounding foolish.

He has been trying to decide whether to take off the good boot.

The power sits beside him.

It does not carry him away. His hand is still on the cable, and he has tightened his grip at its approach.

After a while he lets it examine the sole.

The repair takes very little of what the power possesses. Getting the repair right takes longer. A boot is not simply a covering here. There are journeys worked into it, and the worn places must not be restored indiscriminately.

When it is finished, the worker stands, shifts his weight, and stands again.

“That’s better,” he says.

At the station, the woman runs her thumb over the repaired split. Before going home she asks whether the visitor will return tomorrow.

***

It returns.

In time, people bring it problems that have outlasted their tools.

Some it repairs. Some it leaves alone after being told more.

The ladders retain their extra length. The columns of measurements remain unreconciled. Nothing is done to the child’s tray.

The benches receive particular attention.

At one of them, a brother and sister explain that meeting halfway has lately required both of them to leave before finishing work. They have begun meeting less often. Neither wants to move house.

The power asks to accompany each of them home.

It makes both journeys, then makes them carrying a meal, then carrying a tired person’s weight. For several days the bench is unoccupied while the siblings wait to hear what can be done.

The answer is an iron strip beneath the seat and a small alteration to the approach on either side.

Thereafter the journey takes no longer than it did on the day of the repair, unless either traveler chooses a longer way.

The sister tests it first. She brings a tin of food. Her brother arrives carrying the tools he has lately been leaving behind.

They sit together for so long that the animals beneath the bench come out to investigate the meal.

***

The outer engines require more patience.

The power goes beneath the foundations. It offers each fingertip separately to the things eating there.

For a considerable interval, the eating stops.

What follows cannot be entered in a maintenance ledger. But afterward the power asks the crews for their oldest records, including the ones considered mistaken. It listens to the attendant who says a basin has become too crowded. It listens to the woman who has begun carrying longer tools.

It does not stop a stroke halfway through.

There are bodies held open by that movement, channels kept habitable, hollows whose warmth depends on its completion. There are also foundations thinning where no human arm can reach.

For several strokes it bears part of the load.

While it does so, new supports are fitted. These have wide passages beneath them. Their surfaces can be eaten without bringing the engine down, and the people who will maintain them can reach the joints from above.

The work takes months. Crews come home late, but they come home less often with the stillness that follows a narrow escape.

The engines continue.

***

Not everyone wishes to meet the visitor. It leaves them alone.

Some want things it cannot grant without taking too much from someone else. It says so. Some are angry with it. At the stations where it has been told not to enter, it leaves the handrail untouched.

It is welcome in many houses chiefly because it can take a turn holding an egg.

One evening, in the room above a pump, the old channel animal leaves its place beside the door and settles across the power’s feet.

The animal has been mistaken before. The power remains still while the broad body makes its comparison.

“Not that one,” someone says gently from the table.

But the animal stays. It is tired, and these feet are warm.

The expected person is still coming. There is a loose bolt on the way, and farther on a place where the cable sinks beyond comfortable reach. The power has already visited both. There is nothing more it needs to do to that journey tonight.

It waits with the household.

When the knees at last lift from its ankles, it hears the hand on the ladder below. Someone moves a supper tin nearer the warm pipe. The animal resumes its station, touching both jambs.

The power moves its feet out of the way.
