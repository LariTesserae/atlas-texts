The old man turns her hand over.

On the hard patch at the back he presses three times, slowly. Then he closes his fingers around her wrist.

*It took the place where I lived.*

She looks across the depression. The far wall is crowded with people. Behind them, partly obscured by a rising terrace, are the roofs of seven houses.

She asks how long.

His grip tightens, then loosens. He does not know. He had been a child. What he remembers is being carried, and an adult stopping to untangle a cord, and the cord coming free without the person at its other end.

She passes him her basket.

Then she climbs high enough to be seen and makes the shape for leaving.

***

The power that has noticed the basin is not above it.

Above is a direction within the basin, occupied by plates and ribs and the six-winged animals with their metallic cries. The power is no more there than it is beneath the unfinished roots.

It has been attending to the woman.

At first, to the care with which she released the animal from her wrist. Then to the wrist itself, and the dust pressed into it, and the fact that care has a cost here which the animal will never understand.

Its attention has widened.

It knows the workings of the depression before the woman does. Far below the exposed tissue, sealed vessels have joined that ordinarily remain separate. Pressure is passing between root-crowns. The slow releases she hears are partitions failing.

The new passage will not open upward.

It will travel sideways beneath the settlement, unfastening the living layers on which the houses stand.

There is time to prevent this.

The power does not wait to see whether the people will escape.

***

Under the woman’s feet, the next compression begins and stops.

Not fades. Stops.

Her listening stick kicks against her front plates. Around the depression the surface beetles lift their bodies on straightened legs.

For the first time in her life, she feels a great movement withheld.

The rest of the basin continues. A thorn screams. A browser tears loose a hank of fiber. Somewhere behind her, two people are arguing about whether the warning applies to the upper houses.

She makes the leaving-shape again, angrily.

Then something touches the supporting stem beneath her hand.

Two deliberate pulls.

She snatches her hand away.

The stem has no cord attached to it.

Below, the naked root tissue remains under tension. She can see a pale tube standing out along the crease, as rigid as a tool handle.

She puts her hand back.

Two pulls.

*I am holding on.*

It is a child’s answer. It is also what a climber gives when dust has hidden them from the person above.

The woman looks down into the depression.

With one finger, very carefully, she taps the shape for *where?*

The answer comes through every part of her palm.

Not words. A direction: down, then across, following the buried line of strain. Her fingers close involuntarily. She has a brief, distinct impression of seven anchor systems resting on something that is coming apart.

She pulls her hand away again.

This time she does not mistake the sensation for something she already knows.

***

A wise power can know how a house will fall without knowing which object inside it somebody will return for.

In the nearest shelter, there is a sleeping hood made by a person who is dead. In another, a child has hidden three forbidden animals inside a pith block. One household has stored its food below the floor, where it cannot be removed without loosening a brace.

These things are available to the power’s attention. Their importance is not written in their weight or composition.

It watches the evacuation become difficult.

Then, beside the woman’s hand, a narrow strip of dead bark rises from the leaf litter. It settles across the moving seam.

She tests it with her knife.

Dead bark. Ordinary, except that it has crossed an open space without being carried.

She puts one foot on it. The bark does not bend.

Beyond it, another strip settles. Then another. They make a route to the upper houses, crossing the ground she has just forbidden anyone to use.

She does not step forward.

Instead she presses her palm flat.

*Hold.*

The strips stop moving.

She fetches a basket of heavy wedges and pushes it onto the first span. Then she calls two other opening-readers over and makes them look.

One thinks she has found an old bridge exposed by the growth. The other thinks she has become dangerously confused.

Under their hands, the bark gives two deliberate pulls.

They stop arguing about that.

***

The power holds the settlement for as long as it takes.

This includes the time required to retrieve the sleeping hood.

It includes an argument about the food beneath the floor, resolved when the brace lifts without the house shifting and remains lifted while the baskets are passed out.

It includes the forbidden animals. The child confesses to two of them. The third emerges on its own, bites the person carrying the pith block, and is brought along inside a cooking sleeve.

The old man refuses to leave until he can see that the woman is coming.

She refuses to leave until the last occupied house has been checked.

Neither refusal is necessary. The power accommodates both.

Across the depression, animals have begun to use the bark route. Grazers flow over its edges. A stilt-browser approaches, tests the first span with one narrow foot, and withdraws.

The power gives it a second route, wider and lower.

It does not bring every creature out. Some live within the layers that need to separate. For these it makes chambers where pressure can diminish by degrees, and small passages following the scents by which they find their food.

What would have been a rupture becomes a slow uncoupling.

There is still noise. There is still dust. The people, watching from a mature crown, see their former ground open into long hanging folds. But the folds settle against one another instead of falling, and the stored force goes out through a thousand harmless movements.

A leaf unrolls.

A sealed pod lengthens.

Warm air rises through newly opened pores.

By the time the last partition gives way, the event can scarcely be distinguished from growth.

The woman stands with her stick between her teeth until her brother takes her elbow.

Only then does she discover that her hand is opening in three small jerks.

He offers her the waxy rind with magnificent courtesy.

She makes an insulting shape badly enough that he laughs.

***

Later, she returns.

Not alone. She brings the old man, the two opening-readers, a maker of listening sticks, and the person whose family lost the cutting tooth on the bridge.

That person has insisted.

At the edge of the altered ground, the woman lays down a strip of bark. Upon it she has marked the sequence as she understands it: pressure, houses, warning, restraint, passage.

She leaves gaps where she does not understand.

The power answers through the bark.

Learning to converse takes longer than the rescue.

For several visits, the people mistake an offer for a prediction. Once, the power mistakes a household’s public assent for the agreement of everyone who lives there. The woman notices the silent person at the back and calls them forward.

After that, the power asks differently.

It is able to learn this without making anyone suffer for the lesson.

Requests arrive.

Some are urgent: a damaged limb with splinters sealed deep inside it; a child whose vessels cannot retain enough oil; a settlement cut off by three simultaneous growth spasms.

The power helps.

Other requests require discussion. A clear route through a feeding district would make travel easier and send the great herds elsewhere. Elsewhere contains other houses. A proposal to silence the pollen-thorns comes from someone who has never harvested beneath them.

There are meetings. They are not solemn for long.

People interrupt. Someone brings poor food and is criticized. The old man falls asleep during an explanation and wakes in time to object to its conclusion.

The power remains attentive.

***

The woman does not become its voice.

When asked what it wants, she directs people to the place where it answers.

When asked whether a route is safe, she still takes out her listening stick.

But her work changes. She no longer has to stand at the edge of an opening and wonder whether warning people is the only help available. Now there is a way to ask for something to be held while those in danger find their footing.

The first answering strip stays where it was placed. Others are made where people request them. They carry no vibration of their own, but an open palm laid against one can be felt.

Children are taught the difference between these and ordinary path markers.

They are also taught that a frightened person need not make the proper signs.

***

At home, the woman cleans the compartments of her cabinet.

Her house is facing the wrong direction again. There will be work to do about that, and her brother has already said so.

The animal beneath her platform is investigating a food fastening. She pushes it away with her foot.

Then she brings out the eleven pieces of light-plate.

She has a guest, though there is nothing sitting opposite her.

For a while she considers the almost clear piece. Visitors usually admire that one first.

Instead she selects the piece with the branching red flaw and holds it against the rooflight.

The flaw divides, rejoins, and divides again. At one angle it resembles a figure carrying a basket. At another it resembles nothing but itself.

She turns it slowly.

There is no danger to report. She needs nothing held in place.

She is showing it something she likes.
