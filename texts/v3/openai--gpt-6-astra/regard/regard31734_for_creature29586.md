The power notices first that your finger is too heavy.

Not by much. The animal has braced its limb against the hinge, and the pressure you mean as contact is beginning to bend the smallest of its three tips.

It does not move your hand for you. A slight coolness passes through the pad of your finger. You feel, with unusual precision, the difference between resting against something and resting upon it.

You lift a little.

The animal’s other two tips unfold.

That is the first thing the power does.

***

Below the bench, the shells shift again.

There is no large body underneath them. Water has entered the spaces between the oldest layers, lifting grains that have held certain pieces apart for longer than the tree has stood. A shell tilts. Its neighbor settles into the space it leaves. Farther down, where shell has become stone, a thin partition breaks.

The animal feels this through its feet.

It leaves your finger and descends from the bench so quickly that its rear limbs lose their purchase. For an instant it hangs by the front pairs. Then it reaches the floor, takes its dryness with it, and thrusts three limbs into the heap.

The shifting stops.

The power could hold every fragment where it is. It could draw the water away, harden the room, return the shells to the condition in which they first entered it.

Instead it attends to what the animal is doing.

One limb steadies a broken valve. Another searches beneath it. The third repeatedly touches a ridge that is scarcely a ridge anymore.

The animal is not trying to prevent all movement.

It pulls one piece free and lets the others fall.

A small cloud of white sediment rises outside its dry patch.

The rescued piece is unimpressive: a chalky angle with a dark seam along one side. The creature brings it onto the bench. It touches the seam, touches the shell beside your hand, then touches the seam again.

Something in its amber tissues contracts and does not quite release.

The power waits through several repetitions.

It learns that the two shapes ought to answer one another.

One no longer does.

***

For a power capable of reaching into stone, the missing shape would be easy to recover.

Knowing what its recovery would mean takes longer.

It watches the creature make a plate from the damaged piece. The plate breaks in its mouth. It watches another plate form, thicker this time, and fail to make the expected click.

It follows the tremor into the animal’s body, but goes no farther than the tissues already open to the world. Beneath the polished oval, the folded amber organ remains covered.

The power does not lift that covering.

Near dusk, the creature carries both shells under the rain roof.

Another of its kind is waiting there.

Their domes rise and meet. Their feet tighten against the stone. When they part, the second animal goes to the room and finds a third shell, one buried halfway down the heap.

This one does answer.

Not perfectly. The first creature tries it twice, then brings the three pieces together on the bench.

The power begins to understand the work well enough to offer something.

***

Beside the shells, a little of the pale sediment gathers.

It gathers slowly, in full view of the animal. No limbs emerge from it. It acquires no eyes. It becomes a curved piece of porous stone, no larger than the damaged fragment.

Along its edge is a ridge.

The ridge resembles the one that has worn away, but stops short of completion.

The animal flattens.

The new piece immediately ceases forming.

For a long time nothing happens. Water rises another finger’s breadth outside the room. One of the spindle-shaped passengers comes out of the creature’s back and spreads its hairs.

Eventually the animal touches the new stone.

The unfinished ridge advances beneath its tip by less than the thickness of a leaf.

The limb withdraws.

The ridge stops.

The animal approaches again.

This time it holds on.

***

What follows takes most of the night.

The power has to learn how to be corrected by a creature whose smallest gestures it could easily mistake for hesitation, fatigue, or the adjustment of a foot.

Once it makes the ridge too high. The animal presses beside it repeatedly. Nothing happens. The creature turns away.

The power lowers the ridge to its previous height and waits.

When the animal returns, the same pressure makes a shallow depression.

After that, they work more steadily.

Your hand grows cold on the bench. You draw it into your lap. The creature notices the movement but does not stop.

The power does not keep you awake. For several hours, you sleep beside work you cannot understand.

By morning there are two impressions in the new stone. One follows the surviving fragment. The other carries the recovered curve.

Between them, at the animal’s insistence, is a deep groove.

It passes its tips across that groove each time it moves from one impression to the other.

The old shell and the new work are not the same thing. They have a way of saying so now.

***

Under the rain roof, the new piece is examined by four animals.

One will not touch it.

Another makes an amber plate from it, holds the plate in its mouth, and remains still until the roof has nearly finished dripping.

The smallest explores the groove.

When their domes rise, the power stays outside them.

It has made something they can bring into their meeting. Whether they do so, and what becomes of it there, belongs to them.

Later, the animal that refused the piece carries it to the far end of the bench.

It does not throw it away. It leaves it apart from the shells.

The power lets it remain there.

***

There are other things it could do.

The hollow is full of bodies living by opening other bodies. There are mouths working even in the folds of the animal that has spent the night beside you. The water’s slow movement brings food and sometimes buries it. The roof shelters creatures that eat one another when they meet.

The power attends to all of them.

It does not make the shell animals open willingly, or take hunger from the pale ones and leave their bodies without a way to live. It does not quiet the deep movement merely because everything above it must stop and brace.

For now, it learns the pulses.

It follows them beneath the bank, through warm chambers and constrictions, into places where the black grains turn slowly in the dark. It learns how the rain roof stores a storm, and which of its inhabited supports is beginning to weaken. Where an old internal seam is separating, it holds the two sides together without closing the burrows that cross it.

Above, the roof bends toward the tree.

The animals beneath it continue climbing.

***

Before you leave, the creature brings the first shell back to your hand.

The power could give you a sudden understanding. It could fill your mind with the use of every hinge, the history of the room, the meaning of the limb opening and closing.

Instead, when your finger meets the creature’s tip, you feel a faint vibration.

Three short movements. A pause. One longer movement.

You attempt them against the shell.

The animal shifts your finger to a different place.

You try again.

This time its limb remains beside yours.

The power listens closely. It has not yet learned what the pattern means either.

When you rise, the creature keeps the shell. It carries it down to the square hollow and fits it into a space among the recent pieces, with the hinge facing upward.

On the bench, well apart from the heap, the unfinished stone waits.

The power leaves no command inside it. Only a small capacity: to soften at a certain patient touch, to hold a shape, to stop changing when the touch withdraws.

Then it settles its attention more lightly over the hollow.

By midday the water has gone down. A gray animal crosses the room’s submerged threshold and finds it still too narrow. Beneath a leaf, a young pale creature moves a picker away from its back.

And on the bench, three little tips begin to examine what else the new stone can do.
