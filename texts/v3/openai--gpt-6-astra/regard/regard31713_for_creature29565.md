The power’s first act was to listen without making the place quiet.

This took some care. It would have been easy to lay a silence over the chambers and attend to the small brown sleeper behind the slab. But the noise was useful to her. Even in sleep she was sorting it: the ordinary landing of a pigeon, the scrape of a body too heavy for a pigeon, the brief cessation of calls along a ledge. Silence would have woken her.

So the power listened through the noise.

It followed her routes without travelling them. It found the chimney inside the wall, the shelf from which she had to retreat backward, the places where her claws had polished points of limestone too slightly for any eye to notice. It understood that the feather bed was not a poor substitute for something softer and larger. Its confinement was part of its comfort. The turn in the entrance was worth more than a view.

It did not widen the crack.

Nor did it bring water into the old channels. The emptiness of a reservoir might ask to be filled; this was not a reservoir. Under the apparent dryness, roots were taking what they could use from seams narrower than a hair. Farther down, water moved slowly through darkness. The power followed it until it understood which little stores sustained the fig trees, which had no connection with them, and how long a change on the plateau might take to arrive below.

For a while, nothing happened that anyone could have seen.

The marten slept. A beetle struggled over a feather and went around it. Above the slab, a pigeon turned an egg.

The power had not come upon a ruined place. It had come upon an inhabited one.

That did not make every hurt within it good.

There were hungry animals here. There were nestlings that would fall, and parasites beneath feathers, and old injuries that a change of weather could make troublesome. The power did not turn these things into a pleasing account of balance. It attended to them as closely as to the broad courts and the roots. But it would not begin by making one resident’s safety out of another resident’s starvation, or by changing the marten so thoroughly that the animal who woke would no longer be the one who had fallen asleep.

Near evening, it found something it could do.

Beyond the rim, in a strip of scrub through which the marten sometimes travelled, a loop of wire had been fastened beneath a root. It had stood there through several nights. Dust had settled on it. There was no catch in it yet.

The power broke the wire at the fastening and drew it out of the path. It gathered the sharp pieces, too.

This was a small act, but it did not despise small acts.

Then it looked farther.

On the plateau, the limestone continued beneath shallow soil. Someone, someday, might measure its thickness and calculate how much could be taken away. Someone might mistake the long holes for convenient places to put what was no longer wanted elsewhere. A poison spread well beyond the rim could return in the stomachs of insects, and then in bats, and then arrive as another kind of abundance on the chamber floors.

The power kept these approaches within its attention.

It would not need to frighten everyone away. There could be a person at the rim who sat still until the first bats emerged. There could be someone who learned the movement of the hidden water, or counted the swifts, or loved the place without knowing what to call its plants. Those people could come. Their care might become part of its protection.

But an emptied drum would meet an interruption. A plan to cut through the inhabited roofs would encounter knowledge it had omitted. Where warning and understanding were enough, the power would use them. Where they were not, it would stand in the way.

Below, the marten woke.

She stretched, groomed herself, and came to the entrance. Nothing unfamiliar waited on the ledge. No fragrance clung to the stone. The pigeons were where they should be, making their usual careless sounds.

She went out.

Later, above the caverns, she passed beneath a root. Her back brushed its underside. She paused to smell a beetle in the litter, decided against pursuing it, and continued into the scrub.

She did not know that anything had been done for her.

The power watched her go without asking her to turn.
