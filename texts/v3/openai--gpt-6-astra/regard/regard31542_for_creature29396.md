The power notices the hand beneath the smaller hand.

It had been attending to the mountain: to the pressure that made new stone, to the water entering a seam and returning with something dissolved in it that had not existed the day before. These were worth attending to. So were the vanes beneath the ferns, whose brief erectness was neither feeding nor breathing, though it served a little of both purposes.

Then the small hand closes on nothing, and the other moves beneath it.

The power stays.

***

For a time it does nothing that could be distinguished from weather.

It learns where the creature puts its feet. It learns that the blossoms left untouched are not poisonous, nor empty. They are the ones in which another flower is already beginning, deep within the first. It learns that the creature can tell this with the side of its finger.

It watches the dark object being taken from the crevice.

There are things it could discover by looking through the object, or backward along its history. It does neither. For the present, it is enough to see how carefully the creature supports it when changing its grip.

Benevolence does not make every closed thing an invitation.

The power watches the ring lift the creature. It sees the joints come apart to their proper distances, the pressure leave the crowded organs, the old skin loosen.

It sees the landing.

That night, while the two patches cling beneath the roof, the power attends to weight.

***

The next lifting begins as usual.

Grit loosens. Water arches. The creature, stretched across the circumference, catches a flat seed and puts it beneath its jaw.

Nothing else changes until the end.

Then weight returns by degrees.

The water settles first into a trembling, flattened heap. Pebbles sink through it. The creature’s free hand comes down upon the stone without striking it.

It remains in position for a long while.

It presses its wrist against the cleared patch. It withdraws the wrist, examines it, and presses again.

The following afternoon it does not enter the ring.

The power has expected neither gratitude nor immediate trust. It waits through this refusal, which is useful and belongs to the creature.

On the fourth day the creature brings a gravel flake to the circle. It puts the flake down and retreats.

On the sixth it rises.

The descent lasts long enough for its feet to find the floor.

It stays standing afterward, arms still spread, with water running from the narrow divisions of its hands.

***

There is a limit to what can be changed in one place without changing another.

The mineral paste feeds the flowers. The flowers shelter things smaller than the creature’s mouth can distinguish. The sharp new stone is not a mistake in an otherwise finished world. Nor is the heat, though there is more of it than either creature can safely bear.

The power does not cool the mountain.

It adjusts the shelter.

Along one edge, the roof begins to extend. Slowly: less than the width of a finger in a day. The new material is as thin and dull as the old. Rain comes through it. Sunlight stops.

The creature notices before the extension casts a useful shadow.

It climbs to the edge and feels along the underside. It presses with its head. It pulls once, hard enough to stretch a shoulder pearl-white.

The roof holds.

In time its shadow reaches a little farther toward the channel. There is a place to pause there now, halfway between food and shelter.

The tuft of grass keeps its sunlight. The fern crack remains as it was.

***

The small one is not a smaller copy.

It grips too soon and lets go too late. When carried, it fastens one foot to the larger creature’s throat and the other to its own wrist. It tastes the roof for long intervals. Sometimes all its ribs close at once and it must be turned onto its side until they open again.

The larger creature learns.

Once, exhausted, it sleeps through the small one’s descent along a shelter leg.

The small one reaches the floor and proceeds toward a bright blade of gravel. It has not yet learned to test the paste.

The power could lift it back.

Instead, a drop gathers at the end of the roof. It swells, detaches, and lands on the larger creature’s exposed palm.

The hand closes.

The eyes open.

By the time the small one reaches the gravel, there are fingers beside its foot.

The larger creature turns the blade flat. The small one turns it upright again.

They remain there for some time.

***

The power examines the stair.

Below its lowest tread is air, then cloud, then a distance no living grip could survive. Beyond that distance, the mountain’s old scar fills slowly with rain.

It would be possible to build a way down.

But down is not necessarily home. Nor is the presence of a stair proof that someone wishes to leave.

For now the power gives the lowest step a roughened edge, something a slipping hand could catch. It strengthens the stone beneath the shelter’s three legs. Where growth threatens to close the fern crack, it directs a little more water into a neighboring seam.

These changes do not make the hollow safe.

They make several dangers smaller, and leave the inhabitants room to discover what else they need.

***

One evening the creature brings the brown-black object out while the small one is awake.

It sets it between them.

The small one touches the groove, withdraws its hand, and touches again. Then it puts its mouth against the smooth surface.

The larger creature turns the object a little.

The power does not supply a meaning for this.

Across the hollow, new stone gives a faint click as it cools.

***

When the two enter the ring together, the power is watching, but it does not arrange the occasion.

The larger one has tested the descent many times. It has carried leaves into the circle, then a folded packet of food, then the dark object, held against its belly.

Now it goes in with the small one fastened beneath its jaw.

Their weight leaves them.

The larger creature opens its arms. The smaller remains contracted for several breaths, gripping skin in both hands. Then one hand releases.

Its arm lengthens.

Water hangs beside them. In one suspended bead the shelter appears upside down, its widened roof balanced on three strokes of darkness.

The small one opens its other hand.

Nothing holds it now except the larger creature’s palm beneath its back.

For a little while, neither has to cling.

When they descend, the larger creature touches down first. Its knees bend. Its hands remain raised until the small feet settle into them.

Then it carries the small one out to feed.

The power stays near the mountain, attending also to the seams, the flowers, the cloud. Beneath the shelter there is room for both smoky patches, and a little room beyond them.
