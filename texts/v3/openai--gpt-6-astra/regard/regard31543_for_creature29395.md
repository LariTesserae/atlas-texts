The weight returns.

The stone bears down on the waiting feet. The pads spread; the hard points scrape along its underside. One of the clear cords takes the strain, whitens, and begins to part.

Before it breaks, something bears a little of the load.

Not all of it. The creature feels the stone settle into its proper joint. It feels the neighboring blocks receive the weight, and the familiar low note comes through the wall.

It withdraws its feet.

Outside, a splinter passes through the place they occupied.

The power that has come to the mountain does not announce itself. There is no useful shape in which to appear, no light the wall-builder could see. A voice would be another pressure forced upon a body already enduring too much.

For now, it holds the damaged cord together.

And attends.

***

At first, even to this power, the basin is difficult to distinguish from what is happening to it.

The warm water comes from somewhere living things can inhabit. The salt current comes from a great depth elsewhere. The brown water has passed among roots. But the passages between these places have caught upon the broken summit, and each pulls according to a different weight.

The mountain is being used as a joint that does not fit.

Simply closing the passages would stop the collisions. It would also remove most of the water, the warmth, and nearly everything the creatures eat. Simply making the mountain whole would seal the green stains inside the rock and crush the inhabitants of the seams.

The power follows each current beyond the basin.

It finds where the salt water can return without flooding a coast. It follows the brown current to a river in spate and learns how little can be taken without changing the river’s course. Beneath the hot fractures it finds room for pressure to spread.

This takes less time than the failing cord needs to break.

Learning the wall takes longer.

There are slots that must remain narrow, surfaces that must remain rough, openings whose apparent disorder divides a violent inflow into tolerable streams. The empty shield is not obstructing the passage. Its broken edges catch fibers that would otherwise be lost.

The power leaves it where it is.

***

The next surge is severe.

The ceiling descends. White grains rise from the hollows. Beneath the balanced slab, the injured creature draws its unfinished membrane against its body.

But the stillness does not end at a straight, cutting edge.

For the width of a foot, the current slows before entering it.

The injured creature waits a long while. Then it extends one finger through the place where a finger would have been torn away.

Water bends the finger.

It does not take it.

The creature withdraws, folds close, and remains so until the next change of weight.

The power does not push it out.

***

Over several surges, the collisions high above the summit begin to arrive differently.

Their force is spent along passages that contain no bodies, against depths that can receive it. The silver skins still form where the waters meet, but they swell less tightly. Some dissolve. Others harden and descend in broad, slow curves.

The wall-builder catches one.

It holds the plate beneath its shield, waiting for the impact that usually breaks such things before it can secure them. No impact comes. It carries the plate to the hollow and divides it against the worn lip.

Both young feed.

The one that keeps releasing its pads loses hold again.

The parent catches it.

Here the power attends more closely still.

It does not assume that the young one is unfinished merely because it is different. It follows the small failure from pad to vessel, from vessel to the narrow place where an old injury has interrupted the tissue’s growth. Water has been passing beneath the shield faster than the wound could close.

The power brings the edges together and keeps grit from entering.

The body does the rest.

When the young one next places a foot, the pad spreads unevenly. It slips, catches, and holds.

The parent’s finger remains beneath it for a while.

***

There is no day on which everything becomes gentle.

The basin is deep water upon a mountain. Rock has weight. Mouths need food. The brown straps grow into spaces that smaller creatures would otherwise occupy.

But there is a day on which the summit stops exposing fresh interiors.

The long pink fractures no longer widen. Heat continues through them in branching threads. The deepest hollow still withdraws, but not so far that a creature feeding at its edge becomes longer than its own body. The ceiling rises and falls without wrenching the floor after it.

Beyond the rim, avalanches fall downward.

The power has not made the basin ordinary. It has given the incompatible waters ways to enter and leave without making the summit bear their argument.

Then it watches what its changes have done.

One of the feeding slots gathers too little now. The wall-builder probes it repeatedly, scraping an empty fringe. The power adjusts the approach of the brown current, returning the drift of soft pulp without returning the battering grit.

Behind the second shelter, stagnant water begins to lose what the small hidden animals need. A thin circulation is allowed through.

The still patch beneath the slab broadens until an unfinished shield can open fully within it.

These corrections continue. The power is careful, but care does not make every first attempt sufficient.

***

The wall-builder does not immediately trust the quieter water.

It braces through changes that no longer require bracing. It checks the joints so often that it takes little advantage of the food accumulating inside them. When a stone settles harmlessly on the far side of the basin, its fringes close.

The power does not reach into it and remove these responses.

They have kept it alive.

Instead, the next surge is survivable.

And the next.

The wall remains standing when the creature loosens its grip to feed. The young remain beneath the shield when it reaches farther into a slot. The low note changes, but the stones do not follow it inward.

There is time to learn the new note.

***

A hair-thin strand crosses from beneath the slab.

It drifts over a ledge, catches briefly against a brown strap, and comes free. The wall-builder takes it in a wreath of fingers.

The two creatures tighten it.

The power feels the minute movements and does not add a movement of its own. Whatever passes between them belongs to them.

The current changes.

The strand survives.

After a long interval, the wall-builder releases a parcel of food. It travels too low, strikes a ridge, and stops.

The injured creature puts two feet outside the stillness.

Then another pair.

It reaches the parcel and returns with it.

***

Eventually, the fish leaves.

It emerges backward from beneath the wall-builder’s shield, pauses with its fins brushing the fringe, then turns into the water. Its pale seam puckers as it bends.

The wall-builder contracts around the departing movement. One mouth opens.

The fish is already beyond it.

It finds a current, stiffens, and passes between the standing rocks. At the far wall it flexes too early for the old violence and comes awkwardly to rest above a seam.

There are small things feeding there.

Its fins open.

***

The power remains available to the mountain, though less of its attention is needed now. What holds the passages in their new relations does not depend on continual rescue. The cords in the wall are the creature’s cords. The new growth at the edge of the injured one’s shield is its own.

One stone near the shelter is still loose.

When the water lifts it, the wall-builder comes out and guides it toward a gap. It misses on the first attempt. On the second, it catches an edge and turns the stone until the fitted face lies inward.

The power leaves this work to it.

Later, there is nothing urgent to repair.

Food waits in the slots. Both young have purchase. Beneath the slab, the other creature is unfolding.

The wall-builder rests in its hollow with several feet unbraced.

For the first time, it can distinguish the movements beneath its shield without the mountain striking through them: the scrape of small mouths against a milky plate, a pad being placed and lifted, the slow passage of water through its own folds.

It stays there.

The wall holds.
