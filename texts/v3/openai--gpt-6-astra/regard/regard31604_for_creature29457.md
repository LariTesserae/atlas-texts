The power does not mistake the shell for an empty shell.

It attends first to the water.

There is water entering, water leaving, water that has lingered so long behind a sealed court that the living film along its walls is beginning to come apart. There is the heavier water below, which must not simply be stirred into the water above. There are currents made by mouths, currents made by legs beneath a brood, currents too small to move a grain but sufficient to keep an egg alive.

The power follows each of them.

It also follows the animal’s blunt projection as it tests the particle at the rim. The particle turns. One corner catches. The animal withdraws, then reaches again.

Nothing helps it place the grain. There is no need. After a long interval, it finds an arrangement that holds.

But behind the animal, six tubes away, a passage is closing.

The colony beside it has food enough to grow. Its newest cups have narrowed the passage until a threadworm can still enter but the water scarcely does. Beyond this constriction, an old crown opens against an old wall. Each filament bends where it touches. There is no direction left in which the animal can extend its house toward a better current.

The power attends to this also.

Then, outside the shell, the ice changes.

Not much of it. A seam scarcely wider than a filament softens along the outer surface of the lower valve. It runs from the sea to a place where two ancient fractures almost meet. A little mineral loosens there. The shell remains strong. Nothing living is displaced.

Water begins to enter the closed court through its floor.

At first it carries only the taste of somewhere else.

The crown against the wall withdraws. A sleeper deep in the masonry remains sealed. The tissue between the cups contracts along one edge.

The power waits.

It adjusts the seam until the incoming water is gentler than the movement of a passing leg. The heavier basin does not waver. The mother beneath the ledge continues cleaning her eggs.

By the next change of light, a broken cell has reached the old crown.

The filaments take it inward.

Other openings follow, none wide enough to become a mouth through which the little city could be emptied. They enter at different heights. They give water more than one way around the growing walls. Where a colony begins to close one route, another lies beyond it, not yet needed.

The place can go on building without quite finishing its own enclosure.

Overhead, the pressure of the ice changes too.

The upper valve is not lifted. The chambers touching it have grown to meet it, and several would tear if the roof were pulled away. Instead, a narrow space forms outside the shell. When the ice next thickens, its force passes around that space.

Inside, a flexible bridge remains bowed but does not bow farther.

The power has noticed the eggs beneath it.

Beyond the clear lip, the larva is still being carried.

There is no fright in it that the power can find, no image of its lost shelter. There is a small reserve diminishing as its hairs beat. There are responses to light, touch, dissolved traces. There is the capacity to become an animal with a house, provided that water brings it against a surface it can use.

The power does not return it to the crowded rim.

A little farther under the ice lies a stone. One side is scoured clean; the other holds a fine growth fed by a slow current. Between these sides is a shallow notch, protected but not still.

The current carrying the larva bends.

Its front end touches stone.

It lifts away once, travels the length of the notch, touches again. The power does not fasten it there. For some time its hairs continue beating against the pull of the water.

Then they slow.

Within the shell, the shortened filament is growing. A pale extension appears beyond its thickened end. It will take time to become a useful part of the crown.

The predator passes through a joint beneath it.

The power does not love the predator less. It attends to the branching gut, to the broad exploring head, and to the juvenile standing farther along the joint with its new limbs spread against the wall.

From the sea comes a soft fragment, dead before it reached the ice. It catches where the worm is turning. The head spreads over it. Farther along the joint, the juvenile moves on.

This is not a settlement of hunger forever. The power remains attentive beyond the moment in which the juvenile disappears from view.

There will be other needs, including needs made by what it has changed. Better water will bring more growth. More young will require places to settle. The new seams will acquire films, and mouths will gather near them.

The power does not leave instructions in the ice and depart.

For now, the animal at the opening has secured its grain. It lays a little material along the join. Behind it, where the passage used to end, water moves through a chamber whose occupant has begun to feed again.

Nothing in the shell can see the whole of what has happened.

A crown opens. Eggs shift beneath their mother. In the salt basin, a broad foot fastens to a fresh portion of terrace.

Outside, the ice continues growing.

It grows around them.
