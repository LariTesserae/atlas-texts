The power noticed the hand beneath the shelf.

It could have held the roof in place. It could have warmed the western basin, cleared the channels, lifted the inhabitant caught around the fallen support. It could have made the whole valley bright.

For a while, it held nothing but its attention there.

The small body shifted its attachment. The older one raised another branch to meet it. Water passed through their grooves at different rates.

The power waited through the dark.

***

It learned first that warmth was not one thing.

There was warmth that opened the feeding mats, and warmth that spoiled them. There was water in which a garden flourished but the growth beneath an inhabitant’s mantle slowly died. The three in the western basin had once widened their channel to admit a stronger seep. Later, with considerable effort, they had narrowed it again.

It learned that the pale roots were feeling their way through the mountain. Far above, what they supported had needs of its own. The changed note at the stair belonged to a strain that could not be relieved merely by making the root still.

It learned the routes between gardens by attending to the bodies that traveled them. On a map of stone, two shelters seemed close together. On a map of exhaustion, they lay on opposite sides of an almost impassable country.

And it learned something of the knocks.

A repeated sequence passed from the cleft to the intact roof, then farther east. Nothing was carried afterward. No channel was opened.

The power did not send that sequence back.

There were inhabitants waiting to hear particular others.

***

Its first intervention was beneath the western basin.

A warm passage had narrowed there, deep inside the rock. Mineral deposits had grown toward one another until only a thread of water passed between them. Above, three bodies pressed into a garden whose living edge had retreated almost to its center.

The power loosened a little of the deposit.

It waited.

The water arriving at the garden became slightly warmer. One inhabitant lifted its mantle. Another moved a hand into the channel and held it there through the next dim flowering of the leaves.

The power loosened a little more.

This time the nearest inhabitant pushed a stone across part of the outlet.

So the power stopped.

Over the following days, the stone was adjusted twice. New water crossed the old cultivated grooves. A pale fringe appeared along the brown mat.

The three moved apart by less than the width of a bowl.

That was enough to begin with.

***

Beneath the intact roof, a joint was opening.

The older inhabitant knew it. Each return included a pause beside the downhill pillar, a hand set against the stone while the mantle sampled the water above. Once it brought a chip of shale. The chip would not hold. It took it away again.

During the next great current, the pillar began to turn on its buried foot.

The power steadied it.

Mortar fell. Sand whispered against the outer wall. Under the shelf, the older body gathered the young one close.

The pillar remained where it was.

When the current passed, the inhabitant approached and felt around the base. It cleared sediment from one side, then the other. Its hand found a gap that should have closed under the weight of the roof.

It withdrew.

Much later, it returned with the shale chip.

The power allowed the pillar to settle against it, gently enough that the inhabitant could feel the load arrive.

The chip cracked.

The pillar stopped moving.

For a long time the branching hand rested beside the broken stone.

Then the inhabitant went to the path.

It pressed the flat signaling stone, waited, and pressed again.

***

Help came slowly.

The one from the cleft arrived first, its pale covering frayed by the journey. A second came from beyond the stair. They rested in the garden before entering the chamber.

The older inhabitant guided their hands to the pillar’s foot.

Together they cleared a place for a broader foundation. They brought stones from a collapsed wall downhill. They tested each one by touch, set some aside, and fitted others beneath the roof.

Whenever they needed the pillar raised, the power took a little more of its weight.

Whenever they set a support, the weight returned.

At first they retreated from these changes. Later, one kept its hand against the pillar while another worked underneath.

The foundation took several brightenings of the garden to finish.

When it was done, the power withdrew its support by degrees.

The stones held.

The visitors remained through another dark interval. There was enough food for them now: the power had kept the seep from narrowing while they worked, and the older inhabitant had opened an unused bed beside the doorway.

Before leaving, the pale one rested a branch beneath the shelf.

The young one touched it.

***

Afterward, the older inhabitant began leaving things in the cleared space beside the pillar.

First a cracked bowl.

The power did nothing to it.

Then a length of root cord, detached from a shelf that had fallen in another chamber.

Again, nothing happened.

The inhabitant took both away.

On its next return it brought a small flat stone. It placed this upon two chips, tested the arrangement, and rested with one hand beside it.

The power waited until the hand withdrew.

Then it depressed the stone.

The knock was slight. Beneath the shelf, the young one contracted against its attachment.

The older body flattened to the floor.

A long time passed before it touched the stone again.

Their earliest exchanges contained little more than this: an action, a pause, a repetition. Sometimes the power misunderstood. Sometimes the inhabitant removed the stone and carried on with its work.

The power let those intervals remain empty.

***

There were changes elsewhere.

A resting place formed halfway across the naked shale between the western basin and the cleft. Warm water emerged beneath a low ledge, carrying the minerals needed by the inhabitants’ outer growth.

For a long while it was only a seep.

Then a traveler stopped there.

On a later journey, that traveler brought a piece of brown felt. Another brought a bowl. Someone shifted a slab to divert the worst of the grit.

The power kept the water steady while the garden took hold.

It made no road through the leaves.

At the fallen roof support, it waited until the inhabitant had finished arranging its stones. When the body braced to move the timber, the timber became a little easier to move.

The inhabitant emerged carrying much of its shelter on its back. It rested outside for days, loosening the fibers one by one.

No one hurried it.

***

The young one’s hands developed unevenly.

One divided before the other. It used the new branches to explore the shelf, the older inhabitant’s mantle, the root that had been spared when the garden first brightened.

The clouded pits along the older body’s forward edge remained clouded.

Once, through their slow work with the stone, the power offered help there too. A small area of clear tissue softened under a touch that was neither hand nor current.

The inhabitant pulled away and covered the pits beneath a fold of mantle.

The power ceased.

Much later, the inhabitant uncovered one.

***

There was still cold in the valley. Gardens darkened for days. The great current arrived through the northern pass, and the roots tightened in their holes.

But now a traveler could cross the western shale in two journeys instead of one.

Three inhabitants fed without overlapping.

Under an intact roof, a small body detached itself from a shelf and found stone beneath its own pads.

It advanced a little way down the polished strip. The older one rested nearby, keeping the doorway clear.

Halfway across the chamber, the young one stopped.

Its newly divided hand had encountered a flat stone resting on two smaller stones.

It pressed.

The power, attending elsewhere to a warm passage and a root under strain, felt the knock.

It waited for the small hand to lift.

Then, very gently, it answered.
