The second pair of hands belongs to a neighbor.

The power that has noticed the city does not take credit for them.

For a while it watches the two gardeners working. The root is old and reluctant. Warmth alone will not persuade it; there must be somewhere for it to go. One gardener holds the bend while the other scrapes a little plaster from behind the hinge. They pause, test, scrape again.

Eventually the door opens all the way.

Neither gardener celebrates. One rubs a cramped wrist. The other makes a remark about whoever installed the hinge.

The power pays attention to this too.

***

It could lift the doorstep.

There would be sky enough then, and silence after the stone ceased to sing. There would also be cold air descending through the gardens, moisture settling in the sleeping cells, roots going white in the astonishing light.

It could stop the wind. But the wind carries the oily vapors through the hollow. It brings mineral dust, takes away the brittle skins of leaves, and turns certain shutters whose purpose is not immediately apparent.

It could restore the deserted houses and fill them with inhabitants. It does not do this.

The chalk figure in the old tray-carrier’s pocket has been touched so often that its broken neck is smooth. A new head would not necessarily be the missing one.

The power has time. It does not mistake this for permission to take everyone else’s.

***

Above the doorstep, rain is gathering in a defect beside the jamb.

The defect is recent. Someone has dragged a heavy object across the stone, opening a narrow route into the mortar. After several hard storms, water will reach an abandoned stairway. The roots there connect to three occupied terraces.

The power turns the rain aside.

Not far aside. It gives the worn surface a slope too slight for the household above to notice. Water travels outward and falls where water already falls.

Beneath the step, nobody praises it.

That is satisfactory.

***

Its first visible act is less successful.

It makes a sheltered place beside the lowest street: a small alcove with a baffled entrance, warm walls, and almost no roar. It leaves the opening broad enough to be discovered without anyone having to venture inside.

The child finds it first.

Then three adults arrive, pull the child out, and mark the entrance with crossed slivers of pale leaf.

An inexplicable cavity without the usual vibration is evidently a dangerous cavity.

The power has made something safe that looks like death.

It leaves the warning in place.

The next day, beside it, there is a shallow clay tray.

The tray contains a model of the alcove. Its roof lifts off. A little mica vane at the entrance shows the passage of air. Another vane, inside, scarcely moves. The clay is ordinary clay, and when a gardener takes a shaving from the edge, nothing surprising happens.

For two days, the model is examined.

On the third, someone steals its mica.

This is the first encouraging development.

***

The power acquires hands.

Not a whole imposing body, and not a face copied from somebody loved. It makes a small, plainly unfamiliar figure, with joints that bend in the local fashion and a coat without pockets. The absence of pockets causes more discussion than its arrival.

It waits beneath the arcade, away from the grit trays.

When addressed, it fails to understand.

When addressed more broadly, with great clarity and some irritation, it understands that it is blocking a route.

It moves.

Over the following days it learns useful things: *hotter than it looks*, *mine until returned*, *do not put that there*, and a sign whose meaning changes considerably according to whether the smallest finger touches the wrist.

The child teaches it a name.

The name involves both hands beginning a task incorrectly and then stopping.

The adults adopt this name at once.

***

The power does not remain helpless for the sake of seeming harmless.

When a shutter pin breaks, it catches the shutter before it strikes a flowering bed. When asked to carry a tray, it carries the tray. When an old hand cannot close around a tool, it offers warmth.

Once, the tray-carrier lets it warm both hands for a long time.

The cloudy margins of the joints become clear enough for the fingers to open fully. Beneath the throat plates, the slow contraction steadies.

The power could go farther.

Instead, it makes a questioning motion toward the pale repairs along the ribs.

The tray-carrier draws its coat shut.

Later, it returns and indicates one knee.

Only that knee.

Afterward, it climbs the arcade steps twice, ostensibly because it has forgotten something. It tells nobody what happened. By evening, everyone knows.

***

The quiet alcove becomes a public argument.

One inhabitant wants it enlarged. Another points out that a room in which the ceiling cannot be felt properly would be a poor place to sleep. The gardeners ask whether its construction has altered the draft beneath the western beds.

It has, slightly.

The power corrects this.

Someone asks how.

This takes much longer.

They build six models, two of which are wrong. Fine grit is released into channels so that the current can be seen. A frond is used where a vane would conceal too much. The power learns that what it understands all at once must sometimes be laid out across an entire courtyard.

By the end, two gardeners can alter the baffles themselves.

The alcove receives a shutter operable from inside and outside. Beside it they install a thin strip of stone that carries the doorstep’s vibration directly into the room. Anyone sitting there may rest a hand against it.

Quiet need not mean being unable to know what is coming.

The first adult to enter stays only a moment.

The second brings work.

The old tray-carrier goes in with nothing at all.

When it comes out, it is angry. It has heard its own throat plates moving, and the sound is not as it remembers.

For several days it avoids the power.

Then it asks whether the room might contain a little more of the city.

A narrow vent is opened.

***

At the sealed western passage, the power leaves no miracle.

It continues the existing practice of putting food on the ledge.

Once it leaves a clay strip showing the new alcove, the shutter, the strip of listening stone. Beneath these it scratches the simplest invitation it knows.

The strip disappears.

Nothing else happens.

Weeks later, a bowl emerges with a diagram scratched into its underside: a passage bent twice, an opening, a small chamber.

There is a correction beside one of the bends.

The power studies the diagram. Then it places tools on the ledge.

The tools disappear too.

***

The city does not become agreeable.

The improved knee lends the tray-carrier new energy for an old disagreement about stair access. Somebody takes cuttings from a ring-tipped fern growing beside the quiet room and claims they were already loose. The power is asked to settle the dispute about the council benches.

It examines the chamber.

There is wood enough elsewhere, it says, or tries to say.

This resolves nothing. The benches are not only wood, and the power is made to understand that its suggestion has been beside the point.

It helps mend a household pivot instead.

The ivory animal walks across one of its fingers without hesitation. This settles a question for the child, though which question remains unclear.

***

Much later, the power is taken to the last rooted terrace.

The child stands there with a fruit skin held between two fingers. An adult has a hand hooked through the back of its coat.

The fruit skin is released.

It crosses the white emptiness, rises, vanishes.

The child makes the sign for overhead without shelter.

Then it makes the sign again, differently.

The power looks across the central hollow.

It does not lift the stone.

Along the far wall, beyond the routes used for gathering, there is a narrow shelf too exposed to reach. Above it, one hairline fracture rises through the doorstep at an angle. At certain hours it contains a piece of the outside.

There are ways to bring that piece within sight.

This time, the power makes a model first.

***

The work takes a season.

A covered path must be fastened along the wall. Its supports must not catch drifting grit or steal light from the lower gardens. There are discussions about rails, resting places, and whether the child should be allowed to help.

The child helps considerably and is then removed from the site for helping considerably in the wrong place.

The power does what their tools cannot do. It makes secure anchorages in the stone. It lifts a burden while hands beneath it fit the supports. Where the path crosses a cold seam, it gives the wall a little enduring warmth.

At the far end they build a chamber with room for three.

Its viewing plate is not mica, though mica holds it in place. Through it, by a dry and folded route that admits neither weather nor dangerous glare, a small portion of the world above can be seen.

There is no image painted on the surface. Things move there whether anyone is watching or not.

The first visitors see gray.

They wait.

The gray opens into blue.

One of them reaches up and touches the chamber roof.

***

The tray-carrier comes near the end of the season.

It rests twice along the path, less because of its knee than because the resting places are well made and deserve use.

In the viewing chamber it sits beside the power.

For a long while there is only blue. Then something white advances across it, changing at the edges.

The tray-carrier asks what supports it.

The answer requires several signs the power has not yet learned.

It tries anyway.

The tray-carrier watches its hands, then the cloud.

At last it takes the headless chalk figure from its pocket and stands it on the ledge, with the broken side toward the view. It keeps a finger beside the figure so that it cannot fall.

They remain until the light begins to change.

When the tray-carrier is ready, it puts the figure away and rises. At the chamber entrance it pauses, looking back.

Its hands make a small movement beneath the patched yellow coat.

*Tomorrow?*

The power indicates the path, the chamber, the shutter that anyone can open.

Then, more simply, it answers.

*Yes.*
