The power first attends to the shard.

It is thin enough that the keeper must have carried it carefully. One edge has been rubbed smooth where a smaller hand grips it. Placed across the seep, it divides the water without stopping it: a little toward the living spot, a little past it, enough beneath to keep the underside damp.

The power could move the brine beneath the province. It could lift the mineral weather from the sky.

For a while it watches what the shard accomplishes.

Then it attends to the keeper.

There is an old injury where the left small limb joins the body. The limb moves well enough while warm. During a long crossing it stiffens, and the keeper carries it folded, doing its measurements with one pad and the moist combs of its mouth. This takes longer. The others have learned to wait without gathering too closely around it.

The keeper’s name contains a movement that once required this limb. Those who know it well supply the missing pressure from their own side of the contact.

It is neither the oldest nor the most generous person on the ridge. In one of its cheek pouches it has kept a culture it has not shown the others. The culture makes poor water drinkable, but grows slowly, and the keeper has twice lost nearly all of it by dividing it too soon.

It has also given its place beneath the warmest part of the sleeping slab to a child who is not its own.

The power attends to these things without sorting them into a verdict.

Below the ridge, a mineral throat is opening.

The first seep is good. The water that will follow it is not. A concentrated brine has waited in a blind chamber through the ridge’s long dormancy. When the pressure rises, this brine will come upward before the larger circulation dilutes it.

The keepers will notice. Their cultures will discolor; the mouth combs of anyone tasting the seep will contract sharply. But the change will arrive during a resting interval. The nursery’s porous fragments will draw some of it beneath the slab.

The power does not wait for a child to be hurt so that its arrival can have meaning.

Deep under the shelter, it opens a narrow connection between two existing pores. The first bitter water turns aside into an empty bed of salt-bearing stone. Nothing at the surface shakes.

This is not its whole answer. It is what it does before beginning an answer.

—

The keeper wakes because something is touching the outside of the door.

Not scratching. Not pushing.

Three contacts, widely spaced.

It draws the children closer with a hind limb and places its good pad against the floor. The others wake through the movements of its body.

There is no weight outside that any of them can recognize.

Again, three contacts.

The keeper takes up a point. Another adult eases the door inward by a little.

Beyond the opening lies a short strip of something pale. It rests on the stone without advancing. Its surface is neither hide nor mineral, though it has enough of each to seem possible. At its near end is a shallow depression the size of a wrist pad.

The adults wait.

So does the thing outside.

At length the keeper sets down its point, but keeps it close. It touches the depression with one digit.

The answering movement is very slight.

It is the child’s game from the previous night: a tap, a pause, an answering tap.

The keeper withdraws. The pale strip does nothing.

It touches again.

Over the next several nights, this becomes the beginning of a language.

The power does not put understanding into their bodies. Their bodies are already full of understandings, some of which it has only just begun to notice. It learns the difference between a wrist held still in attention and a wrist held still because its bearer is frightened. It learns that an offered surface must remain offered after refusal.

It makes mistakes in the length of its pauses.

Once it repeats a movement that means something like *bring the small ones here* when it intends only *show me again*. Every adult between it and the nursery lowers its body.

The pale strip retreats at once.

It does not return until the keeper comes outside and taps for it.

—

The old injury is the first thing the keeper asks about.

By then they have established a few reliable exchanges. A place can be indicated. A change can be proposed. The movement for *not this* is understood, and the power has demonstrated that it will stop.

The keeper brings its stiff limb against the offered surface. With its good hand it makes the motion for a passage that will not open.

The power answers by touching the old scar.

The keeper waits, then presses assent.

There is no flash. The limb grows warm, then uncomfortable. The keeper pulls away, and the change stops.

Later it returns.

The repair takes several visits because that is how the keeper chooses to receive it. Tissue draws apart where it has healed badly. A small nerve grows through a place it could not previously cross. The power leaves sensation intact; when something hurts, the keeper can say so.

On the last night, it puts both pads on the ridge.

For a long time it does not move.

Then it goes beneath the shelter and wakes one of its companions.

Their wrists meet. In the keeper’s name, an old movement returns.

The companion answers it twice.

—

News travels with the groups that come to test the new current.

The accounts disagree.

At one shelter, travelers hear that a pale root beneath the ridge repairs damaged limbs. At another, that the ridge has acquired a person who has no underside and cannot be poisoned. Some arrive carrying sick children. Some conceal their children and come alone.

One group brings an empty culture stone and demands that it be made rich.

Another brings a companion who died on the crossing.

The power receives them all, but does not answer every request in the form in which it is made.

An emptied stone can be inoculated. Better still, the group can leave with several cultures suited to different water, and with the means to recognize when each is failing.

The dead companion is harder.

The power attends to what remains, to what could be restored, to whether a living being could truly return rather than something made to comfort the survivors. It does not offer an imitation.

Where return is possible, it asks the one who might return.

Where it is not, it stays through the long contacts by which the others begin to understand. It helps carry the body to the place they choose. It does not describe the loss as necessary.

Soon there are more visitors than the seep can support.

The keeper who first touched the pale surface indicates this with considerable force.

The power has been watching the visitors. It has not sufficiently considered the wear beneath their feet.

Afterward, help is no longer available at only one ridge.

—

Across the uplands, at occupied shelters and old route junctions, a new surface appears beneath certain ledges.

It does not cover the grooves.

A traveler can ignore it, use it to request contact, or ask it to remain absent from that shelter. Its simplest exchanges are taught by other travelers, with all the variation and argument that teaching brings.

It gives warnings in forms that can be checked: the direction of an approaching pressure front, the depth of a dangerous pocket, the interval before a resting place becomes too hot.

The keepers compare these warnings with the stone.

They discover that the stranger distinguishes underground events they have long treated as one. The stranger discovers that its maps of suitable ground omit the importance of a particular bark beetle, the taste of certain softened stems, and the comfort of sleeping where several old names are already cut into the door.

There are changes beneath the country, but not one great correction imposed upon it.

A nursery can have a harmless layer that darkens before its floor becomes dangerously warm. A door can be made lighter to pull from inside without becoming easier to force from outside. A water-cleaning film can be helped to survive a longer dry interval.

None of these requires the keepers to abandon their skills.

Some make skills less urgently necessary. The power does not begrudge them that.

When the old can cross a barren district safely, they do not all choose to travel. Some remain with their small groves because they like them. Others leave so quickly that their companions spend half a cooling interval repairing the stores they have neglected in their excitement.

Children learn surfaces still.

They also have more time for the surfaces that ring.

—

The keeper eventually shows the power its hidden culture.

It takes the fragment from its cheek and holds it between both small hands. The growth is scarcely more than a dark stain.

The power examines it.

Then, instead of taking it or praising the keeper for surrendering it, it offers a second porous stone.

Together they attempt a division.

The first attempt does poorly. The keeper moves the new fragment farther from the water. The power adjusts nothing without being asked.

On the fourth night, both stones are growing.

The keeper carries one to the nursery.

It keeps the other.

—

There is still no sunlight on the upland.

The inhabitants are asked about that, eventually, in ways that allow more than a choice between darkness and a sky remade without their consent. Small places are prepared where different kinds of light can be encountered safely. Some visitors find them beautiful. Some find them exhausting. A broad-winged animal refuses to pass over one until its edge is dimmed.

The matter will take time.

Meanwhile the warm ground continues its immense, uneven work. Herds leave good resting places for reasons worth investigating. Old warnings remain under ledges, with newer grooves beside them. The buried passages are watched, and fewer failures have to become funerals before anyone knows they are happening.

On the ridge, the dark spot beneath the shard spreads into the pits of its stone.

The keeper lifts the shard and checks the water with both hands. Behind it, a child taps the nursery roof.

The keeper answers.

A second answering tap comes from the pale surface beside the door, slightly mistimed.

The child pauses.

Then it repeats the pattern, more slowly, teaching.
