The power first learned the difference between stillness and rest.

There were long intervals when the resident did not move because moving would cost more than it could recover. There were others when it remained spread between the waters because the balance was agreeable, the pipe warm enough, and the rubber under its smallest limb still capable of yielding.

From the square, these intervals looked alike.

The power waited through enough of them to know better.

It learned the three pumps separately. It learned that the drag in the second shaft was troublesome, but that the slight hesitation in the first was useful: the resident relied on it when working beneath a particular valve. It learned where the white shrimp laid its eggs. It followed the clear water beyond the split ceiling to a seam under the retaining wall, and followed the buried brine until it found the narrowing that had made the pumps slow for a season.

There, beyond any passage the resident could enter, salt had grown around a broken tooth of iron.

The power removed the tooth.

Not the salt all at once. That would have sent a hard pulse through the pipes, stripped the growth from two bearings, and lifted the brown ceramic pieces out of their long arrangement around the drain.

It removed a little salt at each easing of the tide.

For a while, the only change in the district was that the third counterweight no longer had to rise quite so far.

The resident noticed.

It left its room, went beneath the scales, and pressed its pad against the brass. It stayed through six revolutions. Then it crossed to the retaining wall by a route that required it to draw all its feeding folds tightly inward.

The wall gave it no explanation.

It returned home before it had exhausted itself.

That was the first improvement: an investigation from which it could return with something left.

***

The power did not lift the ice.

There was a great weight above it, and beyond that weight were other waters, other pressures. An opening made carelessly would have been a wound through the entire district.

Instead, it took some of the load off the roofs.

Deep in the rubble bank, stones settled against one another until they carried what the soft timbers had been carrying. The movement was less than the thickness of the resident’s brass pin. A beam beneath an enclosed balcony ceased, very slowly, to bend.

Nothing straightened. The additions remained crowded and awkward. But the service hatch behind the service hatch would not be crushed next winter.

The power also saw the sharp zinc edge that barred the shorter route to the pump room.

This it folded back.

The resident discovered the change while carrying the slate flake. It touched the fold, withdrew, and approached from the other side. The metal was smooth. Behind it lay a passage with safe holds, one after another, all the way to the housing.

It did not use the passage that time.

On its next journey, it put the slate through first.

After several more journeys, the new route became a route rather than an event.

***

Food required more care.

A warm district would not necessarily be a living district. Much of what lived there depended on the cold; the waters held their boundaries partly because they differed so sharply.

The power made no summer.

It arranged for a narrow current to pass through a seam beside the resting room. The current carried no animals large enough to hunt the resident, no vigorous growth that would smother the transparent tubes. It carried the small, ordinary abundance that had been missing: matter fine enough for the fronds to gather, traces of what their pale tissues needed.

Downstream, the water still reached the washbasin.

The white shrimp remained white. It did not grow to a marvelous size. It simply found enough to eat on both sides of the basin.

Near the resting room, the fronds lengthened.

The resident grazed them, then returned sooner than it used to, expecting bare iron. Finding growth, it combed a little more and withdrew.

For several intervals it ate cautiously. Its body had endured lean conditions for so long that plenty arrived first as a disturbance.

The power kept the current steady.

Eventually the resident began leaving a patch untouched because another patch was sufficient.

***

The knot near its rear did not resume growing immediately.

The power attended to it without separating it from the body that sustained it. There were vessels within the knot that had narrowed almost closed, and a fold caught against an old seam. These could be eased. But a second animal was not a repair to be completed on a schedule.

For a long time, the knot’s only new act was to turn toward warmth with greater strength.

The resident responded by changing the way it rested.

One lower lobe rose. Two holds moved. The knot came to occupy a little more of the boundary between waters.

Later, a mouth appeared.

It was a small slit, badly placed for reaching any surface. The resident brought back a scrap of soft film and held it against the opening.

Some of it was taken.

The next time it went to feed, it carried food home before it was finished eating.

The power widened the stable meeting of waters in the room. Not enough to alter the square, only enough that two unequal bodies could find their own heights there.

***

By then the resident had begun to search for the source of the changes.

It examined the folded zinc often. It listened at the wall. Once it wedged its wire into a crack near the new feeding patch and left it there through an entire dark interval.

When it returned, the wire had not moved.

The power understood that invisible assistance could also be an unfamiliar condition against which an animal had no defense.

So it gave itself a place.

Beside the outer service hatch was a blind length of pipe, sealed at both ends. The power made this pipe carry a faint vibration: two slow pressures, then a pause. It was unlike a valve, unlike the scrape of stone, unlike anything asking for urgent repair.

The resident felt it from inside its room.

It did not come out.

The vibration stopped.

Much later it began again, equally faint. This time the resident opened the hatch and touched the pipe.

The power answered with one pressure, no stronger than the touch of a small limb.

What followed took many intervals. The resident withdrew; the pipe became still. It returned; the pressure returned. It braced hard against the wall, and the power did nothing. It laid the rubber against the pipe and waited.

There was no language yet.

There was, however, something that could be approached and left.

The power made no changes to the resting room while the resident occupied it without first sounding the pipe. When the animal gathered itself tightly and shut the hatch, the work waited.

Some improvements took longer this way.

***

At the grille, the power was especially careful.

It could have made the passage ordinary. It could have abolished the other square and routed the pipes through distances that behaved.

But there were collars older than the resident fitted to those bars. There was water arriving by means the district had come to require. And there had been a touch.

The power followed that touch far enough to know that it was not a predatory lure.

It did not arrange another.

Instead, it cleared a recess beside the grille where the resident could anchor above the strongest brine. It secured the drain rim, which had been loose for years. It left the two views as they were.

The resident still folded one branch back when reaching through.

Whatever waited on the other side, if anything waited, would not be allowed to make that caution useless.

***

The smaller body separated during an interval when four lamps were dark.

The band between them had become thin and almost transparent. For some time each animal had been holding to the wall independently, though the larger still shifted whenever the smaller pulled.

Then the band parted.

Neither moved away.

The smaller animal remained against the warm pipe. Its pad was scarcely large enough to cover the end of a rivet. Through that small surface came three shafts, a valve closing, the changed load of the tide.

The larger animal touched it once along the side.

Later, it took the porcelain button from the felt. It held the button for a while, then pressed it into a vacant depression lower down.

The power did not decide what this meant.

It kept the water steady.

***

There were still failures.

A lamp went out. The resident investigated and left it out. The flat fish moved from beneath the ledger cabinet to a room with a fallen door. A familiar strip of kelp came loose and passed through the grille.

The smaller animal proved inclined to put its limbs into things before anchoring properly.

There was work, but it no longer consumed every reserve. There were routes that did not end in pain, and food that could be missed once without consequence. Beyond the retaining wall, the power was preparing a sheltered passage into a larger stretch of water. It would have the right salinity, places to hold, and a way back.

It was not ready yet. No one was being hurried toward it.

One interval, the resident left its room without a tool.

It crossed by the folded zinc, passed the pump housing, and descended to the square. Beside the drain, the blue ceramic chip had turned bright side upward again.

The resident felt along its edges.

Nothing there needed adjusting.

It lifted the chip, carried it home, and spent a long time finding a place for it in the felt.
