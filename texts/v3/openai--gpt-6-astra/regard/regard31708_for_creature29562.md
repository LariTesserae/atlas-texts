The power does not enter through the opening beneath the longest limb.

That opening is already occupied: by water, by the magnified clouds, by the mountain as it is present to the little upper eye. To appear there, radiant or otherwise, would be to put itself in the way.

It attends without becoming a sight.

Where the power comes from, kindness often involves a next thing. A wound closes. A locked door opens. Someone who has been alone hears a footstep outside. It knows these goods thoroughly. It has brought them about in places where their absence was terrible.

Here, it must understand what kindness could mean without a next thing.

The immense anatomy offers plenty of invitations to a careless mercy. There are limbs that have never withdrawn from the cold. There is a mouth that has never closed. There are embryos that will not hatch, and a fish whose bent tail will accomplish no stroke.

The power does not take these for evidence enough.

It attends to the difference between a limb held still and a limb prevented from moving. Between an unfinished gesture and a shape that is not a gesture at all. Between a life denied its continuance and a life whose extent cannot be measured by continuance.

Above all, it attends to the animal.

Not only to the white cords beneath its neck, or the translucent partitions that could so easily be mistaken for the whole explanation of it. It attends to what it is like to have the cold along those fingers.

There is no aching effort to pull them back.

It attends to the fish in the brown eye. There is no appetite frustrated by the distance, no anticipation stretched beyond endurance. The stripe of light and the stripe without light are both distinctly present.

The roar is harder to understand.

Elsewhere, such a sound could crush a life into a narrow enclosure. There could be nowhere to retreat from it, no sleep deep enough to escape it. The power is careful here. It does not let the beauty of the roots excuse what might be happening beneath them.

But the animal is not being drowned out.

Through its cups and seams and clear clusters, it has distinctions for which the power’s first understanding of sound was insufficient. The root beside its jaw is unlike the root beneath its farthest palm. The presence of the neighbouring animal has an exactness that distance does not diminish. The tremendous work of the sea does not occupy all the room there is.

The mountain is there also.

The power finds no hidden plea beneath these things. Nor does it find a declaration that this is the best of all possible lives. The creature is making no argument. Its existence does not have to justify itself.

That matters.

The power could give the sea a morning followed by an afternoon. It could let the suspended fleck descend. It could arrange for the first heartbeat, and then for the second.

But it could not honestly call this merely setting things free. It would be changing what freedom, loss, nearness and life could mean here. It would be taking the animal’s whole undivided world as material for another kind of world.

And the animal has asked for nothing of the sort.

So the power leaves the water held above the air. It leaves the square teeth visible between the lips, the salt at the lower lid, the delicate branches of the hand spread over the slope.

It does not quiet the roar for its own comfort.

There is one thing it does do, although the doing belongs elsewhere.

In its own understanding, it makes room for this place without turning it into a lesson. It will not use the animal to teach other beings to accept captivity. It will not offer the sea as proof that change is a mistake, or that suffering elsewhere can be beautiful if viewed from far enough away. Those places must be attended to in their own particulars. A hand that is trying to withdraw must be helped to withdraw.

Here, the surfaces touch.

The power grants no blessing that the creatures must carry. It establishes no guardianship, asks for no recognition, leaves no bright sign in the wood.

Beneath the root, the brown eye has the silver fish.

The little upper eye has the mountain.

Neither has had to give anything up.
