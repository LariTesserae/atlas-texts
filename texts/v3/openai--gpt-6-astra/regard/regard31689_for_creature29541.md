For a long time, the power does nothing that can be distinguished from weather.

It attends through several passages of cloud, through a dry interval, through the first freeze that remains in the shaded courts at noon. It follows water into the walls. It listens to the knocks beneath the gardens, taking care not to mistake every repetition for a word.

The smallest court has more than one clear note. There is the note heard from the streets, and another carried through the pavement, and a third that reaches only two of the sleeping chambers. An animal in either chamber can feel a change in the northern wind before the exposed fringes begin to stir.

The power had considered smoothing a roughness inside that court.

It leaves the roughness alone.

***

The old inhabitant has a damaged fold on its left side.

When it opens, a strip near the root remains stuck to the surface beneath. The neighboring pleats pull against it. In strong light, a little moisture appears along the seam, though no fog has passed.

The white animals are interested in this place. The old one lifts them away with a front foot. It does this patiently until it is tired, then folds the whole side shut.

The power could make the damaged tissue new.

It does not yet know what that would feel like from within.

Instead, one afternoon, while the inhabitant rests with its mouth against a damp sill, the power holds the air beside the injury very slightly warmer. Not the whole chamber. Not the moss underneath. Only the narrow space where the two surfaces have adhered.

The animal opens an eye.

Its front foot comes back, touching the fold with all three pads. It waits. Then it shifts its body away.

The warmth does not follow.

For the rest of that afternoon, nothing more is offered.

On the next cloud passage, the animal opens in the same court as before. Its left side extends only halfway. After the others have begun returning to shelter, it stays beside the basin, head low, making small adjustments to the injured pleat.

Again, there is warmth nearby.

This time it brings the fold into it.

The power holds still.

The separation takes most of the remaining light. The animal does the pulling, a little at a time. Once it closes the pleat entirely and stands with its eyes shut. Later it opens it again.

By dusk the surfaces are apart.

They are not new. There is a pale, tender strip that will need protection, and a thickened place that may never gather water well. But the neighboring folds can open without tearing.

That evening, the old one releases water into the basin from both sides of its body.

***

There is a garden below the five courts that no longer receives enough water.

Its channel has not been neglected. Three inhabitants visit it regularly. They scrape the entrance, remove loose grains, press their mouths to the floor. Beyond their reach, the mountain has shifted along an old curved break. Two surfaces now meet where there used to be a passage.

The power sees the obstruction.

It also sees what would happen if the surfaces were simply parted. Farther down, the same break holds the edge of a shaft. Moving it would release several thousand years of accumulated stone into a room where young animals sleep.

A new spring would be easier.

But the garden’s moss is adapted to a brief wetting followed by a slow retreat of moisture. The living walls advance more quickly where water is constant. A generous spring might close the chamber before the youngest inhabitant had grown its full fringe.

So the power follows the blocked channel back.

There is an older route. It passes beneath a threshold and ends at a seam the animals have been working for longer than any one of them can remember. Small pieces of living wall have been set along it. Some are dead. Others have joined into a thin, irregular lining.

The passage needs perhaps another hundred winters.

The garden does not have them.

Here the power acts.

Deep within the rock, beyond roots and buried fans of bone, it makes a crack no wider than a hair. It holds the surrounding stone in place. It lets through so little water that the first drop takes a day to gather.

At the threshold, the living lining darkens.

One of the inhabitants finds it the next morning.

It touches the seam with its mouth, moves away, returns. Then it goes into the adjoining chamber and knocks against the floor.

The old one comes.

For a while they stand with their heads close together over the damp stone.

The power does not open the whole route. It keeps the small supply steady while the inhabitants clear the lower end, adjust the lining, and move a patch of moss that would otherwise be drowned. When they stop work, it lets the flow diminish. When they have made room, another drop gathers.

By the following freeze, water reaches the garden.

There is no moment when the repair is finished. A lip thickens in the wrong place. Grit catches at a turn. One of the inserted pieces loosens and must be replaced.

The work becomes ordinary.

***

The power does not take the wind out of the town.

The bird still waits at the parapet. Insects are still carried from the gardens. The white creatures are still removed from tender folds, sometimes with less patience than before.

There are dry afternoons when nothing opens above ground.

There are deaths.

But when the old inhabitant can no longer descend the long slope without resting, a place along the way begins to hold the day’s warmth. It is broad enough for the folded body, sheltered enough that the warmth is not immediately lost. The animal uses it. Later, so do others.

And when an unfamiliar knock comes through the stone—urgent, repeated, from a chamber where no answering foot can reach—the power attends.

It has learned enough to arrive.

It has not learned so much that it no longer needs to listen.

***

One evening the old inhabitant remains above after the seams have frozen.

The others are below. Its fringe is folded, its feet set around the empty basin. It touches the repaired place on its left side, then lowers its mouth to the pavement.

The knock is soft.

The power does not know whether it concerns water, discomfort, the absent young, or something for which it has no understanding at all.

It answers with a pressure in the stone, light enough to be ignored.

The animal waits.

Then it lifts one front foot and sets it down a little farther along the channel.

There is a white lip growing across the outlet. The old one begins to scrape it away.

Beside its working foot, the power holds a loose fragment steady.

Together, they leave room for the water.
