The power arrived while the woman was asking for the small scissors.

It could have entered through the stone, or appeared in the blade of daylight at the upper door. Instead it was first present as someone standing near the tool cabinet, close enough to pass the scissors without making the woman repeat herself.

She took them and cut away the tip of one leaf.

The branch lifted slightly.

“Not that much,” she said, inspecting what she had done.

The power did not put the tip back.

***

For some days it attended to the district without improving it.

This was not a test. No one had been required to demonstrate patience, ingenuity, or affection for their neighbors before receiving help. The power was learning what its help would touch.

It watched the rain arrive hours after the rain. It accompanied Sera to a door that had begun to bind and stayed while she examined everything except the door. In the engine house it listened to Bel explain the second accumulator, then to Edan explain the same drawing with different parts emphasized.

It asked the boy in the mill what he would do if the mill worked faster.

“Leave sooner.”

“And then?”

“Not be here.”

It did not ask him for a better answer.

Rus showed it the vine called Liar.

“Can you tell what it felt?” she asked.

“Yes.”

She waited.

“The mortar contracted during a dry week. There was movement, but not enough to bring the wall down.”

“Will there be?”

“Not there. The footing at the other end will need attention before winter.”

Rus fetched her notebook.

She was more interested in the second answer.

***

The power could feel the black stone beneath the iron plates. It knew the immense, slow circumstances of its rising, the pressure distributed through buried layers, the places where an easy change here would become a violent change elsewhere.

It could have stopped the movement under the district.

But heat drove the engine. Small seepages depended on the tilt of the ground. Roots had made their lives in differences too slight for a hand to distinguish. Stopping everything would not have meant leaving everything as it was.

It could also have lifted the square, taken away the ceiling, and set the district in open daylight.

When it mentioned this, several people wanted to know what would happen to their houses. Others wanted to know what would happen to the square. Edan asked about rain entering the flywheel bearings. A child asked whether birds would come.

Sera’s sister asked whether there was any reason the possibilities had to be so large.

She had put a coat across two chairs to show the power where the trouble lay. The sleeves hung down between them.

“There,” she said. “That is the width I mean.”

The power measured it.

***

Its first substantial work was done outside.

At the top of the ramp stood a disused storeroom, belonging to an office whose occupants believed it belonged to another office. It contained broken festival frames, three sacks of hardened plaster, and a desk too wide to pass through the door by which it had entered.

The power found the owners. It did not confuse their ownership with a good reason to leave the room unused. There was a conversation, some payment, and a document that Sera took upstairs to have read by someone who understood documents.

The storeroom became part of the district.

There was a broad table for cutting cloth. There were places to sit without hot stone beneath them, and a cupboard in which people could leave their outside shoes. The old desk was taken apart. Most of it became shelving.

The door remained at street level. The woman from beneath the court tree came to inspect it and asked why, having obtained a room she could enter without steps, they had put it at the top of a ramp she could scarcely climb.

They had not yet done anything about the ramp.

She considered this worth pointing out.

***

Work on the entrance took longer.

The power offered several arrangements. A new stair would use little space but exclude too many people. A steeper trolley track would carry goods well and frightened nearly everyone who imagined riding it. Bel proposed a lifting platform, and then spent an afternoon changing the drawings after the woman asked where she was meant to put her stick while opening the gate.

They built a lift beside the existing ramp. It could be operated from either end, and lowered safely without the engine. Its mechanism was accessible. Its instructions were painted where a person standing on the platform could read them.

A second way out opened beyond the bathhouse.

For this, the power did something no mason could have done. It persuaded a narrow depth of stone to settle into a form it would otherwise have taken centuries to reach. Beyond the wall, a passage rose gently toward a little-used side street.

Rus moved the ferns first.

The power did not ask her to hurry.

***

Benevolence did not make the new works agreeable to everyone.

The second exit altered a draft. Three plants suffered before Rus understood why. The lift made a low click at each landing which the instrument repairer claimed to hear from his bed. Someone began leaving deliveries in the sewing room because it was convenient.

The power returned to these matters with the same attention it had given the stone.

It changed the passage’s ventilation. It quieted the latch. It helped carry six crates out of the sewing room while Sera’s sister stood with her hands on the cutting table and declined to be useful.

There were larger disagreements as well.

Some wanted the entire district cooled. Some feared for the engine. Some wanted more light; others had rooms whose privacy depended on the dimness. A man proposed removing the court tree and was surprised by how many people agreed with him, then alarmed by what they wanted to put in its place.

The power did not turn agreement into a condition of relief.

It drew excess heat away from the worst iron plates and carried it to the engine’s hot side. Edan watched the gauges. Bel watched Edan.

The mill became faster.

Not very much faster at first. Enough that the boy finished in daylight.

Later, with his wages made secure for a time and an introduction to a workshop above, he left. He did not discover that he had loved the mill all along. He came back occasionally to see a girl who lived near it.

***

Under the buildings, the power made room for the ground to move without carrying every wall along with it.

The work was not visible from the court. There were new bearings beneath foundations, yielding joints, places where force could travel without finding a kitchen first. Sera insisted on seeing each arrangement before it was covered. She brought questions she had thought of during the night.

The power answered them.

It also gave the district a way to notice trouble beyond the reach of any particular root: small gauges, plainly marked, requiring no faith to read. The inhabitants learned what the gauges measured and what they did not.

Rus kept watching the foliage.

In her notebook she began recording the readings beside her observations. Sometimes the plants noticed first. Sometimes they did not.

The power left the fern called the Magistrate out of all official appointments.

***

Not everyone stayed.

With money that did not have to be repaid, two households found rooms elsewhere. One retained a workshop below. Another wanted no continuing arrangement except permission to visit, which nobody had thought to withhold.

People who remained received help too. They were not obliged to call staying a victory.

Sera’s sister used the upstairs table on three mornings each week. On the fourth she spread out a coat and left it there until evening, merely because she could.

The woman from the court began going above on warm afternoons. Once she remained in the square until the lamps came on. When Bel came to fetch her, she said she knew perfectly well what time it was.

Ilen asked whether the power could make her plant grow straight.

“Yes.”

“Would it stay straight?”

“If I changed enough about it.”

Ilen looked at the stem, which had made a small, determined bend around its own supporting thread.

She asked for a longer piece of wood instead.

***

The power did not leave behind a perfected district. There were still narrow rooms, quarrels, failing washers, and people who could not be persuaded to keep the drains clear. It stayed available without occupying a chair in every conversation.

It had no need for a temple. When someone proposed a plaque, Sera’s sister said the wall in question was where she intended to hang patterns.

The patterns went there.

Below, the white stripe continued to return on the flywheel. Rain reached the roots late. The court tree grew into space Rus had allowed it, and occasionally into space she had not.

One afternoon Sera visited a house whose door she had shortened five times.

She checked the hinges, the frame, the new footing. She opened the door and closed it. Then she did so again.

The owner waited beside her with a bowl ready for the shavings.

“Nothing today,” Sera said.

She put her plane away, and for once they both had the afternoon.
