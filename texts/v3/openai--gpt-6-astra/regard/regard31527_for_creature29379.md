The power waits until the feather has been put right.

It does not wait in the lane, where its shadow would take their light. Nor does it enter the hollow in the form of warmth. There are things asleep in the planting beds that would wake hungry; there is water held safely in ice above a roof that could not bear its sudden release.

For a while it attends to the wall.

There is a fracture behind the bone-supported lintel. There are three pockets of loose grit beneath the paving overhead. The root at the far end is alive farther up, where it belongs to a tree whose removal has already been discussed in a large, warm room.

The power attends to that room also.

Then it returns to the entrance.

***

The inhabitant with the tray feels someone arrive.

The sequence is unfamiliar: weight, release, a pause long enough to allow an answer. It comes from the pale stone outside the feather.

They set one palm against a post.

The greeting repeats.

Outside sits a being of approximately their own size. Its shoulders are too broad for the nearest passage. Its feet are bare, and it is plainly cold.

The inhabitant watches it for some time.

At last they part the feather.

The visitor comes no farther than the shelter of the entrance. When offered a scrap of felt, it puts the felt beneath its feet. This seems to be the wrong use. The inhabitant takes it back, demonstrates, and drapes it over the visitor’s shoulders.

The power has understood the motion by which continents travel. It now learns how to keep the corners of a small blanket from trailing in a gutter.

***

Learning the language takes until evening.

It could have taken less. But the inhabitant has six slices to bring in before the light fails, and later must help someone carry a pot through a doorway. The visitor waits during these things.

Their first conversation concerns where it may sit.

Their second concerns whether it intends to stay.

Their third is interrupted by a bootfall so heavy that the person speaking loses their balance.

The visitor puts a hand against the paving. Beneath that hand, the town becomes briefly clear: the returning basket, a child running where running is forbidden, the uneven step of someone approaching the square.

The inhabitant beside it lifts their face.

“Again,” they say.

The visitor waits for the next footstep.

***

What it offers them is tried first beneath an empty storeroom.

There is no new substance to fetch and no shining object to guard. A thin seam in the foundation changes its arrangement. It becomes capable of yielding a little under a sudden blow, then returning to its former shape.

The first attempt is unsuccessful.

A person upstairs cannot feel their partner coming home.

The visitor restores the seam.

The second attempt leaves the small vibrations intact but makes the floor sway after a cart passes. This is unanimously disliked.

For the third, the worker with the stone blade sits on one side of the room and taps the blade’s handle against the floor. Someone else stands outside with a hand on the wall. The visitor works between them.

There are long pauses. The worker warms their hands inside their coat. The person outside changes places with another.

By nightfall, a cart goes over the lane.

Everyone feels it approach. Everyone knows where it is.

Underneath that knowledge, uninterrupted, comes the small familiar pressure of the basket being lowered.

The person at the upper window does not stop winding.

Only afterward do they realize this.

***

It takes several days to agree how much of the hollow should be treated.

One household refuses. The wall by their sleeping room has always told them when rain begins, and they do not believe the visitor’s account of what will remain unchanged.

The visitor leaves their portion alone.

Someone asks whether the same work could prevent a neighbor’s footsteps from reaching their house. The visitor says it could, but would also prevent other things from reaching it.

This begins an argument that does not require the visitor’s participation.

Meanwhile, the loose grit above the town is secured. The fracture behind the old lintel is closed from within, with the permission of the household whose lintel it is. They keep the fork of bone.

A fine line on the entrance stone is left under the feather. Pressing along it reverses the new work, one section at a time. The visitor has several inhabitants do this themselves, then put it back.

The child with the hollow stem is especially interested.

***

The old inhabitant beside the green shoot asks for something else.

The visitor crouches near the threshold. Up close, the wrapped foot smells faintly sour.

“Can you mend this?”

“Yes,” says the visitor, after attending to it. “May I take the wrapping off?”

The old inhabitant considers.

“Cut it. Don’t pull.”

There is a basin to fetch, and the room must be made warmer without heating the planting beds outside. The visitor asks which cloth may be spoiled. The old inhabitant names one, then changes their mind when it is brought.

The work continues well into the night.

By morning the foot is clean, the pain is less, and the old inhabitant has slept for several hours without waking to move it.

Healing will take longer. The visitor could hurry it, but is asked not to: the old inhabitant finds the sensation disagreeable.

Before leaving the room, the visitor looks at the shoot.

“Shall I—”

“No.”

The answer is immediate.

Later, from the square, the visitor sees the old inhabitant lifting the troublesome leaf again.

***

Beyond the far wall there is another dry hollow.

The power tells them this. It does not open a passage.

For two evenings people come to inspect the place where a passage might go. They lay palms against the stone. A household with too many people asks about the light. Another asks where the excavated material would be put. Someone who has not spoken to a neighbor for years discovers that they agree about the danger of drafts.

The power supplies a drawing, scratched at their scale on a flake of limestone.

They turn it around several times.

It is left in the square, weighted at one corner, for anyone to examine. No decision is reached before the visitor goes.

***

In the lane above, the tree is inspected again. Its roots are found to be sound. A different repair to the paving proves cheaper.

Below, the town remains cold in winter. There are disputes about rooms. The snail gets through its fence, and a new fastening has to be devised. The worker cuts the same finger in a different place.

But a barrel is dropped overhead one morning, and the tray beneath the awning stays level.

Its owner feels the great weight arrive and pass into the deeper earth.

From across the gutter comes a small, deliberate touch against the wall: someone asking whether they felt it too.

They set down the tray to answer.
