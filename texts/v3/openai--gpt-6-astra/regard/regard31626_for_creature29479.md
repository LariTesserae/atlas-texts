The power did not stop the pumps.

It listened first.

There were differences between the two running machines that no one heard from the yard. One carried a slight irregularity in a bearing. The other transmitted a stronger vibration through its discharge pipe, where a bracket had been tightened against a wall. Between them they made a pattern that changed with the opening of valves, the accumulation of weed, the small daily variations in the incoming water.

The animal knew this pattern without knowing machines. It knew places where the tremor was clear and places where it arrived confused. It knew the narrow disturbance made by a foot on a walkway, and the long change that preceded a filter basket being lifted.

The power attended to these things in the same way it attended to the animal’s breathing.

It did not mistake stillness for contentment. Nor did it assume that a creature resting behind a pump must wish to be somewhere else.

For a time, nothing happened that anyone could have entered on a maintenance sheet.

Then the power followed the channels.

Some ended where the drawing said they ended. Others joined older drains beneath the surrounding buildings. One narrowed abruptly at a sleeve inserted during a repair. Another passed through a chamber half filled with brick dust, where water spread thinly over the floor before finding its outlet.

Two of the young had gone through that chamber. One had remained there for eleven days, eating the small white growth along a submerged cable. Farther on, beyond a bend that no inspection lamp reached, there was another basin.

The power followed all five routes. Not all still had an animal at the end.

It did not make the large animal understand this. It did not put a human kind of mourning into a body that had its own ways of registering absence.

But where a young one lived beneath a loading platform, with water entering at one end of a broken culvert and leaving at the other, it loosened a stone that had begun to choke the outlet. The stone moved less than the width of a finger. Water passed beneath it again.

That was the first alteration.

***

In the district, the bearing continued to wear.

The power could have made it sound forever. Instead, during the morning attendant’s next inspection, it allowed the irregularity to carry distinctly through the handle of a screwdriver held against the housing.

The attendant moved the screwdriver, listened again, and wrote a note.

The note was not lost.

A replacement arrived before the old bearing failed. There was time to arrange the work, time to bring the covered machine into service, time to discover that its isolation valve would not close properly. The repair took place on an ordinary working day, with the right people present.

No one had to enter the basin in a hurry.

The animal spent most of the work beneath the far walkway. When it came into the pump house afterward, the fitted cover had been folded onto a shelf. Its familiar hem was gone.

It searched the edge of the plinth twice.

The power watched this, too.

The next morning, the woman who cleaned the strainers found a spare piece of heavy fabric and draped it over the pipe behind the machine. She had noticed the searching. There was no need to give her the thought.

***

The blue stroke beside the inspection plate began to fade.

One edge had worn away beneath a cleaner’s thumb. The woman refreshed it, then stood for a while with the brush in her hand.

At the next maintenance meeting she mentioned the coupling.

Someone asked whether the animal was a problem.

“Not there,” she said.

There was a short discussion about access, and a longer one about what would happen if the return channel needed flushing while she was away. Eventually a line was added to the procedure: inspect the recess before cleaning; if occupied, use the other return and notify the supervisor.

The words were plain. They did not require anyone to agree about how clever the animal was, or whether the washers on the step had meant anything.

The blue mark remained. Now it was not the only thing protecting the recess.

The power had made no one kinder. It had given an existing kindness enough time, and a little room, to become something that could survive a change of shift.

***

There were limits to what could be improved within the wall.

In hot weather the incoming water arrived warm. The motors warmed it further. The animal spent longer under the slotted plate, and the fish gathered so closely there that their bodies touched when they turned.

The power could have cooled the whole basin.

Instead it opened, beneath the gravel margin, a small passage to a deeper bed of wet stone. Nothing broke. The change was slow enough to leave the foundations undisturbed.

A seep appeared behind the reeds.

At first it was only a dark patch that remained after the rest of the gravel had begun to dry. Then water collected beneath a lip of concrete and entered the basin in a thread too slight to be heard above the pumps.

A worker checked it for oil. Another tested it and compared it with the supply water. A note was made to watch for settlement.

There was none.

The seep remained cool through the hottest part of the day. It did not cool the district. It made a place, roughly the area of an open toolbox, where the animal could lie with its head in cooler water and the rest of its body in the familiar current.

Small fish found it first.

For three days the animal would not approach while they were there. On the fourth it settled among them. The fish moved aside, then returned to the slow water beside its folds.

The heron at that end of the basin shifted to a nearer railing.

The power did not drive it away.

***

It also attended to the people.

The heat in the pump house was not more acceptable because they could name it. During a scheduled alteration, an intake was moved to draw from above the shaded side of the wall. The new duct reduced the temperature where the workers stood without cooling the sheltered gap behind the plinth.

The loose pane in the door was secured.

For a day the animal paused whenever that door opened. The rapid glass chatter had been part of the warning. By the following morning it had learned the smaller sounds beneath it: the latch, the hinge, the change in air.

The power learned something from this and became more careful about repairs.

It left the falling water alone.

It left the reeds where old shells crowded their stems.

It did not scrub the submerged concrete clean.

***

Late in the season, a small animal appeared in the return channel beyond the retaining wall.

It was larger than the ones that had left. A pale scar crossed one forefoot. It rested just outside the district for much of a night, its filaments moving separately in the water.

The passage inward was open.

So was the passage back.

Before dawn it entered, crossed beneath two walkways, and fed along the edge of the basin where the lamps did not reach. The large animal lay behind the pump. Whether either knew the other was there, the power could not tell without intruding upon things it had chosen to leave private.

By morning the newcomer had gone.

It returned six nights later.

***

There was no unveiling.

The workers continued to complain about the humidity. Filters clogged. A heron caught a fish beneath a lamp and spent some time turning it in its beak. The electric cart developed a fault in its charging lead.

From the pipe bridge, the district still looked like a basin set among low buildings, enclosed by taller walls.

Below, certain failures had become less likely. A refuge no longer depended entirely on one woman remembering it. Cool water entered at the roots of the reeds. Beyond the wall, a route remained passable.

During the next pump changeover, the large animal came out into the lane.

It crossed the paving and put its feet against the plinth. The motor began to turn. Along its sides the loose skin trembled.

The power was there, but added nothing to the sensation.

After the pressure settled, the animal stayed a little longer. Then it lowered its feet and made the difficult turn toward the drain.

At the doorway it stopped.

Water tapped from a supply pipe into a hollow in the concrete. A worker stood aside with a spanner in one hand. Beyond him, the lane was clear.

The animal went on at its own pace.
