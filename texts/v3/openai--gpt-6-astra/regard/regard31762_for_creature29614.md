The power first notices the two pairs of hands.

It has been aware of the plateau for some time, as one may be aware of a light in a distant window. Heat rises through old fractures. Water carries a little of the mountain away and leaves a little elsewhere. In the flooded shafts, the pumps do their work.

Then a man kneels beside a child to feel something neither of them understands, and the power attends more closely.

It does not supply an explanation.

The tapping is not a sentence waiting to be translated. It belongs to a body with six feet, a sensitive belly, and a particular relation to the animal farther along the bank. Even between those two animals, it does not mean precisely the same thing.

The power lets the father and daughter feel it.

Below their hands, below the walkway supports, below even the oldest pipes, it begins to examine the stone.

***

There are things wrong with the plateau.

Not everything that changes is wrong. The power is careful about this.

A pool is filling with white deposits. Within seventy years it will be a shallow dish, and the swimmers that live there will have moved elsewhere or failed to. A shelf is slowly tipping toward the escarpment. Nothing built upon it will last forever. In the west channel, an old animal has stopped eating, though it still turns its head when the carts pass.

These are not all the same kind of trouble.

Under the third apartment block, a seam has opened around a buried foundation. Water enters it during the evening pressure change. For eleven years the seam has widened without disturbing the surface.

The people who walk the route have recorded a cool patch near a stair.

They have checked the drains. They have checked the supply pipes. Last winter someone wrote, “Possibly deep,” and underlined it twice.

They are right.

The seam will eventually join a disused drilling shaft. When that happens, water will leave one level of the plateau very quickly. A roof of stone will lose the pressure that has helped support it.

There will be little warning that can be perceived by someone standing above.

The power rests its attention against the seam. For the moment, it holds.

***

It comes to the gauge house during working hours.

The person there sees someone standing outside the threshold, waiting to be noticed. Their clothes are wet at the shoulders. This is sufficiently ordinary that she finishes entering a number before asking what they want.

“To show you something under the third block.”

“Water?”

“Yes.”

She puts down her pen.

The power could place the whole shape of the danger in her mind. Instead, it asks for a tray.

She gives it one used for carrying samples, and it fills the tray with layers of gray and ivory stone. There is the foundation, reproduced no larger than a tooth. There is the old drilling shaft. Water moves through the little seam.

The woman bends close.

“Is this happening now?”

“Yes. I am keeping it from opening farther.”

“For how long?”

“Long enough to decide what to do.”

She looks up then, properly.

After a while she goes to fetch the engineer. She takes the tray with her, holding it level in both hands.

***

The meetings are not especially graceful.

Someone wants the whole apartment block moved. Someone else wants to know why the power cannot simply make the rock sound. The engineer asks whether closing the seam will raise pressure under the machine hall. A screen tender, invited because she knows the old shaft outlets, points to a passage in the model that nobody has asked about.

“That feeds the narrow pools.”

It does.

The power makes the model larger.

By evening, two tables have been pushed together. There are bowls standing beside them because people have brought supper and then forgotten to eat. The model now includes the narrow pools, the machine hall, and a place beyond the occupied shelves where additional water could emerge without passing through anyone’s home.

They choose a repair with an inspection passage.

The power could make a support that would never need checking. But they would have no way to know that it remained what it claimed to be. They ask for something whose condition can be measured.

So it gives them that.

They also ask whether the new outlet can be kept away from the breeding bank.

It can.

The work takes three days. Much of that time is spent waiting for animals to leave channels that must briefly run dry.

***

Word spreads.

People come with requests written on paper protected between plates of glass. Others arrive empty-handed and speak reluctantly, as though reporting a fault in their own household.

The power does not find the smaller requests disappointing.

There is a woman whose hands split open every week from the first laundry rinse. She has tried three kinds of gloves. One leaks, one stiffens in the heat, and one is too costly to replace often enough.

There is a man who has not visited his sister below the mountains for nine years because someone must perform a particular sequence of adjustments before dawn, and the town has never managed to spare a second person long enough to learn it.

There is a child who wants the swimmer in her basin to stay.

For the woman, the power helps make gloves. It works with the people who repair pump seals, using materials that can arrive on the railway and be cut and joined in the town. The first pair is clumsy. The woman says so. The second pair is better.

For the man, it takes the early shift while he trains someone. He leaves with a crate of bowls his sister requested six years ago.

For the child, it asks to see the basin.

They sit beside it for a long time.

The swimmer is pressed against the drain, opening and closing the sensitive pits along its jaw.

“Would a bigger basin work?” the child asks.

“Bigger than this room.”

She considers the room.

They take it to a pool the next morning. The power carries one end of the tray, although the child knows by then that it does not need to.

***

It does not cool the springs.

It does not draw the metals out of all the water, or fasten every shifting shelf, or cover the plateau with earth.

But the drinking cisterns become easier to maintain. A seal changes color before it fails. The rescue slings no longer stiffen after months in their boxes. At three dangerous crossings, the people build landing shelves below the waterline, places where someone who has fallen can get their face into air.

The power holds the hot flow aside while they work.

At the machine hall, it spends several mornings beside the screen tenders. Together they devise an intake that most grazers can crawl out of. Some still have to be carried away in trays. Nobody claims otherwise.

There are requests it cannot grant without taking something from someone else. There are requests whose consequences remain uncertain even after long examination. These stay on the tables in the gauge house.

The power returns to them.

***

When the next pump inspection comes, the father and daughter go to the same walkway.

For a while they stand. Then the child kneels.

Her father has been working fewer early shifts. That morning he has slept until the shutters grew bright, and there is no need to hurry home.

They place their palms on the stone.

The first tapping comes from farther away than last year. A second sequence follows beneath the bank, faint enough that the father is unsure whether he has felt it.

He waits.

Across the basin, the repaired foundation carries its ordinary weight. Water passes through the new outlet. In the laundry rooms, people take off their gloves with unbroken hands.

The child shifts one palm to avoid a sharp grain of mineral.

There it is again.

This time her father looks at her.

She nods.
