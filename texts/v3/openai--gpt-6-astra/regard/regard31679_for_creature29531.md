For a long time, the power does nothing that could be distinguished from attention.

It follows the warmth into the shell. It waits through the slow exchange between the animal and the branching life beneath its back. It attends to the small creature sheltering among the worn hairs, and to the roots above, and to the soft thread caught and eaten after the rain.

It does not mistake stillness for contentment. Nor does it assume that every effort is suffering.

There is something to learn from the way the old animal handles its chip. The notch is useful because one foot can brace against it. The thin edge is useful because it catches under the crust. But these facts do not quite account for the animal walking over the chip, pausing, and reaching back. Another stone might scrape as well. This one fits a sequence of movements worn into the animal by repetition.

The power watches until it can recognize that sequence when it begins.

It watches the return to the wall.

Five feet find their places. The sixth presses, slips, presses again. The animal hangs for a while with its weight distributed badly. Beneath the shell, the soft plates draw close together.

Here the power acts.

At the place where the shortened foot is searching, a grain of stone holds.

It would ordinarily have broken free. A minute fracture already passes behind it, left by the swelling and drying of something that lived there long ago. The power keeps the grain attached for another moment.

The foot catches.

The old animal lifts itself into its hollow. Its feelers pass over the familiar surfaces. It brings the chip alongside its body and settles.

Nothing follows it into sleep.

***

The power could open the hillside.

It could make the whole vault transparent, bring water from the deep passages, fill the dry channel, restore the missing joint. But it has been paying attention.

A full channel would cover the mineral crusts. Unbroken light would alter the life within the shells. Water has its own inhabitants; so does darkness. Even the small deprivation of an afternoon sends the animals to places where other necessary things are found.

The hollow is not waiting to become a garden belonging to someone else.

Still, the cramped resting place presses against a tender seam beneath the old animal’s shell. Whenever the animal settles deeply, it must shift again.

During the next morning’s absence, the power loosens a little stone.

Very little. The amount a root might work free over a wet winter.

It follows an old boundary between grains, leaving the weathered surface of the recess intact wherever feet have polished it. The projecting edge remains. So does the rough approach beneath it. Along the cramped side, the wall draws back by less than the width of a feeler.

The loosened grains fall into a crack below. The power has looked inside the crack first.

That evening, the animal climbs home.

It begins its customary adjustment: a lift of the broad side, a turn, a slight drawing inward of the front feet. Halfway through, it stops.

Its feeler travels along the altered wall.

For some time it remains raised, holding most of its weight on four feet. Then it lowers itself.

This time, it does not have to rise again.

Beneath its shell, the little companion moves into the space left by an uncompressed fold of skin.

***

There are other things the power notices.

One of the green coins has curled so tightly that the next drop will roll from it. A root has reached a seam where there is no more nourishment. A young animal lies overturned in a shallow groove, moving its feet against the brightness.

The power uncurls no leaf and directs no root. These still have time and ways of their own.

It waits beside the young animal.

A middle-sized one passes near enough to touch its waving feet, but continues toward the wet wall. The young one rocks itself, rests, and rocks again. One hook almost reaches the side of the groove.

The power gives it purchase there.

The young animal pulls, tips onto its feet, and runs a short distance before settling abruptly. Its shell is almost transparent. Light passes through it and falls faintly on the floor.

The power stays long enough to see it move again.

***

It does not make a promise that nothing will fall, or hunger, or break.

Such a promise would reach into every mouth in the chamber.

Instead, it learns the difference between a necessary taking and a loss that serves nothing. It learns slowly, even with all its wisdom. It is willing to be corrected by a leaf, by a current of damp air, by an animal returning to a place it has made more comfortable and finding some essential feature gone.

When it is uncertain, it waits.

When waiting would merely leave a creature helpless, it helps.

And when it helps, it leaves room for the next movement to belong to the creature.

***

Several days later, the old animal comes back carrying its dark chip.

It has fed well. A little yellow remains around the edges of its shell. At the foot of the wall it pauses while two young pass beneath it, then begins to climb.

The shortened foot finds the rough place.

The hollow receives the uneven back.

For a while, a feeler rests against the newly exposed stone. Then the animal draws it in and folds its front feet beside the chip.

Outside, the grasses lean under the evening wind. A drop gathers overhead, still several days from falling.

The old animal settles its weight.

There is room.
