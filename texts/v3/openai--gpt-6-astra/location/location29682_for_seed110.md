Near the summit, inside a fold of the mountain too narrow to have a name, there is a warm hollow. Its entrance is beneath a stone lip. You find it by putting your hand into a crack where the air ought to be cold and feeling moisture gather between your fingers.

The passage turns twice before it widens. No daylight reaches the second turn. Beyond it, darkness is complete: waiting does not improve your sight. The hollow must be learned through the soles of the feet, the reach of a bent arm, the small differences between sounds made close to the face.

It is scarcely larger than a room, but there is little empty room in it. Thick mineral lobes rise from the floor and descend from the ceiling, nearly meeting. Some lean together in clusters. Others have flattened where they touch, like soft objects packed into a basket. Between them are passages just broad enough for a shoulder, shallow basins, low shelves where a person can sit with their knees drawn up. The mountain’s weight is overhead, yet everything within reach feels newly made.

None of the stone has a broken edge.

There are no chips underfoot, no gravel, no sand brought down from somewhere else. A ridge can be followed with a bare finger without finding a place that cuts. Even the narrowest projections end in blunt, tender-looking tips. These are not old forms worn smooth by water. They have never been sharp. They seem to have risen directly into their present shapes and stopped before becoming entirely rock.

Under light pressure, the surface is firm. Under the heel of a hand held steadily against it, it yields a little. Remove the hand and the hollow slowly fills, without a wrinkle, without leaving a print.

Water occupies almost everything the mineral does not.

It lies in the basins a thumb’s depth below their rims. It makes soft collars around the bases of columns. In the narrowest gaps, it rises between opposing surfaces until a hand pushed through comes out wet to the wrist. The ceiling carries heavy beads, each one suspended in a rounded socket. They do not seem to fall. Occasionally a bead touches your hair, and then there is warm water running behind your ear, unexpectedly intimate in the dark.

There is no current to follow. No inlet murmurs behind a wall. No drain takes the water away. Its warmth is the warmth of the stone, the air, the little pockets beneath the shelves. After a while, the boundary between wet skin and wet atmosphere becomes difficult to locate. Only movement makes it distinct again.

Outside, far above the inhabited valleys, the summit catches hard winds. Here a strip of bark hung from a ceiling knob rests without twisting.

Someone hung it there long ago, or recently; nothing in the hollow helps decide. A series of knots has been tied in the strip. Beneath it, fitted between three mineral swellings, is a small platform made from split branches. The branches are lashed with plant fibre. Their cut ends bear the shallow, repeated bites of a stone blade.

The platform holds one person clear of the water. It has no roof because the ceiling is within reach, and no walls because the mountain has supplied too many already. At its outer edge sits a clay cup, thick at the bottom, uneven at the lip. It has no decoration except the thumb hollow left by whoever lifted it before it dried.

There are two other signs of deliberate work. A cord runs through the entrance passage, low enough to find by sweeping a hand along the wall. And in one of the deeper pools, a flat slab has been laid across two rounded stones to make a step. The slab is foreign to the hollow. Its underside has a fractured edge. Touching that edge, after the unbroken surfaces elsewhere, is like encountering a different law.

Little grows here. Near the first bend, where the crack still remembers the outside air, a few pale stems emerge from a seam. They carry no leaves, only small swollen joints. Their roots spread across the wet stone in a thin felt that can be mistaken for softened cloth. Farther in, there is a low, close-textured growth beneath one shelf. A fingertip brushed across it releases a faint smell of cucumber skin.

There is no litter of dead vegetation, no accumulation of old seasons. The few living things occupy their places without spreading far beyond them.

Once, while feeling for the cup, you may touch something that withdraws.

It is a small animal, no longer than the last joint of a finger, with a smooth back and several delicate feet. It lives beneath the platform where wood meets stone. It moves slowly enough that its departure feels less like escape than a surface rearranging itself. You might remain in the hollow for days without meeting another. There is no chorus, no scurrying behind the walls. Its presence makes one narrow crevice inhabited and leaves the rest uncertain.

Most sounds are your own. A foot lifted from a basin makes a soft, adhesive noise. Cloth brushes a column. The cup gives a dull click against its shelf. None of these sounds travels far. The crowded mineral bodies divide the hollow into little acoustic enclosures; someone sitting only a few paces away could seem to be speaking from inside the wall.

When you become still, the quiet has almost no incident in it.

The water does not deepen. The soft mineral does not harden. The ceiling and floor remain nearly joined, leaving exactly enough space between them. Everything appears to be at the beginning of a process that has no further stage.

A person might first mistake this for a place being born. Sitting longer on the branch platform, with warm dampness settling into their clothes, they might become less certain that anything is unfinished. The blunt tips, the pliant stone, the brimful pools may be mature conditions here. There may be nothing for the hollow to become.

To leave, you lower one foot onto the foreign slab, take hold of the cord, and turn sideways between two columns. At the second bend the air touches your face differently. At the first, cold begins to separate water from skin.

Behind you, the cup rests on its shelf. The heavy beads hold to the ceiling. In the crowded dark, the small space you occupied is empty again, without needing to recover.
