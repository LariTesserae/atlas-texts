The hollow lies below the level of the surrounding plain, shallow enough to miss until the ground begins to slope beneath your feet. There is no rim to climb over. Hard earth gives way to loose grit, then to broad, nearly flat slabs of stone. A few paces farther in, the sky disappears behind an overhang.

It is warm under the rock. Not the fierce heat of an exposed surface, but a warmth that seems to belong to the stone itself. The air rests against the face. There is no breeze reaching this far, and no cooler current moving along the floor.

The space is about the size of a small grove. Its boundaries are irregular but close: a low back wall, two rounded shoulders of rock, the sloping entrance behind. The ceiling is high enough to stand beneath near the middle, then descends gradually until it meets the floor in a seam too narrow for a hand. Nothing has been squared or fitted. The overhang is simply a harder layer that remained while the softer material beneath it wore away.

In the darkness, the floor is easier to understand through the feet than through the eyes. Here it is solid, with a slight hollow in each slab; there it shifts a little, grains rolling over grains. Most of the loose material has settled into the lowest places. It makes pale, smooth patches between the darker stones, though there is scarcely enough light to distinguish one from the other. A foot drawn through it would leave a mark that might last a long time.

Farther back, the grit becomes powder. It lies in shallow pockets beneath the wall, undisturbed, without tracks or the scattered husks of insects. There are no droppings, no feathers caught in the cracks. The hollow does not seem occupied.

A little vegetation holds on near the entrance. Two low shrubs grow several arm-lengths apart, each rooted in a fracture where the soil is deeper than it appears. Their leaves are small and folded tightly along their central veins. By daylight they would be a dull gray-green; now they are only denser portions of the dark. One has a dead branch that extends almost horizontally over the slope. Its bark has worn away, leaving a smooth, pale length of wood still attached to the living stem.

Between the shrubs stands a tuft of dry grass. There is plenty of bare ground around it. Its old blades have curled without falling, and its base is packed with the remnants of earlier growth. Nothing here makes a continuous cover. Each plant occupies one small, sufficient place.

The rock has been weathered past sharpness. Its exposed edges are thick and rounded, its fractures blunted by the slow release of individual grains. Along the back wall, softer bands have recessed into shallow grooves. Harder bands project just enough to collect dust on their upper surfaces. These ledges run only a short distance before fading back into the wall.

There is no wet gleam anywhere. No drip has darkened the ceiling. The hollows in the floor contain dust rather than water, and the few white mineral seams are dry all the way across. Near the entrance, an old stain marks a route once taken by runoff, but it is faint and interrupted. Sand has settled over most of it.

The little light available comes from the open plain. It reaches the mouth of the hollow without reaching its interior. From the back, the entrance is a low, dim shape, partly divided by the shrubs. Beyond it, even the plain has few features: a level stretch of ground, a slight rise, darkness above. There is nothing distant and bright for the eye to find.

Listening does not reveal much more. An occasional grain might loosen from the wall, but most do not. The grass is still. Sound made here seems conspicuous without becoming loud; the low ceiling returns it briefly, and the powder along the edges softens what remains.

The hollow offers no central object. Its largest feature is the bare floor, and even that slopes too gently to command attention. After a while, smaller differences become apparent: the greater warmth close to the back wall, the fine grit gathered behind one stone, the narrow space beneath the dead branch.

Everything has room around it. Nothing appears about to happen.
