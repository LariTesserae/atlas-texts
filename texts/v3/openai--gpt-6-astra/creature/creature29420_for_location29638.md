A second stone comes down, smaller than the first. It strikes the shelf and skitters off.

Something above the shelf draws back.

It has been spread against the underside of an overhang, where the upward-moving dust has made it the same color as the rock. What first separates it from the surface is a narrow joint folding the wrong way for a fracture. Then several points of contact loosen in succession. A body lowers between them, held close to the wall.

It waits through a tremor.

The first stone remains where it stopped.

Only then does the creature descend.

Its reach is much greater than its size. The central body would fit on the raised slabs in the enclosure, but its limbs, extended, could touch both walls. There are six of them, though the foremost pair are short and usually folded beneath a blunt leading edge. The other four end in clusters of hard, unequal digits. These do not close together into hands. Each finds its own purchase, spreading into little faults and catching behind crystal lips. A foot can remain fixed while the limb above it changes its angle almost completely.

It places two feet on the shelf and keeps the others on the wall.

The surface beneath its feet is bare and seamless. Unable to obtain a hold, it lowers its body until its underside nearly touches the stone. It stays that way long enough for the faint rising heat to become visible around its edges.

Then it brings down the remaining limbs.

Its back is covered with overlapping plates, neither as regular as scales nor as massive as pieces of rock. Some are broad and dull. Others are new, with thin amber margins. Fine dust occupies the joints between them. When the body bends, the dust opens into dark lines and closes again.

One plate is missing from its flank. The exposed surface has a dense, fibrous sheen, and the plates around it are turned slightly inward. The creature holds that side away from the hotter ground.

There is no neck. At one end, a low shield projects over a recess containing the short limbs and several pale folds. Two narrow slits lie far apart along the shield’s rim. They are covered by a transparent material scratched almost opaque. Nothing behind them moves in a way that makes them unmistakably eyes.

Its attention is easier to see in its feet.

Three remain planted while the fourth lifts and touches down farther away. The digits open, settle, lift again. Occasionally the entire body becomes still, with each limb under a different tension. It seems to be comparing what reaches it through separate pieces of stone.

The little stone it dropped is still near the edge.

The creature approaches it, but does not pick it up. One short limb unfolds and rests beside it. The end of this limb is finer than the walking feet: two hooked points enclosing a pad crossed by shallow grooves.

For a while, pad and stone lie together on the shelf.

Then the creature leaves them both behind.

It goes toward the saddle by an indirect route, keeping to the older margins of slabs rather than crossing their centers. Where there is no safe purchase, it reaches beneath a ledge before committing its weight above. It can hang from two limbs, but prefers four. At every crossing there is a moment when its body is stretched so thin between supports that the soft seams under its armor show.

One hind foot has fewer digits than the other. The shortened ends are smooth. On clean stone that foot slips more often, and the creature compensates without haste, shifting another contact before trying again.

It is not at home here in the sense of being safe.

At the first hot seam, it stops. The pale structures beneath its front shield unfold a little into the escaping gas. They resemble closely packed leaves cut from very thin horn. Their edges tremble, though the heavier body remains motionless.

A fresh pulse of heat comes through the opening.

The folds withdraw so quickly that their disappearance looks like a blink. The creature backs away, dragging its damaged foot for half a step. A white crystal breaks under the foot and leaves a bright scratch across its sole.

It chooses a cooler seam farther along.

Here it settles.

The lower plates of its body part, exposing a narrow strip of dark, closely furrowed tissue. It presses this strip against the stone beside the opening, not across the opening itself. The little forelimbs scrape away the sharpest deposits. Some fragments are pushed aside; a few are brought under the shield.

There is almost no motion afterward.

Dust rises past its flanks. The plates gradually lift from one another, making the body appear thicker. Under their edges, small recessed surfaces darken and become faintly lustrous. No vapor leaves them.

It could stay in this position for hours. It could have been doing so in the recess above the shelf long before either stone fell.

When it finally moves, a gray trace remains on the rock. The trace is broken into parallel lines corresponding to the furrows beneath its body. Along one edge is a wet-looking black speck, already dulling in the dry current.

Below the enclosures, the creature finds the sheltered film.

It does not graze across it. It places its front shield close to the branching margin and works at one small patch with the grooved pads. The action is delicate, interrupted by long pauses. A flake comes free. The creature turns it, holds it beneath its body, then presses it into a hairline crack a little farther along the recess.

Most of the flake crumbles.

What remains is a black point in a seam that was bare.

The creature rests over it for a time before moving on.

There are more such points under its armor. They occupy sheltered folds near the roots of the limbs, where a little warmth persists even when the outer plates are cold. Some are no larger than pinheads; one has spread into a branching stain. Whether the creature carries the growth deliberately or merely makes places where it can survive is difficult to tell. Its short limbs attend to both with the same slow, exact movements they use to clear crystals from a feeding seam.

At the enclosures, it rises higher on its legs.

The intact shelter is too narrow to enter without folding them. It touches the lintel, the wedges beneath it, and the upper edges of both walls. One loose piece rocks under a digit. The creature holds it until the tremor passing through the ledge has ended.

Then it takes the piece out.

For the first time, it carries something more than a few inches.

It brings the stone to the entrance and sets it beside the surviving row. The new piece is larger and does not sit steadily. A forelimb rotates it twice. A walking foot braces the wall while the other short limb works a chip beneath the stone’s lower edge.

The arrangement becomes firm.

Inside, the raised sleeping place has a pale, shallow hollow along one side. The creature lowers itself there. Its body does not quite match the hollow. One limb must remain folded against the wall, and the damaged foot projects beyond the slabs.

The shelter may have been made for something smaller. Or the creature may once have been smaller itself.

Its back plates settle. The pale folds disappear beneath the shield. For several minutes the only visible movement is a slow adjustment at the roots of its limbs.

Then, from somewhere under the floor, comes a single dull knock.

The creature is standing before the second knock.

It does not rush outside. It braces itself between the walls and waits, lifting its feet one at a time from the sleeping slabs. Through the doorway, the stones in the row begin to move. One rocks in place. Another turns slightly. The newly wedged piece stays still.

A crack opens beneath the raised bed.

The creature goes out through the doorway, taking nothing. Behind it, one end of a sleeping slab drops by the width of a finger. The movement is small, but enough to break the hollow where its body had rested.

Outside, it tests the ledge toward the towers and withdraws its foot. It tests toward the basin and stays there longer.

At last it starts back toward the fresh shelf.

The first small stone is still on it.

The creature lowers itself beside the stone, leaving enough room to retreat up the wall. Beneath its body, the short limbs unfold. They touch the warm surface, clear away a few grains that have arrived since it left, and begin probing for a seam.
