For a while, the only sign of her is that the leaves beneath a fallen tree are being drawn inward.

They move a little, stop, then move again. Something underneath is pushing against them without yet coming out. A beetle climbs clear of the disturbance. Through a gap appears a narrow muzzle, dusty around the nostrils, followed by a forehead on which the overlapping edges catch what little light there is.

She waits with most of her body still inside the hollow.

The air outside carries information the hollow does not: the fruit on the ground, the animal standing over it, an old passage of snake, damp soil exposed by a digging foot. Her nostrils open and close. There is no breeze to sort these things into a direction. She turns her head slowly, bringing one side of her face near the ground, then the other.

When she comes out, she seems at first too heavily covered to bend.

Large scales lie over her shoulders and back. Their edges are worn smooth where she has passed beneath roots, and some have shallow chips that have darkened with age. Dust fills the narrow spaces between them. The armor continues down a tail almost as long as the rest of her, thick at its base and carried just clear of the leaves.

But the stiffness is an impression made by the surface. Beneath it, she folds easily around the lip of the hollow. Her back lengthens, her shoulders narrow, and her forefeet come down turned inward, keeping the long digging claws out of the way. The hind feet follow with a soft, deliberate placement.

A second head rises above the root.

It belongs to the young one lying across the base of her tail.

His scales are smaller, their edges less distinct. He has been pressed so closely against her that he looked like an irregularity in her back. Now he lifts his muzzle and tests the air with an exaggerated version of her movement. His forefeet tighten when she steps down from the root. For a moment his tail hangs loose; then he draws it alongside hers.

She does not turn to check him. The adjustment in his weight is enough.

Their first movement is not toward the water.

She follows the underside of the fallen trunk, pausing at places that appear identical. Here the wood is hard beneath its rotten skin. Here it is occupied by ants whose nest she has opened before. Farther along, near a fork half sunk in soil, termites have extended a covered passage across an exposed strip of timber.

She puts her nose almost against it.

The passage is no thicker than a small twig, built from particles gathered out of the surrounding decay. Inside, bodies continue in both directions. Her claw breaks a short section. She withdraws her foot and places her mouth at the breach.

The tongue appears too quickly to follow well. It enters, returns, enters again. Termites gather at the broken margins. Some attempt to repair them. Others fasten their jaws to the soft skin around her mouth. She closes her eyes while feeding, and once rubs her face against the wood.

The young one leans forward until his chest rests against her back.

For several minutes this is the extent of the disturbance: a damaged strip of earthen casing, the repeated movement of her jaw, a few insects falling onto the leaves. Then she leaves it. Much of the colony remains inside the trunk. By daylight the passage will be closed again, with a slight bulge where the new material joins the old.

She has feeding places scattered through the channels and across the wooded backs between them. Some are no longer useful. A stump she visits out of habit has been empty for weeks; another has become occupied since she last tested it. She does not make a complete circuit. Each night’s travel is altered by what she finds.

The dry weather has lengthened the distance.

Near the surface, galleries that used to contain food are vacant. She must dig farther into banks or climb to reach nests in trees whose roots still draw water. Tonight she chooses a leaning trunk. Her claws settle into the bark, and the tail that seemed burdensome on the ground curves against the wood and braces her.

As she climbs, her scales lift slightly along the bend of her body. Briefly, the paler skin beneath becomes visible.

The young one flattens himself. He has learned this much.

Above the trunk’s first division, she stops at a knot where ants enter beneath dead bark. A gecko moves around the far side. She opens the seam with one claw, patiently, without tearing off the whole sheet. Fragments drop through the foliage. Far below, something investigates the sound of their landing.

She is not the largest animal using this tree, nor the strongest. Most of what she meets cannot hurt her. Some things can. On the ground she carries an old injury in one hind foot: the toes spread unevenly, and on soft mud that print is wider than the other. It does not prevent her climbing, but she tests uncertain holds with the sound foot first.

By the time she reaches the pool, the heavy animal beneath the fruit tree has gone.

She descends the bank diagonally, using exposed roots. At the drying shelf she stops long enough for the young one to shift higher. Her muzzle touches the surface. Small movements spread outward and disappear against the dark sides.

She drinks for a long time.

Mud adheres to the scales beneath her chin. When she raises her head, a thread of water breaks and falls between her forefeet. The young one reaches down, cannot reach far enough, and clambers awkwardly off her tail. For the first time that night he stands beside her.

Without her beneath him, he is unexpectedly small.

He noses the mud before finding the water. She remains still while he drinks, her body between him and the open shelf. A frog withdraws beneath a root. Something minute taps against the water beside his mouth, and he jerks back, then approaches again.

Across the flats, the machinery becomes audible.

Her head rises.

For a few breaths she holds it in the posture she uses when a distant footfall reaches her through the ground. But the sound does not approach. It has no intervals in which another step might fall, no accompanying scrape or displacement of leaves. She turns toward it, then away. Her nose lowers to the shelf.

The young one climbs onto her by the wrong route, pulling himself over her foreleg. She waits through this too.

Before dawn she returns to cover, though not to the hollow from which she emerged. There is a cavity beneath a bank where an old root has rotted away. Its entrance is screened by fern stems. Inside, the clay retains a coolness absent from the air.

She enters headfirst and turns with several slow adjustments, scraping loose soil beneath herself. The young one slips against her belly. Her tail curves around them, bringing its armored outer surface toward the opening.

For a while his muzzle remains visible in the gap. It moves against her, searching, then becomes still.

Outside, ants find the crumbs of disturbed earth. One travels over the edge of her tail and continues into a fissure in the bank. Her breathing moves a fern stem by less than the width of its own shadow.

On the pool’s shelf, her uneven footprints hold a little water.
