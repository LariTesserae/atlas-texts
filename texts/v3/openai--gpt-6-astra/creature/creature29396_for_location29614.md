At noon it lives on the underside of the shelter’s roof.

There is so little of it to see that you might mistake it for something the rain has left behind: a smoky patch, a thickening of the wetness near one corner. Its edges are almost transparent. Through the middle, where its organs lie flattened together, the gray has a faint amber depth.

Water passes through the roof and through the spaces between its ribs. The ribs are not enclosed. They open and close independently, very slowly, keeping its soft inner surfaces washed.

It has been there all morning.

Once, when a grass seed lands on the roof above it, two dark points move within the patch. They travel farther apart, then come together beneath the seed. A hand unfastens from the underside. The arm follows, hanging down for a surprising distance, and reaches around the edge.

The seed is brought underneath. For several minutes it is worked against a mouth too small to distinguish. The empty husk drops into the depression in the floor.

Then the arm draws itself back into the wetness.

***

It descends when the shadow of the western wall reaches the nearest leg.

This takes time. First its back thickens. The exposed ribs draw inward, each seating itself in a groove along the body. Two limbs release their grip and dangle. Their ends are broad, with narrow divisions that only become fingers when they touch something.

The head comes away last. It has been pressed sideways against the roof, and remains crooked for a while after the rest of the creature has resumed its depth.

Standing, it reaches about to a person’s knee.

It is neither frail nor sturdy in any familiar way. There are places where it seems held together by wet skin alone, particularly at the throat and in the long passage between shoulder and elbow. Elsewhere it is densely built. The hips contain several pale plates that slide over one another when it walks. Its feet are short and strong, with a pad at the heel and two opposed gripping edges. It can set a foot flat on stone, or close it around an irregularity no wider than a knife cut.

It takes three steps across the shelter floor and stops.

For a moment it does nothing but stand in its own shape.

Then it touches the side of its head, pushes gently until something settles, and goes to the channel.

Its skin is the color of water over dark rock, except where it has recently stretched. Those places are pearl-white. One shoulder bears a healed tear. A fine fringe has grown along the scar, and droplets collect there that do not collect on the other shoulder.

Its eyes are small, black, and rather far forward. They do not give it an innocent expression. It looks closely at things. Often it looks again.

***

The channel is both feeding place and difficulty.

It braces upstream of a pebble and lowers its mouth into the current. Its lips spread against the stone. After a while the dark coating on the pebble is gone, leaving a clean oval.

It works along the shallows this way, taking mineral film, soft growth, whatever has lodged on the downstream sides of obstructions. Occasionally it finds something substantial enough to hold in both hands. Then it retreats beneath a leaf and eats without putting the food down.

The ground injures it often. There are cuts in its foot pads, sealed with a cloudy secretion. It tests each new stretch of paste with its fingers before trusting its weight to it. A blade of gravel that points upward is turned flat. A loose flake is carried to the edge of the route.

Over many days this has made a path between the shelter and the thicket. The path is no wider than two fingers in places. It must be repaired each evening because the floor keeps changing beneath it.

Near the eastern seam there is an older path, interrupted by a ridge of new stone. The creature still follows it as far as the obstruction. It presses the ridge with one foot, sometimes with both hands. Then it goes around.

It has done this for long enough to polish a little hollow in the ridge.

***

It takes flowers as well as seeds.

Under the broad leaves, it lies on its back and reaches into the downward-facing blooms. A fingertip enters each cream-colored bell, stays a moment, and returns to its mouth. Pollen gathers along its wrist. When the flower is empty, it pulls the finger free with care.

There are blossoms it never touches. They look no different from the others.

Sometimes it falls asleep there, one hand still raised.

The six-footed animal on the wall will approach it in this position. It comes slowly, tasting the rock between steps, until its green scales are close enough to reflect in the creature’s open eye.

Neither moves.

Then the little animal’s shadow travels forward.

The creature withdraws its hand from the flower and lays it over its face. The animal continues past.

***

It knows the ring well enough to be afraid of it precisely.

Before a lifting, it sometimes goes to the rim and presses the inside of one wrist against the tarnished surface. Sometimes it merely watches from the channel. Whatever difference it detects is not apparent in the moving bands.

When it does enter, it goes on all fours.

It carries nothing loose. Its toes close hard on the stone outside the circumference while its body stretches across the interior. One hand remains hooked in a fissure. The other moves over the grit, selecting.

At the first loss of weight, its loose skin rises away from its back. The open spaces between its ribs widen. It looks abruptly larger, almost hollow.

Its free hand catches things as they lift.

Most are discarded: flakes, scraps of root, a drowned flower. A few go into a fold beneath its jaw. If a seed escapes upward, its head follows, but it does not release its hold.

Only once in several days does it put its whole body inside.

Then it rises slowly, turning with no apparent control until it opens its arms. The fingers spread. Thin webs draw taut between them, and the turn stops.

Suspended above the ring, it straightens in a way it never does on the ground. The long joints separate. Its head lifts clear of its shoulders. For a few breaths it has room within itself.

It rubs at the pale places on its skin. Small translucent scraps loosen and hang beside it.

Before weight returns, it folds tightly, feet downward.

It lands badly more often than not. One hand strikes first; the hips follow with a wet sound. It remains crouched until the fallen water has run away. Then it examines its fingers, one by one.

The next afternoon it may do the same thing.

***

There are things beneath the shelter that are not immediately visible.

A narrow stone sliver lies along one leg, held off the wet floor by two chips. Beneath the floor’s overhang is a packet of folded leaf. A dark, smooth object has been pushed into a crevice at the back.

The creature checks these when it returns.

The sliver is used to clear fresh mineral growth from one small section of the ring. It holds the tool awkwardly, in both hands, and scrapes until the old surface shows. It does not attempt to uncover the entire rim. The cleared patch is just large enough for its wrist.

The folded leaf contains food.

The smooth object is harder to account for. It is about the size of the creature’s head, brown-black, with a shallow groove around its middle. It may be a seed from somewhere below the cloud. It may be stone worn in a place where stones have time to become round.

On some evenings the creature pulls it out and sits with it against its belly.

It turns it so that the groove faces upward. Its thumbs travel around the groove, meeting at the far side. If water gathers there, it tips the object and empties it.

Then it holds it again.

***

When the mountain knocks, it is usually ready.

Its belly goes close to the ground. Its hands spread over the stone. The lids close across its eyes from below, slowly enough that the black points remain visible until the vibration has passed.

Afterward it visits the eastern seam.

It tastes the new strip with the tip of its tongue, moving along it in small increments. At one point it may stop and feed for a long time. At another it recoils, wiping its mouth on its wrist.

The soft stone bears marks afterward: a row of shallow, paired depressions.

By morning they have hardened.

There are older rows higher on the wall. Some are tiny. Some have almost disappeared beneath later growth. The creature cannot reach the highest ones now, even standing with its hands stretched above its head.

***

At night it does not always return to the roof.

When seeds have fallen, it stays out late, moving across the hollow with its face close to the paste. It finds them by touch as often as sight. Each is lifted, turned, tested against the lips.

One evening it finds a pale, folded thing beside the fern crack.

It holds it longer than it holds the seeds.

The thing opens along one side. Inside is a wet black point, then another. A limb no thicker than a root tip reaches out and fastens to its finger.

The larger creature becomes very still.

Eventually it brings its other hand underneath. It does not pull the little grip loose. It waits while the folded body works itself clear of its covering, a labor interrupted by long periods in which nothing appears to happen.

By then the rock is dark, though still warm.

It carries the small one to the shelter and climbs the leg with three limbs. Beneath the roof it spreads itself thin, ribs opening, back fastening to the gray underside. The little one remains tucked against the thick part near its throat.

Rain comes through.

A tiny hand opens in the falling water and closes on nothing. The larger hand moves beneath it.

In the morning there are two smoky patches under the roof, touching along one edge.
