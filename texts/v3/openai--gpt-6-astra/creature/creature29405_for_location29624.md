At knee height, below the stopped pipe, there is a pale shape that seems to belong to the plaster. It has the slight swelling of a repair made with too much mortar. Along its lower edge are four shallow divisions.

You would notice those divisions before you noticed that they were fingers.

They are not gripping anything. Each rests in a separate irregularity of the wall, fitted so exactly that there is no shadow underneath. The smallest is broad at its tip and narrow at the root. One has a crescent of clay embedded where a nail might be. The others are smooth.

Following them upward is difficult. What ought to be a wrist widens into a thin, fluted surface, then disappears behind the shape of the buried column. Farther up, something rounds away from the wall. It could be a shoulder. It could also be the place where the mason plastered over a projecting brick rather than knock it back.

The inhabitant is standing sideways.

That is the first useful thing you can understand about it.

Its body is very narrow in one direction and unexpectedly deep in the other. Seen from the entrance, it offers little more than an edge: forehead, breastbone, the folded front of a leg. Most of it extends into the shallow relief of the wall. It has accommodated a length of pipe beneath one arm. A hollow behind its knee receives the bulge of a lime lump. Its back is neither straight nor curved in any simple way, but shaped by a succession of things it has lived against.

It is not trapped. There is a difference, visible chiefly in the hand. Nothing in that hand is braced.

Its head is level with your lower ribs. At first it appears to have no face. Then the light reaching it by several reflections discloses a short, broad muzzle, closed against the chest. Two folds lie where eyes might be. Their edges are thick and rounded, like the pale leaves.

The eyes, when uncovered, are the color of the rest of it. You see them because their surfaces are wet.

This small wetness is startling. Nothing else here has a sheen.

It closes them again, slowly, without squeezing. A little of the pocket seems to have gone dry once more.

The white covering is not fur or scales. In places it resembles the skin over an old scar; elsewhere it has a fine, crowded grain, as though innumerable thin layers have been pressed together. Along the ribs, the oldest layers have become stiff. Younger material shows only at the joints, a grayish cream visible when a fold opens.

The plants grow closest to these openings. One short fan emerges beside its ankle. Another lies horizontally beneath its wrist. It is hard to determine whether the roots enter the mortar or the creature, and the distinction may be less important to either of them than it is to you. At the back wall, some of the hardened white leaves continue directly into the ridges of its heel.

It has been here long enough to acquire attachments.

Not all of them are living. A small piece of tile is held in a fold below its breast. Its edge has been polished by contact with the body. There is no wound around it, no attempt to expel it. The fragment seems to have become useful: a firm surface against which something soft can rest.

The creature is breathing, but not into the air.

A succession of tiny changes passes down its side. The fluted portion beneath the arm grows fractionally fuller; a seam at the hip deepens; one finger becomes rounder as another flattens. Its weight travels through it. Each place yields a little to the next, while the body as a whole remains still.

The roar conceals whatever sounds this makes.

Perhaps it also supplies something. There are thin plates behind the jaw, closely nested, with their free edges turned toward the masonry. They tremble too slightly to be seen directly. You discover the trembling in a narrow band of light that will not quite hold still.

The inhabitant keeps these plates against the wall as another animal might keep its nose near an opening.

It knows a great deal about what lies beyond the pocket. Not views, perhaps, or distances. It knows the long constriction by the pressures that arrive through the bricks. It knows which buried pieces carry the rush cleanly and which soften it. The fluted column beneath its arm may be a particularly good place to listen. The stopped pipe may give it something different.

When you speak, it lifts its head.

It does not face you. Instead, the four fingers separate from their places one after another and settle again, barely farther apart. The movement has the deliberation of someone laying a hand on a table to feel a distant footstep.

Your voice ends. The great sound continues.

For a while you become aware of your own unsuitable construction: elbows, heels, the hot damp fabric beneath your arms. Your foot covers several hollows. Your breathing reaches the wall and returns warmed. You cannot stand without excluding something.

The creature lowers its head, but it does not resume quite the same position. Its shoulder has drawn inward. A fold that was open is closed; the broad muzzle rests a little farther back.

There is now slightly more room beside it.

Not enough to turn comfortably. Enough, perhaps, to stay.
