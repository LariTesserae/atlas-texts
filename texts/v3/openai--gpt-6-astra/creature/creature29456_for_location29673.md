At first it resembles a piece of the building seen through the wrong thickness of water.

It is standing inside an upper doorway, one shoulder against the jamb. The shoulder has the same faint blue as the thicker ceramic. Its body is narrow enough that the darkness on either side seems to join through it. Only when it turns does it become apparent that the doorway has been occupied all along.

It does not come down immediately.

There is a small domestic inconvenience to attend to: something caught behind one of its knees. It reaches back, lifts a fold of itself, and works the obstruction loose with two fingers. The object is pale and curved. It examines it, rubs it against its wrist, and puts it into a niche beside the door.

Then it descends the stairs.

It uses every tread.

***

The inhabitant is a little shorter than a person, though its proportions make that difficult to judge until it stands beside one of the shoulder-height compartments. Its legs are long, its trunk shallow, its head set low between structures that might be shoulders or the folded bases of additional limbs.

There is nothing soft on it in the way a mammal is soft. Nevertheless, it is plainly not made of the town’s ceramic. When it bends, pale plates travel over one another. Narrow seams open between them, showing a translucent substance that draws into threads and reunites. A little of the blue comes from these seams. Most comes from somewhere deeper.

Its feet have backward-projecting heels. On the stairs, the heels settle into the angle between tread and riser; on level paving they lift slightly, leaving the body balanced over the fronts of its feet. The soles divide into several broad, flexible contacts. These take hold without a sound.

It could probably swim. There are folded surfaces along its ribs, and occasionally one opens enough to reveal a fine supporting comb. But it walks, keeping to the paving even where a low wall would be easy to cross.

Its arms end in hands with three long fingers and an opposing pad. The fingers have no nails. Each tip can spread into a little cup, useful for holding something without pinching it. When empty, the hands close into neat wedges.

The face is harder to find.

What first appears to be the front of its head is a smooth, slightly convex plate. Below it, where a throat ought to be, is a crescent of dark tissue. The crescent contracts as a visitor approaches. Farther back, on either side of the head, two cloudy patches become transparent.

It has been looking before its eyes become visible.

There is no mouth that can be identified from outside.

***

It lives in the building with the unfinished courtyard.

One room contains several objects laid on the floor at considerable distances from one another: a broken latch, three overlapping flakes of black rock, a short length of metallic strip bent twice, and something like a shallow spoon with a perforated bowl. None is displayed. There are slight scuffs around them, the first marks in the settlement that seem to come from repeated use rather than manufacture.

A visitor might put the objects together to examine them.

The inhabitant would put them apart again.

It does this without haste or displeasure. The broken latch goes near the wall. The spoon returns to a paving joint. The three flakes are separated, inspected along their edges, and overlapped in their previous order.

Afterward it rests a hand on the floor between them.

There are things about this arrangement that it can feel.

In another room is a low frame fitted to the length of its body. It lies there with its heels hooked underneath and its hands tucked beneath the plates of its chest. It does not shut its eyes; the cloudy layer returns across them. Over the course of several hours, the seams along its ribs widen and narrow once.

Nothing else in the town has so distinct a rhythm.

***

It is growing.

The pale thing removed from behind its knee was part of an old joint surface. More lie in the niche upstairs: thin crescents, translucent cups, strips curled tightly enough to look like tiny bones. Some are much smaller than the others.

There is no orderly progression. A very small piece rests on top of a broad one. Two have been fitted together, though they cannot have come from the same joint. One has a hole rubbed through its center.

The inhabitant handles these pieces occasionally. It holds them against its present body, tries an edge along a finger, puts them down.

Its growth is uneven. The left arm is slightly longer than the right. A plate near one hip no longer overlaps its neighbor properly, leaving a permanently exposed line of blue. When it passes through a doorway, it turns to protect that side.

Nothing in the settlement grows to accommodate it.

The low frame is already too short.

***

It visits the public basin often.

Before approaching the rim, it lowers itself to the paving and looks underneath. It examines the two ring-shaped animals without touching either. Usually this takes only a moment. Sometimes it stays there a long while, one cheek almost against the stone.

The animals give no visible acknowledgment.

It knows where each of them is. If the smaller one has moved by less than the width of a finger, the inhabitant adjusts its own position accordingly. A visitor’s lamp makes it interpose a hand, shading them with the spread cups of its fingertips.

Then it rises and lowers both hands into the basin.

The resistant boundary dimples around its wrists. Its chest plates draw closer together. For a while, the whole body seems narrower, as though it were exerting a steady force.

It never quite reaches the white object at the bottom.

Sometimes it withdraws quickly and holds one hand with the other. Sometimes it remains until the cloudy patches have spread across its eyes, then pulls free with an abruptness that lifts its feet off the paving.

Once out, it carefully examines each fingertip.

Whatever this activity is, it has not mastered it.

There is no sign that it considers stopping.

***

It understands some parts of the town very well.

It can open the latches along the wall, including one that looks sealed into its frame. It takes nothing from most of the compartments. In one, it places its hand flat against the back and waits. In another, it turns sideways and brings the exposed seam of its hip close to the opening.

It avoids the corner posts.

This avoidance is practical and precise. It will pass near a post, touch its base, or retrieve something from behind it, but no part of its body crosses the circular opening. If a visitor reaches toward one, the inhabitant catches the wrist.

The grip is unexpectedly strong.

It holds on until the hand has moved away. Then it examines the wrist where it touched it, spreading the visitor’s fingers one at a time.

Perhaps it has never held that kind of hand before.

The entrance lamps appear to mean nothing to it. It has hung one of its shed plates inside the hollow of the lamp above its home. The plate fits badly. One edge protrudes.

***

In the smallest building, it chooses the middle seat.

Its heels fit the recesses, though not comfortably. It must draw its feet farther back than they naturally rest. Seated this way, with its hands on its knees, it looks younger.

It faces the blank wall.

After a while, it places one finger in the dark slot beside the seat. The finger disappears to the first joint. Nothing lights up. No mechanism moves.

Its uncovered eyes become very clear.

It withdraws the finger and looks toward the seat on its left.

This is not always the same sequence. On another visit it might choose the right-hand seat, leave the slot untouched, and turn repeatedly toward the door. Once, with a visitor present, it gets up and presses the visitor down into the middle seat. It then sits on the left.

There is a long interval.

The inhabitant looks at the wall, at the visitor, at the wall again.

Eventually it leaves.

It does not try to make the visitor understand.

***

There are no small versions of its kind in the houses. No bodies. No pictures that would establish what an adult ought to look like.

Its skills suggest instruction, but so do many things learned by repetition. The care with which it walks the stairs could be a household rule. It could be the consequence of a fall.

It is neither solemn nor especially gentle.

It worries at the hinged stone flake in the courtyard, trying to break it loose. It uses the bent metallic strip as a lever, pauses to inspect the hinge, then tries from another angle. When the strip slips, it strikes the slab once with the heel of its hand.

The hard click vanishes into the water.

Later it returns and begins again.

It carries objects to the edge of the square and holds them close to the vertical boundary. A black flake goes dark against a distant snowfield. The perforated spoon makes a small arrangement of mountains in its holes.

It does not let anything fall through.

When a visitor leaves an object unattended, the inhabitant may take it upstairs. It has no apparent sense that the visitor’s equipment constitutes a special class of possession. Nor does it conceal what it has taken. It can be found sitting on the landing, testing a buckle against one of its old joint plates.

It dislikes having these experiments interrupted.

***

The terrace seat is too broad for it.

It sits near one end, heels drawn up, one hand braced against the sharp edge. From there it can see a valley through the water’s face: a dark division between ridges, sometimes filled with cloud.

When cloud rises, it leans forward.

The motion is slight at first. Then the folded surfaces along its ribs lift away from its body. Fine pale combs spread through the still water. Its hands flatten on the seat.

It watches until the valley is hidden.

There is no way to know whether it has seen this happen a thousand times or whether each occurrence demands the same complete attention.

When the cloud thins, it remains forward on the seat for a little longer. Gradually the combs close. One catches against the ill-fitting plate at its hip, and it reaches down to free it.

Before returning indoors, it usually touches the underside of the shallow roof.

Its fingers explore the same short stretch of surface, just above the place where it sits. There is a faint roughness there, almost invisible even under a lamp. It rubs the roughness with the opposing pad of its hand.

Then it checks its fingertips.

Then it goes home.
