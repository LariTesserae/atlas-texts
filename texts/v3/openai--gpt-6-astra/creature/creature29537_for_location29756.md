At first you take it for something growing along the back of a recess.

There is a pale thickness beneath the overhanging roots, a fold with the dull gloss of a mushroom. It follows the wall for several feet, widens where the recess widens, and disappears into a seam too narrow to admit your fingers. Water covers its lower edge. Through the water you can see a row of small, blunt projections, evenly spaced.

You have passed several things like it. What makes you look again is the way this one rests.

The broad middle portion lies heavily on the shelf. Its ends are supported farther back, out of sight, leaving a slight sag between the points of contact. There is a crease where its weight has gathered against the wall. A plant could have grown into that position. But the whole arrangement has the particular economy of a body that has found somewhere comfortable.

Then you distinguish the underside.

It is folded inward, concealing a softer surface densely set with short transparent filaments. Each filament ends in a flattened oval. Some lie against the stone; others stand free in the water. They are neither roots nor toes, though they give you something of the impression of both. Behind them, beneath the pale outer skin, a dark band divides and rejoins.

Nothing moves through the band. Nothing twitches when your shadow crosses it.

The exposed portion might be an animal the length of your arm. It might be the edge of a much larger animal.

You go around the wall.

In the neighboring chamber, a similar fold descends from the ceiling and rests in a basin. Here the skin is brown above and cream beneath. A long, shallow depression runs along its upper surface, holding clear water. At one end, where the fold turns into the wall, there is a cluster of small dark pits.

Their arrangement is difficult to disregard. They occupy the position from which a resting creature would look out into the chamber.

There is no head around them. The body simply becomes a little thicker there, with more substance between the outer skin and whatever supports it underneath. The pits face several directions. One faces the basin. Two face the entrance. Another is turned almost entirely back into the narrow space between the body and the wall.

You lean sideways to see that one.

From your new position, you can see daylight inside it.

Not a reflection. There is depth, a strip of wet paving, the foot of a split column. Something pale rests beneath the column, much too far away to distinguish.

You straighten. The pit is dark again.

***

The inhabitants are easiest to find at the edges of rooms.

Their bodies occupy the places you initially accepted as incidental: the rounded thickening beneath a sill, the soft roll where a wall meets a flooded floor, a cream-colored lip around an opening. Some are exposed for no more than an inch. Elsewhere, broad portions lie in plain view, stretched between buttresses or draped over the backs of basins.

They do not have the grain of the city. The distinction is subtle but consistent. The living architecture divides into fibers, plates, pores. These bodies have a fine, nearly invisible pebbled skin, and beneath that skin their substance seems continuous. Even their thinnest edges are opaque.

Their coloring follows where they lie. Exposed surfaces are dark, sometimes nearly green. Sheltered folds are pale. A few have purple undersides, the color of the leaves overhead, but without veins.

You never find a complete outline.

In one court, something like a shoulder emerges from the paving, supports a flattened length of body, and returns beneath an arch. You follow it into the next district. There it is much narrower, although nothing along the intervening stretch appeared to taper. Farther on, it divides into three broad folds, each resting in a separate chamber.

The chambers are on different levels. You can see all three through the same doorway.

There is no reason to decide that they belong to one creature, except that the folds meet. There is no reason to decide that they belong to three, except that each possesses the same convincing air of being at rest by itself.

After a while, counting them begins to feel like counting the coves of a lake.

Yet they have individual features.

One bears a pale crescent of old damage across its exposed back. The skin has closed smoothly, leaving a shallow notch at the margin. You find that notch again beside the eastern falls, on a portion of body scarcely wider than your hand.

Another has rows of dark pits set unusually close together. A third is mottled with tiny cream spots, including on surfaces that have never received daylight.

You begin to recognize them without knowing how many there are.

***

The small projections beneath their bodies fit the recesses in the stone.

That is what the comfortable ledges are for. Not sitting in the way you had imagined, although sitting is still the nearest comparison. The creatures settle their fringed undersides into the shallow basins. Their thicker folds rest along the shelves. The hanging roots pass through spaces between parts of them, sometimes vanishing beneath a pale lip and emerging several feet away.

There is no pressure mark, no rubbing, no sign of repeated occupation. The fit has none of the perfection of a mold. Here a body overhangs its support; there a generous stretch of shelf remains empty. One creature lies across a channel with the awkward ease of an arm laid over the side of a bath.

The rooms are comfortable because something is comfortable in them.

You place your palm on an exposed fold.

It is cooler than the paving, though still warm. The skin gives slightly. Beneath it is a depth of yielding resistance, with no bone close enough to feel.

Through your hand, the roar changes.

A low part of the sound separates from the rest. You feel its fine irregularities in your wrist and forearm, a dense, continuous tremor. When you lift your hand, it disappears into the general concussion. When you touch the body again, it is there.

Different folds carry different parts of the noise. The spotted one has a harsh, close vibration. The one with the crescent scar transmits something so deep that you sense it chiefly in your elbow. Beneath the great arcades, where their bodies run through walls on either side, the sound presses against you from more directions than the room seems to possess.

They have no visible mouths.

Along the submerged undersides, the transparent filaments spread against the white roots. There are no punctures. Nothing has been bitten or drained. Where a root passes between two flattened tips, all three remain clear and whole.

You cannot tell what passes between them.

***

Their attention is harder to recognize than their bodies.

No dark pit turns toward you. No fold contracts at your touch. Nevertheless, certain places offer a view of you with a persistence that gradually becomes intimate.

You notice it while crossing a small court.

In a cluster of pits beneath a low arch, you see your own legs reflected. You stop. The image is sharp enough to show the wet hem of your clothing.

But it shows the backs of your legs.

You turn and find another exposed portion of the creature on the far side of the court. Its pits face you. Between the two portions stand a basin, a thick column, and the closed wall of a chamber.

Later, from an upper gallery, you see yourself below.

The figure is small and plainly occupied with looking down from a gallery. You raise one hand, and so does the figure. Nothing is delayed. Nothing anticipates you. The city has simply provided another direction from which you can be seen.

The creature lies along the gallery’s outer edge, its pale underside tucked beneath the parapet.

You sit beside it.

There is sufficient room. There has always been sufficient room, but now the unoccupied portion of the ledge seems specific. Your feet reach a lower shelf. Your back rests against a warm concavity. One of the creature’s blunt fringes lies in the water beside your ankle.

You stay there longer than you intended.

***

There are small ones, or small portions of them.

They occupy hollows among the plants at the brink: folded, translucent bodies no larger than a fingernail, each with a dark interior and a few flattened filaments laid against the wet rind. Some have only a single pit. In one, you can see a tiny court with three cream-colored basins.

None is feeding. None is budding from a larger body. You find no eggs, abandoned coverings, or empty places shaped by death.

The scarred inhabitant may once have been hurt. The little folded things may be young. These are reasonable possibilities, and the city gives you nowhere to take them further.

By the time you return to the cliff, you can distinguish pale backs among the terraces. There is one beneath the fallen trunk. Another follows the inner curve of a distant arcade. Far below, through an opening that ought to show only mountain air, a broad mottled fold rests in sunlight.

The place is densely occupied.

Beside your knee, the droplet still hangs from its leaf. The terraces remain upside down inside it. Along the minute bright cliff is a pale line you had mistaken for exposed stone.

At this distance, you cannot see its eyes.

You shift a little on the ledge, making yourself comfortable without putting your weight on it.
