At the place where the back wall bends into one of the rounded shoulders, a little of the dust has failed to settle.

It is a small difference. The projecting bands of stone hold their usual pale accumulation, but beneath one of them there is a strip the color of the rock before it weathers. It runs sideways, narrows, and disappears into a fold. In better light it might look like a patch recently uncovered by the fall of a flake.

The flake is still there.

Its lower edge rests against the wall. Above it, something thicker occupies the groove: a succession of shallow folds, each tucked partly beneath the next. They have the blunted edges of the surrounding stone, though their surfaces are finer. Loose grains adhere to them without seeming embedded. There is no obvious front or back.

This is most of the animal that can be seen.

The rest fits into the space behind it, which is deeper than the shadow suggests. Its body is broad and remarkably soft. Supported by rock, it spreads into a thin pad; where a fracture permits, it thickens, filling the available depth. The folds are not armor. They are the spare material of a body that can gather itself into a narrow passage and then unfold against a larger surface.

It is roughly the length of a forearm. Its width changes.

One end has a crease that does not follow the bedding of the stone. Close beside that crease are two small dark areas, matte rather than glossy, nearly covered by the forward fold. These distinguish light from its absence. They would not resolve the shrubs at the entrance, or a person standing between them. A person’s arrival would reach it first through the wall and floor: the changing pressure of steps, then the warmth of a body interrupting the slow exchange between stone and air.

Its underside is more elaborate than its visible surface. There are many short, pale feet, arranged in uneven rows, with soft ends that fit over individual grains. Each foot holds a little before the next one moves. The animal can cross the ceiling without releasing dust. When it comes to a crack, the leading feet enter it and spread, and the body follows with a gradual rearrangement of folds.

The apparent stillness is partly a matter of pace. If watched for an hour, it might seem to have done nothing. Seen again the next evening, it would occupy another groove.

It has preferred places.

The warm bend in the wall is one. A shallow indentation beneath the ceiling is another, though the whole of its body does not fit there. In that place its rear edge remains exposed, hanging down like a thick curl of weathered bark. It returns there often enough that the stone beneath it is cleaner than the neighboring stone.

It also visits the underside of the pale dead branch.

Getting there requires a descent along the shoulder of the hollow, a passage through a fracture near the entrance, and a short crossing over the living shrub. It makes this journey only under particular conditions. The wood must have lost the day’s hard surface heat without yet becoming cold. The air must carry a little moisture. On such nights, it lies lengthwise beneath the branch, its folds drawn close, its feet holding the smooth wood.

There, the crease at its forward end opens.

The mouth is a short, soft seam. A fine surface extends from it and presses against the wood, then draws back. It feeds on what grows too thinly to change the branch’s appearance: minute films, wind-carried fragments caught in them, the sparse life that briefly becomes active when the air permits. A visit may yield very little. It can wait a long time for another.

Its wastes are equally slight, dry grains released among the grains already moving out of the rock. It does not keep a nest or bring anything home. The hollow contains no possessions by which to recognize it.

Nevertheless, its passage has altered the place. Several narrow routes beneath the overhang are faintly smoother than they would otherwise be. In one recess, dust forms a crescent around a space repeatedly occupied. A tiny projection has been worn down where its body turns.

There is a smaller one farther back.

It lives in a seam the larger animal can no longer enter. Its folds are fewer and less evenly colored; its feet sometimes find purchase badly, dislodging a grain. The sound is brief and distinct in the hollow. Afterward it stays still for a long time.

The two do not lie together. On the rare occasions when their routes meet, the larger one stops while the smaller travels partly over its edge. Several feet test the unfamiliar surface. The larger body contracts a little under their pressure and then relaxes. When the smaller has passed, both continue.

For now, neither is moving far.

The smaller one is held within its seam. The larger rests in the warm bend, with most of its feet released and its weight supported by the lower fold. Near the mouth, a patch of skin slowly tightens, then loosens. The motion is too shallow to disturb the dust caught along its edge.

Outside, the plain is losing heat. Eventually that change will reach the back wall. The animal will gather itself a little more closely into the groove.

Until then, the place it has chosen is sufficient.
