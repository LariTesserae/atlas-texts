What lives there is easiest to find just after the discharge stops.

Not immediately. At first there is too much water, and everything belongs to the same moving surface. You have to wait until the bolt heads reappear and the four slopes separate again, until the last broad sheet draws itself into channels. Then, in the corner that was meant to stay dry, a little of the wetness fails to withdraw.

It has a thicker edge than water ought to have. Cloudlight catches it in a short, dull line.

That is the nearest thing it has to a back.

Most of it lies beneath the flashing. There is very little clearance there: the thickness of a badly seated screw, a slight bow in the steel between fixings. Its body occupies this error. It is flattened almost to transparency, pale gray where it doubles over itself, otherwise taking its color from whatever is beneath it. Fine grit shows through. A curl of metal lies embedded near one edge, its rust making a small amber inclusion.

It did not put the metal there. It grew around it.

At the exposed margin, the body divides into short, blunt lobes. These fit into the roughness of the concrete, and within each lobe are smaller divisions that cannot be seen without bringing your face very close to the floor. They hold by contact. There are no claws, no suckers, nothing that would leave a recognizable print if the creature were lifted away.

It probably could not be lifted away whole.

Its outer substance is much tougher than its appearance suggests, but only in certain directions. Water can strike it hard without harm. A sideways pull would tear it. It can endure the pressure of the brief flood because there is almost nowhere for the pressure to get underneath. A dry thumb rubbed across the corner would destroy a considerable part of it.

No thumb has had a reason to go there.

It has no head. Nevertheless, one end is more engaged with the world than the other.

This end reaches toward the screw where the backward-running thread passes beneath the flashing. It forms a narrow cuff around the wet underside of the steel, not quite completing the circle. The water travels through that opening. The cuff thickens when the mist is heavy and shrinks when the fans draw drier air across the roof.

Farther in, where the flashing presses close to the wall, the creature becomes opaque. Its folds are packed together there, holding fluid. A person dismantling the corner might mistake them for a little sealant that had failed to cure. This is its protected part. Everything spread across the concrete can be lost, provided those folds remain damp.

It eats what the water leaves against it.

There is more than the court appears to offer. The mist carries minute fragments from elsewhere in the plant, and the wind delivers things too small to be noticed crossing the walls. Some catch in the creature’s surface. Some begin to live there. Its skin is not cleanly distinguishable from the film it grazes upon: an outer softness continually breaking down and being drawn inward, continually renewed. Along a well-watered edge, under magnification, the whole arrangement would look less like a skin than a crowded shore.

Its growth is slow enough that a shift can pass without anything changing. Yet it does move.

When the side channel begins to fill, a faint tightening travels along the lobes on the floor. Their margins draw inward. The creature becomes narrower, more firmly gathered beneath the flashing, before the first surge reaches the corner.

It feels the valve through the building.

The roughness that comes up through the concrete reaches every part of its body at once. There is no intervening distance between hearing and touch. The fans, the pipe, the machinery below are all present as pressure, each with its own persistence. Within those pressures, the discharge has a recognizable beginning.

At first it must have endured the flood without preparation. Some exposed portion was probably folded back, damaged, carried toward the drain. Now the surviving body contracts early.

That is where its memory is: in how readily one part tightens after another.

The continuous roar does not trouble it. Nothing in its body requires quiet. But a stopped fan would alter the place profoundly. Steel would relax. A vibration long held in its folds would disappear. Water would arrive at a different angle. For a while the creature would continue to brace for a force that was no longer there.

It has already done this several times during changes of load. Each time, recovery is a little different.

The blue strip beneath the screw affects it too. When the wind drives the strip against the panel, the repeated impacts pass down through the flashing. The creature withdraws from that fastening and puts out more of itself along the concrete instead. In still weather it returns. Thus the shape of its body includes the behavior of a piece of packaging left behind by an installer.

There are other such influences. The spacer divides the discharge, sparing one narrow route from the strongest current. The rusting curl stiffens a fold. A deposit no thicker than a fingernail raises a margin enough to keep it wet a few moments longer.

It makes use of these things without collecting them.

Nothing about it suggests that it knows the court is high above the ground. It has no use for the mountain, though light from the slot reaches its exposed surface on clear afternoons and causes a gradual retreat. It cannot distinguish cloud from the shadow of someone leaning over it. Either way, after sufficient dimness, it extends again.

Should a worker kneel beside the drain, the creature would receive the event first through the knee.

That pressure would be immense and local, unlike anything in the regular workings of the place. The exposed lobes would tighten. Fluid would pass inward toward the protected folds. Beneath the flashing, its cuff would loosen from the screw.

From above, there would be almost nothing to see: a wet edge becoming less glossy.

Then the worker would stand, taking warmth and weight away. For a long time the corner would remain still.

Eventually a margin would soften. A shallow fold would open. The next mist would bring water beneath the steel, and the water would encounter a surface slightly easier to cross than bare concrete.

This is how the creature is enlarging its shelter.

It leaves behind a clear, adherent trace that does not quite dry between cycles. Each return of moisture follows the trace a little farther. The body follows later, after many returns, extending into a route it has helped make reliable.

Its newest part is only a faint crescent beyond the flashing. It has not yet thickened enough to hold grit. When the court drains, this crescent stays dark for perhaps a minute longer than the concrete beside it.

Under the next mist, it darkens first.
