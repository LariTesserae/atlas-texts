When the pool rises, something beneath its silver margin fails to rise with it.

For a moment there is a pale line between the water and the membrane. It stretches, becomes transparent, then releases at one end. The water takes it against the bank. There it lies among the shavings, indistinguishable from a strip cut off the edge of a filter.

It is part of what lives here.

Most of the rest is underneath things: between the overlapping ceramic sheets, behind the mineral crust on the service coupling, in the flattened space where the pool’s membrane rests against its support. Lift a plate and there would be little to see. A damp print. Several threads drawn out by the lifting, each narrowing until it breaks. On the underside, a patch that stays clear while frost returns around it.

Its thickness varies with the time since the last warming. At its fullest it resembles the skin inside a soaked seed, grey-white, faintly folded. As it dries, it becomes a glaze. The folds settle into the scoring of the ceramic, where enough moisture remains to preserve a few continuous passages.

Water moves through these passages more slowly than it moves through the channels outside. A drop can leave the pipe, strike the ridge, run to the pool and freeze along its edge while the small swelling it causes inside the glaze is still travelling beneath the first plate.

There are no eyes at the advancing edges. The edges divide where they meet obstacles and persist longer in some directions than in others. A thread entering a dry seam dwindles. One that reaches a warm bolt thickens behind its tip. Over several cycles, this makes a kind of decision.

The inspection lamp means little to it. The amber pulses mean nothing. But it registers the lamp’s warmth when someone leaves it lying on the basin. By the time the lamp is collected, a cloudy crescent has developed beneath its lower rim.

For a while afterward, the crescent continues to supply that empty place.

Its food arrives in quantities too small to cloud the water. There is residue washed from the pipe insulation, a trace of lubricant at the roller housing, compounds released as sealants age. Near the coupling, its finest threads share the weave with the green growth. They pass between the plant’s attachments without entering them. The leaves open overhead; beneath them, the pale tissue takes up what the water has carried past.

It grows especially well behind printed characters.

The black ink on the pool membrane makes a slightly different surface from the unprinted material. Along the lower curve of an eight, water holds a fraction longer. A colony can begin there. First a milky deposit gathers against the ink. Then a narrow border follows the figure, closes around its two centres, and sends a strand across to the next digit.

Several serial numbers now have this soft duplication. When pressure swells the membrane, the living outlines spread apart with the characters they follow.

It has occupied those numbers for longer than it has occupied any hill.

The hills require continual abandonment. A useful seam closes. A chamber fills. An extrusion carries a patch upward into cold air, where its exposed surface stiffens before it can withdraw. Such patches remain as faint cloudy areas on the grey ceramic. On a new ridge they look like imperfections in the manufacture.

What survives does not pull all of itself away. Fluids retreat through the narrowing connections, leaving the outer tissue attached. A pale margin darkens inward. Behind it, deeper under the plates, a fold becomes plump.

Sometimes the retreat is interrupted. The feed screw turns, two edges meet, and the connection is severed.

On either side, the remaining tissue closes.

There may be several inhabitants now. There may be one that has been divided many times. Where two living margins later meet in a wet seam, they sometimes join so completely that the place of contact disappears. Elsewhere they turn along one another and remain separate, making parallel threads that receive the same rain.

The relay’s heavy knock passes through all of them.

Immediately afterward, their smallest passages constrict. The response lasts only a little longer than the sound. It is most pronounced near the dies, where a knock is often followed by the onset of pressure. Tissue farther away responds less, unless connected to those nearer portions by an unbroken wet route.

Beneath the arch at the far end, it has made its thickest fold.

The fold occupies the angle between the roof and a supporting scale. It is translucent at the edge and opaque where it doubles back. Water descending from the roof bead touches it before falling. Some enters; the rest gathers again below, carrying a faint trace of the fold’s substance down to the chamber floor.

In amber light, it briefly looks substantial enough to have an inside.

There are small cavities in it, each holding fluid. Partitions flex when the machinery runs. A few dark grains shift from one cavity to another through openings too narrow to see. During the long cold interval, movement dwindles to an occasional change in the shape of a grain’s surrounding pocket.

This portion has never been exposed to a full warming. It has grown on what reached it through other portions, across several seams and beneath the pool.

Near the entrance, the living edge has reached the pipe’s lowest droplet. It occupies the contact between insulation and ceramic, a narrow damp collar being compressed as the ridge advances. With each feed cycle, some of it is lost. With each thaw, it extends a little along the underside of the pipe.

The old place where the rain fell is still thick with its tissue. Below the new gathering droplets there is only a thread, stretched across a shaving and attached at both ends.

The first drop bends the shaving.

The thread holds.
