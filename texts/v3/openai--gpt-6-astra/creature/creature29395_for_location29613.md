The one behind the tallest wall has worn a hollow into the stone beneath it. This is one of the few old surfaces in the basin, though almost none of it is exposed. The hollow is smooth at its center and ringed with short grooves, each ending in a pit. When the creature moves, its feet find these pits without searching.

There are more feet than can be seen at once. They are short, folded beneath the body in overlapping pairs, and each ends in two hard points with a soft pad between them. A foot set down carelessly would be stripped from its joint. It places the points first, tests them against the grain, then lowers the pad. The pad spreads so thinly that the pink stone shows through it.

At rest, the creature is about the size of a person crouching with their head tucked down. It has no comparable arrangement of parts. Its upper surface is a low, irregular shield, dark in the middle and pale along the growing edges. Mineral grains are embedded in it. Some have been incorporated smoothly; others project at angles, carrying scratches from impacts the flesh beneath them never felt.

The front is whichever end it is feeding from. There are two mouths, far apart, each surrounded by a wreath of small, jointed fingers. Usually one is closed against the floor.

Between the mouths, under the shield, its body is soft.

This softness is difficult to witness. Water that penetrates beneath the edge of the shield first encounters a fringe of tightly packed folds. These close against an incoming surge, each braced by the one behind it. Beyond them there is warm gray tissue, faintly luminous along its branching vessels, moving with a deliberateness wholly unlike the water outside. Here the creature draws a morsel apart, or turns an injured foot inward, or holds something small while it struggles.

It has several such small things beneath it now.

They are not all its young.

***

The wall is its work, but it did not begin the wall.

It found two courses of fitted stone on the shelf, perhaps left by another of its kind. Perhaps there were once more walls. It has added to this one over a long period, taking fragments from the floor when the water makes them briefly light enough to handle.

It cannot lift a large stone against its full weight. Instead it waits through the pressure changes with its feet planted and its fingers resting on a suitable edge. When the ceiling descends and the summit starts to rise, it guides the loosened stone sideways. Often the stone is lost. Sometimes it breaks against the wall. Once in many attempts it arrives where the creature can hold it until weight returns.

The fitted surfaces need not be exact at first.

From beneath its shield the creature pays out a clear, tough substance in flattened cords. It runs these through the joints, securing them inside cracks too narrow for a foot. The cords shorten slowly after leaving its body. They can continue shortening for years.

That is what draws the stones together.

The low note comes from water passing over the tightened cords inside the wall. The creature feels the note through its feet. A change in it brings the creature out, even during a bad surge, to touch each joint in turn.

It spends much of its strength keeping one small space habitable.

The opening at the base is not an entrance. Behind it the passage divides and divides again, into slots packed with stiff fringes shed from the creature’s underside. Water is drawn through those slots and discharged through hundreds of smaller gaps elsewhere in the wall. Roots, insect parts, the soft pulp abraded from wood: these remain caught inside.

To feed, the creature inserts one wreath of fingers through the opening. Each finger is thin enough to enter a slot and bends at more places than a joint ought to allow. It removes what has accumulated without loosening the packed fringes.

Sometimes there is almost nothing.

Sometimes it has to empty the wall for days.

***

It has no eyes.

There are two patches on its shield that look like eyes when a distant brightness passes above them. They are shallow basins lined with a glossy membrane. A grain of stone rests in each, tethered by fine strands of flesh. As weight changes, the grains pull differently. If one begins to rise while the other still presses downward, the creature closes both mouths and folds every loose part beneath its shield.

These organs do not tell it what will happen. They give it a little warning.

Its other senses are spread through its feet, its fringes, the clear cords in the wall. A falling fragment reaches it as a sequence of small changes in pressure. The pale fish passing nearby leaves a complicated disturbance that the creature follows long after the fish is gone.

The greater noise is always present. Within it, the creature distinguishes the particular trembling of its wall, the knock of a loose plate against the second shelter, the tremor of something feeding in a seam.

There is another of its kind beneath the balanced slab.

For long intervals they have no contact. Then, when a current runs cleanly between their shelters, one releases a strand no thicker than a hair. If it survives the crossing, the other catches it.

They tighten the strand between them.

The movements passed along it are minute. One body contracts; the other answers after a pause. Sometimes the exchange lasts until the current changes. Sometimes the strand is severed almost immediately, and both creatures retain their broken ends.

The ends are drawn underneath the shields and handled for a while before being eaten.

***

The perfect stillness beneath the slab permits things that cannot be done behind the wall.

There the second creature unfolds.

Its shield separates into overlapping sections, exposing soft seams. Limbs that seemed short extend by unrolling at their bases. A pale membrane is lifted into the suspended white grains and held open. The grains adhere to it in rows.

This is how a new shield begins.

The creature beneath the slab is young, or recently injured. Much of its back is bare. Along one side, three feet end in rounded buds. It has occupied the still patch since the last great rearrangement of the basin and has not yet ventured out to feed.

The wall-builder sometimes sends food toward it.

This is not a reliable kindness. The wall-builder also takes food from currents the injured one could reach, and once gripped its exposed membrane when both were sheltering beneath the same ledge. But when the wall holds an excess, it presses scraps into a wad, wraps the wad in a little clear cord, and releases it at a particular height.

Most miss the slab.

The young one waits with a mouth open at the straight boundary of the stillness. It does not put its fingers across.

***

Beneath the wall-builder’s shield, two young cling beside a pale, narrow animal with crowded dark eyes.

The fish arrived torn along the belly. The creature caught it while feeding and began to draw it underneath. Had the fish been smaller, it would have gone directly into a mouth.

Instead, its fins caught among the folded feet. It struggled, then became still. Its gills continued moving.

That was several surges ago.

Now the fish lies in the small circulation of water beneath the shield, where the creature’s fringes remove the coarsest grit. The wound has closed to a white seam. When food is drawn in, the fish sometimes snaps at it. The creature contracts around the movement but has not expelled it.

Its young are flatter than the fish’s head. Their shields are nearly transparent. Through them can be seen a dark gut, the paired grains of their balance organs, and little rows of feet opening and closing without finding purchase.

One clings firmly. The other keeps releasing its pads.

The parent catches this second one with a feeding finger and places it back against the underside of the shield. It does this repeatedly, without haste. During the heaviest impacts it cups both young in a soft fold and braces the fold against the stone.

There were more when they hatched.

The curved milky plates in the shelter are useful to the surviving young. They scrape material from them with their mouths. For this reason the parent retrieves fragments whenever it can, choosing them from the moving grit with a delicacy it does not use for food.

A whole plate is too broad to bring through the wall’s opening. It holds one beneath its body and breaks it carefully against the lip of its worn hollow.

***

When the deepest part of the basin withdraws, the wall-builder sometimes leaves its shelter.

It goes slowly, keeping one length of cord attached to the wall. Its feet proceed from crack to crack. The shield stays low. Along the way it touches the brown straps but does not eat them; it probes beneath their attachment discs for softer things.

Near the hollow, its proportions become difficult to judge.

A rear foot remains beside a familiar seam while the foremost fingers reach a ledge that ought to be several bodies away. More of the underside is visible than the shield could have covered. The clear cord continues into darkness, although little has been drawn from the coil at the wall.

The creature feeds there.

What it finds is pale and adheres strongly to the rock. It removes narrow strips, working both mouths against the same surface. Occasionally it stops with every finger spread and remains motionless for a long time.

On one such visit it returned carrying an empty shield.

The shield was smaller than its own, split through the center, and worn smooth around the edges. No soft tissue remained. The wall-builder brought it all the way to the shelf, secured it beside the opening, and gradually fitted stones around it.

It is part of the wall now.

Water passes through the break in its middle.

***

As the ceiling bows downward again, the creature behind the tallest wall draws in its feeding fingers. The young one that will not hold on is pressed into its fold. The fish turns once, seeking an easier position, and settles with its scarred flank against the warm tissue.

Outside, the thumb-sized pebble lifts.

The wall-builder releases half its feet. With the others it pulls itself deeper into the hollow it has worn, until the rim of its shield meets the stone.

Then it places the free feet against the underside of the wall.

For a moment the fitted blocks weigh almost nothing. The cords slacken. White powder trembles in the joints.

The creature pushes one slipping stone back into place and holds it there, waiting for the mountain’s weight to return.
