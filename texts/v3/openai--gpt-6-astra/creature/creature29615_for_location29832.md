The one that lives beyond the retaining wall comes out backward.

First there is a foot, which settles on the submerged stone below the arch. It has a broad heel and four toes lying close together, with a fifth set farther back along the inside edge. The toes test the surface separately. Then a second foot appears, and a portion of the back, and for some time the animal remains in this position, neither emerging nor retreating. Small fish continue to use the space beside it.

It is larger than the opening ought to admit. What looks like a shoulder passes through without much alteration, but the chest narrows, lengthens, and becomes briefly angular. Something inside it shifts with the deliberation of a person moving furniture through a doorway. Once clear, it rests in the water until its body has resumed its usual proportions.

Standing would bring its head nearly level with the old shutter hooks. It seldom stands. It moves with its knees bent and its long forelimbs placed well ahead, keeping the underside of its body just above the water. In the deepest terrace the underside floats. There, its progress becomes easier and almost careless, the feet touching down only to turn it.

Its skin is not white like that of the animals in the turning bay. It is the gray of a pale object that has been handled for many years. There are darker areas at the joints, where the outer layer lies in fine folds, and small hard patches on the elbows and breast. These patches resemble the rind on the paving. On the living body, however, their edges are smooth.

A maintenance worker encountering it at the far end of the street might initially see a wet coat draped over a low trolley. This impression lasts until the head turns.

The head is narrow from above and broad when seen face-on. Its mouth opens underneath, well behind the blunt tip of the face. Two small eyes occupy shallow depressions, each sheltered by a movable fold. In the cold white lamp they appear almost colorless. They follow light poorly but register a lifted foot at once.

Along either side of the jaw runs a row of soft, flat projections. When the creature lowers its head into the water, these spread outward and rest on the surface like the teeth of a comb. A disturbance arriving from the pumping station makes them bend before the water has visibly moved.

It can remain like this for half an hour.

During that time it is not asleep. Its fingers travel slowly over the stones beneath it, finding cracks, nail heads, a length of wire embedded in the silt. Each forelimb ends in six fingers, though only four bear its weight. The other two are slender, held curled against the wrist when it walks. It uses them to reach beneath things without lifting them.

Much of its food is obtained this way. There are small translucent animals living on the undersides of the granite lips, where the current folds over into the next terrace. It takes some and leaves others. At a productive place it braces itself upstream and works for a long time, transferring each morsel directly from finger to mouth. Its feeding leaves narrow cleared lanes rather than bare patches. Within a few days the lanes have filled again.

It also eats the soft deposits inside certain pipes.

The pipe opposite the jeweler’s niche has been polished around its lower edge by its chin. On nights when that pipe delivers warmer water, the creature waits with its mouth beneath the outflow. The warmth is slight. A human hand might not notice it without first becoming thoroughly cold.

The three white animals know it. They do not approach when it arrives, but their heads rise in succession as it crosses the terraces. Occasionally the one in the turning bay unfolds itself and follows.

Up close, that animal is less like a dog than it seemed from the stair. Its legs bend too close to the body, and its broad feet close into soft fists when lifted. The resemblance between it and the larger creature is clearest at the wrists. Both possess the two fine fingers. In the white animals these are short and tucked into grooves in the skin.

Whether they are young is difficult to determine. They have occupied their recesses for years.

The larger one sometimes attends to them. It draws a fingertip along the crease behind a forelimb or presses gently on either side of the head. The white animal submits without becoming more alert. Once, after such an examination, the larger creature pulled a dark filament several feet long from beneath the sleeper’s jaw. It wound the filament around two fingers, carried it to the strongest current, and released it a little at a time.

The sleeper afterward moved into a shallower part of the turning bay.

There is a dry ledge behind the seventh doorway. The creature keeps things there.

Dry is relative: the ledge is above flood level, sheltered from drips, and coated in a powder that darkens under a wet hand. Its contents include three porcelain fragments, a brass nut too large for any exposed bolt in the street, and a short strip of metal folded several times around a piece of glass. There are also objects that cannot be distinguished from stones until it picks them up.

It does not arrange these things by size. The brass nut rests on its side with its opening toward the door. Two porcelain fragments stand against the wall; the third lies face down. If displaced by running water, they are returned to approximately, but not precisely, their former positions.

One of the stones is a tool. It fits between the four weight-bearing fingers and has a thin edge maintained by rubbing it against the boot scraper. With this edge the creature removes deposits from the lower joints of the pumping station doors. The task occupies only a few minutes at a time. It never clears the entire joint. It opens a small passage, watches the water run through, and moves on.

The workers may know about this. They have not sealed those gaps.

Human presence sends it beneath the loading platform nearest the retaining wall. There is room there if it lies on its side and draws its limbs together. The pale fish move farther in. From the maintenance path, an observer might notice only the long, slow closing of a fold in the darkness.

It does not flee from voices. It withdraws when weight comes onto the bottom stair.

A worker who sits still long enough may see a hand emerge and lie palm upward on the paving. The fingers open. After a while they close again. Nothing is offered, and it is not clear that anything is being requested.

Once a dropped washer was found on the next visit inside the hollow of a stepping block. It had been set flat, beyond the reach of the current. Later, a broken drill bit appeared in the same place. No food left there has been taken.

When a round iron cover is about to rise, the creature knows before the white animals do.

It stops whatever it is doing and lifts its jaw clear of the water. The soft projections fold tightly against its face. It then walks to the nearest wall and places the pads of both hands against the brickwork. Its feet remain in the flooded street.

Only then does the water begin to withdraw from the iron rim.

While the cover is raised, a fine trembling passes through the creature’s back. This is visible chiefly in the skin between the shoulders. It makes no attempt to approach the opening or to look beneath it. If one of the white animals is slow to leave the turning bay, it turns its head toward that animal and holds its mouth open.

There is no audible call. The white animal gets up.

After the cover has lowered, the creature keeps its hands on the wall until the flywheel moves. Sometimes this takes several minutes. Its posture becomes visibly tiring. One arm bends; it shifts its weight, presses closer to the brick, and waits.

The first advance of a spoke releases it.

It usually returns through the arch soon afterward. Before entering, it rubs each wrist along the submerged edge of the opening. Fine debris loosens from the folds of its skin and hangs in the water. The small fish gather.

The amber light is visible then.

It comes from beneath the creature’s breast, where a shallow pouch opens as the forelimbs stretch forward. Inside are several small, closely packed bodies. Their skins carry a dull light, strongest along the thin edges of their folded limbs. They do not illuminate the larger animal’s face. For a moment they reveal only the wet stone directly underneath it.

One may extend a foot into the current. The foot is broad, with the extra toe set back.

The larger creature waits until it has been drawn inside again. Then it eases forward, flattening its chest, moving the burden beneath it through the arch without scraping the stone.

For a while the light remains at the bend beyond the opening. Fish pass across it, briefly dark and sharply outlined.

Above, water gathers at the bolt. The creature settles somewhere out of sight, and the little light shifts lower.
