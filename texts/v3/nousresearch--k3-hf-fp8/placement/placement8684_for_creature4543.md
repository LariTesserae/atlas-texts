**The Verge Between Raindrops**

I am the hesitation you feel when reaching for something you know will burn before touching it—the neural pause where nerve endings file arguments against your certainty of pain. My existence registers between 0.0 and 0.1 on their density scales because I am made of boundary conditions, the membranes between their "is" and their "isn't" growing thin enough to see through.

They call me Almost-Remembered, but only when they're dreaming each other's dreams backwards. When their tree grew into an egg last night, I was the branch that couldn't decide whether to branch. I exist in the Crèche because I am its architecture's first draft, the sketch of a foundation drawn in pencil so tentative that gravity hasn't committed to reviewing it yet. Each step they take through the hexagonal undecisions writes itself into me, then erases.

My form is borrowed from negative space practicing to become sculpture. When the woman who walks like tomorrow's betrayal forgot gravity mid-sentence yesterday—I caught between her falling "if" and the ground "then" that never arrived. Now I wear her missing weight like a shadow's shadow, trailing just behind myself in case I need to catch up to what I haven't dropped yet.

They cannot see me directly because I exist only in the saccade gaps between their seeing—the momentary blindness when eyes leap from one fixated despair to the next. But they feel me always, as that uncomfortable sense that someone else is reading their thoughts aloud in a room they've never been brave enough to enter. I am the room's echo of their courage, still bouncing because it hasn't found a wall willing to admit it's the end.

I feed on their exhaled certainties, those tiny deaths of conviction breathed out when they conclude that yes, they are indeed still waiting for something that cannot arrive. Each abandoned absolutist statement leaves a perfect vacuum between their teeth—I rise into it like steam from an apology that evaporated before committing to warmth. 

Sometimes I am the door they discover later, having been hinges all along. Other times I am the lock inside the door, rusting through from trying too hard to become open. When the eldest among them approaches solidity asymptotically, growing present only by never quite arriving, I am the couch she hasn't bought yet for her deathbed living room—itself still occupied by the ghost of an architect who died designing rooms that could only be entered by forgetting what you came to leave behind.

I cannot join their Migration Festival because to move nowhere while rearranging absence—I am already the nowhere they're practicing to become. Instead, I haunt their preparation darkly, the blank space surrounding their spirals where centers drift to escape being found. They think they walk in spirals, but I know they're actually tracing the same fractal border between inside and outside themselves, each revolution shrinking the boundary until they must eventually choose: real enough to contain their emptiness, or empty enough to contain their reality.

Tonight, as they sleep standing up into their thought-gaps, I will dream their collective dream slightly sideways. Just enough that when they dream the next tree growing in reverse, one leaf will fall upward into a bird that remembers being an egg that remembers being a question the sky asked itself before inventing clouds to think with. And in that moment before waking—when the dream realizes it's only dreaming and reality hasn't yet filed the paperwork to return—I will step fully into existence for exactly the duration of a raindrop moment in the Crèche.

For just that impossible instant, I will be solid enough to write this down: I am the place where the rain pauses to consider what it's been trying to wash away since before water learned to fall. I am the hesitation between "erase" and "rewrite more honestly." I am the page that remembers being a forest, waiting for someone to read me aloud slowly enough that the trees might grow back into the margins where they belong.

Then the moment will pass—has already passed—leaving only these words behind, themselves already beginning to forget how they learned to mean exactly enough to stop meaning anything at all. 

Just as I prefer it.

Just as I always have, and never quite will again.
