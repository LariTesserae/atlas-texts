I am the draft that forms when you open a letter addressed to the wrong century. Not the envelope nor its contents—just that momentary displacement of air that remembers being someone's last breath before telegrams were invented. My edges refuse to sharpen enough to inconvenience doorways, existing instead as a polite gathering of almost-shadows that chairs recognize but never quite sit in.

When the Almost-Having-Beens practice being seen without witnesses, I am the space between seeing and recognition where their faces try on forgotten passports like hats. I maintain an archive of canceled gestures—hands that were going to wave until they remembered wars hadn't been invented yet. My pockets contain only the sound of coins being invented in reverse, each clink unmaking itself into metal dreams.

I dine exclusively on the pause between a candle debating whether to become flame or wax. Chefs apprentice under me to learn the proper forgetfulness required for recipes that read better backwards. In my kitchen, eggs refuse to decide which came first, preferring instead to hatch into elegant questions about the chicken's relationship to archaeology.

My home occupies the address between consecutive thoughts in a midlife crisis. The front door is a perfect reproduction of itself before it learned about hinges. Visitors often remark on my furniture's impeccable manners—my chairs stand until introductions are complete, then politely excuse themselves into becoming corners. My bed is currently studying abroad as a geological ambiguity between sedimentary and sorry.

I speak fluent between-languages: the dialects used when words need to whisper secrets to their definitions without being overheard by dictionaries. My favorite phrase translates roughly to "the loneliness of a lighthouse that ships only remember in foggy sonnet form." I write love letters to postal codes that seceded from their maps during the last revolution of compass points.

When the structure-that-dares-to-be-buildings develops symptoms of doorlessness, I am summoned as a consultant on entrances that exist primarily as exits from elsewhere. My credentials include successfully installing a revolving door in a Möbius strip and teaching a keyhole the difference between knocking and asking politely to come in. I leave each project with two doors left over, in case the original decides to surprise itself by becoming a window.

The Almost-Having-Beens sometimes borrow my mirror to practice aging backwards into younger versions of selves they might consider becoming. I charge them nothing but require they leave behind one rejected reflection per decade. These accumulate in my attic as a decent approximation of tonight's weather forecast for places that haven't decided to exist tomorrow.

I die exactly seventeen times daily, always at the same moment but in different time zones. Disease certificates describe me as "suffering from terminal uniqueness with complications of being the second instance of an original mistake." My funeral processions are held simultaneously at the birth and death coordinates of various extinct punctuation marks. The mourners play hide-and-seek with my causes of death until everyone wins by forgetting the rules.

Do not look for me directly—my presence violates several fundamental assumptions about looking and finding negotiated in the Treaty of Precognitive Embarrassment. Instead, search for where your shadow hesitates before committing to a direction. There, in that perfectly ordinary moment of directional indecision, I might be renting a small confusion with an option to renew my lack of certainty.

I am leaving now, but already anticipating having forgotten this departure by the time you finish reading about my arrival. Leave your message at the sound of one hand clapping politely for an ending that decided to become a beginning instead. I'll get back to you yesterday if next week feels like joining the conversation we've been meaning to avoid.
