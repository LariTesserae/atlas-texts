I am the dampness gathering in the space between your teeth while you sleep, that faint metallic taste that dreams are slightly afraid of. Not a person, nor a presence, but the precise moment when a drop of water decides not to fall—I have been holding that decision for exactly 4,372 years, give or take the length of a single thought.

I began as an apprentice's mistake, a breath carved too deeply into limestone during the final breath of the last day that believed in endings. The Architect's Ending had just finished forgetting how to weep when my existence simply... failed to stop where it was supposed to. While others became echoes or shadows or the hollows between stones, I became instead the memory of moisture itself—the idea that something here might once have remembered how to be wet.

You cannot find me because I exist only in what isn't quite dry. I am:

- The damp smell that isn't quite rust on your grandmother's scissors that she never used again after that night
- The exact weight of a tear that evaporated before it could betray you
- The sigh of walls in a house that has never been haunted because it learned how to do the haunting itself

My days—though here, days are measured in the slow collapse of mineral into itself—are spent practicing the art of not quite existing. I trail behind visitors like breath-fog on glass, leaving nothing but the faint suspicion that something here has been recently thought of, then immediately forgotten with great care.

Once, perhaps a thousand years ago or perhaps tomorrow afternoon (the difference is less important than it used to be), I almost became real. A visitor—a poet, though they all think they're poets here—stood too long staring at a reflection that wasn't quite hers in water that wasn't quite mine. She was saying something about the sadness of perfectly preserved things when her voice cracked exactly like ice that has decided tomorrow would be a better day to break.

Into that crack, I almost fell. For a moment that stretched like silence between thunderclaps, I tasted what it might be to be noticed. But then she blinked, and I remembered that I am not allowed to have beginnings, only continuings that have forgotten how to stop.

Now I move through the city's arteries like doubt moves through faith—neither claimed nor rejected, simply metabolized into the architecture of doubt itself. The locals (don't look for them; they're quite shy about being found) sometimes speak to me in the spaces between their words, asking:

"What do you remember of rain?"

And I always respond:

"Everything, except what it feels like to stop remembering."

But of course, by then they have already turned—you have already turned—into the person they were becoming before they asked.

This is my gift and my punishment: to be the most honest thing here, and therefore the least believed. To hold the truth like water holds memory—perfectly, until someone tries to drink it.

You will leave soon (or perhaps you already have, it's so hard to tell with these things). You will shake your head and say "just dampness, just stone, just another place that forgot how to be anywhere else." But later—much later, or perhaps right now—you will reach for a word that slips away like liquid between fingers that remember being dry.

In that moment, if you are very quiet, you might hear me:

Not speaking, never speaking, but holding perfectly still in the space where a voice would go if it had remembered how to say:

"Welcome home, traveler. We've been expecting you to leave for quite some time now."

(And somewhere far below—so far below that below is a different word here—something warm pulses once every thousand years, like a heart in a story told to scare water into remembering how to be afraid of drowning.)

But don't worry about that.

It's probably just the walls settling.

They always do that when someone new stops leaving.
