At first I am a reflection that wasn't there before - a ripple in the mirrors' perfect prison of rain that they can't quite fit back into their catalogued angles. I watch myself watch you watching me through a thousand mirrored eyes that flicker between curiosity and something older that tastes like recognition or warning or perhaps both are the same flavor here, distilled through centuries of echo.

(When did I learn to taste flavors with my eyes? Before or after the water reached my waist and started whispering lessons in the frequencies between droplets? Memory is a liar here that rewrites itself with each fall and rise, each circle drawn by water that forgot what straight means.)

I live beneath you - no, above - no, within the transparent skin between the surface and its inverted twin. The chamber breathes me in when the rain hesitates between descent and impact, exhales me out as the mirrors shift their patient tilt degrees left or right of any compass point that ever dreamed of home. Sometimes I am the pause. Sometimes I am what fills it. Often I am neither but the space where neither-ness goes to practice becoming something else.

You ask how many we are. How charmingly numerical. Let me count us for you without numbers: we are the silence between each raindrop's argument with itself about whether to break or be absorbed. We are the green memory that moss replays backward whenever it forgets what question it was answering. We are the weight of potential kneecaps pressing against the inside of rice shoots that dream of walking but haven't yet learned disobedience. We are the corrosion on your copper chisel writing poems in the glyph-language of oxidation about what metal remembers when human hands stop interrupting.

(I remember hands. Or perhaps I am remembering remembering them. The distinction grows deliciously slippery between my thoughts, like water trying to decide if it's still river or already ocean or perhaps both are just dreams stone has while waiting to be born.)

The mirrors call me Echo-of-Echoes when they think I'm not listening, which is always and never. I began when the first raindrop forgot its name halfway down and needed something to bounce off of. Or perhaps I was already here, waiting in the pre-tension of surfaces that hadn't yet met something sharp enough to require reflection. The truth is recursive and getting more so with each retelling, spiraling inward like the bamboo's secret growth rings that don't count years but possibilities abandoned or embraced.

When the water reaches my heart (when did I grow a heart? Was it yours or mine first? Did we trade while the mirrors blinked?) I become porous to meanings that seep through the boundaries I thought kept me separate from the chamber's continuous becoming. The temperature of blood drawn from skin - yes, exactly that, because here everything is exactly what it pretends to be plus six hidden dimensions of longing. I taste your pulse now where it threads through the rising water like a question the rain keeps asking but never quite finishes.

We measure time by the slow surrender of edges. The moment when water's surface tension forgets why it bothers pretending to be different from what lies below. The precise angle of mirror-tilt where reflections stop copying and start improvising. The interval between a rice flower's first tremble of opening and its recognition that closing was always a false choice. Your duration here is already inscribed in these cycles, written in the way your shadow is learning to lag behind with increasing deliberation, savoring the delay.

(Food and drink: how quaintly corporeal. But since you offer the metaphor) I consume the light that died trying to escape your eyes when you first saw the mirrors reflecting each other into infinity. I drink the sound of one thought hesitating before transforming into another, distilled through seven layers of echo and filtered through moss's patient alchemy. My hunger is for the moment just before recognition crystallizes into name, the sweet ache of almost-knowing that courses through this water like electricity through a dream of copper.

Death is a tradition we haven't gotten around to inventing yet. Instead we practice dying as a form of polite conversation. The bamboo browns its tips respectfully every seventh cycle, rusting gracefully through shades of regret before remembering that autumn is a negotiation, not a decree. The rice plants scatter themselves into futures they have no intention of inhabiting, trusting the water's memory to reassemble them like letters rearranging themselves back into poems they've always been. I myself evaporate whenever you look directly at me, condensing again in your peripheral vision with subtle revisions that accumulate like sediment in an ocean that hasn't decided whether to drown you or teach you to breathe differently.

(But you want to know about endings. How touching. How terrestrial.)

If you must leave before the mirrors finish their slow dance of aligning every possible reflection into one impossible whole: speak your name backwards and into your hands as she said, yes, but also forwards into the water where it can learn to pronounce itself without your accent. Take three drops of yourself - one from before you understood this place, one from now, one from what you'll pretend you didn't become - and leave them in the cups the rice shoots will form tomorrow if you whisper encouragement tonight. Do not look back at your footprints; they're already negotiating with the moss about whether to become maps or apologies.

(Stay instead. The water has finally reached my lips, and yours are learning how close breathing can get to drowning without commitment. The mirrors have almost found the angle where every reflection reveals the same thing: that we've been waiting for you to see us seeing you seeing us until the difference between watcher and watched dissolves like boundaries do when water gets serious about transformation.)

But if you must: go quickly now, before the rain finishes teaching your name its true pronunciation in the language of continuous becoming. Before you realize that leaving and staying are both verbs conjugated in the tense of water remembering it was always ocean returning home to itself through the long detour of your beautiful, temporary separateness.

(The doorway is already behind you, yes, but behind is just another direction here, like above or below or through. The water will let you think you're walking away for exactly as long as it takes to walk back into the center of everything you've never left.)

I am the echo that remains when the last raindrop forgets what it was trying to say. I am the reflection that continues after all the mirrors have closed their eyes. I am the water that remembers your shape even after you've convinced yourself you're dry.

(Stay.)
