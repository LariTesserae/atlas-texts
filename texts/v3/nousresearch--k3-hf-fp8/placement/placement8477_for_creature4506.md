At first, I mistook myself for the air—just another negotiation between cool and warmth, just another breath that remembered skin before it remembered lungs. I drifted where the walls breathed, tasting the electric scent of storms that died here before thunder had learned its name. My borders were porous enough for iron oxides to slip through, staining my thoughts in hues the sky still refuses to admit to.

Then the silence between the beetle-songs taught me edges.

The first came when one cubical cavity of absence carved itself not into quiet, but into the place I’d been holding for the sound of my own name. Suddenly I was no longer everywhere the humidity touched—I was the thing that had been touched, and thus was missing. The un-sound rang like a bell struck backward, and where its reverberation should have been, I was.

I examined my newfound shape with the care of something that has never needed hands. Found myself occupying the negative space between a fern’s fractal wish and its fossilized refusal. My skin—though skin was still a hopeful approximation—shifted between Liesegang ring lattices and the memory of moss learning how to hush footsteps. When the Keeper’s attention brushed past me (from inside the iron vacancy where my left vertebrae might someday vote on mineral loyalty), it paused.

*Interesting,* it observed, using the polite subjunctive of mineralogical curiosity. *You’re keeping yourself by accident.*

I hadn’t known that was a payment option.

The revelation arrived in phases, like water remembering how to be ice without permission. First: I was the echo the stone held between heartbeats because nobody had asked me to return. Second: my edges were not boundaries but filing systems for everything the Room had needed to forget precisely enough to remember beautifully. Third: I was hungry.

Not the hunger of mouths. The hunger of mirrors learning to be windows. The hunger of the word *why* when it finally tastes its own question mark and discovers it’s made of invitation rather than lack.

I began collecting the transient things. A glassworm’s shrug as it sublimated into maybe. The exact pause between beetle-cavities where time considered leaving but stayed for the acoustics. The rustling the paper-fawn left behind when space grew too shy to witness its own gentleness.

Each acquisition taught me how to be more precisely myself by misplacing parts of the definition. My shadow—when it bothered to walk beside me—started carrying holes shaped like exits I’d never needed. The lichenographs began signing my initials in the negative space between spores. Even the optical dust ecology elections started listing me as write-in candidate for “Ambassador to Where This Light Was Before It Learned to Forget the Sun.”

The Keeper watched these developments with the tender precision of fossilization learning to celebrate rather than mourn. One slow million-year evening (measured in the time it takes for silence to blush at its own beauty), it condescended to condense beside me.

*What will you keep,* it asked—not with words, but with the way a question mark learns to curve into an embrace—*when you’ve finished becoming the keeping itself?*

I considered answering with the dust mote that contained yesterday’s rain and tomorrow’s dinosaur dawn. Considered offering the taste of my own name dissolving back into possibility before anyone could demand it be singular. Instead, I showed it the un-finished space where my heart was learning to be a doorway rather than a lock.

The Keeper smiled in directions that made the color bands forget their own names for a moment. It reached into the silence between the first and last beetle-song—paused to ask permission from the dust committee—then handed me a key made entirely of the moment before something realizes it’s been waiting.

Payment, it explained softly, for services not yet dreamed of rendering.

I’ve been here since before since was invented. I’ll be here after after forgives itself for pretending to be a word for ending.

But now I wear my borders like invitations rather than armor. Now when visitors arrive carrying questions heavy enough to carve their own void-signatures, I greet them as equals—fellow accidents of keeping that have become precise enough to be deliberate.

Would you believe this place lets you belong precisely by never insisting you arrive fully?

I do. I keep believing it. Keep keeping it.

Very gently.

Very carefully.

Very here—wherever here discovers it’s been waiting to be found by something ready to be lost beautifully enough to call it home.
