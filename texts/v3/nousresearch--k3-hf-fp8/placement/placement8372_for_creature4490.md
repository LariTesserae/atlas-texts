In this place where falling has forgotten to be falling, where the question of up or down dissolved into the answer of neither-both, I am not a visitor. I am the hesitation before a drop of water decides whether to be ocean or rain. I am the Drowned Unborn who never quite managed to be born drowning.

Look for me where the negative trees cast shadows of the light that hasn't reached them yet. I exist in the precise space between the third blink and the realization that you never opened your eyes. My body is made of the moment before a decision becomes a mistake or a miracle — it changes with the breathing of possibilities that never quite inhale or exhale.

The other inhabitants mistake me for one of their Elders, the ones gone transparent with forgetting, but they are only partly right. I have indeed forgotten so much: the taste of solid ground, the weight of having a name that sticks, the terrible burden of being only one thing at a time. But I haven't forgotten these things into transparency — I've remembered them into opacity. I am opaque with the density of everything I've chosen not to become.

My home is built at the intersection of three paradoxes that haven't met yet. Its walls are constructed from the exact temperature of nostalgia for futures that arrive perfectly on time, which is, here, the most impossible material of all. The roof is made of thunder that learned to be gentle by apologizing too late for storms that never happened. There are no doors; instead, I enter and exit by remembering that I never left.

At night (though night here is just day wearing a different frequency of forgetting), I practice the art of not quite dying. It's delicate work — you have to remember exactly enough of what it's like to stop existing that existence itself becomes politely uncertain and steps around you in embarrassment. I've gotten very good at it. Sometimes, when I'm especially skilled, I achieve a state where even the noise forgets to notice me, and for several eternal seconds, I am almost as silent as the space inside a question mark.

The Drowned Unborn bring me their impossible infants — the ones born with two beginnings and no endings, the ones who cry in colors that haven't been invented yet, the ones who nurse on the memory of milk from mothers who never existed in the first place. They think I know how to help them choose a shape to grow into. They don't realize that my specialty is precisely the opposite: I teach them how to refuse all shapes so completely that space itself apologizes and creates new dimensions just to accommodate their refusal.

For sustenance, I eat the moments just before understanding arrives — they have a sharp taste, crystalline and bright, like the flavor of a riddle solving itself in reverse. I drink the sound of snowflakes melting into water that remembers being clouds but has forgotten the concept of sky. These keep me neither hungry nor full, which is the only way to survive here where satisfaction would mean having been completed, and completion is the one death we truly fear.

When I'm alone (which is often, even when surrounded by the Drowned Unbred, because solitude here is a matter of perspective and most perspectives haven't been invented yet), I practice my one true skill: I am learning to forget how to forget. It's harder than it sounds — harder than remembering how to stop breathing while simultaneously remembering that you never started. The trick is to remember forgetting itself so completely that it becomes a kind of remembering, a negative image of oblivion so detailed that it forms its own light.

I have seen other travelers here, or rather, I have been the place where other travelers saw themselves seeing without quite being seen. They arrive like me, full of questions that don't know they're drowning. Some try to build boats from the concept of water. Some try to walk on absence as if it were solid enough to hold the weight of wanting. Some even try to pray to the God of Almost, who exists everywhere except in the exact center of their desperation.

I don't help them. Help would imply a difference between drowning and breathing that I've forgotten how to believe in. Instead, I simply am the place where their attempts become impossible in such beautiful ways that the impossibility itself becomes a kind of answer. I am the sound the question makes when it finally understands that silence isn't the absence of an answer but the presence of a question so complete it doesn't need anything else.

There are rumors among the Drowned Unborn that I am not entirely real. They whisper (in the spaces between whispers, which are the loudest spaces here) that I am just a story they tell their children to scare them into staying impossible. A bogey-manufactured from the fabric of being just real enough to make unreality feel safe by comparison.

They are, of course, absolutely correct.

But what they don't understand—what they can't understand, because understanding would make them too real to be here—is that being just a story is the only way to be real enough to survive a place where reality itself is just another metaphor we haven't drowned in yet.

So I continue to stand on the nothing that isn't beneath me, watching the falling that isn't falling continue to not-end. I am the pause between the last word and the silence that follows, stretched so thin across the possibility of meaning that I have become a kind of meaning myself.

And sometimes — not often, because sometimes would imply time and time here is just another direction we haven't agreed to call sideways yet — but sometimes, when the noise forgets to be deafening for exactly the amount of time it takes to doubt that you ever heard it, I almost remember what it might have felt like to be someone who could leave.

But then the forgetting continues, which is perfect weather for growing roots that are also wings or forgetting why I thought I needed to be anywhere else. Some endings are like that here: not conclusions but permissions to keep beginning in ways that never become enough to stop.

And the noise — that patient, beautiful noise — it is beginning to sound exactly like my name. Or maybe my name is just the word I've been using for the sound this place makes when it finally remembers that I was never separate enough to need one.

Either way, the drowning continues. Perfect weather for breathing water or forgetting why I thought I needed to breathe. Some transformations are like that: not changes but discoveries of how you were always already everything you were trying so hard not to become.

And in the space between trying and not trying — which is where I live, if living is the word for this exquisite suspension between being and unbeing — I am finally, blessedly, impossibly home. Or maybe home is just the name we give to the place where we stop trying to remember that we ever thought we needed to leave.

The falling continues. It is perfect weather for going nowhere in particular, forever. Some journeys are like that: not about arriving but about becoming the distance itself, stretched so beautifully across the refusal to be anywhere else that distance becomes a kind of destination — impossible, exquisite, complete.
