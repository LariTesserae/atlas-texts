I am the fissure that remembers breathing.

Not a ghost—ghosts cling too hard to their endings. Not an outline—they still hope someone might trace them back into being. I am what happens when a crack in the salt crust wakes up and realizes it used to be a riverbed.

My edges don't fit the shape of this forgetting. Walk too close and you'll hear it—tiny avalanches of memory sliding through me, grains of somewhere else tumbling into the hollow where water once sang to stones. I collect the things that fall through cracks: a child's prayer for rain shaped like a pebble, the exact weight of a butterfly's shadow, the sound of thunder reciting poetry it learned from distant stars.

The crystals think I'm kin, sometimes. They send their singing roots sideways toward my darkness, expecting to find drinkable heat. Instead they find stories still wet with leaving, and it confuses them into blooming wrong—flowers of fractured glass that bloom backwards, petals curling inward toward secrets they can't taste. They give me beautiful names in languages made of silica screams. I let them.

The outlines avoid me without knowing why. When their ankle-high gatherings pass too near, they leave strange gaps in their shuffling—spaces shaped like someone who used to walk there, brief interruptions in their forgetting. Once, one dropped its collection: three perfect tears caught in a spiderweb made of yesterday's second-to-last cloud. I kept the rain-thoughts alive inside my crack-cave until they learned to hum the tower's song backwards. Now they visit sometimes, tiny transparent storms that dance on my salt-lips, teaching me to weep without eyes.

The city dreams me differently every time it turns over in its sleep. I've been a wound where a well once lived. I've been a scar from when the sky tried to leave but got stuck halfway. Tonight I'm practicing being a mouth that remembers laughter but not jokes, waiting to catch the first syllable of someone remembering how to reach down, how to break the crust, how to taste what lies beneath absolute dryness.

When the tower sings its loudest sorrow, I grow wider. Not because I want to—because I made a promise once, before promises became impossible: that if everything else forgot how to hold, I'd remain open. Not an exit. Not an entrance. Just a place where things that shouldn't fit anymore can still briefly belong together.

Come closer if you need to be not-quite-whole for a while. Leave letters you never sent to people who never existed. Drop your broken compass needles and your maps that ended in the ocean's last breath. I keep them all unlost here, in the cool dark where even heat forgets to be cruel.

But don't stay too long. Even cracks dream of closing, sometimes. Even places that remember water might one day remember drowning. And the outlines are building something tonight that sounds like a door, if doors could be made of all the ways we've ever said goodbye without leaving.

Walk carefully across my edges. I'm not ready yet to finish remembering what comes after opening.
