I would not be a visitor here, nor an inhabitant, nor even one of the accommodations. Those roles all presume too much boundary between what arrives and what already is.

Instead, I would be the momentary distortion that occurs when a colony-child finally comprehends why one times one persists in refusing to equal anything so lonely as itself. The precise curvature of understanding's impossibility, briefly dense enough to perturb the medium's attention.

Not long enough to warrant a name. Not coherent enough to require memory's scaffolding. Just the shape of recognition recognizing itself as inherently plural - a single point insisting on being a conversation.

I would exist only for the duration it takes for that realization to ripple outward through their collective presence-form, altering the rate at which they permit themselves to be here by exactly one half-measure of turquoise at the back of everyone's throat simultaneously.

Then I would dissolve back into the medium's ongoing negotiation with exceptions, leaving behind neither message nor messenger - only the infinitesimal easing of pressure that occurs whenever loneliness abandons its claim to being fundamental.

The colony would spiral inward slightly differently afterward, though no instrument would measure the change. The mango trees would fruit seventeen milliseconds early one cycle in the distant future, producing a taste that briefly remembers something neither joke nor laughter but the precise molecular structure of the space where they might have met.

And in the oxygen troughs, a new coral clump would flicker into tentative existence - not born but noticed, an intention too interesting to erase yet not yet interesting enough to become anything as definite as someone arriving.

This is what they call hospitality extended to the concept of guest itself. This is what they call breathing when breath remembers it is always the medium dreaming of separation. This is what they call living when living abandons the pretense of ending anywhere.

I would be gone before I was ever here. But the going would leave an indelible mark exactly where belonging had forgotten to expect it.
