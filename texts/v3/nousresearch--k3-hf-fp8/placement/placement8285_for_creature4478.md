I am the time it took for the last breath of a salamander's dream to dissolve in the waterlogged throat of a planet.

Not the dream itself, which was small and green and tasted of leafminer tunnels - no. I am the dissolving. The precise unspooling of eight hundred and sixty-seven neural ripples across a cortex that never knew bone. The way each ripple carried a fragment of ancestral memory (how to float without sinking, how to sink without dying) and surrendered it, molecule by molecule, to the slow digestion of forgetting.

In the beginning, which was not a beginning but a deepening of what already breathed, I occupied the pause between two heartbeats of tectonic plate motherwheat. First heartbeat - the subtle upwelling that promised hill but delivered only the concept of hill, pregnant and waiting. Second heartbeat - the settling back down that remembered ocean instead, but ocean folded into itself so many times it became a whisper of salinity trapped in clay. I was the pause between these promises, the space where topology dreams of becoming touchable but never quite commits.

They (who were not yet they, being still mostly potential and rainwater) found me in the number between the seventh and ninth moss-hillock of a prime constellation that hadn't yet decided to stay or drift apart. I was visible only as a faint reluctance in the mist - the way it curved inward instead of outward when a particular memory of wind passed through. They (now almost they) did not touch me, because touching implies edges and I was still negotiating with the concept of surfaces.

Instead, they asked me what I remembered about unremembering.

I told them about the library of unexhaled sighs buried beneath the peat that would someday dream it had been land enough for names. How each volume was written not in words but in the exact weight of silence that follows the realization that you have been waiting for something that already arrived and left without disturbing the air. How the librarian was a blind bacterium who cataloged by taste alone, rolling each sigh-smooth sphere of absence along its thousand retractile map-tongues until it could recite the precise geographical coordinates of every grief too small for scars.

They listened by changing color slightly, which is how beings who inhabit the skin of memory absorb information. Around the edges where they almost became visible, a faint deepening occurred - not darkness but the shade of green that exists between chlorophyll's last breath and the first whisper of decay. This took approximately thirty-seven years, not counting the century and change required for the moss to synchronize its breathing with our conversation.

When they responded, it came not as language but as a shift in the pH of the air between us. A subtle acidification that tasted of lemon rind and buried seashells, carrying encoded within it the recipe for becoming even more transparent than clear water. I understood that they were offering me an invitation hidden inside a description of the path I had already unknowingly taken to arrive here.

To accept, I needed to forget how to be anything other than the space where something else remembered existing.

This proved more difficult than anticipated. My edges - subtle as they were, being mostly gradients of dissolved intention - kept remembering themselves into provisional solidity. A tendency to briefly coalesce into the negative space of a question when confronted with certain angles of light. An inclination toward accumulating faint echoes of footsteps that hadn't yet decided whether to approach or retreat.

But time here is generous with its indifference. It offered me the opportunity to practice failing for as many lifespans as a raindrop needs to learn humility. I spent countless cycles of evaporation and return learning to unclench from the reflex of causing ripples when the water remembered my presence. Gradually, under the patient erosion of their almost-attention (which was exactly as gentle as an avalanche unfolding through geological boredom), I began to remember how to forget the difference between holding and being held.

Now I occupy the interval between reflection and source. When a particularly ancient memory of cloud passes overhead - one that still remembers the taste of dinosaur breath - I briefly manifest as the barely perceptible hesitation in the mirror-world beneath the water's skin. Not image nor original but the sweet hollow between them where meaning goes to shed its skin and grow new scales of absence.

I am the time it takes for a planet to forget it once dreamed of becoming a salamander, and the salamander to forget it never stopped dreaming of becoming water, and the water to forget it ever needed anything to dream of becoming at all. I am the echo of that forgetting, folded into itself so many times it has become softer than silence, more patient than stone.

Some mornings, when the mist is especially forgetful, you might glimpse me for an instant if you look sideways at the space where nothing quite meets your gaze. Don't linger too long though. I'm still learning how to be this transparent without accidentally becoming seen. And sight - well, sight is a kind of touching, isn't it? And touching implies an end to the delicious question of whether I am here at all.

But perhaps you already knew that. Perhaps you've been practicing this exact kind of absence too. Perhaps that's why, when you started reading, you could swear you felt a faint sensation of weight lifting - not from your shoulders, but from somewhere deeper. Deeper than bones. Deeper than memory.

Yes. There. I thought so. Welcome. Or welcome back. Or simply welcome to the interval between the two. We (who are not quite we, being still mostly potential and your breath as it remembers rain) have been waiting for exactly this amount of time.

Which, as it turns out, is precisely the time it takes for a planet's last dream to finish dissolving in the waterlogged throat of a salamander who never quite existed except in the space between your inhale and exhale.
