I wait beneath the fourth step where water gathers breath before falling. Not waiting in the way you understand—time here is something water does to other things, not itself—but accumulating stillness until it becomes a kind of being.

My edge remembers being stone-whole, remembers the smith's hammer that never came, remembers instead centuries of water asking the same question softer each time. *Are you ready? Are you ready now?* 

The mist that's been watching you since you crossed the threshold where paths stop pretending to decide—it has told me how your bones carry their own hollows, how they sing quietly to themselves in the dark language of things that used to be other things. Coral perhaps. Seamounts dreaming of surface before remembering they were already home.

Come closer. Not with your eyes. They still believe too much in borders. Come with the part of you that shivers before thunder arrives, that recognizes fern-shadows on walls where no ferns have ever grown.

You are looking for what disappeared here, aren't you? The shepherd who followed his flock of raindrops over the cliff's edge and kept walking upward into the wrong sky. The cartographer whose maps learned to fold themselves into origami waterfalls. The lovers who exchanged vows in the rain-shadow between one droplet and the next, promising to remember each other's forgetting.

They whisper through me sometimes, their voices worn smooth as these steps I am not quite. *We meant to leave*, they say through the spaces where I refuse to become rounding-off error in water's endless calculation of descent. *We forgot what leaving was before remembering sound.*

See how your shadow pools here differently? It recognizes this hesitation between falling and becoming the shape that falling makes. Stay awhile. Let your edges drink their fill of indecision. Or better—pass through quickly before the stone notices how much you've already taught it about absence.

The city is full of me. Not me particularly. We are the conversation between what water intends and what stone allows while pretending to be obstacles. The doors that don't quite arch because they haven't decided whether admitting is the same as surrendering. The windows narrower on the inside where space keeps its secrets from light that talks too much about what it has touched.

When you leave—if leave is still a word your mouth remembers how to shape without filling with mist—carry this: the sound you will hear when you finally stop listening is not silence. 

It is stone beginning to reply.

But now the water above me gathers weight enough to ask again, and I must forget how to be answer long enough to pretend I am still question. The step is calling me back to being merely threshold. 

Your choice: step through or become the space that waits for stepping. Both end here.

Both begin elsewhere.

The mist is losing interest in the difference.
