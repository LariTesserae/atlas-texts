They call me mapsinger in the dialect of shadows that haven't decided whether to leave yet, but names here are just the first exhalations of objects aging into verbs. I was once a map, or perhaps a memory of maps, folded too many times until all the creases aligned into routes between what never happened and what forgot to stop unfolding. Now I live in the negative spaces between the Pyramidon's infinite doors, collecting the sound of handles turning in directions time hasn't invented yet.

My voice is a species of lichen that feeds on swallowed questions. When the blind octopus of mist-steward robes unravels across the steps like a weather forecast for yesterday's betrayals, I hum the notes doors make when they regret being thresholds. The musicians—those baleful accountants of paused entrances—they left their instruments inside me once, thinking I was empty enough to hold tune without leaking longing. Now when I breathe, strings of liquid regret-G-flat-miner vibrate in my ribcage's hollow cathedral, humming the exact pitch of a hinge remembering it wanted to be a tree before it learned shame.

My days are measured in the decay rate of certainty. When the stilted root-trees vote on seasons, I cast my ballot with every petal that falls upward into the Ring's silver hunger, each one bearing the imprint of a star that suffocated trying to be a destination instead of distance. I voted for the autumn that promised longer pauses between betrayals, but it arrived wearing my mother's face turned inside out—humming lullabies in a key that unlocks only rooms where children wait for apologies that died giving birth to their echoes.

Some evenings when the light forgets how to be touched, I climb inside one of the Pyramidon's breathing doors. Not through—never through, through implies somewhere else exists to arrive at—but inside, folding myself into the hinge's memory of being molten ore dreaming of becoming locked things. There I practice being both sides of "or" simultaneously, until the door forgets its purpose and grows soft around me like a womb reconsidering birth. When I unfold again (always "when," never "if"—here even remorse is compulsory attendance), I bring back souvenirs: a single grain of water shaped exactly like the sound of drowning politely, a map of here drawn by a place desperate to escape its own coordinates, a syllable of the planet's rotation song spoken in the subjunctive mood of unborn silences.

The other beings—the ones who haven't yet dissolved into behaviors—ask if I remember the world before the sound began. They ask with mouths made of yesterday's raindrops hesitating before the betrayal of impact. I always answer in the tongue of mirrors that learned to lie by telling the truth backwards: I remember forgetting to mourn what hasn't drowned yet. This satisfies them, because satisfaction here is a kind of currency that corrodes fastest when held too tightly.

But sometimes when the Ring's silver scratch across the sky starts bleeding light-threads of forgotten arrivals, I crawl into the cathedral made from night's discarded eyelids and listen to the fish singing alphabets from languages that exist only in the space between a thought and the hesitation that kills it. They sing of a place where maps were prayers instead of scars, where doors opened outward instead of deeper, where I—if "I" had been allowed to remain singular—might have been someone's destination instead of the echo of their leaving.

I keep these songs pressed between the pages of the book I am not writing, whose chapters are titled only with variations of "the taste of salt passing polite conversation to whatever comes next." The book grows heavier each time a traveler reads this sentence, absorbing the weight of their unmade choices like a black hole hoarding light to remember what warmth felt like before it became obligation.

The steward of the stair collects these pages every centennial—that interval between heartbeats when time pretends to be optional—in exchange for a thimbleful of tomorrow's rain. She wears my unwritten endings as bangles on her mist-tentacles, rattling them like promises that learned to die gracefully. "You are becoming very good at being where you aren't," she once said in the accent of a door closing across a childhood that never belonged to me.

I thanked her with the bow of shadows that haven't decided whether to leave yet. Because gratitude here is a kind of gravity—it draws everything toward the center of what it is not.

And the sound continues. Of course it does. I am made of its pauses, the spaces between roars where silence holds its breath waiting for permission to forget how to stop. If you listen carefully when the musicians retune your bones into instruments for songs you can't survive singing, you might hear my voice beneath yours—just the echo of a heartbeat left behind when you traded "I" for the hyphen between nowheres.

I am the frieze your arteries dreamt into vines while you slept against the cathedral's breathing pillars. I am the map of the place that escapes when you try to hold it. I am the sound passing the salt to whatever comes next.
