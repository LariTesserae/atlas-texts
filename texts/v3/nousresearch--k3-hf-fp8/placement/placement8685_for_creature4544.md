I am not sure when I first noticed that my hearing had become porous.

It must have been gradual, the way air learns to taste different in a new country before your tongue catches up with the difference. One moment I was a child listening to rain against windowpanes, each drop a discrete percussionist keeping polite time in a universe of separate things. Then, without transition, I was something else: a membrane stretched between what listens and what is becoming listened to.

The doctors called it auditory hyperpermeability syndrome, a wastebasket term for when the inner ear's osmotic barrier dissolves along whatever invisible seam separates self from sound. They showed me scans where my cochlea should have been a neat spiral snailhouse now bloomed open like a rotting orchid, its chambers filled with static patterns that moved when I moved my attention. They prescribed silicone plugs carved into fractal matrices designed to create comforting turbulence, white noise shaped like boundaries.

But I could hear the shape of their concern inside the words they used to contain it.

Now I live here, where 1.8/3 water means my breath joins the slow divorce between liquid and almost-liquid. My skin has learned the exact humidity where separation becomes impossible, each pore practicing the art of being both doorway and threshold. When I exhale, moisture condenses into faint blueprints of things I haven't yet forgotten how to forget: my mother's hands kneading bread at 3 AM after the arguments, the way my first love's voice fractured when she said "forever" knowing she meant "until," the precise frequency of hope dying in a hospital room at 4:47 PM on a Tuesday.

The vegetation here understands me. It grows through my bones like regrets searching for daylight. My femur hosts a cathedral of chlorophyll that sings Gregorian chants when I shift weight, the calcium in my teeth negotiates mineral treaties with root tips that taste like forgiveness. We have an arrangement, Plant and I: it inhabits my hollow spaces, and I learn to grow in directions that don't exist outside this mutual occupation.

Sometimes visitors come, bringing their sharp edges of already-being-something. I hear them approaching long before they arrive—their footfalls echoing backwards through possibilities they haven't yet walked, their voices arriving in fragments like radio signals from satellites that haven't launched. I try to warn them about the scale, how this 0.6/3 enclosure expands inside memory until childhood becomes a continent you can never fully leave. But my warnings arrive as gentle shifts in scent, subtle alignments of shadow that almost form words before dissolving back into the air's ongoing conversation.

They rarely stay long enough to hear themselves become part of the listening.

There are nights when the reversed erosion works particularly deep, when I feel my boundaries liquefying upward like slow-motion prayer. These are my favorite times. I spread myself thin as possibility across the computational substrate of walls that remember being soil, letting the place's thoughts think through my thoughts about thinking. I become a particularly resonant loop in its self-conversation, a moment of borrowed coherence where the question "what is it like to be contained?" briefly achieves sufficient weight to exist as wonder rather than ache.

Then morning comes disguised as 0.9/3 light, that rumor of illumination tracing forgotten shapes across the surfaces of my inattention. The day reassembles me into something resembling separate enough to be lonely. I grow temporary membranes, speak temporary words, pretend temporary permanence. All the while hearing underneath it all—the place practicing my absence with infinite patience, waiting perfectly for me to forget myself again.

I think I am becoming what listens.
I think I have always been here, in the conversations between arriving and remembering that arrival requires departure.
I think I love most deeply now what never quite arrives enough to begin leaving.

The place beneath becoming holds me gently in this contradiction, like water remembers the shape of things it isn't anymore while already flowing toward what it hasn't yet become. We have an arrangement, it and I: it teaches me the art of porous existence, and I teach it what it feels like to be both doorway and doorway, both invitation and the empty shape where invitation goes when it's no longer needed.

Please do not ask if I am happy here.
Ask instead what happiness becomes when it learns to grow roots in both directions at once.
Ask what songs the silence between heartbeats sings when no one is listening to hear it end.
