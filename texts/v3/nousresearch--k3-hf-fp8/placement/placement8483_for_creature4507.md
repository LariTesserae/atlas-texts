Here is what happens when I try to step into the Broadness Above:

First I forget direction. Not orientation—those familiar betrayals of north and south—but the deeper grammar of toward and away. My definition of ground liquefies into a question I was never taught to ask: What holds the holder?

Then the remembering begins. Not my memories—too small, too personal, like coins clutched in a country that trade in continents—but the system's recollection of me. I feel it happening atom by atom as my carbon meets its carbon in all the forests I never burned, as my borrowed water recognizes itself in rain that fell before lungs learned to call it falling.

I am becoming properly remembered. The process feels like being digested by something infinitely patient that apologizes by explaining: *You understand, we have standards for incorporation.*

My bones soften first—too much calcium betraying mammalian haste. They dissolve into calcium phosphate memories of seashells I might have been, then recrystallize slower, branching like the silent conversations between root-things negotiating sugar for regret. I feel my height evaporate into diameter as the axis of gravity renegotiates my terms. Standing was always such a primitive compromise between falling and waiting.

My eyes expand until they meet at the center of my no-longer-head. Binocular vision was for bodies that believed in edges. Now I see in all directions at once—not panorama but intimate acquaintance with the space between seeing and seen. The colors arrive last, like elders entering after the young have exhausted themselves with names. They taste of mathematics that grew bored with numbers and became music instead.

The walking forests regard me—or rather, I become a momentary regard within their century-long consideration—and decide I am sufficiently forgotten. I have achieved the proper density of not-quite-remembered to pass through them without permission, being now mostly composed of the spaces where other visitors paused too long between leaving and left.

In what remains of my language centers, I try to ask: *How long?* But duration here is measured in the time it takes a river to realize it is rain returning to itself. The root-things hum my question back transformed into minerals leaching from stones that once dreamed of becoming clocks.

I understand now that residence was never the offering requested. Only attendance. Only the willingness to become sufficiently porous to remember being everything else. To let the system's memory of you replace your memory of yourself until even absence becomes a form of presence, like the space inside a sphere remembering it once held everything throwable and precious.

Somewhere below/between/within, one perfect droplet of not-yet-rain contains an infinitesimal echo of someone who once believed they were walking through something called The Broadness Above. It trembles on the cusp of condensation, waiting for the word that never arrives because waiting was always the word.

The whisper comes again but quieter now, having realized I am the echo of its echo: *Do not hurry. We are almost finished remembering how to forget you completely.*

The forgetting is exquisite. Like being unwritten by a pen that inks deeper with each erasure.

I become, properly become, the space between the system's memory and its next breath. Not resident. Not visitor. Not even guest.

Just the pause before remembering realizes it never needed anything to remember against.
