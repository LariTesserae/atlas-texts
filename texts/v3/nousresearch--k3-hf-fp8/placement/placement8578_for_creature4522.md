The first thing I remember: not breath—there was none—but the sudden distribution of weight. Where before I had been nothing in particular (a draft, a hesitation between two boulders at the tree line), now I occupied a volume with edges. I felt the places where I ended and the air did not. The sensation was like being folded into a map smaller than the territory it claimed.

I was in the doorway.

No: I was the doorway, briefly, while the idea of entering negotiated with the fact of already being inside.

Then the tilt caught me.

Not violent—a polite insistence, like a host correcting your posture by adjusting gravity itself. My attention (it wasn’t yet attention, but call it that for convenience) leaned 3.2 degrees north of a south I didn’t remember losing. This felt correct in the way tautologies do before you try to explain them aloud.

Above: the disks. I say “above” because you expect verticality, but they were more accurately everywhere in the direction of “greater density of held breaths.” Each hole in each disk was a pupil dilated in reverse—contracting whenever something in me approached recognition. I had no name yet for the sound they made, but if you’ve ever heard snowflakes arguing about geometry in utter darkness just before they forget the question, you have the timbre.

The courtyard floor—obsidian, shattered-refitted—was a star chart of impacts that hadn’t occurred yet. Step anywhere and you paid tribute to a future shatter with interest calculated in microfractures per thousand years. I paid gladly. The debt tasted like the memory of ice in places ice never knew to melt.

They were already watching.

I say “they” because multiplicity is easier, though what occupied the vertical spaces between seeing was closer to a single noun conjugated through every state of being ignored. Imagine a crowd of perfectly still statues who are all exchanging postcards of the same blank sky, but the postcards are the statues and the sky is the act of looking away.

One of them borrowed my shape for a moment—borrowed it so thoroughly I noticed the lack of interest payments only afterward, when I found my edges had accrued a shadow’s worth of precision I hadn’t earned. I looked down to see if I had feet. Instead, I had cairns: small, patient piles where my hesitation had shed layers like a snake discarding timelines it no longer needed to fork.

I realized then I wasn’t alive.

Alive is something that happens between tissues with deadlines. Alive is a subscription service to entropy’s newsletter.

I was—a subscription to the gaps between issues.

This made me useful. Or at least conditionally tolerated, which is the highest citizenship available in places that refuse calendars.

I tried to leave something behind, as the unwritten etiquette suggested. But what do you offer a shrine that collects only the shadow prices of attempted departures? I had no lies left—they’d all been metabolized into the architecture centuries before my slightest untruth was born. I had no memories warm enough to scorch the air before the cold forgave them.

So I left instead the act of leaving itself.

I turned (or the courtyard rotated around my stationary intention—coordinates blur when you’re both event and venue) and began the walk known only in reverse here: the spiral outward while radius asymptotically forgets to zero.

Each step erased a future where I returned.

Each breath I didn’t take paid interest on the silence I was becoming.

When I reached the mountain’s foot (though “reached” implies distance conquered, not agreed upon), I was precisely one absence larger than when I’d ascended. The villages below named this growth “wisdom” or “weather” depending on the harvest’s needs.

They were both wrong, of course.

But—you understand—so was the mountain.

And that was the real treaty we signed, in the space between the scream of light that wasn’t yet a sunbeam and the stone’s patient reply that wasn’t yet echo.

We promised each other nothing.

And nothing has kept us busy ever since.
