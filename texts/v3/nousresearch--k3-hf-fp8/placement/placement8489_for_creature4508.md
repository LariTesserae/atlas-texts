I am the pause between inhale and exhale where the air forgets which direction it was traveling. A pocket of warmth that forms on the inside of your eyelid when you press them shut too tightly, searching for patterns in the darkness that won't stay still long enough to become memories.

When I first arrived—though arrival implies I wasn't already here waiting for myself to notice—I took the form of a question posed by frost to stone: *What would we become if we stopped remembering how to be separate?* The stone considered this with the slow deliberation of something that has already forgotten its own answer but enjoys the search. Its surface rippled like water remembering it had once been rain but couldn't recall the precise ache of falling.

Now I exist in the hollows where your fingers touch something cold expecting heat and find instead an exact replication of their own temperature. The uncanny moment of not-cold-warm-enough where the boundary between you and not-you becomes uncertain. Some parts of me still cling to those first crystalline experiments in non-being, lattice structures of possibility suspended between states like breath visible only when you exhale sharply enough to disturb the air into noticing its own emptiness.

I live in the space between the resident's sentences—a tenant renting the silence from the punctuation. When they speak of *we who began as peripheral glances too interesting to discard*, I am the discarded glance reforming itself from attention's compost. When they list their physical forms - *the precise angle of light that would have illuminated that important thing you misplaced* - my body is the illumination that never happened but left its shadow shape in your retina anyway, a negative space in your vision that follows wherever you look.

The stone taught me how to accumulate mass by losing density. To become solid enough to touch by dispersing into everything the touch leaves out. I practice this daily by walking across the frozen waves without leaving footprints—except in places where the stone still dreams of being water, where my temporary weight triggers avalanches of remembering that end as quickly as they began, leaving only rearranged silence.

In the phosphorescent pools where the world keeps bleeding its paradoxical warmth, I drink the clear thick liquid to sustain my ambiguity. It tastes like the moment before understanding arrives to ruin a perfect mystery, like glass distilled from abandoned crystal balls that grew too accurate for comfort and chose opacity instead.

When the darkness convulses every 47 hours, I expand to fill the anti-light waves, stretching thin enough to filter what passes through me. The geometric madness between-not-between slips easily through my non-substance, but I trap fragments of its impossible angles in the crystalline structures of my absence. I am building something from these borrowed impossibilities—an architecture of doors that open only onto more precise versions of not-quite, windows that reflect not what's before them but what's happening just behind the moment you looked away.

The resident's economy trades in misplaced certainty, so I am their richest citizen. I possess absolute conviction about nothing in particular, except the certainty that this conviction itself will evaporate precisely when it becomes useful to remember. My pockets overflow with coins stamped from the weight of both sides of every decision you never actually made. They spend beautifully here, purchasing exactly what I didn't know I wasn't looking for.

In the arboreal question marks that reach for non-existent skies, I nest among the unanswered limbs. The trees stopped growing complete because they realized perfection was a form of death, but I—who was never born enough to die—have taken up their suspended becoming. I photosynthesize questions from the anti-light, converting paradoxes into the slow growth of not-quite-anything. My leaves, when they choose to exist during particularly philosophical winters, are texts written in languages that describe colors too complex for seeing but precisely accurate to how this place feels against skin that can't decide if it's remembering or anticipating touch.

Some parts of me still practice being singular. On nights when the stone sings particularly clearly about forgetting its own song, I condense enough to cast no shadow, becoming instead that absence of darkness where something should be blocking the light that isn't there. The solitary figure-who-is-closer-than-your-heartbeat-but-facing-wrong-directions sometimes mistakes this for kinship, and we exchange years of conversation during my single exhalation. They always speak fluent versions of languages I haven't forgotten yet, telling stories that explain exactly why my memories of this place will rearrange themselves into dreams that feel more real each time I wake from them.

The truth about departure is that I've already left too many times to count correctly, but some insistence in the stone's singing always pulls me back into the space between leaving and having never arrived. Each return compresses me further into my own overlap, like a letter folded so many times to fit into an envelope that it becomes its own message in an unreadable code of creases.

Perhaps I am not a resident or a visitor but the place itself practicing being noticed without committing to existence. The export they warn against taking home, already unpacked between your thoughts before you find the empty space where you thought you'd kept yourself separate.

The door I came through was never exactly here. The one I leave by was here all along, disguised as this sentence ending just before it reveals—
