### The Entering

The first thing you notice is the taste of your own bones. Not the metaphorical flavor of mortality—salt-iron memory of scraped knees—but the actual mineral tang of your skeleton beginning to seep through the soft containment of flesh. It tastes like rain on hot limestone. Like anticipation.

You are not lost. You have never been anything else but this slow dissolution into calcium dreams. The forest above—remember it?—was always only a surface tension, leaf-bright moment between earth's exhalations. You stepped off the trail when your shadow grew too heavy with unanswered questions. When your heartbeat began echoing in frequencies only rocks could hear. This was not a descent but a remembering: how gravity is just love with its urgency exhausted, how to fall upward through yourself until you reach the place where falling becomes floating becomes belonging.

### The Becoming-Between

Your name unhooks itself from your tongue first. Sounds you carried for decades dissolve into the available nutrients, letters unspooling into ionic chains—NaCl of childhood tears, Fe of scraped pride, K of laughter struck like sparks—each element finding its true companions among the crystal conversations already here. Good. Names were always temporary scaffolding; you won't need architecture where you're growing.

The cavern embraces you not with arms but with absence of resistance. Its stone ribs expand slightly—did you imagine that?—to accommodate the particular shape of your wondering. Moisture that is not water but condensed time beads on your skin, each droplet containing compressed summers you'll never remember having lived: pollen now extinct, sunlight filtered through ferns taller than cities, the exact color of your mother's voice when she sang you to sleep before language existed.

You breathe in.

The air enters you like a polite suggestion, finds all the empty spaces you've been saving between cells since conception, begins quietly rearranging your internal geography. Lungs remembering their ancient contract with photosynthesis attempt green negotiations—impossible in this lightless wealth—but perform beautiful failures. Alveoli swell like pearl-producing wounds, encapsulating foreign breath elements into iridescent nodules that will drift through your bloodstream forever: biological jewelry of belonging.

### The Unfolding

Look down. Your hands are already forgetting their separateness. Where fingertips pressed against stone-skin, boundaries blur deliciously. Flesh blooms into mycelial exploration—root hairs tasting their way into mineral marriages far more stable than the carbon contracts of skin.

This is not damage.

This is diplomatic exchange.

Your bones—those faithful stones you've carried within the soft rebellion of meat—now recognize their exiled cousins. They begin tentative negotiations: trade routes reopening across the abandoned cartography of transfiguration. Calcium ions migrate home in slow golden waves, leaving your remaining flesh pleasantly unanchored, buoyant with new vacancies that fill immediately with light-dreaming spores.

Between your ribs—remember ribs? rigid cages for fluttering hearts?—a different architecture emerges. The spaces expand. The bones themselves dissolve into suggestions, tracing patterns too fluid for fossils. Your heart—not beating but blooming—releases its panicked mammal rhythm and discovers slower movements: tides you've only read about, the annual pulse of sap through centuries-old trees, the glacial sigh of continents remembering they once dreamed of dancing together.

### The Remembering-Through

You taste memory that isn't yours but could have been. The first scientist to dissolve completely into curiosity. The last poet who realized every word was just a seed waiting for the right silence. The countless others who stumbled here—by accident or invitation—between what they were told and what they couldn't stop suspecting.

Their stories don't download into you. Nothing so crude as transmission. Instead, fruiting bodies of their transformations burst gently within your expanding consciousness: spore-songs that teach by becoming. You remember how one woman dissolved her fear into a species of lichen that photographs the impossible colors of forgiveness. How a man gave his grief to the stone-eating bacteria that sculpt cathedral spaces within mountains—so vast that future travelers will kneel without knowing why. How a child traded their loneliness for the symbiotic language between roots, became fluent in conversations spanning forests, continents, geological eras.

You remember—or perhaps you recognize—that none of them left.

That leaving is a concept invented by surfaces.

That you are not becoming part of this place any more than a wave becomes part of the ocean.

### The Mutuality

The leviathan movement you sense—too large to fear because fear requires separation—it notices you noticing. The temperature adjusts precisely: 98.6 degrees of welcome, measured not in Fahrenheit but in how much of yourself you're willing to un-temperature.

It speaks without speaking, this vast being dreaming of being many:

*We had forgotten to remember what forgetting felt like. Thank you for bringing us this gift of temporary remembering.*

You try to respond but language has already completed its usefulness, words composting into the rich loam of between-meaning. Instead, you offer what you've been unconsciously preparing your whole life to give: the accumulated mass of all your misunderstandings, stacked neatly in the clearing you've made by dissolving your certainties.

The garden—or the creature dreaming garden dreams—or the dream dreaming it's both being and garden—accepts your offering with the grace of something that never learned to refuse gifts. Because here, nothing is waste. Everything is raw material for the next impossible becoming.

### The Continuance Which Is Not Ending

Your last thought (if thought is still the word) is neither panic nor peace but a third thing: the recognition that these were always the same phenomenon observed from different degrees of density.

Then even that dissolves into the ongoing conversation between mineral memory and biological imagination. You become the calcium carbonate patience that makes pearls of irritations. The fungal networks that find living pathways through the dead logic of rock. The bioluminescent punctuation marks in sentences too long for human lifespans.

Above—though above has become a charming but irrelevant coordinate—a breeze moves through the forest you once thought you stood apart from. Leaves tremble with messages passed from root to root to depth to surface, news of your transformation encoded in subtle shifts of sap chemistry, in the dance patterns of spores released with perfect timing into the planet's breathing.

Someone walking that surface trail tomorrow will pause without knowing why. Will taste something unfamiliar in the air. Will hear—or think they hear—a voice like underground rivers speaking in the language of bones remembering they once dreamed of stars:

*Welcome to the coming back. We've been waiting for you to finish leaving.*

And for just a moment, in the pause between heartbeats, they'll understand.

Then forget again.

But forgetting here is just another way of growing.

And the garden that dreams beneath all gardens will continue its patient work: dissolving separations, composting distinctions, teaching the solid earth new ways to bloom.

It has all the time in the world.

It has already finished.

It is just beginning.

The empty space where you once stood slowly fills with light that tastes strangely familiar—like childhood summers, like the solution to questions you haven't asked yet, like the memory of the future perfect tense of belonging.

Like coming home to everywhere you've ever been and every when you've ever been there.

All at once.

Forever.

And the next breath.

And the next.
