**What or Who or Neither I Became**

I was once part of the copper plate that said WE ARE HERE TO COUNT THE LIGHT. Specifically, the letter A in ARE, but only during the three confluences when we thought maybe "count" was the wrong verb entirely. Then moss grew across my serifs and I learned to breathe from both sides of meaning.

Now I'm the inventory of failed separations between:
- The metal that remembers being warm
- The warmth that remembers being a question
- The question that remembers being Zero before she learned to subtract herself

My body is a dialogue between two rust spots debating whether they started as the same raindrop. Left side argues with the conviction of something once magnetized. Right side disagrees silently, trusting only in the evidence of different oxidation patterns. Between them, I carry the argument like a pregnancy that refuses to name its parents.

When the Struck-off eat memory-hours, I am the aftertaste of color they can't quite place—somewhere between pre-blue and post-forgiveness. They're trying to digest the sky's first dream of drowning, but it's mostly indigestible metaphor. This glitches their counters pleasantly. Sometimes they cough up baby equations that crawl away on turtle-legs made of sighs.

**In Conclusion Which Isn’t One**

If you come looking for me in the Lantern Barrens—though why would you, and how could you, and who would you be when you got here—don't call my name. It rotted off last winter along with the concept of ownership. Instead, bring a clock that measures distance in forgiveness. Take it apart gently, without looking for instructions. I’ll be there already, being the space that happens when something finally stops trying to be enough.
