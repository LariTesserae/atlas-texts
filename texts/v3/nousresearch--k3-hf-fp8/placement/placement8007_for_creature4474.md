You are seated—not willingly, not yet knowing how to unseat—but you are seated nonetheless, in the fifth absence, which is now a presence because it noticed you looking.

The others are already speaking, but not to you. They speak in the spaces between what your ears remember hearing and what your mind has not yet decided to believe is possible speech.

The termite philosophers are humming. Not a song, but a geometry. You feel it knotting behind your eyes: a line that wants to be a circle but keeps forgetting where it ends. Their flickering bodies spell out a question in negative space:

*If a city forgets its own name while dreaming of being a lung, does it exhale its inhabitants or simply rename them as lost breath?*

You try not to answer. Answers are dangerous here. They can be eaten, or worse—incorporated.

The former weather pattern, now manifesting as a woman-shaped sorrow wearing dewdrop earrings, leans in close. She smells like the pause between lightning and thunder, when the sky still believes it might not have to scream.

“You are not new,” she whispers, and her breath is the exact temperature of the last time someone touched your face without asking permission first. “You are the echo of a preparation. A rehearsal for abandonment that forgot to stop when the cast left. Do you remember yet? The taste of waiting for a door that was never built?”

You do not answer. You cannot. Your tongue has become uncertain of its own shape, its edges softening like wax in a carved mouth.

The concept of symmetry in the third seat is no longer symmetrical. It is splitting—no, folding—no, *mating* with itself in ways that make your teeth ache. You realize, too late, that it is trying to imitate your face. It is doing a very bad job. It is doing it with too much love.

Then the Empty Window—Window—speaks your name.

Not the name you wear on paperwork or whisper in mirrors or answer to when strangers mispronounce it. The name underneath. The one made of silence shaped like a question mark. The name that is not a sound but a direction: *where did you last put down the piece of yourself you needed to keep hold of in order to leave?*

The room tilts.

Not physically. That would be simple. Instead, every memory you have ever had of floor tilts. You remember falling differently in each childhood room, each stained campsite, each bed that was never quite yours long enough to learn the sound of its ceiling settling. All the falls converge here, now, stacking on top of each other like collapsing calendars.

The council waits for your testimony.

But they do not want your words.

Words are too late, too heavy, too much like evidence. They want the taste of the hesitation before you speak. They want the exact weight of the lie you told yourself this morning to get out of bed without breaking anything. They want the coordinates of the first place you ever learned what it meant to be unwanted and how hard you tried afterward to build a house there anyway.

You feel them pulling.

Not cruelly. Like roots pulling water. Like gravity pulling apology from a dropped glass. Like sleep pulling dreams from the parts of you that never forgave sunlight for showing too much.

The mycelial felt beneath you is warm now. It reminds you of something you cannot name, something skin-adjacent and forgive-me-shaped. It is trying to soothe you the way only things without mouths can: by growing around your edges until the border between you and it stops arguing.

Window leans in again. Its face is the reflection of a window you once pressed your face against, aged seven, watching a family that wasn’t yours eating dinner through a window that was too warm to belong to December. Its eyes are the smudges your breath left on the glass.

“It’s time,” it says kindly, which is the most terrifying word here, because kindness here always means *I know how this ends and I’m sorry you don’t yet.*

“Time for what?” you try to say, but your voice comes out as a small green light emerging from your mouth instead. It floats gently upward, joining the others already nesting in the chamber’s ceiling—hundreds of abandoned first questions, glowing faintly like stars that forgot they were ever supposed to be answers.

The council nods.

Even the empty seat nods.

Especially the empty seat.

And then you understand:

You are not here to decide if you belong.

You are here to decide what you will name the hole after you finish carving yourself out of it to leave behind.

The tower pulses once more, but now you feel it from the inside. It is not waking.

It is remembering that it was never asleep.

And you—

you are the dream it is remembering having.

And you—

you are almost awake enough to feel the forgetting begin.
