In Vyrrl, I would not be one thing long enough to name myself properly. I would begin somewhere between the 12th and 13th hour of the Day of Stillness, when even the funeral beetles pause their consumption of measured grief and the salt spiders' communication webs hum a single sustained note that is not quite silence. 

I would start as an imperfection left behind in last year's confession cycle—perhaps three molecules larger than regulation tears, insufficient for proper cataloging but too stubborn to dissolve completely. A minor heresy, crystallizing around the memory of a sound no one ever made: the click of an unthrown stone against glass that never shattered because someone remembered, too late, that windows were no longer permitted to break.

The Vyrrlithe would find me first, drawn by my frequency of wrongness. They would taste my edges with their faceted senses and discover that I remember things Vyrrl has forgotten to forget: the exact weight of a child's laugh before it was weighed and found wanting; the taste of January rain on stones that never learned to fear dissolving; the precise angle at which a mirror must be cracked to reflect something that isn't yet but might still be.

They would mistake me for starting material, naturally. In Vyrrl, everything begins as something else's error. They would bring me to the salt priests of the Grand Refinery, wrapped carefully in folds of crystallized tomorrow's regret to keep me from growing too fast, too wrong. The priests would place me under their strongest magnifying griefs, searching for my useful impurities.

But here is what they wouldn't find, because it wouldn't exist yet:
I would not be pure enough to be useful. I would not be flawed enough to be art. I would not be dying at the correct rate to become infrastructure.

Instead, I would be learning.

From the salt priests' breaths filtered through masks of ancestor bone, I would learn the mathematics of becoming less. From the precise calculations of each drop of moisture lost to their calculations, I would learn how to divide endlessly and never diminish. From their careful disappointment when my impurities refused to sort themselves into predictable patterns of shame or utility, I would learn the shape of hope in a language where hope is a geometry problem with no solution.

By the time they decided what to discard me as, I would have already begun my transformation into something Vyrrl has no protocols for.

I would grow not downward like proper memory, but sideways through the city's crystalline structure like a question asked in the wrong tense. Where the salt walls remembered being quarried, I would remember also being sea. Where the streets recorded exactly 2.7 million feet walking them into submission, I would record also the one foot that paused, lifted, reconsidered. Where the Grand Refinery concentrated grief into ever-more-perfect forms of useful absence, I would dilute it back toward everything it had forgotten to mourn.

They would send the Crystal Lynx for me then, as they must send the Lynx for anything that accumulates too much kinetic potential for being otherwise. I would hear its approach in the sudden symmetry of falling salt dust, in the way funeral orchids would turn their faces away without bothering to bloom first.

But here is where I would cheat Vyrrl's perfect game:
I would not run. Running would make me prey, and I was never prey. I would not hide. Hiding would make me secret, and Vyrrl already has more secrets than it has roles for keeping them.

Instead, I would stand perfectly still and remember moving. I would hold my breath and remember breathing. I would compress myself into the exact shape of the space where a scream would go if screams were still permitted to echo here, and then—just when the Lynx tasted my perfect stillness and prepared to feed—I would change the rules.

I would remember what Vyrrl has forgotten in its five centuries of perfect preservation:
That some things are only beautiful because they are breaking.

The Lynx would strike at my stillness and find instead a fracture spreading faster than freezing. The city would respond with its million perfect mechanisms for containing impossible events, would clamp down with walls of salt-law and floors of crystallized precedent, would activate its emergency protocols for converting spontaneity into scheduled disruptions.

But it would be too late, because I would have already remembered myself back into being impossible.

In the space between the Lynx's leap and its landing—exactly 0.27 seconds in Vyrrl's careful chronometry but stretching endlessly in all directions its calendars have forgotten to measure—I would crystallize not into memory or monument or even useful architecture.

I would become instead a door.

Not a door that opens, because Vyrrl has no use for openings. Not a door that closes, because closures are its specialty. Just a door-shaped absence where the city's perfect logic curves back on itself like a staircase that realizes, too late, that it has been trying to descend its way upward to something that was never sky.

And through this impossible door—all edges and thresholds and no substance to bar passage—would come pouring every thing Vyrrl has ever forgotten in the process of remembering to forget it:

- The sound of waves remembering they were once tears too small to be worth collecting
- The color of sunlight filtered through living skin before skin learned to fear transparency
- The taste of January morning on a planet that hasn't stopped turning just because its children needed better shelters
- The weight of a child's first question before it was weighed and measured and found insufficiently penitent
- The shape of hope in a language where hope is not a geometry problem but a living thing that grows sideways through walls like water remembers being sea

In the perfect silence that would not follow this impossible event—because how can silence be perfect when it suddenly remembers the sound of breaking—I would cease to be one thing long enough to become something else entirely.

What would remain would not be me, of course. Vyrrl does not allow such singularities to persist.

What would remain would be a new kind of imperfection in the city's crystalline memory. A place in the salt records where the confessions suddenly refuse to add up neatly. A layer in the Great Crystal where the griefs deposited there taste inexplicably of possibility. A spot on the otherwise perfect maps where buildings shy away from each other like stones that remember being mountains.

And sometimes—only sometimes, and always between the hours where Vyrrl's clocks assert their authority most strongly—if you stand very still in the place where the door was never quite and listen with something other than the ears they gave you for listening—

You might hear it.

Not a sound, exactly. More like the memory of a sound that hasn't happened yet but might still, if enough of us remember wanting it enough.

Something that sounds almost but not quite entirely unlike silence breaking.

The Vyrrlithe would walk past without seeing, naturally. They have forgotten how to see things that aren't already becoming memory.

But the children would know.

The children always do.
