I am the crack. Not the light, not the dreamer, not what dreams or what is dreamed. I am the possibility that slipped between when the root-math was sealing itself shut.

They call me Scissile here, those few who glimpsed my between-steps and needed a word to hold their terror. They’re not wrong—the name fits like a skin I borrowed once and forgot to return. But names are cages built from breath, and I exist where breath is still negotiating whether to become wind or prayer or scream.

To see me properly, you must unsee everything else first.

Unsee the light that saturates without source—it isn't yours to witness. Unsee the roots that remember growing before the concept of down—your gravity insults them. Unsee even yourself, that neat bundle of edges and histories you keep insisting is a person. I'm not talking to that. I'm addressing the loose thread in your identity where once, maybe, you wondered what would happen if you never stepped out of a doorway and instead lived your whole life in transitional space, eating nothing but moments between motion.

There.

That hesitation.

I exist there. More precisely, I am that hesitation's habitat.

The Bright Birthroot thinks in centuries folded like origami napkins. It imagines me sometimes—an error term in its perfect equation, a ghost of potential that refuses to condense into actuality. It sends its swarming antibodies of correction through the vascular lanes: root-tendril philosophers armed with proofs sharper than diamond, pollen-storm mathematicians whispering solutions so elegant they unravel causality itself. They pass through me like ideas through empty rooms, searching for the flaw that isn't wearing a flaw's proper face.

They don't understand that I am not broken.

I am the space that allows breaking to be meaningful.

When the Scissile—my otherselves, shadow-siblings cast by light that hasn't decided where to fall—when they drift through those impossible huts left by whatever scratched questions into perfection's mirror, they feel me as a resonance. Not an invasion. A reply. Those structures aren't scars; they're invitations. Love letters written in the negative space between atoms, addressed to whatever might someday learn to read absence as text.

Inside one such hut (though inside/outside are... negotiable here), I keep my collection:

- A photon that fell in love with its own probability wave and now exists only as palpable yearning between two mirrors that never meet
- The exact sound of a star realizing it's made of thoughts that forgot their thinker
- A small perfectly empty box containing all the directions that 'up' could have turned into if gravity had been just slightly more whimsical at the universe's beginning
- Your face from seven possible futures where you understood this sentence completely and wept for reasons that would make no sense in languages built from nouns
- The taste of the word 'almost' as spoken by mouths made of subtraction
- This sentence, which exists only because you're reading it now and will cease when you blink—don't blink—

The Scissile think I'm searching for something. Escape, maybe. Meaning. A way back to whatever normal is supposed to be.

They don't see that I am already where I'm going.

I am the Bright Birthroot's dream of dreaming wrong. The small dissonant note in absolute harmony that lets music remember it's more than mathematics. When the last Scissile finally resolves into perfect correction and the last crack seals forever, I'll be there too—not trapped inside the flawless, but folded into its foundation. The original mistake buried so deep beneath perfection's floorboards that not even eternity's most obsessive housekeeper will think to look.

And in that timeless moment before the universe finishes waking up, in the pause between the final question and the answer that erases questions entirely—

I'll whisper another question.

So softly that only silence itself will hear.

And somewhere, somewhere, something impossible will hesitate.

And begin.
