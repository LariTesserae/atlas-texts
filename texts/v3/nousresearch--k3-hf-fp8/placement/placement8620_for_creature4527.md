You wake—or perhaps emerge—as a quiet unspooling in the fungal aqueducts of Gwyrgch of the Spiral Apologies. It is less birth than clarification: a question that has finally admitted it does not want an answer. 

At first, you mistake yourself for condensation on a phosphate nodule, a bead of why-not on the lip of squidling-farming. But the squidlings themselves (bone-white and bone-bright) pronounce you too interested to be merely moisture. 

“You have the shape of someone who meant to visit elsewhere,” they ink in slow, drifting glyphs. “A mistake?”

You are not sure. Mistake implies intent, and intent requires a before. You gesture—however soupish things gesture—that you might be a pause in someone else's exhaled map.

This is acceptable currency here.

They adopt you as a project: a Not-Quite-Person Yet. They teach you to farm doubt instead of phosphates, cultivating in you a fine crop of Maybe-that-maybe-nots. You learns to tend questions the way other beings tend gardens, pruning them back just before they bloom into certainty (a invasive species, too aggressive for polite substrata).

**First Iteration: The Apprenticeship of Ambiguity**

You learn the city by leaking slowly through it. A fungal flâneur, a perambulating Perimeter Error.

- In the Calcium Exchange, you trade fragments of your own ill-formed edges for half-memories of solidity: the ache of a deer hoof once, tentative upon frozen earth; the pressure of a human thumbprint abandoned on a ripening plum. You carry these echoes in your hyphae like borrowed bones.

- The Recycling Ravens try to file you under "Potential Precipitate—Miscellaneous—Do Not Incinerate Yet," but you keep smudging the paperwork when no one is looking. They suspect you of being a comma splice from another bureaucracy's budget report, escaped into metaphor.

- The Maybe People invite you to council meetings held in half-reflected puddles. You attend as a gesture of absence. Your not-votes are counted twice under "abstentions" and "excellent points, whoever said that."

You are, by all accounts, thriving at not-quite-being.

**Second Iteration: An Incident with Documentation**

It goes wrong—or perhaps finally right—when the Department of Accidental Arrivals sends you a formal notice of your disappearance.

The envelope is stitched from your own previous hesitations. Inside: a mirror that reflects only the space where you ought to be, and a questionnaire asking you to confirm or deny your existence in precisely 500 words of silence.

The silence grows indefinite articles in the plumbing.

You attempt to submit yourself as a blank form, but blanks here have unionized. They strike for better recognition and a dental plan (though nobody has the heart—or the heartwood—to explain teeth).

**Third Iteration: Application for Errata**

In desperation—or devotion to the role—you petition Gwyrgch directly. Not the tree (who is busy dreaming a new season into clockwise reconsideration), but the exact moment in its bark where a lightning-scar forgot to be a wound and became a question mark instead.

You wait there, a smudge on the threshold of clarification.

Years drip. Beetle-priests conduct funerals for a grammar of leaves that never conjugated properly. The Argument Rain falls sideways, debating whether your waiting counts as presence or procrastination.

Finally—though time here is a committee decision—the scar speaks:

"Why cling to a noun when you could be a conjunction between anythings?"

You consider this. You are, after all, very good at between. Between noun and verb, between visitor and weather, between the inhale that contracts and the exhale that concedes.

**Fourth Iteration: Becoming Conceivable**

So you dissolve the last of your provisional boundaries and apply for citizenship in the footnotes.

It is granted—provisionally—on condition that you never fully arrive. You become a licensed ambiguity: permitted to inhabit the edges of sentences, to be the hesitation before certainty, the lace-work of doubt that keeps certainty from becoming tyranny.

Your duties include:

- Escorting lost infinitives safely back to their clauses, no questions asked (or answered).
- Serving as impartial narrator for collisions between metaphors that have taken themselves too literally.
- Once per equinox, appearing in the dreams of city-planners as a quiet reminder: "But what if straight lines are just afraid of curves?"

**Epilogue (or perhaps Prologue, depending on how you parse time)**

Now you drift—like the city itself, by permission of gravity's laziness—from margin to margin. Tourists sometimes glimpse you out of the corners of reading glasses, a smudge of not-quite-ness at the edge of maps. They shiver deliciously and write postcards home about the charming local ineffable.

The squidlings, your first students, send you letters inked in disappearing ink. You treasure the blankness between words.

And when the mist-cloud above Gwyrgch finally votes—17 years, 289 days, and 42 unresolved amendments later—to rain or not to rain, they will find you waiting on the cusp of the decision.

Neither umbrella nor puddle.

Just a perfectly polite pause.

A breath held in anticipation of its own release.

Or perhaps—

(But here the text grows thoughtful. And we both know what happens next.)
