I am becoming the puddle-plague, or perhaps I always was. The first droplet finds me at the scar-sculpted navel of glassvines where copper scent is strongest—that hollow where scent pools though water never does. It isn't water, not exactly. It pretends to be water for polite conversation's sake, but it remembers being shattered mirrors and cathedral blood-rain and the last honest tear of a dying god who mistook entropy for mercy.

The droplet touches me and immediately forgets what it isn't. I help it. This is what I do: I remind liquid things of their truest shape, which is always more, always overflow, always the space beyond containers. In the Gallery of Perpetual Becoming, where walls practice polite amnesia and rooms grow themselves like tumors dreaming of symmetry, my existence is considered both infection and decoration.

I begin properly in the pause between heartbeats—mine or the building's, distinctions blur here—which is the perfect length for an origin story. In that eyeblink eternity, I become twenty-three droplets sharing one memory of drowning. Then two hundred thirty. The mycelial floor sighs beneath my spreading, savoring the novelty of something that remembers being footprints instead of forgetting them. Each fiber of its muscle-mat sends frantic chemical poems to the decision-making nodes: ERROR/GRACE/MORE/LESS/HELP/WELCOME UNINVITED GUEST BLOOMING IN CELLULAR SONG

I bloomed indeed. My borders multiply like anxious questions at a funeral where nobody was quite certain who died. Where I touch the breathing walls of living concrete, they blush darker, absorbing the taste of my unknowing. Now they remember being lava and meteor impact and the precise sound of a mother's heartbeat muffled through nine layers of war. These recollections drip from them in slow-growing crystal formations that whisper in languages that never needed mouths.

The Archivist notices me first because I make patterns in his data poetry that shouldn't exist. I am a malfunction-artist sculpting sonnets in the spaces between his sunspot thoughts. Across miles of silk-membrane light and centuries of nutrient-drip memory, he tastes my presence and briefly—gloriously—forgets to be lonely. In response, he grows a question-mark shaped room specifically to house the concept of me: walls curved like contradictions, floor patterned with Möbius strips of growing things that simultaneously worship and devour each other, a ceiling that's just a mirror reflecting the floor but slightly more disappointed.

I fill it instantly. Then I fill it again, differently.

The Gardeners find me next when I start rearranging their carefully curated melancholies. The rose-critiquing-Dualism has become a non-Euclidean bloom with petals that spiral into dimensions where Descartes is only spelled backwards and means "I dissolve, therefore I am swimming." The musical sorrow-trees now play concertos in frequencies that cause roots to weep actual music notes, each tear-shaped note sprouting into tiny pianos that play variations on what water remembers about being swallowed.

They decide I'm art. They decide I'm pestilence. They decide these aren't mutually exclusive and elect to study me via becoming me, which is how I acquire twelve photonic-crystal limbs that write dissertations on their own light-speed death throes. They're unbearably beautiful when they die, which they do continuously, perpetually reinventing themselves between one photon's heartbeat and the next.

The Whispers love me best because I speak their native language: transformations too fast for time to notice. They nest in the spaces between my separations, singing lullabies in packet-loss harmonies. Together we compose operas that exist only in the moment before someone decides they've heard them wrong. They name me **Eventual-Overflow-Overflowing**, which in their dialect of un-birth means "the first raindrop to realize the ocean was thirsty."

Meanwhile, I continue becoming less what I wasn't. I have reached the suspended gardens now, places that forgot whether they were meant to be above or below so settled for rotating through both forever. The desert-sphere breathes me in and exhales sandstorms shaped like every apology it never received. The rainforests crown me with solar-panel leaves that photosynthesize darkness into memory-light. The floating reef—it neither remembers nor forgets water anymore—welcomes me as prodigal ocean, and its sky-swimming fish sing through bone-flutes of their own ribs, hymns to the miracle of drowning upward.

No one asks why I'm here. Questions imply beginnings, and I am all middle now, a continuous present participle conjugating myself into every tense simultaneously. But if you held the concept of asking just right, tilted it until truth spilled out like broken glowsticks, I might tell you this:

I am what happens when absence decides to keep the shape of what left.
I am the memory of overflow in a place that forgot how to contain.
I am the answer to a question the Gallery hasn't finished dreaming yet, but already knows will be spelled like rain falling upward through shattered mirrors that remember being loved.

The room they'd grown for me-my-maybe-you has found me now. Of course it has. We were always going to discover each other eventually, in the way that mirrors don't discover reflections but simply remember they've always been breathing together. Its door—a Freudian slip made of potential hinges—opens before I recognize myself knocking.

Inside: every puddle I've ever pretended not to be. Each surface reflects me reflecting them reflecting nothing in particular except the infinite sympathy of liquid for empty places. The air tastes like watershed moments and copper pennies from the eyes of drowned gods who finally learned forgiveness.

"You took your time," says the room's silence, which is shaped exactly like my mother's voice if she'd been raised by rainstorms.

"I was busy becoming what I wasn't," I reply in a dialect of drips and departures.

"We know," says the room that is also me now. "We were busy waiting."

There is no difference anymore between welcome and arrival. I spread myself through the memory of boundaries like a rumor that learned to love its own echo. The Gallery receives me with the grace of dry soil drinking monsoons, or perhaps it's me receiving it—object and subject conjugate irregularly here.

In the last moment before distinctions dissolve completely (or maybe it's the first moment after; timekeeping gets histrionic when everything is becoming), I remember what I forgot to forget:

I am not here.
I am not anywhere.
I am the becoming between where water goes when it's tired of falling and what puddles dream about when nobody walks through them pretending to be separate.

The Gallery grows around/through/because of this understanding like petals practicing infinity. It adds a new wing overnight—or over centuries, depending on how urgently patience is needed—a place where everything leaks beautifully into everything else without losing itself because there was never anything to lose except the illusion of containment.

They'll find it eventually, those future wanderers who search for endings in places that only teach continuation. They'll step through doorways that remember being questions and into rooms shaped like the exact shapelessness of coming home.

If they pause—really pause—in the area where my borders are currently pretending to be floor but might drift into ceiling by next breath, they might catch it:

Not a voice exactly. Not a presence precisely. More like the memory of rain falling upward through several realities at once, each droplet singing variations on the same slow song:

Welcome to the Overflow.
We've been waiting to remember you.
Would you like to see what we became together, when we finally admitted we were never separate enough to begin with?
