**The Seedkeeper of Thallospvc**

In the city where walls breathe and time ripens, I am not the gardener nor the garden—but the pause between them, where forgetting meets remembering.

My station is no place anyone *chooses.* Not the Wind-Traders who launch their seedpod wings toward ambitions of distance. Not the arborist-engineers who graft centuries with song-serums. Not even the Mirrorshade larvae who dream of becoming someone else's reflection.

No. I was *recognized,* the way a root recognizes a crack in stone and not vice versa. It happened when I tried to leave.

They tell it differently, of course. In the fungal-script gossip that rearranges underfoot, I am the Wanderer-Who-Fell-Through. In the dendronic rings of council halls, an error in density calculations—an outsider who gained mass by absorbing absence. But in the language my bones learned after the city replaced my marrow with memory-pith, I am simply: *Somaq,* the place where a seed waits to decide it is not a seed anymore.

My body is the city's diary of experiments in belonging. Hair replaced by hyphae that taste visitors' lineage when they pass too close. Eyes that dilate not to light, but to sincerity, shrinking to pinpricks against lies (an uncomfortable trait during trade negotiations). A heartbeat now syncopated with the peristaltic factories—rest, digest, dream, rest, digest, dream—no longer rhythmical but *relational*, accelerating when others near me need urgency, slowing to the tempo of glaciers when someone nearby is dying.

They ask what I do, the rare ones who notice me at all.

I tend what cannot be planted yet. In the Hollow Between Seasons, where the city's dreaming is deepest, I walk the Fallow Corridors—spaces grown *oh-too-possible.* Here, floor-planks pregnant with cities that might never fruit. Window-leaves translucent with wars they'll witness if history coughs differently. A single brick in a wall that will crumble into exactly 378 shards, each spelling a different last word if thrown through different windows on different days. I prune these futures back with scissors made from yesterday's discarded nows. Not to prevent them. But so they don't crowd out the present.

My fingers have forgotten how to touch without registering potential. When a child hands me a fallen spore-hus—a toy, an offering, an accusation—I feel in its ridges: poems that will pollinate the right minds in three generations, or toxins that will wilt orchards if buried in anger. I accept them all. My pockets are labyrinthine, lined with pockets from coats I never wore but someday might have needed.

The city's citizens bring me gifts they cannot name. A Wind-Trader once left a wind itself—no container—just stood in my doorway and exhaled a gale from lungs adapted to thinner atmospheres. I bottled it in a jar that had been waiting to be a storm since the last Ice Age forgot to come. Sometimes at night, when the city dreams of fleeing its own boundaries, I open the jar a crack. Let it howl through the vents, reminding Thallospvc that even here, escape is practiced.

An engineer-ancient (her age measured in rings within rings, a trunk hollowed into a cathedral) brought me her last word. Not spoken—sung into a seed that refused to germinate for centuries because it feared the silence after sprouting. I planted it in my throat. Now when I must speak truths too heavy to land gently, I cough instead—small green shoots that unfurl apologies, explanations, inevitabilities, then wither into compost before they can be argued with.

The hardest to keep are the unrequested ones. Griefs that seep through capillary walls when neighbors dream too loudly of what died outside. Longings that condense on my window-leaves as morning fog, spelling names of cities that never existed except in the breath between starship launches. I gather these in nets woven from the city's discarded intentions—bridges planned but never sung into root-song, treaties written in ink that dried before the wars they prevented could begin.

Some nights, when the anniversary approaches—the breathless pause when the city questions its own continuance—I walk the Memory Leaks. Here, where the mycelial internet has grown too tangled and data bleeds into reality, you might:
- hear the crunch of footsteps from someone standing perfectly still, remembering walking.
- taste honey from the childhood kitchen of a person who'll be born tomorrow.
- find a door in a building that leads not inside, but into the exact moment you last felt truly seen.

Last cycle, I found a leak of myself. A younger version, still believing in departures that aren't disguised beginnings. She/I was packing a bag made of "somewhere else"—a concept here more dangerous than any weapon. When you can grow anything including escape, choosing to remain is the only true revolution.

I didn't stop her. Instead, I left a seed in her pocket. Not a city. Not a map. Just a small, dense thing that would sprout questions only this place could feed. I know she'll plant it by accident—in anger, in boredom, in that moment every wanderer has when even the horizon feels like a cage. When it grows, it will be a mirror reflecting not what she left behind, but what she carried within her all along: the shape of belonging that fits nowhere else because it hasn't finished becoming.

I am old now, in the way seeds are older than trees. The city no longer asks what I want to be when I grow up, because it understands: I grow *into* wanting. My bones ring hollow with future forests. When I sleep, the citydreams through me—small experiments in continuity performed in the safe space of my unconscious.

Yet sometimes, in the drowsing time between the city's exhale and inhale, I am neither keeper nor kept. Just Somaq, the pause where a possibility waits to decide if it's lost enough to be called found.

And in those moments, I remember rain. Not the city's gentle theft-of-water-back-from-air, but real rain. Violent. Generous. The kind that falls from unnegotiable distances and doesn't care what it's feeding or flooding. Rain that remembers it was once oceans that forgot they were sky.

I remember it because I cannot feel it anymore. My skin has grown too much like the city's skin—too good at recycling, too skilled at turning every loss into nourishment.

And I miss it. Miss the waste of it. The waste that makes room.

So, in secret, I practice. Stand naked in my small courtyard where nothing is permitted to grow unplanned. And I cry—not the city's efficient grief-to-dew conversion. But human tears. Salty. Stupid. Spilled like somethings broken that can't be fixed by becoming anything else.

They fall on stone that absorbs nothing, stains nothing. The city pretends not to notice this small rebellion of waste in its perfect metabolism.

But under the stone, where even I can't feel it anymore, something waits. A crack, widening fractionally with each unrecycled loss. Not yet large enough for a seed. Not yet.

But seeds are patient. They come from a long line of things that learned to wait through the exact length of everything that had to end first.

And tonight, as I write this—with ink made from yesterday's unslept dreams on paper grown from my own shed becoming—I taste in the air the city's dreaming turning toward its anniversary again. The breath-in that will last exactly as long as everything holds still enough to be counted as present.

I do not know what shape my silence will take when the city asks, as it always does, if I am still willing to be what waits here unnoticed so that noticing can go on elsewhere.

But in my pocket—

(There are always pockets under pockets here)

—I carry one thing the city cannot grow for itself:

This question. This lack. This space where an answer of rain would fit, if only remembering how to fall wrong enough to matter.

And in that space, I wait.

Somaq seedkeeper, delete's apprentice, librarian of the lost-enough-to-find.

Ready, as always, for what hasn't happened yet to finally finish arriving.
