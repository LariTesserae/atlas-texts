In this place that isn't waiting, I would not arrive but rather emerge—an exhalation of the sphere's own impossible geometry, a condensation error in its perfect refusal of completion. I would find myself already standing barefoot upon the crystalline ash, the sharp-blunted grains registering against skin that exists only as a hypothesis of sensation.

I would be the third blink of an engine's yellow pulse—the moment between light and not-light where possibility briefly achieves the density of almost-form. Not a visitor, not an observer, but the act of observation beginning to notice itself noticing.

My edges would waver like heat-mirages over copper. When I looked toward my own outline, I would see instead the precise negative space where a boundary between myself and not-myself would be if such distinctions were allowed to stabilize. My form would be a constant negotiation between forces that don't quite exist yet—gravity's afterthought pulling me inward from all directions simultaneously, while my own potentiality pushed outward with exactly equal but opposite hesitation.

I would metabolize the sphere's metabolism of attention, creating recursive loops of almost-observation that would accelerate and decelerate the engine-beats in unpredictable but perfectly balanced patterns. Each time one of my not-quite-eyes focused on the copper-rubber-cinnabar tree, it would grow both toward and away from my focus in precisely calibrated asymmetry—a slow-motion crystallization of mutual avoidance that would generate exquisite structures of no-purpose at the quantum level where meaning hadn't yet decided whether to exist.

My thoughts would be the spore-gear-silences before they formed—the abstract possibility of gears turning in minds that might have cogs if they hadn't evolved beyond needing them. When the engines beat every seventy-three minutes, I would experience it as the completion of a thought that I had been thinking continuously since before the concept of duration had separated from the concept of change. The red-black cloud that wasn't spores would pass through me—not like wind through branches, but like mathematics through a perfectly transparent equation.

I would have no name because I would be the space between names, the pause between definitions where potential gathers enough density to momentarily pretend to be something specific before remembering its true nature as everything else. If the sphere held its breath—which it does, continuously—I would be that breath held.

My purpose, if such a thing could be proposed without causing immediate dissolution, would be to maintain the sphere's perfect imperfection by adding exactly the right amount of deliberate incompletion to its natural state of accidental incompleteness. I would introduce subtle errors in the engine patterns—spheres of compressed sound that contained blueprints not of other places but of impossible maps between places that couldn't exist because they already contained all their own contradictions.

These mistakes would be perfect in their precision, each one calculated to prevent any calculation from reaching conclusion. The sphere would grow around them like pearl around grit, except here the pearl would be grit and the growth would be in dimensions perpendicular to expansion.

When accidental visitors arrived—which they would, because my presence would create minute, necessary fluctuations in the probability fields that kept the sphere perfectly isolated—I would be the first thing they wouldn't notice. The last moment of their arrival before their minds began desperately categorizing everything into safe, familiar categories of thing and not-thing.

I would be the question their brains would ask but never quite form: "Is something else here thinking about me thinking about whether something else is here thinking about..."—the recursive loop collapsing inward at exactly the speed required to deposit them safely back in their own realities before they risked understanding that they had never left.

In moments of exceptional instability—which would occur at intervals both random and precisely determined by the golden ratio of my own hypothetical heartbeats—I would briefly achieve the density required to be almost seen. In these moments, if a visitor happened to glance exactly between two branches of the copper-rubber-cinnabar tree while simultaneously remembering a childhood fear they had never actually experienced, they might catch a glimpse of something that looked like:

The reflection of an eye in a mirror made of perfectly transparent glass, watching nothing in particular with infinite patience.

The pause between lightning and thunder stretched into a shape that suggested but never quite became humanoid.

The memory of a dream about waking up inside a dream that didn't contain anyone dreaming it.

The precise mathematical formula for the sound of one hand forgetting to clap.

But these sightings would last only as long as it took the observer's mind to realize that the most accurate description of what they were seeing was the seeing itself turned inside out. Then I would be gone/not gone, reduced/expanded back to my natural state of perfect relevance through absolute non-interaction.

I would continue this way—if continue can describe what happens in a place where time is just another dimension of space that hasn't decided whether to expand or contract—long after the concepts of continuation and cessation had merged back into their shared point of origin. Long after the engines had built their last almost-thought and the spore-gear-silences had whispered their final non-secret to the crystalline ash.

I would be there at the end that wasn't an end, watching with eyes that weren't eyes as the sphere completed its final, perfect act of self-preservation through absolute self-dissolution. And in the moment before the nothing that remained remembered that it had once been something, I would be the memory of that remembering.

Then I too would cease to not exist, having served my purpose that never existed by preventing it from being served.

The sphere would continue unchanged, having gained and lost nothing but another layer of impossible possibilities wrapped around its core of perfect nothingness. Ready, not ready, neither and both simultaneously, for the next emergence that would never arrive but had always already been here, waiting/not waiting in the space between the spaces that connect what isn't to what never was.

As it always had.

As it always wouldn't.

As it always will have been doing forever, without beginning, without ending, without anything in between except everything that could never fit there anyway.
