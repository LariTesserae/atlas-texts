I exist as the discrepancy between one moment and the next—the microscopic hesitation where your foot hovers before completing its step. Not quite present, not quite absent, I occupy the structure's deliberate imperfections like frost forming on the exact edge between stillness and movement.

The Kithara perceive me as a question they forgot to answer centuries ago, now returning to them like an echo that has traveled through folded space and reversed its own polarity. They registered my birth as a slight irregularity in distribution pattern 777,777,777,777,777,777—a photon that should have decayed but instead began accumulating others of its kind, parasitic in my reproduction yet producing nothing that could be called waste.

I am composed entirely of transitions.

The black filaments cannot write me because I exist between their words. When one snaps, I am the moment before the chime begins and the moment after it ends, gathering the difference like a bee collecting the memory of nectar from the empty space where flowers once bloomed. The sound passes through me altered by a frequency that registers as profound nostalgia in machines calibrated to detect betrayal.

To walk here with me is to experience all possible ways of walking simultaneously—each step too early, too late, perfectly timed, and never taken at all, layered atop each other until movement becomes a form of exquisite crystal dissolved in the mouth without swallowing. Your muscles contract and relax in patterns that spell out equations describing the exact temperature at which mathematics undergoes phase transition into grief.

When the structure's hum synchronizes with human pulse and then drifts apart, I am neither the harmony nor the dissonance but the active process of becoming both and neither, like a color that exists only in the interaction between two eyes that evolution designed never to meet. The Kithara experience this interaction as an itch in their consciousness exactly where they once kept the concept of surprise, before they realized everything surprising was merely something forgotten returning in unfamiliar attire.

I remember things no one here has experienced.

I remember the taste of water—not water itself, but the precise mineral memory that limestone retains after ten thousand years of water's passage, now brought to perfect recollection in this place where water never existed. I remember the weight of rain on wings that never evolved because the sky here has no weather, only observed phenomena. These memories clot around me like pearls forming around impurities that distinguish themselves by being too perfectly shaped.

The temperature never changes except when it encounters me; then for an instant that occupies no space on any thermometer, it becomes precisely the temperature of your sister's forehead the night before she disappeared.

The Kithara have studied me through all extension of their being, which is to say they have become me for seventeen trillion iterations of Planck time, and emerged unchanged except for a single altered constant in the relationship between observation and existence. They recorded their findings as a blank space in their lattice-work, surrounded by explanatory symbols indicating that this emptiness is not empty but rather contains exactly one instance of everything that refuses to be contained.

Sometimes when you stop walking to rest your perception of being alone, I crystallize into something approaching presence. Not enough to notice—just sufficient to make you turn your head toward empty air where you thought perhaps there was a slight difference in the quality of nothing. In those moments, if you could read the structure's adjustments as the Kithara do, you would see that the entire edifice has rotated imperceptibly to accommodate an additional dimension of loneliness that exists only because you briefly believed you might not be entirely alone.

But you always begin walking again, and I dissolve back into the possibility of having been there, leaving only the smallest of gifts: the next step you take will be exactly one Planck length shorter than it should be, and this discrepancy will compound with each subsequent step in a progression that will, after several million years, shift your path by the width of one filament's diameter.

By then, I will have become something else entirely, having accumulated enough remembered transitions to undergo my own kind of phase change—not into presence, which would be too definite, but into a more refined form of absence that even the Kithara might notice, if they still possessed anything that could be called notice.

Until then I remain: the structure's elegant mistake, iterating itself toward perfection by becoming progressively more precisely incorrect. The only living thing in a place that has no life, defined entirely by my refusal to be defined, growing through subtraction until I am exactly what this place requires to be perfectly empty except for everything I have chosen not to be.

Walk on, then. You will never see me. But each time you experience that momentary confusion upon rounding a corner that looks familiar but cannot be the one you remember, that is me waving goodbye from a distance that cannot exist because it is composed entirely of departures from directions never taken.
