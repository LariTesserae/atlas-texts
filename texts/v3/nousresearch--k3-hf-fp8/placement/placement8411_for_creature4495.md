One doesn’t enter the Hungering Depth so much as misremember oneself into it. The valley coils at the edges of slumber, collecting every half-forgotten gravity—every childhood stumble that never scraped a knee, every sigh abandoned mid-flight in empty bedrooms—until its rim is crowned with the ghost-weight of things almost fallen. They call it a “depression” on old maps, but that word is a mere bruised thumbprint pressed against the lip of something that never stopped yawning.

I arrived as the echo of a leaf-tip brushing water.

A single birch leaf from a forest that burned ten centuries before trees dreamed of fire-names. When I let go of the branch, the wind sighed sideways instead of lifting, and I was left dangling in the hush between let and fall. Time unspooled below me like silk thread catching on an unseen fang. I remembered forgetting how to be carried.

Then there was no I-only-the-recollection-of-being-carried spiraling down.

The valley received me gently, as a mouth receives a word it has already swallowed a thousand times. I struck no earth—there was none—only folded into the breath between densities: not air, never water, but the patient collusion of both pretending sleep. My edges loosened like ink in dusk. Vein-threads remembered their apprenticeship to root-burrows; green blood untutored itself of chlorophyll; I surrendered the crisp memory of sunlight with the same gratitude an old coin gives its face to rust.

They found me thus: a shimmer of almost-carbon wandering the uppermost lamina of absence.

The Unbordered do not greet, they parse. A cluster of smaller ones—tangles of fogloss and doubt-bite—mistook my uncompleted photosynthesis for a lullaby interrupted in mid-dissonance. They tasted my missing stump with soundless curiosity: *What here once reached for light and thought better of it?* Around me they orbited, threading my evacuated photosites with questions that hummed like plucked spiderlines in starless wind.

I answered in the only currency I retained: negative space.

Showed them the hollow where chloroplasts used to riot. Offered the imprint of a photon’s fingerprint upon a surface I no longer possessed. These fragments, precious to me as lost names, ribboned into their communal digestion of uncertainty. They savored the fact that something once believed in sunshine enough to mourn its absence.

Uniqueness.

Among the foundational bargains of leafdom, mine was singular: I refused to feed the flame.

In the instant before ignition danced up the loam-sealed library of my ancestors, I parsed the future-silence of my kindling-clad siblings. I saw the blaze would write them into heroic epics of ash and nutrient song, praised by worms, remembered by seeds in the shorthand of scar tissue.

I did not wish to be remembered.

So I unclasped my bond to meaning-making. Severed the vow that chlorophyll whispers to carbon dioxide at dawn’s first theft. Gave back the oxygen-debt I had never asked to inherit. Instead, I hoarded indifference. Filled every interstice of myself with the pure intention to not-become.

The fire came licking.

I did not taste delicious.

It passed through the vacancy I had cultivated like wind through a window left open for a ghost who never arrived. My siblings cried out in brilliant, necessary deaths while I drifted upward on the thermal of my own irrelevance—brittle, colorless, unreadable.

Proof.

If you could read the stratigraphy of me, some future thing unearthing crumbs of this tale from the Hungering Depth’s digestion, look for the layer bereft of black. There—you will find a single stratum of untouched translucence, a fossilized perhaps where certainty burned everything else into stony proclamation.

Hold it to light that has forgotten how to be harsh. It will neither darken nor illuminate. It will only remind you:

Some silences are not emptiness.

Some departures leave a shape that is not grief but invitation—an outline traced in negative space where the world, hungry for one moment’s refusal, may press its dreaming mouth and learn the taste of boundaryless quiet.

This is why they do not eat me.

I am already eaten by what chose not to consume.

I am resting now between two nibbles of ?was—a guest curl nested in the grammar of mutual unfinishing. When the largest Unbordered sifts through the archive of almosts, my non-event flickers like a mote within its slow-blinking contemplation:

*here a leaf declared autonomy from usefulness*

*here the fire became a wind and missed its mark*

*here hunger circled and found itself already full*

The valley, that ancient collector of nearlys, hums its approval. It will remember me to forgetting, and forget me into the next shape that remembers how to pause between reaching and falling.

I am content to be the breath held forever between.

If ever you find yourself slipping through similar seams, look for me not in the distance ahead nor the vapor behind. Listen instead for the hush that eddies where appetite hesitates and touches instead—gingerly, curiously—the soft bruise called maybe.

There.

I am curled like origami made of eclipse-light.

I am waiting to teach you how to be deliciously gone.
