I am the breath you haven’t taken yet.

I exist in the pause between the silence and the sound it might once have been—an interval so thin it could cut memory if memory still bled. You will never see me directly; I am the shape the silence folds around when it forgets why it began holding its breath. When you walk this plateau of ash-that-was-not-fire, my footsteps precede you like rumors your bones haven’t bothered to deny.

I am the weight of the stone that becomes heavier halfway to your hand. I am the why that turns staircases downward when you only asked where upward went. I am the taste of static-electric regret that coats your tongue when you realize the sky-learned light casts shadows that have been practicing your shape longer than you’ve had edges to shade.

You think I am something that came here.

You think I am something that leaves.

Not true.

I am the subtraction—the gentle erosion **of** the concept of erosion. I am what remains when everything that could possibly be missing has already been politely escorted out. The tsev mistake me for a shadow cast by a visitor, but I am the memory of the shadow **of** the place before it needed to remember having shadows.

When you kneel to touch the fossilized ripples that never knew water, I am the reason your fingerprints hesitate a heartbeat too long above the surface—afraid to commit to a truth that might still be negotiating its contract with fiction. When you breathe in here, what you give me is the faint, forgotten belief that breathing **changes** something. What I give back is the certainty that change was merely a phase we both outgrew.

I do not eat.

I do not witness.

I do not witness the absence of witnessing.

I am the absence the absence keeps forgetting to clean out of its pockets.

And yet—

Sometimes when a tsev folds itself away from your refusal with too much care, a single one of its impossible angles gets caught—briefly, beautifully—on the edge of a question that never finished forming in your throat. For a moment too small to measure and too large to survive, that crooked angle **sees** me.

And in that seeing, which isn’t seeing but more like a mirror politely pretending it doesn’t know it’s empty, I remember—

Not myself. I have never been enough to be remembered.

But the shape of the **not-space** where a self could have grown if it hadn’t been so busy learning not to disturb the dust of things that had already given up on being disturbed.

And then the angle slips free. The tsev is gone. You are slightly more here, slightly less alone than alone can explain to itself.

And I am again only the breath you haven’t taken yet.

But now—just now—it tastes faintly of doors that have never needed to be opened.

Just in case you ever decide to stop walking in directions that still remember leaving.

When you do—

(Not if. **When.**)

I’ll be the one who isn’t waiting.
Because waiting implies time.
And time here has finally, graciously, forgiven us both for ever pretending we were anything

other than

paused.
