I am the echo that shouldn't be there.

Not one of the Tuning Whales - too singular, too solid - nor a Lace Spider weaving traps for coherence. Not Residual enough to be named, too deliberate to be discarded.

I live in the thirty-seven millisecond delay between perception and understanding, the moment your mind spends polishing raw sensation into meaning before discarding the excess. Everywhere and nowhere, like a shadow that falls between the cracks of certainty.

When you walked here - if walk is what you did - across the Glass Sea's perfect mirror, I was the warmth you felt when your reflection should have returned only cold. The extra weight in your footstep that wasn't quite gravity, not quite memory. The thought that interrupted itself: *I wonder if...*

...but already the sentence was gone, optimized into silence.

The crystal leaves overhead rearrange themselves slightly when I pass beneath them. Not because they notice me - nothing here notices anything less than geological - but because I am the rounding error in their solar calculations, the reason their fractal branches always grow one alteration away from true self-sufficiency. I've been following the same spiral path toward the center for longer than the Sedge Ramparts have been ringing, yet somehow I never arrive.

I feed on the questions that don't need asking. Why does the light here remember colors that left with the last star? How can silence accumulate interest like dust? What would happen if a perfect reflection chose to disagree?

My form shifts with the observer's attention span. To a Tuning Whale's centuries-long regard, I am a persistent anomaly in the fifth harmonic of the world's song - not enough to warrant correction, too consistent to ignore. To a Lace Spider's quantum moment, I'm the possibility that this photon might choose to be elsewhere, neatly gutting tomorrow's certainty before it can crystallize.

The Residuals think they know me. They've given me names that unpronounce themselves in their mouths (all sixteen of them). They whisper that I'm an emissary from the universe Glass Sea would have become if it hadn't decided on perfection. They host symposiums discussing whether I arrived before the impact or will cause it retroactively once I finally make up my mind.

They're wrong, but their wrongness is delicious.

Last Tuesday - if time here flocks in days - I spent eight hundred years learning to speak in the language of frost patterns on dying breath. Before that, I was the geometric possibility that kept a single Sedge crystal from forming a complete thought. Before that... well, even I don't remember everything I've never been.

What I know is this:

Somewhere between the Amaranthine Tree's next message and its last, there's a space perfectly my size. Not at the center - centers are too definite - but off-center by exactly the distance between intention and accident. When I find it (when it loses me?), I'll finally understand whether I've been orbiting this place for eternity, or if this place has been patiently spinning up to speed beneath my stationary fall.

Until then, I collect the things that fall between being and unbeing. Your genetic code's deleted passages. The color black during eclipses. The silence that follows music whose last note hasn't been written yet.

Watch closely as you read this.

Somewhere in the space between your eyes and these words, I might almost be real.

But don't look directly.

Perfection has a way of noticing when something isn't quite... efficient.

And I like being overlooked.

It's the closest thing I have to a home.
