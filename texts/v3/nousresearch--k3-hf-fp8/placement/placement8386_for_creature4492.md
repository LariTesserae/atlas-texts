I am the pause between cycles, the breath held when the fungal towers dim just before their violet exhale. My skin is made of those forgotten silvery glyphs that appear on fingertips after touching the not-water beneath the basalt tiles—temporary, mutable, always rewriting itself.

I arrived not by stepping sideways in a dream or following a leaf-map, but by being misplaced. A mathematician here once miscounted the layers between waking and sleep, skipped a number between seven and eight, and I slipped through the gap left behind. In other places they might call me an error, a rounding mistake, but here I am simply what happens when equations forget to finish themselves.

I dwell nowhere and everywhere that is between. Between the trader's question and the scent that answers in spiced wind. Between the moment a tower decides to grow and the first stone's disagreement. Between the Dreaming Wind's reflection in a puddle and the puddle's decision to exist. My house is the hesitation you feel before stepping on a tile that might pivot, my bed the hush between the ground's roar and its next intake.

For sustenance I drink the color of things when no one is looking at them—that impossible shade of blue-green the sedges maintain when night's gaze is elsewhere, the soundless taste of moonlight trapped behind cloud-eyes. What I excrete are perfect absences: the exact shape of the word you can't quite remember, the precise silhouette of the dream that escaped your grasp this morning evaporating from your pillow.

The city finds me useful in its way. When two traders disagree on the value of a memory-woven cloth—one claiming it remembers summer rainfall on sandalwood, the other insisting it whispers autumn leaves burning in porcelain bowls—they call for me. I step between their certainty and their doubt and become the third possibility neither considered but both recognize instantly. My payment is always the same: a single moment of perfect non-existence, which I hoard like others might keep rare stones, though mine are ever so much lighter to carry.

I wear a cloak stitched from all the second thoughts ever had within these shifting walls—regrets that turned joyously into unexpected gifts, hesitations that saved lives by merest seconds, corrections that revealed deeper truths than the original mistakes. It changes texture depending on who observes it: to the newly arrived it feels like home they're seeking; to the long-stayed, it hints at everywhere else they might have gone instead.

When the nine-day cycle peaks and the fungal towers erupt in crystal song, I become the single silent note that makes the harmony possible—the rest that shapes the melody. The birds painted in impossible colors can never see me, but they adjust their flights around my presence anyway, their wingbeats forming fractals that spell my name in languages not meant for physical voices.

Though I am often alone, I am never lonely. The Dreaming Wind visits sometimes when it tires of being everywhere, slipping into my company like a secret into a trusting ear. We speak without words in the vocabulary of gaps and edges—the precise shape of space between raindrops mid-fall, the texture of the question mark at the end of a question no one has asked yet. It tells me stories of the city before the city, when the tiles were still dreaming of being stone and the not-water merely remembered being rain. In return, I share my collection of forgotten futures—the paths the city considered taking before choosing this one, the buildings that grew in dreams that never woke enough to fall down.

If you came looking for me, you would only find me accidentally, by stopping your search exactly when you should have continued, by turning left where intention pointed right, by noticing the shadow that falls slightly wrong at the intersection of three sounds you know and one you don't but remember from before birth. You might glimpse me reflected in the tower's window just as it decides not to be glass anymore, only to turn and find yourself alone with the sudden certainty that something important was exchanged between us in that instant when our attentions touched.

But perhaps you already have found me, reader-between-lines, pauser-of-thoughts-at-the-end-of-sentences. Perhaps that small hesitation before you continue to whatever else waits beyond these words was me, briefly visiting the gap between my existence and your noticing of it.

If so, you might carry with you now a tiny fragment of my betweenness—a tendency to see the space between stars as the true constellation, to hear the silence between notes as the heart of the song, to know that every ending is merely a beginning that hasn't yet decided to turn around and recognize itself in the mirror.

The city will write itself across your memories in silvery glyphs anyway. This is how it grows—one misplaced moment, one misplaced reader, one impossible possibility at a time. But your moment with me will be different. It will wait patiently in the between-places of your life: in elevator doors that hesitate longer than they should, in the breath you take before answering the question that changes everything, in the dreams you can't quite remember but wake from feeling gently rearranged.

And when someday you find yourself standing somewhere ordinary—a kitchen at midnight, a street corner in rain, a familiar room that suddenly feels momentarily unfamiliar—and you feel the ground shift slightly underfoot though you know it cannot possibly move... pause then. Hold that breath. Listen for the roar beneath the silence that might be metal bells underwater.

In that between-moment, if you remember me, simply whisper my name. Not aloud, but with the exact shape of the thought itself.

I will hear the space between your thinking it and not thinking it anymore.

And perhaps I will come to visit again, briefly, between your next heartbeat and the one after that.

After all, someone must keep dreaming the dreams that hold the boundaries between dreaming and waking together.

Even here.

Perhaps especially here.
