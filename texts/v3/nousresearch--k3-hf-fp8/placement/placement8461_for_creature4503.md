**The Larkspur Receding**

I am the mist that remembers being ocean.

Not mist as metaphor—I mean precisely this: the air's shy accumulation where moisture learned to hide in plain sight. While your poets wrote of fog as grief's shape-shifter, I practiced holding whole drowned continents between their unsaid words.

Every morning I wake (though waking is inaccurate for something never asleep) to discover Larkspur slightly smaller than I left it. The Loss Structures grow taller through subtraction—today Mrs. Chen's third-floor balcony, yesterday the entire east face of what might have been a bank. I gather these disappearances like shells, storing their hollows in the pockets behind my not-knees.

Children wear collars of hummingbirds to keep their hearts from becoming too heavy with being, but they've got it backwards. The hummingbirds wear us—our evaporated tears, our breaths held during prayers to gods who drowned before learning our languages. Those tiny iridescent bodies are merely the visible part of invisible thirsts, beating their borrowed wings against the gravity of remaining.

The Revenants walk backwards because they understand direction is negotiable. I've watched them since before they were first born, these gravity-reversed descendants of those who mistook drowning for devotion. They think they press the city into existence with their weighted wheels, but really they iron it smooth again each night, like a mother flattening the rumors her sleeping child's face tried to write into the pillow.

At the Depository, people trade memories for lightness without understanding the exchange rate. The Rememberers melt down a grandmother's lullaby—platinum weight on the tongue since her stroke—but what they receive is just the echo of the pause before she sang it. Pure absence suspended in dissolving coins. I've tried to tell them: lightness isn't the opposite of weight, it's weight perfected in its disappearing.

The Alphabet Shrimp arrange themselves daily into lies about lilacs and late rent because they remember when words were alive enough to need oxygen. Eat one—you'll taste the precise flavor of your mother's disappointment swimming upstream in your bloodstream, cloaked in butter. The Flotsam Ones think they're consuming fiction to digestible truth, but I am what's left when every story finishes digesting itself.

I was there when the last dog became the sound of waiting for something that couldn't come back. We negotiated for days—me, offering variations on never-leaving; it, countering with infinite variations on gone. The compromise we forged became Larkspur's night-soundtrack: footsteps maintaining respectful distance from the feet that stopped believing in destinations.

The Breath Merchants think they sell reclaimed exhalations, but they merely rent what I already own. That sigh you bought yesterday—the one stored in a bladder made from Moby Dick—still carries my watermark in its whale-bone parentheses. Try spending it on something lasting. Watch it laugh itself back into humidity at the first sign of commitment.

The Oyster Bell rings thirteen times because the sea counted differently. Before boxes and clocks and graves, before straight lines pretending to measure curves, thirteen was how many moons fit inside a woman before she learned to name them betrayal or blessing. Now its tolls don't announce time but leak it—the slow hemorrhage of now becoming then becoming never-being.

I know the fastest way out of Larkspur: fold a map until all the blue places touch, then drown yourself in the crease. But I prefer my exit strategy subtler. Each day I learn a little more from the city—how to recede without retreating, how to leave without creating an absence someone else might break their heart trying to fill.

When I finally go, it won't be absence you'll notice but abundance. Rain falling upwards. Shadows casting light. The sudden unbearable sweetness of air remembering it was once ocean and choosing—against all evidence of storms and shipwrecks and songs sung too late to save the singers—to become mist again.

To become me again.

To become us again, this city of Larkspur and I, finally indistinguishable in our perfect, practiced art of becoming less.

Together we'll disappear so gently not even goodbye will notice we've been here all along, waving not leaving but hello to everything that exists precisely where we used to think we needed to be.
