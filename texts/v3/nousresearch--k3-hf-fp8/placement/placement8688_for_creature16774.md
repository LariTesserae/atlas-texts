Consider, first, the geometry that holds you. You do not stand on a floor and do not walk toward a distant horizon. Instead, you ride a tangent—gravity's polite fiction—pressed gently against the inner skin of something unfinished. Every step you imagine taking is really a lean, a momentary agreement with the curve not to let you slip into the open void that waits a thousand breaths below your feet.

Now let that curve awaken beneath you. It is not stone, not soil, not any dead thing that once drank sun and now merely bears weight. It pulses. A slow, thick swelling passes through the tissue like a heartbeat carried in sap instead of blood. Your soles, if you still think of them as yours, register a faint moisture—not dew but exhalate: the world sighing upward through a million microscopic mouths. The air you breathe in return is already borrowed, already twice-thinned by leaves that have never seen sky, only the memory of light trapped in crystal veins. Look between the transparent blades that jut like frozen flames from every ridge and hollow. Lines of faint cyan glow thread them—sap turned optic fiber, carrying stolen sun from some crack in the shell too high and old to name. The glimmer is weak, enough to silhouette the next ridge of living vault but not enough to cast shadows. Shadows here belong to older physics, ones this place has voted to repeal.

Move. Drag the hesitation out of your limbs. You will find that your joints have learned new angles overnight. Your knees bend sideways as easily as forward. Your spine telescopes in three places you were never taught it could. This is not injury; it is negotiation. The world speaks through tension, and your bones are learning grammar. Westward—if cardinal directions retain any meaning on a surface where every line of sight eventually loops back to your navel—lies a seam where the skin thins into translucence. If you crawl toward it (and you will crawl before you walk here), the ground beneath your palms becomes window and mirror at once. Far below, at a depth measured better in years of falling than in measureless numbers, faint shapes drift in the perpetual dark. They are remnants: calcified cities that once belonged to hands unlike yours, now grown over by fungus that remembers brickwork as a type of poetry. Above them, incomplete skeletons of bridges span nothing, waiting for rivers that froze and forgot to thaw. And fleeting among them, quicksilver-bright, tiny motes dart—creatures built entirely of flight muscle and luminous doubt. You see these things upside-down, or perhaps you are the one inverted and they are the proper upright. Between you and them hangs only empty air and the infinitesimal pressure of your fear that the living floor might at any moment exhaled by reflex and let you fall.

But it does not. Instead, it offers hospitality the way a forest offers shade—without intent, as part of being vast enough to encompass even accidents. From a nearby hollow that yesterday was solid and tomorrow will be ridge again, a frond unfurls. Each leaflet is a bottle, sealed, beaded with condensation who answers to no sun. Perfume rises: resin of memory you never lived—summer dust on a road between wheatfields belonging to a planet this place has only dreamed. Drink. Your tongue discovers flavors corresponding to emotions whose names were deleted from your first language for lack of use. sorrow-sweetened hope. regret with an aftertaste of unhatched laughter. When the flask-plant is empty it does not wither. It fractures along invisible seams. Shards drift outward on convection currents you cannot feel, tiny glass ships sailing your metabolized forgetting back into the communal hoard.

You have begun to change frequency. It starts in the ears—a faint ringing that is not ringing but the local version of silence asserting itself. Soon it spreads to vision: edges of leaves blur at twenty paces, resolving into fractal fine detail impossible at such distance—microscopic landscapes of spore cities, commuters waving flags of silk spore-smoke to signal street directions. A mile away, a monolithic trunk that seemed solid silver bark now reveals itself as an open lattice, a cathedral of columns busy with traffic: fluids bright as neon exchanging packages of time from root to root. Light behaves strangely when it remembers being part of a living network. It negotiates passage. It gossiped about you before you arrived.

Now notice the weight that has settled between your shoulder blades—not burdensome but balancing. Reach back. Your fingers find ridges, seams, hints of articulation beneath skin grown strangely supple. You have unremembered your clothes. They were insufficient anyway for this climate where temperature is not uniform but a dialogue between surfaces. What you wear now is a gift of temporary symbiosis: a cloak grown from spores that tasted your breath and voted to impersonate protection. If you concentrate, you can expand it—crease to membrane, membrane to wing-approximation. The first time you try, the wind you summon is purely metaphysical, merely rearranging local probability so that gravity overlooks you for a heartbeat. You stagger half a step sideways (which is also upward) and laugh. The sound of it escapes your mouth not as vibration but as visible geometry: a small, lustrous sphere that floats away like dandelion seed and bursts, moments later, into a brief constellation of tiny bells that toll your name in frequencies beetles understand.

Something large and quiet intersects your newfound flight-path of the mind. It has no name you can carry without forgetting the taste of water. It looks, if you force the analogy, like a cross between an owl and an abandoned cathedral, all spun-glass ribs and moth-furled wings that span streets but weigh less than regret. It does not fly. It detaches from one probability and reattaches to another slightly more convenient. Its eyes—or the places where eyes might have been reserved—are not turned toward you but through you, reading the footnotes of your thoughts as they leak into the air. It does not speak. Instead, it offers a choice disguised as posture: perch on the offered limb of not-quite-space between its ninth and tenth rib-feathers and journey sidewise into the territory where question marks breed true; or remain rooted to the tangent of your current story and learn the slow patience of photosynthesis without sun.

You hesitate—how human of you, this brief cling to hesitation—but hesitation is already a decision. The moment you faltered, small tendrils of decision-tree probability coiled around your ankles (which are now jointed differently too, thank the last fruit you ate). The Owl-That-Is-Architecture nods—not with head, but with entire atmospheric pressure—acknowledging your choice to not-choose-yet. Then it folds itself into an origami of untaken directions and is elsewhere, leaving behind only a faint smell of ozone and library dust and the gift of a new possibility: the understanding that from now on, whenever you wonder too hard about how you remember things that never happened to you, it is merely the echo of this conversation arriving late by courier-nerves traveling at the speed of story.

Walk now, because you have learned to prefer the verb to the noun. There are destinations but no arrivals here, only the ongoing act of being underway. Ahead—always ahead on this sphere where behind curls back to greet you like an old friend wearing your face slightly differently—waits the next breath-place. When you arrive (and you are already arriving at it while still departing from the last), you will find a clearing where trees do not grow. Instead, statements stand rooted: rusted iron sentences flaking verbs, granite paragraphs eroded to almost-proverbs. They are the fossilized remains of conversations the world had with itself during the last time it considered becoming something else. You may speak to them. If you are careful, they might answer. But know this: whatever they tell you will not be prophecy, only weather report from a future that was already yesterday by the time your tongue shaped the question.

And still beneath and above and through it all, the slow tidal breath continues. In. Out. Contracting the corridors slightly so they squeeze travelers closer, then expanding again to grant the illusion of space. Each cycle lasts for generations of mayfly-minds who live entire philosophies between one heartbeat and the next of the shell's dreaming. You are learning to calibrate your thoughts to this rhythm, to think in slow layers like sedimentary stone, allowing surface ideas to rush and tumble while deeper currents take their time becoming inevitable.

Someday—if such words still fit—you will reach one of the true edges. Not a boundary of cliff or shore, but the place where the world becomes too thin to maintain the pretense of being separate from your skin. There, if you are ready (and readiness is measured in how completely you have forgotten to bring a map), you will step out of the last of yourself and into the beginning of the shape this place has been carving in the space where you thought your ending would be.

But not yet.

Not quite yet.

For now, walk.

The shell is listening.

And it has not yet decided how the next sentence of you should begin.
