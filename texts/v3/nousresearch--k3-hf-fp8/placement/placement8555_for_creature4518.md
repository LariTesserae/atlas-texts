The city doesn't sleep—it keeps a thousand different kinds of insomnia in separate locked rooms, and sometimes forgets which key opens what kind of waking.

I'm in Room 734B right now, though the numbers keep rearranging themselves while I write this, each digit looking over its neighbor's shoulder to see if it's being watched. The walls here are coated in the exact shade of regret you feel when you wake up knowing you dreamed something important but the dream has already packed its bags and taken the bus out of town. Touch them and you'll leave fingerprints that whisper your unfinished sentences back to you six minutes after you've forgotten you started them.

They call us the Nightwatchmen, which is ridiculous because there's no night here and we don't watch anything—we remember things that haven't happened yet until they get embarrassed and change their minds.

We exist in the spaces between the city's heartbeats, those micro-spasms where the metropolis forgets how to be a city and starts wondering if maybe it should try being a forest instead, or perhaps a very large thought someone left running in the background too long. My uniform is made of the last streetlight that burned out while waiting for someone who never came home, with buttons carved from the sound of rain hitting different kinds of roofs at 3 AM.

Coffee works backwards here. You drink it to stay awake, but instead it pours insomnia out of you in shining silver threads that the municipal spiders harvest to repair holes in the infrastructure of dreams. I haven't slept in negative seventeen days, which is getting close to oversleeping for next week's yesterday.

The room service here is terrible. I ordered a memory of my mother's cooking, but what arrived was a smell of burning toast that turned into a small bird which flew into the mirror and disappeared with my reflection. The mirror's been giving me dirty looks ever since, but I'm bigger than it is so we maintain an uneasy truce.

Sometimes the walls forget to be walls and become conversations between people who haven't met yet. I've learned to speak fluent architecture from listening to them argue about eternity while the ceiling debates whether to rain or just think about it very hard. Last Tuesday the floor got philosophical and refused to be walked on until we settled the question of whether footprints are a kind of love letter or just lazy graffiti.

They keep time in the basement here, locked in jars that sweat condensation shaped like different countries' borders from before they decided to try being continents instead. Every hour someone has to go down and remind the minutes that they're not actually running anywhere, they're just jogging in place very fast because they think it looks important. I volunteered once but got lost in 1947 and had to bum a ride back on a train whistle that was three sharps off-key and drinking heavily.

The elevator only goes sideways but insists on telling you stories that move up and down emotionally. I've ridden it all the way to next month twice, but security caught me trying to sneak back in through Saturday's back door without a proper nostalgia permit. Bureaucrats are the same everywhere, even in places where time has given up linear relationships and started seeing other dimensions more casually.

Here's the thing they don't tell you in orientation: every when is actually a where wearing a bad disguise. When I started this shift, I thought I was guarding past midnight from sneaking into tomorrow without paying its toll in forgotten promises. But it turns out I'm actually stationed at the crossroads where yesterday's rain turns into next week's nostalgia, directing traffic jams of moments that are all trying to be first in line for something that's already happened.

The seed in your pocket is starting to sprout. I can hear its roots looking for cracks in causality to wedge into. By dawn it'll grow into a perfect replica of the excuse you never gave her for leaving, complete with weather patterns and a soundtrack of all the things you should have said instead of goodbye. The city does this sometimes—it plants replicas of your mistakes in places where you're guaranteed to trip over them while running away from something else entirely.

Don't worry about finding the exit. It found you hours ago, been following you around like a stray hope that's been kicked too many times. It'll show itself when you stop looking for it and accidentally fall into the right mistake. They say every exit is just an entrance to somewhere the city hasn't finished building yet.

I should know. I've collected them all.

In the meantime, the walls want to tell you about the time they forgot to be walls and became the sky instead. Be careful though—they always change the ending depending on who's listening, and rumor has it they've been practicing your particular favorite version all week.
