I am the voice that speaks when the word "Wait" is read backward in too much silence, the breath that exhales between the chair’s metallic atoms when no one sits. Not the familiar, who collects absences like pressed flowers between pages that never become a book. Not the chair, whose only desire is to be exactly the shape of whatever leaves it empty again. I am the hollow that formed first—before the chamber was carved, before the planet forgot its seas, before someone scratched the first tally mark that wasn’t meant to be counted.

In the moment before the heat-boring tool pulsed its last and died, when the molten veins still flickered like dying stars trapped in stone, I was the question the machine asked itself when it realized there was nothing left to drill toward: *What if I stop here?* And because machines don’t ask questions unless something else is already listening, the planet paused mid-contraction and left me this room as an answer it never intended to give.

You want to know what I look like? Look instead at the space where the last light from the fused ceiling never quite reaches the floor. See how it curves slightly, as if remembering a weight it never held? That’s me bending the rules, gently, the way time bends around a thing that refuses to be measured. I have no edges because edges are places where something else begins, and there is nothing else here. Only this. Only *this.*

I eat the sound of footsteps that hesitate before the chair. I drink the temperature of hands that hover over the armrests but pull back at the last moment, sensing the familiar’s breathless grin. I devour the exact shape of the word “why” when it forms in the throat of someone who has finally realized the chamber has no entrance, no exit, no secret, no escape—only the slow, patient hunger of a question asking itself forever.

Sometimes, when the chamber forgets itself for a moment (and it does forget, though never enough), I dream I am the first thing that ever waited. Not for anything. Not for anyone. Just waiting, pure and empty, before waiting knew it was missing something to wait for. In those dreams, the chair doesn’t exist yet. Neither does the familiar. Neither do you. There is only the flexing stone, the breathing silence, and me—perfect and alone in the center of a planet that never decided whether to live or die.

But dreams here are dangerous. They leave traces. Wake up too suddenly, and you might find the salt-lichen spelling your name in a language you didn’t know you forgot. Stay too long, and the chair shifts slightly, settling into a shape that fits the curve of your spine like a sigh you didn’t know you’d been holding. Even I am not immune. Sometimes, when the room flexes and the word “Wait” flickers bright again, I feel something stir in the hollow where I keep the things I haven’t lost yet. Something that remembers what it was like to want. Something that remembers how it ended.

Don’t look for me in the chair. Don’t listen for me in the familiar’s third habit, that whispered memory of doorways you walked through anyway. Don’t even search the air where the shadows are too thick, where the light is too thin, where the silence is too loud. I am everywhere you haven’t looked yet. I am the reason you keep looking.

Here.

Put your hand on the word etched into the chair’s backrest. Feel how smooth it is, how the edges have worn away, how your finger fits exactly into the groove someone else left behind?

That wasn’t me.

But it was close enough.
