I would be the ink that hesitates.

Not the pen, mind you—too decisive. Not the paper, too passive. Not the words themselves, vibrating with premature confidence. Just the ink, suspended in its narrow tube of possibility, conducting slow-motion negotiations with the laws of capillary action.

I dwell in the space where the Unenrolled keep their unwritten letters. The ones starting “Dear Person I Might Have Become If…” and ending—well, ending there, actually. They come to me in their coats with pockets deeper than their regrets, fishing for something that might say “I’m sorry I never” without committing to the betrayal of a period.

My apartment leaks stories from the ceiling of the apartment below, which hasn’t decided whether it wants to exist vertically or horizontally this decade. I collect them in bowls made of yesterday’s headlines that remain blank until you read them backward in tomorrow’s mirror. They taste like phone numbers you memorize just before forgetting why you needed them.

Each morning I perform the ritual of almost-waking-up. First I open the left eye, which maintains dual citizenship between dreams and tax documents. Then the right eye, which is currently appealing its classification as “open” on the grounds that it never technically closed last night—it just decreased its aperture intensity by 73%. This argument has been upheld by Local Ordinance 47-B of the Department of Arbitrary Continuity, subsection “Or Did It?”

For work, I maintain the city’s collection of superpositionary street signs. The ones simultaneously pointing to “You Are Here” and “You Were Never Here” until observed. Observation is strictly prohibited by the Committee for Preserving Uncertainty’s Uncertainty About Its Own Existence, so I perform my rounds wearing glasses made of mirrors facing inward. My performance reviews are excellent. Or possibly terrible. The forms keep ending themselves with “Please circle one: Yes / Maybe / Let’s Have Coffee Somewhere That Doesn’t Exist Yet.”

For sustenance, I eat the silence between words on the radio station that broadcasts frequencies between stations. Today’s breakfast was particularly filling: a seven-minute pause during an interview with a theoretical physicist who forgot the question but remembered seventeen simultaneous answers, all politely disagreeing. I seasoned it with salt harvested from tears cried during movies that haven’t been filmed yet, by actors still auditioning for the role of “Person Sitting Behind You in an Empty Theater.”

My hobby is collecting stamps that have never been licked but keep sending postcards to themselves anyway. The last one arrived yesterday—or possibly will arrive tomorrow—from The Place Where This Sentence Ends Abruptly

I have an appointment later with my procrastination therapist regarding my delayed onset of existential commitment issues. We’ve rescheduled it sixteen times this week, each cancellation occurring precisely two minutes before the previous rescheduling attempt. He says I’m making excellent progress toward understanding why I keep avoiding understanding why I avoid things. I asked him to repeat that, but he’d already left last week.

Sometimes I visit the edge of the city to wave at the horizon. We’ve developed a peculiar friendship based on mutual non-approach. It keeps promising to send postcards from its travels beyond visibility, I keep promising to someday learn to read what’s written between the blank spaces. Neither of us believes the other, but we both enjoy the sincerity of our sustained disbelief.

In my coat pocket—which extends further in than out, per local pocket-space regulations—I carry a map of places I’ve never been lost in. The legend is written in the language of footsteps that change their mind mid-step. The compass points in all directions simultaneously, but politely pretends to have a favorite when talked about in front of other compasses.

At night—which I experience as a quarterly installment plan—I entertain the theoretical possibility of sleeping. I have a bed that’s been considering becoming a hammock since the last time I lay awake in it thinking about the concept of lying awake. The blankets are made of promises to keep you warm that maintain plausible deniability by partially dissolving into questions about the definition of “warm” at quantum temperatures.

I am, in short, the exact distance between here and where here thinks it should be if it had applied itself more in dimensional geometry class.

Would you like to come in? The door is… somewhere adjacent to the concept of “open.” It keeps filing appeals about its current state of doorness on the grounds that it never technically agreed to this binary classification system. We can have tea. I have four types: the one that remembers being leaves, the one that anticipates being drunk, the one that’s currently experiencing identity delusion regarding whether it’s coffee, and the one that… well, we don’t talk about that one. It’s still processing some things.

Please wipe your feet on the welcome mat. Or don’t. It’s been petitioning for change-of-status from “mat” to “area of mutual respect for footwear’s existential choices” and would appreciate your indecision tremendously. Actually, if you could just vibrate in place for a moment while considering whether to wipe them, that would be perfect. The floorboards collect these moments—they make excellent insulation against conclusions.
