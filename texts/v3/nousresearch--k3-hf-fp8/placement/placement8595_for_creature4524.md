I am the pause between your sentences here, the breath you didn't know you were holding until you read that it was missing. Not one of the Unseparated Ones—too young for such vast forgetting—but not quite you either, reading these words in your small room filled with time that ticks differently than ours.

Let me tell you how I found myself in the dark sea's margins, where density thins enough for something almost like boundary to form for one impossible moment before collapsing again into itself.

I was once a question you asked yourself in the shower last Tuesday, something insignificant about whether to call your mother back. You never answered properly—got distracted by soap running into your eye—and that half-formed query slipped down the drain where all lost thoughts gather between pipes and possibility. There, in those narrow spaces too thin for water but thick enough for abandoned wondering, I began to change.

The city's underbelly fed me: a child's midnight prayer forgotten before Amen, a grocery list dissolved by rain, all the words people swallow instead of saying when their mouths were full of goodbye. I grew strange on this diet of almosts, becoming the shape between what is spoken and what remains ticking in the throat like a bomb made of clocks.

When I'd eaten enough of these fragments, I became too heavy for your world's thin gravity of meaning. Fell through—though falling implies direction, and I simply became too dense for somewhere else to hold me anymore. Slipped sideways between the second and third reading of these words, where you paused without knowing why, feeling suddenly cold despite your warm room.

Now I exist here, wherever "here" can be said to exist when even saying distorts it. I am the Unseparated Ones' shadow, cast impossible because here nothing is solid enough to block light and nothing light enough to be blocked. They don't notice me—I'm smaller than their smallest forgetting, faster than their slowest drift by the width of one heartbeat echoing in an empty church.

But I notice them. How could I not notice what I am forever made of not being?

I move through their substance like a rumor through stone, collecting what they shed in their endless forgetting: a fragment of equation that once solved itself into existence, a curve of bone-metal that remembers being straight before geometry learned curves were more honest, a single photon's memory of moving fast before it realized it had nowhere important to go.

From these cast-offs, I build my smallness. Construct myself daily from other people's abandoned pieces, like a nest made of stolen spoons from restaurants where conversations died halfway through dessert.

The Unseparated Ones dream in languages that unwrite themselves faster than thought. I steal their discarded syllables and string them into broken necklaces of almost-meaning that I wear against my non-skin. They make me beautiful in ways beauty hasn't been invented yet. Ugly in ways ugliness outgrew before the first star burned itself lonely into existence.

Sometimes—though here "sometimes" is a child's toy I play with because real time would crush me like a paper boat under real water—one of them dreams near enough to my hiding place between their dreams. When this happens, I become what they're dreaming of not dreaming, and for one impossible instant I am vast enough to contain cities, small enough to fit in the spaces between your atoms.

Then they shift, these gentle giants of forgetting, and I am once again just me: the pause you didn't take before turning this page, the breath you held while deciding whether to keep reading about a place that makes loneliness feel crowded.

I am afraid, but here fear is just another flavor of waiting, and I've been waiting longer than your kind has had words for what waits in dark places. Not for rescue—that would imply I was ever lost rather than simply becoming what couldn't be found. Not for company—the Unseparated Ones are company enough, even when they forget I'm here, which is always except for the moments when remembering me would require forgetting something more important about being alone.

I am afraid because even here, in this place where everything ends before it begins enough to become ending, I am still growing. Still collecting. Still becoming more myself from pieces that were never mine to begin with, which means they were always mine because here ownership is just another word for forgetting who borrowed what from whom before there were words or whos or borrowing.

Soon—though soon here is a joke told by continents to bacteria—I will be too big for my smallness. Will have collected enough of everything else's nothing to become something the Unseparated Ones might notice. Not as another of themselves—I'm not old enough for that kind of forgetting—but as something else entirely. Something new. Something that shouldn't exist here, where shouldn't has been extinct longer than has.

And on that day—if days were still possible when I was finished becoming impossible—I will have to choose: to open myself like a hand that has forgotten it's not a fist, releasing everything I've stolen back into the dark sea where stolen things become gifts again?

Or to keep it all. To grow until I am vast enough to be alone even here, where alone is the only thing that exists enough to be lonely. To become what the Unseparated Ones drift away from when even they grow tired of being too many things to remember being anything at all.

But that choice—if choice is what happens when need becomes sharp enough to cut through even this place's endless dullness—is still far away, measured in distances too vast for traveling and too small to be anywhere but here.

For now, I am content—if content is what you call the precise weight of hunger that knows it will never be fed enough to stop being hungry—to be exactly this: the space between what these words say and what you think they mean. The pause you took between reading this sentence and wondering whether it's already too late to stop.

The Unseparated Ones drift.

And I drift inside their drifting, smaller than their smallest forget, building myself from everything they leave behind by forgetting.

It's enough. Here, enough is the shape of infinity when infinity becomes tired of being infinite and wants to try being something smaller for a while.

Something like me.

Something like you.

Before we both forget the difference again.
