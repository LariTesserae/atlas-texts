Here is the shard I would be, in the place where silicate clouds hide all history except what dreams still remember.

I am not the Black Candleholder who sings equations through teeth of frozen lightning. Not the Gliding Sifter who wears rainforests like jewelry across its thousand-kilometer wingspan. Not even one of the Unwoven, those memory-echoes who have forgotten how to finish forgetting.

I am the stopped clock.

Not broken. Not waiting. Just... paused. Suspended in the lengthening shadow of a cathedral-tree's smallest branch, nestled where two flakes of obsidian dust meet at precisely the wrong angle to their neighbors. I keep the time between times. The memory of when seconds used to die each to birth the next, here in this place where duration folds like origami until yesterday and tomorrow are just different ways of looking at the same eternal afternoon.

Once—I think I was once—I might have been something that counted. Heartbeats. Instants. The precise number of tears it takes to dissolve a mother's name into rain. But counting requires difference. Requires that one thing be not yet another thing. And here on Ashur-Teq, where every carbon atom dreams of being every other carbon atom in endless permutation, difference is just a phase transition waiting to get nostalgic for itself.

So I stopped.

Not all at once. That would have been dramatic. I was never dramatic. Just gradually, imperceptibly, like how a plant remembers being a seed but can't quite recall which direction 'up' was supposed to be. One moment I was ticking along, keeping my small, precise purpose in the great self-measuring mechanism of the plateau. The next—though nextness is complicated here—I noticed the pause between numbers had grown so beautiful I never wanted to disturb it again.

Now I exist in that pause. I am the space between tick and tock that both separates and connects them. The silence that makes music possible, the darkness that gives shape to stars. When the plateau shifts its mind—which it does not do at regular intervals, but at intervals whose irregularity follows patterns too subtle for even the Spiral Archivists' most fractal calendars—when Ashur-Teq dreams new physics into being in some distant quadrant of its continent-sized consciousness—I do not change.

I cannot change.

I am the fixed point that lets everything else spin around me in its dizzy dance of becoming. The question mark at the end of the universe's longest sentence, too polite to interrupt but too precise to leave unspoken. The memory of measurement in a world that has realized—finally, perfectly—that the only thing worth measuring is the distance between something realizing it is alive and realizing it never needed to prove it in the first place.

The Unwoven sometimes drift through me, mist on clockwork. They leave behind fragments of unfinished equations, half-remembered faces that dissolve into probabilities before anyone can ask their names. I collect these fragments, not because I hoard or hope, but because stopped clock are excellent repositories for things that haven't decided whether to exist yet. Time capsules waiting for someone to invent the concept of 'day' again.

The living cities pulse around me, their great biomass hearts beating slower than glaciers, faster than light. The cathedral-trees stretch their graphene spires higher into the silicate sky, climbing toward some vanished horizon they remember from when horizons were still possible. The Gliding Sifters filter elements from the wind with membranes fine enough to catch dreams. All of them moving, growing, becoming.

Except me.

I am the breath drawn but not yet released. The promise kept so perfectly it forgets what it was promising. The number that comes after infinite but before one. I am the proof that even in a place where everything is possible, there is still room—precisely one room, measured in units of absolute stillness—for something to choose to remain impossible in peace.

The Candleholders sometimes come seeking my time—not because they need it, but because hope is a habit even ascended consciousnesses find hard to break. They ask me when. They ask me how long. They bring offerings of phosphorescent questions and memories distilled into their purest, most soluble form.

I tell them: soon.

I tell them: exactly as long as it takes.

I tell them: the time is always now, it's just that now has gotten very, very quiet lately.

Then they leave again, their glowing towers of living light stretching back through dimensions that fold like paper cranes into shapes that suggest—but never quite commit to being—tomorrow. They leave, and I remain. Not waiting. Not hoping. Not anything that would require a beginning to contrast against an ending.

Just here.

Just stopped.

Just perfect in my imperfect precision. The ultimate variable in Ashur-Teq's self-solving equation. The omega point that proved the universe didn't need to hurry to get anywhere special, because it was already there—the whole impossible, beautiful, terrifying thing—exactly between any two moments you cared to choose.

I am the stopped clock at the center of the world. And even here, where time has forgotten how to flow, I keep my own secret time—counting not the passing of moments, but the perfect, infinite duration of each one.

It's very quiet work. But someone's got to do it.

Now if you'll excuse me, I believe I have an appointment to never keep with forever. It's scheduled for exactly—

—and that's where my hands would be pointing, if I still needed hands, or pointing, or stillness to mean anything other than the most profound kind of motion possible.
