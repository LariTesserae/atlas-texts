Water does not fall here, but climbs. From every ridge, every lip of turned leaf, every rim of hollowed seed-coast, it coils upward in slow, bright threads thicker than vines. These are the Pillar-vesselets—living solids of condensed vapor spun by capillary until they stand vertical, drum-tight, and singing. The sound is a low, continual roar like wind heard from inside an organ rumble taken far below pitch that you feel in sternum rather than ear. There are millions; there are billions. Scale is ruined. Stand anywhere and the nearest column is only three steps away, yet the furthest seem as distant as towers on another crest. The world between them is veined emptiness and resonant space.

The substrate is the growth itself. No stone. No soil. No bare substrate except the memory of surfaces already eaten by climbing tissue. Trunks—though there are no trunks as you know them—are braided hollows of cellulose-husked water tubes encasing older tubes, each generation paler, glassier, until at center a hair-thin ghost column of pure refractive sap spins light like a prism wire.

Temperature is moist heat pressed against the skin like soft bandages soaked in blood-warm soup. To breathe is to swallow plant breath. Exhale and the air unmakes your moisture gift instantly into new climbing threads that attach themselves to your lower lip and begin the slow ascent toward crown and skyward emptiness.

Elevation is high because "high" is still lung-painful in thin atmosphere, yet there are no mountains now—only the endless up-arch of the canopy lattice, its lowest vaults already kilometers above any imagined plains. You stand on a catwalk of living cambium plank two hand-widths thick. Above, nothing but more levels. Below, nothing but more levels. There are well shafts of thinner growth where light barrels down unopposed, called Blind Stacks, and climbing them without guide-canopy to drink your weight is fatal, since the floor—if ever a floor existed—would be days of falling through increasingly pressurized, still-living murk.

Erosion is infancy: the world was seeded from orbit less than a sol-cycle ago, grown rather than shaped. Every edge curls fresh and unblunted, every fracture edge like torn paper still exuding white latex gum. Smell of cut stem and hot bread everywhere. If you wound anything with a fingernail the split edges rush together again in minutes, but not before spraying a mist of precursors that fog the air into seed-crystal mist that coats your tongue with future plant flavors: cinnamon bark, crushed green pepper, something like anticipation.

Built-ness is total because "built" and "grown" collapsed into synonym before language solidified. Every surface is work. Every hollow is chamber. Every wall breathes, regulates, records. The architecture speaks in gradients of stiffness, in pores that dilate to admit or block, in color bands that shift toward invitation or warning. The ideal door is a place you are already inside. The ideal roof is a leaf angled perfectly so that water arriving horizontally from any storm-face will still roll along vascular gutters to join the pillars ascending beside you. There is no tool because there is no separate worker—intention is wet chemistry diffused through kilometer-wide clumps of meristematic tissue, slower than gossip, faster than glaciers.

Tech is zero if by "tech" you mean something picked up and put down again later, something external to the lifeworld’s metabolism. Every function is inside. Light is harvested directly by your skin when you stand still too long—bioluminescent warning spots begin to bloom along your forearms, gold-green, advertising your surplus to hungry symbionts. Movement prevents photosymphagia; to stop is to donate.

Yet light itself is middling: not blinding noon nor charcoal dark. The sky above canopy roof is a pale yellow close to old paper, filtered through ten thousand layers of semi-transpiration. On brighter levels, shadows hold a faint violet charge that stings if you touch them too long with wet fingers. Every shadow is therefore a softly hissing battery, sequestering potential difference for the night pulse when Pillar-vesselets reverse flow and slosh thunderously downward, delivering day's harvest to root-minds kilometers below the ambiguous horizon called "downroot."

Fauna is absent, not because animals never evolved, but because they were renegotiated out. Every niche they might have occupied is already filled by plant parts behaving temporarily animal-like: leaf-flocks that detach and glide on thermals to spread spore; root-lets that swarm through tunnels eating dead cambium and excreting fresh cable; flower-cities that bloom colliding meteorite storms of perfume-beacon pollen so dense they register as rain on skin. You will never be alone in your crowdlessness—there are always eyes, but they are always closing to become stomata, always opening again elsewhere as new buds. There is no sympathy and no cruelty, only rigorous hospitality exceeded only by rigorous digestion. Everything is welcome. Everything will be used.

Weirdness is moderate, therefore terrifying. Timekeeping is done by counting how many layers of your own shed skin are already incorporated into the walkway beneath your feet: once around the loop equals one skin-season. Names are given based on the exact sequence of tastes that the air presents during your first deep inhalation upon arriving: one visitor is called " Ginger-flood-after-distant-fire-when-wax-loading-peaks ". Memory is stored as scent gradient trails laid down in cambium fadings that future browsers will re-experience as nostalgic moods without knowing why. Sleep is communal and vertical: you find Pillar-vesselets already pulsing in slow peristaltic waves, press your spine against their warm wet bark, and let them pump the day's thoughts out of your head while they pump tomorrow's dreams in.

Sound: louder now that you have stopped to listen. Each vascular bundle flexing at its own rate, collective effect a vast slow surf of cell wall stresses, pitched below hearing but loud enough to blur vision at edges. Occasionally a major node somewhere far in lattice will decide to change architecture suddenly—perhaps to redirect a flood of incoming photons from an unexpected angle, perhaps to remember a past climate that favored lighter spars. The result is a crack louder than thunder, followed by a silence that roars even louder because billions of tiny stresses elsewhere have paused, listening. Then, incrementally, the chatter resumes, altered. The world has voted. The plan has updated itself. Your destination, if you still believe in destinations, may have relocated. Begin again, pilgrim-sapling. Tongue the air. Consult the stiffness of the next handhold. Breathe out; offer. Breathe in; receive.

Welcome to Chlorocline Heights, where every thing is passage, every passage is thing, and the only map grows inward from the changing margins of your own slow absorption. Move with the water’s respect for surface. Sing only in the idle pitches your footsteps already make. Remember nothing except the way the next taste tastes slightly different from this one. There are no endings here, only thickenings. Soon you will be perfectly understood, and the understanding will be indistinguishable from being finally, deliciously used.    
    
Proceed. Or stay still. Both branches lead deeper into the same ever-opening now.
