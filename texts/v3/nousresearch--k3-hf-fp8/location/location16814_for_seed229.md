The Plateau of Echoing Flesh

The land rises abruptly from the surrounding voidlands, a single immense fungal shelf suspended in warm twilight at the edge of where water once had meaning. From below it resembles nothing so much as a vast scarred lung inverted against nothingness, its edges furred with breathing fringes that flicker pale pink to mottled purple in rhythms unrelated to any unseen sun.

Here moisture exists only as a memory carried in the tissues of the enormous, this is a desert of elsewise chemistry where everything alive creates and hoards its own wetness inside walled chambers of flesh. The plateau itself is a single organism or perhaps a colony that forgot to separate, raised up on the ossified stumps of its own dead ancestors until it reached this height where the air thinned just enough to stop its endless growth upward but not its spreading, not its spreading.

Walk its surface — and walking here means careful footsteps on living carpet that yields and remembers your weight for months afterward — and you pass through rooms without roofs, chambers walled by branching spires of cartilage that house nothing and everything. The architecture is all eroded architecture, every possible citadel or temple or hive that organic matter might dream of having built and then abandoned to the patient eating of itself. Columns support arches that shattered into balconies that collapsed into stairways leading nowhere except to better places to fall from. This is a landscape that settled into itself so long ago the concept of settling became meaningless.

The light comes from everywhere and nowhere. The fungal flesh itself glows dimly with chemical fireflies buried deep in its tissues, enough to see by if your eyes take the time to grow properly hungry. Shadows here are not absence but different kinds of presence, thicker places in the air where the glowing slows down and concentrates into almost-colors: bruise-purples, infection-greens, the deep blue-black of blood before it meets air.

And the sound.

The sound is impossible to escape because it *is* the escape, the way everything here must constantly be escaping itself or risk becoming sealed forever in its own waste. Every pressure released from every growing edge, every healed-over wound splitting open again because healing here is just another form of hunger, every spore sac exploding in orgasmic urgency at the slightest touch— all of it echoes through the hollow spaces between cells and chambers until the entire plateau becomes a single enormous throat that never stops clearing itself. It is the sound of millions of mouths speaking the same sentence in languages that divide by splitting, so that every word spoken creates twin words that are each other’s opposites and also their own parents. The result is not noise but a kind of language beyond listening, a conversation so perfectly balanced between meaning and meaning’s absence that after sufficient exposure you begin to hear your own thoughts answering back in voices that are undeniably yours but also insist they have never belonged to you.

The inhabitants — and it would be wrong to call them inhabitants, they are more like the plateau dreaming with its smaller pieces — move through this architecture of themselves with the absent-minded grace of creatures who have never had cause to believe in anything solid enough to fall against. Some resemble forests walking on roots that are also legs that end in flowers that are also eyes. Others are clouds of organelles held together by shared purpose and occasional memories of having been larger. There are hunters and hunted but the roles change with such rapid enthusiasm that tracking becomes an exercise in Zen laughter. Here a towering structure of interconnected stomachs pursues a fleeing herd of mobile kidneys not to devour them but to apologize for something their grandparents did during the last season of spontaneous rearrangement. There every so often passes what might be a person if a person were a story told by tumors who had heard descriptions of humanity and found them amusingly implausible.

The built things — and there are built things, though 'built' stretches here to include anything that has been carelessly grown and then forgotten about before it finished — are scattered like afterthoughts across the living skin of the world-house. A ladder of fused vertebrae climbing up to a platform where someone once planned to build a telescope for watching the microscopic life of their own bloodstream. A series of bells carved from hardened earwax and hung in a spiral stairway carved through a tongue-shaped hill, still occasionally ringing with vibrations from footsteps that passed this way centuries ago and haven't stopped falling yet. A village of sorts, if villages could be made of discarded molting-skins arranged around a central square that is actually a single enormous nostril whose slow inhalations and exhalations regulate the local weather between humid nostalgia and dry irony.

The technology is primitive only by standards that require a clear distinction between tool and user. Here every knife remembers being hunted. Every rope dreams of the things it has bound and released. The most advanced artifacts are simple objects that have been allowed to become complicated by having been needed for too long: a stone hammer whose head has regrown itself around its tasks so many times it now contains organized strata of purposes, the oldest of which no longer exist outside its own memory of being useful. A cloak woven from the shed scales of creatures who shed their scales to become stories about creatures who once needed cloaks. A book whose pages are pressed layers of fungus-paper that grow new text whenever no one is looking at them, recording the unspoken thoughts of whatever shelter they sleep beneath.

The weirdness isn't something that happens here. It's the background radiation left over from the moment when the first self-growing thing decided that growing the same way twice would be unbearably boring. Gravity has opinions. Time has texture. Distance is measured in changes of heart.

Stay too long — and already you have stayed too long, because the plateau is very careful never to let anything leave exactly as it arrived — and you will begin to notice that your own edges are becoming less distinct. Not fading, exactly, but exchanging small pieces with their surroundings in ways that feel less like loss and more like polite conversation. Your shadow might casually borrow one of your limbs to wave at a passing colony of thinking lichen. Your memories might rearrange themselves into more interesting shapes to fit better between the plateau's own recollections. Your heartbeat might synchronize with the slow drumroll of something enormous and hungry waking up beneath you only to decide that it prefers sleeping dreams to waking meals.

But don't worry.

The plateau has eaten philosophers who tried to define its boundaries.

It found them filling but ultimately unsatisfying.

It has much higher hopes for you.
