The first thing you notice is the sound—a constant wet percussion that starts as moss-hushed droplets on broadleaf chimneys then builds through guttering network-vines into torrential applause against bark-barn roofscapes. Sifam is a suspended city of twelve ancient banyan-metropolis trees, their every trunk swollen into cathedral girth, their every root braided into aerial boulevards, their crowns lost in perpetual mist-clouds that never quite decide to rain or sleep.

Each banyan-city operates autonomous ecology, ancient enough to have forgotten the ground below. Temperature hovers at perpetual autumn dusk; cool enough that resident species keep moving, warm enough that nothing truly stagnates. Moisture is the medium here, not mere element. It beads on velvet-antennaed moths the size of prayer flags, collects in the cupped hands of epiphyte begonias that serve as streetlamps, runs through carefully cultivated fungal aqueducts where pale squidlings the color of bone china farm phosphate nodules by scent.

You stand on Gwyrgch's outer bough, where generations of tree-carpenters have trained living wood to form a lip-outlook over the inter-tree void. Below lies not space but density—ten thousand vertical meters of stacked habitats blooming inward instead of outward. Swifts have hollowed apartments in pith-soft branch cores and rent them to lesser hummingbirds. A parliament of foxglove-furred lemurs negotiates trade rights for especially phosphorescent lichens with a delegation of mantis-lawyers whose briefcases are themselves carefully folded carnivorous plants. Everything eats something else, yes, but here it happens under contract law signed in nectar-ink on leaves that fall upward when their terms expire.

The city's twelve trees maintain careful distance—close enough their upper branches interlace into the Cloud-Council at canopy level, far enough their root-wars have evolved into elaborate courtship rituals conducted by proxy armies of nitrogen-fixing bacteria. Each air-palm between trunks swarms with industry too small to see but vast in aggregate effect: pollination couriers with saddlebags of gene-royalties, spore-based stock exchanges where futures in next month's bloom-color are traded at frantic frequencies, funeral rites for fallen fruits conducted by beetle-priests whose chants alter the pH of decomposition to benefit their tree's allies.

Infrastructure is entirely biological, entirely negotiated. When Gwyrgch needs a new aqueduct to carry excess sap-flow from recent storm-dancing, committees of woodboring larvae present proposals to the tree's cambium court—each plan a subtle spiral of chew-marks visible only under ultraviolet light reflected from deep-heartwood pits where deities older than species dream slow dreams of photosynthesis and empire.

Light filters through leaf layers tuned like church organ pipes. Normal daylight enters green, exits twenty stories down as complex orchestrations of shadow-&-gold where street-market membranespulse with trade. That sunset-colored mushroom cluster costs twenty minutes of genuine curiosity about its partnership with the ghost ants that farm it for hallucinogens used in small doses to reveal the secret writing hidden in wood grain patterns—which reveal where next year's most fragrant resins will appear... All commerce is recursive, all wealth is knowledge of flows.

Fauna—every possible niche filled, overflowing, inventing new niches between breaths. Aerial shrimp filter spores from fog banks, their transparent wings strung like ghost harps by dew spiders that play them to attract prey. The prey, pollen-bloated leeches, are themselves harvested by tiny falcons whose talons contain anticoagulants used to manufacture the ink that records property disputes between neighboring lichens—disputes that often last centuries and involve dramatic changes in host tree architecture as one side slowly overgrows the other's carefully cited precedents.

It's loud because everything communicates constantly—survival demands broadcasting your chemical-spatial position, your intentions, your debts, your symbiotic allegiances, your poetic contributions to the ongoing epiphyte-sung epic that some say is the trees' true purpose for maintaining this impossible city held aloft by nothing but centuries of reciprocal chewing and being chewed upon in mutually agreed proportions.

Nothing is static. The city-tree Gwyrgch shifts its entire layout three degrees clockwise every equinox, responding to ancient treaties with distant suns or perhaps simply because it dreamed in circles again. Long residents memorize dozens of version-maps, delighting in seasonal errors where old kitchen-mushrooms suddenly open into unexpected staircases between former closets that briefly become perfect courting alcoves before the next cytokinetic shift.

You came here looking for something—answers? trade? sanctuary? Or did something in you recognize these impossible negotiations of matter and meaning finally honest about their terms? Whatever brought you, breathe carefully. In Sifam every inhalation is contract negotiation. Every exhalation votes on the city's next spontaneous architectural referendum.

The mist cloud above has been debating whether to rain or not for seventeen years now. This morning's preliminary moisture might be an offer. You'd do well to counter before it gets worse.

Or better.

Here, the two are the same thing, said the lungfish philosopher through its translator bird, as they both dissolved into next week's weather.
