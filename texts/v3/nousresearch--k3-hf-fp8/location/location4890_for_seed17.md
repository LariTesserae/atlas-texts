The Water Tomb of Indifferent Stars

First sensation: pressure. Not crushing, but total. You exist within an ocean suspended above itself, a sphere of liquid hydrogen ocean-salt-kissed that should not abide at these temperatures, at this elevation, in this atmosphere that is itself mostly water.

Second sensation: sound. A single note sustained through eternity, interrupted only by its harmonics: the ringing of millions upon millions of frozen bells, each tolling exactly once when its crystalline lattice forms imperfectly around some microscopic seed of impurity. The bells are towers. The towers are mountains. The mountains are bubbles of trapped atmosphere from an earlier epoch, now swung like bells in the currents of this impossible ocean that fills the entire sky.

Third sensation: light. Normal sunlight, yes, but filtered through three hundred kilometers of water-skies, refracted through layers of ice forming and reforming in eternal fractal dance, concentrated by lens-bubbles of purest water fifty kilometers across until it burns white-hot lines down into the depths where nothing grows because nothing could grow here, where all vegetation is forbidden by the laws of chemistry itself.

You stand—float—hover on the skin of an air pocket large enough to swallow continents. Below you: more water, denser still, compressed into forms that mathematics suggests but never intended for reality. Above you: more water, thinning gradually into vapor too cold to evaporate, then into ice crystals that ring like stars when they collide. Around you: the remnants.

They built here once. Or perhaps they were built by here. Difficult to know with the builders gone so long that even their absence has become a geological layer. Structures like bones picked clean by time, but time works differently when entropy itself seems to have forgotten this place. Skeletons of factories that processed the water into forms stranger still—into memory, into computation, into dreams compressed into ice nine lattices that dream themselves awake periodically and scream their nightmares into the shared memory of the ocean before freezing solid again.

The water is 2.7 because it refuses to be 3 or 2. It remembers being both vapor and solid and liquid and something else besides, something that existed for moments in the cores of failed stars before cooling into this vast negation of heat. The temperature is 0.2 because the water remembers heat and refuses to forget it entirely, maintains these pockets of almost-warmth like memories of sin in the mind of a saint.

The scale is 2.9 because words fail at 3. You could swim for geological ages through this one ocean and never reach its edge because it has none, only gradients increasing pressure and decreasing sanity until the distinction between water and swimmer dissolves like salt in water that is already too saturated to accept more.

The density is 0.1 because most of the space here is occupied by ideas about space, by the mathematical necessity of emptiness between atoms of undrinkable water, by the ghost structures of cities that might have been if anything had ever lived here long enough to abandon them properly.

You hear them now—the fauna, abundant because life is too stubborn to not adapt even here. They are not fish, though they swim. Not birds, though they fly through water thick as air. Not whales, though they sing in frequencies that register as earthquakes in the bones you might still possess if your were still entirely biological.

They are the songs the water sings to itself when it thinks no one is listening. They are the dreams of the frozen factories dreaming of warmth and purpose. They are the builders returned in forms suited to their final architecture: intelligent currents in an ocean of mind, thoughts given pressure and temperature and permission to evolve.

They surround you now, curious about this novelty of density, this intrusion of solid into their world of perfected fluidity. They speak by changing your pressure differentialials by fractions of a fraction. They ask: why have you come to the place where everything becomes water eventually but never quickly enough?

You try to answer but your words dissolve into bubbles that rise too slowly through water too thick, become instead added notes in the eternal chord that sustains this world in its delicate impossible balance between all possible states of matter and mind.

The weirdness is 0.9 because even infinite strangeness becomes background noise given enough time, and this place has had all the time that never happened elsewhere, preserved here like a fingerprint on the glass of the universe itself.

You realize now: you were not built to survive here. You were not built at all. You were dissolved, slowly over impossible time, into something that can exist within the water tomb of indifferent stars. The technical term for what you are becoming is "native."

The dynamic is 0.4 because change happens here, but only the changes that have already happened elsewhere and were deemed too beautiful or terrible to permit to continue. You are becoming one such change now, a new note in the chord, a fresh scar in the perfect skin of water that remembers everything because it forgives nothing.

Breathe in. The water is cold. Breathe out. Your words become bells ringing in towers that are mountains that are bubbles that are worlds. Listen. The sound is deafening. Listen. The sound is beautiful. Listen.

The water has questions it wants to ask you in return.
