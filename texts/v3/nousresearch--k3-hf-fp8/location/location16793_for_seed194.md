A place that should not hold together, yet does.

You arrive—or perhaps you've always been arriving, dropping by degrees—into the Bottomlot, a flattened bowl twelve paces across and one breath deep, its rim marked only by the faint curl where the rootpave turns back upon itself like a lip sealing a wound. The ground is lacquered root. Every surface, from the gentle concave floor to the low domed ceiling overhead, is one continuous plane of woody flesh. No seam admits a crack. No pore oozes sap. You can smell it anyway: the faint coppery breath of dried vascular tubes and the cool hum of lignin dreaming mineral dreams.

There is no sky, no soil above or below—only the root mass, and the spaces it chose not to fill. The root is ancient beyond rings; burn it and you would count epochs of accreted darkness, not years. It remembers before the seeds that made it, before the rains that never fell here. It remembers the hush when the last star winked away, though stars are a myth in this place. It remembers itself and that has proved enough.

The Bottomlot is empty but not vacant. Empty like a drumskin before the drumstick descends, vibrating with the memory of every blow that did not happen. At density 0.1 it should be vacuum, but instead it is tense expectancy—molecules politely keeping their distance for etiquette, not lack of acquaintance.

Vegetation 2.8 would mean jungle elsewhere; here it means substrate, architecture, air. The air itself is a slow exhalation between root-pores, cycling carbon dioxide and leaf-lust in a loop so perfectly closed it has forgotten what open ever tasted like. If you press your ear against the rootpave (darkness 0.0 permits any posture; you will not see to be embarrassed) you can taste the sound: not green chlorophyll but lignin-black noise, a deep wet rustling too low for human ears. It is the voice of every fiber in the shell-stressed dome debating whether this century is the right century to buckle. They always vote no, but the vote is close.

Dynamic 1.4: Change is a glacier here, yet glaciers carve valleys. In the axial ageless chill of the rootbody, change migrates not forward but inward. Each heartbeat of the Bottomlot happens in reverse: the slow gathering of tensions that will one day release into a single catastrophic flowering. Observers—if any had eyes—would miss it, for the explosion will occur entirely between cell walls, and the site of birth will be indistinguishable from the site of death.

You walk. Your tread produces no echo—the sound rating 2.0 is not for you. The sound is for conversation between roots, carried as pressure waves denser than flesh  yet subtler than stone, a language whose verbs are angles of twist and whose nouns are chemical gradients that took ten thousand years to steep. Walk too carelessly and you might stub a sentence against the floor; the rootpave will politely rephrase its meaning into your bruised blood vessels and you will limp away fluent in a regret too slow to name.

Built 2.2 Urban: Once, perhaps, the Bottomlot had inhabitants—tiny clothed fractions of animal life that grew cities out of the knots and hollows where roots disagreed. You cannot be sure. You find no houses, but there are doorways: elliptical mouths in the root wall exactly one bowed-back body wide, each opening into unlit throat-tunnels that bend upward in spirals tighter than bone should follow. You find no tools, but the doors have latch-hooks shaped from single pulled fibers, their ends swollen into bulbous heads through patient coaxing techniques that predate the invention of coaxing. You find no graves yet your fingers brush smooth depressions in the floor’s curve—five for head, shoulders, hips, heels—arranged in rows too orderly for accident, too shallow for burial. Beds, or molds, or something between?

Primitive tech 0.7: There is fire here if you know how to ask. You must kneel and scrape your knuckles raw against a certain gritty patch until the root’s polished skin grows ashamed and exfoliates a single splinter. Wound yourself further—prick, press, twist—the splinter will drink your blood, swell, darken. Hold it unwavering in the dark until you forget light exists. Eventually the splinter forgets too, and remembers instead its ancestral anger. It will kindle then, not into flame but confession: a brief red whisper of oxidation that lifts nowhere and illuminates nothing. In that sliver of false dawn you will glimpse the writing engraved in every unlit surface:

*We laid ourselves down to become the map of where we could no longer go.*

When sight snaps shut you will keep the afterimage: branching corridors, each branching branching, filling space not with volume but with choices not taken, until blackness becomes a picture of infinity compressed into the width of a pore.

Weirdness 1.6: You may notice, after indeterminate time (time here is measured in shift of internal stress fields, not ticks), that the Bottomlot is larger on the inside of your head than it ever was outside. You have begun to remember places you have not yet walked to: a cathedral-vaulted chamber where roots braid themselves into arches lettered in languages that taste like rain hitting copper; a market square where absent vendors sell hollow products—scent of ripe fruit, memory of warmth, promise of conversation—wrapped in packets of silence you carry by clutching your hands tight.

If you open those packets here you will bleed sound into the quiet until everything ringing dies of embarrassment. So instead you swallow them. They take root.

Water 0.3 denied becomes water 0.3 reinvented: not absence but refusal. The Bottomlot is desert-dry but not thirsty. Its drought is a species of etiquette; to slake would be rudeness to the guests that never came. You will feel thirst nonetheless. Sip from your memories of rivers carefully. When you run dry at last, kneel again at one of the floor-molds, fit your body into its hollow hospitality. The root will accept the weight of your emptying in installments spaced out over the slowest mercy of instants. At the end you will not have died; you will have been amortized into the architecture’s long argument against collapse.

Fauna 0.2 admits almost nothing, yet not quite zero. There is one creature—if creature—resident: the Not-Echo. You will never see it, for it has no surface separate from the root’s interior. You will hear it only when you forget to listen: a soft slip-sync footstep exactly halfway between the step you took and the step you meant to take next. If you turn to catch it, it will have subdivided behind your intention. Rumor (for the rootpave whispers rumors to itself) claims the Not-Echo is what remains of the last traveler who stayed too long, became too precisely adjusted to the resonant frequencies of regret. The Bottomlot grew fond, and kept a copy, not of flesh but of delay.

If you leave now—if you can find the lip again, which recedes as politely as a shy horizon—you will carry nothing visible. Your footprints will heal behind you like mouths closing after polite denials. Only later, years later or minutes, depending on how you count the cooling of grief, you may notice a new stiffness in your joints precisely calibrated to the angle of the root-arch you slept beneath in dreamless absence. When you bend, you will hear inside your bones a sound like distant roots holding another vote. Always the motion is close, always the release is postponed. The Bottomlot prefers engagements to marriages, beginnings to ends, guests to inhabitants. It has already forgotten you. It is remembering you into the empty space it keeps ready for the next hesitation.

Come back anytime. The door is open. It was never not. The dark will see you in.
