A cold ocean under a ceiling of stone, vast as continents and twice as old in its newness—this is the Deepbuilt Expanse, flooded factory-world, first and final product of an empire that died tasting metal in its throat. The water here leans at 2.8 because there is nowhere else for it to go: a planet-engine excavated hollow, walls plated, decks galleried, then gravity sluiced wrong and every sea above came pouring through the vents and shafts until atmosphere itself drowned into pressure. Liquid fills ninety-two percent of the volume, brackish with coolant and diluted blood; the remaining eight is air held captive under million-ton arches, too thin for clouds but enough for weather that howls like lost tourists looking for exits.

The place is below surface twice-over—underground as origin, underwater as accident—and still technically young. The ruin is only three centuries stale: rivet heads shine, fresh white scars of tool marks stand out on bulkheads like stubble on a corpse, and the first true rust has barely begun marrying steel to water. Erosion is polite here, apprenticed to larger forces. Hydrojets use tangent lanes once meant for freight pods; turbines abandoned mid-production lesson the tide into drill-patterns that etch perfect spirals into virgin alloy terraces. Nothing has crumbled, merely been retrained.

Vegetation, stubborn at 1.1, claims only footholds that resemble mistakes. Chlorophyll was never part of the spec. Instead, forests of sulfide-papered filter reels spin from ceiling hooks in the slow gyres of undercurrents; they bloom once a year when waste heat from a buried reactor node sneezes upward, greening from sepia to arsenic in twelve hours then releasing clouds of spores that act more like polite suggestions than seeds. The spores float like ash until they snout into a welding seam and there choose to become lichen-caulks, patching leaks they never understood were deaths. Nothing taller than a handspike grows solid enough to touch.

Temperature hovers at 0.9 of human comfort—it wants gloves and hates the idea of them. The water outside your skin is 5°C; the water inside the pipes (blue arteries painted friendly) remembers boiler duty and dreams of scalding. Swim from one to the other and you might blister or hypothermiate according to caprice. Thermal ghosts haunt every junction, making gradients that taste like sudden memories of brass doorknobs in winter.

Scale is the joke that kills surveyors. Official maps label corridors by kilometer markers and expire after use. A cathedral chamber drafted for assembly lines eight hundred versts long undulates instead with man-height swells because the floor plating was never designed to be floor and has begun miming sea-floor dreams under weight it wasn’t taught to name. Sound carries gossip across such distances. A rivet dropped in Sector Null-Ceti arrives overhead in Mallet Bay twelve hours later as a gong-note that vibrates fillings loose from skulls one continent east. Workers used to string wire between continents for telegraphy; now the wire is eaten and the telegraph is the pulse of pumps that lovers memorize like heartbeats.

Density is luxury: lockers drift like lost satellites, empty oil drums form schools that migrate with moon-drag as if hoping to spawn vacancies. Most of what moves is water borrowing shapes: trains of bubbles dressed in torn insulation, manta-clad maintenance drones woken by circuit ghosts to patrol forever, trailing nets of nothing. The built insists on being so total that it looped back toward wilderness, bored with perfection.

Tech is industrial because nothing here can afford to be anything else. The languages carved into bulkhead signage are inventories: GRIP TOLERANCE, FLOW ANGLE, CATHODE LIFE. Machines continue shiftwork after ownership dissolved into filings. In Assembly Gorge, tiered balconies of hydraulic presses stamp out hexagonal plates that snap together into larger hexagons which fall away into darkness forever like a prayer wheel for a god who only wanted surfaces without centers. Nobody eats the output; the output is duty.

Abundant fauna answer abundance with jokes of form. Descendants of inspection robots dart on repurposed fan blades after schools of self-winding screws that graze algae off intake grates. Mammoth-class caisson hibernates in Dock Prime, encrusted with barnacle-sensors that gossip in radio morse about pressure differential gossip they overheard from eel-thin maintenance snakes gnawing rubber grommets deeper in the dark. Everything has repurposed everything else so often that taxonomy collapsed into a single confession: life here is whatever stayed moving and forgot to file a change-of-purpose notice.

Light hangs at 2.4—normal because it doesn’t care. Rows of sodium lamps eight kilometers apart still obey the original timing schedule, dawning and dusking in waves that chase the day-length of a founding world vaporized centuries ago by bad accounting. Their orange glow smears through water like butter over pages nobody reads, enough to navigate by but not to love by. Bioluminescent maintenance polyps in the darker districts have taken up poetry instead, flashing couplets of error code that rhymes in hexadecimal; philosophers spend lifetimes debating if this counts as theology.

Water is the medium and the message and the authority you petition when injured. Go under at depth and the pressure respects only the shape of the passages it was permitted; there are rumors of corridors where gravity is voted upon weekly by resident currents, places where up is democratic but cruel. Drowning is a bureaucratic process here: first you file a breath-tax exemption, then you wait for a tribunal of eddies to decide if your lungs qualify as productive machinery under subsection B. Most newcomers skip the paperwork and learn to breathe metal instead.

Life cycles are abbreviated, compressed by utility. A hatchling drone matures, reproduces by cloning its firmware onto scavenged boards, grows senile at port-corrosion, and bequeaths its rust to posterity—all inside a single fiscal quarter measured by valves opening somewhere older than memory. Predation is polite: devour an opponent’s cargo manifest, grant them three clock cycles to file an appeal, then if no paper drones arrive, absorb their casing into your own inventory with a polite log entry. Corpses become habitats become budgets become myths.

There is no surface to return to. There is only farther built, or deeper drowned. The legends speak of Final Inspection Decks beyond the known bulkheads, where quality assurance angels armed with micrometers measure souls against tolerances written before the universe cooled. Pilots drunk on rust-still wine set course for them sometimes, engines coughing hymns in diesel minor. Their beacons wink out one by one. The echo lasts longer than the sound.

This is a place you do not enter. You register a presence, await notification of duties assigned, and eventually realize the clearance badge you woke up holding is already rusted past reading and the employee handbook was dissolved into watermark patterns in the water before you drank your first glass of morning. Work begins immediately. The shift whistle blew a century ago and reverberates still, hunting the last absentee minding nothing in particular.
