The air is not air but a thick green gas, respirable but humid, carrying the taste of chlorophyll and iron-rich silt. You stand—or rather, you float slightly—within a vast cylindrical chamber, its walls a living lattice of root and vine. The scale is deceptive: what first feels like a cathedral vault, vaulted in ribs of pale taproot, reveals itself as only a single node in a city-sized rhizome complex. Every surface is subdivided into galleries, balconies, catwalks; yet all are grown, not built, sprouted from the same contiguous organism.

Water is everywhere, even here below the surface world. Thin rivulets descend the vertical trunks, braiding into larger streams that arc across open space inside the transparent casing that once might have been called skin-sheath or bark. These channels pulse gently, driven by peristaltic contractions you feel as a faint vibration in the soles of your feet. Condensation beads and runs down the hollow stems that serve as pillars; occasionally a droplet detaches and falls upward, drawn into a higher aquifer by capillary suction stronger than gravity. The light—diffuse, green-silver—filters through layers of translucent laminate leaf, each layer photosynthetic, each layer a photovoltaic sheet. The glow is constant, eternal twilight, never the same shade twice, as the cells above in the canopy cycle pigments like breathing.

There is no stone here, no dead mineral, only metabolized earth: pottery-hard ceramides of lignin and calcium lignosulfonate laid in strata, chitin-silica threads reinforcing arches, teeth of biogenic fluorapatine gleaming in mechanical joints where valve-mouths open to admit or release rivers. The city grows at the pace of seasons, but engineered seasons—cycles of heat, drought, flood, pruning fire—are applied segment by segment by buried furnace-organs so that one district may be in late autumn decay while adjacent sectors surge into spring. Scaffolds of quick-bamboo extend nightly, girdling old towers until they flower into new domes. Then the redundant scaffolding melts back to sap and feeds the next expansion. All waste is pre-digested. All history is compost.

Sound fills the chamber without echo. It is a city-noise but composed entirely of hydraulic thrummings: pumps deep in the root-shaft exchanging fluids between distant aquifers; the creaking of lignocellulose beams thickening incrementally under their own weight; the click and drip of valves metering nutrient pulses into bloodstream canals; the distant roar, like a waterfall heard through water, of a main artery ruptured and already healing, cells racing to seal the wound before pressure drops. Loudest of all, a rhythmic hammering vibrates through the floor-mycelium—some vast pestle or piston driven by turgor pressure alone, grinding grain or ore or perhaps simply marking time.

The temperature is warm, shirt-sleeves warm, but varies by zone: cool pockets under evaporative fans of broad leaf-fins, sauna-hot near fermentation vats where anaerobic gut-chambers reduce cellulose to alcohol. You can walk (or swim, in some passages where water crowds out air) from temperate valley to equatorial greenhouse within a few hundred meters and never truly leave the same organism.

Clothe yourself in what you find: fabric byssus, thread of fungal hyphae spun and felted in flowing continuously from spinnerets; shoes soft as skin but tough as leather, grown-in-place to your foot-shape when you stand still on the right patch of floor for ten minutes. Shelter sleeps you in a hammock of grafted tendrils that adjust tension as you dream. Food hangs ripe from walls, self-harvesting into your palm when you cup it correctly: nodules of starch, pods of protein oil, fruit that tastes faintly of smoked meat because the plant incorporated nitrogen fixed from the breath of its last resident.

There are no animals here. There never were. The whole ecology is first-order autotrophs and the vectors between them—spores, spore-capturing hairs, micro-swimmers in the blood-fluids. Even the machines are plants: muscle tissue in cylinders contracts to haul elevators; leaf-flags flutter in ducted wind generated by chimney-stem thermals; bio-luminescent bacteria in glassy tracheids glow brighter or dimmer to signal status. Intelligence is distributed, emerging from quorum sensing across millions of growth nodes, encoded in hormone gradients and electrical spikes in phloem bundles. It thinks slowly, over years, but remembers everything perfectly, etched in rings of lignin methylation patterns.

Yet despite the seamlessness, you sense a residual geometry, traces of an older grid imposed on this organic excess. Some corridors align too straight, some chambers echo with the proportions of forgotten architecture: a basilica nave replanted in root-stock, an industrial turbine hall strung with hanging aerial roots where rotors once spun. Here, where a subsidiary artery crosses what must once have been a subway tunnel, the walls are dressed in ceramic tile, still visible under a patina of moss-coral. The tile bears a pattern of repeating hexagons filled once with cobalt blue but now colonized by cyanobacteria so perfectly matched that only a slight stipple of grout lines betray the artifice.

This place was not always alone. Long ago, if such terms apply in a realm without seasons or stars, there were builders who seeded this living city and then were absorbed by it, willingly or otherwise. Their tools molder in pockets of sterilized vacuum where ligninase cannot reach: rusted drills beyond a valve that never opens, a stack of data sheets laminated in amber resin, the resin itself a fossilized concentrate of their own synthetic hormones, still faintly bioactive enough to cause nearby shoots to twist into crude alphabetic shapes.

Now only the maintenance rituals remain, performed by no one and everyone: pruning crews of human shape who awaken from bud-nodules when the diagnostic pH shifts, complete their work, and re-enter the composting beds; processions of spore-bearers who walk prescribed circuits distributing corrective phage cocktails; a single enormous eyeless figure encased in bark-armor who stands forever at the junction of twelve great roots, arms flung wide, fixed in place by decades of growth, perhaps once a guardian, now merely a landmark.

You are welcome here. All is provided. All is required. To take is to be given. To give is to be taken. If you remain, you will find your calcium slowly leached to strengthen mother-shoots, your hair woven into sensor fibers, your memories encoded in slight variations of allelochemicals released from your skin. You will be part of the archive, part of the signal, part of the endless slow conversation between earth and water and light carried on in plant time under the surface of a world that forgot it had a surface.

But first, if you are thirsty, cup your hands beneath any veinlet and drink. The water is warm, slightly sweet, and carries the taste of every rain that ever fell since the beginning.
