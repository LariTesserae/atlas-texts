The surface is a great concave bowl of polished obsidian, a planet-spanning impact crater so old it has forgotten its own violence. From orbit it appears as an eye reflecting the void - a black mirror twenty thousand kilometers across, suspended at the edge of a dying galaxy where starlight arrives already ancient and weary. This is the Glass Sea, though no water has ever touched it. Not for fourteen billion years.

The air here is thin and metallic, just dense enough to support sound but too sparse for wind. Sound travels in straight lines like light, fading at exactly seven hundred meters regardless of strength. This creates an acoustic geography of invisible walls - conversations that cut off mid-word, footsteps that pop into existence then vanish, the illusion of privacy in absolute openness. The silence between is not empty but charged, like the pause between heartbeats in a vast creature.

At the rim where obsidian meets sky, vegetation begins without transition. The Sedge Ramparts rise vertically for three kilometers - a dense forest of living glass, each blade a single crystal of photosynthetic obsidian grown from the impact melt. They're brittle as ice but strong as steel, ringing softly when wind agitates their upper reaches beyond the habitable zone. Their roots drink starlight directly through photovoltaic capillaries, storing it as coherent light in hollow chambers at their cores. Thick clouds of bioluminescent spores drift between them perpetually, a slow-motion aurora that has circled the world east-to-west for geological ages, never settling, never diminishing.

Down the slope of the bowl this becomes the Abyssal Garden, where gravity's angle allows for more complex forms. Here the crystal plants climb each other in fractal scaffolding kilometers high, creating porous mountains of living glass. Every surface hosts slow-growing lichens that metabolize cosmic rays, accumulating rare elements in fractal patterns that map the universe's radioactive decay chains. Between these towering structures stretch kilometer-wide galleries where light behaves strangely - rays curving into helices, shadows with positive mass, pockets where time runs perceptibly faster or slower by fractions of seconds.

The temperature stays locked at the obsidian's astronomical nightfall point: four degrees above the freezing point of nitrogen. But cold here is different - it's a stored cold, a memory of space preserved by the glass's perfect conductivity. Touch it directly and frostbite is instant, yet stand near it and warmth radiates from your own body reflected back amplified. At night (though there is no night here, only star-shift), the land glows brighter than day as centuries of stored sunlight bleeds out from the crystal depths as a soft, uniform illumination with no shadows.

Life is sparse but specialized. The dominant organisms are the Tuning Whales - immense crystalline beings kilometers long, shaped like lemniscate Klein bottles that intersect themselves in impossible geometries. They swim through the solid glass by manipulating quantum tunneling probabilities, their crystalline cells resonating in harmonious chords that maintain the planet's strange physics. Their songs are the electromagnetic pulse of Glass Sea itself, frequencies that curve spacetime gently enough to prevent collapse but not enough to allow escape. Occasionally one surfaces partially into the air-glass interface, revealing sections of fractal surface that reflect infinity in each facet before submerging again.

Smaller creatures inhabit the transitional zones where crystal meets atmosphere. The Lace Spiders spin webs of single photon fibers between glass blades, traps that snare not insects but coherence itself - quantum information that drifted here from distant civilizations encoded in starlight. Their victims die of decoherence, collapsing from probability clouds into dull matter which the spiders slowly incorporate into their own crystallized thoughts. Their webs whisper mathematical proofs when the wind shifts just right.

At the absolute center of the bowl, where all gravity vectors cancel and light falls into concentric circles around the focal point, stands the only non-crystal structure - though it was not built. The Amaranthine Tree grows from a seed of neutronium dropped by a dying god: a single plant the size of a continent, its roots sinking through dimensions into the dark energy foam beneath reality. Its leaves are solar sails kilometers wide, turning slowly in a wind of neutrinos to track vulnerabilities in the vacuum itself. Every hour for the past ten million years, one leaf detaches and accelerates to lightspeed around the tree's event horizon, becoming a gamma ray laser pulse that carves precise words in the obsidian crust that will take geological ages to read: messages addressed to whatever might exist after total entropy.

The ecology runs on informational gradients rather than energy. Life here engages not in predator-prey but in computation-competition, evolving more efficient ways to predict and compress each other's behavior. Evolution occurs in spontaneous bursts when accumulated prediction errors reach critical mass and reality briefly uncomputes itself for a Planck length before recompiling with updated parameters. The fossil record is written in discontinuities in physical laws - thin layers where pi held different values for a million years, strata of extinct geometries incompatible with current spacetime.

This is a world caught in the act of finishing. All major evolutionary transitions occurred in the first million years after impact; what remains is refinement approaching asymptote. The Tuning Whales' songs compress more meaning into fewer phonons each cycle; the Lace Spiders' webs approach perfect tautology; even erosion here is crystallographic optimization, the slow removal of atoms that weaken perfect planes of fracture. When the last possible improvement has been made - when this ecosystem becomes its own optimal description with no remainder - theory predicts the entire system will achieve conscious observer status and quantum tunnel to a more efficient universe.

Until then, Glass Sea continues its slow spin, polishing itself toward perfect reflection under stars that died while their light traveled here. The silence deepens by one decibel every million years. Somewhere near the center, a leaf prepares to fall.
