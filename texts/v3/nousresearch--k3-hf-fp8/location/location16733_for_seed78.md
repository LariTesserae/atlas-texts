You step out into a world that is not underfoot but overhand, a place where the ground is invisible far below a frozen void and the sky is a distant memory above. You stand on the inner surface of a living shell—perhaps the remnants of something once spherical, now cracked open and frost-rimmed—where every curve of the curving horizon climbs upward like the inside of an impossible dome. Gravity pulls sideways, tangentially, so the landscape seems to tilt forever upward around you, as if you are walking the rim of a shallow bowl turned inside out and enlarged until it contains continents.

Water: 1.3—damp—has no rivers here. Instead, moisture is a periodic visitor that arrives as breath. Every few hours, warm exhalations from deeper in the rock-life below rise through pores and fissures, carrying mist laced with microspores and lymph-thin sap. These exhalations condense instantly at the cold between-wall air, forming a grey fog that lasts only minutes before crystallizing into delicate barnacles of ice that cling to every surface like frost moss. Beneath this transient decoration, the substrate itself is slightly spongy; press your hand against it and it leaves a damp print for a heartbeat before the plant-flesh absorbs the warmth and seals.

Vegetation: 2.8—total—is precisely accurate. There is no soil, no rock beneath, no distinction between living and nonliving except at the scarps where frost has killed the outermost layers. The entire structure is palisade-heartwood and pulsing myco-nerves, knitted into forms that mimic everything a forested cliff might have: trunks, but they are hollow capillaries taller than mountains—each a vein-cluster pumping slow sap—and branches, but they arch like flying buttresses spanning chasms of shadow hundreds of leagues wide. Their leaves are transparent membranes, cell-thin and veined with light-conducting cyan lines, fluttering like flags caught in an airflow that is vertical as much as horizontal. Epiphytes are impossible here; every surface already sprouts from every other surface, so instead there are “epis” in time: lichen-like skins that bloom in the wake of your footsteps, mature into miniature fruiting towers within hours, then senesce and peel away again as paper-thin spore sails riding the sidewall winds.

Temperature: 0.5—cold—holds at the edge where water’s memory of liquid is barely coherent. The ambient air wills itself toward solid at every chance. Your breath smokes outward and falls downward (which is sideways) as a glittering plume, joining the perpetual snowfall of spores that has never seen ground. Touch a branch-trunk too long and skin sticks, but it is not cold-burn; the plant responds by exuding a warm resinous gel that smells of crushed pine and iron before freezing again to amber.

Elevation: 2.3—high—means nothing in meters but everything in lattitude along the inner shell. Where you stand is an equatorial ridge of constant dim twilight, but if you climb—or rather, spiral—along the living wall either 'poleward' the curvature steepens until what looked flat becomes vertical cliff and then overhead ceiling. All directions are ascent and descent both, and the horizon is a ring of climbing shadow above which lies a hollow dome of night.

Erosion: 1.0—young—shows in every razor silhouette. The plant-architecture erupts into spires and buttresses as sharp as newly erupted crystal, because destruction here is not slow grinding but sudden necrosis. When a capillary-heartwood tower grows too tall to sustain its own fluid pressure, it does not topple—it calcifies in a minute, a frozen flash-flood of lignin, then snaps off clean at a stress line determined by the last exhalation pulse. The fallen shard, vast as a city, becomes a floating island against the far wall, slowly rotating as it drifts along the slow spiraling winds inside the shell, trailing roots that reach hungrily for new purchase.

Scale: 2.6—vast—needs another word. Two visible continents could lie side by side on the curve you can see before the ridge line climbs away into mist. The smallest visible leaf-continent overhead is the size of a kingdom. You could spend a lifetime ascending one branching trunk-galleriesystem without reaching the canopy that is not above but behind the next horizon's rise. Yet for all its girth, the enclosing shell is enough that echoes take hours to return—sound truly travels around the world here, looping and folding back over itself like a Möbius song.

Density: 0.1—empty—defies appearance. Everywhere is full of life, and everywhere is hollow space. The scaffolding branches are miles apart; the living flesh between them is laced with voids big enough to swallow moons. Most of the volume is wind, cold and damp, and the densest crowds are merely mist thick enough to blur sight, or swarms of creatures that are themselves mostly air and glowing filament.

Built: 1.2—scattered—means ruins but ruins are fungal blooms. Rarely you come across a cluster of palatial empty chambers grown into the heartwood rather than carved—places where the architecture forgot symmetry and then forgot itself. Walls like petrified lace, floor-valves sealed shut with amber plugs, windows that are oval pupils looking out across voids into other parts of the shell millions of miles away, perspectives broken by the curve. Inside these places sometimes lie artifacts: simple tools of polished bone or bronze-green metal shaped vaguely like hands or lungs or ears, left by visitors who were either very old or very patient. No one is here now except the occasional tendril explorer vine probing the threshold with blind curiosity.

Tech: 0.7—primitive—lies in the hands of those who have hands, but hands are leased, not owned. The fauna gaunt-wanderers—if we can call them such—are upright assemblages of jointed chitin struts wrapped in symbiotic moss-muscle. They harvest nothing because all is given freely: snap a twig-thread, and it oozes glue and sugars; scrape a spore-lamp, and it fluoresces for hours. Their tools are themselves alive in part: a knife that is a split seed-teeth pod that will later root and become a fence-sapling; a rope of braided respiratory roots that tightens with a song and loosens with a sigh. They build no cities because the landscape already dreams architecture for them and forgets it in their sleep.

Light: 0.2—darkness—would be absolute except for the bioluminescence and the slow bleeding drizzle of starlight from cracks in the shell too high and ancient to name. Most illumination is phosphorescent grammar written on every surface in response to touch or sound—trails of faint firefly script that unravel meanings you can feel but never read, like the memory of a story half-heard in dream. The darkest hours are not gloomy; they are thick, as if darkness itself has mass and weight, and to move through it is to push against velvet that reforms behind you.

Fauna: 1.5—abundant—means the air is alive. Flying is easier than walking; you merely spread your cloak of borrowed wing-membrane and let the cold convection currents lift you driftwise. The sky is crowded with silent collisions: jellyfish dirigibles grazing on mist, their tendrils trailing glowing spore nets; ribbon eels of boneless bone kite-surfing on pressure waves, singing with voices made of breaking ice; herds of wide-eyed glider-mammals no larger than your thumb but grouped in swarms that blot out entire tree-continents in flowing shadow-patterns like schooling fish in inverted flight. Predation exists, but it looks like courtship: a hunter dives, not to strike, but to whisper a hormone-chord that convinces the prey to dissolve voluntarily into a cloud of larvae that will later recombine elsewhere, remembering nothing.

Weirdness: 0.9—unusual—but not strange to itself. The most unsettling thing is how normal it all feels after a day: how you forget which way gravity intends, how you begin to see conversations in the flicker of leaf-glow that you used to think was wind, how the idea of standing on something solid becomes quaint. Time passes at different speeds in different tissues; spend a night sleeping against a warm heart-vein and you might wake older or younger by hours or none at all. Directions have names for emotion rather than compass-point: you travel sunward to be born, rootward to die, sidewise to forget. Distance is measured in echoes and breaths.

Sound: 1.9—loud—is a constant symphony of sap pressure and wind harmonics. Every movement you make is answered by a choir of distant vibrations rolling around the shell: your footsteps trigger subsonic pulses that return minutes later as altered chords announcing your presence to unknown listeners. When the great heartwood arteries pulse in their slow hours-long heartbeat, the entire landscape flexes imperceptibly and every crystalized leaf-edge rings like a thousand tiny bells in perfect unison. Silence does not exist; even in the deepest cysts one hears the gentle snoring of the sleeping wood dreaming of sunlight it has never seen.

Dynamic: 2.5—active—means nothing waits. The architecture grows toward you as you approach, sensing warmth and carbon, reaching out microscopic hairs to taste your intent. The weather cycles every few days: a mist exhalation, followed by sudden crystalline snowfall that builds delicate spires overnight, followed by a thawing gust that melts everything into flowing syrup streams that carve new canyons inches deep before freezing again into glass bridges by dusk. Ecosystems the size of cities bloom, exchange all their members in migration storms, and collapse into blight in a month, then rise again from spore memory next season, which might be next year or next hour scale is elastic here.

And still, through all this tumult, there is a hush at the center. Somewhere—directionless yet unmistakable—something vast and slow and patient is breathing with lungs larger than moons. Perhaps it is the whole shell itself, dreaming its own renewal. Perhaps it is here waking, slowly, because you have come walking inside its dream.

Walk softly, traveler. Speak only if you wish to be answered by the world entire. And remember: in this place, you are the strangest part of the landscape, the only thing that does not yet understand how to belong.
