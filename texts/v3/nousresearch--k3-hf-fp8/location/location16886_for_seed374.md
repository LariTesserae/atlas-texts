I step onto a black plain where the ground isn't quite solid. The surface looks like cooled lava, but it gives slightly underfoot, like skin over a bruise. The temperature is feverish, the air thick enough to drink. Sweat gathers instantly at my temples but doesn't evaporate.

The sky hangs low overhead, a dome of iron-dark clouds that never quite rain. Every few minutes, pressure builds until lightning flickers silently within the cloud mass, illuminating nothing. The light stays constant at dusk, dim enough to read shapes but not colors.

The sound is immediate and overwhelming. It comes from everywhere - a continuous bass roar that vibrates my ribs, punctuated by sharp metallic clangs that seem to originate inside my skull. After the first few seconds, I can't tell if it's external or if my bones have begun to ring like struck metal.

Structures rise from the plain as far as I can see. They are not buildings but stacks, irregular towers of found objects fused together: rusted machinery, animal bones, plastic containers melted into new shapes, circuit boards wrapped in hair, fragments of statues with smooth limbs ending in grasping hands. Each stack reaches a height between three and thirty meters, then stops abruptly. The spaces between them form narrow corridors that twist out of sight. No two towers are identical, but all share a pattern of growth from larger at the base to smaller at the top, like mineral deposits left by evaporated water.

Between the stacks, movement. Small shapes - too quick to focus on - dart between shadows. Sometimes one pauses long enough to reveal itself: a creature like a child's drawing of an animal, four-legged but with too many joints, covered in overlapping plates that clack together as it moves. Their eyes reflect the dim light in flat silver disks. They make no sound of their own, their passage swallowed by the roar.

Water is everywhere and nowhere. The air tastes of metal and salt. Moisture beads on every surface but never forms droplets. When I touch a stack, my hand comes away wet, but the stain on my skin shrinks and vanishes within seconds. The creatures drink by pressing their mouths against the structures and staying motionless for minutes at a time.

I walk carefully between the stacks, following corridors that seem to widen ahead of me but narrow to crawlspaces when I reach them. The ground slopes imperceptibly downward. With each step, the air becomes warmer and heavier, the roar deeper. The towers lean closer together, their materials becoming finer: now I see stacks of buttons, bottle caps, sea glass, teeth threaded on wire, tiny bones arranged in geometric patterns, computer chips laid out like scales.

The creatures grow thicker here, moving in streams that flow around me without touching. Some climb the towers, adding tiny objects from pouches at their throats to the growing masses. Others descend, carrying away pieces that flutter or spark in their mouths. The exchange is constant but balanced - no tower grows or shrinks visibly, though closer inspection shows every surface alive with microscopic changes.

Time has stopped here, though I continue to move through it. My watch has stopped at the moment I arrived. The shadows never shift. The lightning never breaks the clouds. My hunger and thirst remain constant, not increasing but never satisfied.

I come to a place where the towers open into a circular space the size of a stadium. In the center stands a single structure unlike the others - perfect in its symmetry, a cone rising to a sharp point hundreds of meters above. It is black and smooth and reflects nothing. The creatures circle it endlessly in a dense unhurried spiral, climbing up one side and down the other, each carrying a single glowing thread in its mouth.

As I watch, I realize each thread is connected to something in the towers around us - a wire flickering with unknown signals, a vein of crystal pulsing with inner light, a strand of something organic that moves like seaweed in still water. All converge here, feeding into or being fed by the black cone. The roar originates here too, vibrating up through the ground and out through the air, shaping every stack and corridor in this endless city.

I understand then that this place is not built but accreted, layer upon layer of the discarded and forgotten, drawn together by sound and warmth and the perfect balance between addition and loss. It has always been forming and will never be complete. I can stay here in the unchanging moment between growth and decay, adding my own discarded fragments to the towers, or I can try to find my way back through the corridors that may not lead where I remember.

Behind me, between one heartbeat and the next, my footprints have already filled themselves in.
