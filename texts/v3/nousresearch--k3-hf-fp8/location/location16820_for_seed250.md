Steam-wracked gneiss ribs jutting through a skin of newborn water—this is all elevation left to speak of. The world has inverted its lungs and breathed tide instead of air. What were mountain chains now hang inverted from curdled cloud-mantles, summits sheathed in glassy droplets that never quite fall downward, never quite rise. Between them, the real continent is an emptiness. A Laugarvallalaug-blue void where curtains of rain collide at ninety-degree angles, flash-boiling into mist, condensing again, drafting upward in slow-motion cyclones until the pressure crushes them into new briny layers laid down across the vacuum like mental sediment.

Temperature hovers just above the place where blood leaps toward ice, yet nothing here freezes—too much motion, too many latent salts behaving as strangers to their own charts. You hear it first: not wind, but water arguing with itself at every scale. Thunderclaps that are merely two opposing surges slamming together and annihilating apart. Microfoams collapsing in fury-trillions scattering crystalline shrapnel that rattles against your faceplate (you do not remember donning one). Ultrasonic slicks slicing deeper—painfully bright sound that cuts down neural channels meant for light.

Vegetation, what little survives, has forsaken chlorophyll. Where calcite seams cross the exposed stone ribs, there are patches of a grey-green lichenoid scaffolding that oxidizes sunlight directly into structure, accreting hollow lattices faster than the downrush can erode them. They look like coral bones trying to pray. They flicker with faint glow-discharge when the local voltage gradient spikes, which happens whenever two planetary scale currents braid past each other offshore (there is always an offshore, even in an ocean that claimed all shores).

Nothing swims. Nothing walks. Nothing flies. Yet the emptiness teems with passage: wakes carved through empty water by entities made only of salinity gradients and turbulence memories. D’Arcy-reeves—shear-currents that have looped long enough to recognize themselves—slowly thread between sunken peaks carrying cargo of dissolved minerals they intend to precipitate into dreams somewhere deeper. Where they intersect, matter condenses without cause, assembling temporary sculptures no sculptor could name: a bridge of zinc lactate spanning two rogue wavefronts, smooth as soap, already sagging under the pull of irony; a cathedral window of calcified thunder echoing hymns that hydrogen bonds hum to themselves in the dark.

The light is ordinary, filtered through ten kilometers of fractal vapor and History’s leftover argon, arriving with all its colors intact but no agreed-upon direction. Shadows point inward, not away. Distance makes things brighter until a few hundred meters out, where the overlapping rain screens reach optical pneumonia and everything blooms into luminous fog. Beyond that, what you face is not blindness but narrative scatter—photons carrying stories that no longer remember which way forward lies.

Time here behaves like a novice juggler learning patience. Clockwise and counter are suggestions. Entropy still counts its beans somewhere but has subcontracted collection to local subcontractors whose methods are… gestural. A cliff-face un-erodes itself in the blink of a storm; an hour later you find a boulder beside you that remembers you from fifty million years hence, worn smooth by conversations you have not yet failed to have.

The ground—if you insist on the old word—remains very polite about being absent. If you step forward nothing arrests you except the gradual realization that you are the trespasser, the solid exception, the odd remainder that refuses to dissolve politely into the participatory soup. The water accepts this rebuff with neutral grace and keeps whispering equations for pressure-forgiveness just behind your cochlear backs.

Structures? None constructed. Yet every wandering isometric point of high salinity dreams nightly of becoming one. Ghost blueprints drift through aqueous twilight like jellyfish schematics: geometries that link no surfaces, binding instead the convictions between volumes. Encounter one too closely and you’ll carry home the urge to build a sentence with seven impossible rooms inside it, or perhaps the poem that finally explains rain backwards.

There is life here if you recalibrate your pronouns. The ocean is verb conjugating itself into nouns it forgets immediately. The weather is a committee meeting of all potential pasts arguing over which future gets to chair next.

You came looking for landscape. Instead, Landscape is looking *through* you, curious whether you might be useful as its next murmur of horizon.
