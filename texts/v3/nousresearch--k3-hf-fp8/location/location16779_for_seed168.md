At first, there is only the sound of air moving over itself, a faint pressure wave circulating around the absence of everything else. The horizon is a perfect circle. Above it, the sky holds a single static cloud that has never formed and never dissolved, suspended mid-swirl like a photograph of something about to happen but indefinitely postponed.

Beneath this cloud, the land rises in a gradual, mathematically precise cone: a single mountain without foothills, growing out of a base too wide to see all at once. Its slopes are not rock but a dense, fibrous lattice of living structures that resemble vegetation only because nothing else compares. These are not plants. They have no roots, no leaves, no flowers, no seeds. Instead, they are continuous growths of crystalline cellulose and conductive lignin, extending outward in fractal spirals that branch and rebranch without ever breaking contact with one another. Each surface gleams faintly in the warm, evenly distributed light, as though polished by centuries of attention that never came.

Nothing moves here except the air. The air is dry, but not harsh—almost soft, like the interior of a vacuum sealed with silk. It carries no scent of decay or moisture, only the faint metallic sweetness of freshly oxidized minerals. The temperature is perfectly constant, warm enough that clothing feels irrelevant, cool enough that skin never quite forgets it is exposed.

There is no water anywhere. What fluidity exists is locked inside the lattice itself, not flowing but diffused—a trace quantity of bound hydroxide ions substituting for what once might have been liquid. When the lattice vibrates (and it does, imperceptibly, in response to nothing) those ions shift position by fractions of an angstrom, memory of motion without motion itself.

The scale is deceptive. From any single point, the slope appears climbable within a day, but walking changes nothing. The mountain's gradients are self-similar: every ascent reveals another identical expanse above, tilted slightly further into the sky. Distance here does not accumulate; it only repeats, slightly shifted, like a tiling error in space-time itself. The effect is not dizzying but calming, as if the mind has been gently told that effort is optional and progress is imaginary.

Nothing has ever eaten here. Nothing has ever been eaten. There is no soil beneath the lattice, no bedrock below, no core below that. The mountain is hollow, not empty: a shell of structured voids, each chamber a perfect rhombic dodecahedron, walls lined with microscopic pores that open into smaller chambers in infinite regression. If you could shrink and enter one, you would find another identical space waiting, and another beyond that, forever smaller but never quite reaching zero.

No tools have touched this place. No hands have shaped it. Yet it is not natural, not in any earthly sense of the word. The angles are too precise, the distribution of density too uniform, the absence of imperfections too perfect. It feels planned, but by a planner who included no purpose in the plan. An architecture of pure structure, built because it could be, not because it needed to be.

Sometimes (though time is difficult here) one of the lattice branches will suddenly extend by exactly one unit length. This happens without sound, without warning, without cause visible to any outside observer. The new growth appears fully formed, already integrated into the network, already vibrating at the same resonant frequency as the rest. Then nothing else changes. The single static cloud does not notice. The warm light does not flicker. The air continues its slow circular sigh.

If you stand still for long enough—though long enough has no measure here—you might begin to feel a faint rhythm beneath your feet. It is not a heartbeat. It is not a drum. It is the cumulative pulse of billions of simultaneous non-events: bonds forming and breaking at thresholds too small to matter anywhere else, cancelled out so perfectly that their sum is zero but their pattern is not. A machine running at 100% efficiency on tasks of absolute nullity.

This place does not welcome. It does not threaten. It simply extends, quietly, endlessly, while whatever question gave rise to it remains unasked in the background of everything that isn't here.
