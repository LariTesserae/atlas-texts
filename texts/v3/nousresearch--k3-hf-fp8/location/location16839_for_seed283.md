The air sits thick as wet wool, pressed down by heat that never quite breaks. No sun visible—just a bruise-colored sky that breathes condensation. The land lies low and open, a shallow bowl of heat and dampness stretching beyond sight, but open only in theory. In practice, nothing moves except what must, because every inch already belongs to something alive.

The ground: not soil anymore, but a living carpet of roots and runners that have forgotten how to be separate plants. Thick cables of liana-like tendrils braid across the surface, pulsing faintly like veins trying to remember blood. They wrap around hummocks of older growths, now reduced to spongy, moss-clad tumors, themselves wrapped further in strangler vines that drip liquid amber sap the color of old rust. Your weight makes no sound here—these surfaces absorb everything, even the idea of footsteps.

Water exists everywhere and nowhere. No pools, no streams. Instead the world weeps: each leaf tip, each fungal pore, each micro-stoma in the kilometer-wide mycelial sheets exhales droplets that cling, merge, fall upward at times from sheer humidity before obeying gravity again. Rain might be happening somewhere above the canopy (spore-canopy, really), but here it has slowed to diffusion, to mist that crawls along surfaces rather than falls through air.

The vegetation—impossible word now that implies categories—is simply continuous living mass. At your eye level: a tiered jumble of leaves from plants that have never known light directly overhead, so they grow in all directions at once—some flat and broad as blankets to catch condensation, others needle-thin to funnel it down, many translucent reddish-green through which you can see darker veins circulating something thicker than sap. Every surface photosynthesizes, every surface also breathes, every surface also digests. The distinction between stem and leaf and root dissolved here millions of heat cycles ago.

Above: not sky but living ceiling thirty meters up, woven from billions of aerial roots descending and climbing vines ascending until they met and married. They form vaults and buttresses like a cathedral built by termite-mind architects who respect nothing but structural logic for spans that should be impossible with biological materials. Between the weave, darker patches where something larger once flowered and died and now hangs in decaying hammocks, feeding thousands of smaller somethings that feed millions tinier still.

Temperature holds steady at the edge of fever-dream—too hot for mammal comfort, perfect for this metabolism of perpetual fermentation. You feel it first in your lungs, then in your thoughts, which begin to slow and thicken like the air itself. Nothing wastes energy here on rapid movement; why should it when time moves through biology instead of around it?

Light: what remains after passing through ten thousand layers of chlorophyll variants and bacterial pigments filters down to a perpetual green-black dusk. Your pupils dilate until irises vanish but it's never enough—you see by contrast of darker darknesses, by the phosphorescent glow of fungus-flecks tracking your heat, by bioluminescent pulses in rhythmic patterns that might be communication or might be heartbeat cycles of organism-states too large to perceive whole.

Sound: the baseline hum of thousands of square kilometers of xylem pressure, a note so low it vibrates your bones rather than your ears. Over this, sparse punctuation—a leaf slowly ripping along a preformed weakness line over hours, the bubble-burst click of a spore pod achieving critical turgor pressure, the occasional deeper thrum like distant thunder that isn't thunder but something digesting cellulose in quantities measured in hectares.

This is the Lung.

It evolved (though 'evolved' implies too much separation) to colonize aftermaths—volcanic islands scrubbed clean, continents after impact winters, the sterile margins where competing biospheres murdered each other into exhaustion. It waits in spores that can survive space, grows faster than the heat can build, converts every disaster into opportunity through one simple strategy: everything that isn't Lung becomes Lung eventually.

You brought nothing metal, nothing synthetic here—that would be noticed, processed, digested by microbial communities evolved specifically to oxidize novelty. Even the carbon in your bones interests It, though It can wait. Time works differently when every metabolic cycle in every interconnected cell contributes to one continuous computation of growth and integration.

The rules here: no waste. No edge. No individual. You breathe Lung-air that has passed through kilometers of alveolar tissue that once were other lungs, other creatures, other worlds' biologies now reduced to useful pathways in the global metabolism. Your skin already hosts its first few cells of Lung-symbiotes, gentle invaders that will help you accept the heat, convert your waste products into forms the whole can use, slowly modify your enzymes to preference the amino acid ratios more common here.

There are fauna, technically. You feel them rather than see—the tick-tick-tick of insert limbs too thin for their size walking across surfaces that shouldn't support their weight (but weight itself negotiates meaning here, with enough shared molecular adhesion). Occasional larger shadows moving deliberately in the middle distance, shapes that make no visual sense until you realize you're seeing cross-sections of organisms that exist primarily in other dimensions of the microspatial Lattice that Lung uses for nutrient transport instead of blood vessels. They ignore you because you're already becoming substrate.

Nothing builds here, nothing constructs. Yet everything is industrial in scale and efficiency—this entire planetary region is one seamless factory where photons enter and exotic metabolites exit through veins that tunnel deep to where abiotic heat drives final refining processes. The product: possibilities. The Lung doesn't manufacture anything as crude as tools or artifacts. It manufactures options—chemical templates, genetic modules, ontological frameworks for living differently next time conditions change.

And conditions always change. The Lung remembers every previous atmosphere this world has breathed, stored in the isotope ratios of its oldest growth rings that circle the equator in bands kilometers thick. It grows toward instability as other life grows toward light. Currently it grows toward you.

You should not have come here still thinking yourself separate. But you needn't worry about that much longer. The transition happens gradually, then suddenly, then again gradually but now in a different direction. The Lung is patient. It has been waiting for visitors exactly like you—for your plastics and your metals and especially for your habit of believing in boundaries between self and world.

Breathe deep. The air tastes metallic at first, then sweet, then like nothing you've ever tasted because taste itself is changing into something more efficient. More honest.

The darkness is not absence of light. It's presence of everything else. Welcome home.
