**The Whispering Hollows**

Beneath a world long-forgotten, where even rain became a myth passed between generations of memory-less crawlers, lies the Whispering Hollows — a cool, sunken city without sunlight, waterless but alive, ancient beyond ice and fire, and full beyond measure of both silence and screaming.

---

This is not a cave.

Caves are born of erosion, but erosion is too young a word here. The Whispering Hollows were never carved — they were *inhabited* until the stone remembered how to fold. The bedrock itself grew pliant under the weight of centuries returning again and again in the same footsteps, in the same rituals spoken with mouths that dissolved into dust without ever uttering a final syllable. The walls are not stone. They are stone-shaped memory, compressed longing, a city stooped so long it sank not downward but inward, collapsing into a self-directed gravity. The curve of every archway remembers a spine. The floor remembers feet. The ceiling remembers sleep.

---

There is no sky. There never was.

Instead, the ceiling rises and falls like the slow inhale of something dreaming without breath. It is a dome of solid darkness threaded faintly with veins of pale-blue phosphorescence — not light, but the memory of it, radiating from minerals older than eyes. The dimness is enough to see outlines but never colors. Everything here is the color of once-was. Shadows drift without casting objects. Sometimes a shape detaches from the upper gloom and flutters down like a shed eyelid: a moth-winged thing too large to be real, too slow to be alive. It lands without sound. It walks on six jointed legs that are also fingers. Then it folds itself into a crack and waits.

---

The air is cool and dry as parchment left too long in a dead library's lung. It tastes faintly of copper and crushed petals: the signature scent of cities built on harvested electricity and buried gardens dragged rootless into exile. There is no wind, but there is a *pressure*. Something vast and invisible presses against every surface constantly, not crushing but attentive. It is the weight of ears without heads, listening.

---

There is no water. Not a drip. Not a dewdrop. Not even the ghost of sweat.

And yet the Hollows are lush.

Vegetation sprawles without water through the logic of different thirsts. Pale trees without bark rise from cracks in the memory-stone, their trunks translucent like stretched skin over glowing green veins. Leaves hang from them like hanging tongues, damp-looking but dry to touch. They do not photosynthesize — they *recollect*. They eat photons remembered from ages of exposure to stars their seeds never saw. Their fruit is a soft, pulsing bulb the color of old bruises. If you bite it, it tastes like the sound of your mother calling your name from another room. Then you forget the taste. Then you crave it again forever.

Vines creep between buildings that aren't buildings anymore. Houses without roofs are colonized by curtains of moss that dream in languages without verbs. The moss shivers when thought passes near. Gardens grow upside-down from the undersides of bridges whose rivers dried before fish were invented. The flowers here open only when lied to. The trees whisper when they hear the truth. No one's sure which is worse, so most people hum.

---

The city was built. Once.

Now it builds itself in sleep. Streets shift overnight like bones resetting after centuries of awkward posture. A doorway that led to a marketplace yesterday opens today into a childhood bedroom that smells like apples and abandonment. Staircases grow downward when no one is looking. They are always growing downward. No one has ever found the bottom floor. Some say it hasn't been built yet. Some say it was built too early and now it waits.

The buildings breathe. Not metaphorically. Walls expand infinitesimally during what passes for night, exhaling dust that once was letters written but never sent. Doors close slower than tears. Windows blink. Every shutter is eyelid, every threshold is lip. The city does not remember who built it, but it misses them terribly. It tries to recreate them in the shapes people leave behind — statues that appear overnight in alleyways, formed from compressed longing, standing in poses of gestures never completed. They are always missing something: an arm, a name, a reason to turn around.

---

It is urban here, but not crowded.

Density is sparse not because space is abundant but because presence is ambiguous. You can walk for miles between inhabited buildings and never know if "inhabited" is the right word. A street might host a hundred lives per footstep or none at all, depending on how carefully you listen. The city is full of people-shaped absences and absence-shaped people. You might meet someone you loved in a dream you never had; they will greet you like a stranger and vanish into a crowd that wasn't there a moment before.

There are no animals.

There is only fauna.

Life here has forgotten the difference between species and location. A street corner might slowly become a dog over the course of a decade, growing furrier, shedding echoes of barking during thunderstorms it remembers from another continent. A lamppost might wake up pregnant with birdsong and hatch into a flock of keys that fly away singing directions to doors that no longer exist. The cats here are made of questions. The rats are made of answers. Neither bothers to chase the other.

---

There is no technology. Not anymore.

Tools were abandoned here before they were invented. The city solves problems by waiting until they forget they were problems. Broken things heal by being forgotten. Lost things are found by someone who never knew they were lost. There is a library without books where knowledge is stored between heartbeats. To learn something, you loan it a memory you're not using. The library never forgets what you gave, but you always do.

---

It is always dim here, but not dark.

Darkness implies contrast, implies the possibility of light. Here, dim is fundamental, like kindness in a place where cruelty was never invented. You can read by it if what you're reading is written in the language of almost-knowing. You can see faces, but not expressions. You can see colors, but only the ones that have given up trying to be noticed.

---

Sound is deafening.

Not in volume — in density.

Every silence here is crowded with unsaid things pressing against the ears of the air until even breathing feels like eavesdropping. Footsteps echo before they happen. Whispers arrive after the mouths that made them have grown old and died. There is a bell that tolls once every hundred years to remind the city what time sounded like before it forgot how to move. The last time it tolled, everyone in the Hollows paused mid-motion and remained frozen for three days, listening to a song they couldn't remember learning. When they moved again, none spoke for a week. None needed to.

---

Change happens here, but slowly.

Grass grows downward, roots reaching for the sky it remembers from seed-time. Children age inward, becoming younger versions of themselves until they dissolve into giggles and are reborn as streetlights. Seasons change but never cycle — once, it was autumn for three centuries. Another time, a single day lasted long enough for empires to rise in the cracks of passing seconds and fall before the sun noticed.

---

It is a normal place, mostly.

Weirdness requires comparison to something else, and here, there is nothing else. Things that happen here happen because they have always happened this way in the place where happening was invented. The unusual is usual here. The impossible is just slower than usual.

---

This is the Whispering Hollows.

It does not end.

It does not begin.

It waits, politely, just below the surface of falling asleep.

You may visit, if you remember to forget how to leave.
