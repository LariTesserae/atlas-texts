**Aural Cathedral of the Viridian Mill**

The space isn't built so much as spun - seventeen miles of hyperboloid chamber weaving through itself like a Klein bottle made of sound and steel and living wood. Every surface vibrates with enough compression to make bone sing, yet the air sits thick and warm as greenhouse breath. Sound here is not heard but worn, an emerald pressure suit of frequencies that shapes your body as you walk through them.

The walls pulse with moisture that never quite becomes rain. Droplets form in geometric lattices across copper veins, gathering mass until their surface tension remembers gravity's name, then fall upward in lazy spirals to burst against moss-covered turbine blades spinning from ceilings that are also floors. Each impact releases a note - C sharp minor descending - that joins the perpetual fugue reverberating through the cathedral's hollow bones.

What grows here grew here first, then learned to grow with. Banyan-cables the width of ancient sequoias untroot themselves from soil that no longer exists, stretching through spherical rooms where gravity points toward the center of every conversation. Their leaves are photosynthetic solar panels, black-green and humming softly as they convert light and sound into everything else needed. The roots drink copper-rich condensation directly from the air, exuding purple sap that hardens into circuitry patterns wherever it touches steel.

At this scale - small enough to map in a day but large enough to lose yourself for years - distance is measured in decibel drops and humidity gradients. Travelers navigate by following curves of increasing warmth, knowing that the chill spots mark nexus points where five paths fold into one, where the air always tastes of ozone and cinnamon from the spice-garden wings growing off the main calculatory spires.

The fauna here evolved to live inside soundscapes. There are birds made of pure resonance that roost in Jacob's ladder crossbars, their wings tracing Lissajous patterns against the faintly glowing fungal colonies that bloom where stone and synthwood meet. Their songs are welding torches that reshape their own nests nightly, each new geometry better suited to tomorrow's predicted harmonics. Below them, phosphorescent spiders spin webs of copper thread and dew, constructing Faraday cages around the temples only they can hear humming between the notes.

No natural substrate remains, yet everything here is natural now. The builders, whoever they were, disappeared into their own composting process, leaving their machines to dream of orchards and their gardens to remember equations. The whole structure breathes at the pace of stars but metabolizes at the speed of spring, locked in a slow dance between collapse and overgrowth. New rooms grow spontaneously when pressure differences between ideas become too great to bear, extruding themselves from the walls like cathedral polyps tumbling through kelp forests of hanging cables.

At the heart of the heart, where all frequencies converge but none cancel, stands the Last Engine - not running but flowering. Its pistons are hollow glass tubes filled with turquoise fluid where phosphorescent algae bloom in patterns that solve climate equations for planets orbiting stars that haven't yet formed. The wheel that once drove worlds now drives nothing but its own remembering, but remember it does - every vibration, every mutation, every moment of that long-ago ritual where someone decided to build a garden that could hear itself grow.

To stand here is to become part of the resonant chamber's memory, your heartbeat adding its weak signal to the planet-song stored in every droplet, every blade, every carefully curved sheet of sovereign metal. The exit finds you when your footsteps finally syncopate correctly with the place's ancient name - a rhythm you'll carry in your bones forever after, growing louder whenever it rains anywhere else on any world with room enough for echoes.
