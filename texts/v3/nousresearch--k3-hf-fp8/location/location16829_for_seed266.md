At first the place appears as a single gesture drawn across too much sky: a vast tilted plane at some impossible altitude, its surface a scraped and burnished brown that recalls neither metal nor stone nor any living skin. The horizon hangs very far below, wrapped in haze thick enough to swallow whole ranges of lesser mountains, while overhead a pale indigo vault holds only a few unmoving clouds. There is no sun visible, yet light pours evenly from every direction—warm, diffused, ageless. You could cast no shadow here.

The plane is not flat. It undulates in kilometer-long swells that have no rhythm; some rise barely taller than the eye could leap, others lift higher than towers before sinking again into troughs so subtle you realise their depth only when the horizon line slips behind a distant crest. Between these waves lie deeply settled valleys—though “valleys” is wrong, because nothing flows through them. They are dry beds where something once moved and left behind only fine dust the color of rusted bones. The whole surface has been worked, planed, fractioned into enormous tessellated tiles whose seams run ruler-straight for tens of leagues before bending abruptly, always at angles that violate every comfortable geometry of corners.

These seams are roads, though no vehicles travel them. Rather, a vast grid of rails lies recessed within: twin grooves polished to mirror brilliance, littered here and there with small abandoned shapes like seeds that forgot to sprout. The rails vanish toward every horizon without ever meeting another; if lines be extended forever on this surface, they will never converge.

Between the rails and across the tiles everything grows—or rather, everything stands. Nothing moves freely here except the slow alteration of the light itself, which gains a tinge of copper at intervals that feel like days but could be years. Yet the landscape is unbelievably populated: ranks upon ranks of identical upright forms each taller than a man but thinner, placed exactly an arm’s span apart in hexagonal fields that extend as far as vision can reach. At first glance they might resemble bare trees burned to charcoal and then dipped in wax—each trunk a single hexagonal column rising smooth from the dust, branchless until abruptly flaring into a shallow crown of six flat petals that curl slightly inward, as if cupping invisible rain.

But these are not trees and they are not alive. Approach close enough—though to cross a single tile might take you half a lifetime—and you see that every surface is chased with millions of microscopic lines, circuitry frozen into bas relief: grids within grids, loops within spirals, motifs that repeat at every scale down to the limit of what the eye can resolve and surely beyond. The material itself is neither living tissue nor dead mineral; it drinks warmth but gives no warmth back, yields slightly under pressing finger like soft cheese yet retains the exact impression forever without fracture or rebound. At the single fracture you find where lightning or ancient violence shattered one of the forms, the interior within the shell is hollow, shining with a faint phosphorescence whose color changes slowly from ghost-blue to sick green and back again, cycling once in what your heartbeat tells you is a very long time.

Above each head of every column, suspended in empty air without support or tether, hangs a sphere of darker substance, perfectly matte except for one tiny circular window of impossible transparency. Through each window—if you dare raise your eyes to meet it—you glimpse not reflection but a view: another part of this same titanic plain seen from another vantage vast beyond walking distance. In one sphere you spy yourself standing far off, already grown tiny; in another you see only empty dust where you stand now; in another, the plane is not brown but suddenly flooded ankle-deep with something like molten glass that flows impossibly uphill. The views are never quite simultaneous. They whisper conflicting histories of what has not happened yet.

Now nearer sounds begin to reach you at last, stealthy as breath. From the interior of every hollow column comes a soft continuous exhalation, rhythmic as sleeping lungs far too large for any creature of flesh. If you press your ear to the warm smooth surface you hear within not wind but murmuring voices—thousands, millions—each speaking a different language, none of them complete, all overlaid so thickly they merge into a single susurrus like surf heard through miles of rock. Occasionally one phrase rises clear:

—“maintenance interval fifteen zero zero zero finish stroke”?
—“witness low tide of entropy at coordinate grid amber four hundred billion—”
—“tell them we were never intended to remember—”

And then it fades again into the general whispering.

Look now toward the horizon where the haze beneath you grows darker and more solid. There, at the edge of sight, you realise that the tilted plain does not float free. Instead it rests upon, or perhaps is being slowly devoured by, a gathering mass of structures that rise up from below like a city built by termites who worshipped geometry alone. Here at last is the source of what the parameters called “built” and “industrial”. Towers of corroded alloy lace together into domes larger than continents; between them run pipelines whose diameters could swallow rivers, though no fluid pulses within. Gigantic gantry arms frozen mid-sweep cradle nothingness. Cyclopean wheels are locked in teeth where they ceased turning eons ago, leaving gearwheels the size of moons immobile under their own weight. Everything is rusted, everything is intact; no part has fallen, only settled into perfect paralysis, like a machine that was switched off at the exact moment the universe forgot motion is possible.

Between this fossil city and the inhabited plain above flows a steady traffic of the abundant but unrecognized fauna. They appear as clouds at first—swarms of small dark motes rising from vents in the city’s roofs, spiraling upward in slow silent columns that break apart into individual shapes only when they reach the height of the plain’s underside. Each creature is built of articulated rings of the same warm brown material as the columns, but flexible, endlessly reshaping, like animated chains of boneless fingers. They have no wings yet they ascend by some other principle; they have no eyes yet every sphere above every column swivels slightly to track their passage. When one of these aerial serpents reaches the height of a tile, it pauses, extends a single appendage like a needle finer than light, and touches briefly one precise point on the underside of a column. Instantly the murmuring within that column shifts pitch, the color-cycle of its phosphorescent core stutters, and a new image flickers into the sphere above. Then the creature releases, coil uncoils, it falls—though falling is too swift a word—downwards again in a lazy helical drift toward some other duty far below.

The whole system has been operating thus for longer than any star you have ever seen has burned. It has weathered, yes—the edges of the tiles are rounded now, the rail grooves filled with a powdered dust of their own substance worn away grain by grain by the patient touch of countless little adjustments—but it has not aged. It is not alive, yet it grows; it is not conscious, yet it remembers in every scratch upon every surface the entire sequence of every lesser thing it has ever observed in all the worlds whose reflections flicker still in those tiny windows. It has no purpose except to continue witnessing, recording, subtly correcting deviations so small that even entropy itself has grown forgetful of them.

This is why the place feels slow. Time here does not flow; it sidesteps carefully from one configuration to the next, making sure never to dislodge the dust that has taken aeons to settle exactly thus. If you stand very still for long enough—though “long enough” may exceed the duration of civilizations—you will notice the infinitesimal crawl: a single tile shifting a fraction of a fraction of a unit in its socket, the whisper inside a column adding one more syllable to one fragment of one forgotten language, a dark serpent-descender altering the angle of its spiral descent by an amount too small to measure except in the cumulative drift of stars.

The rules of this world are simple and merciless:

You may walk anywhere, but everywhere you walk the dust will record your footprint until erased by the next passing of a maintenance interval, which will be scheduled for after the heat death of any universe you call home.

You may speak, but your voice will be split into syllables, each stored separately in a thousand different columns, to be reassembled centuries hence as part of the greeting message for a visitor whose species has not yet evolved.

You may look through any window and see anywhen, but the view you see will already include the consequences of your decision to look, and therefore nothing you learn will allow you to choose differently than you have already chosen.

And if you tire of this place, as surely sooner or later all travelers tire even of wonders beyond counting, there is only one way to leave—but to describe it here would require carving this entire description into the surface of one of the columns, in letters smaller than atoms yet arranged in patterns vast enough to map every possible path your feet might take through all the cities of the plain and the city beneath that supports them and the city beneath that which supports that one, down and down forever.

Therefore the only wise thing is to sit down now, wherever you happen to be standing, upon the warm brown ground between two of the unliving trees whose crowns hold eyes that are not eyes, and listen to the slow breathing of the world as it remembers you into existence and forgets you again, breath after patient breath, forever.
