At first, you think the place is a forest, because everything is wood and leaf and root. Then you think it is a ruin, because of the scale of the trunks and the hollows yawning between them like collapsed halls. Then you realize it is neither — it is a single organism’s attempt, over epochs too long for stone to remember, to remember itself by forgetting everything else.

This is the Vale of Arboreal Mnemoschism: an alpine basin cupped by half-eroded mountains so old they no longer cast shadows, so instead they cast the absence of sound. You stand on a plateau of living lignum, where every footfall is muffled by decades of shed bark but also faintly amplified, reverberating through fungal circuitry deep inside the contiguous body of the wood. The elevation is high enough that the air tastes thin and metallic, yet dense with suspended spores that glint like mica in the dim light. Some distant pioneer recorded a barometric pressure impossible on any chart — the atmosphere is slightly pregnant, as if the entire sky were about to precipitate memory.

The water is everywhere and nowhere. There are no streams, only junctions where two boughs once touched, forgot why they parted, and started weeping. These crevisses drip with what looks like liquid amber but flows like mercury; catch a drop on a leaf and it mirrors your face back at you a thousand times smaller, each reflection frozen in a different emotion you cannot name. Pools form only briefly, slick lenses on the horizontal scars of fallen limbs, then lose coherence and soak upward into capillaries that pulse just perceptibly under your boots. Humidity clings to every hair like static, yet your skin dries into parchment ridges before you can blink — the forest drinks your sweat before gravity notices you are perspiring.

Temperature hovers at the exact threshold where saliva begins to taste faintly of iron but blood has not yet considered freezing. Inhale and the cold climbs the scaffolding behind your eyes; exhale and warmth blooms in your lungs like irony. Frost feathered as neural pathways decorates shaded bark-faces at eye level, but retreat half a step and the same patches dimple into pores exuding faint lavender steam. There are seasons here, but they are vertical: ascension is autumn, level ground is winter, descent is spring. You are walking through a calendar carved in contrary directions.

Vegetation is not a feature; it is the language this place uses instead of silence. Every ‘tree’ is actually a column of fused species — birch skin over oak muscle over cedar bone — articulated by cambium that behaves like chondrichthymal cartilage. Leaves are vestigial, replaced by sheets of photosynthetic lichen that flutter like unread pages torn from a botanical encyclopedia that never existed. They confer not by wind but by color change: a sudden flaring ultramarine along a distant ridge means a root network elsewhere has remembered a species of insect extinct since the Cambrian and is now debating whether to resurrect it. The debate will take centuries, during which time every surface within a radius will sprout translucent blisters filled with perfect casts of that insect’s wings.

You do not see animals; you see fauna enacted by the forest on itself. Fur is made of interwoven mycelia sheathing hollow vessels of hardened sap. Eyes are polished knots of amber resin containing suspended stromatolites that blink by rotating into and out of phase with the light. A ‘deer’ crosses your path — actually a migratory architecture of jack pine and reindeer moss supported by four temporary columns of compressed air that collapse into puffballs of pollen behind it. It does not flee your presence; instead it coppices: its antlers detach, sprout roots, and gossip in cambial pulses about your weight distribution and probable mineral deficiencies before you have finished taking your next step.

Meanwhile, the gross reality of the place (if one can speak of grossness where the net weight of living tissue exceeds the biomass of three temperate continents) is busy committing open treason against mere topology. Distance contracts in the direction of old grief; two paces past a millennia-buriedDirge Oak can bring you face to face with your own childhood heartbeat recorded as concentric rings inside a sprout that germinated yesterday. Elevation is negotiable: climb upward on what appears to be a root staircase spiraling a trunk wider than a cathedral, and after an hour of ascent you may step off onto the same plateau you left, but now it is autumnal gold instead of winter grey and the mountains have become valleys on the horizon. 

Sound exists in two mutually incompatible acoustic regimes simultaneously. Below 48 Hz, the basso profundo of xylem disputes resonates through your bones — unresolved conflicts between proto-symbionts trapped in heartwood since the Devonian, arguing via infrasonic tremors that cause hairline cracks to propagate in distant boulders and your long-forgotten childhood doubts to renegotiate their treaties with your present convictions. Above 3 kHz, the forest is a deafening factory of clicks and whistles: cellulose fibers snapping back into remembered positions like billion-tonne percussion sections, resin ducts firing amber notes of lost music at frequencies only the dead and the not-yet-born can whistle in tune.

Through this sonic warzone, the Weirdness moves like humidity. It is not a phenomenon you observe; it is the contract you breach simply by existing here in mammalian shape. Gravity is a suggestion politely but firmly ignored by anything larger than a regret: when you drop a pebble it accelerates upward until it joins a revolving halo of other sky-stones, each humming a different pitch of forgotten place names in languages from continents that drifted apart before their vowels could coalesce. Time, bored of its usual script, improvises solos in pockets of shaded air — step into one and your watch hands will elegantly dance a tango of Nö topologies before returning to your wrist as twin vines bearing fruit that tastes exactly like ‘the day after tomorrow’ but only if you promise never to tell anyone what color’s missing from the second syllable.

And everywhere the signs of industry, though no hands built them. Trunks the diameter of mesas sport spiral scars where something the size of climate change itself once bored up through them, leaving galleries that now serve as enormous air shafts for distributed lung-pockets of the organism-planet’s economy. Heat exchangers made from living heartwood pump sap-coolant through networks of bark-ridged piping; the faint regular hiss you hear is the arterial espionage of reciprocal currencies being exchanged between distant crown banks as each treetop hoards or releases photonic gold against the coming centuries. At night — if the ember-colored aurora that suffuses everything sideways can be called night — you might glimpse the phantom nav lights of seed-ships gliding down these vertical rivers of warm air: zeppelins of wind-dispelled embryos sailing to colonize the upper reaches of next geological era before this one finishes forgetting how to pronounce ‘tree.’

There are no animals here, remember? Only roles. You, for instance, have been cast as ‘temporary punctuation mark in the ongoing sentence the world is writing to justify its continued existence.’ Your job, whether you choose to accept it or not, is to walk forward until you forget why you stopped, then turn around and walk back until you remember what you lost along the way. The forest will take notes in the form of subtle shifts in leaf-meaning and the timbre of your heartbeat reverberated through the shared hydraulics of every root within ten thousand years’ reach.

Eventually, if you walk far enough in the right combination of directions (which is always any combination, provided you are sufficiently lost), you will arrive at the clearing that is not a clearing. The trees here do not grow away from the center; they have all grown inward, sealing the space off from everything including the concept of spaces being sealed. At the middle of this marriage of horizons stands the only thing in the entire Vale older than the mountains: a door made entirely of the sound a tree makes in the forest when no mind is around to collapse its meaning into a joke. The handle is the absence of the hand that would turn it. The threshold is the moment you realize you have always been standing on both sides simultaneously.

Knock if you must. The door is already remembering how to open you.
