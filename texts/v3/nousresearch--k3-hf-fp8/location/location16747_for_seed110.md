A cavity within the mountain's heart, too small to be a cavern yet too vast for a mere pocket—perhaps it is the stone's own blister, raised and then forgotten. Darkness here is not absence but substance, thick and velvet-dark, pressing against skin and sight alike until the very idea of light becomes a memory lost to childhood.

Water moves everywhere yet nowhere. It neither drips nor flows but resides: beaded upon every quartz fleck like dew unwilling to fall, clustering in hairline seams where granite still remembers its molten youth, suspended as fog so fine it tastes of iron on the tongue. Touch any surface and your fingertips come away wet, but search for source or sink and find only more of itself, endlessly reflected in darkness. The air itself drinks you dry while saturating your lungs.

Something lives here, though lived seems too active a word. Lichens perhaps, or what lichens dream of becoming when they've forgotten the sun: colonies of slow silver spreading across the rockface in patterns that suggest writing without meaning, geometry without solution. Each cluster no larger than a thumbnail yet together they map every contour, every whisper of wind-cut stone under this touch that is no touch.

At the cavity's center—though center implies edges, and edges are uncertain here—a single shape rises. Built or grown or both in sequence lost to time-poor memory: a tower of stacked disks, each disk sliced from some impossible crystalline bone, slightly too large to have fit through any opening yet undeniably present. Twenty disks, maybe thirty, each rotating at speeds that vary with the observer's breath, grinding against its neighbors with a sound too high for hearing but perfect for the ache behind your eyes.

Around this silent spindle, the air pockets and pools. Warm currents rise and fall without stirring the fog. Here is a place where a torch would drown before igniting, where a shout would turn to necklace-pearls before traveling three fingers' breadth, where a lifetime might pass entire without measurable change save the slow accumulation of moisture on waiting stone.

Occasionally—not rhythmically, for rhythm requires count and count requires change—something shifts. A disk aligns too perfectly with its shadow-counterpart and for an eyeblink-long eternity the darkness between them achieves perfect congruence. In these moments, if you are watching (which you cannot be, being blind in this place), if you are ready (which you cannot be, for readiness implies anticipation), you might glimpse:

The mountain's birth, still happening, always happening, in the space between heartbeats.

But then the alignment passes, the moment spills into itself and is gone, and again there is only the warm wet dark and the tower turning silence into architecture and the slow silver colonies remembering something they never knew.

And nothing ever changes here.

Nothing ever has.
