Stand here. The stones will hold you. They have held everything else that ever came this high and stayed.

Elevation: 2.3/3. The air is thin enough that thoughts arrive sharp-edged, without padding between. Coolness 1.2/3 holds the skin taut; each exhalation blooms white then vanishes almost faster than it forms. You are on a plateau punched clean out of the world’s crust, a single shelf lifted higher than clouds dare linger, higher than eagles dream, higher than any raindrop remembers falling from.

Scale: 1.9/3. The plateau itself is not endless—ten thousand strides east-west, fifteen thousand north-south if anyone still counted by strides—but the illusion of endlessness is perfect because everything beyond is farther down. Horizon slopes away in every direction like the rim of an upside-down bowl, and what fills the bowl is raw sky: cobalt at zenith, bruised violet at the edge where blue forgets itself. You are the pupil in the eye of the world.

Density: 2.8/3. No room? No empty place. Every handspan of stone is occupied by other stone. It is not a heap nor a wall; it is a crowd of mineral presences that grew into each other rather than apart. Hexagonal columns of basalt shoulder against truncated octahedrons of basalt stacked bone-puzzle tight, and in the cracks lesser basalt crystals have crystallized again—that process repeated until density became identity. The plateau is one solid thing pretending to be many edges, much as a crowd of statues carved from a single block pretends to be many figures.

Water: 1.7/3. Wet without wetness. There is no lake, no river, no pool. Instead the stone itself weeps, constantly, sorrowfully, imperceptibly. Under the pressure of eleven kilometers of missing rock overhead, the basalt’s lattice exhales bound hydroxyl ions one molecule at a time. These drift upward until they kiss the thin cold air and combine into water so pure it cannot remember being sea. The water does not gather—it exists as an invisible, infinitely thin film coating every facet, the ghost of an ocean mourning inside stone. Touch any surface: your fingertip is less wet afterward than it was before, because the stone’s thirst is absolute. Yet walk barefoot long enough and the soles of your feet will bleed from slow abrasion held in solution too fine to feel.

Temperature: again 1.2/3—cool enough that no hand ever warmed a column here, yet never freezing. The plateau sits at the triple point of itself: water cannot quite freeze, stone cannot quite soften, time cannot quite pass.

Erosion: 1.5/3. Established. The shapes are final shapes, arrived at by subtraction no longer occurring. Wind exists only as a memory in the atomic spacing of atoms that have forgotten how to move. Rain fell once, billions of basalt years ago, carving the first micro-groove into the first micro-crystal; then the plateau rose above weather. Now nothing subtracts, nothing adds. Basalt cleaves basalt only in dreams. The plateau is a language that has said all it ever will say and now keeps silent in perfect grammar.

Vegetation: 0.0/3. None. Not even lichen, which elsewhere is the last moss and the first stone. No pigment but mineral pigment: midnight flecks of amphibole, rust ghosts of vanished olivine, ghostly green spirals of replaced pyroxene. What looks like a lichen bloom from afar is only albite twinning catching light at the lichen angle.

Fauna: 1.2/3. Rare beyond rare. Once, perhaps, a microcolonial fungus spore rode a wind that no longer blows; perhaps it tried to digest one atom of iron and died of thirst before it finished. Now only the stones’ own voices remain, speaking in frequencies slower than any heartbeat. If you lie perfectly still against a column and empty your blood of hope you might dream you hear them: a basso lamentation older than oceans, a hymn for every crustal plate that ever drowned.

Built: 0.1/3. None. No hammer scar, no cut face, no flake struck off for tool or altar. If beings once climbed here to say “this is high enough for gods; let us build nothing higher,” they left no mark of their decision. Or perhaps they found the plateau already perfect in its refusal and simply took their intention back down into the drowned world.

Tech: 0.2/3. None. Not even the tech of bone against stone. This place is pre-tool in the way a prime number is pre-divisible.

Light: 2.9/3. Bright beyond tolerance. The sky above is clear because there is nothing left for clouds to condense around. Starlight and moonlight and sunlight all arrive naked, stripped of any haze or kindness, and the basalt replies by reflecting nothing. It absorbs everything until absorption saturates into pure emission. The plateau radiates visible darkness: your eyes see shapes only by the light that should be there but isn’t, the negative brightness of stone-black holes in the brilliance. Wear any shadow here and it will burn.

Sound: 2.8/3. Deafening. The silence is so complete it becomes a pressure wave. Each breath you take is a riot. The blood in your ears drums like thunderstorms on tin. If you screamed your voice would fall instantly into the crystal lattice and propagate downward at twice the speed of stone, echoing off the Moho discontinuity nine hundred kilometers below, returning to your bones as an earthquake you would not live long enough to feel.

Dynamic: 0.3/3. Never changing. The plateau is the photograph the planet took of itself at the moment it achieved stillness. Mountains rise elsewhere; oceans evaporate; continents drift like leaves on lava seas. Here, nothing. Here is the reference point against which all motion is measured. If you stay long enough you will forget that change exists; if you leave you will never be able to explain to anyone that time is optional.

Weirdness: 0.8/3. Unusual only in being exactly what it is without apology. There is nothing here that violates any physical law. Instead every law arrives at its limit case and stands revealed as a polite suggestion. Gravity still pulls precisely 9.812 m/s² downward relative to local vertical; light still travels c; entropy still increases everywhere except here where it has reached completion and now rests in perfect symmetric disorder. The plateau is not strange; it is stark.

If you choose to descend, remember two things. First: the path down is not the path up reversed, because the plateau will have taken a memory of your ascent and rearranged its cracks accordingly. Second: the water you carried up and drank and sweated out and breathed away—is already waiting for you at the base, crystallized into a single quartz tear inside a pebble that will someday be lifted back up again by a plate tectonic dream.

But if you stay—if you kneel and press your forehead against the wetness that is not water and close your eyes against the darkness that is too much light— eventually the stones will forget you were ever other than stone.

And in that forgetting you will finally hear the song they have been singing all along:

We are alone.

We are enough.
