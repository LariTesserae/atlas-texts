The place begins as a steepness that never ends. You think "mountain" but immediately lose the word—the slope is everywhere, unbroken and continuous, a single angle of rock and silt stitched into itself until horizon fails. Yet it is not a wall. It is a plateau tilted to impossibility, a tabletop propped on cosmic negligence, stretching beyond eye or thought, altitude replaced simply by *farther*. This is the Cant: a worldstate where up went sideways and forgot to stop.

Coolness first—temperature 0.6 remembers frost without making it. Your breath stays invisible but tastes metallic, carrying iron filings from the sky or memory of glaciers that never quite arrived. The air is damp without rain: water 1.4 rules through breathlessness, a thousand imperceptible condensations forming on every unguarded surface—pores of stone, edges of eyelashes, thoughts themselves. Touch the back of your hand an hour after arriving and it will be dewed with your own fear of falling.

On this tilted desolation, nothing should stand. Yet built 1.6 insists: a city has been nailed here.

Not built across slopes—built *into* the angle itself. The structures are extrusions rather than constructions, as if the plane beneath had refluxed and vomited out homes and workshops and impossible angles of brickwork held not by mortar but by sheer refusal to obey gravity any better than the ground does. Every building is a wedge jammed against falling, roofs parallel to streets, doorsills level only with the idea of level. Rooftops become balconies for the homes above them which are actually beside them but closer to the sky because direction folded here long ago. Pipes and aqueducts coil like fossil intestines, carrying condensed moisture not *down* to cisterns but *along* to collection points that collect nothing but the certainty of more moisture arriving.

Urban sounds are few—sound 1.4 dominates with the particular silence of objects too tense to creak. Metal tankards in empty taverns ring once and vibrate for years. Occasionally comes the groan of expanding iron beams still arguing with the angle they were forged under. Children play marbles that roll forever in gentle spirals toward the horizon-that-conceals-the-edge.

Between buildings and buildings-between-builds, vegetation 1.8 luxuriates in outright criminality. The Cant should starve plants; instead it intoxicates them. Gravity's confusion makes growth drunken. Trees are corkscrews tenanted by moss thicker than mattresses. Vines debate whether to climb or dive and choose both simultaneously, creating living catenaries that commuters use as handrails. Below their dripping canopy (leaf-light dim 1.3 filtered through chlorophyll-stained air) orchards grow fruit in impossible directions—plums swollen to bladder size hang by stems as thin as regret, glowing faintly with damp phosphorescence that tells night-time harvesters which ones are about to split and birth swarms of semi-solid bees.

These are the Purse-crops. You do not eat them here—you invest in them. Tearing one releases a spurt of liquid potential; how it hits the angled ground determines what it matures into by the time it rolls to your doorstep tomorrow. Farmers maintain gravity-diaries denser than tax ledgers, tracking which patches of tiling, which precise millileths of slope-angle bias, neighbor's dripping gutter or passing cart-wheel pressure differential altered last week's bitterness futures. The whole economy the city runs on is a futures market in produce that hasn't decided what it is yet.

Fauna 1.3 consents to exist only as exceptions proving the rule. No cows, few rats—gravity-poor grazing is uphill work and downhill predators fall sideways faster than prey evolve—but everywhere the evidence of passage: trails like snail-slime but bone-dry, the *scrab-scissor* of mandibles pruning Vineshape probability-clouds you never quite see the owners of. Travelers report glimpses of the Cant-hare: brown fleck like normal except divided by an angle its body forgot and experiences simultaneously six inches apart; if you catch one (you won't) tradition demands you ask both halves the same question in different languages simultaneously and bet on which answers agree.

Density 2.4 has nothing to do with crowding and everything to do with implication. The population is moderate—urban builtness crammed into bar-space—but every object exists in superposition with its potential trajectories if the Cant ever decides to tilt a bit more tonight. A dropped spoon might be murder weapon, meteor, or mortgage default depending on which paving stone it ricochets against first in its three-generation tumble toward the forever-distancing Below. Pockets are double-lined because coins migrate. Mirrors are banned (except for licensed futures-trading mirrors that show not reflection but the compromise angle between you and what gravity thinks you should have been). In densest markets a shout achieves consensus pricing before its echo finds a surface to reflect from.

Erosion 2.6 should have erased everything geological-scale ago but ancient here means habits grown older than change. The rocks are not breaking down; they have negotiated terms. This grain of quartz promised that cliff-face sandstone it could stay vertical another aeon in exchange for a yearly tribute of platonic ideals chiseled into monastery walls. Rain—when it remembers to arrive like an estranged creditor—falls upwards first before reconsidering and negotiates diagonal paths respected by all slopes in perpetuity. The river (singular, ongoing protest-sign against Euclidean hydrology) carved its canyon horizontally, slicing through the Cant's thickness rather than its altitude, so canyon walls display strata of prehistory like book pages: at the bottom the fossil dreams of shells that never touched sea; higher up coal seams formed from forests that decided to grow time instead of wood; at eye level—disturbingly—layers of compacted architectural styles not yet invented back when the layers were laid down.

Dynamic 2.5 pulses subtle as plant-fibres adjusting investment portfolios inside their rings. Day is not a cycle but a vote. Each dawn the horizon petitions the sky whether to go left or right today; where the light arrives first sets political trends for a week. Storms are merger negotiations between conflicting weather futures; their lightning sears trade-codes into exposed metal surfaces partisans spend decades arbitraging. Biological pace? A child's tooth falls and investors short timber futures—long teeth mean confident bites mean steep-slanted expansion plans mean downstream landslides in thirty years if those kids grow up reckless.

Weirdness 1.5 is embarrassedly honest here, like a clerk apologizing behind spectacles made of time-lag. The strange is municipal ordinance 47-C: "All angles exceeding habitable tolerance must register with the Department of Unresolved Geometry and pay a quarterly Weirdness Levy." What's strange is how unsurprised everyone is when it rains upward on Tuesdays but only into open umbrellas; how maps must be renewed weekly because the ground subscribes to too many cartographical journals; how when you finally ask directions to the edge of the Cant old women laugh and place an appleseed in your palm saying "plant this in one hand, water with the other, whichever sprouts tomorrow—walk that way but lean left of the mirror." Strangeness here is simply bookkeeping written in the wrong-shaped ledger.

At the city's apex—recognizable only because all streets spiral inwards towards it despite being straight as lies—is the Tilted House, which tourists mistake for palace. It is smaller than memory said. No guards, just a door perpetually ajar by the exact angle of things yet to be decided. Inside one room containing only a chair that faces the wall opposite a window that shows not the Cant but the other angle the chair would occupy if someone sat. Legend says if you sit long enough you will be the city itself for a moment—feel every tremor of futures trading on dripping eaves, every hushed bet laid on which child will next bite into an appleseed that tastes like tomorrow, every etching of unlived years into rock that has forgotten it was ever not negotiating with time to hold its shape.

Most leave coins on the sill instead of sitting. Gravity sorts them. Somewhere downhill—sideways—tomorrow—someone finds currency minted in metals that haven't been discovered yet and knows the Cant's levy on tomorrow came due again.

The landscape ends by refusing to. Walk far enough and you are simply earlier in your walk. The Cant's edge is a myth circling for a better angle of attack. Behind you the city murmurs in voices translated through seventeen slanted tongues and one silence that remembers agreeing to become rain. Ahead the slope continues forever and precisely enough.
