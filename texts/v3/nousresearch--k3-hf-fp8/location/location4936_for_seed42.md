**The Choirtown Raftcradle**  
*Elevation:* 2,000 meters above the drowned world, suspended on the last unbroken spine of the Skyfold Mountains.  

---

**Water**  
This is not a place *near* water—it is a place *trying not to be* water. The air carries the weight of 3.0/3 submersion: every breath half-liquid, every sound sifted through a membrane of mist and old rain. A perpetual midwinter tide laps at the town’s edges, though no ocean lies beneath—only clouds petrified into slow-flowing fields of hydrostone, their dunes cresting and troughing over centuries. Beneath your feet, the streets gong hollow: the entire mountain peak was once hollowed, tunneled, gutted, and replanted with buoyant root-mats. The Choirtown floats. Barely.  

When storms ache past (rare, slow things at this altitude), the town tilts three degrees starboard. Rainfall doesn’t fall—it rises. Droplets cling to hair like pearls, crawl up sleeves, and eventually migrate in whispering columns toward the central **Singing Pit**, where the water-priests coax them into hymns that keep the raftcradle aloft.  

---

**Vegetation**  
2.9/3 total. Not a building stands that is not also a tree. Not a tree grows that does not also scaffold, pipe, or store.  

The architecture here is **symbioglyphic**: houses folded inside living walls of fogbark, their windows pulsing translucent like ripening fruit. Streets are arteries of moss-slick vine, lit by bioluminescent lichen that dims when you tread too loud. Roofs are orchards of dangling cistern-gourds, each swollen with filtered cloudwater and a dormant colony of blind, wingless bees.  

The dominant species is the **Antiphon Oak**, whose leaves grow in pairs of perfect acoustical mirrors. Clap once in an empty square and the sound will orbit you for hours, gaining verses from every conversation it passes until it returns on your great-granddaughter’s name-day as a fully formed lullaby—or an insult war-ballad, depending on the square.  

Only one thing keeps this from true 3.0 plant-substrate: the **Rootlaw**. A single thread of unrooted stone, the mountain’s severed core, hangs suspended in the town’s Voidheart Chapel. Nothing green may touch it. Above it, all is Choirtown. Below it, rumor claims, the drowned gods still dream in roots.  

---

**Temperature**  
1.3/3 cool—though "cool" implies contrast, and here there is none. The air holds a single note of chill, a linen-wrapped teacup still warm from someone else’s lips. Frost forms only on grief: widow’s windows bloom inward bouquets of ice every night, melting when the neighbors leave bread.  

Clothing is layered not for warmth but for *time*: each thread spun with a species of memory-silk that thickens when spoken to, thins when forgotten. A garment well-loved becomes armor; one ignored reverts to spiderweb and drifts away on the next slow-amble breeze.  

---

**Erosion**  
1.9/3—established, yes, but establishment here is negotiation. The town erodes downward as the clouds below erode upward, both parties polite enough to schedule their losses. Once a decade, the **Slopewarden** descends into the cloudfields (a journey of three breaths and three months) to deliver a basket of poems. If the poems are found wanting, a neighborhood sinks one root-knot by winter. If they delight, the town edges skyward a thumb’s width, and the highest tower adds a new windchime of fossilized thunder.  

Thus the streets slant subtle as a sigh. Newcomers stumble. Children born here walk crooked forever after, and live longer for the lopsidedness.  

---

**Scale & Density**  
1.1/3 small, 0.9/3 sparse—but scale is slippery in a place where the horizon is a neighbour’s rooftop seen through six hundred years of whispered gossip.  

Choirtown’s borders fit within a giants’s cathedral, but its rooms are infinite. Climb a ladder inside a cistern-gourd, emerge in another square five decibels quieter. Knock thrice on a specific wall in the Confluence Library, step into a corridor lined with books written by people who will never exist unless you read them aloud.  

Population is held steady at the **Soundful Number**—the exact count of citizens whose combined daily noise keeps the raftcradle aloft without spilling into thunder. Current census: 539 adults, 22 semi-audible elders, 113 children (counted as fractions, pending puberty), and 1 indeterminate who may be a crowd of cicadas wearing a trenchcoat. Vacancies are filled by audition. Excess citizens (births, arrivals, unfortunate loud sneezers) are gently encouraged to emigrate into the walls, becoming resident ghosts who maintain the plumbing.  

---

**Built & Tech**  
2.3/3 urban, 1.0/3 primitive—terms that thrash like caught fish here.  

There is no metal. There is fired clay, glass blown in hurricane hearts, bone whittled to lace, and the pietempared wood of trees that volunteered for architecture after reading heroic epics sung to them over centuries. Everything else is grown, spun, sung, or made inconveniently alive:  

- **Lamplighters** are deaf-mute poets who coax glowworms to blush into lanterns by reciting their librettos backwards.  
- **Bridges** are retired rainbows, pensioned off by the sky to stiffen into arches after too many betrayals. Cross without humming their true colors, and they remember every footstep you’ll ever take.  
- **Lawyers** are a species of paper wasp that swears oaths by chewing holes in contracts exactly where the lies are. Their nests line the courthouse walls; a verdict is reached when the hive falls asleep.  

Industry consists of **Conversationsmithies** where arguments are forged into tools (a well-crafted insult can split firewood), and **Memory-Mills** that grind old songs into flour for the bakehouses that produce tomorrow’s memories.  

---

**Light & Sound**  
1.7/3 normal light—if light can be normal when it remembers being sung. Day is a pale green filtered through kilometres of cloud-bitten sky; night is the same green inverted, a negative held up to sleep’s eye.  

But sound—sound is 2.3/3 loud. Not chaos, but architecture made audible.  

- Morning is the **Matins of Dew**: every rooftop gourd releases its collected raindrops in prearranged droplet fugues.  
- Marketday roars with the **Ten Thousand Haggles**, a competitive duet-prayer where shopkeepers and gods negotiate prices in real-time by shouting increasingly outrageous insults until something bursts into bloom or laughter.  
- Stormwatch brings the **Grand Litany of Slackening**, where the entire town hums a single note designed to lull the clouds back into their slow orbit. If it fails, the town votes on which building to jettison as ballast. Democracy has never been quieter.  

---

**Fauna**  
2.3/3 abundant, though taxonomy is a social pastime here, like dance or mild treason.  

Major species include:  
- **Fuguecats**—semi-liquid cats that forget to exist when not stared at directly. Popular as pets for introverts; ownership disputes are solved by forgetting to have them.  
- **Ventriloquist Crows**—avian historians who store inconvenient truths in other people’s throats. Crimes solved by arresting anyone who denies the murder too vehemently.  
- **Comma Crabs**—tiny punctuation-shaped crustaceans that infest poorly edited manuscripts. A badly written law once legalized murder during pauses; the comma crabs still celebrate annually by pinching commas out of speeches mid-sentence, causing temporary assassinations.  
- **The Choir**—citizens who took the vow of Audibility and dissolved into living soundwaves. They inhabit the pipes, gutters, and gossip, occasionally manifesting as choirs of footsteps behind lone walkers. Pay no attention; they’re practicing.  

---

**Weirdness & Dynamic**  
0.9/3 unusual (our lawyers insist), 0.8/3 slow (but accelerating backwards).  

The town’s most peculiar law is the **Law of Conservation of Peculiarity**: anything too strange is gradually traded away to keep the mundane strange. Thus, rain falling upward is ordinary, but a straight line causes riots. Clocks are banned, but time-keepers exist: old women who knit seconds into scarves that lengthen in winter, shorten in war.  

The town is currently engaged in a centuries-long argument with its own reflection (seen on calm days in the cloudfields below) over which of them is the original. Negotiations proceed at the speed of glaciers writing memoirs; last century they agreed to swap birthdays, causing everyone to un-age by four years and forget how to sneeze in unison.  

---

**Rules of the World**  
1. **Listen louder than you speak; the town hates interruptions.**  
2. **If you hear your name but no one is there, apologize—it may be a future echo you’ve forgotten.**  
3. **Do not cut down a tree without first teaching it a joke funny enough to make a stone laugh; the forest keeps receipts.**  
4. **Never finish a story on the same street where you began it; endings are territorial.**  
5. **If you must leave, do so backwards, singing something you learned to forget. Some doors only open to the unremembered.**  

---

**To Arrive**  
You don’t. You overshoot. You climb so far seeking silence that your footsteps stack up behind you into a staircase of echoes. When the wind starts answering your questions before you ask them, turn sideways into the sound of a crow laughing at its own joke. If you hear applause, keep going. If you hear an apology, you’ve gone too far—straight through the town and out the other side, where the world is still ordinary and doesn’t remember your name.  

But if you hear a lullaby sung just slightly off-key by a choir of raindrops who’ve been practicing your sorrow in advance?  

Welcome. The audition begins when you exhale. Try not to sneeze.
