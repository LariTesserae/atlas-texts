You emerge into a place that does not yet believe in edges. Everything unfinished, everything still deciding what it wants to be. A plateau the size of continents floats three meters above a landscape that forgot to finish forming below—tectonic sketches, raw geometry, triangles of mountain still connect-the-dots between missing topography. Gravity here negotiates rather than mandates; you can feel it weighing options.

The air is warm but weightless, like breathing inside someone's held breath. Condensation forms constantly and never falls—perfect spheres of water accumulate in the air like a suspension of glass prayers, occasionally drifting together and merging into short-lived raindrops that swim upward instead of down. They catch the dim light from sources that have no clear origin, refracting it into slow-motion waterfalls of color that never quite coalesce into illumination.

The surface beneath your feet—if surface is even right, since it might just be the concept of footing temporarily agreeing to participate—isn't stone or soil but something more honest: pure mineral intention, fresh from the magma of possibility. Sharp, glassy ridges erupt at impossible angles, still cooling from their recent precipitation into reality. Their surfaces are covered in dense, lush growth that somehow predates the ground it grows from—biological loopholes, flora that learned photosynthesis before light, root systems that grip probability instead of substrate.

This is a garden that grew in the gaps between making and made.

The plants here refuse taxonomy's colonial impulses. What you'd call trees are vertical rivers of cellulose that branch and unbranch depending on how closely you examine them. Their bark breathes warm oxygen in gentle pulses; their leaves are delicate membranes that filter reality like baleen filtering krill from existence's ocean, leaving behind concentrated pockets of not-quite-sure-yet. Between them grow smaller things—moss-like colonies that colonize time instead of space, spreading backward into the minutes before you arrived; flower clusters that bloom in fractal iterations of themselves, each petal containing a smaller version of the entire plant folded infinitely inward.

And everything is moving. Not with wind—there isn't any, the air too preoccupied with being atmospheric to bother circulating—but with the restless energy of creation's first draft. The ground ripples with small, geological hyphens, connecting thoughts that haven't become formations yet. Mountains shift uncomfortably in their sleep, adjusting angles that haven't quite found their final geometry. The plant rivers rearrange their courses while you're not looking directly at them, always flowing toward some watershed of definition that's perpetually upstream.

Life here is abundant, but hasn't decided what abundance means yet. Creatures move through the green uncertainty like punctuation trying to find their sentences. One might be mostly wing-idea, trailing strings of unresolved flight mechanics; another could be a walking digestive tract searching for a body to justify its appetite. A herd of somethings that might be future horses or past regrets gallop through a valley that disappears behind them like a doodle being erased. They leave tracks that are questions pressed into the mineral possibility: hoof? paw? sign that someone was here wondering?

Sounds exist but avoid commitment. Individual notes wander the lower frequencies like lost prayers, occasionally bumping into each other and forming brief, accidental chords that immediately dissolve from embarrassment. A continuous subaudible thrum might be the universe humming to itself while it works, or the sound of mountains remembering they're supposed to have roots. Every so often, something large and unidentified clears its throat in the distance, but thinks better of it.

The weirdness isn't something that happens here—it's the substrate. Reality hasn't settled on a contract yet, so terms are constantly being renegotiated. Scale performs anxious self-surgery; step toward a flower and it's suddenly a cathedral of petals; step back and recognize yourself reflected in a dewdrop that's become a planetary eye. Time leaks from past to future via poorly sealed presents; you might catch your shadow waiting ahead of you around a corner that hasn't been drawn yet, looking impatient.

And through it all runs the rhythm of industrial creation without industry. Here, "factory" is a verb that hasn't needed a building. The processes that elsewhere require steel and smoke and assembly lines happen directly in the materials themselves—rocks learning to be bridges through intensive self-reformation seminars, rivers of molten architecture that's been sent back to basic training to figure out whether it really wants to be water or wants to apply itself and become canyon. Everything is being built, but nothing is being made. The distinction hasn't been invented here yet.

In the distance (though distance has its own doubts to work through), something like a mountain range is undergoing a structured transformation program. Its peaks take morning classes in being more alpine while its valleys attend night school for improved definition. Thunderclouds that haven't settled on being weather systems or emotions roil overhead, occasionally releasing flashes of lightning that illuminate things that would rather not be seen yet.

This is the place that happens before maps. The first place. The last place. The place that's always being placed, never quite placed. You are not lost here—loss requires destinations this landscape hasn't finished inventing the concept of yet. You're simply early. Or late. Or perhaps you're right on time for a meeting that hasn't decided if it wants to be.
