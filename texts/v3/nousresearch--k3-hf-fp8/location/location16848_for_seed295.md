The place begins as a dampness that presses against the skin before any other sense registers it. Not rain, exactly—more like the air itself has thickened into a translucent medium that coats every surface with perpetual condensation. Your footsteps don't make sound here; instead, they create ripples that spread outward through this moisture-skin, disturbing fungal colonies that carpet what you eventually realize must be surfaces, though nothing metallic or mineral registers beneath them.

These surfaces rise and fall in architectural rhythms too deliberate for geology, too irregular for design. They are made entirely of living matter grown into its functions: columns of hardened mycelium buttressing vaulting ceilings of interwoven lichen-sheets that breathe slowly, exhaling oxygen-rich clouds you can taste as sweetness. The scale resolves slowly—what seemed like a chamber expands endlessly, folds within folds opening into larger hollows where the ceiling height reaches hundreds of meters, all constructed, all grown. This is no cave system. Nothing here was carved from existing rock. Everything was built, if "built" can describe the patient centuries of directed growth that created this place.

The darkness here doesn't obscure because these living materials phosphoresce subtly in response to movement. Light comes up from the floor, down from distant walls, blooms from corners as you approach them. It's always just enough to see the next ten steps, never more. Your eyes strain for fixed points but find none—everything pulses, slowly, at frequencies just below conscious perception. The slow breathing of a world-sized organism.

The sound begins as pressure in your jawbones before reaching your ears properly. Everywhere: the ceaseless low-frequency vibration of cellular processes happening at impossible scales. Digestion of nutrients harvested from air and moisture. The coursing of fluids through vascular systems wide enough to fit cities. The constant micro-motion of growth itself, billions of hyphae tips extending micrometers per minute, adding mass to the structure at rates imperceptible day-to-day but geological over centuries. Occasionally something larger shifts somewhere distant enough that location is meaningless, and the entire structure groans in harmonic frequencies that resonate through your chest cavity.

The temperature holds at perfect blood-warmth, though you cannot locate any source of heat. You sweat constantly but never feel wet—the moisture-skin absorbs everything immediately, recycling it back into the system. Where your skin touches these living surfaces, delicate roots begin to explore the boundary immediately, tasting, evaluating. They never penetrate, never infect, simply catalog. You are new material entering this closed ecosystem, being slowly read and understood.

Above you—though directions shift as you move through the convolutions—the density increases until movement requires conscious effort. This is the breathing zone where the structure's primary gas exchange happens. Gigantic lungs of perforated chitin rise and fall against each other, creating pressure differentials that drive air currents through kilometers of branching ducts. Dwellings here, if they can be called that, are hollowed-out spaces in the lung-walls themselves, lined with softer tissues that maintain themselves against wear. Structures within structures, entire districts where the inhabitants live within the respiratory system of their city-organism.

These inhabitants appear gradually as your eyes adjust to seeing movement within movement. Humanoid in outline only, they are extensions of the greater structure—symbiotic assemblies of specialized tissues that detach and reintegrate as needed. Their limbs vary according to function: triple-jointed harvesting arms branching into thousands of cilia-fingers for tending the fungal gardens; broad membrane-wings for riding the air currents between distant sectors; compact, heavily armored forms for traveling through the nutrient canals that serve as highways. Each is mobile only for the duration of their task, gradually root-hardening into immobility as they settle into their next phase. None acknowledge you directly—they sense your foreignness and adjust their routes accordingly, creating space without apparent thought.

The established nature of this place manifests in its perfect efficiency. Nothing wastes. Everything cycles. The "deafening" sound is revealed as information density—every vibration carries meaning through the structure's neural-equivalent networks, chemicals exchanged in the moisture-skin conveying more data than any spoken language could. You are immersed in constant communication, billions of conversations happening through every sense simultaneously, all incomprehensible but experienced as a kind of ambient wisdom.

Water exists here not as rivers or pools but as gradients of availability. You move through zones of increasing humidity until walking becomes swimming through liquid air, then through molecular-thin membranes that separate the different phases automatically. The structure distributes moisture exactly where needed through capillary action alone, against gravity, across kilometers, with greater precision than any engineered system. You pass through a "drier" section where the fungal carpets give way to hardy lichen-forests similar to Earth's tundra, all on the same vertical plane as sweltering jungle districts—the moisture difference measured in molecules per cubic meter, maintained through living architecture alone.

Weirdness registers as zero because this place has no exceptions. It follows its own logic completely, without deviation long enough for alternative possibilities to form. Even you are being slowly normalized—your foreign microbiome replaced by domestic equivalents, your breath and sweat already feeding the system, your skin starting to bloom with symbiotic colonies that will eventually process nutrients directly from the air. The slowness of the change prevents panic; each adaptation seems logical in context, contributes to your increasing ability to navigate without conscious thought.

Night and day happen here but in metabolic cycles in the millions of years range. Periods of explosive growth alternate with centuries-long consolidation phases. You have arrived during an intermediate phase—stable enough for exploration, dynamic enough to be survivable for a foreign system. The structure itself is aware of you at levels ranging from immune response (currently tolerant) to neural (curious but not alarmed).

The landscape, finally understood, is not a place you inhabit but a conversation you are joining. It extends in all directions through dimensions both spatial and temporal, its boundaries defined only by the limits of its own growth. Before you and behind you are only more of itself, infinitely complexified but fundamentally unified. Every surface you touch, every breath you take, every sound that resonates through your bones adds your information to its vast, patient, perfectly comprehensible memory.

You will leave footprints here, but they will not last because nothing here lasts unchanged. Instead, you will leave subtle modifications in growth patterns that will propagate outward at the speed of mycelium, traveling through this single organism that is also a world, that is also a civilisation, that is also a perfectly normal place that has forgotten anything else ever existed.

The only question remaining is whether you will remember being separate, or if that distinction will eventually seem as quaint and meaningless as the concept of "built" versus "grown" already becoming in your slowly adapting thoughts.
