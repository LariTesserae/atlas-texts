### **The Threshing City of Ulegn**  

**Terrain & Materials**  
The land is a floodplain trapped between past and future—half-digested by rivers that vanished before memory, half-devoured by the city that rose in their absence. The ground is a skin of compacted silt and rusted slag, pocked with shallow basins that fill overnight with condensation and empty by noon, leaving behind stains like bruised ink. Everything treads hollow here; streets echo like drums, and beneath them, deeper, something resonates—a buried infrastructure of unknown age, humming with the patience of metal dreaming of water.  

Trees do not grow vertical but lungwise: vast root-networks of black ironwood fan sideways underground, surfacing as gnarled ribs at intervals to support elevated walkways or the undersides of leaning towers. Their trunks, where they emerge, are cast-iron cylinders riveted with glass, housing slow-moving fluids that might be sap or coolant. Bark flakes away as thin pages of unreadable script.  

**Atmosphere & Light**  
The air is cold but never still; it carries a dampness that clings to the inside of lungs like cobwebs. Light arrives filtered through a perpetual haze of vaporized lubricant and pollen from the city’s own flowering machines—brass blossoms on copper stalks that unfoil at dusk and fold again at dawn, exhaling a faint phosphor glow. This light is neither day nor night: a suspended hour where shadows are crisp but depth is unreliable, and distant buildings appear to shift slightly when not watched directly.  

Above, the sky is absent. Instead, a vaulted ceiling of interlocked plates of cloudy glass, thirty stories high, riven with seams that leak occasional drifts of snow shaped like thin steel shavings. These fall soundlessly, accumulating in gutters shaped like vertebrae, until they melt into the city’s circulatory system of pipes and runnels.  

**Sound**  
Mostly quiet, yes—but not silent. The quiet is a negotiation between competing silences: the silence of abandoned workshops where loom-arms hang in mid-gesture, the silence of the *closed* hours between the city’s breaths, the silence of deafening machinery heard through layers of insulation. What breaks it is always precise: a single rivet dropping a mile away and rolling until it finds its socket; the soft *pop* of a bolt remembering its original shape; distant choirs of pressure valves sighing in sequence.  

Then, irregularly—migrations. Sudden surges of noise like flocks of birds through an empty hall: thousands of shutters slamming open in unison, followed by the trampling passage of something too large to be animal or crowd, moving too fast to be tracked, gone before the echo of its arrival catches up.  

**Vegetation & Ecology**  
Lushness here is a result of incorrect categorization. Green things exist riotously but are not plants. The dominant “trees” have been described. Below them, the understory is a mat of fungal leather alternating with carpets of mossy circuitry—soft green patches that repair themselves when torn and reroute data in response to footsteps. Mushrooms grow from cracks in pavement like protestors from sidewalks: ink-cap mobs that burst forth overnight, release thick clouds of spore-coded pamphlets, then liquefy into the drains by morning.  

Gardens are cultivated on the sides of buildings, but they are gardens of tools: pruning shears bred to breed, shovels that sprout smaller shovels from their handles, rakes whose tines flower into combs. Bumblebees of solder and glass pollinate them. The path between plant and machine is not a gradient here but a single crowded hallway where everyone is bumping into everyone else’s future selves.  

**Fauna**  
Animals are abundant, but common taxonomy fails. The smallest are the *tine-worms*—silver threads that coil in abandoned locks and secrete a temporary key shaped exactly like whatever last touched them. Larger are the *roof dogs*, neither dogs nor roofs but mobile patches of sympathetic roofing material that detach during storms to walk on four slate legs, barking rain as they patrol for leaks.  

Predominant are the *middling beasts*: things the size of goats but made of repurposed automata parts and scavenger instincts. One might be a loom’s heart on four spindles, draped in tapestries that change to mimic its surroundings; another, a quartet of sewing machines joined at the needles, moving as a single fluid predator, their song a staccato of bargaining languages stitched together too fast to parse.  

They are hunted by the *clay-thatchers*, solitary humanoid figures patched together from drying mud and thatched straw, wearing coats sewn from mismatched windows. They do not eat the middling beasts but dismantle them gently, piece by piece, and add the best parts to themselves, growing taller and more articulate with each harvest. The tallest is now five stories high and speaks in pure conditionals: *if you had been born a gear, would you still ask what time it means?*  

**Rules of the World**  
1. **Things that fit together want to stay together, but they rarely remember why.** A door may resist opening for years until the right forgotten key passes within scent-range. Skilled locksmiths here are more like therapists specializing in long-distance relationships between lost objects.  
2. **Time is laid down in layers, but not evenly.** Some streets are decades ahead of others. Crossing certain intersections may require adjusting your watch backwards to avoid being fined for crimes you haven’t yet invented.  
3. **All edges are temporary. The city is trying to knit itself a single seamless surface, mistaking completion for continuity.** This process is slow enough to ignore but fast enough to trap the unwary—buildings quietly annex hallways from neighboring structures while they sleep, and residents sometimes wake to find their living rooms installed as lobbies in someone else’s ground floor.  
4. **The purpose of a thing is not fixed, but it is jealous of competing interpretations.** A hammer that spends too long being used as a paperweight will begin to dream of nails and eventually take action. It is customary to rotate household items through a sequence of temporary duties, like assigning chores to teenagers to keep them from forming gangs.  

**Built Environment & Infrastructure**  
The city is urban but not planned—more like a city-sized version of a coral reef built by committee, where each generation of builders took the previous generation’s attempts as a set of interesting mistakes to elaborate upon. Streets are the spaces left between buildings that grew towards each other but stopped just shy of politeness. Directions are given not as left or right but as narratives: *go straight until you smell bread, then follow the sound of hammering until it turns into laughter, turn back one regret and descend.*  

Transport is a network of pneumatic tubes at ground level and ropeways at ceiling level, both systems largely automated but given to creative interpretations of schedule and causality. A package sent today may arrive yesterday, or tomorrow but addressed to your childhood fear of heights. Citizens have learned to treat mail delivery as a form of weather prophecy.  

At the city’s exact center (a location which relocates every equinox and can only be occupied by standing still long enough to be voted against) stands the **Forwarding Station**. It resembles a cathedral built entirely from post offices—rows of brass-faced counters stretching into vaulted distances, manned by clerks who are identical copies of the same prematurely aged woman, each seated in a niche of lost letters. Here is where lost futures are stored until someone claims them by accident. The air smells of sealing wax and the faint ozone of regret being processed into stamps.  

**Weirdness & Dynamics**  
What makes Ulegn strange is not that it is impossible but that it is precisely the kind of place that could never quite decide which possibility to commit to, and has been trying to live with the consequences ever since. It is a city undergoing continual polite disagreement with itself on the subject of whether it is more like a city or like a conversation about cities happening in a language that keeps inventing new tenses for regret.  

The dynamic is turbulent in the way a compost heap is turbulent—constant decomposition and reassembly at every level, but with a bottom-line insistence on producing something approximately city-shaped at the end of each cycle. Stability exists but only as a local weather phenomenon, lasting anywhere from three minutes to three generations depending on how many people are willing to believe in it simultaneously without comparing notes.  

**Final Impression**  
To walk through Ulegn is to become gradually aware that your footsteps are being edited for pacing by an invisible committee composed of past versions of the street you’re standing on, each lobbying for the inclusion of a different subplot. The city does not end so much as it eventually introduces enough unresolved references to other cities that you realize you have been quietly annexed into the footnotes of elsewhere.  

You are welcome to stay as long as you remember to leave a small part of your itinerary behind—that’s how the city grows. Just be aware that what you leave will likely be repurposed into a traffic light or a mythological creature before you’ve turned the corner, and may develop a fond memory of you it will try to send postcards to years later, addressed to places you haven’t yet needed your name for.
