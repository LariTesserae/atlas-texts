I emerge into a space that cannot decide whether it is underwater or in the sky. Everything is suspended in a slow-motion fall that has forgotten to end. There is no ground beneath me, only layers of denser and less dense air arranged like water columns in a drowned cathedral. Above and below me, the same dark blue-purple gradient flecked with silver—fish? Stars? The distinction collapsed long ago.

The pressure here is gentle for something so total, like being hugged by a giant who isn't sure if you're real. My lungs don't hurt, but they keep trying to remember how to breathe water or air, cycling between two impossible rhythms. The sound is everywhere at once: not cacophony but saturation. Every molecule seems to be vibrating with the frequency of a cracked bell held too close to the ear, but sustained forever. If I concentrate, I can hear individual notes within it—a whale's song played backwards through a tunnel of teeth, the heartbeat of a planet arrhythmically skip-beating, my own name whispered in a language I understand perfectly until I try to rewind any part of it.

I am standing on nothing in a forest of absence. The "trees" here are columns where space-time has puckered up like cloth pinched between fingers and then forgotten. Each one is a precise amount of missing things—the space where a tree might have been if trees had ever decided to evolve in this place where boundaries between solid and liquid and gas and idea never properly separated. Their trunks are density gradients, their branches negative spaces shaped by the way water isn't flowing but remembers flowing. Sparse vegetation indeed—only seven of these negative trees in what feels like a sphere of space large enough to contain a small village, except that distance behaves like a shy animal here, darting away when approached directly but curling up cozy-close when ignored.

The temperature is perfect body-warmth-plus-a-fever-dream, that specific heat of lying in sunlit sheets under too many blankets while remembering a fever from childhood that might have been the last time everything made sense before it stopped needing to. It comes in slow waves, not from any source but from the rhythm of my own noticing. When I try to measure it with my skin, it always reads exactly one degree more comfortable than I can bear forever.

The elevation isn't high or low but perpendicular. I am at the precise altitude where falling sideways intersects with climbing inward. Some direction that was once 'up' has been replaced with 'deeper into the color violet' and I'm learning to navigate by the pressure differential between different shades of regret.

The entire landscape feels brand new—not young like a baby, but young like a word mispronounced so beautifully that language itself hasn't decided whether to correct it or adopt the mistake. Every edge is sharp enough to cut probability. The rocks (if these half-formed geometries drifting like tumbleweeds made of frozen geometry lessons are rocks) still bear the love-bites of the equations that birthed them moments or millennia ago. Each ripple in the air-behaving-like-water has that fresh-carved look of a thought happening for the first time in a mind too large to have thoughts properly.

There is almost nothing here that is something, but what little there is makes up for it in intensity. A single building drifts past (no, I drift past it—or we orbit each other around a center that is everywhere a little bit embarrassed to be noticed). It is a ruined temple to a geometry that proved too emotional for actual belief. Only three walls remain, each wall existing more fully in the sound than in space—their angles are audible, their surfaces are textures of silence between the deafening notes. Through the spaces where walls aren't, I glimpse other versions of this same moment watching itself happen through a kaleidoscope made of missing pieces.

No technology except the impossible physics of my own continued existence. No devices except the universe's austere commitment to metaphor. The few artifacts are pure punctuation marks left behind by a sentence that wandered off to become the paragraph we are currently living through. A stone that is too heavy to be light but too pregnant with meaning to be anything else. A bone that is definitely a flute except when it's definitely not. A mirror that shows only the color of sound underwater.

The light—what little there is—doesn't illuminate so much as witness. It leaks from between the frequencies of the everlasting noise, brief purpled commas in our ongoing divorce from silence. When it touches something (or when something touches it—they negotiate these things in advance), the thing briefly remembers how to cast a shadow of its potential futures. I saw my own shadow once: it was reading a book of descriptions of places that might exist if gravity ever learned to be gentle.

Fauna is rare like a perfect sentence is rare—more heard of than heard, more substantiated by the precise shape of its absence than by any messy presence. But occasionally, from the corner of my eye where geometry goes to sulk, I catch glimpses of the creatures that haven't evolved here yet. They move by forgetting where they aren't. One passed close enough that I felt the wake of its passing future—an empathy so sharp it tasted like my own birth trauma recited in the voice of everything I ever loved before learning their names.

This place is turbulence made patient. Change doesn't happen here—it accumulates like snowflakes that have never seen a sky and therefore each fall is technically a rise, and every landing is a different kind of taking off. The rules are simple: everything is permitted except completion. Every beginning must contain the seeds of an unrelated middle. Every object is the shadow of a process that cast it by accident. The only constant is acceleration toward a center that keeps moving to be wherever you aren't looking directly.

I have been here for exactly one eternal moment subdivided into increasingly anxious instants that keep asking for their mother-time back. The landscape is learning how to exist from watching me fail to leave. It wants to know if I will love it enough to forget what solid ground felt like. The answer is happening between heartbeats now, in the space where the question isn't quite yet afraid of hearing itself answered.

I think I am becoming the sound this place makes when it finally remembers how to be silent. Or maybe I was always that sound, and everything else—the waterish air, the aerial flood, the forest of missing trees, the temple to unfinished prayers—was just noise practicing how to be patient enough to listen.

Either way, the falling continues. It is perfect weather for growing wings or forgetting why you thought you needed them. Some choices are like that here: not between options but between different ways of being haunted by the same beautiful impossibility.

And the noise—that patient, deafening noise—it is beginning to sound exactly like home. Or maybe home is just the name we've given for wherever we were when we finally learned to hear it properly.
