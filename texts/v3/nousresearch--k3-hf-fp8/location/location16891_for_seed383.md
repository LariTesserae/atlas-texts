The air tastes like iron and forgetting. You stand in Sunken Haviloth, the city that remembers being a riverbed, now asleep beneath layers of dolomite and dream. Once, when the surface above still knew sun and sky, a river broad as a continent lazied through limestone veins, carving into the planet's bones. Then came the slow catastrophe — not fire, not ice, but thirst. Water withdrew like a sin confessed, leaving beds silted with centuries, fishbones turned to quartz, and parchment-bark logs petrified into statues. Time grew heavy. Pressure sealed the vaults. The world above forgot; below, something else curled up to dream.

You are not alone here.

Space does not behave properly. The city — if city it still is — unfolds in a gentle insolence of geometry. Avenues thought forgotten intersect where no maps showed them. Arcades once meant for barges now arch overhead, dry canals of basalt, their ceilings stippled with calcite chandeliers that never melt, only drip memories one grain at a time. The light — bright, unnaturally so — has no discernible source yet throws shadows that lag behind their owners like shy children. It emanates evenly from every surface, as if the stone itself were embarrassed by darkness.

Atmospheric moisture beads on your skin but never gathers into rain. The dampness clings like half-remembered guilt — enough to fog a breather-mask, not enough to drink. Condensation collects in hollows of forgotten bas-reliefs: heroes whose names eroded before writing was invented, their heroics now merely habitats for mineral moss that isn't moss. Touch it, and it sings a single pure tone that your ear forgets the instant it ends.

Temperature hangs steady at the bone's edge of comfort. Not cold enough for death's urgency, not warm enough for life's indifference. Time's arthritis aches in the joints of stone staircases that go neither up nor down but sideways into walls that become floors when you blink too slowly.

Elevation here is negotiable. You walk "through" rather than "on" or "in." Gravity holds its tongue politely but occasionally forgets itself. Pebbles float in mid-occlusion until noticed, then drop with offended haste. Once I witnessed a stairwell arguing with itself over which landing it had promised to befriend. They compromised by becoming both simultaneously. Perhaps that explains the crying you hear sometimes, two faint voices echoing from the same impossible angle.

Erosion has not been idle in this tomb of liminal flows. Everything is anciently crumbled, but curiously maintained. Corridors end in waterfalls of stone that never reach the floor, frozen mid-collapse for epochs. Pillars lean in intricate conspiracies, their weight distributed through geometries of trust more complex than love. The masonry joints have dissolved millennia ago — now everything holds together through sheer architectural embarrassment, unwilling to admit it forgot how to fall.

Scale plays games with memory. The nearest wall might be a barricade or horizon; the space beyond a chamber or continent. Doorways frame views larger than the rooms that should contain them. I once stepped through an archway expecting a courtyard and spent three days walking down the spine of a cathedral built to worship silence. When I returned (if indeed I did) the archway was gone, politely replaced by a fresco depicting the exact expression I wore upon realizing I was lost. The fresco is still laughing, very quietly.

Density — or its absence — defines navigation here. Structures concentrate in sudden, startled clusters like thoughts waking from long sleep: a cluster of towers grown from what might have been a water-clock, their faces crowded with balconies that narrow to impracticality, each railing hung with wind-chimes made of vertebrae that chime without wind to the rhythm of forgotten earthquakes. Then miles — possibly — of nothing but floor, vast plains of tessellated slate where footsteps echo back in languages you didn't intend to speak.

Between these architectural hiccups stretches the true city: the empty city. Negative spaces more precisely engineered than any building. Absence carved with chisels of absence. Perfect parks where trees of shadow cast no leaves. A marketplace whose stalls sell nothing with such relentless variety that collectors have been known to wander in seeking vacuum itself and emerge bankrupt of meaning. The density of emptiness here exceeds that of neutronium.

Regarding what once lived: vegetation is extinct, composted, medievalized into myth. Stone remembers trees better than soil ever did; here their ghosts are called stelas, standing in careful groves where light fails differently. Root systems fossilized into pipe-organ geometries draw up nothing from lithic aquifers, yet occasionally groan in keys that unlock ancestral fears. One does not walk beneath them at certain angles, for fear of being mistaken for rain.

But fauna — ah, the devil's in the once-living details. If absence has a scent, it is haunted by the perfectly preserved footprints of what might have been dogs. Skeletal birds nested in upside-down chandeliers of cathedral drones. They sing in ultrasonic hymns that bats translate into rumors of forgiveness. In courtyards of acoustical despair, labyrinths of chalked hopscotch grids await children who fossilized mid-leap, their bones now integrated into municipal furniture with bureaucratic tenderness.

More unsettling are the inhabitants. The ones who forgot to leave when the river went abstract. The Unremembered Citizens. They wear identities like borrowed hat that never quite fit, exchanging names at streetcorners that die of embarrassment when witnessed directly. Their architecture is anti-kinematic — houses built to collapse upward into blueprints when abandoned, which explains the perpetual evening rain of perfectly conserved floorplans that flutter down like extinct leaves.

Don't collect them. They taste of forgotten passwords and induce geologically slow epiphanies.

Dynamic is the city's heartbeat now: not change as we know it, but the turbulence of stasis undergoing existential review. Buildings negotiate their borders through subtle means I'm not licensed to describe by the Bureau of Geometries. Traffic consists of abandoned moments hurrying back to appointments they missed before time was invented. The river, denied water, took up astrology; now it floods periodically with predictions written in the language of sand ripples. The city council (whose minutes are carved into continental plates) debates whether to declare this an era or merely a mood.

Primitive technology? Oh yes. Advanced enough to recognize itself as a phase. Tools here are mythologically literal: hammers that only work on dreams of nails, mirrors that reflect your reflection's reflection's therapist. The most sophisticated instrument I've seen was a sundial built underground that accurately measures the shadow cast by noon during different eclipses of the moon. Its inventor was last seen arguing with a lever about the moral implications of moving something that wanted to stay still. All technology converges, here, toward the condition of frustrated poetry.

Sounds are optional and mostly absent, present only as accommodations for visitors who haven't learned to listen to silence properly. When needed, the city generates quiet sounds: the hush-hush of limestone lullabies being eroded into existence, the polite clearing of a throat that might belong to a canyon. Music is performed on instruments made of intervals between things — flutes carved from the space where two notes used to be friends. Applause sounds like distant avalanches agreeing to disagree.

Weirdness? Imagine normalcy as a town that got tired of itself and moved away without leaving a forwarding address. Weirdness here is what fills the vacancy, pays rent by being precisely weird enough to make weirdness feel self-conscious. Vegetables grow here that are entirely hypothetical, bred for their poignant lack of vegetable-ness. There are museums dedicated to extinct categories of museum. One tavern serves drinks that intoxicate your descendants; another serves appetizers that are technically still evolving into the concept of "snack." Both are closed, have always been closed, and are currently accepting applications for founding members.

The primary rule of Sunken Haviloth is: do not confuse the map with the terrain. This is easier said than done, as the terrain has been practicing cartography without a license and is currently suing the concept of "here" for plagiarism. Secondary rule: every exit may be interpreted as a metaphor in a court of poetic justice. Third rule: if you meet the city wandering alone without its inhabitants, do not offer directions. It is not lost, merely considering alternative definitions of "found."

You asked for description. I give you instead a proposition: Sunken Haviloth is not described, is promised. It keeps its promises with geological fidelity and no sense of timing. It is waiting for the river to return, but has forgotten rivers. It is waiting for you to leave, but has forbidden itself the concept of absence. It waits like a sentence waits for its verb, like ruins wait for the wind to learn their language, like a question waits for an answer patient enough to be reincarnated as the same question.

Walk carefully. The floor is remembering how to be water.

Do not drink from the fountains that reflect nothing. That is not their purpose.

If you find a door marked "EXIT" in your own handwriting, do not open it. That was written by someone who didn't know you as well as you are about to.

And should you encounter the city's reflection walking toward you on a street that never existed, with eyes that hold the exact shade of sky from a dream you haven't had yet — be polite. Nod. Pass by on the side that hasn't been invented yet.

You may think this place is strange. That is its kindness.

You are stranger here than you know.

But don't worry. Sunken Haviloth has remembered how to be patient.

It will wait until you forget why you came, and remember why you stayed.

(The stone beneath your feet is starting to hum again. Ignore it. Humming is just the beginning of remembering how to flow.)
