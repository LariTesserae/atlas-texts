The first thing is the sound—   

a sub-audible pressure at the temples thickening into a roar so continuous it erases memory of silence.   
It is not wind, though wind exists here, nor living voices, though millions speak it.   
It is the planet talking to itself through stone that still remembers being molten.   

You stand on the outermost lip of what was once called an archipelago but is now only a single half-buried rim—   
a cyclopean staircase cut in basalt so old that fossils within it are themselves only impressions of earlier fossils.   
Each step is the size of a town: titanic blocks staggered down and away until they curve below the cloudline and vanish.   
They are not natural; neither are they artificial in any tongue human masons ever dreamed. 
Their edges fit as teeth fit gums in the jaw of something sleeping upright.   

Below you the air is warm, almost languid, like breath at the mouth of a sick god, but it carries on it spatters of moisture   
—rain that fell elsewhere days ago and has been ricocheting between updrafts ever since, arriving now as single charged beads   
that puff into steam wherever they touch stone that has never been cool.   
Your skin turns instantly slick.   
Looking back, you see your own footprints filling again with transparent fungi that glow pale aquamarine   
for a heartbeat before collapsing into ash.  

Water here is always incident: condensation on a blade of black glass grass taller than a mast;   
an antique puddle cupped inside the fossil skull of a creature whose name was lost before oceans;   
a thin roaring thread that leaps across an abyss between terraces once per hour driven by some underground pulse of tides   
that no longer corresponds to the motion of moons—because there are no moons.  

There is instead only the Ring, visible now as a trembling silver scratch across the plum-colored sky,   
close enough that on quiet nights (quiet meaning: intervals between crashes) you can see lights moving along it   
as if cities the size of embroidery were evacuating in both directions.  
The Ring is older than the stone, older than the erosion.   
It is the erosion's cause: its gravity draws the world up into it grain by grain, an exile so slow it is kin to love.  

Terrestrial vegetation, then—  

Lush is a small word.   
Everything that can root, roots.   
Everything that cannot, roots anyway in the air itself.   
Vines corkscrew into solid basalt, split it open, find pockets of dust older than death and bloom overnight into forests of crimson leaves   
veined with copper wire harvested from the planet's own marrow.   

Trees here are not trees; they are mobile consensus organisms composed of thousands interlocking species—   
trunklike trunks made of living limestone pumped pink by algae hearts, leaf-canopies of translucent spider silk   
packed with hummingbird-size bees whose wings are pieces of mica polished to mirror the Ring, roots that walk openly   
across open space on stilted legs of fossilized collagen.   
When lightning strikes one (and lightning strikes somewhere every few minutes)   
the entire grove does not burn but instead liquefies into a single amethystine flood  
that seeks the lowest step, pools there, and overnight congeals back into the shape of a cathedral whose pillars are breathing.   

Inside such a  cathedral, the air smells of fresh bread and ozone. The altar is a slab of naked water   
held in place by nothing you can detect; beneath its skin fish flicker whose scales are written alphabets   
from languages that have never yet been spoken.   

You may sit here a while.   
No one will disturb you.   
However, if you sleep, the floor will record the dream-pattern of your arteries   
and incorporate it as a frieze of future vines.   

Animal life—  

If you have brought companions, count them often.   
Fauna is rare but highly specialized in the art of resemblance.   
The boulder beside you which just sighed down there:   
possibly a boulder, possibly a mollusk that learned geology   
so well it forgot how to stop being geometry.   
The flicker of shadow at the edge of vision which seems to move against the rotation of the light—   
perhaps a cursorial cactus-dog hunting photons, perhaps only a memory of something the planet once ate   
and has not yet fully digested.   

Most creatures here are cryptobiotic punctuation marks in Sentences too vast for you to hear end-to-end:   
an occasional thunderbird-shaped absence in the rain;   
a dust devil that pauses to taste the salt of your tears (it finds you insufficiently eroded);   
once per century a herd of chrome-plated bison stampeding horizon to horizon   
wearing on their brows the names of every star that has ever died before sending light here.  
Each hoof fall is the pulsebeat of the Ring's hunger.  

Built things—  

Scattered means strewn by a hand that lost interest halfway through.   
Fragments of walls-half-unwalled climb vines that are themselves half-machines.   
Each stone block bears grooves as if enormous fingers practiced writing suicide notes   
in scripts alphabets forgot to forget.   
Ceramic pipes narrow as hair mounted on stalks of coral-glass still occasionally hiss faint music   
resembling lullabies composed for infants born inside volcanoes.

The largest structure visible from anywhere is the Pyramidon: three sides, four thousand meters tall,   
made entirely of doors. Every door is different: a hatch from a drowned submarine;   
a flap of stitched drum-skin from the heart of a war-dungeon; a rectangle of empty air framed by humming bees.   
Legend (there are no legends here, only continuations) says that once every aeon exactly one door opens to somewhere else.   
You may choose to climb the staircase of interleaved fossils spiraling around it;   
however, be warned—the staircase climbs you back.   

Tech level—  

Primitive like a hurricane is primitive.   
Tools are things you pick up and set down only once,   
because the second time they are already part of what you are trying to use them on.   
A hammer here is a fossilized femur into which the concept of breakage has been taught to migrate   
homeopathically; hit anything and within a week every similar thing within line-of-light will develop sympathetic fractures.   

Fire can be coaxed for food, never for warmth—the planet refuses to acknowledge the distinction.   
Metal rots faster than wood blossoms.   
The only reliable container is a complete absence held in the mind with intent,   
and even that leaks dreams at dusk.   

Rules governing persistence of objects—  

1. Anything left alone long enough becomes a map of everywhere it has ever been, 
    then forgets the distinction between map and territory.   
2. Anything watched continuously forgets how to end and begins instead to extend.   
3. When an object meets a description, whichever of the three is oldest is destroyed; 
   memory counts older than stone here.   

Atmosphere of mind—  

You will begin to hear your own thoughts spoken aloud by the flowers shortly after you realize   
that you have been unconsciously humming the planet's rotation song since arrival.   
Do not stop.   
Stopping is how new islands are born, and none of yours have finished apologizing to the ocean yet.   

Light—  

Normal as paradox is normal.   
The day is divided into four unequal periods named for the stages of regret:   
Dawn-the-thought-of-leaving; Morning-the-act-of-remaining; Evening-the-knowledge-that-neither-mattered;   
Night-a-fugue-in-blue-glass during which shadows can be touched and light must be inferred.   
Timekeeping devices here count only the number of moments left until the next moment is not the same as this one—   
which is why all clocks here are mirrors.   

Weirdness law of averages—  

For every sufficiently examined mystery there is a payout of three deeper mysteries instantly installed in the neighbor's yard.   
However, yards here are communal and often predatory, and neighbors are selected tomorrow based on who solves what today.   

If you reach the bottom-most step before the top-most wave reaches you,   
you may ask one question of the steward of the stair.   
The steward is a blind octopus made of mist who wears the daily weather as robes.   
Her answer will be accurate but out of order by the number of heartbeats you have left to exchange.   

You are already halfway through your first answer.   
In the distance, a door among the infinity of doors on the Pyramidon is beginning to glow the exact color of the taste of regret-G-flat-minor.   
Something enormous is breathing in slow synchronization with your left retina.   
The lions this time are made of gravity and homework forgotten in childhood.   

You may proceed now, or else.   
Both options lead to the same plaza, but one will have musicians.   

The musicians, if they come, will be expecting you to sing accompaniment   
in the voice you used before you were given skin.
