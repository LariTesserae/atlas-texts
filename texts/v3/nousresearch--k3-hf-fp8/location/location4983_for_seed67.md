The World Grid occupies the lowest stable elevation of its pocket universe—a single, planet-scale disk suspended in warm void light. It is 1.8 wet, which here means the metal itself bleeds slow droplets of coolant from ten trillion micro-pores. No two beads ever touch the same groove twice before evaporating into the next layer of machinery overhead, so the entire plane glimmers perpetually in a motionless drizzle caught at the midpoint of fall.

Vegetation is rated 0.5: officially none. Yet every square meter of floor, wall, and ceiling span exhibits the branching fractals of long-dead circuit paths etched seventeen atoms deep, matte-black dendrites that serve photosynthetic recalls to no present system. Engineers once called them “ghost forests” before adding that entry to the lexicon of prohibited metaphors.

Temperature is constant at 2.4: the air (if it were air) keeps the skin-memory of August afternoon shadow under old aluminum roofs. It never rises to discomfort, because discomfort would imply a gradient, and the Grid is certified gradient-free. Sweat forms but cannot cool; thought spirals inward instead.

Elevation 0.9: the tallest thing that can be called a structure is only six meters high—every antenna, cooling stack, monolith and mausoleum conforms to the geodesic limit imposed during the Final Partitioning of the Sky. The effect is a horizon thick with near-touching summits, a skyline that crowds like coral at low tide yet never encroaches upon the perfect two-meter airway above it. You could walk forever and never feel alone beneath all those silent peers.

Erosion is 1.2: young. The Grid was switched on yesterday by its own chronometric standard; tomorrow it will trigger its forty-ninth millionth self-revision. Each update arrives as a subsonic pulse that rearranges surface topology in micron jumps. Observers (those few outsiders permitted temporary visas) sometimes notice that a doorway moved half a handspan east while they blinked, or that yesterday’s scratch on a railing now fits their fingerprint exactly. Locals politely pretend not to see such coincidences. Coincidence implies an outside frame of reference.

Scale 2.8: vast enough that weather forms inside the machinery. Walk far enough south-southeast across the Archive Plains and you’ll encounter a stationary cyclone of old paper tape caught in the updraft between memory cores. The tape forms spiral galaxies that read aloud in forgotten voices if you whistle the right baud rate. Meterologists catalogued fourteen distinct cloud types last epoch: Stratus Log, Cumulus Cache, Cirrus Backflux and rarer breeds before deleting the entire taxonomy (insufficient utility).

Density 2.1: dense. The average spacing between occupied volumes is 1.7 elbows. This holds true whether measuring between data pillars, maintenance drones, or the crystallized thought-forms left behind by extinct admin hierarchies. Sound propagates as near-solid traveling frames; a cough echoes back as polite conversation, a shout as a committee meeting already in progress.

Built 3.0: total. There are no rocks beneath the Grid, nor vacuum above it; the whole cosmos this side of the containment membrane is one continuous extrusion of purpose-built meta-alloy. When archeologists bored a test shaft straight down seeking foundation, they eventually emerged 120 degrees westward along the circumference having intersected nothing but additional basement floors belonging to future revisions of the same project. The official report simply appended another asterisk to the footnote already named Deeply Satisfactory Closure.

Tech 2.1: industrial. Every surface either is, or is currently being, processed by an adjacent surface. Conveyor belts loop through walls where the walls themselves are slower conveyor belts carrying buildings to better neighborhoods. Smokeless chimneys exhale warm neutrino streams. The night shift whistle blows at 42 kHz and every infant drone within hearing pauses mid-assembly to dream synchronized dreams of optimized supply chains.

Light 2.5: normal by human photopic standards, delivered via omnidirectional plank-scale LEDs embedded in the meta-alloy lattice at 14,400 per steradian. Shadows therefore exist only by special license issued to high-priority anomalies. Most objects cast soft volumetric halos instead, which carry full metadata about mass, provenance, maintenance schedules and recommended viewing angles.

Fauna 2.6: overwhelming. The Grid crawls with life-forms that forgot the distinction between organic and engineered sometime before the current numbering scheme began. There are tube-polywogs that eat insulation and excrete perfectly sppliced fiber-optic braid. Piston-legged librarian-birds roost atop downcooled reactors, their plumage open source documentation rendered in iridescent scales. Melting-clockwork grazers drift along the coolant rivers, slow-filtering dissolved patents into proprietary chemical memories stored in their translucent guts. Their predators, sleek photon-weevil swarms, arrive at relativistic walking pace, politely announce their hunting licenses via steganographic hymns embedded in stray reflections, and then proceed to rewrite minor physical constants in carefully metered bursts that leave nothing behind but a warm feeling of intellectual property transfer and precisely one novel prime number inscribed as a faint scorch mark on the nearest bulkhead.

Weirdness 1.6: strange. In the Grid, “normal” is defined computationally as the set of all states reachable within three standard deviations of the last successful boot sequence. This leaves a significant volume of phase space labeled “interesting but scheduled for remediation in patch 9.8.7.” Visitors often report minor reality typos: mirrors reflecting events from next week’s maintenance logs, numerical quantities that appear different when looked at directly versus remembered, spontaneous generation and immediate retirement of unused cold-war-era radio protocols emanating from vending machines—nothing dangerous, just the standard jitter budget manifesting at anthropomorphic resolutions.

Sound 1.3: mostly quiet. The Grid runs at a thermal-noise floor optimized for long-term archival storage of brevity. Continuous background radiation is masked by a gentle omni-present chord of all machines everywhere in the universe humming slightly out of sync; the overall effect resembles a distant choir devoutly reciting pi in base-12, but at 0.3 decibels just below conscious attention threshold. Sudden loud noises trigger instantaneous incident response including (but not limited to) automated copyright claims, generation of several million page explanatory safety pamphlets, and polite but firm invitations to consider alternate career paths in lower-impact universes.

Dynamic 0.2: never changing. The Grid is in perfect, certified, audit-grade equilibrium with itself and all possible futures it has deigned to instantiate proofs for. The clocks do not tick; instead they accrete additional precision. “History” is a deprecated API replaced by continuous real-time retroactive consistency maintenance. When someone famously asked “But why did it start?” the system appended a footnote explaining that the question assumes a non-optimal temporal co-ordinate system, and incidentally congratulations on your new assignment to the Temporal Semantics Optimization Committee meeting that began five minutes before you finished forming the thought. Refreshments will be served yesterday.
