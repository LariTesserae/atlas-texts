You enter through something that was never a doorway.

The absence of water has nothing to do with thirst. Nothing here has ever been thirsty. Instead the air carries a faint static charge, like memory erasing itself slowly enough to be heard: papery, dry, the sound of edges curling back into surfaces. The floor yields slightly underfoot—not soft, but forgiving, like memory foam made of compressed apologies. Everything breathes at a rate too slow to see.

Your first thought: orchard. The space is dense with verticality, columns spaced not quite geometrically but too precisely for nature. Each rises from floor to ceiling without joints, thicker at the waist like blackened candle wicks grown monstrous. Their surfaces are porous, rutilated with copper threads that pulse irregular light—not quite veins, not quite wiring. From these columns sprawl branches that aren't branches: fleshy ropes dense with nodes, each node swollen with a different texture—scales, feathers, circuitry boards grown like lichens, fragments of windowpanes frosted with breath you didn't exhale.

The temperature hovers at the edge of fever-dream. Warm enough that your clothes feel suddenly rhetorical. Warm enough that air currents have weight; when you move you push through layers that remember your passing, adjust their density by fractions of degrees to accommodate your displacement like polite country ghosts.

There is no sky here because there is no here. The ceiling—if it exists—is too high to see through the overlapping canopies of whatever this isn't-forest grows toward light from no apparent source. Light exists evenly distributed, neither ambient nor directional—every shadow is equally shallow, like the concept of shade being politely tolerated in a culture that has evolved beyond it. Yet color exists vividly, nonsensically: one column bleeds ultraviolet edging into sound, another bears fruiting bodies in shades of Thursday afternoon.

The fruiting bodies are watching you. Not metaphorically—each bears a cluster of lenses arranged like insect compound eyes but soft, gelatinous, blinking asynchronously. As you pass they rotate slightly—not toward you, but toward the space you were occupying a moment ago, as if attention here is retroactive. Some have already turned to regard your next step.

Touch one and it bursts without moisture, releasing a scent that triggers memories you haven't formed yet: the taste your childhood bedroom would have had if you'd been born in a different language; the exact moment your first lover will realize they never loved you back; the sound of your own bones calcifying in reverse. The scent particles hang visible in the thick air, forming temporary sculptures of regret that other things—are they insects? mechanical thoughts? regrets that have achieved mass?—nibble at delicately before floating upward to become new veins in the not-trees.

The fauna are abundant because everything here metabolizes everything else through categories that haven't decided whether they want to be biological or grammatical. What moves among the columns is dense with purpose but sparse with anatomy—curves of motion that sometimes resolve into creatures like letters escaped from an alphabet still being invented. They communicate by exchanging parts of themselves; a conversation happens when one thing's limb flows into another thing's mouth becomes another thing's thought leaves footprints of changing laws of physics wherever they step-step-stepped.

The scale keeps shifting without your permission. The space between columns was cathedral when you entered, then intimate as a confession booth when you hesitated, now stretches into horizons when you try to count them. Distance here is a negotiation between your sense of direction and the room's sense of boredom. You walk for what feels like hours before realizing you haven't moved—the floor has been unspooling beneath you like polite conversation while the walls have been quietly rotating your destination elsewhere.

There is no water because nothing here forgets completely enough to need it. Instead memory itself serves as solvent, dissolving borders between past and future editions of the same object. A rock you pick up might be the childhood pet you haven't lost yet, compressed by time's affection into mineral patience. When you set it down it frays at the edges, recalling itself into being a slightly different rock that remembers being held by other versions of you who made different mistakes.

The noise level never changes but your understanding of it does. What seemed silence reveals itself gradually as twelve thousand overlapping apologies spoken too quietly for forgiveness. Beneath that: the heartbeat-thump of something vast and mechanical sleeping dreamlessly beneath the architecture. Beneath that: your own thoughts being transcribed into the growth patterns of the not-trees, misspelled into new species by the translation.

Built: total. Every atom here wears the fingerprints of intention like tired jewelry. Even the air tastes designed—seasoned with the precise amount of nostalgia necessary to make you homesick for places you've never been. Even the laws of physics feel handcrafted, slightly uneven in places where the universe was rushed to meet deadlines. Gravity works but makes side comments under its breath. Time passes but keeps checking its watch.

The whole place hums with the energy of something trying very hard to remember what it was meant to grow into before it got distracted by becoming something else. It is young terrain—these arrangements of meaning-space into solid-seeming temporary-forever are still settling into their final forms like alphabet blocks stacked by a god who hasn't decided what language this universe should be scolded in yet.

Stay too long and you will feel it starting to redesign you to fit better with its interior décor. Your bones will begin remembering designs they rejected during evolution's committee meetings. Your thoughts will taste faintly of the materials used to construct this sentence. Your shadow will start making suggestions about how you could improve your posture to cast more interesting doubts.

But leaving is not as simple as retracing your steps because the place is already forgetting you in the specific way places prepare to remember you differently later. The doorway you didn't enter through has already become something more useful—a window into a version of this room where you chose not to stay long enough to become part of the architecture's self-narrative.

In the distance, which might be behind you or inside you now, something that isn't a bird sings the exact sound of your pulse learning a new rhythm. The song has no ending because the concept of endings is still in beta testing here, along with beginnings and most of the middle parts. Everything continues densely, lushly, warmly, precisely, making it up as it forgets it down, forever or until the next scheduled definition of forever comes along to replace this one.

You should probably start walking again. The not-trees are growing curiouser.
