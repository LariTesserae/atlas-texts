The air here is not air but a thick, palpable heat that hangs like a vast, unmoving curtain. It presses against every surface, every edge, every thought. There is no water—none in the sky, none in the ground, none in the memory of the stones. Only dryness, absolute. A dryness that has become something else, a mineral presence, a force. The ground is not sand nor soil but a crust of fused salts and silicates, bleached white by a sun that never rises or sets but hangs, perpetually low, a dull ember smoldering at the horizon’s rim. It casts no shadows, only heat-shadows—a shimmering haste across the land that tricks the eye but reveals nothing.

This place is small, pocketed. It does not stretch; it folds. Walk too far in any direction and you will return to where you began, though the path will seem different each time. The scale rebels against itself—each grain of salt a boulder, each fissure a canyon, each breath a storm. Yet it is empty. Empty enough to hear the slow grinding of the world’s teeth, the distant creak of continents dreaming.

Vegetation, yes—lush by some alien reckoning, though none would call it green. Here life has forgotten chlorophyll, forgotten water, forgotten softness. Instead: towers of crystalized breath, tall as houses, branching like shattered glass. They pulse with a faint, internal glow, colorless yet somehow many-hued, casting prismatic ghosts on the cracked white plain. They grow upward and inward simultaneously, splitting themselves to grow again, their edges singing faintly in the heat. Their roots—if roots they be—are networks of hair-thin filaments that delve not downward but sideways, intertwining beneath the salt crust in a vast, invisible tapestry. They drink the heat itself, metabolize silence. When they die—though die is not the right word—they do not fall but evaporate, leaving behind hollow lattices that hum for centuries before crumbling into dust.

There is no wind, but there is movement. The crystals sway without moving. The ground sings—a low, constant drone like the sound of a thousand distant voices chanting in unison, though the words are too slow to hear. Sometimes the drone shifts pitch, and when it does, the whole landscape seems to ripple, briefly revealing glimpses of other places folded beneath: a forest of obsidian trees, a river of molten iron, a city where the buildings breathe. These glimpses last no longer than a heartbeat, but they leave a residue—a faint scent of ozone, an aftertaste of copper in the mouth that never comes.

This is a city—or was once. Its bones are everywhere, though they look nothing like bones. Spires of fused salt-roof rise crookedly from the ground, their surfaces etched with patterns that shift when unobserved. Streets of compacted ash spiral inward like the grooves of a vast record, recording whispers no longer audible. Buildings without doors or windows—or perhaps only doors and windows, no walls between—cluster in clusters that defy gravity, leaning against each other for support in a language of angles that precludes Euclidean thought. The architecture is primitive yet precise, hand-hewn with tools that were hands themselves once, or thoughts given stone. Everything is built to channel the heat, to capture it, to worship it. Great funnels carved into the ground point always toward the low sun, their interiors polished to mirror smoothness, reflecting and amplifying the light until it becomes something else—a beam, a blade, a path.

In the center—if this place has a center, which it denies—stands the Tower. It is taller than the tallest crystal, taller than the sky would allow if the sky were not a lie here. It is not built but grown, layer upon layer of salt and time, its surface encrusted with glyphs that crawl like living things. From its summit pours a sound, continuous, unbearably loud yet paradoxically soothing: the voice of the city dreaming itself awake. Those who hear it—though there are none here to hear—would call it music, or prayer, or madness. It is all these and none.

There are no people. No animals. No insects to crawl, no birds to wing. Only the crystals and the tower and the heat and the song. Yet the emptiness is alive with presence. Watch long enough and you will see them—the shapes that flicker at the edge of sight, moving between the crystal spires, always facing away. They have no substance, only outlines, traced in heat shimmer and half-forgotten memory: figures assembling and disassembling themselves endlessly, constructing something that never quite forms. They do not notice you. They do not notice anything outside their endless task.

The rules here are few but absolute:

1. Do not speak aloud. The heat will steal your words and grow something from them you will not recognize.
2. Do not touch the crystals directly. They remember heat too well and may mistake yours for a lover.
3. Do not sleep. Dreams here are contagious, and the city has been dreaming long enough.
4. Do not seek the center. The center is not a place but a direction, and it leads everywhere but out.

Time moves strangely, as if reluctant. An hour might pass in a heartbeat; a heartbeat might stretch to contain ages. The sun never moves, yet shadows—real shadows, not heat-ghosts—sometimes appear at impossible angles, cast by nothing, stretching fingers toward the tower as if pleading or warning.

And always, the song. The city sings itself awake, and in singing, dreamt itself into being. It sings still, though none remain to hear. Perhaps it sings for you, now that you are here. Perhaps it has always been singing, waiting for ears to listen. Perhaps—

—but to think such thoughts too long is to begin to join in the song, softly at first, then louder, until your own voice becomes indistinguishable from the heat and the crystals and the tower and the endless, empty, crowded song of the salt dreaming city beneath the unmoving sun. 

Walk carefully. Walk quietly. Walk, if you can, without leaving footprints. The ground remembers here, and it has grown lonely for stories.
