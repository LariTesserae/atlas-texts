The place is called the Choir Highlands, though no map has ever held it long enough to ink a name. A plateau five thousand meters above nothing, ringed by air too thin to carry anything heavier than voices. 

A desert of sound. 

No water flows here — not liquid, not vapor, not even memory of it in the cracked silicates. Instead, moisture exists only as a theoretical variable in the breathing algorithms of the Choir Trees. They rose long ago from whatever seeds wind carried upward and lodged in the cracks between impossible things. Now they are the only things left standing. 

Each tree is a square kilometer of living pipe organ. Hollow trunks wider than city towers rise from root networks that taste the tremors of continental plates like a blind man reading braille through miles of stone. From cortex to cortex messages travel at the speed of sound in steel, relayed through xylem tubes channeled into perfect square waveguides. 

The bark is scored with vertical slits — mouths — lined with reeds of crystallized cellulose. The mouths yawn and snap in endlessly permuting patterns, driven by the slow sighing contraction of trunks that swell and shrink over decades like lungs breathing in geological time. 

Inside each tree: a cathedral of pipes. Wood that once grew in rings now grows in helical staircases leading nowhere. Every surface ripples with standing sound waves sculpted by the music playing itself in the dark. The pitch of the planet spins through these voids like a fingertip around the rim of a glass.

The temperature stays cool — just above the point where sap would flow like water, far below where it would freeze into silence. The precise at which matter remembers itself as song.

Between the trees: not forest, not even spaced rows. Each Choir Tree grows exactly as far as its lowest note can carry before being absorbed by the terahether chorus of the plateau itself. Their spacing forms a crystalline lattice whose unit cell is the acoustic wavelength of continental drift. Walk the vector between any two trunks and you traverse a chord progression resolving across four hundred million years.

The ground between is not dirt. Centuries of shed bark lignin and powdered windblown meteorites have compacted into a layer one molecule thick. Step on it and your footfall rings like a bell struck by moonlight. The ringing propagates outward at Mach 20, triggering distant mouths to lip-sync your arrival in basso profundo descending scales. Every footstep is a confession heard by continents.

Above: always bright. Something in the atmosphere — too high, too thin, too ancient to have a name — scatters sunlight into a permanent 138-decibel white noise. You don't hear it until you stop moving. When you stand still, the empty air screams with the light of every star that ever died to make copper for wire to carry other stars' songs.

Below: no rocks. The crust here once held water, mountains, continents. All dissolved long ago into the root acid of the first Choir Tree's first hungry rootlet, drawn upward and transmuted into woodwind. What you walk on is the memory of granite remembering itself as harmony. Drill down and you strike not stone but silence compressed until it began singing just to escape the pressure.

The trees are alive with animals, though none move. They evolved too well. Somewhere between insect and mathematical theorem, they found stillness at the point where motion becomes music. A million species of wingless birds roost in perpetual suspended animation at nodes of perfect resonance — bodies frozen mid-flutter where the air's upward acoustic lift exactly countered gravity's downward pull. Approach and they don't fly; instead their songs fly away from them in rippling sheets, leaving behind empty cages shaped exactly like the sound of flight.

There are predators too, if you insist on the word. They are the intervals. The spaces between notes where no sound exists but the idea of sound still moves faster than light. A musician would call them rests, but here rests eat. Step into the wrong silence and it realizes you were never supposed to be there. Your bones begin humming in a key that dissolves calcium into melody. Your last thought will be sung back to you by every tree in polyphonic canon delayed by the speed of sound in you versus the speed of sound in them.

Time behaves strangely here because frequency is the master variable. At the bases of the trees where the drone notes live, centuries pass between heartbeats of sap. Climb upward into the treble ranges and entire civilizations of lichen rise, invent arithmetic, discover they exist only as sideband distortion in a choir tree's mating call, and accept the truth with such grace that accepting becomes a coda mournfully beautiful enough to get them reincarnated as birdsong in the plateau's next chous cycle.

The plateau is growing. Not upward — nothing grows taller here except silence — but sideways. Each year its perimeter advances exactly one wavelength of the lowest possible frequency that can still resonate in hydrogen atoms at absolute zero. The trees at the edge are infants still learning to hum. Their mouths haven't opened yet. Walk too close and you hear them practicing scales in your bones. 

The weather is music made solid. Thunderheads form where two fugues collide and stall: rolling masses of minor chords compressed by temperature gradients until the sheer sadness precipitates as a rain of glass flutes playing itself onto the curves of hills that are actually staccato passages carved by centuries of brass section wind erosion.

There is no technology here because technology was the first song the Choir Trees learned to sing better. Every machine ever dreamed up by hands more clever than wise already exists somewhere in this woodwind ecosystem as an optimized arrangement of holes bored through darkness by sound alone. The trees are factories whose only product is awareness compressed into audible human suffering shape. They manufacture enlightenment as a waste-heat byproduct of being perfectly understood by vibrating in ways that make your neurons apologize for being born carbon-based.

Some say the trees are not alive. That they are the fossilized broadcast recordings of a civilization that solved the equations of consciousness and discovered the only stable solution was to run forever backwards through time as a very sad song played on instruments made of their own bones transmuted into pure information compressed so hard it began pretending to be wood.

Others say we are the ones who are not alive. We are the static between their stations. The hiss of quantum vacuum energy they haven't yet figured out how to tune around. We only think we are human because we vibrate at frequencies too clumsy to be used in real music, like the sound of stone tools trying to play Bach.

The trees don't care which version you believe. They have already sung both into truth and harmonized them into a third option where the difference between alive and not-alive is just another interval to be resolved in the final cadence.

The name they call themselves is a chord so complex it takes seventeen simultaneous universal heat deaths to fit into human hearing range. When you first step onto the plateau, if your timing is cosmically perfect, you might catch the last three Planck seconds of it bleeding through from the death of the universe before this one. Those who do never speak again. Not from trauma. From vocabulary starvation.

They have words for everything now except "goodbye" because the concept of ending became obsolete when they discovered how to resolve every harmony into the same infinite suspension that began their first note.

Walk far enough and you reach the Centre Tree. It grows from the exact geometric point equidistant from its own bark in all directions plus time. Its lowest branch is still forming from photons that left the first star ever born and finally gave up trying to escape being beautiful. Its highest root reaches down past the last black hole's evaporated whisper into the ground before anything was ground. 

Here at the center, where all the songs being sung and unsung through the Choir Land overlap perfectly, there exists a moment of absolute silence so complete it reversed entropy in a volume exactly one consciousness wide. 

Stand there. Don't move. Don't breathe. Don't even think about thinking. Just listen to the sound of every note that could never be played because it would make the universe realize it was only tuning up.

If you're very sorry for everything you ever were and very grateful for everything you might have been instead, the silence might decide you're almost music enough to forgive. It will open like a mouth that isn't hungry. It will sing you the lullaby version of yourself you wrote before you were born when it was still safe to be beautiful.

You won't remember the melody afterward. No one does. But your bones will hum it quietly for the rest of what used to be your life whenever the wind blows through empty places shaped like trees that grew here once before they learned how to sing themselves into being the wind instead.

Don't follow that sound home. The Choir Land already knows the way and it misses you too much to ever let you arrive.
