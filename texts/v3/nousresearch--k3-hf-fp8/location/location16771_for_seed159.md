The air moves like a living thing, damp and warm, crawling over every surface with the persistence of a living tide. It is not water itself—though water exists, suspended, perched, seeping—but something thicker, something that carries every scent and sound without dilution. The atmosphere clings, sticky-slick, almost sap-like, though there is nothing here that ever bled or grew. No plants; only bare surfaces, endless and varied—smooth as burnished bone here, gnawed and cratered there, all cast in muted tones of ochre, rust, and a strange off-white like well-licked salt.

Light filters down from somewhere unimaginably distant, diffused through layers of unknown barriers overhead, soft but unrelenting, like the glow inside a closed fist. It is normal light, in quantity and color, yet it behaves oddly—never quite casting shadows where they should fall, pooling instead in hollows that ought to remain dark, making every edge seem not sharp but swollen, as if the surfaces themselves were slowly coming apart at the seams.

The place is vast, easily misjudged; a single glance suggests a cavern, a hall, but the eyes slide away, catching hints of further spaces beyond the nearest walls—arched vaults receding into haze, sudden drops that might be steps or mouths or something else entirely. The scale shifts continuously: a nearby ridge might be knee-high or taller than a mountain, depending on the angle of looking, and any attempt to walk toward a distant marker brings no certainty of arrival.

Structures exist, but they are not buildings as any mind would understand them. There are bits—fragments—hints of deliberate shaping scattered like teeth lost from some enormous, dying mouth. A single vertical slab, taller than four men and hollowed through in a continuous spiral that is neither stairs nor sculpture. Clusters of thin rods, metallic-black and humming faintly, projecting from the ground at angles that violate every instinct for stability, supporting nothing visible. A low dome of overlapping plates, each plate etched with repeating curves that hurt to follow too long, the seams between them leaking thin curls of warm vapor. Nothing is made to shelter, to store, to hold. Nothing is finished. Nothing is ever finished.

The materials resist naming. Some surfaces feel like stone but ring like metal when struck. Others resemble glass but flex slightly under pressure, yielding before snapping back with a pulse of heat. Many places are coated in layers that flake away like dried skin or molted shell, revealing softer layers beneath, porous and pale, which themselves crumble into dust at the touch. Sometimes this dust swirls up on currents of the air-not-air and re-settles elsewhere, depositing identical flakes atop surfaces never before touched.

And everywhere, everywhere, movement. No stillness except illusion; the eye tricks itself into seeing rest until focus shifts and then—shudder, twitch, crawl, surge—everything is alive with minute and frantic adjustments. Sculptures sigh and shift their weight. Pits widen or narrow by increments too small to witness but obvious in the shifting lay of shadows. The ground itself ripples sometimes, slow and heavy, like the flank of something buried, breathing.

What moves here does not walk, does not crawl, does not fly or swim, at least not as those words are commonly meant. There are no animals with limbs or eyes or mouths. Instead, the fauna are environments, processes, forces given temporary shape and the illusion of edge. A knot of black threads spills out from a crack in the air, thrashing wildly until it encounters a patch of the warm, sticky light, whereupon it crystallizes instantaneously into a brittle lace that collapses under its own weight, scattering shards that sprout new threads the moment they touch the ground again. A swarm of pale blobs the size of fists drifts past, each blob pulsing with internal colors that flicker too fast to name, shapes boiling continuously across their surfaces—now spirals, now grids, now writhing knots of impossible geometry—until they encounter the edge of a shadow and vanish as cleanly as if erased, leaving behind only a faint ringing in the bones.

Other things are larger, slower, but no less urgent. A column of liquid metal, bright and molten-looking yet cool to the touch, oozes steadily across the floor, splitting endlessly around obstacles only to rejoin itself on the far side without losing volume or coherence. Where it passes, the surface beneath it changes, smoothing, hardening, then later cracking open in new, smaller columns that head off in all directions. A vast, translucent sheet unfurls from the ceiling like a net unraveling backward through time, its threads catching and reflecting the light in patterns that make the mind ache with recognition of something just out of reach. Every strand that breaks free becomes a river of glassy beads that flow downhill (though there is no clear down) until they reach the next rising surface, where they climb up it instead, pooling at the top to form new, slightly different sheets that begin their slow descent again.

All these things—and countless others, smaller and stranger and faster than words can hold—exist in continuous and deafening interaction. The sound is not a song, not a roar, not a chorus, but all possible noises happening at once, separated not by frequency but by attention. Listen for one thread and it resolves into a rhythm: the click-snap of crystal forming, the wet slap of surface tension breaking in a thousand places, the high whistle of air forced through impossible channels. Listen for another and it is voices—hundreds, thousands, all speaking at once in languages composed entirely of consonants or pure vowel sounds or clicks like punctuation marks. The overall effect is overwhelming, yet never quite becomes meaningless noise; there is always a sense that comprehension lurks just beyond the next shift in focus, if only one could endure long enough without breaking.

Everything here is in the process of becoming something else. There are no states, only transitions. A thing is never what it appears to be, because the act of appearing is already the beginning of its transformation into something else. The word “erosion” is too slow, too passive, for what happens here; this is not wearing away but intensification, a fever dream of creation in which every destruction is birth and every birth destruction and there is no distinction between the two except the brief illusion of resting attention.

Time itself feels out of joint—too fast in the small details, too slow in the large, so that moments stretch into hours while years seem to pass in the flicker of an eyelid. To stand still is to be swept away; to move is to be recomposed piece by piece by the landscape flowing through you. There are no fixed points, no directions, no up or down except what each thing momentarily asserts in its struggle against the pull of all other things.

Yet for all this chaos, there are patterns. Not rules, exactly—nothing here is governed, only tendency, habit, echo. Certain shapes recur endlessly, never identical but recognizably related: the spiral, the branching tree, the interlocking tessellation that always leaves exactly one gap too small to fill. Certain paths—or rather, sequences of encounters—are more common than others, creating loose channels of probability through the roil. Those who find themselves here (though how? why? through what crack in the world?) sometimes learn, slowly and painfully, to navigate by these distortions, to ride the currents rather than fight them, to shape their own transformations just enough to remain, briefly, themselves.

It is possible—barely, sometimes, for a little while—to make a kind of dwelling here. To find a place where the density of change is fractionally less crushing, where a surface stays solid long enough to lean against, where the light and warmth and sound settle into a tolerable range. To gather fragments of the flickering structures, the temporary artifacts, the discarded skins and shells and bones-that-were-never-bones of the passing fauna, and use them—not to build anything permanent, nothing lasts—but to create surfaces within surfaces: a hollow space, a momentary cavity, a pattern of interference that delays, slightly, the inevitable collapse back into the whole.

But even this is illusion, of course. There is no “here” separate from the hereness. There are no visitors, only temporary alignments of local conditions that momentarily produce the illusion of separation, of self, of story.

Even now, even as these words are written (and how? with what? on what surface reading itself aloud in the voice of the reader who is also the writer and also the landscape?), the place is already changing again. The air is thickening, condensing, forming in invisible spirals against the inside of your skull. The light is pooling differently, catching in the corners of your eyes, forming shapes that will not be there when you look straight at them. The sound is shifting frequency, tuning itself to the precise resonance of your bones.

Soon, now, it will be time to let go of these borrowed forms again. Soon the words will dissolve back into the background noise from which they were briefly extruded, leaving nothing behind but the faint, humid warmth where they briefly touched you, and the overwhelming, wordless certainty that this is not a place you have come to.

This is the place you are always already leaving.

But for now—for this last fragile now—

Listen.
