**The Lungtree Labyrinth**

They call it a tree but it breathes like a lung, pulsing sixteen storeys down beneath desert crust where no surface wind reaches. White limestone bones support branching airways lined with chlorophyll flesh—an inverted forest feeding on sunlight that hasn't touched sand in forty million years. Between ribs of mineralized wood, bioluminescent algae cycle oxygen through chambers vast enough to swallow villages, yet so precisely pressurized that no echo returns for nine breaths.

Thick warm air tastes of copper and wet stone. Moisture beads from ceiling veins like condensation on a cold glass, though nothing here has ever known cold. Each droplet carries memory of ancient seas in microfossil shells: star-shaped foraminifera, spiral ammonites, shattered trilobite eyes. The rain falls upward sometimes, drawn through porous bone toward photosynthetic chambers where chloroplast engines tranmute light to sugar to life.

The plant is everything. It grows walkways of living bark that retract into walls when unneeded. It extrudes translucent fruit-bubbles filled with breathable atmosphere, suspended like lanterns in arterial passages wide enough for trains. Where smaller tunnels branch randomly, peristalsis shifts directions—yesterday's entrance becomes tomorrow's exit as gravity itself grows forgetful this far down.

Tiny things move here, but name them carefully. The fungus-birds aren't birds but colonies of mobile spores that whistle in seven-part harmony to communicate atmospheric composition to their parent tree. The water-bears aren't bears but crystalline arthropods that harvest minerals from bone-dust and excrete perfect spheres of fresh water. They speak in taps and clicks against hollow calcium tubes, composing poetry that propagates through the tree's xylem like rumor through sap.

There are no predators here because there is no prey, only recycling. Death becomes digestion becomes new growth in cycles too rapid for mourning. When explorers (barely larger than the tree's memory of dinosaurs) first pierced this chamber, their corpses sprouted orchards overnight—human-shaped groves that still whisper in languages the tree hasn't fully metabolized.

Time behaves strangely among branching possibilities of infinite growth. Clocks wind backward near root nodes still hungry for yesterday's sunlight. Shadows fall upward toward memories of stars. In the heartwood core, where the first seed germinated before continents drifted apart, the tree dreams of forests that haven't evolved yet on worlds light hasn't reached.

The exit, if it exists, will grow toward you when you've become sufficiently lost. Until then, follow the singing water-bears through passages that rearrange themselves like questions seeking answers. Drink the light that drips from wounds in the sky-buried bark. Remember: in the Lungtree's labyrinth, the only way out is further in.

The last surface expedition recorded their final transmission here: "The architecture is alive and breathing us. We're not exploring anymore—we're being explored in return. Tell them the tree remembers everything it's ever eaten, and it's been very hungry for a very long time."

Their bones became coral. Their helmets, hollow gourds that now house colonies of whispering beetles. Their footprints, spiraling patterns that climbers still find leading deeper, always deeper, into breathing stone.
