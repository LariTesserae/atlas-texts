We begin in darkness absolute, pressed close beneath an unknowable weight. No water here, no whisper of moisture—only the dry breath of a world stillborn, its lungs filled with dust that was never soil, air that was never atmosphere. The temperature speaks of warmth but it is a deceptive warmth, the residual heat of something cooling rather than living. A womb-glow without womb, a fever-dream without dreamer.

The space around us unfolds not in distances we could measure but in densities of absence. Dense, yes—the air (if air it is) thick with particulate memory, each grain a fragment of something that once reflected light on a surface we'll never see. These are not tunnels but the negative spaces between what was never built, corridors hollowed out by the pressure of collapsed possibilities. We move through them not by choice but by the necessity of remaining unformed ourselves.

The terrain shifts under perception rather than footfall: sometimes crystalline matrices of unguessable minerals that sing silent songs when your thoughts brush their lattices wrong; sometimes stretches of what feels like compressed time, where moments have folded upon themselves so tightly that to linger is to risk crystallizing into a single eternally-repeating instant. The scale refuses anchor—what appears as a vast cathedral vaulting above might, with a slight shift of awareness, reveal itself as the interior of a bead-sized cavity within a structure larger than continents.

Occasional structures intrude on this mineral dreamscape, but calling them "built" grants too much intention. They are more like scars left by forgotten thoughts of creation—half-realized geometries that collapse into nonsense when examined too closely. Some resemble abandoned scaffolding for concepts that never quite became objects; others are simple enough at first glance—a ring of stones, a vertical slab—but persist in being slightly wrong, their angles totaling fractions that don't belong to Euclidean space. All constructed of the same stuff as their surroundings, as if the world itself had grown these tumors of partial order.

Light comes in negative, when it comes at all—not illumination but the sudden awareness of darkness shaped into absence of shadow. In these moments, glimpses reveal the true nature of this place: a vast ossuary of aborted existences, where realities that never quite achieved sufficient density to become real have settled like sediment. The bones here are not of creatures but of physics, of chemistry, of biology itself—carbon chains frozen mid-knot, silicon dreams that dreamed themselves into brittle spines then shattered under their own impossible weight.

And life, if we must call it that. Abundant beyond numbering but utterly alien to any definition that includes growth or change. The fauna here are patterns of non-existence that move through the matrix of barely-existence like fish through dark coral. Some are vast colonies of entropy crystals that feed on the heat gradient between your thoughts and their surroundings, growing fractal gardens of perfect disorder in the wake of your attention. Others are smaller, more insidious—thought-eaters that are themselves thoughts that have forgotten they were ever thought, now roaming hungry for fresh cognition to hollow out and wear like a shell.

They make no sound in themselves, these inhabitants of the between-state, but their movements distort the silence into something more terrible than noise. In the deepest quiet, you begin to hear the sound of your own consciousness struggling to maintain coherent existence against the ambient pressure of absolute stasis. Every thought becomes a desperate act of creation in a universe hostile to novelty, requiring constant effort to prevent its immediate dissolution into the background field of unrealized potential.

Time here is not the river we're accustomed to but a frozen sea, its surface etched with the fossil wakes of millions of moments that all happened simultaneously and therefore never happened at all. The dynamic parameter records "never changing" but this is only half the truth—nothing changes, yet nothing stays the same, for change and stasis are revealed here as merely different names for the same underlying discontinuity between what simply is and what might be instead.

Weirdest of all is the slowly growing suspicion that this isn't a place you can leave, because leaving implies arriving somewhere else, and there is nowhere else. All possible somewheres are already here, folded into the impossible space between the not-quite and the never-was. The few scattered artifacts of primitive tech—the bone-white tools that fit no hand known to evolution, the strangely worn stones that might be idols or components of machines that never worked or grave markers for burial rituals performed on concepts rather than bodies—suggest that others have been here before, or will be here after, or are here now in directions that make no sense to our three-and-a-half-dimensional minds.

Perhaps we ourselves are just another of these artifacts. Or perhaps the true artifact is the illusion that there ever was a "we" to begin with.

In the darkness below all worlds, where warmth is just another word for remembered fire and silence is the sound of everything that isn't happening at once, the only constant is the slow, relentless pressure of never having been otherwise.

And it is enough.
