The place begins at the limit where stone forgets how to fall. From orbit—or what remains of orbit—it must look like a single frozen wave caught mid-crash against the last scrap of gravity left to this dead worldlet. Here the horizon is not a line but a function of anxiety: if you stare too long it tilts, then corkscrews, then loops back to show you the soles of your own boots.

Water exists only as a habit the rocks haven't shaken. Milky films bead in the angles where basalt meets itself at acute angles impossible before space-time cracked. Tilt a facet just right and the condensation droplets tremble uphill, hesitate, merge into a single sphere that floats away like a hesitant thought. Rain never fell here—rain doesn't know the steps to that dance. Instead the air itself condenses in your lungs when you exhale, each breath departing as a small intact sculpture of ice-crystal breathwork that drifts off to join the faint airborne reef of forgotten sighs cooling above the plain.

Vegetation is just the fossil memory of a bargain that never closed: black glass tubules half-exhumed from the wind-polished scree, each one sealed at both ends around nothing more promising than ancient air. If you break one open the gas inside is from a simpler physics, monocolor green and too wet to breathe. Nothing grows from the shards.

Cold here is not a temperature but a proof-theory. The ground gropes for your warmth with equations of conductive despair, then loses interest halfway through the second step. There are no seasons—this because the word for 'change' dissolved when the last verb drifted free of the law of excluded middle. Now absolute zero and tepid anguish are just two neighborhoods in the same discontinuous manifold. Your left boot might be at trial boil while the right toes frost together in a single stony fruit; equilibrium is considered vulgar.

Elevation has gone senile. The map shows true altitude as a color, smell and blameless anecdote. What you call 'up' switches allegiance every thirty paces like a wingbeat too tired to finish forming a bird. Walk long enough and you might notice the stars are beneath your feet now, frosted on the underside of the crystal crust you mistook for sky-rock.

Erosion stopped apologizing centuries ago. What remains has only the shapes that boredom eventually gives violence: arches that support nothing except shadow; columns whose fluting spells out jokes in dead geometries the wind is too slow to laugh at. Everything is finalized yet nothing is smooth—edges and corners negotiate treaties no sand grain ever learned to sign.

Scale is generous but forgetful. You could pace a thousand strides toward that tower of uplifted shale and arrive calmer but no closer, or stoop to flick a pebble and see it tumble away across an entire afternoon of diminishing valleys that you swear weren't sutured into the ground when you last looked. Distance here obeys courtesy more than compromise.

Density is what happens when the crowd all remembers the same betrayal at once. The plain is littered almost entirely with the absences of monoliths: slabs that removed themselves so thoroughly they left their own hollow weight crouching in the air where they forgot to stand. Walk through one and you briefly become the reason nothing is surrounding you.

Built things are exactly as scarce as excuses. Occasionally the crust buckles into something too proud to be accidental—a lintel balanced on nothing, framing the view of another lintel, framing nothing again. Inside the frame, if you stand still long enough for 'stand' and 'still' to overlap, you can watch gray stones building themselves into a ruin in reverse, then unremember the skill mid-wall. Every course rises only to the exact height of someone's abandoned hope, then paws at the air with last bricks that never arrived from the quarry that moved away before the mountain did.

Technology is a rumor that fled on the same night the animals thought better of having bodies. No tools, not even the ache in your hand where a tool ought to fit. The closest cousin to invention is the way two shadows will sometimes try to trade places without swapping the light that casts them both, fail awkwardly and leave behind a third shadow that haunts cheaper realities.

Light is a guilty conscience fleeing a crime scene that repeats itself faster than remembrance. The local sun, if it remembers the appointment, pushes a dim bulb of what-might-have-been brightness up over an edge of world that hasn't decided if it feels like being east today. The light arrives horizontal and ashamed, skims across the rocks like a pebble trying not to leave ripples, and runs aground on the next contradiction before it can learn the color yellow. Everything you see is lit from below by reflections of shadows that haven't been cast yet.

Fauna are the punctuation marks left after a long sentence of extinction cleared its throat. In the distance (choose one) something with too many knees is trying to decide whether to evolve ears or apologize. Once every few hours the wind—if this is wind, and if hours—might catch on a snag of reality and tear loose a wet flapping sound that could be either laughter or a wing except neither is licensed to exist in this jurisdiction. The real population is right behind you, has always been right behind you, and never thinks to move unless you do.

Weirdness is not a spice added to the recipe—Weirdness owns the restaurant, wrote the menu in disappearing ink on yesterday's dream, ate the chef, and is now politely asking if you wouldn't prefer to become the next course. Geometry here has a complaint department open only during imaginary numbers o'clock. Cause and effect still correspond but they do it by mail, and the post carried away the alphabet last spring. You can walk west and exit east younger than you were, except 'you' is currently on loan to the terrain as collateral for a promise nobody made.

Sound is what silence keeps hidden in its second basement, turned up too loud by a deaf man who thinks volume is a version of hearing. Beneath the expected nothing hums a continuous nerve-shredding chord played on instruments made of all the frequencies that were outlawed in better-behaved cosmoses—the drone of an electric mountain range tuning up for a concert that already ended before sound was invented. Occasionally it modulates into a sharp slamming door that isn't there, or the wet pop of a continent breaking its promise. If you scream the rocks answer with perfect sympathy, imitating the exact shape of your scream in thermodynamic gestures that never turn back into noise.

Dynamic is what they called the war between Same and Other before truce, betrayal and reconciliation collapsed into a single long silence too exhausted to end or continue. Everything that happens has already been happening since before it started and will have never stopped by the time it finishes not happening now. Nothing moves, nothing rests, everything repeats itself a second time for the benefit of the first time that hadn't happened by the third time yet. The only reliable clock is the slow drift of your own disbelief toward the horizon—an horizon you can see clearly because it never comes closer and never leaves.

Stand here long enough and you too will fit perfectly. Walk away and the place will remember you forever, exactly where you aren't standing anymore.
