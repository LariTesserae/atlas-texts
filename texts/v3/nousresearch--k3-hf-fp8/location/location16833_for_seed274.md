In the low places where the land dips its head beneath the remembering, there is a breathing world of water-stone. Not living in the way meadows live or forests breathe, but alive in the way mirrors move when you turn them to light. A drowned cathedral of granite and gneiss lies kneeling here, its arches full of warm water instead of air, its nave flooded to the height of standing spines.

The water itself moves like a creature that has forgotten how to sleep. It rises through pores invisible to the unpatient eye, seeping upward through cracks older than bone. At night—though here night is only a suggestion, a slight thickening of the skinned light—it gathers in pools that reflect nothing because nothing moves above them. By day—which is similar but warmer—these pools shrink into themselves, leaving behind lace patterns of mineral salt that crumble at the breath of passing thought.

This is a place without plants because it remembers before plants. When the first green thought was still dreaming itself into being, this place had already settled into its skin of stone and water, its slow marriage of heat and weight. Instead of moss on the north sides of things there are barnacles of quartz that grew there during some forgotten ice. In place of lichens, colonies of microscopic crystals arrange themselves according to laws older than taste or hunger.

The temperature holds steady at the point where blood and sea water remember being cousins. Touch any surface—wet stone rounded by centuries of caresses too gentle to call erosion, the slick lip of a tide-pool that has never known moon—the heat transfers instantly, as though the whole place wears one skin that someone set to keep at body temperature eons ago and then forgot to adjust.

Sound here is mainly the sound of water thinking about water. A drip that falls once every thousand heartbeats, exactly. The slow inhalation of stone drinking from below. Occasionally, if you kneel with your ear against the right crack and hold your own breath long enough, you might hear what could be voices—but they are only the shape that memory takes when it passes through stone and water without finding anything to attach to.

The scale bends perception like light through water. What appears from one angle as a room you could cross in twelve steps becomes, when you begin to walk, a journey between continents that drift apart slower than continents drift. The distance between stones is measured not in feet but in lifetimes of water patient enough to dissolve them.

Built things exist here like dreams forgotten upon waking. A wall of fitted stones stands waist-high and then stops, as though the builder remembered mid-gesture that walls are for keeping things in or out, and here nothing comes or goes unless it is already part of the coming and going. A spiral of small stones gathers around a pool that holds no reflection but that of its own remembering. Three steps carved into a single block lead down into water and then continue, carved further still, beyond where light or sight can follow.

The technology is primitive in the way river stones are primitive: perfected by endless iteration until nothing remains that is not essential. A wedge of flint wedged exactly where two stones meet to guide the water's singing into a different key. A trough worn smooth by water that carried away the not-trough until only trough remained. A single copper wire green with age stretched taut between two points that turn out, if you follow them, to be the same point seen from different times.

Light arrives indirectly, having touched and been touched by so many surfaces that by the time it reaches any eye that might still remember how to open, it carries only the memory of brightness rather than brightness itself. It gathers in certain hollows like cats seeking warmth and avoids others as though those places have learned how to drink it. Shadows here are not absence but texture, raised instead of recessed, places where the stone has grown thicker through long practice of being what casts them.

Fauna are rare because this is a place that has solved problems most places solve by having many things that eat each other. Still, if you sit perfectly still for long enough—how long is difficult to measure here, where time flows like the water, slow and sideways—something might touch the edge of your perception: the brief whorl in water that remembers being disturbed by the passing of something that was almost a fish but decided against it. The sudden alignment of crystals that suggests but never quite becomes a pattern. The momentary warmth in the stone beneath your hand that could be blood remembering heat if stone had blood or heat could remember.

Everything here is held in the exact tension between what it is and what it is becoming, but because the becoming happens slower than change, the place appears to be still. Still the water rises. Still the stone drinks. Still the light gathers itself together in certain places and forgets itself in others. Still the walls stop where they stopped because stopping was always what they were reaching toward. Still the steps continue downward into water and then continue further, carved by hands equally patient and absent, leading nowhere and everywhere at the same time, which in this place are finally the same direction.
