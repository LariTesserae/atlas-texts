The ground pulses gently, rhythmically, a slow heartbeat that rises up through bare soles and trembles in the bones of small animals burrowing too shallow. It is not quite mud, nor stone, nor flesh, but something that remembers all three and has chosen none: a surface crusted with translucent plates like fingernails grown too long and abandoned, flexing faintly where unseen currents pass beneath. Water sits in every crease and fold of it—never running, never still—oozing upward in beads that swell into pearls and then burst back into the pores of the land like shy eyes blinking open to look and quickly shut.

Above, there is no sky. There is instead an inverted forest.

Roots thicker than rivers hang down from a ceiling that cannot be seen, vanishing into the breathing dark. Each root drips constantly—not water, but something thicker, luminous with a ghostly violet phosphor that hisses softly when it touches the ground. Where the drops accumulate, life sprouts instantly: coils of vine ending in mouths that whisper wetly, mushrooms with gills like feathered lungs that flare in and out as if tasting the air they exhale, patches of moss whose fibers twitch in semaphore patterns no linguist has survived to decode.

The scale is wrong. Everything is too close and too far at once. Walk five steps and the nearest root-cluster might recede to the horizon; turn back, and you will find yourself standing beneath it again, its dripping now loud as drums inside your skull.

Temperature stays constant: warm as the inside of a mouth that has just eaten something alive. The heat does not radiate from any source; it presses inward from all directions, a gentle but inexorable embrace that slowly unscrews the will to move quickly. Sweat blooms and is licked away by the air itself, which moves with purposeful tongues seeking salt.

There are no walls, yet every direction is corridor. The roots braid and unbraid overhead, forming tunnels that fork and fork again with the random precision of frost cracking glass. Between them hang curtains of small creatures—bat-winged, skull-faced, wearing bodies like robes half-dropped—whose eyes glow not with light but with absence: two round holes punched through space showing something darker than even this place behind. They do not fly so much as levitate in place, wings beating in lazy, asynchronous rhythms that generate the air's strange currents. Occasionally one will cough up a pebble of pure sound—a chime, a scream, a lullaby sung backwards through broken teeth. These sounds do not fade; they lodge in the ears of the living and begin to hatch.

Below the breathing crust, things move in synchrony with the pulse. Blind worms with bones of flexible crystal thread through channels filled with warm, viscous darkness. They do not eat; they absorb. Knowledge, memory, the shape of fear itself. Occasionally one presses too close to the membrane above and a section of ground bulges upward like a bruise. If touched in that moment, it will remember your fingerprints forever, and later—when you have forgotten this place entirely—it will birth something wearing your hands.

The density is unbearable and ecstatic. Every cubic meter swarms with presences: spores that speak in bursts of perfume, mites that build cities on the backs of dancing midges, networks of mycelia transmitting dreams at the speed of thought. To close your eyes is worse than seeing; the darkness behind your lids becomes suddenly crowded with the branching diagrams of every living thing's future deaths.

Yet there is order, if you know how to listen. Beneath the deafening chorus of individual hungers runs a deeper music: the slow, grinding bass of continents not yet born, the high pure whistle of stars learning to burn. The pulse quickens slightly now, and everywhere the dripping roots respond by lowering themselves by inches all at once, graceful as dancers bowing before the song begins.

This place has not been here long. It is still deciding what rules to keep. Gravity is optional today, murder is merely a suggestion, and time flows uphill in some neighborhoods just to see what will happen. The only certainty is change itself, occurring at a rate that would drive a clock to madness. A seed dropped here might grow into a tree, a cathedral, a new mythology, or simply grow hungry and eat the hand that dropped it—all before it hits the ground, which is already reaching up softly to catch it, lick it clean, and begin again.
