Rain never falls here, but the air holds water like a secret. Everything breathes it in—walls, walkways, the undersides of leaves wide as ship sails. Mist rises from vents patterned like ancient script across the floor of the sunken city, reheated by pipes that snake a kilometer down into thermal bones older than the builders' names. Moisture beads on metal, on stone, on skin, but never gathers enough to drip. The city drinks back every drop through capillary brickwork before thirst can begin.

Vegetation did not reclaim this urban lattice—vegetation was its architect. Towers knit themselves from living wood grain stiffened with lignin-harder than concrete and lined inside with soft vascular pith that transports water, nutrients, light itself. Streets are twin tunnels of aerial roots braided overhead into vaulted arches, each pair grown centuries apart and grafted at the meeting seam by patient arborist-engineers whose tools were song and serum in equal measure. Windows are translucent leaves, shuttering to heartbeat rhythms. Lamps are bioluminescent fungi pinned in brass filigree crowns, dimming only when the city's collective dream drifts deeper.

The scale deceives: map-makers insist the outer wall encompasses no more than a village square, yet every turning corridor opens into galleries that feel like cathedral nave or oak hollow simultaneously—a trick of curved sightlines and hollowed interior girth. Density is equally uncertain. At any moment you might walk a kilometer across plaza-spanning fungal mats ankle-deep and never see another walking shape, save for the crooked shadows of the Wind-Traders on their errands above, gliding from tower to tower on sculpted seedpod wings longer than houses. Then a fissure in a seemingly solid wall will fold back like eyelid skin to reveal a workshop thrumming with dozens of low, singing voices, all tending coppery mechanisms that digest leaves into paper-thin glyphs.

Temperature lingers in the cool cradling zone between spring dusk and cellar shade, never settling cold enough for frost, never warming enough for true summer lethargy. Citizens wear layers of fungal silk spun pale green and a copper-red lichen cloth that shifts darker when clouds pass—though real clouds are legend here, seen only as pale smears in memory-tapestries older than memory. Instead, the sky is a shoal of drifting lensleaves, each translucent pane a kilometer overhead, filtering down refracted sunlight into soft focus light that never quite casts a sharp shadow.

Erosion here is historical, finished. The city achieved the shape it wanted centuries ago and hardened itself against further change like a tree trunk after heartwood forms. What wears away now is only dead matter—spent spore husks, shed bark accretions, mineral dust filtered out of the irrigation mist. Entire districts tilt a few degrees off true, pillars grown thicker on one side to compensate for a settling that ended before their grandparents' seeds were pollen dreams. But nothing here is ruinous decay, only planned senescence feeding back into the living whole.

Built and grown intersect in every surface. Bridges begin as seed-cable catwalks stretched by drones of hardened chitin between infant towers; centuries later those same cables have swollen into corridors lined with organ-pipe trunks whose hollows carry the city's messages in bursts of compressed scent. Factories are stomachs scaled to neighborhood size, their peristaltic walls gently pulsing as enzymes break down harvest into plastic resins, medicine, music stored in hardened nectar discs. Each evening shift-change releases a harmonic sigh like distant organ chords as valve orifices dilate and contract.

Tech is visible but never vulgar. Gears are carved from nut-shell steel alloy, teeth interlocked with the tolerance only growth achieves slowly. Motors are muscles electrolytically stimulated to contract on command; when they fail they are fed to shredder grubs and reabsorbed as raw protein. Electricity flickers through mycelial nets woven between floorboards, a nervous system whose sparks are too faint to shock but strong enough to light filamentacious algae strands in decorative filigree when touched.

Fauna is rare, and rarely what it seems. The city’s true wildlife moved indoors when the last forest outside petrified into silica cliffs. Now a visitor might glimpse a flash of purple wingtip belonging to the Paper Owls who roost in library rafters, feeding on ink-fungus and excreting neatly paginated pellets; or catch the scent-song of a dozen pollen-foxes passing invisible through a crowd, their fur cloaked in chromatophore patterns mimicking whatever background they traverse. Keep watch too long and you might notice how your own reflection in a pool of still water slowly blinks back with different-colored eyes—it is only an Arboral Mirrorshade larva testing the fit of its next disguise.

Movement never ceases, but it occurs at overlapping tempos. Humans walk at walking pace. Saplings stretch perceptibly during conversation. Seed-shell airships launch once a season, rising on hydrogen generated by fermenting fruit tanks deep in communal basements, their departure bells tolling through the stem-pipes years before lift-off and years after landing. Beneath all, continent-width root networks exchange chemical news between distant sister cities at the speed of a glacier's dream.

Soundscape layers accordingly: surface quiet punctuated by the occasional creak of adjusting wood, the low hush of mist through vent slits, the rare bird-or-imitator whistle. Mid-level undertones of machinery—digestive gurgles, rhythmic leaf-press thuds, the murmuring hush of paper being sorted by blind grubs reading ink by taste. And deeper, almost felt rather than heard, the slow fibrillation heartbeat of a city dreaming itself continued existence through every live and latticed cell.

Rules here bleed between biological and civic. Ownership extends exactly to the depth your planted bloodlines have interwoven their mycorrhizae with the city's root-lattice—sell a house and you transplant a graft-snippet of your own marrow’s memory into the buyer’s arm-bone. Timekeeping divides into Growth decades, Harvest quarter-years, and constant Now measured in scent-rotations of the ambient air. Privacy is respected physically but remembered chemically—walk any corridor and you leave behind a faint ghost biography in shed skin oils, read only by those trained to empathic botanics, diffused and diluted by morning misting until harmless nostalgic spice.

Weirdness lurks not in spectacle but coherence—every strangeness fitted so perfectly into its niche that questioning feels like forgetting. Visitors wake the first morning thinking their dreams leaked into daylight, second morning dreaming in the city's rhythm, third morning uncertain whether they arrived or merely germinated here from a seed planted centuries before by their own future footsteps departing.

The city is called Thallospvc, a word spoken as a single breathy pull inward through the teeth that mimics the sound roots make drinking. It has no gates, only thresholds where the enclosing root-walls grow perceptibly thinner and the streetlight fungi glow shyly fainter. It has no population count, only the city's current estimate of its probable traits this decade—creative anxious prosperous nostalgic. It has no history books, rings in the oldest tower trunks read aloud by appointed dendrochronologians during drought-pending councils.

Above all, Thallosvpc is patient. It has wagered existence itself on the gamble that if you build a living habitat with enough grace, given enough time it will learn how to dream its inhabitants into creatures who deserve to live inside it. So far, the city dreams on, and the taproot clocks continue turning toward an anniversary measured only in the slow swelling thickness of the world trunk's heartwood core. The anniversary has no scheduled date. The celebration, when it comes, will last exactly as long as a single breath held by every living thing inside the walls—a pause in which the city forgets to exhale, and for one strange suspended moment becomes perfectly still, perfectly alive, perfectly aware of itself as a place that exists entirely because it wanted to be shaped into somewhere worth staying in until the last light dims. Then the city breathes out, mist rises again from the street-vents, and everything continues, damp and green and eternal as growing ever is.
