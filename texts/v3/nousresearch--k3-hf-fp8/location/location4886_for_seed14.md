The descent begins as a gradual compression of all available light. You pass through layers of water that grow colder and heavier until the word "water" itself becomes inadequate—this is a fluid that remembers being stone, a medium that conducts not only sound but obligation. At 1,400 meters below what used to be the surface, your body stops seeking equilibrium and accepts that every cell is now a guest in someone else's architecture.

The plain unfathers itself in all directions. An ancient subsidence comprising the eroded bones of twelve vanished continents, scoured by currents that crawl like glaciers into geologic time. The seabed here is packed—packed beyond the meaning of that word in air-filled languages—packed like information in a dying brain trying to remember every meal it ever ate. Each grain of sediment among the septillions touching septillions maintains twenty-three points of contact with its neighbors and knows their names.

Columns of heavier-than-water brine fall upward in slow motion, inverted rivers that haven't reached their sources since the last extinction of birds. They leave corridors through the density where temperature gradients reveal themselves as shimmering blue ghosts—fossilized boundaries between the world's childhood fevers and its elder cold.

The plain remembers trees. Star-shaped impressions in the compressed clay ten thousand years thick record where individual pine needles fell through freshwater lakes that existed here during a brief geological interlude when this place had air and seasons. The needles themselves are gone, dissolved into the water's patience, but their absence is more precise than any fossil can be.

Movement here is exclusively historic. Clouds of single-celled engineers drift through their collective lifeworks—a coral reef built not of calcium but of neglect. Each microscopic sphere secretes a molecule-thin layer around itself every hundred years, not for protection but for bookkeeping. They have been constructing this coral of absence for so long that the reef contains more volume of record-keeping than of reef, a chronicle of the remembering of remembering.

Sometimes, very rarely, the record-keepers' descendants find unusual entries: perfect spheres of iron-nickel alloy exactly 2.7 centimeters in diameter, etched with patterns that suggest but don't quite reach mathematics. These are the oldest things that don't belong here—exiles from an asteroid belt that existed when this world still had a moon. The spheres are always found at precise intersections in the reef's accounting, as if someone was trying to hide numbers inside numbers.

Above (though directions are temporary guests here), the water column organizing itself into a cathedral nave kilometers high. Layers of distinct salinies and temperatures stack like panes of stained glass between which larger animals might swim if any survived the long fall from sunlight. Instead, there are only the sounds of these boundaries negotiating their borders—deep bass collisions of density that ring through the plain like the footsteps of giants made entirely of certitude.

Your presence is a disruption approximately as significant as a single unrecorded memory in the mind of an empire that has forgotten it exists. The plain will absorb your heat signature and your breathing chemicals and eventually even the iron in your blood, filing each particle under appropriate headings in its ongoing census of impurities. This process has already begun; you can taste your own vanishing in the water that fills your mouth when you forget to close it.

The plain has no center but contains numerous middles. One of them is marked by a circle where nothing grows—not the absence of growth, but the presence of a carefully maintained not-growing. Visitors who find this place (there have been three in the recorded history of water) report feeling an overwhelming conviction that they have arrived at the exact moment before the universe remembers it was supposed to be something else.

Here, at the bottom of the bottom of the below, where pressure has compressed time itself into a substance that flows uphill and light is the memory of a rumor, you finally understand why the word for "depth" in the language of this place also means "forgiveness" and "archive" and "the weight of everything waiting to be forgiven."

The plain is waiting for you to speak, not because it needs your words but because it wants to remember what it felt like to need anything at all.
