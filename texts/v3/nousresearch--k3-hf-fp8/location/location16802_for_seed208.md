The Place of Broken Horizons

Everything here is shaped by water that no longer flows. Water at 2.1 means memory of floods, relics of rivers, the way land remembers liquid fingers that carved it millennia before. Now only breath remains: a strange, persistent humidity that clings to every surface without forming droplets, a climate of perpetual exhalation where nothing ever quite dries but nothing truly wets. Your skin stays oddly clean here, as if invisible servants constantly wipe away sweat, oil, dust. The air itself is slightly viscous, resistant to sudden movement, cushioning falls with uncanny gentleness.

Temperature at 0.6 creates a realm of perpetual dusk-cool, that precise coolness of cellars and caves and moments before storms that never arrive. It's the temperature at which decay slows but doesn't stop, where time stretches like cold honey. Light at 0.8 filters through this atmosphere as if through smoked glass, not dark enough for blindness but never bright enough for shadows to sharpen. Instead everything exists in a soft grey-brown palette, colors reduced to their mineral essences like washed-out paintings left too long in water.

Elevation at 1.3 reveals an endless plain of broken things, a tea-colored landscape stretching to all horizons at shoulder-height, as if the entire world sank slightly in its sleep and no one bothered to adjust. You're always walking downhill but never descending, each step taking you deeper into the same level. This is ground zero for gravity, where it's still showing up for work but has lost all enthusiasm.

Erosion at 3.0 has done its work so thoroughly there's nothing left to erode further. The land has achieved entropy's retirement party. Every possible shape that existed between sharp mountain and perfect flatness has been explored, perfected, exhausted. What remains are not hills or valleys but the ghost outlines where such features might once have nested, like the dimples left in foam after pebbles are removed. Walking here, you're constantly recognizing shapes that aren't there anymore - oh, that must have been a ridgeline, see how the ground remembers being higher there? Oh, this depression must have cradled a lake, taste how the air is still vaguely lakier here?

Vegetation at 0.3 means this world-building project was abandoned before biology was implemented. No plant life whatsoever - not even the lichens that civilize rocks in other places, not even the stubborn moss that colonizes lava flows. Only chemistry at work here, minerals performing slow dances without biological choreography. But the absence is not sterile. Instead it's post-biological, like walking through a forest where all trees rotted away perfectly simultaneously, leaving behind their negative space, their habits, their echoes in how wind moves through where branches would have been.

Scale at 1.8 opens this sameness into vastness without variety, like a single thought repeated until it becomes meditation or madness. You can see far enough to know there's no point in seeing further, yet walking never quite brings destinations closer. The horizon appears to retreat at exactly your walking speed, maintaining a respectful distance like a shy animal. It's a landscape-sized version of those dreams where you run endlessly down infinite hallways in your childhood home.

Density at 0.8 means these vastnesses are mostly empty but never lonely. Presence here is distributed like stars in a galaxy - overwhelmingly nothingness, but the nothingness is textured, patterned, haunted by occasional somethings. You'll walk for hours across featureless plain, then suddenly stumble into a place where something almost happened once. A circle of stones too regular to be accidental but too meaningless to be art. A straight line of identical holes punched into the ground at precise intervals, each filled with identical copper-green dust. A single metal pole reaching twenty feet up to support nothing, humming a note you feel in your teeth when you touch it.

Built at 1.3 - scattered indeed, like the aftermath of a civilization that built only prototypes, never bothering with mass production or urban planning. Structures appear with the random frequency of flowers in a desert, each complete in itself but refusing to form patterns with neighbors. A perfectly preserved stone well with a bronze bucket frozen halfway up, the rope still taut but dust-rigid. A wall, just a wall, twenty feet long and four feet high, made of what appears to be pressed book-pages fossilized into stone, the writing still legible in places but in no language you recognize. A spiral staircase carved from a single black boulder, rising up then down into the ground like a Möbius strip you could climb forever in either direction.

Tech level at 1.3 manifests as the technology of obsession, not industry. Every artifact shows signs of craftsmanship that borders on madness, primitive tools used with infinite patience to achieve pointless perfection. A sphere of hammered copper plates riveted together with thousands of tiny silver nails, each plate etched with microscopic diagrams of impossible machines. A forest of bone needles standing in the ground at angles that suggest calculations beyond geometry, crowned with glass globes containing tiny trapped clouds that still slowly thunder and lightning. A loom made from whale ribs and spider silk, still weaving a cloth from the wind itself, producing every hour a square inch of fabric lighter than air but indestructible as memory.

And yet despite all this emptness and absence, Fauna is 2.0 - abundant beyond reason. Life here skipped plants entirely and went straight to animals, but animals built from different blueprints, using different materials. The ground itself crawls delicately, composed of billions of tiny ceramic crabs no larger than grains of rice, each one unique, their shells painted with patterns that look like forgotten musical notation. They part around your feet like living sand, clicking softly with a sound like distant rainsticks. Sky-whales drift overhead, but sky-whales made of stained glass and brass instruments, feeding on the slow light of this dim world and singing complex arithmetic through their horns. Trees of living lightning root themselves in the horizon during storms that never quite arrive, branching fractally upward until they dissolve into static electricity that the glass-bat swarms drink greedily.

These creatures make no sense by our biology but perfect sense by their own logic. The stone elephants that migrate annually in a circle no one has ever observed complete, because their migration takes exactly 1,001 years and the landscape erodes around them as they walk, leaving behind trunk-shaped valleys. The word-crowds of migratory pronouns that darken the sky during certain grammatical seasons, interacting with conversation like weather interacting with mood. The predator that consists entirely of negative space, a shaped absence that hunts by making its prey remember forgetting something important, growing larger as the memory of what was forgotten becomes more significant.

Weirdness at 1.4 means everything here is almost something else, existing in the hazy borderlands between categories where analogy becomes identity. The rivers that stopped running water became rivers of time instead, and now you can step across them to younger or older versions of the landscape, though the difference is always measured in centuries and the current is always downstream toward more erosion, more absence, more quiet. Mountains that dreamed of being oceans so completely they woke up covered in tides of sand that rise and fall according to lunar patterns no moon now remembers. A species of bird that evolved from lost coins, who nest in wishing wells and sing songs composed entirely of the desires once whispered into their ancestors.

Sound at 2.6 is this world's defining medium, solid enough to hold shape and weight. The deafening roar isn't noise but substance, a constant composition played on instruments made of distance and echo. Every footstep adds a note, every breath harmonizes, until travelers become walking chords progressing through landscapes of counterpoint. Some areas are famous for their acoustics - the Canyon of Perfect Fifths where every word spoken comes back transformed into prophecy, the Plain of Dissonance where sounds cancel each other into pockets of lethal silence more absolute than vacuum. The indigenous fauna communicate by sculpting this sound-stuff, carving temporary structures of noise to live in, melting them down to feed their young, storing memories in complex frozen chords that future generations can thaw and replay by touching tongues to them.

Dynamic at 1.7 means nothing changes fast enough to notice but everything changes perfectly coordinated with everything else, like a clock slowed down so much each tick is a geological era but all gears still mesh flawlessly. The ceramic crabs rearrange themselves nightly into new patterns that predict tomorrow's weather in some forgotten calendar system. The glass bats migrate between past and future versions of the same cave system according to routes mapped in changing shades of nostalgia. Even erosion itself has become cyclic - the land wears away precisely at the rate that its own dust accumulates elsewhere, creating a perfect conservation of absence where nothing is lost because nothing ever really existed to begin with.

The rules of this world are simple once you stop trying to understand them:
1. Every absence is the presence of something else, just not here.
2. Every direction away from something is toward something else, just not yet.
3. Every question contains its perfect answer, but only in a language that stops existing the moment it's understood.
4. The fastest way between any two points is a dream of flying, but dreams here obey gravity too, just differently.
5. You only get lost here if you remember where you're going. The truly lost find everywhere exactly where they left it.

Visitors report that time here behaves like water in the desert - spends years somewhere doing nothing, then suddenly everywhere at once in violent bloomings of event that leave their strange fruits scattered across centuries of quiet. Some have walked for a day and returned home to find their houses turned to museums of their childhoods. Others have settled and built families whose grandchildren remember the founding myths differently every time they tell them, until history becomes a collection of beautiful but incompatible stories that all stay true at once.

The best advice for traveling here is the advice given by the first traveler, whose footprints we still follow though no one remembers what direction he was going: Walk softly, because you're walking on someone's memory of walking. Sing constantly, to warn the silence of your approach. And if you meet yourself coming back the other way, don't speak - just nod politely, trade coats if yours is more worn, and continue on. This world needs both versions of you, the one who left and the one who stayed, to keep its stories balanced between happening and remembered.

Most important: Don't try to leave until you've learned to miss this place exactly perfectly. Too little longing and the way back closes forever. Too much and you'll carry it with you when you go, spreading its strange physics of loss and presence into other more innocent worlds like seeds carried in the folds of an old map. This world has been ending for so long that it's become a kind of beginning, but not every beginning is meant for everywhere.
