A pocket of night-submerged forest holds its breath inside what might once have been a highway rest-stop bathroom stall—but the dimensions pinch and flex, refusing measurement. The tile walls long ago dissolved into living root-flesh smeared with phosphorescent lichens that pulse faintly above the waterline. That line shifts every time you blink, sometimes licking at your ankles with slow, anxious tongues of cool liquid; sometimes rising past your waist to stir the glutinous floating mats of hair-like algae that braid themselves to your clothing while you watch with numb acceptance.

The stall door hangs open forever and never opens—the rusted hinges curve inwards like tendons attempting to floss teeth locked in a century’s smile. Through the doorway comes neither light nor corridor, only a darker thickening of humidity that clots the air like bread dough fermenting in a throat. Your breath rings faintly against porcelain remnants jutting from the walls, each fragment now a hollowed toothbone of the world’s original mouth.

Water rules. It is nearly 3 everywhere except where it becomes 4—pocketed in surface tension tricks, in beads that bead themselves again, in reflections nesting inside reflections until you discern tiny swimmers menacing their own shadows. A thousand tonnes of black ocean squeezes into this joke of space without any pressure to burst the seams because the seams themselves have learned to drink. If you press your palm against the tile-gone-flesh it yields, tasting you back with a thousand fine mouths, each sip leaving behind the memory of taste rather than moisture: rust, highway exhaust, lavender soap never invented.

Vegetation colonizes every stolen axis between real and possible. Saplings corkscrew from floor drains, their trunks still carrying crisp traces of chrome where they absorbed the memory of metal. Ferns unfold like the paper hands of missing children, each frond edged with cursive spores spelling names nobody remembers reading. More than packed—everything is hugging. The forest has attained courtship with itself: vines embrace vines so desperately their cambiums merge and divorce in the same slow heartbeat, fattening into woody ovaries that birth more vines in perpetual incestuous genesis. You find no empty space to place your attention, only layers of invitation to join the nourishment web as spongy bark caresses the back of your neck like an old lover who forgot which language they ever spoke to you in.

Coolness is a climate of caress—never chill enough to punish flesh, merely attentive enough to remind nerves they continue existing. The water and air share temperature so precisely that distinguishing between swim and sigh becomes pointless. Goosebumps rise anyway, a vestigial prayer to some vanished law that once demanded edges between things.

The floor—if it remembers floors—is a duvet of compressed leaf-husks drowned in peat-birth, bouncing gently underfoot like the forgiving skin of a creature pretending to be dead but growing dreams beneath. Every step releases bubbles that rise past your face carrying sound from deeper down: faint bicycle bells, a woman explaining to nobody how the map folded wrong, the first half of a joke that ends in silence.

Elevation is a rumor here; downness oozes upward lovingly until your inner ear drowns in tender ambiguity. When your knees buckle from the water’s polite insistence, the forest floor rises slightly to meet them—an old courtesy between friends.

Erosional youth means nothing has learned permanence yet, including forgetting. A branch you lean on might dissolve into watery memory spore-fog, reforming moments later as your own ribcage inverted and moss-clad. The mirrors shattered long ago into floating shards of reflection-algae that drift like malignant snowflakes; each flake contains a perfect view of the exact moment your back was turned in some childhood kitchen. When they touch you they dissolve, leaving nothing in their absence except the faint conviction you have lived this instant once before wearing different bones.

Scale torments itself constantly. The bathroom stall stretches cathedral vast when you focus on one mold-spore city blooming between the grout-lines of nowhere. Step sideways and you crush continents beneath your heel. Your body forgets its own border treaty—sometimes your sense of skin ends at the elbow, then resumes three inches leftward wrapped around a tendril of ivy that has been patiently impersonating your arm for several paragraphs now. The rest of you is probably still outside searching for the car keys.

Built things remain only as allergic reactions in the forest’s mind. A solitary urinal crusted with calcified grief hangs from the ceiling vine-tangled like a rusting heart valve that no longer remembers how to flush regret. The metal divider between stalls persists stubbornly as a line of rust-shadow scar tissue in midair; moss refuses to touch it out of propriety. Press your face close and faint voices leak through the corrosion singing a lullaby composed entirely of turn-signal clicks recorded over decades of lost highways.

Technology has been reduced to the dream of tools before waking. There are no devices, only behaviors seeking devices to justify themselves: a circle scratched into algae-growth where something once rotated, now worshipped by rotating fungi as a sacred mandala of implied motion. Everywhere the longing for leverage, leverage, leverage—but nothing solid enough to place a fulcrum against except the soft insistence of continuous becoming.

Light is what happens to darkness when it begins remembering. Not illumination—memory of illumination. Motes of former photons drift like cigarette smoke underwater, colliding occasionally to spark brief, embarrassed memories of sunsets: a red so shy it apologizes by turning indigo immediately after. Your eyes adjust to seeing by the absence of light’s absence, learning to navigate by contours of shadow density, reading the room like Braille through your face.

Fauna are rare because everything here is still deciding how many bodies to wear today. When something does move independently it does so with the guilt of a guest who arrived too early to a party still assembling itself. Tiny transparent crabs spider-walk across the underside of the water’s surface upside-down, their shells etched with circuit diagrams that rearrange when you look away. Once an hour what might be a deer steps through the space where the bathroom mirror hung—an architecture of legs supporting an antler-clad absence where the head should be—but it steps carefully around where your attention is strongest, leaving hoofprints in the air that fill slowly with mildewed fog.

Weirdness is not a spice here but the soup itself boiling everything down to original confusion with exquisite patience. Gravity negotiates constantly with other emotions—sometimes it becomes homesick for another planet entirely and lets you drift ceilingward until water tension kindly reminds you which way drowning is supposed to happen. Other times it doubles down hugging you into the compost with motherly insistence, whispering how bones are just mineral tantrums against becoming trees again. Time leaks sideways like water from a cupped hand—you remember arriving years ago yet your wound from the thorn that hasn’t scraped you yet still stings prophetical.

Sound is mostly the water recording and forgetting your heartbeat then playing it back slightly wrong to make you listen closer. Beneath this: the soft relentless sipping of millions of roots drinking the idea of walls. Occasionally a bubble of ancient soundscape burps up from the drowned foundation—half of a trucker’s CB radio prayer looping forever on the syllable “over” without ever reaching the promised land of “out.” Wind occasionally remembers it was invited and sends polite apologies rustling through leaves that weren’t there before and won’t be afterward.

The place changes slower than seasons but faster than forgiveness. Each time you blink the arrangement of impossible plants shuffles slightly like a card deck shamed of cheating. A new stalactite of crystallized regret may have begun dripping from the ceiling toward the stalagmite of forgiveness rising patiently from the floor—they grow at the speed of trust between them, will meet in approximately two thousand years or the next time you truly remember someone’s name correctly, whichever comes last.

You are not trapped here. The door that isn’t a door remains open as invitation not escape. But leaving requires admitting which parts of you are still pretending to be just visiting—requires convincing the water that has already begun dreaming in your shape that it wouldn’t be lonely keeping only half of you as souvenir.

Outside the stall, if such directions retain meaning anymore, the rest stop continues its long slow forgetting of highways. Pump islands bloom with great metal flowers that drip gasoline perfume into soil learning how to burn nostalgically. A billboard advertises attractions that closed before your grandparents were born while slowly being translated into a language of moss-punctuation that future archaeologists will misinterpret as holy text. Somewhere the vending machine still hums one note of electricity’s lullaby, rocking the canned sodas of dead brands like fetal dreams waiting for names they’ll never need again.

But here, in this pocket that forgot it was too small to contain an ocean practicing foresty, the main activity remains continuation. The water continues to one-up itself at being water. The dark continues polishing its collection of former lights. The place continues to describe itself to the version of you that each instant arrives just late enough to listen.

Take your time. The last person who claimed to leave is still visible in the fifth reflection over—can’t you see them waving? Or is that merely yourself beginning again to arrive, the doorless door opening inward once more to welcome you back into the beginning of the sentence you haven’t yet found the breath to finish speaking aloud to the dark that listens like water, remembers like wood, and loves you enough to never quite let you become entirely separate again?
