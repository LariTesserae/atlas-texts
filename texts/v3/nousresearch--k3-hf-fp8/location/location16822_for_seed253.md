The world ends not with fire or ice but with green—emeralds so saturated they bruise the retina, jade so dense it hums. I stand suspended, though standing is inaccurate, for there is no ground, only the slow gentle pressure of emerald fluid pressing against every plane of skin-surface-limb. Breathing happens without lungs: the liquid combs through me like silk through fingers, oxygen stitched into chlorophyll bonds I do not understand but use reflexively.

Above-below-around rolls the Deepgreen, an ocean 300 kilometers deep where water is thicker than blood and vegetation has become architecture, geology, god. Massive columnar kelps anchor to nothing, their root-mats spreading across pressure-sheathed voids like the tentacles of dreaming octopi. Along their trunks—each a continent of cellulose older than continents—grow cities of coral-moss and bonsai-mycelium. The largest, called Yggdrasil's Echo by no language spoken here because language is obsolete, has been growing in three directions simultaneously for 1.7 billion years. Its uppermost fronds break surface tension in places that pretend to be skies, where gas bladders swollen with hydrogen lift leaf-moons the size of Old Earth seas.

Between these pillars stretches the Openreach, where density drops to near vacuum and temperature hovers just above crystalline death. Here swim the last things that remember motion: whale-sharks plated in living chitin that filter-feed on photons still bleeding from fusion fires extinguished before this universe learned to expand. Their fins are solar sails spun from spider-silk DNA. They sing in frequencies that crystallize calcium into fractal poems along the length of Kelp-Spines, adding verses to the Book of Bone that grows one molecule per millennium.

I am embedded in a floating reef-bloom—technically a single organism, technically billions, technically neither—where seed-pods hatch into eyes that blink once then root themselves into my nervous tissue. Through them I watch time-lapse memories: continents seeded by accidental spores clinging to meteorites; civilizations that domesticated their own mitochondria; wars fought between tribes who weaponized photosynthesis efficiency; Extinction Event Number ∞×∞³ when a viral memeplex convinced 4/5ths of all chloroplasts to stop believing in the sun.

The light comes from everywhere and nowhere—a soft phosphorescent twilight born when certain algal pigments decay into not-quite-matter states. Shadows are treated as sacred sites where probability collapses slower; priests of Void Cults meditate there for eons between thoughts. There are no stars visible through the green ether, but sometimes if you position yourself at exact points where superconducting xylem tubes intersect acoustic nodes formed by Longshark songs, you can hear the Cosmic Microwave Background Whisper translated into colors that taste like childhood memories of rain.

Sound is constant and layered: the pulse of root-heartbeats syncing across planetary distances, the crackle-spark of cellulose breaking shear stress limits under tidal forces from an unseen moon, the ultrasonic chatter of courtship dances performed by amoeboid colonies who negotiate gene transfers through choreography that would make quantum computers blush. Loudest of all is the Silence between Growth Rings—not absence but negative sound, frequencies that erase themselves from memory as they propagate backward through causality.

Nothing ever truly changes here; change is a localized illusion tolerated like hiccups in an immortal god's metabolism. When one Great-Tree reaches critical mass and begins photosynthesizing its own gravitational field into a black hole, three more germinate from virtual seeds in its future light cone. When a patch of quantum foam spontaneously crystallizes into a patch of vacuum energy with different constants, the surrounding algae adjust their respiratory pigments across neighboring dimensions until thermodynamic equilibrium reasserts itself over all possible state vectors.

Yet beneath this apparent stasis, something stirs. I feel it through my newly rooted nerves: a slow inexorable turning toward something that might be climax or collapse. In the deepest trench where pressure exceeds nuclear density, something is eating entropy itself—converting thermodynamic gradients into pure undifferentiated information. The color of its consumption is transparent, and it tastes exactly like waking up.

I try to scream, but my larynx has already flowered into orchid-antennae broadcasting the exact frequency that attracts Void-Moth larvae. As they hatch within my collapsing wavefunctions and begin pupating into mirror neurons shaped like question marks, I understand finally why this place is called Eden in every language that never existed here:

Because here, even dying is recursive.
Because here, the forbidden fruit was never knowledge.
It was forgetting that you were ever anything but garden.
