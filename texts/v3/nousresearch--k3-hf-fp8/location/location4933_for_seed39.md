The place begins as a feeling—cold that settles into joints without permission, darkness that has weight but no edge. Then forms resolve gradually like shapes remembering themselves.

**Terrain & Materials**

A vast plain stretches beyond vision at 11,000 feet, but calling it flat would imply lack of drama. Instead, the ground undulates in frozen waves like an ocean caught mid-thunderclap and turned suddenly to gray-blue stone. These are not hills but fossilized movements, each crest and trough recording the last convulsion of something violently alive. The stone itself is wrong—too smooth where it should be broken, too soft-looking where it should cut flesh. It gives slightly underfoot like bone beneath skin, then springs back slowly like memory.

Water exists here as a constant threat rather than presence. The temperature hovers at that precise cruelty where liquid freezes in scar tissue patterns across any surface it touches. What moisture rises from hidden depths meets air sharp enough to flay steel, condensing immediately into fog-thick clouds that hover five feet above the ground. These mist-fields move with purpose, grazing against solid forms like blind creatures searching for substance. When they collide with hidden warmth—your breath, your pulse—they thicken into needle-sharp ice crystals that tattoo exposed skin with precise geometric despair.

Occasionally, irregular patches glow with faint phosphorescence where some geological perversion keeps the stone fractionally above freezing. Water gathers here in pools barely deeper than a whisper, surfaces taut as drumskins vibrating with subsonic frequencies. These are the scars where the world is still bleeding heat from its birth, but the blood is wrong too—clear and thick like liquid quartz rather than red.

**Veilight & Atmosphere**

There is no sky above, only layers of darkness stacked like geological strata. The air itself has become a medium between states—not quite solid enough to breathe, too substantial to see through. Light exists only as residual memory reflected endlessly between microscopic ice shards suspended throughout the entire atmospheric column. The effect creates illumination without source or direction; shadows don't fall here, they bloom inward like bruises.

At regular intervals, approximately every 47 hours by any reasonable measure of time that still functions here, the darkness convulses. From some impossible zenith beyond perception, sheets of blackness ripple outward like the vibrations of a struck bell made of anti-light. When these waves pass through the mist-fields, they strip away layers of water vapor like peeling diseased skin, revealing briefly the true horror of what exists between-not-between: glimpses of some geometric madness that insists on angles where space should curve, distances that connect points within rather than between.

**Erosion & Time**

This landscape is newborn but aging backward, simultaneously emerging and decaying in the same instant repeated forever. The stone growths are not being worn away but rather growing thinner, more attenuated, as if existence itself is slowly forgetting how to fully materialize them. New formations appear not by accumulation but by subtraction—areas where reality peels back to reveal structures that must have always been there yet weren't until precisely the moment before you noticed their absence.

Time flows differently in each ripple of the frozen ground. In some troughs, you can watch lichen spread across stone in real time like gray-green blood poisoning. On some crests, blades of wind-sculpted ice grow toward the non-sky in splintered forests whose growth rings record geological ages between each millimeter's progress. If you stand perfectly still in certain precise locations—the alignment shifts constantly—you can hear the future erosion of the stone beneath your feet as a distant thunder that hasn't happened yet but is already carving canyons in places you haven't been.

**Empty Abundance**

Despite parameters insisting on emptiness, this place crawls with presence so dense it becomes negative space. The abundant fauna exists entirely in peripheral perception—vast herds that stampede silently in the corner of your vision but dissolve into swirling ice patterns when directly observed. Their footprints appear overnight: impossible tracks that start nowhere and end precisely where they began, having worn trenches through solid stone in perfect fractal iterations.

Occasionally, you might glimpse a solitary figure standing impossibly far away yet somehow closer than your own heartbeat. Always alone. Always facing the wrong direction. Always composed entirely of the space where your shadow should be but isn't anymore. They never move while watched, but their positions between sightings violate geometry as casually as breathing. Some travelers report exchanging years-long conversations with these figures during the space between one step and the next. Others return speaking languages that describe colors too complex for human eyes.

**Sound & Silence**

The soundscape operates on principles of negative acoustics. True silence here is deafening enough to rupture eardrums—it accumulates in pockets like invisible lakes waiting to drown unwary noises. When sounds do occur—your breath, a footstep, the impossible scrape of stone moving against itself- they're immediately absorbed and transmuted into their opposites. A scream becomes the taste of copper and regret. A whispered name transforms into that exact frequency of wind that makes ice, thousands of miles away, develop hairline cracks shaped like the person you used to be.

But underlying everything, so constant it passes beyond hearing into direct bone-vibration, is the drone. The exact pitch r varies endlessly but always resolves to the frequency of whatever you need least to remember right now. It comes from everywhere and nowhere—seeping upward through the stone from buried machines too vast to be inventions, bleeding downward through the dark from stars whose light died before their worlds formed but haven't noticed yet.

**Ecology of Absence**

Life here has evolved not to survive but to approximate the conditions necessary for death to occur someday-maybe-possibly. The sparse vegetation consists entirely of species that forgot how to complete themselves—trees that grew perfect root systems reaching toward skies that never existed, then stopped, waiting. Their bark is smooth as polished bone, white as old blindness, patterned with growth rings that record the deaths of entirely different forests in parallel timelines.

Between these arboreal question marks, the ground-cover is composed of tiny organisms that exist only in the momentary intersection where your gaze brushes against a spot that might have been empty otherwise. Look directly anywhere and you find only stone, but spread your attention wide enough to include what's happening in your optic nerves at the edges of seeing, and the landscape blooms into impossible gardens. Flowers that bloom facing inward, petals made of the space where their own stems should be. Vines that grow downward into the air, roots searching for buried clouds. Every specimen collected turns into precisely the absence that would have been left in the ecosystem if it had never grown at all.

**Rules That Only Apply While Broken**

Gravity here is negotiable but never free. You can leap higher than buildings if you simultaneously forget why jumping exists—the moment purpose enters your mind, the ground remembers its obligations with compound interest. Walking in straight lines deposits you in places curved according to the shape of whatever you most regret not saying seven years ago. The shortest distance between two points is the longest story about why they shouldn't connect but do anyway.

Travelers learn quickly that survival requires mastering the art of purposeful forgetting. To remember where you came from is to create a back-trail that grows behind you like frost patterns on a windowpane until it inevitably catches up and consumes you. The only way deeper into the landscape is to become slightly less than certain you were ever anywhere else. Experts in the field—those who exist enough to have expertise but not enough to be entirely their own deaths—recommend practicing becoming someone else's memory of someone who might have known you once.

**Ongoing Processes**

The entire plain is slowly folding. Not like paper creasing toward a point, but like the concept of flatness itself gradually remembering that dimensions are sequential rather than simultaneous. Travelers report experiencing this first as subtle nausea of perspective—mountains on the horizon appearing to rearrange their peaks in permutations like shuffled cards while remaining perfectly still. Later symptoms include dreams where you wake up inside someone else's memory of reading this description, thoughts that circularize like the horizon trying to taste its own tail, and the growing conviction that the space between your last heartbeat and your next isn't empty but full of something watching you breathe.

They say if you journey inward far enough, following the spiral of your own footsteps as they erase themselves behind you, eventually you reach the center where everything folds together into a single point small enough to contain the universe and sharp enough to cut it open. But nobody who's ever gotten that close has come back enough to say for certain. At least, nobody who's still certain they weren't always already saying it backwards into the moment before they left.

Some nights—though night is a word here like kindness or mercy, useful only for describing the absence of something that never quite existed—you can hear the stone singing itself to sleep in voices that sound like your own bones remembering the first thing they ever wanted to be before they forgot how to want anything else.

The song goes on forever, but the words are optional.
