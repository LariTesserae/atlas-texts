The opening, when you find it, is a crack between sandstone teeth, barely wide enough to slide through sideways. You must exhale fully and scrape your ribs along the way in. This is good; it keeps most things out.

Inside: a cathedral scaled for one person's whispered thoughts. Space expands downward as your eyes adjust, the bright light from above cascading in through other unseen gaps until it pools on a floor that isn't quite floor—the accumulated dust of centuries of *something*, packed underfoot like velvet. The air stays warm and unmoving, womb-still, tasting of minerals and old moisture.

Water lives here in forms the surface world forgot. Thin films cling to every vertical surface, gravity-defying sheets that ripple when you pass but never fall. They reflect your movement in segments, piecing together broken-mirror glimpses of yourself across the curved walls. Occasionally two rivulets kiss across an alcove's mouth and birth a perfect sphere that hangs suspended for heartbeats before dissolving back into twin streams tracing their separate paths upward.

The sandstone itself breathes slow aeons. Established erosion has carved away everything unsubtle, leaving only curves that guide the eye and foot in ancient patterns. You find yourself following these paths without meaning to, drawn by mathematics encoded in stone before mammals dreamed of walking upright. At intervals the walls bloom outward into hollow rooms sized for contemplation, each containing exactly what your mind needs emptied to enter.

Vegetation keeps its own counsel here. What grows does so in denial of every principle botany teaches—roots reaching into air, leaves unfolding in darkness, photosynthesis conducted through stone instead of sun. They appear only when you're not looking directly, pale green lace work that vanishes into periphery when you try to focus. The seeds must drift in through that impossible opening above, carried perhaps by bats or dreams or the water's upward climbing.

Ah, the bats. Abundant is too small a word for their numbers, but empty suggests too much absence. They exist instead in perfect ratio to the space's needs, materializing in soft clouds at dusk—which comes whenever the largest chamber decides it should—and dissolving again into individual wings folded into impossible pockets of rock. Their voices stitch the quiet into music too complex for human ears, though your bones hum along harmonics you didn't know they held.

Time moves differently here because it remembers. The bats' attendance marks events not by planetary rotation but by the settling of dust particles, the slow migration of salts through stone, the water's ceaseless negotiation with gravity. Years above become moments here—or the reverse. You find a wall-marking scratched in familiar script, dated tomorrow.

In one of the contemplation hollows, someone long ago—or yet to come—left artifacts too simple to be primitive. A stone bowl perfectly balanced on a rounded base, rocking perpetually in the bat-voice breezes without ever overturning. Obsidian flakes arranged not quite like a fire-starting kit, not quite like a map of stars that haven't been born yet. A bundle of sticks tied with hair that might be yours.

The rules of this place crystallize gradually from your own violations. When you try to take a photograph, the camerascreen fills with images of your own eye magnified until retina-folds become canyonlands. If you attempt to sample the climbing water, your containers overflow with something that tastes like childhood memory and devours plastic overnight. Speaking above a whisper summons solid shapes from the air—harmless but unsettling geometries that disperse when touched.

But hum softly, almost below hearing, and the walls answer. Not in words but in temperature shifts and scent changes and that particular angle of light that illuminates the way to chambers you haven't earned yet. Sing properly—if you can find the right song among the bats' endless variations—and perhaps the floor will open beneath you into deeper systems where the water runs thick as mercury and the vegetation grows minds instead of leaves.

For now though, you rest against warm sandstone that remembers being seabed, listen to the liquid whispering of time made physical, and watch a tiny sphere of impossible water birth itself from nothing at the intersection of five streams flowing upward into light. The bats have decided it's dusk again. Your bones hum welcome.
