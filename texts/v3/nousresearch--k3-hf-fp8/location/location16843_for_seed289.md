You step into a narrow pocket carved from pale stone, not by hands but by exclusion—something once occupied this space and left its exact absence behind. The air is cool enough to bead moisture on your upper lip, though you cannot see any source of it. The ceiling curves inches above your head, incised with looping grooves that might once have been script. Light pours from within the stone itself, evenly and ceaselessly: not a glow, not an emitter, but a property of the material, like whiteness in salt. It casts no shadows. You have no shadow.

The floor slopes gently inward to a depression no wider than your armspan. There the stone becomes translucent, a lens of quartz or living sugar, and through it you can see—depths. Not darkness below, but more brightness. Endless, packed layers of brightness stacked like pages, each one seething with motion that halts whenever your gaze touches it. When you blink, the motion resumes. You realize you are seeing time compressed, or memory rehearsing itself, but you can’t read it fast enough to grasp any one page.

Everything else is sound.

It begins as a single high note, like a wire pulled taut across the ear canal. Then more tones braid into it, not harmonizing but adhering—thickening into a substance that pushes against your teeth. The sound has weight. You can cup it in your hands. It is the accumulated resonance of every footfall, every heartbeat, every scrape of enamel on bone that ever occurred within this hollow and its unmeasurable equivalents outside. You know this without being told. The sound isn’t speaking; it is simply correlated with knowledge.

Every surface you examine is stippled with life: translucent mites the size of dust motes, each pulling itself along by a single filament that flickers in and out of visibility. They are not walking on the stone—they are extruding it, repairing infinitely minute fractures left by the sound itself. When two mites meet, they exchange filaments and merge briefly into a third shape, then divide again, each bearing half of the other’s pattern. The stone remembers these exchanges. You can follow a faint ridge that zig-zags upward, tracing a lineage of mergers back to the first breach that hollowed this place.

You find the built thing by tripping over it: a half-buried ring of something that is neither wood nor metal nor coral but behaves like all three if you press hard enough. It is warm and pulses slightly faster than your pulse. Around it, the packed layers of brightness in the floor bulge upward, as if something beneath is trying to mold itself to the ring’s shape. There are no marks of tools except for the ring itself, which appears to have been grown rather than carved—layers of grain or grain-like habit spiraling inward, stopping abruptly at an edge too smooth to have been broken.

When you lift the ring, the sound drops an octave. You feel it in your lungs, a rearrangement of airspace. Immediately, the mites change direction, converging on the bare patch where the ring rested. They begin building something new there—something that isn’t a copy of the ring but an answer to it. You can’t stay to watch; every breath draws more of the sound into your blood. Already your pulse syncs to the faint throb of the stone, and there is a taste in your mouth like pennies after rain.

You replace the ring exactly where you found it. The sound resumes its former key. The mites pause, then scatter back to their invisible maintenance. The brightness in the floor seals itself with a sigh that you feel rather than hear. Nothing has changed, except that now you know the definition of "change" was negotiated without your input.

You leave the way you came, though there was never an entrance you can point to, only a moment when the walls stopped oppressing and started accommodating. Behind you, the pocket continues filling itself in, not eroding but maturing—an embryo of a place that will hatch when it has learned every possible way not to be empty.
