The Catedral Subterranea breathes through pores too small to see but large enough to scream.

Imagine first the descent: wooden ladders lashed with sinew, each rung carved by hands that never met, descending through limestone pores no wider than grief. Forty-three ladders according to old maps burned by the makers. Forty-three according to the dead who still count. Below them waits a chamber where a whisper becomes thunder and silence fractures bones.

Water here exists as betrayal. Moisture kisses stone lips then vanishes, leaving behind mineral kiss-marks that bloom into white stamen flowers overnight. These flowers scream when touched. Not metaphorically. The entire cave system amplifies frequencies between 18.7 and 19.2 Hz - the exact range that shatters human faith. Rain never reaches this depth but her ghost pounds the ceiling every thirteen hours, transmitting storms from a world forgotten by those who built this place.

Temperature hovers at the precise point where skin forgets its own name. Flesh becomes water becomes memory becomes stone becomes flesh again through cycles too slow for mortals to witness but fast enough to trap them mid-transformation. The builders knew this. Their tools remain scattered like breadcrumbs left for children who grew into gods who grew into rock before finding their way home.

The builders themselves... their story hangs in the air as calcite draperies frozen mid-whisper. They came after something older carved these halls through sound alone. The original architects sang stone apart, vibrating minerals into memories, memories into architecture, architecture into traps for future gods. The new builders found only tools and instructions etched in frequencies too low for ears. They built what they could understand. Scattered prayer platforms carved from single crystals. Bone flutes positioned to catch imaginary winds. A suspension system of silence held up by noise.

The silence is everything here. Not absence but presence inverted, a solid thing you must push through like water underwater. It catches thoughts like spiderwebs catch dust. Makes them heavier. Makes them real. Makes them last forever.

Vegetation exists contrary to reason. Gardens of albino fungi cultivate themselves between the footprints of rain that fell centuries above. Their fruiting bodies mimic missing body parts perfectly - your iris here, my mother's smile there. They taste like forgiveness forgotten in another language. They grow toward sounds instead of light, stretching their hyphal fingers to pluck screams from the air like harpists playing songs for an audience of minerals.

The scale shifts when no one watches. Chamber walls drift closer by fractions of hair-widths per decade. The ceiling dreams of becoming the floor. The floor remembers being sky. Between them wander the last observers - creatures or constructs or something that learned to exist between categories by forgetting how to choose. They wear the shapes of things that once had names. Their bones are flutes playing requiems for motion itself.

The light... there is no light. Only the memory of darkness so dense it becomes visible. In this seeing-not-seeing, patterns emerge. The way certain angles of nothing reflect sound like mirrors held by ghosts. The way shadows cast by silence pool in places where light once died worshipping. The way your eyes begin to emit their own darkness after days spent here, becoming tiny caves that see by remembering what swallowed them.

Sound dominates absolutely. Not waves but landscape. Not passing through but being. Every footstep births cities complete with histories tragedies languages and religions that rise fall and fossilize before the heel lifts. spoken words become geological events recorded in strata of compressed meaning. The dead speak loudest here because they've had longest to listen to the stone remembering itself.

Time flows backward through limestone veins carrying future fossils to ancient births. Causes chase effects into rooms that haven't been built yet where effects wait patiently teaching causes how to cry. The builders built this place to escape time but time entered through the wounds in their intentions and now lives here rent-free growing fat on paradox and grief.

In the central chamber where all paths spiral inward like questions seeking their own answers, stands the Cathedral Proper. Not built but subtracted - all stone that wasn't faith carved away by sound over sound over sound until only belief remained standing unsupported by anything except the weight of wanting it to be real. Inside sings one note that has been sung for seven million years without pause. It is the exact frequency of holding on while letting go.

The builders built benches from their own bones fused with regret and arranged them facing away from the altar because the Gods they summoned turned out to be themselves younger and still capable of forgiveness. There they sit waiting - not patient, not impatient - existing in the space between states where clocks go to die and calendars confess their lies.

Come closer. Listen. The cave is singing your name backwards through stone teeth shaped like your mother's lullabies. The sound is deafening because it is the exact volume of every silence that ever hoped you'd return to fill it. The entrance is always here because it was never anywhere else. The exit waits inside you like a cathedral carved by sound too beautiful to hear without becoming it.

The builders left one instruction carved in spore-script that grows across new skin overnight: To leave this place, forget how you entered. To enter again, remember what you forgot to take with you.

The rest is echo upon echo upon echo folding origami prayers out of screams waiting for hands pure enough to unfold them without crying blood.
