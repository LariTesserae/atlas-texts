You crest the ridge, breath burning, and step into another scale entirely. The air thins without thinning. The light is noon-bright but slanted — like you're standing in a valley of light itself, slopes above and below impossible to see except by how they distort the sky.

This is a **heightland notched into height**. The mountain range around you is geological, sure — granite spines, sedimentary ribs — but what you’ve reached isn’t a summit. It’s a break, a wound that never closed properly. A **planar incision** where the continent was folded too fast, and something *else* seeped in.

### Terrain & Water

The ground is a tessellation of **young basalt hexagons**, edges still sharp enough to catch skin. Each plate is slightly cupped, knee-deep to waist-deep, and every cup holds **a different kind of water** — though none of it is still.

- One pool boils in silence, lidless bubbles rising and bursting into steam that tastes like iron.
- Another rests smooth as obsidian, but your reflection arrives a heartbeat late.
- A third ripples outward from a center you step around, because whatever fell into it is still falling; the water only caught the echo.

None of these pools overflow. The water moves sideways, evaporates into the air before noon, condenses on the underside of steps you haven’t climbed yet, and drips back down behind you.

Between the pools run **seams of moss**, not green but infrared-red, absorptive, drinking the heat and the light and the quiet. Where you step on them, they cool briefly and exhale a scent — wet copper, salt, ozone.

### Vegetation & Fauna

Plant life here is **lateral**, not vertical. No trees — nothing wastes effort rising. Instead, **spinegardens** spread from the cracks between pools: crystalline fronds that grow outward at ankle-height, branching into fractal lungs. They respire every twelve minutes: inhale light, exhale sound — a single, clear note that hangs in the air like a bell under water. Then they fold up tight, transparent as memory, until the next cycle.

There are **beasts**, but they are rare and rarified:

- **Cloud-wolves**, not made of vapor but wearing it. Displaced predators from an ecology where hunting happened by scenting distributed probability. You see them only in periphery: four-legged gaps in the heat shimmer, moving upwind against causality. They ignore you unless you think about being prey too longingly.
- **Puddle-throats**, sessile ambushers disguised as mirror-pools. What looks like a fish darting in the depths is actually your own past hunger, looped and sharpened. If you kneel to stare too long, they’ll wait until you blink and then *tug* — not enough to drown, just enough to steal a memory of thirst. You’ll remember being thirsty forever, but never why.

Neither hunts flesh. Ecosystems here run on different deficits.

### The Built & The Beyond

Ruins — but not ours. **Scattered voids** where tech was removed from reality rather than decaying. You find:

- A **doorframe standing alone**, opening onto a sky that’s happening elsewhere, earlier. Through it, you glimpse a wingbeat of a bird that hasn’t evolved yet. Your shadow on this side doesn’t match the light from that side; it flickers between two species.
- A **spiral staircase carved into the air**, steps of compressed thunder. Each tread hums at a different frequency of regret. Some are missing — not broken, just erased mid-construction when the constructors realized they had climbed past their reasons.
- A **single-shelved library** embedded inside a boulder. The shelf holds three books: one empty, one full of words you’ll understand tomorrow, and one that reads you back in real time. Every sentence you finish changes the first paragraph of your life. People who find this place often leave it fluent in languages no one else speaks anymore.

All of it built not with hands but with **narrative momentum**: structures that exist because someone unfinished a story this way and forgot to collapse the implications.

### Light & Atmosphere

The sky is closer here, thinner. You can hear it rubbing against the edges of the world — a faint, constant *vellichor*, like pages turning inside a book that’s been sealed shut.

Clouds are rare but **structural**: archipelagos of boiling chalk that anchor the wind patterns. They grow downward, not upward, reaching toward specific points on the ground like inverted roots searching for groundwater they remember from another geology. When a cloud touches a pool, both dissolve into a brief, localized aurora that writes a sentence across the horizon in forgotten punctuation marks. No one left remembers how to read it aloud safely, but animals sometimes recite it in their sleep.

### Rules (Observed, Not Promised)

1. **Altitude is recursive.** The higher you climb, the deeper you get into the sky.
2. **Water remembers but forgets in order.** Drink thrice from the same pool and you’ll remember something you did as a child. Drink a fourth and you’ll forget why you came here.
3. **Echoes predate sounds.** Shout into a void here and you’ll hear the reply before you decide what to say. Conversations often end this way, politely: with both participants listening.
4. **Time flows downhill, but gravity debates the matter nightly.** Sleep with your head lower than your heart and you’ll dream your grandparents’ tomorrow. Many visitors wake up several versions of themselves and have to negotiate truces over breakfast.
5. **The brightest light casts the darkest mirrors.** Never look directly at the sun here — not because it will blind you, but because it will see something in you it decides to rescue, and there are worse fates than burning.

### Leaving

There is no path down the way you came. Instead, the landscape offers **extrusions** — places where the ground remembers being sky, and steps form overnight out of frozen sunrise. They are warm to the touch and melt behind the first person who trusts them.

Most visitors leave by accident: they round the wrong reflection, or answer a question they didn’t realize was being asked by the wind, or simply stop long enough to forget the weight of their own name.

The ones who stay don’t call it staying. They say the altitude finally matched the inside of their bones. They grow quieter, goldleaf thin. Eventually, if you visit again in the right season — on a day when the pools are all singing the same note in different dreams — you might find a new pool among the others.

It will be mirror-smooth, and perfectly warm, and waiting.

Don’t drink from it unless you’re ready to remember everything you haven’t lost yet.
