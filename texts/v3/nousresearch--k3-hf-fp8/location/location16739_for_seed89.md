The place begins as a hole in the mountain's skull—one of a hundred, but this one still bleeding air. You enter through the throat of a collapsed vent, where ancient lava walls have cooled into black ribs slick with ammoniac condensation. The water exists here as an afterthought: droplets that birth themselves from minerals and die on stone within minutes, splitting light into desperate patterns before darkness swallows them. Not rain. Not drip. Sometimes a whole vein of the ceiling will briefly weep, a single silver thread writing and wiping a word no language ever approximated.

The chamber—because despite its cathedral curves it insists on the intimacy of a chamber, a space carved for something that kneels rather than stands—is warm with the fever dream of depth. Your breath plumes once then curls back on itself, ashamed. Perhaps it remembers this was all under an ocean once, that these minerals arranged their marriages under miles of pressure and never properly annulled them when the waters fled. The air tastes of old wedding rings.

Vegetation is a category error here. What grows grows without sun, without soil, without the concept of time as growth measures it. On the sloping walls where seepage gathers longest exist beds of mineral bristle—microscopic crystals assembling and disassembling their lattice structures like crowds changing opinions. Some formations achieve the temporary dignity of visually resembling moss, or perhaps coral skeletons copied without understanding their function. Touch one and it shatters into dust that smells faintly of citrus and sorrow. The chamber accumulates these tiny deaths like conscience entries in a stone notebook.

The floor is alive with movement that registers first as optical illusion—your darkness-adjusted eyes seeing patterns in static. Then individual components resolve: millions upon millions of somethings moving with single-minded purpose none of which involve you. They are each exactly the size of a childhood regret, segmented in that way that makes segmentation seem like a coping mechanism, armored in material harvested from the walls themselves. Their collective appetite has polished the stone beneath them to such perfect smoothness that standing becomes a theological position. When the entire mass shifts direction in perfect synchrony, which it does every seven minutes and forty-three seconds precisely, it produces _the sound_.

Imagine thirty-seven varieties of rain falling onto thirty-seven varieties of metal while being conducted by a lightning strike that holds grudges. Imagine the entire concept of rust deciding to speak suddenly and in chorus. Imagine pain expressing itself as melody through the medium of rockslide. It is loud enough to vibrate your teeth independent of your skull, terrifying until you realize you recognize certain phrases from lullabies sung in the language before language. The sound lasts exactly twelve heartbeats then cuts off with surgical precision, leaving tinnitus that apologizes.

Between soundings you notice the light. Or rather, you notice vision persisting where light has no business being. Certain stains on the walls—mineral? organic? political?—radiate awareness rather than illumination. Objects and shadows swap roles when unobserved. Your shadow has begun following other visitors briefly before returning to you with faintly guilty posture. (There are always other visitors, you realize now. Their footprints intersect yours at angles geometry forbids but etiquette requires ignoring.)

The elevation sickness of the deep manifests as philosophical vertigo: you understand suddenly that every mountain is hollow, that solidity is merely the most popular lie rocks tell each other. At the chamber's exact center—which relocates subtly when approached directly but can be stumbled into while fleeing—stands a column that might be stone or might be the fossilized moment of indecision between stone and not-stone. Through its translucent layers you can see preserved entire ecosystems that evolution forgot to invent: butterflies with gear teeth for wings, trees grown in fractal spiral towards impossible suns, oceans where waves break in equations.

Touching the column is not prohibited because prohibition belongs to worlds where consequences follow choices directly. What happens instead is the column touches back, inserting into your mind a perfect memory of falling upward towards a sky made entirely of floor. You emerge understanding that this chamber exists simultaneously at the mountain's peak and its root, that elevation and depth collapsed into each other centuries ago due to an argument neither remembers clearly but both refuse to apologize for first.

The exit—when you find it, which depends on what you are willing to admit you came here to lose—rejects bodies in the same shape they entered. This is not punishment. Simply, every space must exact its toll of dimensional change, just as every passage requires some small sacrifice of the concept "before."

Outside, if there is still an outside, people will notice something slightly off about your gait, as if your bones now understand secrets about gravity that keep them perpetually in negotiation with the ground. Time will feel improperly salted. Fourteen minutes and thirty-three seconds after each sunset, wherever you are, you will briefly taste minerals and citrus and desire a place you cannot name but never wanted to leave.

The mountain will wait. Mountains are patient in the way only foreverthings can afford to be. Inside its skull, in one of its hundred holes, the sound rehearses the next version of itself. The millions polish floor space for future footsteps. The column records this visitation with all the others in its infinite stone ledger:

"Visited. Changed nothing. Changed everything. Left something behind that was never theirs to bring. 

Would welcome back if welcome still existed here. 

Which it does. 

Which it doesn't.

Repeat."
