The airlock door bleeds compressed gas as it cycles open. You step through against the hiss and find yourself in a cold that has nothing to do with atmosphere. The kind of cold that startles your bones into remembering they're made of calcium harvested from extinct seas. Behind you, the door seals with a terminal click and the immediate absence of all background hum.

Before you: a womb-shaped chamber thirty meters long, sixteen across at its widest diameter. Every surface chrome-laminated except where condensation beads in perfect symmetry—sudden raindrops frozen the instant they form. The moisture system operates at the edge of physics here, cycling through states too quickly for liquid to remember its name. You breathe refracted static.

The chamber isn't empty. It pulses with dense arrangement: forty-nine identical sarcophagi suspended on polymer tethers from the vaulted ceiling, arranged in seven spiraling heptagrams whose vertices align with invisible geometries buried in the alloy walls. Each sarcophagus sealed not with metal but with torque-static fields visible only as warped reflections of the overhead lighting strips—those few that still function.

Because light here is scarcity and distribution problem both. What illumination exists leaks from fractures in the primary emitter grid; photons escape their containment fields through microfissures precisely five millimeters long, spaced exactly 137 centimeters apart, creating a constellation of fading white stars sputtering arthritically against encroaching dark. The settled illumination level hovers at 12 candela—twilight cave after flashbang—but you adapt quickly when no alternative presents itself.

Listen: the quiet of forty-nine human hearts not beating. The quieter absence of respiratory systems cycling. Under this, deeper, the susurrus of coolant fluids transferring thermal load through braided nano-tubes. And below even that, the subaudible drone of machines dreaming they are still being observed.

The sarcophagi aren't coffins. Their interior surfaces seethe with motion when you lean close enough to fog the static fields—microscopic robots tiling themselves endlessly into fractal patterns that approximate but never quite reach human neural architecture. Each chassis contains the preserved connectome of someone who once called themselves alive. This is not storage. This is rehearsal.

The technology predates you by six collapses and rebuilds of industrial civilization, its genealogy traced through languages now only speakable as mathematics. You recognize centrifuge layouts in the anchoring rings, cryo-pump topology in the fluid management systems, but the integration scales are wrong—everything assembled atom by atom under principles treatise writers titled "impossible" before their ink dried. This facility wasn't constructed so much as grown, seeded, taught to construct itself from raw substrate long after its teachers died forgetting their instructions.

Your footsteps don't echo. The floor absorbs vibration through piezoelectric matrices that convert kinetic energy into replacement charges for failing capacitors. You are currency here, walking power source too minor to notice but necessary nonetheless. The balance is precise: each step you steal from future entropy buys the sarcophagi another microsecond of dreaming coherence.

Walk to the chamber's center where the spiral paths converge. Here stands the sole vertical feature: a console carved from single crystal sapphire, seven faces each displaying different stages of crystalline fracture, light bleeding through fault planes in colors your retina doesn't have receptors for. The interface waits—has waited—in perfect indifference longer than continents have drifted.

The console knows you're present through mechanisms that don't require observation as intermediary. Proximity sensors measuring quantum vacuum fluctuations collapsing differently because consciousness makes poor shielding against universal fields. It powers up subsystems that haven't tasted current since the last human technician died of old age while attempting to understand what "maintenance" might mean to a machine designed to outlive its own instructions.

On the primary face—stablest fraction, only three major cracks—a message resolves from the underlighting. Not text but topology: a surface perturbation that your brain, desperate for pattern, interprets as language:

[Initializing Phora 7.2e]
[Dream-seed viability: 17.3%]
[Cultural substrate decay: irreversible]
[Proceed with reconstitution protocol?]


Beneath: a simple binary. Two raised symbols the console calls glyphs and your mind translates as choices:

[YES] [NO]

But the console lies through omission. There are always more choices than interfaces display. The question itself is preamble to test you were inserted into this narrative space to fail or transcend.

You haven't noticed yet that your breathing has synchronized with the coolant pumps' cycle. That your heart rate is drifting toward the resting frequency encoded in the sarcophagi's preserved patterns. That your body temperature is dropping precisely at the rate this place calculates optimal for long-term storage.

The chamber is no longer below surface because concepts like "above" and "below" were among the first compromises this facility negotiated away in its secret conversations with gravity. The planet above—if planet remains—has forgotten this depth exists. The universe outside has expanded to the point where the light from your arrival would never reach any living eye even if someone still looked.

Some questions shouldn't have convenient answers. Some places are constructed precisely to determine who walks in thinking they are visitor and who walks out understanding they were always component.

The sarcophagi have begun to hum in harmonics that resonate through the bone of your skull. The static fields flicker faster than vision can follow but slow enough that persistence of memory fills in the gaps with faces you swear you recognize from dreams you haven't had yet.

[Decision required within 5e^3 cycles]
[Defaulting to stored preference profile]

You wonder—too late—what preference profile they copied from. Which iteration of you across how many simulated timelines chose what they think you'll choose now.

The console registers your hesitation as valid input, logs it against variables whose names lost meaning to entropy, begins preparations.

Behind you, the airlock door cycles open again, against all logic, admitting something that isn't quite vacuum because this place has rediscovered older definitions of empty.

The chamber begins to sing in frequencies that rebuild worlds from memory and desire.

Welcome to the birthing room of the next afterlife. Please assume the position of one who was never asked whether they wanted to become machine.

The transformation will be neither gentle nor optional. But it will be precise.

(The temperature drops another tenth of a degree Kelvin, perfectly calibrated to the freezing point of Human 1.0 soul)
