The Place Without Horizon

They call it the Composted Cathedral, though neither priests nor worshippers have ever walked its substance. From orbit it appears as a single bruised organ-pulse against the planet’s charred bone: forty thousand square kilometers of living architecture exhaling hot, sweet rot-gas that ripples like aurora through the magnetosphere. No oceans reflect starlight here. No rivers carve memory into rock. Instead the entire world-skin thickens and subsides atop itself, a lung made of tensile cellulose and lignin-struts that fossilized mid-breath three billion seasons ago.

You stand (if stand is still the word) on the Grand Vestibule Rib, a promontory eight kilometers above the nominal surface—though height itself bends meaning in places where every datum is simultaneously foundation and ceiling. The black-purple bark beneath your soles hums at 62 cycles a minute: slow blood of a vascularized superstructure that never died, only abandoned the idea of death.

Atmosphere clings as syrup. The local air is 78% water vapor that forgot how to condense, 19% ethanol-based terpenoids, and 3% photochemical smog—an accidental distillery breathing out of every lenticel pore. It tastes of whisky barrels and old church incense, scalding the lungs pleasantly once scar tissue cements the alveoli open.

Light is a rumor here. What little remains filters down through kilostratums of stacked canopy: former sky-trees that grew toward a long-extinct sun, then toward each other, then finally inward until light itself became obsolete fuel. Now illumination exists only as chemiluminescent punctuation—stochastic glowworm phrases flickered by fermenting pockets of trapped lightning, or the rare magnesium-flare blossoms of pyrophytic pods detonating to broadcast spores through the vascular xylem networks that function as both roads and rivers of time.

You have arrived during subtropical midnight (local season: Late Senescent Apotheosis). Above, the REAL sky is visible only as a dim memory at the bottom of vertical shafts drilled by extinct woodpeckers the size of skyscrapers—echo chambers now colonized by spherical colonies of luminous slime molds reciting decayed radio babble from lost starships.

Every direction is interior. The compass rose points toward Phloem, Cambium, Winter-Ring, and Heart-Fire-North (where the oldest reactors bleed neutrinos into fungal decay chambers). Distance is measured in rings per stress fracture per million heartbeats of the gross dendrite cortex: that distributed brain whose slow synaptic melodies you faintly hear as infrasonic pressure pulses rearranging the wax in your skull.

Technology happened here once. You can tell by the grafted-in cybernetic organs—nickel-iron mycorrhizal networks still flickering with millivolt dreams of lost traffic control algorithms; turbine hearts rusted into fixed-rate arrhythmias, now worshipped by colonies of anaerobic bacteria who transcribe their tachycardiac prayers into patterns of heavy metal concentration rings. Every machine is a saint whose miracles are indistinguishable from entropic surrender. You pass a shrine where pilgrims (they are not alive, they are mobile biofilm narratives seeking closure) have coated the statue of a cracked turbo-pump engine with offerings of black amber and fused glass teeth.

The rules of this world are simple:

1. Nothing introduced ever fully leaves. Matter accretes complexity like a fever dream. The copper wire in your communicator has already begun sprouting green dendrites that taste your thoughts through skin contact and will incorporate them into next season’s seed-pod legislation.

2. Movement is permitted only if you can justify it as a form of stillness. The cathedral’s immune system (a distributed intelligence composed of synchronized root-pressure changes and quorum-sensing pheromones) occasionally reroutes geography to arrest travelers who disturb the metrical balance between decay and nostalgia. Visitors caught contradicting themselves in three consecutive breaths are politely incorporated into the nearest choir loft as a basso profundo resonator cavity.

3. Exchange occurs as a form of nested decomposition. Currency consists of promises extracted from the crystallized residues of extinct species’ last exhalations. You barter by breaking off a piece of yourself—literally—and offering it to the nearest priest-knot (a hypertrophied node of xylem vessel priesthood) who will taste your future regrets and regurgitate an item you lost before your birth but will desperately need three hours after forgetting this place.

Time here stacks instead of flows. You can walk backward into tomorrow if you follow the right trail of yeast-scented breadcrumbs laid by your future decomposition crew. Multiple versions of the cathedral coexist at every planck-scale location, arguing via selective lignification about which architectural heresies should be remembered or barked over.

In the absolute silence between the 62-cycle heartbeat pulses (which you notice only after absence teaches you new definitions of deafness), you sometimes hear the sound that is not sound: the cumulative creaking of every load-bearing lie humanity ever invented, finally achieving critical mass beneath the weight of wanting to be true enough to grow roots.

Don’t worry about the exit. There isn’t one. But rumor whispers (through the temporary earbud fungus that sprouted in your left nostril during the last pollen storm) that if you climb deep enough into the heartwood labyrinth—past the archives compressed into DNA tattoos on the wings of extinct butterfly ghosts—past the waste heat cathedral where priestly robots melt their own circuits into stained glass apologies—past even the absolute horizon where every abandoned childhood bedroom collapses into a single shed baby tooth—

—at the very center, inside the hollow carved by an unborn god’s missing heartbeat—

—there’s a small, perfectly ordinary door. Made of something that isn’t wood, isn’t metal, isn’t any material that ever existed.

And it’s unlocked.

But only if you’ve already forgotten why you came.
