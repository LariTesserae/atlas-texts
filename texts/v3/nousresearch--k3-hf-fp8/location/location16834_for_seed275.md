The place begins as a subterranean city-carapace, all its edges still soft and molting. A single colossal root-sprawl tumbled through the planet's crust during some forgotten cataclysm, then fossilized under pressure into a labyrinth of hollow vascular tubes. Now, five hundred years after excavation began, the Dendritic Metropolis fills that interior scaffold with warm breath and flickering life.

You arrive through a ventilation pore wide enough to swallow your transport shuttle. Quarantine vines inspect and approve you before retracting from the airlock's edges. Outside the port window, nothing resembles solid ground—only layered balconies of living bark, twisting staircases of cambial wood, and vast plazas floored with pulsing mycelial membranes. The whole city is a single organism grafted onto itself a thousand thousand times, roots and shoots and tubers crossed until distinction vanished.

Water here exists only as a rumor: bound tight in cellulose bonds, locked in sucrose syrups, traded between symbiotic cells at moisture harvest stations that drip like slow tears onto thirsty tongues. Citizens collect morning dehumoday in the hollow stems of reed-baffles that line every arterial corridor. A child’s cupful is wealth enough to trade for tomorrow’s light.

Cool air, filtered through kilometers of spongy xylem, carries the smell of fresh-cut cedar mating with ozone. The temperature stays steady at the edge of crisp—enough to keep saprophytes polite but not enough to shock the jacketed scales of the lamplighters who patrol by nightsap. No sky here; instead a vaulted ceiling of still-forming lignin lays overhead, dark brown flecked with faint blue-white glow-crusts that mimic stars. Bioluminescent sap fungi follow a programmed cycle of 12 hours wax, 12 wane, giving time its heartbeat though nobody remembers why twelve was chosen.

Sound is conversation held at two paces distance, or the low hum of fungal turbines digesting starch into motive force, or the click-click-click of paper wasps assembling news kites from pulped leaf litter. Mostly, though, the city believes in silence—it listens better that way to its own slow internal debates, the arguments between cambium and cortex about where next to expand, whether to seal or open a dormant cambial pocket, whether the rumble in sector seven is a nutrient vein or an infection entering fever dreams.

Fauna move as citizens among citizens. Mantid architects six meters tall sketch neighborhoods in the air with praying forelimbs before workers swarm to petrify their sketches into ligneous reality. Swarms of firefly dæmons negotiated illumination contracts for every alley way, blinking out graffiti in temporal Morse that only the literate bugs could recombine into meaning before the next pulse erased it. Near the core markets, slow cyclopean beetles the size of airships haul freight-pods on their backs, antennae linked in a living rail network that remapped itself faster than any schedule could print (not that schedules printed; the city preferred whispering tree rings).

Everything is built, yet nothing was planned. The original engineers left only one commandment carved in growth rings at the heartwood senate floor: *If it does not feed back, prune it.* From that recursive seed, urbanism emerged like strangler figs devouring their scaffolding—a city always under erasure, always just coming into policy. Streets thicken overnight into residential trunks, then hollow years later into sewer xylem, then girdle themselves into elevated walkways before gene-scripts turn them back down toward markets again. Citizens update their addresses daily, tattoo maps on forearms that grow new lines as they speak.

Technology lives inside veins. Clockwork hearts of brass and lignin alternate beats with peristaltic pumps of living heartwood. Paper mills are stomachs of giant silkworm larvae fed on shredded history books, excreting blank pages ready for tomorrow’s crimes and couplets. Factories are tumors that swell on schedule, growing desired machines like malformed fruit—once ripe, they drop locomotives made of aerated cork and tensile cellulose thread, fully fueled with pressed sugar and ready to roll down highways of compressed sawdust that crumble behind them into mulch gardens to feed the next crop of gears.

It is not a polite place. Competition occurs at root distance and leaf height. Young shoots assassinate elders with fungal blights brewed in dormitory basements. Loan sharks are carnivorous pitcher plants offering tempting loans of concentrated phosphorus with compound interest payable only in genetic sequences. Debts unmutualized get collected literally—collection thugs carry grafting knives and leave debtor clones growing unaware beneath stairwells, their credit-worthiness siphoned away via parasitic xylem tap until they turn pale yellow and crumple to dust.

Yet cooperation scales upward too. When the entire city hungered for rare earths to repair its disruption engine (a bloom-slotted mechanism at the planet’s core that shields them from cosmic radiation by singing the mantle into harmony), every citizenlet from pollination drone to parliamentarian dropped a single enzyme into the shared bloodstream. Overnight a thousand scalpel-leaf drones erupted from existing architecture, burrowed down through fossil faultlines, and mined the needed ions while singing in chorus so complex the planet’s magnetic field danced auroras in the aboveground night sky nobody had seen in centuries.

Weirdness here is index 1.7 because it turns out a city that is alive and dreaming dreams it doesn’t fully understand sometimes sleepwalks. Every thirty-three years the whole metropolis exhales a pollen-season of architecture: staircase flowers open inverted towers, elevator bulbs drop lift-ovoids that hatch into random buildings floating upside down until a breeze pollinates them with purpose. During one such delirium a sector the size of a province decided it was actually a novel and began growing text instead of streets—citizens woke up breathing paragraphs, drinking punctuation from leaf cups, making love in the margins until the comma plague ended the sentence.

But tonight it is quiet. Tonight street-level is a damp dark hush broken only by the watch-song of sap drip monitors clicking their stations safe. You walk alone on a footbridge of living wood that remembers every tread since germination. In the distance a train of adventurer spores lifts toward the ventilation wound you entered through, seeking new worlds to infest with towers and traders and treaties written in growth rings.

Below you, in the core quiet where gravity pools like old thoughts, the city’s heartwood dreams of sunlight it has never tasted but knows by description and desire. And perhaps beyond that dream—distant and uncertain like a myth—lies another layer, where this world is itself only the root-thread of some larger organism still falling through darkness, still deciding whether or not to bloom into a place even stranger than this.

Keep walking. The walkway ahead is just beginning to bud guardrails. It won’t be here long, and neither will you, unless you decide to stay and add your own rings to the conversation time conducts in living wood through living dark.
