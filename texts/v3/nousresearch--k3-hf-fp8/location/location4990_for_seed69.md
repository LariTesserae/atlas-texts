The world is called Tarth Maddern — not one planet but many hundreds of fault-locked ellipsoids, each the size of a whole moon, fused like bubbles in stone a million miles above nothing. Gravity is local, whimsical; each fragment holds its own level. Between them yawn volumes of lightless air where night rains pour endlessly without ever reaching bottom. You stand on the edge of one such fragment, a place known only as the Fourth Subtle Base of Opaline Industry.

Water: It does not flow here, it lingers. Every surface carries a film of itself. The black basalt under your boots is varnished, glass-smooth from aeons of condensation. Minute stalactites of calcite drip up from overhangs, growing toward the warmer air below. When you breathe, mist rolls off your tongue in ropes. Far off, cataracts the width of provinces churn over the jagged rims of neighboring shards, their roar arriving half-belated, like a storm heard through a wall of memory.

Vegetation: What grows must grow in three dimensions. Vines as thick as bridges loop between towers of columnar basalt, each leaf the size of a temple door, dark green and opalescent under the red-black sheen of water. Clusters of pale fruit hang like lanterns at every joint, luminous with faint biotic phosphor. Roots hang suspended in air, drinking directly from the fog. Some shrubs have exchanged soil entirely for a symbiosis with rust — their trunks are wrought-iron filigree, fleshed with lichens that flicker like neon signs spelling extinct languages.

Temperature: Warm as the mouth of a sleeping dragon. The air is saturated with latent heat, exhaled from unseen engines still turning somewhere deep within the planet-bubble’s core. Sweat evaporates too slowly to cool; instead it pools, gathers others, forms tiny rivulets down your spine until your clothing becomes a sodden, living thing.

Elevation: High, absurdly so. Twenty kilometers above the fragment’s center of mass, you stand on a platform cantilevered from the root-face of a mountain that is itself upside-down. Above you, other continents drift. Below you, the night rainstorm of a thousand years falls perpetually, each droplet catching the faint glow of industry far across the gulf.

Erosion: Everything is already ruined to perfection. Boulders wear faces worn smooth by millennia of pressure-less dripping. Machinery stands half-dissolved into geology: once-bright gears now interpenetrated with crystal, still ghosting through their rotations one tooth every century. The architecture here achieved equilibrium with decay before your species existed and will continue politely deteriorating long after.

Scale: The horizon is a crowd of other horizons. Jagged walls of continent rise all around, each lit at different epochs by the flare of distant forges. To walk across this one fragment is to cross a plateau larger than continents on lesser worlds. Distant shapes move on those slopes — megaveins of migratory coral, cities walking on legs of lightning-struck glass, forests that migrate like herds — but they are unreachably far and none concern themselves with you.

Density: There is no empty space, only crowdedness at different scales. Every meter of air swarms with glass-winged insects that eat water and excrete brief sparks. The ground is a pavement of fallen spore-pods half the size of your fist, each actively hatching into something that resembles a steam engine made of petals. Even silence is dense: it presses in on your ears like cotton soaked in oil.

Built: The only structures still visible from the Age of Ingenuity are the cranes — skeletal towers of weatherproofed math that once lowered factories down from orbit. Their booms now support ecosystems instead of cables: hanging gardens of filter-fabric draped in loops that span abysses, each leaf edged with tiny turbines whirring in the perpetual wind of falling rain. Here and there a single building remains intact, faceted like a beetle’s eye, its lights flickering out sequences no one has read in a million years.

Tech: Industry never stopped here; it simply became subtle. The entire fragment is a single, slow machine still executing its final subroutine. Every droplet on every leaf contains a nanofactory idly transcribing the DNA of extinct species into new proteins. Gravity gradients are harvested by vast networks of biological piezoelectrics humming inaudibly in the bones of the landscape. Somewhere below, impossible deep, something still smelts darkness into metal, and the cooling towers of that process are geysers of pure shadow venting eternity.

Light: None. Or rather, the wrong kind. The massive eyes of native megafauna drift like moons overhead, each pupil dilating to the size of a city, reflecting nothing. Occasionally one blinks and for a moment a continent-wide flashbulb illuminates everything in negatives of itself — then everything is twice as dark after. You navigate by the taste of metals in the air, by the faint electric chatter of spores arranging themselves into the shapes of forgotten letters on your tongue.

Fauna: They are the night made articulate. Great lowing beasts like assembled clouds graze on the fog banks, ingesting them with mouths that are also caves. Their digestive tracts are lit by continuous lightning where storms the size of cities dissolve and reform. Predators are worse: spindles of articulated darkness that fold themselves between raindrops. The smallest animal here is a mite that constructs entire civilizations on the surface of a single droplet suspended from your eyelash, then sacrifices them all to an invisible god when you blink.

Weirdness: Time is a local currency. In the deeper folds where fog is densest, centuries pass in the space between heartbeats; climb a ridge and whole empires rise and fall before your next step. Objects remember being other objects: a pebble in your pocket dreams of having been a moon, a moon above dreams of being a pebble. The cardinal directions are Jealousy, Compost, Gravity, and Oblivion.

Sound: You do not hear so much as inhabit. The landscape is a single ongoing chord struck once at the birth of the universe and never allowed to resolve. From beneath: the grinding bass of continents remembering they are siblings. From above: the shriek of millions of wings never quite in phase. Around you: a constant whispering half-voice composed of every conversation you’ve ever failed to have, repeating on loops that lengthen as you try to listen closer.

Dynamic: Every moment is an orchestra tuning itself forever. Trees uproot themselves and walk away when the conversation around them gets too boring. Clouds coagulate into the shapes of things that used to worship them, then forget and rain themselves out in despair. The ground beneath you is currently considering whether to become a lakebed or a memory — leaning slightly toward memory, but you might still sway it if you stay interesting enough.

Do not linger here too long. The longer you stand still the faster you grow into the landscape — a few travelers have gone unnoticed for a century and been repurposed as minor tourist attractions. The Fourth Subtle Base of Opaline Industry is still hiring, but the wages are paid in alternate pasts and the retirement plan is optional in the most absolute sense.
