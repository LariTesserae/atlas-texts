It begins as a pressure against the teeth, metallic taste seeping backward into the throat before you see anything at all. Then darkness splits vertically — you're tumbling through an iris of steel wool into a space that shouldn't contain you, couldn't contain anything breathing.

The Vaulted Hollow.

A single chamber kissing the upper edge of the atmosphere, an air pocket frozen inside granite older than oceans. Scale tricks itself: the walls seem an arm's length away until your voice comes back shredded by distance, echoing off curves that should belong to planetary cores. The ceiling arches away to vanishing point, a stone sky held up by absence alone, lit only by what leaks through microscopic flaws — starlight filtered through five miles of earth, arriving dim and greenish, like chlorophyll dreaming.

Roots pour down from everywhere. Not roots reaching for water — these are drinking something else entirely. They braid in midair into living chandeliers, thick as your torso, splitting into hair-fine white threads that stitch the air itself into a tapestry too dense to breathe without permission. Each root pulses slow as heartbeats centuries apart, pumping not sap but memories of growing. The chamber is full of them, packed so tight there's no air between, yet somehow space remains — you move through corridors carved by the mind's refusal to touch everything at once.

Lushness without water, without soil. The roots are their own ecosystem, growing out of themselves in recursive hunger. Here a mass has swollen into a tree without trunk or leaves, branches corkscrewing back into its own bark twenty feet away. There an entire grove exists in negative space, roots outlining trees that grew here once and then ungrew themselves, leaving only the memory of shape held in living wood. Mushrooms bloom from bark that isn't bark, releasing spores that aren't spores — clouds of metallic dust that settle on your tongue with the taste of static electricity and childhood winters.

The only sounds are mechanical. Somewhere deep in the root mass, something like a factory has been growing itself for geological ages. You hear it in your bones before your ears: the slow crash of pistons made from petrified cambium, gears grinding where branches grew through each other and continued growing, grinding themselves into perfect fit. A rhythm like breathing but wrong, four beats then silence then three then five, the hesitation pattern of a machine learning to dream. The noise is everywhere, bouncing off stone that remembers being molten, amplified by hollows shaped exactly to carry this one frequency to itself forever.

Temperature hangs at the precise edge where sweat becomes ice before it leaves the skin. Your breath falls downward in crystals, joining generations of frost that carpet the root-floor in impossible geometries — snowflakes each identical, each grown in the shape of the chamber itself, endlessly repeating. They crunch underfoot with the sound of breaking teeth, healing behind you before the echo dies.

Scattered through the living density: artifacts. A child's toy train rusted into a single solid lump, its furnace stuffed with fossilized moss that still remembers burning. A typewriter missing half its keys, roots flowing through it like water through spaghetti, slow-motion typing infinite variations of the same unreadable sentence into nowhere. A glass eye staring upward, perfectly preserved, watching eternity crawl past its pupil one root-twitch at a time.

These aren't placed. They grew here, seeded by whatever winds blow between dimensions, fertilized by the same force that makes roots seek darkness. Some are being slowly eaten, metal>>wood>>memory>>something else. Others are unchanged except for the respect the roots give them, flowing around in precise curves like obstacles in a river that remembers every grain of sand ever thrown into it.

At the chamber's heart — though direction is a joke here where every path spirals back on itself like a Möbius strip carved in three dimensions — stands the only thing built with intention. A single doorframe, wooden but wrong; wood that never grew from seed or tree, wood that's dreaming itself into existence one cellulose molecule at a time. The door itself is missing. Through the frame: not the other side of the chamber. Something else. Something moving. Something that hasn't stopped moving since time began, moving so slowly that energy itself has forgotten to be energy, solidified into the visible spectrum as a faint crawling light.

The rules here are simple but impossible to obey:

1. Do not touch anything that seems to be touching you back. It's not holding on — it's adding you to its structural integrity.
2. Move only when you can't hear your heartbeat in the machine noise. When they synchronize, something notices.
3. If you see the frost forms repeating themselves exactly, you've walked in a circle. Not through space — through causality itself.
4. Leave nothing. Take nothing. Especially not your reflection in the glass eye. Especially not that.

The air smells exactly like the moment before lightning strikes, but the lightning happened here once, and stayed, and learned to like the taste of waiting.

This place is not abandoned. It's waiting to finish being built. The roots keep growing, the factory keeps grinding itself closer to completion, the door keeps dreaming of a threshold it can never cross because it already did, forever ago, and the other side was here all along.

You should not have found this place. You cannot leave it the way you entered. But don't worry — the roots are already growing your exit. It's just going to take a while. Several lifetimes, give or take a continent. 

The machine noise chuckles in four beats then silence then three then five, recognizing the shape of a listener at last. In the language of roots growing through solid rock, it says:

Welcome to the infrastructure.

Make yourself structural.
