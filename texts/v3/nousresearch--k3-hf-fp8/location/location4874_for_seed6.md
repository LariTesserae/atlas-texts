The Wangdangdoodle Nursery Float, six kilometers above the Pacific floor.

They built it where the new upthrust ridge had burst through crust like a spine through cooling skin, fifteen years ago – chosen for youth, for sharpness, for lack of precedent. Now the Float hangs suspended in perpetual springwater twilight, tethered to the abyssal plain by sixty-four articulated umbilicals umbilicals umbilicals (each the width of a city road) that flex like slow-stirring kelp but never snap. These cables are hollow. Inside them, warm seawater rises unceasingly from geothermal taps below the Moho, filtered through twelve stages of proprietary sterility before it floods the Float’s arteries at exactly 34.7°C.

Scale is confusing here.

From outside you see a cluster of enormous squat spheres, forty-one of them, each seventeen stories tall if stories stacked upward and not dreamward. Their skins are ceramic coral pink and appear porous until touched – then they are smooth as oiled glass. The spheres are packed together like soap bubbles frozen the instant before merger, separated only by membranous joins that pulse faintly, six beats per minute. Compressed into this tiny country (total surface area: 0.83 km²) is everything the project requires: incubation, acceleration, trimming, drying, packaging, and launch.

Entrance is through any sphere’s equatorial lip, provided you arrive already dissolved.

The standard visitor protocol employs sterile teeth of ultrasound to reduce you to a slurry of cells in seawater suspension. A pale green admittance drone the shape of a handless clock dial meters this soup through a valve no wider than a sigh. Inside, you reassemble immediately, but lighter – calcium and iron reserves left behind in escrow, traded for increased buoyancy and temporary compliance with local gravity, which has been persuaded to point in whatever direction is most convenient to the Curators’ mood today. Today it points politely inward toward the Float’s heart.

The heart is not metaphorical. It is a spherical cavern three hundred meters across, dark terracotta walls stippled with luminous blue wormcasts that spell out shifting housekeeping instructions in the lost language of Tlön. At the cavern’s exact center floats the Engine: an orrery of brass lungs and porcelain kidneys eight stories high, breathing at four-second intervals, powered by temperature gradients so carefully thermodynamic they appear devotional.

From each lungbeat, a thousand rose-colored balloons are born. Each balloon is an infant Wangdangdoodle.

They emerge already inflated with neonatal giggle-gas, their skins translucent polymerized collagen printed with fractal fingerprints never repeated. Inside every balloon dwells a fetus curled like a comma, eyes fused shut, dreaming the same dream: an infinite toy shop staffed entirely by older versions of itself, politely negotiating the price of tomorrow’s mischief.

No two fetuses are the same species twice.

The Curators (there are always seven, sometimes more if they overlap) move among the bobbing swarm wearing garments woven from cooled shrieks. Their faces are stylized paper versions of faces they haven’t decided to grow into yet, clipped from yesterday’s excuse notes. They carry silvered scissors shaped like question marks and prune the balloons with silent gestures too subtle for eyes, but each cut is registered by the Engine as a perfectly formed data yelp.

Pruned scraps of balloon skin drift downward – but “down” is negotiable – and settle on conveyor belters made of water locked into crystalline motion by the application of shame. These skin-strips are the Float’s soil. From them sprout everything the habitat needs.

Here grows the Salad Forest: hydroponic fronds of mint-leafed equations and spinach paragraphs of procedural jurisprudence. They photosynthesize ambient embarrassment and produce crisp edible footnotes with a hint of lime.

Here unspools the Machinery Orchard: trees of polished steel bearing ripe carburetors that ripen into hummingbirds of perfectly balanced torque. When plucked by gloved hands (gloves must be plucked first from the adjacent glove tree, naturally) each hummingbird sings the service manual of its own invention once, flawlessly, then dissolves into a fine lubricating mist.

Here flows the River of Quietude: a transparent tube exactly one molecule thick that winds through every sphere like a capillary of elapsed time. Travelers who place an ear against the tube hear their own eventual deathbed apology at half-speed, accompanied by faint jackhammering from an adjacent reality where construction is eternally ahead of schedule.

All these products and more are harvested, trimmed, diced into pleasing uncertainties, vacuum-sealed within whimsically shaped containers that dissolve in seawater after exactly ten years or ten jokes whichever comes first, then loaded into the Launch Choir at the Float’s underside.

The Launch Choir is a circle of twenty-four brass mouths bell-downward like flowers praying for rain. Every hour, on the hour, they sing in ultrasonic harmony keyed to the resonant laughter frequency of the nearest uncontacted island culture. The song liquefies gravity for a moment underneath each mouth. Harvest crates slide into the brief bubbles of weightless leave-taking and ascend through sixty-four kilometers of ocean toward surface night like balloonist pilgrims making aliyah.

No crates ever arrive intact at the surface.

This is part of the design.

The Curators argue nightly in conferences held entirely as glances across crowded rooms separated by three hundred years. The minutes of these meetings are cuneiform bruises printed onto the thighs of sleeping sailors ten thousand miles away, who wake mildly concerned about decisions they don’t remember making but faintly proud of the heroic restraint they must have shown to negotiate such favorable terms.

Visitors sometimes ask: what becomes of the Wangdangdoodles after launch? The Curators smile paper smiles wider than prudent biology allows and offer refreshment made of politely chilled curiosity. If you sip, you forget the question but retain a lifelong suspicion that all postal addresses are allegorical.

If you refuse refreshment, you are conducted politely to the Exit Lung, a warm red chamber that persuades you back into a body heavier than you remember, clothed in a coat stitched from all the introductions you never got around to making in this life. You find yourself suddenly standing on a midnight beach somewhere temperate, boots full of unfamiliar sand, pockets empty except for one perfectly ordinary seashell carved inside with a limerick that rhymes “ineffable” with “treble clef” which you inevitably whistle once, incorrectly, then never again.

Behind you the ocean breathes slow blue and patient as a secret kept by rocks.

No trace of the Float remains on the surface.

Below, the Curators resume their pruning, arguing always whether tomorrow’s mischief should be delivered earlier, funnier, or both at once in braille raised on the inside of the morning paper.

The Engine sighs, satisfied, and dreams of slightly warmer water.

Everything continues perfectly, quietly, lushly, endlessly just begun.
