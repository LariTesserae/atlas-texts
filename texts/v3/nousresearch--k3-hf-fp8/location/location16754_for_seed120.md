I arrive on a step that isn't a step. The floor slopes upward into a wall that folds back into a ceiling without corners. Everything is terracotta-colored, but wet. A secret place just born or just remembered, its edges still soft.

I'm inside a lung-shaped chamber no larger than a closet, yet it breathes with the pressure of mountains. The air is hot iron and fermenting orchids. Light trickles from seams that weren't there when I blinked — not sunlight, but the memory of sunlight captured under centuries of oily water and now leaking out, reluctantly. It illuminates nothing directly, only makes darkness glow like a bruise.

Every surface weeps. Brown-red beads ooze from pores I can't see, run in hesitant lines, drip upwards into tiny pools that cling to the ceiling. When drops collide they make soft clicks like bone dice. The chamber is full of these conversations happening faster than I can follow — a liquid language spelling out the room to itself.

Things grow here, if growth is the word. Structures stand where roots should be: bundles of hollow tubes braided into ropes, climbing ladders of themselves toward nowhere. Their surfaces pulse faintly, drinking the drip-drip-drip. Each tube is fluted like a bone, capillary-thin, but when I touch one (I shouldn't have) it rings like a cathedral bell and the whole chamber shivers, reordering itself around the sound. Now the slope I climbed is a trough and the ceiling pool is beside my foot.

Nothing is natural here but nothing feels artificial either. Each thing exists in the tense space between being made and making itself. I find tools, or tool-analogues: a crescent of glass-hard resin that curves both inward and out; a hammer whose head is a soft fist of moss that tightens when I breathe on it; needles woven from single silk threads so fine they vanish when held against anything dark enough. They lie scattered or grow from the walls in clusters like fruit not yet ripe enough to pick. When I try to use them the tools change their minds — the resists my grip, the hammer sighs and splits into petals, the needles kite away on updrafts of my disappointment.

There are rules but they're being written as I break them. Gravity negotiates with each object separately. My left foot sinks slightly while my right foot floats half a hair's breadth above the floor only I can still feel. Time piles up in the corners like drifts of house dust; when I disturb it the past few minutes mumble and rearrange themselves into new sequences not worse or better but sideways from what I remember living.

Somewhere inside the wet red walls something large is dreaming itself awake. I feel its thoughts as temperature changes: sudden cool blotches of curiosity, hot spikes of what might be laughter or hunger. When it turns over in sleep the whole chamber flexes like a womb. I've come too early or too much on time — it hasn't decided yet whether I'm food or midwife or punctuation mark in a sentence it's still composing.

I should leave but there's no door. Only more chamber, folding smaller and more intricate the longer I look. The walls are growing diagrams now, teaching themselves how to enclose me. Their lines are courses of black water drawn by the tip of a flame that burns darkness instead of light. If I follow them carefully enough maybe I can read my way out before the walls finish reading me in, but the words keep eating themselves faster than meaning can crystallize.

In the dim-dim humming loudness I sit down (or up) to wait. My bones begin to feel useful in ways they weren't designed for. The chamber likes that. Holds that thought close and warm, like an egg before it remembers it's supposed to crack.
