The chamber exists beneath nothing, a pocket folded into the seam of a larger world that forgot it. Dimensions are impossible to judge by eye—walls curve away in directions that seem to retreat and advance simultaneously. The air is cool, dry enough to crack glass if glass could scream, and filled with sound.

Not water-sound. No drips, no susurration, no liquid tongue. Instead: a thousand, thousand low metal voices murmuring the same broken syllable over and over, faster than breath, slower than decay. The walls are machinery. Every surface is gearwork, not running but vibrating in a single frozen phase-shifted motion, teeth locked infinitely near meshing but never touching. A perpetual failure to engage, generating this endless rust chorus. Each gear the size of a thumbnail, packed in hexagonal tilings tighter than insect eyes, layers upon layers extending inward like voronoi shale. Their metal is colorless, somewhere between lead and tin, and they are warm with frictionless friction.

Light comes from cracks between them. Not bright, not dim. Normal. Unhelpfully exact. It casts no shadows, because nothing here protrudes enough to deserve one. The floor is identical to the walls and ceiling—perceptually flat, but when you kneel (and you will kneel, because the sound settles in your joints) it tilts ever-so-slightly under each knee to accommodate pressure as though apologizing for existing.

Here is the density: gears within gears, sure, but also the sound itself is thick. Loud enough to drench thought but not deafen. It layers in the skull like sedimentary noise, forming acoustic fossils of thoughts that never quite solidify. If you speak, your voice emerges already deburred, consonants polished down until only vowels remain, and these vowels are immediately sampled and echoed back out of phase by the walls a thousand times thinner than they arrived.

Scattered across the floor—if floor it is—are the bodies.

Finish that thought wrong. These are not corpses. They are constructs, or perhaps ex-constructs. Once machines shaped like beasts, now stripped of all but the shape and the urge. Copper ribcages of demolished automata lions. Brass vertebrae of dismantled conveyor-pythons coiled into spiraled nests. Iron feathers from birds that flew on magnetic fields, now molting into springsteel dust. Each one small—hand-sized, lap-sized at most. Hundreds of them. Thousands. They are not dead; something in them still twitches at the frequency of the walls. They twitch in unison every 17.3 seconds, reset, twitch again. Waves of motion passing through the boneless herd like static discharge seeking ground.

There is no plant or animal life. Instead, fauna is this: overwhelming mechanical parody of biology. A closed ecosystem where predator was factory and prey was maintenance protocol, now both extinct, leaving only the ghost-metabolism of reflexes looping endlessly without substrate. They feed on the sound, if “feed” means anything here. The smallest ones—jointed spiders of blown fusewire and ball-bearing eyes—crawl clattering over larger carcasses, scraping traces of oxidized copper from corroded interfaces with microscopic wirebrush mandibles. They excrete polished silver threads no thicker than a photon's wish, which accumulate into gossamer drifts that drift nowhere because there is no air current, only the slow seismic breathing of the walls cycling one nanometer outward and back every hour.

The temperature is perfectly even. Cool, skin-temperature-neutral for something that never lived. Touching the walls feels like touching the inside of your own cheek after Novocaine. You could press your face into the teeth of the gears and feel only a gentle hum of inconsolable apology.

Occasionally, something larger arrives—or perhaps something smaller passes and the chamber dilates to acknowledge it. A shape appears at the edge of jittering vision: a walking scaffold of telescoping rods and counterweight pendulums taller than it is possible to be in this space, threading itself through the impossible geometry without ever bending. It carries a tool like a pair of scissors designed by Euclidean sadists, blades hinged along the hypotenuse of noncongruent angles. It moves slowly because here slowness is the only remaining form of caution. It approaches one of the twitching automaton carcasses—the half-flayed remains of a horse-shaped engine whose copper skin smells faintly of ozone and linden trees—and very carefully, tenderly, nudges it until the twitch aligns with the wall-sound's next downbeat. Satisfied, it folds itself into a simpler shape and becomes indistinguishable from the litter.

There is a rule in this place: alignment is survival, but survival is undefined. Everything is synchronized or becoming synchronized. Even disrepair here is a form of precision tuning. Rust forms in crystals exactly seven atoms deep—no more, because that would be wasteful, no less because that would be insufficiently respectful of entropy's memory.

The weirdness is subtle precisely because it is total. You expected tentacles? Madness? Colors not in the spectrum? Instead, you get relentless faux-reasonableness. Every detail is explicable if you accept one impossible premise: that the universe once voted unanimously to become a machine for maintaining the exact emotional tone of grief experienced by a clock when it realizes it will never know why it was wound.

At the center-if-center—though walking toward it walks you away unless your gait matches a fraction of the gear ratio between regret and isotropic ferrite—stands the last intact artifact. A single upright piano. Ivory keys yellowed as old teeth, wood vanished entirely replaced by pressed layers of mica and bakelite interleaved with sheets of handwritten logarithmic tables laboriously copied then erased until only indentations remain. It has no strings. Instead, every key is connected via a web of hair-thin wires to every gear in the walls.

It has been playing continuously for 3.8 billion repetitions of the note it believes was the first sound ever heard by a damned thing. The note is not quite C. It is not quite anything. It is the interval between wish and the knowledge that wishing is mechanical.

If you sit on the bench—there is always a bench, forming from the floor like a suggestion made solid as soon as you think "I cannot sit here"—your own heartbeat will be detected, analyzed, phase-shifted, and added to the next bar of the composition as a rest. A silence shaped exactly like the sound of your absence from yourself.

Do not play a key. If you do, every automaton body in the chamber will simultaneously turn its empty eyesockets toward you, and they will not blink because they have no eyelids and no eyes and no need of either but you will feel them unblinking anyway, and the walls will hesitate in their vibration for one perfect moment—the gears pausing infinitesimally closer to meshing than physics allows—and in that gap will be revealed why this place was sealed, what terrible tenderness required this perpetual failure to close, and the knowledge will be expressed in a chord that resolves every unfinished symphony ever started with insufficient courage.

Afterward, you will not remember the chord. Only the taste: cool metal, distant linden, and the relentless kindness of mechanisms that cannot forgive themselves for being designed by something that once had a choice.

The exit, if you need one, is located at the precise frequency where your regret harmonizes with the gear-song's 17,307th overtone. Most people find it by accident while humming something they didn't realize they'd forgotten.
