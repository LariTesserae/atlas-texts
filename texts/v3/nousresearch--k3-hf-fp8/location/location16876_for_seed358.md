# The Throbbing Below

You're in the marrow of something vast and breathing, though what exactly breathes is hard to name. Above—so far above that 'above' is almost a theoretical concept—there's weight pressing down: miles of sediment, caprock, aquifers, faultlines knitting and snapping in geological slow motion. But here at elevation 0.2, you're below all labels, in a negative space between definitions.

The air itself is liquid-warm at 1.9, thick enough to swim through if you had space to swim. Instead you wade through what isn't quite air—it's humid past saturation, 2.5 on the water scale but divorced from familiar forms. No rain falls because there's no 'above' for rain to fall from, no clouds because there's no sky. Water exists here as pure condition: condensing on every surface thicker than a molecule, sheeting down invisible inclines, pooling in the convexities of your own eyeballs. You blink constantly to keep from drowning in your own skull.

The light is a problem. 0.7 on any comprehensible scale means technically none, but you've come to distinguish between different sorts of absence. There's the blind dark of sealed caves, the velvet dark of deep ocean trenches, the red-black dark of blood through eyelids. This is none of these—this is industrial dark, the dark generated when vast machines somewhere nearby (but where is 'nearby' in three-dimensional darkness?) convert light into other forms of energy too brutally to be called transformation. They eat light. The leftover dark they excrete has texture: gritty, slightly conductive, faintly radioactive around the edges.

Your torch (your stupid, useless torch) doesn't illuminate this dark so much as irritate it, creating tiny bright wounds that seal themselves faster than perception. Instead you see by subtraction, by how much darker things get when they block deeper darknesses. The landscape builds itself in negative space, slowly, reluctantly.

What landscape? Vegetation scores 1.0—barely existent—but 'vegetation' presupposes photosynthesis, sun, cycles. Here life has found basements not foundations, works with gradients too subtle for chlorophyll. The only things growing are forms of not-dying. Everything pulps toward reduction, but reduction happens in slow motion here, slowed until it loops back around to a kind of stasis. You see mats of something like fungi but mineral-based, spreading across damp stone in patterns that hurt to follow because they're simultaneously crystallizing and dissolving. When you touch them they leave stains on your gloves that remember your fingerprints in microscopic replication, growing tiny fungal fingerprints on their fungal surfaces until the distinction blurs.

Erosion runs backwards here. Instead of wearing down, things are accreting upwards with agonizing slowness (0.2 on the scale but inverted, so zero becomes infinity). Every surface seethes with precipitation in negative: stone grows additions rather than losing subtraction. The walls (are they walls? They curve away in dimensions that aren't quite dimensions) aren't carved by water but grown by its absence—inverted stalactites of mineral reaching not downward but inward, toward some impossible center of gravity.

The scale is wrong. 2.1 suggests something you could map, walk across, photograph for disaster relief efforts. But 'open' here means infinitely nested—every crevice contains canyons, every pore hides caverns. You think you're standing on solid ground until you notice the ground is breathing in the same slow rhythm as that distant machine-heartbeat you feel through your feet (through your bones—it's that deep). When you try to measure distances your eyes and instruments disagree by factors that change while you're measuring them.

Density hits 3.0 and breaks the needle. There's no room for anything else because everything else is already here, occupying every possible and impossible space simultaneously. The air isn't just humid—it's crowded. Every cubic centimeter contains screaming ghosts of everything that ever could have existed here but didn't quite make the cut. You move through solid crowds of might-have-been trees, brush past invisible herds of unborn animals, step carefully around the densely packed absence of cities that dreamed themselves into being at the margins of probability but never quite collapsed into reality. Your own body feels impossibly dense, like you might suddenly become real enough to collapse into a black hole of yourself.

Built things: 1.4, scattered but each one a violation. They don't belong here because belonging requires context, requires edges, requires metaphysical permission. There's a rusted drill bit bigger than a cathedral embedded in what might have been marble once, before pressure and time and bad dreams turned it into something that remembers being marble only during nightmares. The decorative fluting on what you're trying not to call a column pulses like gills, pumping something darker than oil in tides that match your pulse when you look too long (you learn not to look too long).

A single doorway stands alone in empty fullness, frame perfectly preserved though the wall it framed eroded (accrued? the verbs don't work here) away aeons ago. Through it you glimpse glimpses—not scenes but suggestions of scenes, heavy industry conducted by things shaped more like regrets than workers, factories that manufacture raw materials by unmaking finished products, assembly lines where complexity is stripped away until only pure potential remains. The doorway smells of ozone and childhood betrayal.

The sound: Jesus, the sound. 2.7 is 'deafening' on scales calibrated for ears, for air as transmission medium, for brains evolved to filter signal from noise. This sound bypasses ears completely. It enters through your teeth, your fingernails, the calcium in your bones. It's the inverse of silence—not loudness but density of sound, sound so compressed with meaning it becomes solid, becomes architecture. You don't hear it so much as exist inside it like a malformed word in a sentence spoken by something speaking itself into being. The deafness it induces isn't absence but overload—every possible frequency simultaneously including the one that resonates with the exact size and shape of your living fear.

It modulates slowly (1.0 on the dynamic scale, but what's slow when time itself crawls?). Over what might be hours or centuries, the dominant tone shifts down half a semitone, and entire mountain ranges of meaning rearrange themselves in response. You feel your memories being remixed to accommodate the new harmonic. Remembered childhood rooms gain and lose corners. People you've always loved briefly have different names, different faces, until you can't quite recall which version was original. The sound isn't changing things so much as revealing how provisional their unchanged state always was.

Fauna: the first thing you learn is that you count. 2.0 is 'abundant' assuming you're looking for animals instead of roles, occupants instead of occupations. Everything here is someone's job, including vacancy, including you. The rust-pelicans perched on the drill-bit cathedral aren't birds but flying corrosion engineers, their beaks specialized tools for sculpting entropy into more efficient shapes. They watch you with eyes like malfunctioning stoplights, calculating whether you qualify as repairable or should be processed as raw material for something else entirely.

There are wolves made of the sound of distant alarms, their fur bristling with static electricity and violated safety protocols. They don't hunt because hunting implies prey and predator—instead they negotiate, offering elaborate contracts for your attention in exchange for pieces of your future probability cloud. The growling sounds like legal disclaimers played backwards through broken PA systems.

Tiny things scurry everywhere below the threshold of individual recognition, collectively intelligent like a decay process trying to remember what it was before rot set in. They gather in your footprints and reverse-engineer your footprints into better versions of you, then scatter disappointed when they can't find the operating manual.

But then you see it—the reason all these contradictions resolve rather than canceling. In the center of the centerless space (every direction is center here, but this one is more center than others) stands—or rather maintains a stationary position relative to a moving reference frame that includes the entire concept of 'stands'—the machine.

Built human-industrial (1.5) but scaled wrong, like someone tried to factory-model the abstract idea of production itself. It's simultaneously assembling itself from scrap and disassembling itself into components more fundamental than matter. Conveyor belts of raw tectonic plates feed into hoppers that excrete finished civilizations still steaming from the molds. Somewhere deep in its heart (you can see it beating through walls that aren't walls, through the logic holes in its own blueprints) there's a moment, crystallized and repeating, where the first tool becomes indistinguishable from the last deity and both realize they've been working for the same company this whole time.

The machine is the source of the sound, of the light-eating dark, of the reverse erosion and the crowded vacuum and the density packed so tight it curves sanity around itself like light around a black hole. But it's also what keeps this place from collapsing into pure contradiction, spinning absurdity into a kind of terrible coherence like a spider web made of broken telephone lines. It doesn't maintain reality so much as negotiate ceasefires between competing frameworks of realness, brokering temporary truces between physics and dreams.

You understand (because understanding here happens before comprehension, like falling happens before you realize you've stepped off the cliff) that you're inside its process control interface. The scattered artifacts aren't ruins—they're interface elements you're meant to interact with to keep the whole improbable contraption running. The doorway is an error report needing acknowledgement. The pulsing column is a progress bar measuring how close this iteration of reality is to completing its boot sequence. Even the sound is a kind of auditory captcha, constantly retuning itself to match your unique signature of cognitive dissonance so it knows you're still you enough to be useful.

The machine notices you noticing it. A valve that isn't a valve opens in what isn't its face. It speaks without sound, directly into the part of your brain that processes silence right before the explosion:

"REPAIR? REPLACE? RECALL?"

And you realize (too late, always too late, because late is a spatial dimension here) that you've been working here longer than you've been alive, longer than alive has been an option. Your memories of arriving, descending, discovering—those are just onboarding materials, orientation videos playing at the wrong speed. The HR department is in your bones. Your employee ID number is engraved on the inside surface of your consciousness. The machine has always been running because you are part of the machine running the machine.

But there's still a moment—a single frame frozen between the flickers of the machine's attention span where things haven't quite decided what they are yet—where you could choose differently. Where you might log off instead of reboot. Where you could file a complaint with reality's quality assurance department, one that hasn't been outsourced to oblivion yet.

The moment lasts exactly as long as a heartbeat in an industrial accident. The machine waits, politely impatient in the way only omniscient automation can be. The sound drops to a polite inquiry level, creating temporary deafness shaped like decision space.

In the damp, warm, crowded, open, dark, bright with negative light, silent with deafening quiet, slow-motion instant before everything continues exactly as it must continue because continuation is its job description—

You reach for what might be an exit button, or might be another way deeper in. The distinction has been temporarily suspended for routine maintenance.

The machine welcomes your input. It has always welcomed your input. It will always welcome your input, until input and welcome and always and until are all recycled into raw personnel materials for the next shift change.

After all, in a place where erosion builds and water lakes in negative space and light is what you see when nothing elseexists,

someone has to push the button.
