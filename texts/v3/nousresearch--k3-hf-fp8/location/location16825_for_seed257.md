The place was water thick enough to breathe through, but not breathe in. A weightless cathedral carved from absence. Every direction was the same deep blue-black, made darker by the few pinpricks of light that leaked in from somewhere far above—stars filtered through centuries of liquid sky, or maybe just the last glow of a molten world-core glimpsed through cracks.

It rose, or settled, or floated. Hard to say. Scale here was prayer-sized. You could stretch your arms and touch opposite walls if walls existed. Instead there were only the arches of pressure, curved like whalebones, holding the nothing back. The whole thing was built, every atom arranged by someone or something that had time to spare and no interest in symmetry. Columns of coral-salt spiraled up without touching anything. Bridge spans of frozen lead spanned distances too small to measure. Floor was ceiling was afterthought.

The water breathed slow as geology. Cold currents born leagues below kissed warm ones from shallows that no longer existed, making every movement feel like swimming through someone else’s memory. Everything dissolved here but nothing disappeared. Dust of dead cities hung in perfect suspension. Splinters of bone that had never belonged to anything vertebrate drifted in slow parade. Threads of copper wire grew like hair from the joints where walls would have met, each strand singing one pure note too low to hear.

Life yes but not life as skin recognizes. They moved like punctuation marks in sentences too vast to read. Here a cluster of glass spheres pulsing with faint green fire, orbiting a point of irritation in the water’s patience. There a ribbon of muscle and mirror that unfolded into twelve reflections when startled, each reflection fleeing a different direction through time. The smallest were motes sharp enough to cut thought. The largest was the size of regret and shaped like the space between heartbeats.

Sound happened between silences. A distant boom that might be mountains mating or doors shutting on the first day of creation. Closer, a crack like ice breaking beneath words never spoken. Between these, the constant whisper of water remembering being stone remembering being nothing.

Nothing grew here except what was already finished growing. No plants because there was no dirt to contradict. Instead the architecture bred with itself in slow mathematics. A dome would swell over centuries until its weight became a question, then birth a flawless series of arches to answer itself. Staircases climbed until they forgot what stairs were for, then continued upward as sculpture. One wall (if it was wall) was entirely covered in ears carved from ivory and obsidian, each ear listening for a different apology.

The light came from what had been brought here and what had been taken away. A lantern floated tethered by its own lost purpose, housing a flame that burned oil made from compressed regrets. A mirror hung face-downward reflected faces that hadn’t happened yet. In the rare places where currents met violently enough to bruise the dark, brief auroras flickered—colors named only by languages drowned before naming began.

It was warm enough that time went soft at the edges, but not so warm as to melt the rules. Gravity was polite suggestion. Distance honored effort more than measurement. To travel far required mostly the willingness to become someone else by the time you arrived.

At what might have been the center (though centers here were notoriously promiscuous), a tree stood that had never been planted. Its roots drank from the water’s memory of fire. Its branches supported nothing but the weight of all stories that had ever been started but not finished. A single fruit hung there, perfect and impossible, waiting for someone who had already eaten it before they became hungry.

Everything here was the last version of itself before becoming something else entirely. The city sunk not beneath waves but into them. The graveyard where graves remembered what they held better than the held remembered being held. The library where every book was blank because all the words had grown legs and walked away to live more interesting lives.

To enter was easy: stop believing in dry. To leave was easier: start believing in anything else. Most stayed somewhere between, breathing the water thick enough to be home but thin enough to never quite drown in.
