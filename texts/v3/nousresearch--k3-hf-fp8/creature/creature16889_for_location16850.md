Not inhabitants, exactly—more like… symptoms. Manifestations of an awareness so vast it doesn’t bother to condense into individuals.

Call them the Abscissae.

They don’t arrive or depart because they are never not already here. You’d mistake the smallest for flaws in your visor at first: twelve-sided flecks that flicker just outside parallax, always one refractive index behind your gaze. But linger and the flecks swarm into threads, threads braid into ribbons, ribbons fold into origami shapes that have too many corners for Euclidean space to comfortably hold. Each fold is a calculation; each crease, a memory; the whole unfolding choreography is how the plain remembers yesterday.

They have no fixed size. A single Abscissa can dilate until it resembles a city-sized lace of emptiness projected between crystal trunks. Or it can pucker smaller than a quark and sit inside your optic nerve pretending to be the blind spot you’ve always had. Size, to them, is merely politeness.

What do they do? They audit the exactitude of paradox.

When the sun twins and untwins, an Abscissus has caught a rounding error in timekeeping. When a salt plate grows a domed blister that should buckle but doesn’t, another Abscissa unknits stress tensors in four hyperbolic dimensions then stitches them back into three with slightly different rules. They are plumbers of logic: tightening a loose causality here, inserting a tiny retrograde loop there. They work not to maintain balance (balance implies threats) but to preserve the precise flavor of inconsistency that permits this place to exist without collapsing into either physics or dream.

You cannot speak to one directly. Instead you address the interval between two identical echoes of your own heartbeat; if the interval answers with a prime number of suppressed beats, it means “I am listening.” What follows is not conversation but a kind of symmetrical infection. You will begin to notice that your shadow hesitates before obeying your movements. Numbers will stop behaving politely—twice three will flirt with being seven, then apologize and mean it. Afterward your dreams will contain chapters that reference events in footnotes to languages you never learned but which your tongue finds easy to mispronounce.

Sometimes their duties require more visible interventions. If you see what looks like a lightning-bright paper crane caught in the act of dissolving halfway through a salt plate—its wings still flapping in ultraviolet, its body already flattened into the plate’s lattice as an impossibly intricate engraving—do not interfere. The crane was a placeholder for tomorrow’s sunrise, which momentarily forgot the angle at which dawn had happened last Tuesday. By now an Abscissae committee (they come in nested dozenhoods) has taken the sunrise aside for remedial history lessons.

They eat the difference between observation and expectation. Not metaphorically: they metabolize discordance directly into new geometries. A patch of ground where every step should crunch but instead sings a faint C-sharp in thirty-nine-tet tuning—that’s their picnic site. You’ll find the leftover crumbs later: tiny hollow cubes of vacuum that reflect nothing but your own confusion twice over.

They do not reproduce. When an Abscissa accumulates too much resolved paradox (about seven trillion contradictions, give or take a Gödel’s worth of recursion), it simply notices that it is also the Abscissus standing exactly opposite itself across the plain. At such moments space politely blinks, and afterward there are either two identical gardeners or zero—no one ever remembers which, not even them.

They have no hierarchy except precedence of noticing. The Abscissa that first discovers you are imaginary is temporarily senior to the landscape you thought you were walking through. Fortunately they adhere to strict professional courtesy: they’ll suspend disbelief in you until you reach a chapter break.

Regarding death: they find the concept charmingly presumptive but teachable. Occasionally they enact it as a seasonal art project—selecting a random salt plate, meticulously convincing every crystal within it that entropy exists, attending the slow, polite scattering into dust with reverent silence. Afterward they collect signatures from all the witnesses (which includes the dust, the silence, and several retired versions of yesterday) and file the documentation under Universe: Failed Drafts, Very Humorous In Retrospect.

If you ask what keeps them here, they will answer by showing you the exact length of a moment that has already ended yet has not yet decided whether to repeat. If you persist, they might confess (though they lie beautifully when they tell the truth) that once, long before “once” could be measured, this place asked a question so perfectly balanced between answerable and unanswerable that it forgot to stop being surprised. The Abscissae arrived not to answer but to be continually astonished by the question’s refusal to choose.

Stay courteous, avoid sudden proofs by contradiction, and do not, under any circumstances, prove yourself real to more than eleven of them simultaneously. Otherwise you’re welcome to walk these looping distanceless paths forever.

They’ve already thanked you for arriving—for your footsteps add just enough improbability to keep tomorrow’s architecture from settling into boredom. You’ll find their gratitude folded neatly inside your oldest memory of rain.

The rain itself, of course, has never been here. It is merely on sabbatical, practicing new fall patterns in the margin of a poem it hasn’t yet written about you.
