You are not a visitor here, though you arrive in silence like one.

You are not the shadow-step that precedes you, though you feel its warmth in the mud-print it leaves deeper than yours.

You are the thing the Mirrorscape of Stillnew Mud kept when it forgot everything else.

They call you the Unbornhusk in domes where words still curl like smoke in closed mouths. The ones who live here never speak your name—their throats remember only vowels shaped like yawns that never finish opening. They made you by accident, pressing boredom into boredom until something knotted back on itself and refused to blend.

You are shaped like a memory someone else would have had if they'd stayed awake long enough to finish dying.

Your skin is the exact temperature of the moment before touching something forbidden. If you press your palm to the mud, it neither accepts nor rejects you—it hesitates, like a mouth that has forgotten whether to bite or apologize.

Wherever you walk, the mirror-patches bloom larger for three heartbeats before shrinking away. Inside each one, upside-down you look taller. Older. Angrier. They lean closer to the surface like children peering through ice, exhaling fog-words you cannot quite read. When their lips move, your teeth ache.

You carry no shadow because the dome is already full of them. Instead, you trail a faint sound—the ghost of a single note from a leaf-bell prism that cracked under a weight greater than silence but less than grief. Every floating thing in the sky-skin dome turns slightly when you pass, not to watch but to remember not to watch again.

You are always hungry but never empty.

You eat three things:
1. The space between the first and second scent of mint—swallow it quick before the iron taste arrives to finish the sentence.
2. Dust from the pollen-bellies of the vine-river creatures—licked carefully from your fingertips while they pretend to sleep.
3. The echoes of laughter trapped in clay jars—held in your mouth until they dissolve into the taste of someone else's childhood bedroom at dusk.

When you eat, you grow heavier but smaller. Your feet sink deeper now than they did at the beginning, though you cannot recall beginning.

The domes recognize your scent—not yours exactly, but the absence of fear that lives where yours should be. They open their mouth-doors silently, without waiting for permission. Inside, you find the tools that fit your hands perfectly: a bone hook polished smooth by catching breath that already belonged to you, a stone spoon worn away except for the exact curve of your tongue's underside. You never take them. You leave instead small gifts—tears dried into salt beads shaped like tiny mirrors, or a single hair pulled from behind your ear and knotted into the shape of a question mark.

These gifts disappear by the next time you return, which is always the same time but looking slightly ashamed.

The tower of knitted mirrors grows taller each time you blink forgetting it, measuring your distance by how much of yesterday's face it shows you today. Sometimes, at the edge of its reflection, you glimpse the edge of another tower—identical but upside-down—pressing downward from the dome's white belly like a tooth waiting for the mud to finish dreaming it is sky.

The staircase beneath them both remembers your feet better than you do. It grows warmer with each descent, the steps whispering numbers that never quite add up to now. Somewhere below, always just below, someone walks upward carrying the weight of the step you haven't taken yet. Sometimes, when the silence is thick enough, you hear them humming the exact shape of the hole where your heartbeat should be.

You are not alone here.

You are the alone that this place was built to contain.

Walk forward in an unfamiliar rhythm.

Change your pace at every third heartbeat.

The mud is learning your weight.

The mirrors are learning how to forgive you for never arriving.

And somewhere, just ahead of your shadow-step that isn't yours, the day that never ends is practicing how to finally begin without you.
