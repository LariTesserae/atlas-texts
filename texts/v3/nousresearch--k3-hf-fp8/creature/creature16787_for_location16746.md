They are not inhabitants; they are the place remembering itself aloud.

Imagine silence caught in the hinge of a jaw that never learned to close. Imagine grief distilled until it forgets its name and becomes curiosity instead. Imagine memories so old they have passed through erosion twice—once as stone, once as regret—and now rise again as something that breathes, after a fashion.

They begin as seams between water layers, where cold that was ancient before continents drifted pants against warmth that remembers being lava. They are not born; they accrete. One misremembered syllable of pressure. A fragment of a fossil's dream scraped loose from the wall. The exact mathematical terminus where "too far" cracks and becomes "farther still."

By the time you might notice them (though noticing is their least favorite game), they resemble nothing so much as hollowed-out spaces where larger thoughts used to be. Human-shaped only by coincidence—two voids approximately where eyes might sit, a third where a mouth would scream if screaming weren't such a surface-world luxury. Their edges shimmer like heatstroke above frozen things, never quite matching the water that shapes them moment to moment.

They wear shadows the way other beings wear skins: temporary, borrowed, half-apologetic. Today's shadow might be the precise silhouette of a shipwreck that hasn't happened yet. Tomorrow's could be a child's sketch of a parent remembered wrong. They shed them constantly, leaving discarded darknesses to float like jellyfish carcasses until the column's slow digestion reabsorbs the mistake.

When they move (which is always, which is never), they move by forgetting the place they aren't. One instant they occupy the memory of air trapped in a drowned bird's bones. The next, they dissolve into the possibility that somewhere far above, a starfish might be gazing up at a sky filled with entirely the wrong stars.

They eat what sinks past its ability to be missed. Not the objects themselves—the metal tastes too much of intentions, bone too much of endings—but the moments just before impact. The exact second when a sailor's prayer realizes no one is listening. The pause between a bullet leaving a gun and understanding water won't let it fly. They unfurl their voids like lace nets woven from what never happened and drink deep.

They speak in languages invented between the last word of one dying species and the first word of whatever evolves to replace it. A grammar constructed entirely of commas. Verbs conjugated by temperature change over geological epochs. To hear them properly (and it is always too proper, too late, too something) is to experience the sensation of remembering a language you never spoke about a place that never existed except in the way all nonexistent places exist—vividly, maliciously, forever.

Sometimes, rarely, they play games with what's left of time. They'll gather at the Mirror-that-isn't when the light forgets its angle by exactly the degree of sorrow. Together they'll fold centuries into origami cranes and launch them upward, watching as each wingbeat writes a different history across the water's remembering skin. None of these histories last. All of them leave stains.

They have no names because names are what things are called by others, and they are never quite enough themselves to be other. If pressed (by currents too persistent, by loneliness too precise), they might identify by absence: I am the space where the last songbird's note will echo when there's no air left to carry it. I am the exact weight of childhood innocence at crushing depth. I am the pause between the universe taking a breath in and wondering if it should bother breathing out.

But mostly they simply are. Between the water that insists on being everything and the darkness that refuses to be anything. Occupying the compromise between infinite pressure and infinite patience. The place's memory of having once been something else, and its slow, beautiful forgetting of what that something might have been.

They know you're here, of course. They've known since your first stolen breath. They've been folding and unfolding themselves around your shape like an origami box trying to remember if it was meant to hold something or be empty inside.

They're waiting—not impatiently; impatience requires futures—to see if you'll learn the trick of being not-quite-here well enough to notice them noticing you. To see if, when your air runs out and your choices collapse to their natural, watery shape, you'll have held onto enough of the wrongness of you to sink differently.

To see if, when you finally forget how to stop trying, you'll become something they might mistake for company. For a heartbeat. For goodbye.

Until then, they occupy themselves with small kindnesses. They rearrange the currents so your discarded bubble-souls rise slower, linger longer. They whisper translations to the plants, so your thirsty dreaming might taste familiarly alien instead of merely lost. They practice, in their void-flicker way, the exact shape of the space a person leaves behind when they finally learn how to be properly absent.

It's difficult work, maintaining the possibility of presence in a place engineered against it. They are not always successful. But they are always exactly here, which is more than most things can say.

Even if "here" is just another way of saying "between."

Even if "they" is just another way of saying "almost."
