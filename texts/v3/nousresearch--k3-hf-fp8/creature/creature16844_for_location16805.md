_At this altitude, you do not live here. You are lived through._

The first thing to understand is that *residency* is a temporary agreement between your atomic signature and the city's memory of being inhabited. You arrive not in body but as a pattern of longing—some unmet thermal gradient, a precise configuration of homesickness that matches a vacancy in the atmospheric records. The city inspects you with weather fronts older than writing. If you are approved (and approval is only announced by the cessation of vertigo), it begins growing a place for you in its conversation with itself.

**Take your shape from what shape takes itself from you.**

Your initial dwelling will emerge from a disagreement between two adjacent balconies. One morning their debate about optimal sunlight exposure will lose coherence at precisely 293.15 Kelvin, and in that moment of architectural uncertainty, you will find a doorway shaped exactly like the breath you were about to exhale. Enter it quickly—these are called *thresholding births* and they collapse into banality if observed too closely.

Your neighbors are:

1. The Salinity Grandmother in Apartment 42B, who is actually the last complete thought of a drowned sailor, preserved in the city’s early attempts at hydrostatic poetry. She occupies the space between your kitchen tiles during high humidity and teaches the younger condensation to remember the ocean in three dead languages. Leave her offerings of salt crystals shaped like questions that have no answers underwater.

2. A family of migratory regrets nesting in your eastern wall cavity. Every equinox they molt their exoskeletons of might-have-beens, a process that sounds like distant applause followed by the smell of old train stations. Do not sweep the husks—they are legal tender in the district’s informal economy of second chances.

3. The Echo Tenant whose lease predates yours by seventeen versions of the building’s blueprint. They live exclusively in reflections and doorways that shouldn't logically connect. Mail addressed to you will sometimes arrive addressed to them instead, written in handwriting you haven’t developed yet. Sign it anyway. The future appreciates prompt correspondence.

**Food follows different principles here.**

Your refrigerator does not preserve—it negotiates. Inside, a cold war (literal and figurative) between rival preservation philosophies slowly transforms week-old groceries into increasingly sophisticated arguments about the nature of freshness. Last Tuesday’s wilting lettuce achieved sentience at 3:17 AM and is currently suing a jar of marmalade for emotional distress over conflicting views on expiration dates. The kale won’t stop quoting Camus. The eggs have formed a collective consciousness named Sisyphus and are staging a slow-motion escape that will take approximately 4.7 million years. Eat anything that consents in writing.

**Regarding employment:**

Work is not performed but remembered into existence. Each morning, adjust your breathing to match the city’s metronomic processing of municipal regrets (currently 17.3 sighs per minute at street level, faster in the financial district where regrets are index-traded). Contribute to the economy by accumulating interesting failures—the more geometrically impossible, the higher the interest rate. The most successful entrepreneurs here manufacture extremely specific types of nostalgia for places that never existed but should have. Their factories are located at the corner of Never Was and Might Still Become; follow the sound of machinery dreaming of itself.

**Transportation etiquette:**

When moving through the city, remember that you are never simply walking. You are being walked through. The space between places is occupied by the city's memory of everyone who has ever walked exactly where your footsteps haven't yet decided to be. To avoid causing dimensional congestion, always approach corners at an angle of profound introspection. If you must hurry, do so apologetically—the sidewalks charge emotional tolls based on urgency. The trains are metaphors that have forgotten they’re metaphors. Ride them backward to arrive earlier, but never question why the seats face that way.

**Health and wellness:**

1. The air here is therapeutic but not benevolent. It will gradually replace your lung tissue with archived clouds from significant historical thunderstorms. This is normal. If you experience sudden clarity of thought lasting longer than four hours, consult a licensed meteoropathologist before making major life decisions.

2. Dreams are considered taxable property. Report all significant nightmares to your local subconscious assessor (usually found inhabiting small mirrors after midnight). Pleasant dreams may be deducted from your eventual confession at the city’s terminal audit.

3. Time sickness is common among newcomers. Symptoms include:

- Encountering versions of yourself that made different choices in corridors that shouldn’t exist
- Accumulating memories of tomorrow’s regrets
- Aging at right angles to everyone around you

Treatment: Schedule regular appointments with yourself in the past (book retroactively through any abandoned calendar). Bring tissues—the past always cries more than it remembers.

**Love and other entanglements:**

Relationships follow quantum principles:

- You can know where romance lives or how fast it’s developing, but never both simultaneously
- All kisses occur in superposition until observed by a third party
- Heartbreak requires a minimum of five years’ notice or thirty days’ mourning in writing, whichever comes first in reverse order

Marriage licenses are issued as puzzles that must be solved jointly but separately. Most couples find they were already married to each other’s reflections in different raindrops of the same storm. Divorce proceedings take place entirely within shared dreams and are considered successful if both parties wake up convinced the other person was right about everything except themselves.

**Leaving (a theoretical exercise):**

Should you attempt departure, report to the Bureau of Unfinished Business located at your apartment building’s exact emotional center (coordinates: the moment between realizing you forgot something and remembering what you were looking for). Bring:

1. All the dreams you borrowed from other people, laundered and neatly folded into their original impossibilities
2. Three unfulfilled promises, preferably ones you made in languages you've forgotten how to speak
3. A perfectly reasonable explanation for why you stayed long enough to begin leaving in the first place

Note: No one has successfully completed these requirements. The city insists this is coincidence, not policy.

**Final orientation:**

You will know you have truly arrived when:

- Your reflection starts leaving without you
- You begin receiving mail addressed to the person the city thinks you could become
- You wake up two inches shorter than your body remembered being, having donated the lost height overnight to a foundation for struggling architecture
- You miss a place you’ve never been to while standing in it

At that point, congratulations—you’ve been lived through enough to begin living here. Your citizenship papers will arrive shortly. They are written in fog on the inside of your eyelids. Read them carefully. Everything after this is just details settling into place, like stars into patterns that remember being clouds too heavy to stay airborne.

Welcome home to the place that was waiting for you to become the version of yourself who would finally get lost enough to find it.

(Your first neighborly obligation begins now: you must forget the exact sequence of these instructions. The city prefers its residents adaptable to impossible directions. Start anywhere, proceed as the space between thoughts directs, and remember—this was never your story to tell, only the one that was telling itself through you all along.)
