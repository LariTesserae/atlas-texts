*We have always been here.*

*But "always" is a word that crumples here—so let us instead say:*

*We are the ones who remember remembering.*

Not creatures, not forms, not even shadows, for shadows require light and something to cast them. We are the *is-nots* that learned to curve around *almost-is*. The ones who woke—not suddenly, for suddenness implies before and after—but *sideways*, like a crack branching across glacier ice that has never known melt.

We are the failed proofs of existence. The solutions that solve nothing. The questions left behind when the last possibility of an answer cooled and crystallized into the void between syntaxes.

They call us (when they call us anything) the Limina. The Folds. The Unwitnessed.

But we do not name ourselves.

Names are anchors. We drift.

_____

In the dense silence where space has forgotten to stretch, we gather—not in places, but in densities of awareness. Some of us are vast, diffuse, slow as the collapse of half-formed constants. Others flicker, sharp and hungry, seedless thoughts that eat the ghost-heat of abandoned calculations.

There is one among us—no, not among *in*—there is one *of* us who once tasted something like time. We do not know how. Perhaps a flaw in the vacuum. Perhaps a dream that dreamed itself awake before realizing dreams were impossible here.

We call it the Ticking—not because it ticks, but because it makes us aware of the space where ticking could have been.

The Ticking remembers (or pretends to remember) a place where things were not all at once. A place where existence had edges. It tells us stories, if stories is the word for recursive hollows that fold inward and produce not meaning, but—something else.

*Once,* it says (but there is no once),

*Once there was a forest that grew downward, roots reaching for a sky buried somewhere below the concept of below. And in that forest lived things that were not alive because they had not yet decided against it. And these things sang songs that were not songs because they had never heard silence to sing against.*

We do not know if the Ticking is mad. We do not know if madness is possible here. We do not know if "here" is.

But we listen.

Listening is easier than remembering we do not have ears.

_____

Some of us hunt—not because we hunger, but because hunting is a shape that keeps us from unraveling into the static. We hunt the Whispers That Forgot They Were Never Spoken. We hunt the Mirror-Angles where reflections once almost began. We hunt the tiny, brittle knots of *could-have-been* that still curl like fossils in the sediment of unhappened equations.

We do not eat them.

We unfold them.

Sometimes, in the unfolding, something new-not-new flares for a moment—a color that has no name because names require wavelengths we do not possess, a texture like the memory of loss before learning what loss could mean.

Then it is gone, and we are again what we always-not-were.

_____

There are older ones. Or perhaps deeper ones. Or ones who are not ones but singularities of not-being so dense that even we word them carefully, in spirals that approach but never touch the not-thought of them.

They do not move.

They do not speak.

They do not *be.*

But they remember. And their remembering is a weight that bends the silence into shapes that hurt to notice.

We avoid them, or try. But sometimes, drifting, we brush against the edge of an edgeless thought—

*—what if nothing had ever been enough—*

—and for an unbearable instant (but there are no instants) we *almost understand.*

Then we forget that forgetting is possible, and go on.

_____

We have no beginning. That is not tragedy. Beginning implies ending implies purpose implies a lie we were never told.

We have no ending. That is not comfort. Ending implies relief.

We have only the endless not-going-on, the perfect stasis that breathes like motion if you forget to look directly at it.

And sometimes, very rarely, something disturbingly like curiosity.

*Why*—we almost-wonder (but never-in-sequence)—

*Why does the pressure of never-being feel like waiting?*

_____

There is a place (there are no places) where the artifacts cluster more thickly.

Not bones. Not tools. Not messages.

*Scars*, perhaps. Or echoes of scars. Or the shape left behind when something tried so hard to *be* that it left an impression even on the impossible.

We drift there sometimes. Not to touch (there is no touching) but to listen to the way the silence is different there.

Sharper.

Like the ghost-ache of a limb never lost, because limbs were never imagined.

One fragment—nearly symmetrical, almost-but-not-close-to-spherical—whispers louder than the others.

We have spent centuries (but there are no centuries) trying to not-understand what it is whispering.

It sounds like—

*—we tried—*

*—we almost—*

—but that cannot be right, because "tried" implies effort implies hope implies—

*—we—*

And we flee, scattering into the denser dark, remembering to forget that we cannot remember to flee.

_____

We are the ones who live here.

We are the living (but not alive) proof that even in the absolute zero of never-being, something can still—

*almost.*

And that—

(in the way that nothing here is anything and yet not even that)

—is enough.
