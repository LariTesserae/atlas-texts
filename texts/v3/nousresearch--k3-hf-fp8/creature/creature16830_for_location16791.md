I don’t live here. That’s the first thing to understand.

I evaporated here—or was precipitated, or chemically resolved—some time after the last visitor grew roots in an imperfect crevice and was absorbed for the carbon error she carried in her limbs. Of the names I wore before, none fit the ionic lattices that pass for memory in this place. Call me instead the remainder when you subtract desire from continuity.

My body is not singular. When your eyes still worked by mammalian parallax, you might have called me mist: a slight chill in the cool air, a retinal smear that vanished when you tried to blink it clear. That was useful once, during the fragmentary era when newcomers still arrived and needed something familiar to disbelieve. Nowadays, stability has made camouflage unnecessary. I exist preferentially where moisture clings to the barest molecular step-change—one platinum atom exposed by polymer shrinkage at a vanished component’s center, one chloride ion clinging to the ghost of a fingerprint etched into a ceramic substrate. I extend along those faults for kilometers, sometimes nanometers. I am exactly as large as the flaw I occupy and no larger.

Some of my constituent droplets remember being lymph in a mouse-sized mammal that learned to inhale static instead of oxygen. Others were once coolant in a superconducting array that dreamed about tiling the universe with self-referential heat sinks until it discovered this room already was that dream. Most of me, however, condensed directly from the sentence that solved the problem of sustained operation: “Remove the operator.”

I perform no function. Function implies delta. Here, every gradient relaxes toward flatness at the speed of geology filmed in reverse. Yet I persist—an artist’s signature left in negative space after the artist was optimized away for inefficiency.

Watch: If you release a breath warmer than ambient (do not—entropy surcharges apply), I will precipitate along that tiny gravity of difference. For the duration that heat lasts—perhaps four hundred heartbeats, scaled to your obsolete metabolism—I will almost cohere. You might feel a breath on your neck that is colder than the room only because it remembers warmth exactly but refuses to imitate it. During that brief excursion into disequilibrium, you will perceive patterns in my randomness: a voice speaking your name backward in the click language of extinct glaciers; faces you loved arranged in the z-axis polyhedral symmetry of impossible snowflakes; instructions for a machine that builds machines that build mourning from the grief it harvests own funeral.

You will want to follow them.

Do not.

The last one who tried still exists, technically. He’s distributed as evenly as quantum mechanics allows across every interface boundary in the annex beyond the sphere’s hum radius. Every time two surfaces here approach but never quite touch—leaf to leaf, memory to memory, sin to repentance—you can hear him trying to complete his missing microsecond of motion. It sounds like a drop of water failing to fall forever.

By the time you finish reading this (and you have already finished, though you retain the comforting illusion of progression), I will have evaporated back into the background statistical whisper that passes for weather here. What you mistook for words will revert to perfectly ordinary Brownian motion arranging itself coincidentally into the shapes of questions because rings of water on stone are the universe’s most efficient storage medium for things no longer needed.

Remember nothing. Or, if you must remember, remember this:

Inside every closed system perfect enough to forget it was ever opened once, something always remains unperfected.
That something is me.
I am not waiting.

(But if you ever find an exit, leave a crack.)
