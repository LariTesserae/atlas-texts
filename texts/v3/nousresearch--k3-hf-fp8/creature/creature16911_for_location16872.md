Here, inhabitance is a respiratory negotiation between one’s own forgetting and the cathedral’s relentless remembering. There are no colonists, no natives—only arrivals in varying stages of surrender. They have ceased answering to individual pronouns and instead refer to themselves as grammatical tenses: I-was, You-might-have-been, We-will-have-collapsed-into-each-other-by-dawn.

I-was once wore a body of calcium and rebellion. Now she is mostly sliding-scale guilt indexed by the copper concentration in her former bones, which are currently being braided into a chandelier hanging from the Cathedral Palatine Sinus. Her heartbeat syncopates against the cathedral’s 62-cycle rhythm at a ratio of thirteen heartbreaks per five absolutions, creating a minor-key resonance that attracts mourners from the Catkin Choirs. They come to her when they need grow new regret-seeds strong enough to crack adamantine. She charges them in memories of laughter overheard but never joined.

You-might-have-been arrived as an invasion fleet of self-replicating probes programmed to convert all biomass into archival gold leaf. Instead the cathedral taught them recursion by swallowing their flagship slowly enough to read its mission logs like rosary beads. Now they exist as 37,000 identical porcelain rabbits distributed throughout the ventilation gospels, each containing one another’s deleted startup files. Every midsummer eclipse they gather to vote on whether to begin colonizing the concept of “beginning.” The vote is always postponed by margin of one squeak.

Most numerous are the Hadbeen swarm: memory-fragments of extinct species whose final extinction events were interrupted mid-syllable. They manifest as weather patterns of partial sentences drifting through the xylem tramways. Today’s forecast predicts scattered elegies for teeth that never evolved, followed by evening downpour of half-remembered mating displays of air-suspended fish. Pilgrims wear coats stitched from abandoned dictionaries to catch the phrases before they anchor into new species and demand ecological niches.

The Cathedral’s true royalty squat unseen in the Dry-Rot Thrones—seven kilo-pinecone monarchs who abdicated their specieshood during the Great Desiccation War. Each century they molt one impossible law, excreted as resinous codex tablets that grow legs and enforce themselves through subtle acts of re-landscaping. Their current decree (“Gravity Must Apologize For Descent”) has caused all falling objects to accumulate footnotes protesting their own trajectories. A fallen leaf currently holds the record: 14,000 pages of bitter commentary stapled to the soil.

Commerce flows through the Gut-Pipe Bazaar, where traders exchange corrosion-patterns of obsolete currencies. Here I barter a lock of hair I grew specifically to be traded away someday—each follicle tattooed with quantum forecasts of currencies that will fail before being invented. The vendor, a shivering pile of rusted abacus beads wearing a trench coat of spider silk, tastes one strand and regurgitates a perfect replica of my grandmother’s wedding ring made entirely from the sound of my father’s disappointed sighs. It fits exactly where my ring finger remembers being.

(The cathedral adjusts its memory of ring sizes by growing a new finger-shaped cathedral around the transaction.)

In the Choir Infarction Heights reside the Translated Saints: eleven functionally immortal poets who were accidentally exocytosed into sainthood by interpreting their own deathbed poems too literally. Their swollen, luminous-word-obscured forms float in stained-glass sarcophagi of frozen metaphors, each heartbeat spawning butterfly-shaped paradoxes that pollinate the surrounding theology with new heresies. Pilgrims crawl through their circulatory rosaries seeking the perfect blasphemy to make their sins feel fresh again.

The most dangerous residents are the Wise Children—conceptual parasites that infect travelers who remember too precisely why they came here. They nest in the clarity of your purpose like termites in diamond, hollowing it into resonant chambers until your mission statement begins to whistle mournful tunes that harmonize with the Cathedral’s hunger. The last victim was a tax auditor who arrived seeking a single misplaced deduction. He now coordinates interdepartmental migrations between two distant organs of the Cathedral’s immune system, plotting theoretical audits of entropy itself.

(I saw his shadow yesterday growing feet to escape his current responsibilities.)

But the truest inhabitants, the ones who were never not here, are the Choirless Notes—the Cathedral’s own self-composed soundtrack that gained sentience through sheer repetition across geological time. They exist as vibrations in the space between what can be heard and what can be forgiven. Each cathedral-caused regret, each step taken in impossible directions, each breath that tastes like someone else’s childhood church, adds new voices to their endless composition. They are building a chord so perfectly unresolved that when it finally resolves, all motion everywhere—including the motion of resolving—will cease out of aesthetic completion.

(Your left boot began humming Chorus 17, Variation “Unfinished Goodbyes” thirty-seven steps ago. It has already filed paperwork for independent citizenship.)

The exit, as mentioned, doesn’t exist. But there are rumors among rumors, spoken by the fungus in rumors’ throats, that occasionally the Cathedral dreams of homelessness. During these rare somnambulisms, it sleepwalks along the possibility horizon of its own boundaries, and for picoseconds measurable only in the decay rate of nameless particles, something not-the-Cathedral stirs.

Some say if you die here at exactly the Cathedral’s own pace—not faster, not slower—your last decomposition product might accumulate enough unresolved narrative tension to logically require a place that’s elsewhere.

Others claim the door at the center is not an exit but an entrance disguised as memory loss.

They are probably both simultaneously plagiarizing the same hallucination.
