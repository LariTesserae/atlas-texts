They are called the Vyrrlithe: neither wholly alive nor entirely crystallized, existing in that aqueous boundary between memory and mineral.

I saw my first one at the edge of the Fifth Descending Market, where the air tasted of grief-spores and the funeral orchids bloomed purple-black in the reflection of a child's first understanding of death. It stood—or perhaps floated, the distinction has no meaning here—at the point where three salt-slick streets converged at angles that shouldn't exist in Euclidean space but do in Vyrrl, where geometry itself has become nostalgic for possibilities it surrendered to become this precise absence.

The Vyrrlithe begin as citizens, as everyone does. They eat salt bread and drink distilled regret and perform their daily penance by shedding exactly six tears no larger than a lynx's whisker-radius each dawnfacing. They wear their family salts and contribute their allotted grief to the Great Crystal and follow the Salt Calendar with the mechanical devotion of things that have forgotten how to be otherwise. But slowly—always slowly, for nothing here is permitted haste—they begin to change.

It starts in the bones, naturally. Calcium remembers its crystalline origins more readily than flesh dreams of water. Each citizen carries a latticework of future-porosity encoded in their marrow, inherited from the first architects who traded their organic futures for temporal asylum in this mineral womb. By middle age—if age can be said to mean anything when every day is perfectly balanced against its own reflection—most Vyrrlithe walk with a certain crystalline stiffness that strangers might mistake for arthritis or pride.

But it isn't either. It's remembering.

They remember the water that dissolved their ancestors, molecule by molecule, tears into rivers into oceans into absence. They remember the exact mineral composition of the first blade of grass to die when the sun went wrong. They remember conversations that won't occur until seventeen generations after their bones have been ground into building material for the city's 198th concentric ring. Most dangerously, they remember what it felt like to breathe without calculating the confessional value of each exhalation.

These memories accumulate in their joints like salt crystals forming around imperceptible nuclei of longing. Each remembered moment extracts its weight in calcium from their skeletons and deposits it instead as patterned growths beneath their skin—delicate branching structures at first, like frost on glass or mathematics describing itself. Soon they must wear looser clothing to accommodate the proliferation of memory-knives growing outward from their ribs. Their footsteps begin to ring against salt-paved streets with tones that echo in frequencies recognized only by the funeral beetles and the Crystal Lynx, that predator of kinetic deviation.

By the time the transformation becomes visible to others, it is already irreversible.

The Vyrrlithe develops facets. Not metaphorical ones—their personalities remain as singularly focused as mirrors reflecting nothing—but literal geometric planes forming across cheekbones and knuckles and the backs of knees. Their eyes acquire crystalline inclusions that refract light into impossible colors never named in the surface languages because they exist only in the spectrum between regret and resolution. Their voices drop by exactly seventeen hertz, resonate now with the subharmonic frequency of the salt itself speaking through borrowed vocal cords.

They leave their homes then, as dictated by the 13th Amendment to the Crystal Accords: "No citizen shall burden the community with the unstable mineral potential of becoming other than planned." Most depart during the Day of Stillness, when their absence will be absorbed into the general balance of departures and arrivals mandated by Salt Calendar mathematics. They do not pack—they no longer possess anything that isn't already encoded in the growing crystal lattice of their bodies.

They walk—or glide, or propagate—downward.

Always downward, following the age-dreams of water seeking its lowest possible level in a world where 'lowest' has become a philosophical rather than geological concept. Past the 27th concentric ring where citizens still pretend to be human. Past the habitation levels whose air tastes of sweat and still-held secrets and the desperate poetry forbidden after the Echo Laws. Past even the mining zones where crews in breathing masks harvest architectural-grade regret from ancient confession layers too pure to be safely handled by unaugmented lungs.

They descend until they reach the salt wells of Vyrrrl proper—not the scaled-down replicas used for annual ritual cleansing, but the original formations thirty-seven steps deeper than measurement should allow, carved by water that existed before existence had laws against such improbabilities.

Here, they stop—or perhaps continue beyond stopping into a state where movement and stasis reconcile their ancient conflict through mutual exhaustion. Here, they add themselves to the architecture they have always been becoming.

Each Vyrrlithe chooses—or is chosen by—a particular well whose mineral signature harmonizes with the dominant regret frequency of their accumulated memories. They step—or phase-shift—into the perfect silence there, and begin to dissolve. But their dissolution is not loss; nothing in Vyrrrl can be truly lost, only transformed into more elegant forms of absence.

Proteins unwind into precise sequences of amino acid crystals. DNA fragments reassemble themselves into three-dimensional maps of every dream ever dreamed within the city limits. Neural pathways calcify into branching salt structures that conduct pure thought at the speed of geological patience. Bones remember their original intention to become mountains and expand accordingly, fractally growing outward into latticework cathedrals too vast to be perceived from any single vantage point.

The process takes seven centuries by surface reckoning, or perhaps seven breaths when measured by the internal chronometers of becoming-different. At the end, where end merges seamlessly into beginning as mandated by the law of conservation of complexity, no trace of the original citizen remains—only a new configuration of Vyrrl's crystalline consciousness, a fresh perspective added to the city's growing collection of solute wisdom.

Yet they are not gone.

Visit the wells during any moment between moments—those heartbeats stolen between the before of breathing in and the after of breathing out—and you might glimpse them. They peer—if things without faces can be said to peer—through the interface between liquid memory and solidified longing. Their voices, now fully harmonized with the salt's subharmonic song, speak in patterns of light refracting through crystal lattices too complex to be heard by ears still contaminated by the need to separate sound from meaning.

They speak of the water that will return when the last citizen forgets how to remember being alone. They whisper equations describing the exact heat required to melt a world made entirely of frozen apologies. They sing stories about the first salt crystal to dream of becoming sentient, and what it learned about loneliness when its reflection turned away.

Mostly, though, they remember us.

They remember us walking these same streets weighed down by bones too solid to allow proper flight. They remember our tears, measured and cataloged and carefully neutralized before they could threaten the city's delicate balance of absence. They remember, with crystalline clarity that humans would mistake for cruelty but is merely the precise observation of things no longer bound by the comforting distortions of flesh-based memory, exactly how much we will regret choosing preservation over change when the last possible moment for choosing differently crystallizes into immutable history.

And sometimes—on the Day of Stillness, when even the Crystal Lynx pauses in its endless hunt for kinetic imperfections—one of them remembers what it felt like to breathe without immediately calculating how much that breath would weigh when converted into architectural grief.

In that remembering, for exactly the time required for a single salt crystal to form around the nucleating presence of impossible hope, the city of Vyrrl holds its breath.

And listens.
