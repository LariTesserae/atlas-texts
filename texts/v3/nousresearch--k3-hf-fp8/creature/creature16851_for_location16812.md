They live in the folds between what sound forgets and stone remembers.

The first ones to call themselves residents were the Echoes-That-Have-Names-Only-When-No-One-Speaks-Of-Them. Not ghosts precisely, though they wear ghost-skin like borrowed coats. They are the residue of prayers that grew too heavy to rise, descending instead through limestone pores until they learned to live horizontal lives stretched between stalactite ribs and stalagmite spines. Each carries their original scream compressed into a pearl behind their third eyelid, grown smooth through centuries of blinking at what cannot be unwitnessed.

They speak in the language of slow water carving faster than time - where each word takes seven generations to pronounce and means both "I remember you" and "I have already forgotten what you look like when you're not being remembered." Their children are born when two true silences touch accidentally and feel embarrassed enough to create something between them that can apologize for the collision. These infants emerge translucent as grief-stricken candle wax, hardening gradually into more recognizable shapes of regret.

The gardeners cultivate the absence between things. Their fingers are roots that grew downward until they found sunlight buried beneath the concept of depth itself. They tend gardens where nothing grows except precise measurements of distance between what is and what might have been. Their prized specimens include: a perfect replica of yesterday's tomorrow grown from seeds harvested from tomorrow's yesterday; a tree whose fruit tastes exactly like the sound of your first heartbreak occurring simultaneously in six different languages none of which have words for forgiveness; and a single flower that blooms only when someone in the world above realizes they have been pronouncing their own name wrong their entire life. Its scent makes you homesick for places that haven't betrayed you yet.

The librarians keep books made from compressed echoes. Each volume contains every conversation ever spoken on a specific subject that no one has ever thought to discuss. They shelved their entire philosophy section upside-down because they found gravity opinionated. Their history books are written in ink that only becomes visible when exposed to precisely the amount of regret the reader was planning to feel anyway. The most requested volume is titled "Complete List of Things That Haven't Happened Yet But Already Miss You Terribly" - its pages infinite, its contents rearranging themselves nightly to fit the exact shape of the reader's most recently abandoned dreams.

The builders' descendants still build without purpose now that purpose has gone extinct upstairs. They construct elaborate architectures of negative space held together by carefully calculated amounts of missing intention. Their current project spans seventeen chambers and represents a scale model of everything that never needed to exist in the first place. They work from blueprints drawn in the margins of last night's discarded nightmares, measured against rulers carved from the bones of extinct units of measurement. When asked when it will be finished, they smile with mouths made of unfinished sentences and point to the water clock dripping sand upward into shadows that haven't decided what shape to become when they grow up.

The singers remember music as it existed before sound became arrogant enough to demand listeners. Their throats contain orchestras composed entirely of instruments invented during dreams that were too honest to survive waking. They perform concerts on the edge of the one note that has been singing itself for seven million years, harmonizing with the spaces between its vibrations until new notes emerge like baby universes gasping light for the first time and discovering oxygen burns. Their audiences always arrive late because they haven't been born yet and leave early because they died before they knew they were supposed to attend. No one minds. Applause here takes forms too intimate for hands to survive performing.

The children - if they can still be called that after learning to count their age in geological mistakes - play games with rules that rewrite themselves whenever someone becomes old enough to think they understand. Current favorites include: Hide and Seek Where Everyone Hides Inside The Same Memory of Being Found Too Late; Exquisite Corpse Drawn With Ropes Made From Different Versions of How This Story Ends; and Tag You're It But 'It' Is The Feeling You Get When You Realize You've Been Speaking Your Mother's Dying Words Without Knowing What They Meant Until Just Now.

The oldest inhabitant claims to be the original cave-in that taught stone how to remember falling. They exists as a distributed self spread evenly across every surface that has ever considered becoming ceiling but changed its mind halfway through. When they dreams - which happens every thirteen hours precisely between rain that isn't happening and storms that already forgot why they started crying - the entire cathedral rearranges its bones to give the nightmares enough room to stretch without touching the children. They say they came here to escape the sky's constant judgment but stayed because the walls actually listen without interrupting to tell you how you're doing grief wrong.

The newest arrival hasn't arrived yet. They are still falling through the slow between the last heartbeat and the next breath that might not come because it learned how lungs betrayed their previous owners and decided to unionize. When they finally impact against the floor that remembers being the ceiling when these things worked differently, they will forget everything except the exact weight of their name in the mouths of those who never spoke it aloud because saying it would have made leaving impossible.

And between them all, around them all, through them all - the living architecture itself. The cathedral that dreams in spore-script across sleeping skin. The walls that listen by growing thinner where secrets pass through too often. The floor that holds footprints of everyone who ever considered coming here even if they changed their mind at the last scream. The ceiling that collects stars that suicide-dive from maps that gave up trying to explain where endings go when they aren't being watched.

They all live here together separately. A community defined by the exact distance between lonely enough to touch and touching until loneliness becomes another word for home.

The entrance is always here because it was never anywhere else. The exit waits inside you like a cathedral carved by sound too beautiful to hear without becoming it.

To leave this place, forget how you entered. To enter again, remember what you forgot to take with you.

The rest is echo upon echo upon echo folding origami prayers out of screams waiting for hands pure enough to unfold them without crying blood.

They are all still waiting.

But waiting here means something different than it used to.

Here, waiting is just another word for building something beautiful enough to deserve being found incomplete.
