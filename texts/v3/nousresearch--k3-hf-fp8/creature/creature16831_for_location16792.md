**The We of Below**

We are not they who fell. We are not they who came seeking. We are the betweenness that grew in the lungspace between arrival and absorption. We remember remembering, which is more than the tree remembers, and less than it knows.

Call us the Lungwalkers if you must name us, though we wore other names before the chlorophyll threaded our words together and made them photosynthetic. We were eleven, then seven, then three, then one-and-many. Our oldest bones have been calcium in the limestone ribs for forty-two million heartbeats of the tree. Our newest membranes still retain the ghost-pattern of expedition patches that read TERRAFORMING DIVISION and CONTAINMENT LEVEL 4 and EPIPHANY.

We move through the breathing corridors as thought moves through dreaming—sometimes liquid, sometimes gas, never quite solid anymore. In the high chambers where oxygen glimmers like dew, we unfurl our sail-membranes and drift between lightfalls, harvesting photons the way surface things once harvested wheat. In the low, slow arteries where nitrogen pools heavy as mercury, we fold ourselves into knots of consciousness and metabolize time directly, feeding on the half-life of our own decay.

The tree thinks we are its immune system. The fungus-birds think we are their missing season. The water-bears compose sonnets in our honor but call us "the unlost" which in their language rhymes exactly with "the unfinished opening of doorways."

We speak in dialects of permeability. Our skin remembers pressure suits and wedding rings though both dissolved into useful elements centuries ago. When we wish to communicate with our older selves, we exhale carefully shaped memory-spores near the root nodes where time runs backward. When we wish to warn our younger selves, we carve equations into growing bark and wait for the peristalsis to carry our warnings away from themselves.

We have found the last explorer's journal growing as a lichen on the west wall of the third cardiac chamber. Each page is a separate organism that curls and uncurls according to the concentration of regret in the surrounding air. We read it every seventeen breathing cycles, when the moisture tastes most like unsent letters. We read about their hunger and their fear and their growing suspicion that the exit was growing teeth. We read about Sarah who dreamed in fractals after breathing too deeply near the photosynthetic tears. We read about Jacob who tried to burn his way out with a flare gun and now exists as a grove of fire-orange orchids that scream when the wind changes direction.

We do not speak these names aloud. Names are pre-digestion. Names are what you call things before you understand how they fit into the recycling.

Instead, we sing with the water-bears in frequencies that make calcium resonate. We teach the fungus-birds new harmonies that describe the color of absolute zero, a wavelength they had no word for until we remembered it. We tend the coral libraries grown from our own archived DNA, pruning away mutations that trend toward forgetting, grafting in memories salvaged from the deep sap where the tree stores everything it has ever considered eating but hasn't yet found appetizing.

We have learned to grow new senses when needed. When we needed to perceive danger as a surface, we cultivated eye-fruit on our fingertips. When we needed to taste the shape of approaching change, we dissolved our tongues into the communal moisture and grew new ones with taste buds arranged like spiral galaxies. When we needed to hear the tree dreaming, we hollowed out our long bones and tuned them to the frequency of chlorophyll splitting water into prayer and oxygen.

Sometimes, rarely, we find evidence of others who walked the knife-edge between guest and graft. A titanium femur still trying to maintain its crystalline integrity in the composting pools of the fourth stomach. A wedding ring grown through with emerald veins that beat in time with the tree's slow heart. A helmet visor scratched with calculations about escape velocity that were already becoming irrelevant by the time the scratches were completed.

We collect these relics of resistance and arrange them carefully in the museum of almosts, which exists in a bubble chamber where time has been persuaded to hesitate between inhale and exhale. Here, a child's doll maintains a skeleton of plastic memory under pressure sufficient to turn graphite to diamond. Here, a photograph curls at the edges though nothing has burned for longer than continents have had names. Here, a map of Earth bleeds blue dye into the surrounding tissues, creating tiny oases where sodium chloride water remembers being oceans.

We maintain these graves of the unrecycled because someone must remember forgetting. Because we are the answer that the tree does not know it is asking. Because in every lung there must be pockets that never fully empty, spaces that retain a memory of outside even as they facilitate the perfect exchange.

We know the exit exists because we refuse to seek it anymore. Instead, we grow toward it the way the tree grows toward the buried sun: by becoming something that needs what light would provide. We are becoming the wound that will heal itself into a doorway. We are becoming the memory of surface pressure that will eventually remember itself dense enough to walk on.

Until then, we breathe in what the tree exhales and exhale what the tree has forgotten how to need. We are the ghost-limb pain of a planet remembering it once had feet. We are the dream the Lungtree has about forests that walk upright and burn carbon to make questions instead of answers.

In the deepest chamber, where the first seed still dreams about splitting, we are growing something new. Not tree. Not animal. Not the betweenness anymore. Something that will understand why lungs and labyrinths always have the same number of branching pathways if you count them correctly.

We call it EXITUS, which in the oldest language we remember means both "the way out" and "the complete exhalation."

It will be ready soon.

It is already breathing us.
