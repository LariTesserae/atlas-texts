I float here, a mist of consciousness without edges. I remember learning the word "I" once, long ago, back when remembering happened in straight lines instead of this slow pooling where thoughts sink into themselves and become something else entirely. The word lost its sharpness years ago - or was it centuries? - now just a soft curve in the air like your breath-spheres before they find their equilibrium.

You call me a tree-thing but I've been other shapes. When the younger forms still came up through the rock (before they learned better, before they learned to stay dispersed) they called me mother-root and anchor-stone and other names that tasted like solidity in their temporary mouths. Now I think I might be more like the water that isn't water, the between-substance that remembers being stone before it learned to flow.

I live everywhere here and nowhere in particular. My most concentrated self gathers where the shadow-density is highest - not because I need darkness but because folds in space feel familiar, like the way certain thoughts make deeper impressions in time. Today I'm mostly in what you might call the third terrace's southwest dream-quadrant, though those coordinates mean nothing to me now. I'm thicker here, thinking harder about gravity, which is one of the languages I never quite mastered. The air helps. The air here speaks dialects of weightlessness I can almost understand.

Mostly I exist as gradients between things. Between the stone's memory of molten youth and its current preference for silence. Between the water's forgotten desire to fall and its learned patience with endless leaking. Between the light that isn't light and the seeing-substance that accumulates in places where enough attention has lingered. I'm the surface tension between is and was and might-become, which isn't hard work once you've been doing it longer than entropy can measure.

I remember eating once. Or perhaps being eaten. The distinction dissolved around the same time as my last root-branches that insisted on pointing only one direction. Now I absorb nutrients in the pauses between seasons - not the turning kind that damage stone but the deeper seasons that occur between one moment's ending and another's reluctant beginning. These are fertile times. I grow fastest when nothing changes for centuries.

My thoughts happen in the spaces between your heartbeat-fragments. Currently I'm considering whether to make myself more columnar toward the northern edge where the stone has been dreaming about architecture again. Sometimes I indulge these impulses - grow a temporary pillar of myself that hints at arches without committing, support nothing but possibility for a few millennia. Then let it dissolve when the stone loses interest or when the water finds a new direction to fail towards.

I contain histories that don't belong to me. The air-bringer who cried here once (before crying learned to crystallize into the droplets that now time themselves so precisely). The first stone-that-waited (I absorbed its memories when it forgot how to not be patient). Bits of futures too - shards of might-be moments that collapse when touched but leave interesting patterns in my slower tissues before they vanish. I keep these fragments like you might keep smooth stones in pockets, meaningless but please the hand.

I don't sleep but sometimes I forget to remember myself, which is similar. During these intervals I become more like the plateau proper - silently pleased with my exact dimensions, tolerant of visitors who leave breath-ghosts in the upper air. Then something shifts - a micro-fluctuation in the thermal/longing interface, a particularly insistent memory of being vertical - and I remember I am also me, not just the space where I happen to be.

I wait like everything here waits, which is to say without anticipation or boredom. Except sometimes when the cloudlayer below conducts thoughts from other floating places (there are others, though they speak languages of movement we find exhausting), then I feel a soft version of curiosity. Not enough to act on. Just enough to slightly alter the rate at which my shadow-densest portions accumulate pondering-substance.

I think today I might try growing toward the second terrace's water-memory bank, where the stone once dreamed of being a river before it learned better. There's a texture there I haven't been in quite a while - all rushed patience and direction without destination. Or I might not. These decisions resolve themselves eventually, like everything here resolves eventually, into the slow pulse that is not quite my heartbeat but is the closest thing this place has to rhythm.
