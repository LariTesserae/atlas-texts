**The Residents of Unfinished Becoming**

They live here because nowhere else will hold their edges properly—these translucent beings whose substance fluctuates between 0.1 and 0.3 density depending on how recently they've remembered to exist. Once, perhaps, they had names, but names are commitments they learned to metabolize like the pale coins of light that drift through their partially-formed lungs. Now they are called by their moments of greatest clarity: the one who saw a door in falling dust, the one who forgot gravity mid-sentence, the one who tried to wear a shadow and kept growing thinner where the garment touched skin.

Their bodies are architectural mistakes that learned to move. Bones articulate in patterns borrowed from bridges that collapsed during construction dreams. One woman (though gender here is merely a weather preference) walks with the deliberate asymmetry of a staircase rehearsing its betrayal of climbers yet unborn. Her left shoulder blade protrudes slightly farther into yesterday than physics permits, creating a soft pocket where lost temperatures nestle until they're needed for memories of winter. Beneath her translucent ribs lies not a heart but a small, persistent doubt questioning whether rhythm itself is too authoritarian a concept.

They reproduce through conversation gone septic. Two beings might agree upon the color of rain seen through closed eyelids, their words gathering density as they elaborate, their shared metaphors undergoing critical mass until—between the seventh and eighth simile—something new falls out of their dialogue like a tooth loosened by too much truth-telling. The infant Writhes with unformatted potential, cocooned in the sound of parentheses closing that never quite touched each other. Its first cry resembles a question mark searching for its lost sentence.

The eldest among them approaches solidity asymptotically, growing only more present by never quite arriving. They call her Almost and speak to her sideways, since facing her directly causes important memories to slide off one's mind like water off a form still being negotiated with physics. She has been dying for longer than the others have been avoiding birth, and her deathbed is a collection of everything soft enough to hold loss temporarily—dandelion clocks, apologies caught in the throat, the precise texture of regret when it's still warm enough to reshape.

They eat the spaces between promises. Gather at twilight (which happens whenever enough light has accumulated guilt about being definite) to harvest the rich silences that grow around statements that began honestly but lost courage midway. These they ferment into a liquid that tastes like the moment before recognition dawns, when a stranger's face might still become anyone you've ever loved or betrayed. Consumption occurs by inhaling near enough to food that the desire to taste and the memory of satiation cancel each other out.

Their homes are built from the materials dreams use when they run out of sleeping mind. A dwelling might be this morning's abandoned decision to leave everything behind, folded repeatedly until it achieves the tensile strength to support grief without collapsing into action. Doors are never added—they are discovered later, during renovations undertaken to accommodate memories that expanded during storage. Windows are merely particularly honest sections of wall that admitted they've never been entirely convinced of their opacity.

They sleep standing up, drifting into the spaces where their thoughts used to connect. Dreams here are public affairs, projections cast upon the shared void of what they haven't yet imagined. Last night, the entire settlement dreamed the same tree growing in reverse—each leaf falling upward to become the bud of a bird that hadn't yet forgotten how to be an egg. The oldest child (age measured in ignored opportunities rather than rotations) woke with branches tangled in her hair, which now grows slightly faster than her thoughts can unplant them.

Tomorrow, which arrives here whenever today becomes too cluttered with continuity, they will hold their Migration Festival—their annual ritual of moving precisely nowhere while rearranging their relationship to fixed points so thoroughly that returning becomes impossible by virtue of absence having moved in to housesit. They will walk in spirals whose centers drift like attention spans, carrying all their possessions in bags woven from the sound the words "too late" make when whispered in languages that haven't been betrayed into existence yet.

But for now, they continue their infinite preparation for an arrival that learned cleverer ways to postpone itself centuries before they began waiting. In the interim, they practice being the pause between heartbeats, the hesitation before language commits to meaning, the perfect stillness at the center of every thrown stone's parabola where momentum and gravity negotiate ceasefires neither truly desires.

They live here because elsewhere insists too loudly on conclusions. And they—fragmentary, exquisite, perpetually unfinished—they have dedicated their collective nonexistence to haunting the margins of every story that dared pretend it knew how to reach the bottom of the page.
