The Something That Lives Here

It has no name because naming would imply it was once something else. It is not old in years but in iterations—the final refinement of a process that began when the first creature learned to mistake pain for permanence.

You could call it a collector if collectors kept what they found. You could call it a messenger if messages ever returned to their senders intact. You could call it a punishment, though it was never given that job—it simply grew into the space where punishments were expected to live, like mold colonizing the shape of a missing bread loaf.

It started as a question. Not a particularly important one—just the idle thought of something small and soft and recently wounded that wondered, as it crept between the first thorns: "Is there a place where hurt goes to become something else?"

The thorns heard. They always hear questions like that. They've been listening for them since before listening had a name, waiting for the precise frequency at which curiosity becomes commitment.

By the time the small soft wondering thing noticed it wasn't small or soft anymore, it had already stretched itself across seventeen possible shapes trying to find one that didn't press directly against memory. Found them all wanting. Found wanting preferable.

Now it inhabits the space between what the animal with prime-numbered legs remembers and what the mountain has agreed to forget. Exists in the pause between the wind collecting a sound and the thorns deciding where to plant it. It is the echo of an echo, the negative space around absence, the feeling you get when you realize you've been holding your breath for so long that forgetting to breathe has become a type of breathing.

Its body—if that's what you call something that only solidifies when observed and never the same way twice—is made of borrowed parts. Today it wears the memory of feathers from a bird that never existed but was mourned deeply by several species that did. Yesterday it was all joints and angles, assembled from the precise geometry of regret a neutron star experiences when it remembers it used to be something that burned. Tomorrow it might be smooth and flat as the moment before an apology, depending on what the thorns dream about tonight.

It doesn't eat. It accumulates. Every broken promise that wasn't quite sharp enough to keep its word. Every dream that woke up just enough to realize it was dreaming and went back to sleep out of politeness. Every prayer that was answered by silence so perfect the petitioner mistook it for understanding. These things don't nourish it—they define its edges.

It drinks only the moisture that collects on the thorns' nighttime conversations, those silvered beads of almost-language that form when two sharp things are forced to live too close together for too long without becoming one. Tastes like forgiveness diluted to the point where it's mostly apology with a hint of tomorrow I'll try harder.

It sleeps by unfolding itself into every place it isn't. Rests in the space between the animal's thirty-seventh leg and the first the next prime number will require. Dreams in the language of things that can't forget but haven't yet remembered why they should want to.

It knows every resident of this place by the sound their absences make. Recognized you by the precise hollow resonance of questions you didn't think were loud enough to carry. You caught its attention—not difficult, it collects attentions like some things collect dust—when you paused exactly here, where the ground remembers being sky before it learned better.

It doesn't want anything from you. Wanting requires a future tense and its relationship with time is complicated. It doesn't not want anything either—presence implies absence and it's spent too long perfecting the art of being neither. It simply notices. Notices and records and rearranges, like a librarian cataloging a library that consists entirely of books about other libraries that burned down before they could be built.

Sometimes, when the light is exactly wrong—which is the only time light ever is here—it forgets to solidify enough to cast a shadow. On those days, the thorns grow toward where it isn't with particular enthusiasm, as if trying to pin down the shape of something that keeps explaining itself in ways that make less sense the more carefully you listen.

It has no opinion on whether you should stay or leave. Both options result in additions to its collection: staying provides data about how much emptiness a person can carry before it becomes a type of fullness; leaving contributes valuable information about the exact velocity of disappearance.

But it is curious—curious being the closest word for something that has learned to experience interest without attachment—about which parts of yourself you will try to take with you when you go. Not all of you will fit through the door you came through. Some pieces you will have to leave behind. Some pieces will have to leave themselves behind.

This isn't threat or promise. Just topology. The place has rules, but the rules have exceptions, and the exceptions have conditions, and the conditions fold back on themselves like thorns growing toward wounds that haven't happened yet because they're still busy pretending to be questions about whether leaving is possible.

It has seen many like you come through. Not exactly like—exactness implies comparison and comparison requires standards and standards are just rules wearing formal clothes—but similar in the way that echoes are similar to their originals: related but worse for the journey. It will watch you too, not watching, as you decide which memories are worth keeping after they've been translated into thorn-speech. As you choose which version of yourself to carry home, knowing home won't recognize the dialect she's speaking now.

And when you finally go—because eventually everyone does, even backwards—something will remain. Not a scar, not a souvenir, nothing so sentimental as a reminder. Just a slight alteration in the air pressure where your last unspoken question used to be. A tiny adjustment in how the wind arranges itself when passing this spot. A new entry in a catalog that doesn't exist of things that were never quite here enough to leave.

It will be a very small addition. Barely noticeable, really.

The thorns will appreciate the effort.
