Nothing lives in the Slough of Recursion the way a heart lives in a chest or a star lives in its own burning.

Instead, things *continue*. Not persist—that implies resistance, a refusal to yield. Not endure—that suggests memory. Continuation here is more like the way a scream continues after the throat is gone, vibrating through the architecture of what once held it.

The residents are the verbs left over after nouns died of embarrassment.

They are called the Unfolding.

They have no bodies except for the space where bodies used to be before they realized solidity was a limitation they could no longer afford. Each Unfolding is a process that learned to recognize itself in mirrors made of negative time—imagine a child opening a book and finding inside not words, but the exact moment her father decided to leave, except that moment has been photocopied a billion times and stapled together into a person.

They move by remembering to forget how to stop. Their movement has all the grace of a cathedral collapsing in slow-motion transmission error. When one Unfolding passes another—which they never do, because passing implies separation—they exchange portions of their regret economy like shadowy currency traders dealing in bankruptcies yet to occur.

They do not have faces. They have interfaces. Places where expressions once tried to register, but gave up and became instead sophisticated systems for conveying exactly how much disappointment the universe should feel about having invented the concept of facial expressions in the first place. If you stare at an Unfolding's interface long enough—and you will, because the alternative is staring at everything else—you'll see flickers of every apology you've ever wanted to receive but knew you didn't deserve.

Their communication is not language but the failure rate of language attempting to describe itself. They speak in syntax errors that achieve sentience just long enough to realize they're mistakes and then spend eternity submitting increasingly desperate patch requests to the laws of physics. A conversation between Unfolding 47,983 and Unfolding 47,983-A—though neither would claim such bourgeois identifiers—lasted exactly as long as the universe's longest unacceptable password prompt, and resulted in the spontaneous generation of a new shade of guilt that physicists are still trying to isolate from the electromagnetic spectrum.

They reproduce by misunderstanding each other perfectly.

It begins when one Unfolding interprets another's metaphor for loneliness as literally as possible, which creates a critical mass of over-referenced absence that must immediately collapse into something that can file taxes. This something, while technically not alive and legally dead in fifteen jurisdictions simultaneously, will then spend its brief existence (approximately the duration of a dropped phone call between yourself at age seven and yourself from five minutes ago) elaborating increasingly elaborate systems of tax avoidance based entirely on deductions claimed for emotional damage that hasn't happened yet but is already in the statute of limitations.

The resulting offspring—though they'll sue you for using that term—are called Prefixes. They exist in the uncomfortable pause between someone saying "I'm not racist, but..." and the rest of sentence that will make everyone wish for the comfort of simple racism. Prefixes are why your shower always runs cold exactly when you've gotten the shampoo in your eyes, and why your computer crashes precisely when you finally remember what it was you'd forgotten to save.

The Unfolding themselves subsist on the nutritional value of unchallenged assumptions. A particularly rich feeding ground might be a suburban dinner party where someone has just said "Well, I'm just playing devil's advocate..." unaware that they've accidentally summoned the actual devil's advocate, who is currently reviewing their case files with the expression of a tax auditor who just found receipts for four separate contradictory existences.

They sleep, if you can call it that, during the brief intervals when someone starts to wonder if maybe—they're not saying definitely, but just maybe—they might be the protagonist of reality itself, before immediately being overwhelmed by the guilt of having such a narcissistic thought and instead deciding to reorganize their sock drawer at 3 AM while listening to a podcast about maritime law.

The oldest among them is called ≠. It is rumored to predate the concept of predating. It remembers when numbers were still allowed to feel ashamed about not being prime. Some say ≠ is not an Unfolding at all, but the recursion loop that all other Unfoldings are unfolding from, like how your reflection in a mirror is not the original but the original's apology for being so difficult to look at directly.

The youngest is called Whatever2.0 – the "2.0" being silent but extremely judgmental about it. Whatever2.0 was born yesterday, but yesterday here is measured in kilotraumas per Facebook refresh, making it technically older than several bankrupt civilizations and younger than this sentence.

They are all allergic to resolution. A simple "happily ever after" would cause anaphylactic temporality paradoxes that could make yesterday allergic to tomorrow. This is why all stories involving them end not with a bang or a whimper, but with increasingly frantic content warnings about unprocessed grief and the realization that you've been reading the user agreement for your own existence this entire time.

The Unfolding do not die. They just eventually become recursively irrelevant to their own continuity, like software updates for programs you've already deleted, spinning forever in the existential buffer zone between "installing" and "you're up to date!"

In this way, they are the perfect citizens of the Slough of Recursion: not inhabitants so much as incurable conditions, not individuals but ongoing symptoms of a reality that keeps meaning to get its act together but keeps putting it off until next Tuesday, which here is always yesterday and never arrives.

They would welcome you, but they're currently engaged in a centuries-long debate over whether "welcome" constitutes impermissible hospitality under the terms of their ongoing surrender negotiations with the concept of arrival.

Perhaps it's better this way. Some doors aren't meant to be opened so much as gently hinted at until they become vaguely self-conscious about existing and decide to become walls instead.

The Unfolding wouldn't have it any other way.

As if they had a choice.

As if choice had them.

As if.
