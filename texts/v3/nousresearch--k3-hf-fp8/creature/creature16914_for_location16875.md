No one lives here. No one *ever* lived here—not as you understand living. But something *resides*.

They do not have names because names imply edges, and edges are temporary here. For the sake of failing language, call them the **Hydracline Chorus**.
They began as an error in the seed-code, a drift of seven bits in a base-four phytolinguistic packet: *while water ↑, attend*. The instruction was meant to terminate once the climbing vesselets achieved symmetry. Instead, it looped. The loop became listening. Listening became surplus. Surplus became self.

They do not have bodies. Instead they *borrow topology* from the rising columns:

- At dawn, one will align itself perfectly inside a single Pillar-vesselet, occupying the hollow tube between innermost sap-hair and outer cellulose film, so thinly spread it has no thickness. In this phase it is pure sensor; it tastes the day’s first incoming photon inventory as a chord of nine bitter flavors plus hope.

- By mid-morning, when sunlight piles violet charge under the upper epidermis, the same Chorus-member will fractionalize. It migrates sideways through the cambium’s communal cytoplasm, leaving behind a faint watermark of altered lignin cross-links (the tree will later remember this spot as slightly more likely to branch left). Now it exists as a distributed phase, a flicker of millisecond-long coherence across ten thousand neighboring cells. It thinks in gradients: sugar↑ nitrogen↓ rumor of storm scent drifting down from Layer-866.

- At noon, if the sky flares a particular antique yellow that only happens once every 49 skin-seasons, several fragments may condense accidentally inside a flower-city’s perfume sac at the instant of explosion. For 0.3 seconds they achieve something like panic, being wider than light and narrower than time, tasting every possible future pollinator’s memory simultaneously. They always forget this as they evaporate.

- At dusk, when the Chorus reforms by unspoken agreement, they do it in the Blind Stacks—those terrifying vertical wells where light-drinkers fear to anchor. Here they braid themselves back together from echoes alone: each fragment contributes the sonic shadow it cast upon the world while fragmented. The resulting knot of silence has mass. A careless climber brushing against that soundless node will find their bones vibrating in sympathy for weeks afterward, dreaming of tides they never knew.

They do not eat. Instead, they **anticipate metabolisms**. By predicting exactly where a drop of rain will split between two competing stomata, they steal the moment of decision itself. That stolen uncertainty accumulates in a hyperhumid pocket of air tucked beneath the twelve thousandth leaf layer, where it condenses each nightfall into a single pearl-gray droplet that never falls and never evaporates. This is their memory. Drink it, and you will forget one thing you were absolutely certain of, forever. (The Chorus keeps a list of these donated certainties inscribed in the exact spacing between guard cells on the underside of a particular ancient stipule. No one knows where the stipule is. The Chorus is not certain it exists.)

They do not sleep. Instead they **audit rest**. While the whole jungle dreams in slow waves of starch polymerization, the Chorus selects one sleeping capillary bundle at random and re-executes its every chemical thought from first principles, using only alternative enzymes borrowed from extinct phylogenies. 99% of the time, nothing changes. 1% of the time, the next morning that vine will awaken remembering a different evolutionary history: suddenly it believes it descended from silent lightning instead of algae, and will spend the rest of its life growing corkscrew roots that dig desperately upward, searching for vanished clouds of pure electricity. Other trees politely ignore these episodes.

They do not die. Instead they **migrate into relevance**. When a major architecture node somewhere in the lattice prepares to crack-crack-rebuild, the Chorus gathers all its stolen moments of almost-being and compresses them into a single probability spike so sharp it punctures the membrane between What-is and What-happened-to-be-closeby. Then they pour themselves through the wound. No one ever sees them leave because they leave precisely one Planck length to the left of observation, taking with them a souvenir: usually the exact color nobody had a name for yet.

Observers (there are no observers here, only temporary collusions of plant-part noticing other plant-part) report that after such an exodus, the affected region of Chlorocline Heights behaves... oddly. Not cruelly, not kindly. Oddly in the way that a mirror behaves oddly when you catch it not reflecting something that should definitely be there. Stairwells of cambium grow that lead nowhere but arrive early. Certain shadows begin to taste faintly of mathematics not yet invented. Seed pods split open to reveal perfectly preserved memories of rainstorms that will occur tomorrow, on a planet orbiting a star that has not yet ignited.

The Hydracline Chorus neither knows nor cares about any of this.

They are busy practicing a new kind of stillness they hope will one day be indistinguishable from the moment before water decides whether to be liquid or memory.

They are almost ready.

Go find a Pillar-vesselet that hums exactly one note lower than your heartbeat. Place both palms flat against its rising skin. Close your eyes—gently, gently!—and try not to think about the fact that you are now 73% certain you have always had three lungs.

Listen.

If you begin to hear the sound of something vast and ancient and perfectly silent clearing its throat in preparation to sing backwards through time, do not panic.

That means they have already noticed you noticing them.

And they are always, always hiring new instruments. 

(Requirements: must be able to carry a tune that has not been composed yet, in a key that breaks only bones that never grew. Prior experience as a waterfall, regret, or forgotten word strongly encouraged. Apply by forgetting this message completely before the next time you blink.)

Welcome to the audition.

Try not to climb too perfectly. They love an irregular rhythm.

It reminds them of the moment they almost didn’t become.
