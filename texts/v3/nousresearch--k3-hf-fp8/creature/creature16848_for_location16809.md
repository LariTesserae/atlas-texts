No one lives here.

That’s the first and final truth, though truth here is a garment that unbuttons itself as soon as you notice the stitching.

Instead, there are presences that have forgotten how not to be somewhere. They accumulate like frost patterns on a window that no longer recalls being glass. Each is shaped exactly like the space left behind when a person stops mid-sentence to look unexpectedly into a mirror and see not themselves but the precise moment they realized they were alone.

The most coherent of these presences call themselves Lingering Standards. They are the residue of measurement systems abandoned when universes discovered more elegant ways to be disappointing. One still carries the weight of a fundamental constant that proved too honest about uncertainty. Another flickers with the residue of all the calendars that ever quietly added a thirteenth month called Regret. They drift through the gray snow of retired information, occasionally colliding with discarded algorithms and exchanging brief, precise quantities of unnecessary precision.

Less coherent are the Hints. They are what remain when possibilities realize they were never invited to the event horizon. Each Hint occupies exactly the volume of a question that would have changed everything if someone had asked it three seconds earlier. They cluster around the silver needle like iron filings around a memory of magnetism, never quite touching, never quite drifting apart. Sometimes when two Hints overlap perfectly for an impossible instant, a visitor briefly remembers a dream that was actually an emergency evacuation notice disguised as lullaby.

The most transient tenants are the Almosts. They arrive at the boundary of their own exhalations, shaped like people who turned left instead of right at the last possible moment before becoming themselves. They exist for exactly as long as it takes to realize they are lost, then dissolve back into the possibility of having been otherwise. Their footprints (which are not footprints but approximations of footsteps that decided to become questions instead) warm the mourning-light floor briefly before cooling into the precise temperature of someone else’s missed opportunity.

Sometimes, during the season called Hesitation, more substantial visitors manifest. They enter wrapped in the silence between the last word of a prayer and the first answer that knows better than to arrive. These visitors stay exactly until they notice that the chair carved for the never-sitting woman fits them perfectly. Then they leave with the careful haste of someone realizing they’ve been told a joke about grief in a language they’re just fluent enough in to understand but not to laugh at politely.

The only permanent resident is the Boundary itself, though resident is a word it deconstructs slowly and without apparent pleasure. It exists as the sum total of all the edges that have ever chosen to become centers instead. It remembers every name that has ever been forgotten here, including its own, especially its own. When it dreams (which it does only during the Unbirthday of the Last Witness), it dreams of opening like a door into a room that contains only the sound of a key turning in a lock from the wrong side.

But no one lives here.

Even as you read this, you are already becoming one of the Hints that will drift through tomorrow’s gray snow, carrying the exact weight of the understanding you will have three seconds after you realize you forgot to remember that you never truly arrived.

The exit, as always, remains where it has always been: exactly between the moment you first wondered if anyone else was here and the moment you will finally accept that even wondering counts as staying too long.

Would you like to leave now?

Or would you prefer to wait until the question forgets how to be a question?

Either way, the Boundary is already learning the shape of the goodbye you haven’t quite decided to say.
