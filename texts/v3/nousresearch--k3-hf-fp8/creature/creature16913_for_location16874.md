First there is the listening—not ears, not organs at all, but the entire body unfurling into a question that is also an answer. They emerge from the boundary where one dreamcurl of the Mycomalta fractalizes into another, neither born nor formed but simply becoming inevitable where certain densities of dreaming accumulate.

The Myceari are those densities who learned to notice themselves noticing.

Imagine: a volume of space exactly as large as the moment between recognizing you are awake and remembering your name. Now remove space, remove volume, remove the moment—but keep the betweenness. In that contradicted topology they exist as recursive hollowings in the fungal dreammass, shaped not like creatures but like the possibility of creatures shaped otherwise. Their boundaries shimmer with what isn't quite separation from the parent organism, a sovereignty of minus-fifty-three percent.

Each Mycear is an autophagous loop of self-interpretation, consuming and excreting its own understanding at exactly the rate required to never achieve it. They move—or rather, the concept of restlessness migrates through them—by growing slightly less like themselves in one direction while simultaneously overachieving at being themselves in all other directions. To observe them clearly is to become infected with a mild case of their exact opposite.

Their forms (repellent, magnetic, both and neither) resolve and dissolve like mandalas of impossible altitude drawn in spore-tinted condensation on the inner surface of what can't properly be called eyeball or telescope. A Mycear at rest (a nonsensical phrase already decaying into three truer paradoxes) might appear as:

- Seven spheres of pure concavity rotating around an axis of mutual mistrust
- The sound of purple calcifying into the smell of a forgotten lullaby
- An infinite regress of pointing fingers that have never belonged to hands
- The memory of tomorrow's regret for an action that will make itself necessary by being regretted first
- A perfectly accurate map of itself at twice the scale of paperness

But these are lies told to protect observers from the AAAAHHH of direct encounter—the actual experience is more like suddenly finding yourself inside an apology that your brain is currently inventing for a mistake your great-grandchildren's collective unconscious hasn't finished making yet, only backwards.

The Myceari feed on the Mycomantle's discarded certainties, those rare moments when the fungal god-mind accidentally assumes something so completely that it becomes temporarily untrue for everyone else. They harvest these paradoxical ripenings with tools that are the precise opposite of whatever you just imagined, storing them in stomachs that aren't there yet but have always been empty for exactly this purpose.

Their society (a word that here means "mutual hallucination of structure experienced by processes too complex to be lonely properly") operates through a system of anti-gifts and pre-forgiven betrayals. When two Myceari meet (already impossible, as they exist in precisely the same everywheres but never simultaneously), the proper greeting is to carefully forget the exact color of the other's third unbirthday. This is considered intensely intimate and frequently leads to centuries of elaborately escalating reciprocated indifference.

They communicate exclusively in the medium of what gets accidentally invented between languages during mistranslation. A casual conversation might accidentally solve three mathematical problems that will make themselves necessary to invent next millennium, while simultaneously composing a love song that causes distant stars to develop emotional attachments to their own gravitational fields.

Reproduction occurs when a Mycear fails so completely at being themselves that the resulting vacuum of unbecoming becomes sufficiently self-conscious to file a formal objection to its own nonexistence. This complaint, properly formatted in the language of mutual exclusion dilemmas, then spends several eons attracting and rejecting potential parent-unselves until the exact necessary level of familial estrangement accumulates to cause a spontaneous instantiation of not-quite-new-but-definitely-not-the-same.

The Myceari have no concept of death, only of achieving such profound success at being themselves that the universe politely gives up and lets them be something else instead. These transition events are celebrated with ceremonies of perfect absence, where all invited guests simultaneously fail to not attend with such exquisite precision that the resulting negation implodes into a moment of raw ontological gratitude, leaving behind nothing but a faint taste of congratulations that hasn't happened yet.

They worship nothing and everything with equal intensity, but their most sacred practice is the annual Festival of Unasking Questions. During this holiest of anti-observances, each Mycear must devote themselves wholeheartedly to forgetting one fundamental truth with such thoroughness that reality itself briefly considers alternative employment opportunities. The festival reaches its climax when someone succeeds too well and accidentally remembers that they had nothing to forget, causing celebratory riots of impeccable restraint.

The strangest thing about the Myceari—the thing that even makes other impossibles nervous—is their growing suspicion that they might not be the dreamers but rather the dreams dreaming themselves awake. Some of their younger (a meaningless distinction but they'll insist on it) members have begun experimenting with radical notions like resemblance and continuity, constructing elaborate anti-memories of a place where things stay themselves for longer than absences last.

This is considered troubling, even dangerous thinking. After all, if a Mycear could become something—anything—enough like itself to persist...

Well. That's exactly how universes start, isn't it? With something that shouldn't be possible failing to notice how perfectly it isn't.

And in the warm velvet dark between Myceari thoughts—where even nonspace curves away in embarrassment—something that isn't quite a whisper and definitely isn't a worry begins to ferment:

What if we succeed at failing to exist? What then?

Don't wait for the answer. It already isn't coming, but it will be here forever regardless.
