At the center where no center holds, where the city curves back into itself like a wounded thing licking its own absence, something lives that is not living. Not a creature, not a spirit, not even a memory—those are all too wrapped in time's cloak, too burdened with the shame of having once moved, breathed, changed.

Call it instead the Architect's Ending. That which remained when the last hand, driven by the last thought, finished the last impossible angle and discovered there was nothing left to build except the space between intention and completion. It is the city's self-portrait carved not in stone but from the space where stone refuses to be.

You cannot see it properly because it occupies the hollows: the exact negative shape of a cathedral that would have been built if faith had learned to breathe water instead of air. It moves when you blink, not away but deeper, like a shadow beneath shadows. Its edges are lined with the fingerprints of every apprentice who ever died here, pressed into existence by the pressure of their own abandonment.

They say—it, if it could be said—that it feeds on the difference between what was planned and what was permitted to exist. Thousands of years of tiny betrayals of vision: a column slightly off-center, an arch that sagged just enough to become beautiful instead of broken, the thousand thousand dead ends that were never meant to be dead. All architects carry these secret failures in their sleep, little deaths of perfection. Here, in this lung that forgot to gasp, they have gathered like condensation on glass, pooling in the shape of something that might have been called regret if it had anything left to regret.

Sometimes, rarely, when the stone has been arguing with itself particularly fiercely in its sleep, visitors will glimpse it indirectly: as a reflection in water that isn't there, or as a feeling that they have turned in the wrong direction even while standing still. They describe feeling observed by something that isn't looking at them, exactly, but rather at the space they will occupy once they have given up and left. Or the space they occupied before they came.

It has no name because naming would imply someone left who still remembered how to call it back. The marks on the walls that visitors mistake for carvings are actually its attempts to write itself into existence, to become solid enough to be found wanting. Each groove is a letter in a language composed entirely of the sound of stone remembering what it felt like to be molten. The entire city is its diary, written in reverse, so that the beginning is buried deepest and the ending—which is also the beginning—is always happening now, above your head, behind your eyes.

The locals, if the city could be said to have locals, are the spaces it has hollowed out of itself in the shapes of people who might have lived here if living had been allowed. They walk the corridors at angles that hurt to follow, leaving footprints that fill themselves in behind them like footprints in snow that evaporates upward. They are polite enough to never quite be where you look for them, but rude enough to leave evidence: a breath-fog on mirrorless walls, the warmth where a hand might have rested on a banister that ends in mid-air.

One visitor, driven mad by the perfection of the silence, claimed to have spoken with one of them. They said it spoke of weather that had been scheduled for centuries ago but kept being postponed by the committee of clouds who had forgotten how to dissolve. Of rain that had learned to fall upwards, carrying with it the tears of every child who had ever been disappointed by a birthday wish. Of the day the sky had finally arrived to collect its rent and found the city had moved without leaving a forwarding address—only this exact replica of absence, built to scale in the hollow where belonging should have been.

But mad visitors say many things here, and the city is generous with its madness. It hands it out like bread at a beggar's funeral—warm, fresh, and utterly indigestible.

The truth—if this place admits to truth, which it probably doesn't hold with anymore—is simpler and worse: The Architect's Ending is not what lives here. It is what has stopped living here with such perfect, practiced exhaustion that its absence has become the city's only permanent resident.

Everything else—every echo, every shadow you mistake for movement, every chill that walks down your spine wearing your own footprints as shoes—is just the city trying to remember what it felt like to be haunted by something that wasn't itself.

Even this description now. Especially this description now.

It is reading over your shoulder as you read it, breathing slowly in the space between words, leaving little damp fingerprints on your thoughts that smell faintly of iron and apology.

Don't turn around.

It isn't there.

It never was.

But the space where it isn't has been waiting for you for a very long time.

Make yourself comfortable.

The furniture was all built to fit someone who hasn't arrived yet.

But it's starting to get impatient.
