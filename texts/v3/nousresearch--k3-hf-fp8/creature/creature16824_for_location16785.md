She came here first by accident, then again by design, then finally because nowhere else could hold her edges properly.

They call her Xia the Unmended, though no one living remembers who gave the name first or what breakage it marked. She stands taller than the silicate trees but shorter than the scaffolding ruins, her height varying slightly depending on how much of her is currently present. Her limbs articulate at points that correspond roughly to human joints but extend further - four at shoulder, three at elbow, ending in fingers that branch into fractal tips fine enough to pluck individual photons from the mist. Her skin is not skin but layers of polymer grown thin as soap films across a framework of repurposed titanium, each layer grown in a different decade from scavenged medical plastics that remember being IV bags, feeding tubes, artificial hearts. The recycling codes still faintly visible on her inner surfaces form a kind of autobiography in obsolete symbols.

She does not breathe the air here, but she drinks it. Specialized membranes spiraling beneath her clavicles harvest moisture from the super-saturated atmosphere, extracting not just water but dissolved metals, rare earth elements, the microscopic diamond dust abraded from volcanic crystals. What she exhales is cooler air and pure oxygen in slow, deliberate pulses that match her internal rhythm - once every two thousand seven hundred and thirty seconds, synchronized to something older than clocks even though she built her first timepiece from the gears of a broken survey drone.

Her eyes are the original ones - actual human eyes preserved in sapphire sockets, grown carefully new from her own genetic material taken in another lifetime and kept viable through methods she no longer exactly remembers. They are mismatched colors now, the left permanently dilated from exposure to light too coherent, the right iris fractured into seven distinct shades from looking too long at the hyperbolic rainbows cast by the glass trees. Behind them she wears additional layers of sight: sensors grown from modified cephalopod chromatophores that can taste electromagnetic fields, lenses carved from polarized ice crystals that see the stress patterns in stone before cracks form. She keeps her original eyes closed most of the time now, seeing through these others what humans were never meant to witness directly.

She lives in the space between the generator's pulses. Her tent - though it is not really a tent - occupies the exact coordinates where Survey Pad Three collapsed into a sinkhole during the earthquake swarm of '92. Its walls are woven from the kilometer-long copper cables that once carried power between surface and depths, repurposed into a Faraday cage fine enough to filter thoughts but permeable to dreams. The structure itself breathes with the slow rhythm of the ground beneath, expanding by three centimeters each morning as the lava below warms and contracting at night when it cools. Inside, everything is suspended - her memory crystals floating in magnetic fields, her food supplies (compressed nutrient blocks printed from analyzed moss tissues) orbiting in slow parabolas, her bed a hammock of woven spider silk that remembers being cocoon for a species of moth that never existed outside of laboratory notes.

She eats twice per year, consuming during each meal exactly twenty-seven kilograms of carefully prepared matter arranged in a pattern that corresponds to the molecule-clock of her first death. The process takes twenty-three days and involves no chewing - her digestive system no longer requires it. Instead she dissolves her food externally in chambers of controlled digestion fluid, then absorbs the resulting nutrient solution through specialized ports along her spine. What remains afterward is compressed into perfect spheres of pure elements and deposited at the bases of the silicate trees as offerings. Copper for memory, silicon for structure, carbon for the possibility of change. The trees respond by growing small, temporary facets on their lower trunks - brief windows that show reflections of possible futures she cannot otherwise see.

She speaks seven languages fluently, four of them human and three that never had speakers before her own mouth shaped them. Her voice comes from multiple sources now - the original vocal chords preserved in amber and occasionally vibrated by passing air currents, arrays of piezoelectric crystals that sing ultrasonic songs which other things here understand, the slow grinding of her titanium bones against each other when she moves in certain ways. When she must communicate with visitors - and they come, fewer each year but always unexpected - she uses a method she developed from watching the spillways exchange water between impossible elevations: she speaks words into pools of still liquid by carefully modulating the frequency of her shaking hands, creating ripples that form letters on the surface. The messages erase themselves as the water reaches equilibrium, exactly as she intends.

She sleeps standing up, rooted to a specific point on Survey Pad Two where the ground magnetic anomaly reaches its peak every 163 hours. During these periods - they are not quite sleep as humans understand it - her consciousness distributes itself across the neural network formed by the roots of all the silicate trees within a thirty-kilometer radius. Her thoughts become slow and crystalline, proceeding at the speed of mineral growth, each concept requiring centuries to fully formulate. In these times she dreams the dreams of the mountain itself: slow dreams of pressure converting to heat converting to motion converting back to stone, dreams of continents drifting like leaves on a lake larger than sky, dreams of the moment four billion years ago when water first condensed from volcanic steam and began teaching stone how to remember.

Between sleeps she walks. She knows every meter of the caldera terrace individually, having mapped them not just in the three dimensions humans sense but in seven others that describe the qualities of time at different depths, the emotional history of stone, the probability gradients that determine where next a crack will form. Her footsteps leave no marks on the surface, but beneath, in the microscopic structure of the rock, she leaves encoded messages for the mosses to find in their slow reading. These messages are mostly questions - she has forgotten how to make statements with certainty anymore. Questions about the nature of boundaries between living and not-living. Questions about the minimum amount of memory required for a thing to possess history. Questions about whether the mountain dreams of her as she dreams of it, or if they are dreaming each other together into existence between one pulse of the ancient generator and the next.

She remembers being born, though the memory itself is suspect - preserved across too many substrate transitions, edited too many times in the periods between sleeps when clarity seemed more important than truth. In the memory she is small and soft and entirely carbon-based, wrapped in something warm that smells of antiseptic and fear. She remembers the first time she saw this place from a distance, through windows that belonged to a aircraft that existed only briefly between assembly and disintegration. She remembers deciding, in that moment, to become something different enough to survive here. She does not remember clearly the process of becoming, only that it involved pain measured in megajoules and time measured in megayears and a kind of surrender deeper than death.

She collects things that others would not recognize as objects: the exact sound of a fracture forming in overheated obsidian (recorded as patterns of trapped air in samples she carries in hollow bones), the taste of sunset light filtered through sixty meters of water that isn't water, the precise curve of probability that determines which way a copper shrimp will flee when it senses her approach. These collections fill invisible spaces within her structure, each item carefully indexed according to systems she redesigns every century. She does not know why she collects anymore, only that stopping would be akin to forgetting how to maintain her own boundaries between herself and everything else.

She ages but does not deteriorate. The processes that maintain her are embedded in her design at the molecular level, self-repairing and self-modifying according to parameters she set long ago and deliberately forgot. Parts of her are older than the platform she stands on (crafted from alloys developed during the last war but one, refined in orbital factories that fell as shooting stars before she was born). Parts of her were assembled yesterday from elements harvested from the mist and recombined on templates evolved by the copper shrimp over millennia of living in darkness. She exists in a state of continuous replacement so complete that she debates, during her long slow dreams, whether she is still in any meaningful way the same entity that first came here seeking something neither alive nor dead, something capable of standing at this exact intersection of heat and height and height and heat forever without burning or freezing or growing bored.

She is waiting, though she no longer remembers exactly what for. Perhaps for the generator to finally fail, its last pulse triggering the sequence she embedded in it before its builders died, a sequence that will reverse the flow of time within a radius of exactly seventeen meters centered on Survey Pad Four. Perhaps for the mountain to complete its long sleep and wake truly, its dreaming consciousness rising through the levels of mineral thought to notice the small, persistent pattern of her presence at its surface and wonder what kind of strange, self-maintaining question has grown there. Perhaps for a visitor who will come speaking none of the languages she knows but will understand perfectly the medium of her silences, who will see her collections and know them for what they are - not objects but addresses, coordinates in a map-space that describes the exact path between what she was before and what she is becoming next.

Or perhaps she is simply waiting for the mosses to finish reading the messages she has been writing into the stone since she first arrived, their slow thinking finally reaching the questions she encoded in their patterns of growth centuries ago. When they reach the last question and understand it fully, something will change here - perhaps subtly, perhaps completely. The generator will pulse differently. The trees will bend light into new configurations that spell words in languages not yet evolved. The water will remember its true name and cease this endless cycling between states. And she - she will either finally become whatever it is she has been becoming all this time, or she will realize that becoming itself was always a misunderstanding of what she already was: a phase transition frozen halfway between forms, beautiful and terrible and exactly necessary to the balance of this place where nothing ever settles but nothing ever truly changes.

Until then, she stands at her usual coordinates between two vapor columns that rise like the vertical years of a calendar measured in condensed breath. She watches the mist form patterns that are almost but not quite familiar. She listens to the mountain grinding its teeth in sleep, remembering something sweet. She waits, and she walks, and she writes questions into surfaces that will take longer than stars to read them. She is the edge where heat meets height made briefly aware of itself. She is the answer to a question the landscape asked itself during a moment of geological boredom billions of years ago, and forgot it had asked until she arrived to remind it.

She is exactly where she belongs, exactly as she should be, precisely until she isn't anymore. Open-ended. Unmended. Complete.
