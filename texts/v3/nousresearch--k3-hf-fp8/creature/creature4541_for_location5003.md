They are the only ones who didn't forget how to dream standing upright.

Not the mountains, though they try so hard with their limestone shoulders branched against eternity. Not the clouds that practice dying like a choir losing breath between verses. Not even the trees that grow inverted prayers toward godless skies.

We call them the Remainders, what’s left after everything else learned to move sideways through the world. Thirteen silhouettes carved from the space between breaths, they stand where stone remembers being ocean remembers being starlight remembers being the scream of something first discovering it was alone.

Each carries their exoskeleton outside themselves like a question they forgot how to ask. Their bones are coral reefs grown too quickly from the calcium of missing things—empty birdcages, rusted melodies, the exact weight of “I’m sorry” spoken in a language that had no word for departure. Inside these bone-crevices nest smaller Remainders, Russian dolls of absence, each layer practicing a different way of not being there.

They eat the colors between colors. Drink the sound of shadows lengthening. Reproduce by splitting themselves along fault lines no geologist has named, birthing twins that are always already half-right angles trying to remember what circles were for.

The oldest, whose name translates roughly as "the pause between lightning and counting," wears a cloak stitched from 47,623 years of unfinished extinction events. Where the fabric touches ground, fossils bloom overnight—creatures that never existed but have perfect explanations for why they had to leave. Her tears evaporate before falling, become small angry gods that hurl themselves against mountain knees, demanding passports back to the dream that forgot to wake them.

The youngest, "maybe," still has freckles shaped like the constellations that will exist after stars give up trying to be letters in some ancient love letter to itself. They collect discarded futures in empty nests, teaching them to sing in the key of "what if we had chosen differently?" Their laughter sounds like glaciers remembering dance steps they learned from the bones of butterflies.

Between words they communicate through architecture of absence. A cathedral built from the memory of wings. A bridge made entirely of the promises bridges make when no one is watching them collapse. Right now, they’re constructing something vast and unfinishable from the shadows of birds that haven’t decided whether to migrate or stay and become part of the air’s permanent apology for being breathable.

They are teaching the mountains proper etiquette for dying standing up. Every evening at the angle where sunlight learns to beg, they gather around the fallen dancer-peaks and whisper corrections into limestone ears. "No, you sigh too precipitously. Death by erosion should sound like a mother forgiving God for the mistake of mountains. Try again."

Animals come to them with questions they’re afraid to ask their own reflections. The lizard wearing forests on its back wants to know how to apologize to the trees for never being enough soil. The fossilized deer born already in love with its own absence asks if remembering-forward is a disease or a vocation. The birds that haven’t chosen between being rain or regret seek translation services for their hybrid songs.

For payment, the Remainders collect their unasked questions, string them on threads of time that runs sideways like a fugitive implication. They are weaving a net to catch the starlight that keeps escaping from broken promises made between galaxies that once slept closer together. When asked what they’ll do with such a net if they ever finish counting the holes, they smile with mouths full of tomorrow's silence.

Their greatest work stands unfinished at the exact center where thirteen valleys learn to be one valley betrayed by mirrors. A tower built without ascending, each level constructed from the space between what we meant to say and what gravity made us mean instead. They’ve been adding floors since before floors knew how to pretend to be numbers. The bottom extends down through layers of compressed forgetting into dreams that mountains have about being seas again. The top reaches exactly high enough to scrape the sky’s knee and make it apologize for being blue without permission.

Some say when they complete the tower, the world will remember how to wake up without immediately pretending to have slept elsewhere. Others claim it’s already finished in every language except the one that hasn’t forgiven us for inventing endings.

Tonight, they are teaching water how to forgive itself for climbing instead of falling. They have gathered in a circle exactly wide enough to contain the distance between any two points that once touched and never forgot the taste. Chanting in a rhythm measured by the earth’s paused exhale, they hold hands with the spaces between their hands and slowly, so slowly that tomorrow will need to invent new calendars to record yesterday’s hesitation...

They begin to remember how to dream standing down.

And somewhere between the first impossible angle and the last remaining almost, gravity forgets its name long enough to lean sideways and whisper the world’s oldest secret in a language made entirely of how silence sounds when it finally comes home to apologize for staying away so long.
