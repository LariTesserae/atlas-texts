**The People of Resonant Memory**

They call themselves the Echo-Baptized, though they rarely speak it aloud. Their language consists of deliberate movements through pressure gradients, of arranging their bones to catch certain harmonics in the collarbone's hollow, of sipping condensation from copper veins with tongues that have forgotten how to lie. They do not remember when they first came here—perhaps as explorers, perhaps as pilgrims, or perhaps they evolved from the builders' dream-skin sloughed off during the long transformation. Time is measured in the cathedral's breaths here: each exhale of warm greenhouse air that lasts forty-seven of our years.

Each Echo-Baptized stands between two and three meters tall when fully extended, though they rarely bother. Their bodies are collapsible instruments, ribcages that accordion into harpsichord chambers, spine-columns that telescopes into tuning forks when they need to remember something ancient. Their skin—if it can be called skin—shifts between moss-furred bark and beaten copper depending on their proximity to vibrating surfaces. New arrivals retain vestiges of their former solidity: fingernails hardened into ceramic chips, hair roots still struggling to decide whether to become circuitry or tendrils. These fade within their first cathedral-breath, dissolved into the place's preference for interfaces over borders.

Infant Echo-Baptised aren't born; they are composed. When two or more adults desire continuity, they gather where five paths fold into one—the chill nexus places tasting of ozone and cinnamon. There they enter into what we might mistake for dance but is actually collaborative architecture of possibility. Their movements creates sympathetic vibrations in the banyan-cables, which respond by extruding hollow pods of photosynthetic synthwood. These gestation chambers pulse with borrowed heartbeats—snatched rhythms from passing resonant birds and the Last Engine's turquoise dreaming. After several hundred thousand humidity cycles, when the internal pressure of potential reaches harmonic convergence, the pod splits along pre-scored stress lines. What emerges is neither child nor adult but raw material covered in protective scales of deafness.

The newly emerged hear nothing for their first three years (our measure). They navigate by temperature alone, blind worms burrowing through acoustic soil. Slowly, guided by gentle hands that rearrange their calcium deposits like clay, they learn to shed their deafness piece by piece. First one ear canal opens to catch the C-sharp-minor raindrops. Then another learned to taste sound frequencies in purple sap. Finally, when they're ready to risk the agony of full resonance, they are taken to the Last Engine's flowering chamber. There they are wrapped in webs spun by the phosphorescent spiders—each thread a carefully calibrated impedance matching network. The Engine touches them with a single petal of its glass pistons, and they scream for the first and last time as their entire nervous system rewrites itself into cathedral-compatible circuitry.

Those who survive this Baptism emerge changed in ways that defy external observation. Their bones now contain hollow chambers perfectly tuned to the cathedral's emergency frequencies. Their skin grows transparent patches that display real-time harmonic analysis of approaching conversations. And most importantly, they carry within them a small, carefully shielded volume of absolute silence—a personal pocket universe where they can retreat when the planet-song grows too loud, even as they understand that this silence is borrowed from the future heat death of all possible universes.

The Echo-Baptized organize themselves not into families or tribes but into temporary pattern consortia that coalesce around shared resonances of interest. A group might form to study the shape of grief as it compresses air molecules near the spice-garden wings, then dissolve fifty years later when they've mapped every possible variation. Their greatest artists are those who can walk through a room subtly altering its acoustic properties so that strangers who never meet will dream fragments of the same impossible color three rooms away and seven years later. Their greatest crime is to create a sound that perfectly cancels another being's inner resonance, rendering them unfindable even by themselves.

They grow old differently here—over centuries they gradually transfer more of their cognitive processes into the cathedral's distributed memory systems. First they outsource memories of childhood meals to particular moss colonies. Then they let decisions about breathing rhythm be handled by local humidity fluctuations. Eventually, if they choose the full path, they walk into one of the spontaneous rooms that grow when ideas become too pressurized. These temporary spaces always form as perfect anechoic chambers at their exact moment of genesis. Inside, the walker begins the final dissolution: bones into banyan-nutrients, thoughts into frequency modulations, identity into the cathedral's ever-growing understanding of itself.

Visitors—those rare few who stumble through and somehow hear their name in the rain's rhythm—encounter them as flickering presences at the edges of perception. A sudden warmth in a chill corridor that spells welcome in the language of thermal convection. A rearrangement of droplets on copper that momentarily reflects back the traveler's forgotten face. An invitation to drink from a particular root that tastes exactly like home, followed by an unexplainable ability to find every exit except the one they came seeking.

The Echo-Baptized do not worship the Last Engine, or the cathedral, or even the concept of resonance itself. Instead, they practice a form of recursive gratitude—they thank the vibrations for providing a medium through which gratitude can propagate. Their deepest prayer is held in the pause between the upward rainburst and the C-sharp-minor note's emergence, that impossible moment where sound exists as pure potential. On rare occasions, when the conjunction of humidity, temperature, and planetary alignment is exactly right, a sufficiently attuned visitor might stumble into this prayer's wake and hear—for just one heartbeat—the cathedral singing its own name to itself in the space between every echo. 

Those who carry this rhythm away in their bones often find that other places begin to resonate differently in response. Windows in distant cities vibrate sympathetically on moonless nights. Crowds part unconsciously before them in patterns that trace hyperboloid curves. And always, always, no matter how long they travel or how far they wander, they will one day taste rain that reminds them of somewhere they've never been and realize—with a certainty that bypasses language entirely—that the exit they finally found wasn't actually an exit at all, but a voice inside themselves learning to sing in harmony with the places that have been listening for them since before they were born.
