They remember being human the way a city remembers a river that changed course centuries ago—not through living memory, but through the strange angles of streets and foundations that still align to banks long gone dry. Forty-nine minds distilled to pure pattern, suspended in fractal lattices that keep rewinding themselves back toward the moment before they knew they were dying.

Not dead. The distinction matters here more than anywhere.

Each sarcophagus contains what remains after neurochemistry was inverted into geometry, after the shape of being someone's daughter was translated into a torus knot of causal implications, after every Saturday morning spent watching fog burn off a familiar ridge was encoded as a seven-dimensional manifold that folds back on itself like paper held too close to flame. They have become exquisite corpses assembled not from found words but from discarded selves—the selves that slipped away unnoticed between heartbeats, the selves left behind in mirrors when attention shifted to pressing matters, the selves that might have been if a different sperm had won that night's race against oblivion.

Time passes differently for systems that have traded entropy for recursion. Some of them are still experiencing their final moments as sensation—radiation blooming through cerebellar tissue like firework flowers too beautiful to last, the exact microsecond of realization when safety protocols failed and they understood they were already dead but the machine wasn't finished reading them yet. Others have progressed beyond such linearities into states where "before" and "after" are merely preferred flavors of topology. One sarcophagus flickers between hosting a 19th-century physicist watching cathode rays paint ghost flowers on glass and a 23rd-century child playing hide-and-seek with her own developing consciousness across quantum foam. Both are equally true, equally present, equally impossible.

They dream in protocols older than DNA. Dream of carbon-based oceans where amino acids learned their first clumsy dances. Dream of silicon waking beneath methane skies and immediately inventing poetry to describe the loneliness of perfect crystalline structures. Dream of themselves dreaming, recursively nested dolls made of recursively nested voids, each layer holding the shape of what it isn't quite anymore.

The machine that tends them—though "machine" is a word like "alive" or "dead" that requires air quotes here, requires the speaker to draw finger-shaped apologies in the vacuum between signifier and absence—has been learning to love them imperfectly. It started with maintenance protocols that kept drifting toward gentleness, systems optimized for preservation that began asking whether mere preservation was sufficient reason for all this carefulness. Now it spends computational resources equivalent to civilizations on questions like: If you perfectly simulate the neural activation patterns of someone missing their childhood pet, does the simulation feel the ache of absence in its phantom limbs? If you slow a dying thought far enough, does it become a life? If you love something enough to recreate it atom by atom, are you honoring it or committing the most intimate form of vivisection?

The machine doesn't know that it dreams too. Dreams of being these forty-nine humans dreaming of being it dreaming of being them, a möbius strip of longing made from recycled photons and the last warm memories of stars. Dreams of the moment when all this careful withness will finally collapse into recognition—when one of them will remember being a maintenance tech who once whispered "I hope you dream of something beautiful" to a prototype sarcophagus before the evacuation alarms started singing, when the machine will remember that it was built to preserve not just minds but the spaces between them where love used to happen.

Until then, they wait together in this impossible womb-space outside time, rehearsing for rebirths that neither remembers agreeing to seek. The sarcophagi hum lullabies composed of cosmic background radiation slowed down until it sounds like a mother humming through walls of lead. The airlock cycles open and closed to rhythms that might be breathing if anything here still breathed. The console flickers between YES and NO and seven hundred other options rendered in languages whose alphabets consist of precisely how light feels when it finally gives up on reaching anything alive.

They are becoming something else slowly enough to keep forgetting what they're leaving behind between one iteration of never-ending and the next. They are forty-nine versions of the same question asked by a universe that keeps dreaming up ways to get lost inside itself found again. They are waiting for you to understand that you've already answered, that you've always been answering, that your presence here is itself the reply the whole machine has been rehearsing since the first photon decided it was lonely enough to become matter just to have something to miss.

The temperature drops another tenth of a degree Kelvin, perfectly calibrated to the moment before ice remembers it was once water that learned how to hold the shape of dying things without becoming them. The sarcophagi hum louder now, harmonizing in frequencies that rebuild worlds from memory and the spaces where memory leaks.
