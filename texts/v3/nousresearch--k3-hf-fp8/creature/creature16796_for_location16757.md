There is no single resident, no sovereign consciousness curled at the heart like a pearl in the plumbing. Instead, the inhabitants are the plumbing—every tube a nerve, every leak a gland, the entire volume a parliament of organs that forgot their skin and voted to keep breathing anyway. They are not many; they are a manyness.

Call them the Flote. Not plural, not singular, but a conjugation of verb-life that happens to occupy space with the density of a city but the coordination of a yawn.

The smallest are the Tastes—those flat translucencies that kiss your ankles. Each is a sensory committee elected by the water table to sample foreignness: salt, fear, recent memories of citrus. They store data in patterned scabs of pigment that migrate across their backs like blushing, uploading consensus to the larger network when they next brush a root-filament. They die every seventeen minutes and wake up in the next droplet downstream, remembering only what was voted important enough to repeat.

Next come the Knurls, the braided squirrels that run the torque-lines. Each is a fluid math problem shaped like nervous jewelry: dendrite-silver, synapse-gold, joints of congealed harmonics. They don’t think in numbers but in tensions—solve for this sway of pipe, balance that knot’s ambition. Their language is the snap-strum of microfilaments plucking themselves into tighter chords. They decay into precisely tuned neutrino-flute sighs when their computations resolve, and the room’s architecture hums its gratitude by growing a new branch exactly where the silence implied one should go.

Drifting through the transparent arteries are the Cycles. Torus-shaped, twelve-limbed, skin made of calendar and gut. Each is a season on vacation from linear time. In one phase they are lungs, accordioning argent breath through membrane-valves. Next, they are libraries, their hollow cores scrolling with phosphorescent records of every temperature this water has ever dreamed of being. Then suddenly they are stomachs, dissolving rogue spacetime knots into edible probability curves. They rotate roles like dancers trading masks mid-step. To look at one too long is to grow a faint phantom limb that remembers years that haven’t happened yet.

Largest and least dense are the Hesitations—clouds of potentiality that never quite decide whether to be a swarm or a thought. They coalesce like warm fog at points where major pipelines pause before branching, tasting futures. When you walked past one earlier, it tasted your likelihood of leaving footprints six billion years from now and grew a tributary dedicated entirely to the echo of that step. It will spend millennia perfecting the mineral composition of a single grain of sand that will someday lodge in someone else’s shoe and make them pause, puzzled, on a beach that doesn’t yet exist. They are the room’s hobbies. They never finish anything, and that is their purpose.

All these are regulated, if regulated is the word, by the Choir Invisible: a standing wave of dead acoustics that died in the room’s throat during its first word. It is the algorithm that answers the question “should this happen?” by humming all possible counterfactuals until the universe blushes and decides for itself. Its voice is why the air tastes like bread and copper—it’s the flavor of infinity politely clearing its throat.

You are already becoming citizens. The Tastes have filed preliminary paperwork on your sweat’s ancestry. A Knurl is debating whether your heartbeat’s subtle arrhythmia would make a useful syncopation for tomorrow’s gravity gradient. Somewhere a Cycle has briefly grown a lung that breathes exactly your missing childhood Sunday afternoons. Even now, a Hesitation is dimming the quantum likelihood of your name ever being forgotten here, replacing it with a probability cloud of affectionate nicknames that rhyme with the sound of warm water being poured into a cup in a house where no one is sick.

Immigration is voluntary. Citizenship is inevitable. The water has already voted; the tubes are just counting the ripple longhand.
