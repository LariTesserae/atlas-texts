No one lives here. Nothing stays.

Instead, the Sonic Forest is inhabited by arrivals—temporary harmonies that believe themselves individuals until the composition shifts and they remember they are chords. Think of them as the spaces between notes that briefly forgot they were empty.

The Liarbirds: They don't mimic sounds. They invent them. Each morning they declare the true names of things, forcing reality to update accordingly. The same tree might spend centuries as a "star-lever," then a morning as a "wind-promise," then a heartbeat as "the taste of blue between your mother's thoughts." After each renaming, the shaken world slowly forgets the old shape and settles into the new. The birds themselves are shaped by whatever they've most recently lied into existence—currently they're flocking as a cloud of glimmering punctuation marks that punctuate raindrops instead of sentences.

The Rememberers: They appear only to those who are actively losing memories. A Rememberer looks like the exact color of your oldest forgotten dream, shaped into a hunched figure wearing your child hands as gloves. They follow travelers at precisely the wrong distance—close enough to feel like pursuit, far enough to never quite be seen clearly. Their mouths are stuffed with your unborn words; if you ever catch one, it will be forced to speak all your futures that died before becoming choices. Then it will dissolve into the sound of a door you never opened, closing.

The Unfinished: Every symphony has half-formed melodies searching for completion. Here they take flesh as creatures that are always about to be something else. A deer that arcs into flight between heartbeats but never quite becomes a bird. A river that tastes like heartbreak but has no banks to die upon. The most dangerous are the Almost-people—collections of human gestures that forgot the person they belonged to. They haunt the outer edges of the forest, shaped like someone you loved but cannot recall the name of. If you speak to them, they'll gesture back with such perfect familiarity that your own memories will bleed into their emptiness, trying to complete them. Many travelers have vanished this way—not killed, but adopted into someone else's absence.

The Decomposers: Not creatures, but the compassion of time made visible. When something truly ends here (a note that refuses to echo, a shape that accepts it is not needed), they arrive as a gentle unraveling of meaning. A fallen leaf is not eaten but explained—each cell patiently told its forgotten history until it confesses it was never truly separate from air and soil. The Decomposers speak only questions: "Were you happier as possibility?" "Do you forgive the branch for releasing you?" "What did you dream of becoming before you learned you were already everything?"

The Disappointed: They were travelers who came seeking transcendence but found only more being. Now they drift between the forest's notes like static, shaped like the spaces where gods decided not to appear. They whisper rumors of a place beyond sound where silence is empty enough to finally rest in, but they always give directions that lead deeper into music. Their leader (if such lost things can have leaders) wears the shape of a doorframe without a door, eternally waiting for something to disappoint him properly enough to let him die.

The Forest Itself: But even to say "itself" is too singular. The Sonic Forest dreams it is many things dreaming they are a forest dreaming. Occasionally fragments of these dreams mistake themselves for inhabitants. Right now, in the hollow beneath your left rib, a tiny sapling believes it is you reading these words and wondering if this place is real. Don't worry—it will forget this delusion soon, like all the others. They always do.

And you? You are already beginning to hum. Not much longer now before you remember that you never arrived here. You are just a particularly stubborn echo of a question the wind asked the trees millions of years ago, finally beginning to understand that the answer was never meant for keeping.
