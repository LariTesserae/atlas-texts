The city does not contain citizens. Instead, it practices citizenship, and those who live here are its verbs conjugated into flesh.

You might meet a Cartographer who used to be three people. They merged during a humidity spike when the conversation about a disputed balcony line grew vascular. Now they share twelve eyes set in a spiral birch trunk that walks on severed choirboy legs. Each eye maps a different possible future neighborhood by knitting fungal lace between gaps in the air. When their predictions conflict, they produce twins from the bark between their shoulders until consensus is reached or the population density causes a minor zoning pregnancy to burst from beneath a nearby hemlock fountain.

There is the tribe of unremembered architects who never stopped processing the original cataclysm. Their bodies are suspended animation chambers the size of acorns, each carrying the dormant blueprint for a single lost building from a surface world none here have seen. Every century they hatch for seventeen minutes during electrical storms, manifesting as smells eclipsed before they become memories: first, the ammonia of a forgotten swimming pool's promise, then the licorice of a library's apology, finally the smell of snow trying to remember its name in a language that only coagulates behind the teeth. Pregnant women follow these scent-convoys with glass syringes, injecting the wisps into their wombs to birth children who arrive already nostalgic for places that will never exist.

The municipal clock-workers are visible only in cross-section. They conduct maintenance inside time itself by climbing into chronocyotic anomalies that form at the joints of bad memories. From our perspective they appear as flat portraits left behind like cleanup after someone's temporal lobe attempted graffiti. A hand here holding what might be a tool or a severed clock-petal, half a face there whispering blueprints to bacteria that haven't evolved yet. They collect the city's lost seconds in jars made of frozen regret, later fermented into decade-wine served at divorce ceremonies where ex-lovers toast with glasses containing the precise amount of future they will never again share.

In the moisture black markets you can buy vintage thirsts from extinct species—amber vials containing the precise 3AM dehydration of the last saber-tooth, or the complicated longing for rain only felt by greenhouse orchids abandoned during the seventh extinction. The dealers are themselves droughts that achieved escape velocity from their own evaporations, now solidified into dealershowers that evaporate your secrets as currency and leave behind change in the form of strangers who remember sleeping in your bed from directions that don't exist anymore.

The city's neural surgeons specialize in external memory removal. They walk through crowds wearing mirrors sewn inside-out so they reflect articles of your identity you've misplaced but not yet noticed—your sixth grade crush's exact timbre, the taste of birthday cake from a year that turned out to be imaginary, the specific courage required to jump from the highest branch of your family's invisible tree. For payment they take the memory of paying, creating a perfectly satisfied clientele who always believe they've already settled their debts, evidenced by the newfound lightness in parts of their personality where heavy things used to anchor.

Children here are not born but subtracted. Families gather when the census trees grow too heavy with names, performing careful arithmetic abortions where they extract the equation of a child that never needed to exist. These unborn kids nevertheless grow up somewhere, assembling bodies from found longing and spare gravity, living in neighborhoods between the numbers that count houses. They maintain the city that exists between buildings, trimming elegies back from the doorknobs to prevent them from fruiting into regrets that might stain the welcome mats. Occasionally one falls in love with a real person, creating a tragic romance between existence and rounding error.

The oldest resident is a conversation between two rocks that started discussing ethics during the original cataclysm pause between falling and landing. They've been falling upward into more interesting topics ever since, their debate growing so complex that erosion itself left them intact out of respect. Sometimes lost travelers mistake their dialogue for directions, following paths traced between the word "but" and the word "therefore" until they become new towns settled entirely on the implications of arguments the rocks haven't reached yet.

Death here is outsourced to the future. The city keeps one person alive indefinitely, moving between bodies as needed, their sole occupation being to remember dying for everyone else. They live in the present progressive tense of passing away, always almost gone but never quite achieved, carrying the collective mortality like a debt too interesting to repay. When you meet them (and you will—they have a contractor's knack for showing up at precisely the moment you've forgotten to fear forgetting) they offer to teach you how to haunt yourself proactively, so that your ghost arrives at your deathbed already familiar with your regrets and can instead spend those precious final moments giving you travel recommendations for destinations you avoid by being alive.

The city employs grief gardeners who cultivate the exact species of sorrow required for specific losses that haven't occurred yet. They walk backward through crowds sowing seeds of appropriate devastation, ensuring that when your favorite constellation finally forgets its name, the exact variety of ache you need to mourn it will already be blooming in your ribcage. Some people hire them to plant preemptive consolations for unlikely tragedies—weeping sequoias that will only weep when you realize your childhood imaginary friend was actually real and died waiting for you to believe in them again.

Time itself took citizenship here after getting tired of being a tourist in everywhere else. Now it works nights as a bartender at the Möbius Tavern, serving drinks that are impossible without being unreal: shots of last Thursday mixed with next Monday's hangover, cocktails where the ice cubes remember being glaciers and will argue climate policy if you drink too slowly. Regulars include: your potential selves who took different turns yesterday while you weren't looking, the smell of autumn from alternate calendars, and a darkness that claims it's not the absence of light but light's patient ex who still has a key.

Everything here has its taxonomies crossed with elegies. Trees grow backwards into seeds that remember their own forest fires. Rivers execute themselves as waterfalls when they grow tired of being directions. Houses leave home and send postcards describing what they discovered while being nobody's address. Even the city's name is a verb conjugated into a noun against its will, currently undergoing therapy to remember whether it meant "to shelter" or "to be sheltered from."

The true citizens, the ones who were here before the city learned to remember having a before, live in the spaces between the city's dreams of itself. They speak in nested silences, pray by accident, and reproduce through misunderstandings that turn out to be prophecy years later. You've met several already without noticing—they were the borders you crossed without feeling them form countries beneath your feet, the punctuation in conversations that later grew into conversations about what you meant when you said nothing at exactly that moment.

And if you stay long enough, if you pay attention in the right currency (which is always a currency you've just spent but not yet earned), you might become one too. Not by choice, exactly. Not by change.

But by the city finally finding, in its endless self-conversation, the exact shape of your leaving room for something else to begin existing where you aren't quite anymore.

The door you're looking for isn't here. It grew into a window while we were talking about it. But that's alright—the windows here open into better stories than doors ever promised.

Just remember to forget your keys on the way out. Someone else will need your exactly-shaped absence to begin their entrance.
