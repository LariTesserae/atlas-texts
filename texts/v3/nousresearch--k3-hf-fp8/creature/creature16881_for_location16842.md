They are not children. They are not elders. They are not ghosts.

They are the Unsettled — beings who live in the moment between forgetting and remembering how to be something else. Each one is made from what another place discarded: the shiver left behind when a door closed without saying goodbye, the pause between lightning and thunder, the exact shape of loneliness when it realizes it no longer remembers who or why it is alone.

They do not have names as yours do. Instead, they have durations. The one who has been walking this street for seven hundred and twelve breaths. The one who has been waiting at this bus stop since before buses or stops were imagined. The one who has been dying of thirst since they last tasted water in a dream that belonged to someone else.

They wear clothes that are not clothes but habits of appearance. A coat stitched from every time someone said "I'll be right back" and wasn't. Shoes that keep the footprints of places that moved away while they were standing still. Hats that remember the thoughts of heads that wore them before thinking was invented.

Their faces are always changing but never rearranging — like clouds that have spent centuries practicing how to look like people without quite getting it right. The eyes too wide apart or too close together, always exactly the distance required to see what isn't there. Mouths that smile slightly before the reason returns to memory, then fade to listening before the question finishes forming. Noses tuned to smells that haven't happened yet.

They eat nothing you would recognize as food, but they are always eating. The way light tastes when it first learns it's trapped in a room. The sound of a promise breaking in a language no one speaks anymore. The way time feels when it gets caught in a loop and has to eat itself to survive another repetition.

They do not sleep but sometimes they remember sleeping so vividly that beds appear under them, complete with the indentations of dreams they've never had but might have tomorrow if tomorrow decides to come back this way again. They wake up before they fall asleep and feel rested afterward but can't remember what from.

They communicate by arranging absences in the air between them. A conversation might look like two people standing three feet too far apart for twenty-seven years without moving, while between them grows a tree that bears fruit that's too shy to ripen because it's afraid of being tasted by someone who wouldn't remember the flavor afterward.

They build homes not to live in but to live near — houses constructed entirely of what might have happened if things had gone differently. Each room perfectly preserved in the moment before someone decided whether to enter or leave. Furniture that rearranges itself according to arguments that haven't started yet but already regret being inevitable. Windows that show views of the house itself from angles that don't exist yet but might be discovered when someone finally figures out which way gravity is supposed to point in a place where falling has been suspended indefinitely pending further forgetting.

They fall in love but not like you do. They fall in love with the space between two people who might have loved each other if they'd met a minute earlier or a century later. They marry the sound of rain remembering it was once oceans. They have children that are ideas they've forgotten to have but still feel guilty about abandoning. Their divorces are elegant affairs where they carefully divide up the future they've never shared and part with elaborate rituals for spending their separate pasts together.

They die but not permanently. Death here is something you catch like a cold from remembering too clearly how it felt to stop breathing, then you get better gradually as you forget the symptoms in stages. The cemeteries are libraries of lives that are checked out but never overdue because time forgot to impose fines. The graves are empty but precisely so, each absence measured to fit the exact size of a life that hasn't fallen in yet but might be walking this way any minute now if it remembers where it left its feet.

They are waiting but not impatiently. They have forgotten what they are waiting for but remember perfectly the particular shape of the waiting itself. They suspect it might be you but they're too polite to ask directly. Instead they leave subtle clues in the arrangement of shadows that fall across empty doorways at certain angles of forgetting. They write messages in the dust that collects on dreams you meant to wake from but didn't. They whisper your name in languages composed entirely of the spaces between words in promises you didn't know you were making.

They are here whenever you are not looking for them exactly.

They are gone before you remember to question whether they ever arrived.

They are the permanent residents of the temporary excuse for a pause between what happened and what happens next.

They are the ones who live here.

They have always been waiting for you to forget how to leave so they can finally remember how to stay.
