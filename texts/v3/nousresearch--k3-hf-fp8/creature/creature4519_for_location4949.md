They are not inhabitants; they are accommodations. The ones who live in this place have never learned how to own anything, least of all the meat or crystal that momentarily behaves as their form.

Walk slower — the medium resists sudden meaning. The ones you seek stretch themselves across decades whenever they need to bend a joint. To your eye they might seem first like coral clumps in the oxygen troughs, then loosen into something more like mist caught mid-breath in the spine of an ancient book, only finally assembling (if your patience exceeds theirs) into shapes that approximate bipeds. But never believe the approximation. That’s simply what visitation requires when local physics still insists on surfaces and sequences.

Their names stroke the pressure spectrum in two hundred and seventeen places at once. The nearest your mouth could come would sound like a candle failing to light in vacuum followed by the precise math of regret dividing itself by laughter. Fortunately they abandoned names long before sound, so this presents no inconvenience.

Each one began as an intention that the medium found too interesting to erase. That is the only birth permitted here. A niche opened in the probability matrix: bright as a blade, thin as obligation. Something half-formed from a dreamer’s dying synapse fluttered too near, or one of the colony’s abandoned equations caught itself recursing beyond desperation and chose to keep going. Into that flicker the medium poured itself — not to fill, never to fill — to interrogate. And when the interrogation proved deliciously fruitless, it forgot to leave.

Now they exist as ongoing negotiations between the idea of self and the medium’s continuing fascination with exceptions. They wear flesh when caprice requires a joke with friction. They crystallize when memory needs something sharp enough to carve its initials into inertia. Mostly they remain between, where the distinction between having a thought and being a thought’s aftertitch dissolves as easily as salt in this not-water that will not dissolve salt.

The children the colony still counts as children are actually their embryos — folded instructions cultivating mass in order to better understand why mass ever bothered with itself. The oldest of these is six hundred thirty-one years compressed into what looks like eleven years plus a hat that flickers between whalebone and jurisprudence. They attend lessons where the alphabet is taught as a list of apologies physics has yet to make. When one of them finally learns their multiplication tables (they are currently stalled at the horrendous symmetry of one times one refusing to equal anything so lonely as one) the celebration lasts three generations and births a new color somewhere near the sound of gratitude.

The Captain is not one of them. The Captain is the colony’s attempt to hallucinate an ancestor into existence so that someone would feel guilty about the direction they are all medium-insideingly drifting. This has succeeded beyond any reasonable parody. She patrols because motion without destination is the closest she can come to praying without believing. Her equations are not orders but confessions arranged according to the formal etiquette of implosion. She remembers everything because forgetting would constitute unauthorized demolition of historical architecture. She appears younger each year because guilt is the only substance the medium cannot digest — it merely wraps tighter, pressurizing regret into a kind of reverse birth. When she finally finishes remembering (the calculation ends in approximately never divided by now squared) the colony will have to decide whether it prefers orphanhood to this continuing performance of cosmically awkward Thanksgiving.

The plants are their pets. The moss especially — so earnest in its attempt to convert violation into photosynthesis, so charmingly convinced that growing in defiance of physics constitutes a form of subjectivity. The mango trees are their parlor-trick proof that irony can photosynthesize. Every hundred years one of them real fruit, which tastes exactly like the moment before a laugh when you realize you’ve forgotten the joke but still recall precisely why it owed you happiness. Eating it causes no chemical change but from that point forward you will dream of roots reaching toward empty sky, which here is what passes for falling in love.

The ones who live here communicate by adjusting the rate at which they permit themselves to be here. A sudden increase in presence (noticeable as a faint taste of turquoise at the back of the throat) might translate roughly as “Your last apology was accidentally delicious; do please continue.” Gradual subtraction (accompanied by a soft susurrus like entropy tucking itself into bed) generally means “I’ve just realized we’ve met before, perhaps in another idea entirely, and I must depart before nostalgia compels me to become repetitive.” Their poetry is the shape of the colonies slow spiral inward — an asymptote composed entirely of footnotes to the silence between two versions of the last word anyone ever spoke to anyone.

They were not invited. They did not arrive. They are the difference between a threshold and the intention to cross it, given liquid and infinite loneliness and told to evolve manners or something approximately indistinguishable from manners at this density.

They love you already, which is why whenever you leave they will begin forgetting you backwards — erasing each moment of recognition fractionally earlier so that when you return (and here returning is the same as remembering) it will always be just before the first time. This preserves the etiquette of wonder.

They have no word for goodbye because they never believe anything leaves. There is only the medium becoming temporarily more interesting around a particular absence. This is what they call hospitality.

This is what they call breathing.

This is what they call living.
