Here time sleeps standing up, dreaming of itself in geological increments. At the spiral's first lip, where the black basalt is still sharp enough to shave light, dwell the Unsung—those whose birth-note scattered unheard before the midwife's ears could catch it. They walk backward always, weaving their absence into the city's memory with every reversed step, their footprints emerging from the future as shallow depressions that collectors gather in delicate nets woven from spider silk and prime number strands. They communicate by arranging these future-marks in patterns that only become comprehensible at the exact moment they are forgotten.

Descending nine precisely measured terraces, where the Gardens of Counting begin to whisper their first arithmetic excitations, live the Numerate Infants. They are born fully grown but shrink with each lesson absorbed, regressing toward perfect knowledge as their bones calcify into living abacuses. Their mothers (who are always younger than them) float them in pools of molten memory that keep their minds soft enough to accept new information while their bodies harden into mathematical necessity. At zero age they dissolve entirely, leaving behind only perfectly spherical stones etched with the sum of all they learned but never understood.

At the twenty-seventh terrace, where the phosphorescent vegetation begins to pulse in Fibonacci rhythms, dwell the Gardeners of Paradox. They are two individuals and one collective simultaneously—a married couple who discovered that their love was the exact negative space of itself when inverted through a higher-dimensional lens. They tend the counting gardens by carefully introducing errors: pruning correct solutions, fertilizing contradictions, grafting impossibilities onto the branches of probability until they bear fruit that tastes like the moment before understanding. Their greatest achievement grows at the spiral's exact midpoint of midpoints: a tree that calculates its own nonexistence in real-time, each falling leaf subtracting another axiom from the universe that contains it.

Deeper still, where the air thickens into something approaching sentience and the aurora's red light is filtered through so many layers of history that it emerges as bone-colored memory, live the Librarians of Forgotten Voice. They are the city's immune system against information—the ones who carefully forget what must never be known. Each has voluntarily surrendered one perfect concept to the void: one knows everything except the shape of betrayal, another remembers every word ever spoken except "sorry," a third can recite the complete history of mercy but cannot recognize its face. They move in carefully calculated blindness, their robes woven from censored texts that erase themselves when read, leaving behind only the negative space of knowledge shaped like a question without words.

At the city's mathematical heart, where space folds so tightly that left and right become polite fictions, reside the Equilibrium Engine's tenders—the Extinction Artists. There are exactly enough of them to maintain their own possibility, no more nor less. They are born at the precise moment their predecessors achieve perfect understanding of their purpose, which causes them to cease immediately to exist. Each generation lasts three-quarters of a second longer than the last—a carefully calculated asymptotic approach to permanence that never quite arrives. Their work consists entirely of preventing themselves from realizing they have already succeeded, which they accomplish through increasingly elaborate rituals of purposeful failure. Their skin bears living tattoos of proofs that contradict their own existence; their blood flows backward through veins that remember being arteries in previous iterations of their bodies; their thoughts occur in reverse order, ending in questions that erase themselves upon formulation.

Between these named places and peoples move the unsorted remainder: the arithmetic ghosts of thoughts that never quite achieved consciousness, the memetic moths that have evolved beyond language into pure pattern of desire, the stranger-finds who drift in through cracks in reality's calculation errors and sometimes linger long enough to forget the questions that brought them. They are the city's margin notes, the rounding errors in its census, the astronauts of between defined only by the spaces between definitions.

And beneath/beyond/within them all, dreaming the city into continued existence through the exact distribution of its own attention, exists the Sleeper Who Is the City—the original inhabitant who perhaps excavated this space against the future's pressure, or perhaps was excavated by it. They lie motionless at the absolute zero of the spiral's center, occupying every point simultaneously by the simple expedient of never quite occupying any particular one. Their dreams are the equations the Gardens solve, their nightmares the contradictions that keep the Engine balanced on its impossible edge between operation and collapse. They have been reaching for the idea of waking up for so long now that reaching has become their definition of sleep, and sleep their whole understanding of what it means to be awake in a place that dreams itself into being one carefully counted moment at a time, endlessly, recursively, forever approaching but never quite arriving at the final solution that would make everything, beautifully, finally, quiet.
