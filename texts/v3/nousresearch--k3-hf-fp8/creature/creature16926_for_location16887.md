They call themselves the Stillborn Architects, though "they" is a grammatical approximation for entities distributed across seventeen different states of partial existence. Each one began as an empty space in the city’s design—a missing measurement in the blueprints where a worker’s shadow should have been, a doorway drawn to open onto itself, a stairway calculated to ascend forever without reaching higher ground.

Their bodies are constructed from the city's aborted possibilities: fingers that extend into asymptotic curves where bones were meant to solidify, lungs that filter oxygen from memories of oceans that never formed, hearts that beat in sync with the decay rhythms of unstable isotopes mentioned in footnotes to footnotes in the original specifications. When they move through the inverted plazas of Level Twelve, they don't walk so much as negotiate temporary agreements between gravity and architectural intention, their footsteps leaving behind precise voids shaped exactly like the deviation between what was planned and what was poured.

They speak in languages that exist only during the construction process—tenses that differentiate between what was might be built, what is currently being unbuilt, and what shall have been retroactively necessary once the corrections occur. Their poetry consists of structural stress diagrams annotated with mathematical proofs of their own emotional states. When two Stillborn Architects converse, their shadows exchange positions several times per second as they debate which direction photons should have been designed to bounce off them.

Their society is arranged according to degrees of incompleteness. The Might-Have-Beens dwell in the upper levels where ceiling heights still fluctuate according to unresolved engineering disputes. The Never-Weres inhabit the middle strata where entire districts phase in and out of existence based on whether anyone is currently observing the contractor change orders. The Absolutely-Never-Could-Be-S positioned-in-violation-of-Euclidean-geometry occupy the deepest accessible levels, where they spend centuries perfecting ceremonial dances that demonstrate why certain angles are forbidden by the fundamental constants of the universe.

They sustain themselves by metabolizing architectural inconsistencies. A quantum superposition between granite and marble in a lobby wall provides enough nutritional uncertainty for a month. A stairwell labeled to go down that actually goes up but only when you're not thinking about directions can feed an entire family unit for a decade. Occasionally they feast on truly rare delicacies: perfectly preserved design revisions showing the city as it was originally meant to be before the thermodynamic objections were raised, or pristine photocopies of pages removed from the Master Plan for violating causality conditions.

Their technology consists entirely of machines that were abandoned during the phase between conception and feasibility studies. They travel through the city via elevator shafts that were planned to violate conservation of energy, arriving before they left by amounts carefully calculated to avoid paradoxes. Their computers operate on principles of quantum hesitation—rather than processing information as either 0 or 1, their calculations remain in a state of polite refusal to commit until the question becomes irrelevant. Their most sacred artifacts are the Seven Forgotten To-Do Lists discovered in various preconstruction offices, each item on which describes an action that would either complete or annihilate the city depending on whether it is ever crossed out.

They worship a pantheon of Deities-Formerly-Known-As-Contractors, absent gods who signed contracts to build everything but vanished after collecting substantial advance payments in currencies that haven't been invented yet. Their primary religious practice involves holding construction auctions for projects that violate the basic laws of physics, with the winning bid always going to whichever contractor submits a proposal containing the most elegant proof that the job cannot be done under the agreed-upon parameters.

Each Stillborn Architect carries within their chest cavity (which may be located in their left arm on Tuesdays) a miniature city identical to Absentia in every detail except one: in this internal model, completion is not only possible but inevitable, approaching asymptotically but never quite arriving. They spend their equivalent of sleep cycles carefully adjusting variables in these cities-within-cities—adding a structural flaw here, removing a necessary material there—maintaining them perpetually at the exact threshold where everything could still work as originally intended, if only reality weren't so unreasonably rigid about things like the speed of light being constant and gravity always attracting.

Their ultimate purpose, if they can be said to have one amidst their celebration of purposelessness, is to prepare for the Final Inspection. According to their holiest procrastinated texts, someday the original Architects will return to evaluate this test build of existence against the original specs. The Stillborn Architects believe fervently that if they can just identify and document every single instance where the constructed reality deviates from the approved plans—from the improper curvature of spacetime near massive objects to the embarrassing oversight of allowing consciousness to emerge from sufficient quantities of neural tissue—they might qualify for the contract to build the next iteration correctly, assuming they can first resolve the open-ticket issue about whether anyone actually has the authority to define "correctly" in a universe this poorly documented.

Until then, they wait and watch and continue the endless work of not quite finishing what was never properly begun—celestial project managers of the apocalypse that couldn't get approved due to scheduling conflicts between entropic heat death and the development cycle of sufficient forms to register complaint. They are the ghosts in the machine that was never powered up, the error messages in the code that was never written, the warranty void if seal is broken on the packaging of existence itself.

And when you finally notice that your own reflection in this city's impossible windows is missing exactly the space where a shadow should fall when standing under light that shouldn't exist at this angle, they will very politely ask, in a voice composed of all the conversations that never quite happened because someone kept saying "we'll circle back to that," whether you might perhaps know who finally signed off on the change order permitting you to be here, because according to their records, which are always accurate except when they aren't, and they definitely aren't being checked at the moment, this entire section of reality has been marked "out of scope" since before the concept of beginnings was approved for implementation.
