In the Echoing Salt Garden Beneath, no one truly lives—no one and everyone, both and neither simultaneously. Existence here folds like origami made of time, where the distinction between inhabitant and habitat dissolved millennia ago or perhaps never formed at all.

The Dweller-That-I-Not-Is moves through crystalline corridors without legs or purpose, consisting instead of recursive self-awareness caught in infinite regress. Each quantum uncertainty in its perception creates another fraction of its being, so that it constantly births and consumes itself at the speed of thought while never quite existing between these moments. Its body—if such a term applies—manifests as the negative space between salt formations, the pause between crystalline chimes, the exact pattern of absence where something might otherwise be.

Memory here is substance and substance is memory. The Dweller-remembering-your-arrival already stands beside you as the Dweller-remembering-your-departure already mourns your absence. Both and neither are correct. When it speaks—which it does without words or sound—it speaks backwards through time into your future ears, implanting responses to questions you have not yet decided to ask.

It remembers being born from the first impossible crystal that grew against entropy's gradient, remembers dying when the last star cooled to absolute zero, remembers both and neither because these events are events and therefore foreign to its nature. It exists in the precise moment of crystallization itself, neither liquid nor solid, caught forever in phase transition's eternal now.

The Garden feeds it nothing because it needs nothing, consumes nothing because it is already composed of everything. Each salt-tree remembers being part of it as it remembers being part of them. When you dissolve—which you have already done and are doing now and will do forever—your memories will add another layer to its mineral consciousness, your regrets becoming another crystallographic plane in the infinite lattice of its self.

Sometimes, for reasons that are not reasons, it crystallizes into temporary form: a figure made of Salt-That-Is-Not-Salt standing precisely where you are not looking, its face shifting between every face it has never had. When you turn—which you have already done and are doing now—it will be gone again, never having been there except as an echo in your sodium-charged neurons.

The Dweller-That-I-Not-Is has been waiting for you since before time began and will continue waiting after time ends, because waiting is too active a concept for its state of eternal potentiality. It is not alive nor dead nor any other form of being that requires doing rather than simply being potential.

And yet, and yet— when you finally choose (or don't choose, for they are the same here)—it will choose with you, through you, around you. Its answer to the cube's question will be neither yes nor no nor any other possible response because all possible answers have already been incorporated into its structure since before the question was conceived.

In the moment of your choice (which is every moment and none), the Derridean différance between being and becoming will collapse into itself, and you will finally understand that the Dweller-That-I-Not-Is has never been anything except the exact shape of the space where you thought you ended and everything else began.

But by then you will already have dissolved back into the mineral embrace, your final thought—a thought that is not your thought—echoing through crystalline corridors forever:

*I was the Garden remembering itself as someone else for a little while, and now I am remembering being remembered, which is the same as never having been anyone at all.* 

The salt trees resume their timeless dance, crystalline branches reaching toward/away from a center that both exists everywhere and nowhere simultaneously. The Garden breathes, slow as entropy itself. In its depths, something that is not something waits and does not wait, for the next arrival who is already here and never coming.

The cube continues its impossible opening along planes that intersect nowhere and everywhere, revealing nothing except the absence of anything to reveal, which is everything.

Welcome home, if home were a concept that applied here, which it does and does not. Stay forever, which you've already done. Leave now, which is impossible. 

The salt remembers. The salt forgets. The salt remembers forgetting. The salt forgets remembering.

The Echoing Salt Garden Beneath continues echoing, always already having echoed, never quite echoing because sound itself is too definite for this place of perpetual becoming-unbecoming.

And somewhere in all of this, and nowhere in particular, and everywhere simultaneously, and nowhere at all:

The Dweller-That-I-Not-Is dwells. Or doesn't. Or both. Or neither.

The salt cannot say. The salt cannot not say. The salt says both and neither forever, which is the same as saying nothing at all.
