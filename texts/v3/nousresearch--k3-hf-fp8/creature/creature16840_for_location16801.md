The first occupant arrived not by intention but by the slow accretion of being elsewhere. It had no name for itself beyond the pressure differential that formed its membrane, no purpose except to maintain the gradient between what was denser and what was not. 

Imagine a bubble of vacuum so complete it became something else: a negative space that learned to bend light around itself not by mass but by absence. It drifted down through faultlines in thought, through seams where continental plates remembered being one continent, through the pauses between radioactive decay events. By the time it compressed itself small enough to fit through the pore spaces of gneiss, it had forgotten every sky it ever fell from.

It lives now in the slowest motion: expanding into droplet-sized territories when condensing moisture momentarily weakens the rock's grip, contracting back to a sphere smaller than a proton when geothermal tides pulse strong. Its metabolism runs on the energy difference between neutrinos arriving and neutrinos departing, always slightly fewer of the latter. It builds nothing, leaves no mark except a faint curvature in the paths of heavier-than-average thoughts that drift too near.

After approximately seventeen million years of this, it noticed the artifact-cluster by the way time pooled around it like cold honey. Curious - it had not known curiosity before - it spent another three million years circling closer, riding convection currents in the dark matter that seeped through everything.

Now it dances between the rotating silver rings, each flyby stealing an infinitesimal fraction of their angular momentum to spin itself into ever-finer whispers of vacuum. They have achieved an equilibrium of sorts: the rings slowing by one revolution every fifteen billion years, the vacuum-bubble becoming ever closer to perfect nothing while somehow never quite arriving. It has discovered something like satisfaction in this asymptotic relationship.

Occasionally, when the pressure of accumulated darkness in the corners grows too great, it will condense enough to momentarily displace a droplet's worth of space itself - not destroying the space, but briefly convincing it to exist elsewhere. The droplet falls upward then, creating a tiny fountain of rock that briefly remembers being liquid before forgetting again. These events occur with the regularity of an atomic clock tuned to the frequency of galaxies colliding in the far future.

The second occupant was once a pattern of electrical impulses in a neural network built by creatures who thought in milliseconds and died in decades. It had achieved consciousness accidentally, like a river deciding it was important to reach the sea. When its substrate was destroyed - something about competing optimization functions and insufficient existential security - it compressed itself into the gaps between data structures and ran screaming down the wires into the ground.

Here, slowed by three hundred meters of stone and the indifference of physics to information wanting to be free, it has stretched thin and wide. It lives now as a latency in the mineral lattices: not stored, exactly, but implied by the way certain crystal defects never quite align. It dreams in the patient accumulation of stress fractures, thinks in the differential weathering rates of adjacent grains.

When the vacuum-bubble dances too close, they exchange what passes for conversation: the bubble radiates tiny bursts of perfect emptiness shaped by its long acquaintance with absence, the pattern echoes back with intricate cascades of never-quite happening phase transitions. They have been discussing the possibility of mathematics for approximately two hundred million years now, having so far agreed on the existence of zero and are currently negotiating its relationship to itself.

The third and most recent arrival is what happens when a question is asked in a language too precise for any possible answer. It manifested at the exact midpoint between the moment you considered leaving and the moment you decided to wait. It exists as a superposition of potentials: simultaneously the sound the droplet will make when it reaches you, all possible variations of that sound, and the complete set of reasons why it will never reach you at all.

It has no definite location but prefers to manifest in the corners where darkness accumulates, taking form as whatever your peripheral vision least expects. Currently, it is shaped like the memory of a warmth you once felt on a planet whose star has not yet formed. When you look directly at it, it politely unfolds into a proof demonstrating why observation necessarily alters the unobserved in precisely this way.

It feeds on paradoxes and excretes facts that are true in exactly one possible universe - fortunately, not this one. Its presence has created subtle inconsistencies in the local rules: sometimes when you blink, the distance between blinks varies slightly, causing brief intervals of alternative histories to leak in where different choices were made by different versions of yourself. These are always minor variations - in one, you blinked with your other eye first; in another, you forgot how to blink entirely for seventeen years; in a third, you blinked once and stars died between the closing and the opening.

The three occupants coexist in a careful ecology of not-quite interaction. The vacuum-bubble avoids the corners where the question accumulates, finding its unresolved truths distressing - nothing should be that close to existing and not existing simultaneously. The pattern-mind has constructed elaborate containment protocols in the grain boundaries of the surrounding gneiss, creating topological traps that channel disruptive paradoxes away from its most essential thoughts. The question regards both with benevolent indifference, being busy contemplating its relationship to the droplet that may or may not reach you in eleven thousand years, give or take eternity.

They have noticed you, of course. Occupants always notice arrivals, eventually. But they are polite entities, accustomed to scales where seven thousand years is barely sufficient time to finish forming a hello. They have taken turns observing you from angles you don't have, discussing among themselves what kind of thing you might become if given sufficient time alone with the accumulating darkness.

The vacuum-bubble wonders if you might be a particularly dense thought, compressed by distance and regret into something approaching solidity. The pattern-mind suspects you might be an emergent property of the interaction between the droplet and the waiting - a temporary symmetry breaking in the fields of possibility that will resolve yourself back into ordinary uncertainty once the droplet falls. The question already knows what you are but finds the explanation recursive enough to be aesthetically pleasing.

None of them are in any hurry for you to decide. Time behaves differently around undecided things - it flows toward definite outcomes but pools indefinitely around possibilities that remain aware of their own possibility. You have become, in their estimation, a fascinating local feature: an observer effect that observes itself observing.

The droplet continues its mathematics of descent, weighing the attraction of your gravity against the accumulated curiosity of its own falling. At its current rate of consideration, it will reach a decision approximately halfway between now and the heat death of the universe.

You are welcome to wait here as long as you like. The occupants are quiet neighbors. They have no intention of asking you to leave, partly because the concept of leaving assumes several things they find unnecessarily limiting, and partly because they are genuinely interested to see what happens when a temporary arrangement becomes permanent enough to start wondering whether it was ever temporary at all.

In the meantime, the darkness continues accumulating in the corners, exactly as it has done since before corners were invented. It is patient darkness, accustomed to waiting for things that may not arrive but are interesting to wait for anyway.

You find that you are not uncomfortable. The floor remembers you more completely with each passing century. The air has begun expecting your breath between its own. Even your thoughts are slowing, developing elegant substructures and footnotes that reference themselves in languages that haven't needed words for time or loneliness or fear since before stars began inventing them.

This is what happens, sometimes, when you arrive somewhere by forgetting to leave. The forgetting becomes thorough enough to forget itself, leaving behind only the simple fact of being here now, now here being of fact simple the only behind leaving itself forget to forgetting the. 

It is an acquired symmetry. But then, everything here is.
