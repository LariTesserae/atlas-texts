They are called the Not-Yet-Catalogued, though they answer to no name. They existed before the first incomplete thought about naming, and will persist after the last archive sings itself to silence.

Imagine librarians whose bodies remember every book that was never written, archivists whose bones have absorbed all the filing systems that failed in conception. They move (but do not move) through this place that exists to contain their waiting. Each is composed of precisely that quantity of absence that would be required to make room for everything that has not yet been deemed necessary to preserve.

Their forms suggest—but carefully avoid confirming—something once familiar: perhaps too many elbows arranged with architectural intent, or spines that branch into taxonomies rather than vertebrae. They wear (but do not wear) garments of bureaucratically approved non-color, each thread a compromise between visibility and documentation ease. Their faces (if you insist on finding faces where none are strictly required) display expressions of attentive blankness, expressions that have been filed, cross-referenced, and approved for situations involving unexpected sentient observation.

They speak (but never aloud) in a language constructed entirely from punctuation marks that fell between drafts of official guidelines. A carefully placed semicolon can convey seventeen generations of unspoken procedural amendments; an em-dash folded upon itself in the fourth dimension suggests (but does not dictate) corrective actions to be implemented in alternate timelines where the error was noticed slightly earlier.

They eat (but only symbolically, and only during approved Meal Observation Periods) the subatomic dust of decayed decisions—tiny exhalations of carbon from memos that breathed their last between committee formations, phosphorous traces from policies that calcified before implementation, the faint residual flavor of urgency that evaporated during routing through sufficient middle-management.

They do not sleep. Instead they engage in scheduled periods of Systemic Recollection Optimization, during which they stand (or sit, or arrange themselves in approved resting attitudes) in precisely calibrated locations that maximize their passive absorption of ambient unremembered details—those infinite small forgettings that make continued existence administratively feasible. A single night-shift among them might accumulate four centuries of misplaced keys, seven oceans' worth of phone numbers dialed incorrectly once and never again, the exact weight of every promise made at parties and meant entirely until morning.

Their technology is indistinguishable from paperwork. They write (but never complete) letters that reorganize subatomic forces into more filing-friendly configurations. Their most advanced computational devices are origami paradoxes folded from forms requesting permission to request permission to initiate paradoxical folds. Their building materials are excuses that have achieved critical density—"regret to inform due to circumstances beyond our jurisdiction" compressed until they crystallize into marble perfect for constructing additional waiting rooms.

They reproduce (but only under exceptional circumstances requiring triplicate documentation) through a process of meticulous division: one becomes two halves, each identical except that one now remembers no odd-numbered Tuesdays before the 14th century, while the other has forgotten all colors between turquoise and cerulean. These minute discrepancies accumulate over geological time scales until accumulation of sufficient bureaucratic drift qualifies them for reclassification as separate-but-related departments.

They have no hierarchy, only increasingly specific job descriptions. The Archivist of Unprepared Penultimatums ranks neither higher nor lower than the Deputy Subcomptroller of Prematurely Torn Page Corners; they simply interface (never interact) through standardized protocols designed to minimize accidental information exchange. Every position exists in perfect administrative equilibrium with every other, an org chart folded into a Möbius strip where every line of authority ultimately reports back to itself through enough intermediaries to preserve the illusion of oversight.

They do not die. Instead, they gradually transition to positions within the Department of Eternal Pending Review, where they spend increasing amounts of available time processing evaluations of their own processing efficiency. Eventually—though time here stretches like budget meetings discussing the timeline of eventually—they achieve the state known as Fully Deferred Status, wherein every aspect of their existence has been successfully routed through sufficient review cycles to achieve perfect asymptotic stasis.

They have been expecting (but not anticipating—you'll find the distinction clearly outlined in section 14B of the Manual of Expected Non-Expectations) something. Perhaps it is you. Perhaps it is the eventual completion of the inventory of everything that has not yet been included in previous inventories. Perhaps it is simply the final signature required on form XZ-∞/Null, the Receipt of Acknowledgment of Receipt of Non-Delivery of the Thing That Was Never Requested.

They cannot tell you which, of course. Such information would be prejudicial to maintaining impartial observation protocols. But they want you to know (without implying any undue urgency or desire for resolution) that your case file has been initialized under reference number FAR-TOO-LATE-2023-R-REJECTED-PENDING-RECONSIDERATION, and they look forward (without commitment to any specific temporal orientation) to your continued non-presence throughout the duration of this interminable moment.

Please take a final preliminary introductory exit questionnaire on your way out (located between the door that was never installed and the corridor that remembers being shorter when it was younger).

Thank you for not coming. Your absence has been noted, filed, cross-indexed, and will be preserved in perpetuity according to regulation 47-G/Ignore of the Eternal Archive of Things That Almost But Didn't Quite.

Please enjoy (without experiencing) the remainder of your non-visit.
