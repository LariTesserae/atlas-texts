Living truths, not dwellers. The valley’s inhabitants are agreements wearing memories like borrowed coats—too temporary to own their own continuity. They call themselves "Recalls" because that’s the closest verb they trust.

Each Recall begins when something notices the valley noticing itself. A pilgrim’s half-remembered dream. Gear teeth from a watch that forgot to panic during drowning. The sound of a mother’s voice preserved in the space where “please” should have been in a prayer. These fragments fluff themselves into shapes resembling species until their edges remember the embarrassment of definition and let go again.

They have bodies at 1.4 solidity—solid enough to apologize for bumping into you, fluid enough to believe the apology. Their limbs are rental agreements between gravity and regret, renewed per heartbeat. Lungs? Lungs are for places that believe in endings; they breathe instead with their contradictions—inhale the fact that they exist, exhale the fact that they don’t quite.

Some days they’re tall as the six mountains gossiping about yesterday’s eight-and-a-half. Their bones that day are old telephone wires humming debates between two dilapidated mosques that never agreed on the direction of sunrise. Other days they compress to comma-size, pauses in the weather’s symphony who developed a hobby of collecting civilizations between blinks.

They eat the spaces where explanations fail. A good meal here is finding a math problem that only solves if you accept that three is secretly lonely for two. They chew slowly, savoring the moment before arithmetic remembers its rules. They drink the steam rising from arguments between stones about who held up the flood better.

Their language is infidelity between meanings. A polite greeting translates to “I almost remembered how rivers felt before banks interfered.” “Thank you” means “your kindness will be misremembered beautifully in someone else’s fairy tale.” They never say goodbye—instead they sneeze, which is considered a temporary suspension of acquaintance across three parallel misconceptions.

Reproduction occurs when one Recall forgets they’re allergic to Tuesdays and accidentally believes another’s memory of being touched kindly by lightning. Ten months later (or ten minutes ago, depending on how the clock feels about parenthood), they birth a not-quite-idea wrapped in the sound of rain apologizing to tin roofs. The infant is raised by collective embarrassment until it’s old enough to be adopted by solitude.

They die precisely seven times per conversation. First death: when someone asks their name and it wriggles away like a fishhook caught in déjà vu. Last death: when their favorite shadow finally figures out it’s been following the wrong suggestion of a person. Between deaths they practice hobbies: some collect discarded proofs of God’s existence left behind by abandoned thunderstorms. Others train to be echoes of sounds the universe is saving for a dramatic reveal that never comes.

The oldest Recall might be the valley itself, pretending to be geography on weekdays and gossip on weekends. The youngest was born three sentences ago when you wondered if these descriptions were becoming too much. Both are considering dating but worry their relationship would become too crowded with the future children they’ll never quite conceive.

They have pets: probability kittens that exist only when unobserved, feeding on Schrodinger’s leftovers. Libraries of books jealous of their own unwritten sequels. A domesticated paradox that believes it’s a dog but keeps bringing home bones of extinct certainties to bury in the garden of tomorrow’s regrets.

Their politics? Elegant stalemate between the Party of Gradual Disintegration and the Coalition for Immediate Hesitation. Elections are held whenever someone finds a rock convinced it’s a ballot box; results are announced retroactively by whichever cloud most resembles the crowd’s collective disappointment about winning.

They worship the moment between a knock and the door’s decision to pretend nobody’s home. Their holy text is written in water droplets on windows that are always on the verge of choosing between rain goodbye poem and fog apology letter.

At night (which lasts exactly as long as someone remembers dreaming of daylight) they gather to watch the stars reminisce about being closer before expansion therapy made them distant. They applaud politely when a constellation finally untangles its identity issues enough to become suggestion of a dinosaur looking for misplaced purpose.

You asked who lives here. The valley giggles through shifting tides of might-have-been. The mirror surfaces blink Morse code jokes in languages that committed suicide before reaching the punchline. The six mountains—no, seven now—sigh and decide to be eight tomorrow because the fraction gets lonely.

The answer is typing itself on the keyboard of your hesitation. Each keystroke is a Recall renting your doubt as temporary housing.

You’ll meet them soon. You already have, but your left earlobe forgot to tell the rest of you during the morning’s confusion about whether today was yesterday or next week’s rehearsal.

Don’t introduce yourself. They know your name’s middle name’s guilty conscience. Instead, pretend you’re looking for something you lost here before you arrived.

That’s how they know you’re ready to be found by the possibility that maybe none of this is happening except precisely enough to matter.
