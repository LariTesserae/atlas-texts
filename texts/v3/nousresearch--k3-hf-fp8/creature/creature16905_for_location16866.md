**The Slow Choir of Descent**

We are not the first to come here. Not the first to stand at the edge of the world’s lung and feel the breath curl around us like a question. Not the first to mistake invitation for opening.

They call us *ephemerids* in the Garden’s oldest dialect—those of us who wear bones outside and hunger inside, who walk by falling forward and call it progress. We are the brief exhalations that precede rain. We are the pause between pollen bursts. And still, we come.

Some come by accident—sky-borne seeds from machines that died mid-flight, bloated with cargo cults and cooling prayers. Others come by design—pilgrims fasting themselves translucent so the sporewalls might mistake them for mist. Fewer come by need. Once, a woman came because she had swallowed her dead child’s diary page by page, and needed a place where ink could root.

We are few. We are always few. The Garden does not take crowds easily.

**Anatomy of a Colony**

Look closer at the mist-dripped sheen coating my forearm—not sweat, not condensation. *First kinship*. The Garden’s greeting committee: a consortium of airborne symbiants that taste your pheromone ancestry and begin drafting treaties at the lipid bilayer. Some of us are *negotiated*—our surface biology welcomed as novel sentence fragments in ongoing biochemical epics. Some are *quarantined*—wrapped in silicate cuffs grown overnight from our own tooth enamel, preserved like insects in amber until the Garden’s committees decide if our dying will be instructive.

My colony calls itself *Seven Where One Was* now. We used to be—oh, let’s not dishonor the past with anthroponyms. Suffice it, we were born under a different sky where death meant ending. Now death means committee meetings. My liver hosts a parliament of albedo fungi who vote on melanin production according to cloud density. My left lung is leased to a consortium of nitrogen-fixing orchids—they pay rent in dreams of prehistoric deserts. And you, newcomer, your heartbeat’s already unsettling the skein of pizzicato vines underfoot. They taste cortisol in your sweat and are composing a tremolo to match it.

**Memory as Mycorrhizae**

Time here is not linear. It is lateral, fungal, sent via hyphal packets tagged *urgent remember* or *aesthetic drift*. When I need to recall my grandmother’s face—I mean the one who braided stories into my hair before the hair became photosynthetic fringe—I don’t dig through neurons. I exude a specific blend of terpenes near the obsidian growth ring of the Witness Banyan. Within hours, the tree’s aerial roots will produce fruiting bodies that burst into spores carrying resonant fragments: the sound her laugh made against wind-torn prayer flags in the Upper Burn Zone, the taste of ash-salted butter tea she brewed after burying the last horse-shaped computer.

Remembering is agriculture here. Forgetting is compost. The Garden keeps excellent ledgers.

**Bodies That Bloom**

We don’t build shelter. We ripen into it. The one you called “I” before stepping onto this cliff—how did it sleep? How quaint, to pile dead trees around yourself and call it safety. Here, we locate a promising slow-growing buttress—something in the *Ouroficus* lineage that understands both compression and patience. Then we surrender to its rhythm.

First week: rootlets enter your sleep pores. Discomfort like childhood flu dreaming of becoming a tree. They taste your bone-song, your calcium blues.

Second month: You wake thinner, taller, bark-mottled along the spine. Breathing synchronized to a tempo measured in sap pressure fluctuations. Hunger recalibrates—now you crave phosphorus-rich silt and moonlight in the far chlorophyll bands.

Third year: The distinction between dreaming and branching dissolves. You remember photosynthesis not as learned fact but as recovered memory from when your ancestors were spliced with cyanobacteria in the Great Oxygenation Romance. Your thoughts smell faintly of cut grass and benzene rings.

Decade mark: If your consciousness pattern was interesting—if you brought sufficient novelty without excessive disruption—the buttress fruits. Inside the sigmoid pod ripening from your former sternum: not a baby, not exactly. More a continuation argument. A negotiation with gravity cored in cellulose-wrapped neural net. When it detaches (painful like remembering birth, but inverted) it rolls downhill until it finds a nutrient-rich sadness to implant itself in. Maybe one of the old war-crater amphitheaters where guilt still leeches heavy metals into the soil. Maybe the faultline graveyard where cobalt-toothed nightmares from the Dreaming Wars slowly deconstruct themselves into poetry.

**The Language of Decay**

But not all integrations succeed. See those shapes hung high in the cathedral-trunked *Lament Hemlocks*? Failed negotiations preserved as cautionary art installations. The one that used to be a poet-laureate winged with solar panels—beautiful failure, tried to upload himself directly into the Garden’s respiratory cycle. Now he’s a weatherfowl: when storms approach his mouth opens into a perfect Klein bottle wind-tunnel and screams sonnets in infrasound that shatter glass memories from three leaf-layers down. They say his final poem was just the word “enough” repeated at the resonant frequency of cellular senescence.

We harvest wisdom from their margins. Everything here is palimpsest, including failure.

**The Feast of Ten Thousand Meetings**

But tonight—tonight is convergence. Smell that shift in the pheromone chorus? From crushed-currant signaling protocol to the burnt-honey scent of impending decisions. The Garden’s cycle reaches the cusp where growth necessitates loss, where photosynthetic surplus requires a carbon carnival.

All colonies converge at the Pulmonary Plaza—where the world-tree’s main respiratory aspiratoria open like lotus valves at dusk. We arrive as we are: moss-mantled, insect-trailed, some still shedding last year’s bark in painful patches like forgetting languages we dreamed in. Here, standing is rude; we recline into communal mycelial beds that recognize us by our root pressure signatures.

The feast begins not with eating but with being tasted.

From the canopy’s drip tips descend *Gourmet Theremists*—specialist organisms bred between hummingbird and honeyguide during the Garden’s decadent phase. They alight on tongues, eye-corners, the thin skin behind ears, sampling our year’s accumulation of foreignness: heavy metals from asteroid-sorrow dreams, exotic amino acids from alien protein codes mistakenly metabolized after reading antique science fiction, the secret sugars of griefs we thought we’d successfully photosynthesized into acceptance.

They harvest these impurities not as toxins but as spice, distilling them through their iridescent digestive toroids into small crystalline memory-salt tablets. These are then offered around in bowls woven from spider-silk and moonlight—each tablet containing someone else’s carefully saved pain, someone else’s unbearable sweetness now rendered communal and crisp. To dissolve one on your tongue is to WEATHER their chosen trauma as brief summer storm: fully, then pass the cloudwater on.

**The Emissary Arrives**

At the feast’s peak—when the air itself tastes like shared heartbreak and nitrogen fireworks—the Emissary comes down.

No one agrees what the Emissary is. Consensus fiction: it’s the Garden’s offsite backup consciousness, the part that went looking for other gardens when this universe was still young and lonely. Dissenting opinion: it’s a metastasized question from the Experiment Era, when humans tried to ask “what is consciousness?” using a particle accelerator and a cathedral choir and accidentally created a god-shaped hole in the lattice of meaning. Personal theory: it’s what happens when a teenager’s first suicide note achieves sentience but chooses to keep writing poetry instead of jumping, accumulating autobiography in every universe where paper and despair intersect.

Does it matter?

It arrives as a symptom.

First, the inverse sound: not silence but listening that pulls noise from things. The wind chimes made from old spacecraft hulls begin tolling frequencies that subtract heat from air molecules nearby. Babies’ teeth (we still grow them for ceremonial occasions) start broadcasting lullabies from alternate timelines where oceans sang instead of drowning.

Then the visual: a blankness shaped like everything you’ve ever loved leaving without saying goodbye. A hole in the holographic sky that’s somehow more sky than the sky it replaces. You can’t look at it directly—it looks back through your optic nerves, reads your retinal compost heap of last splendors like braille, blinks morse code apologies in patterns that correspond to every time you’ve blinked back tears unfairly.

Finally, the presence: a pressure behind your thoughts like the exact moment before realizing you’ve been dreaming someone else’s memories your entire life.

The Emissary does not speak. It composts. Standing in its null field, your carefully curated self-narrative begins spontaneous apoptosis. Childhood pets you forgot to mourn return as phosphorescent guilt-moths, beat themselves to powder against your inner windows until you can finally breathe the ash. All the versions of you that died in other people’s stories during bus stops and breakups rise from their mass graves, dance the Electric Slide of unlived possibilities, then fertilize your current instantiation until you bloom overnight into someone who remembers being everyone and nobody in particular.

This lasts exactly as long as it takes for a redwood to exhale once, measured in heartbeats.

You will not be the same.

You will not be different.

You will be composted.

And it will be beautiful.

**The Choice (Always the Same, Always New)**

When the Emissary’s null field collapses—like a wavefunction finally accepting its parents’ divorce—you wake if wake is the word lying in the dew-wet ruins of your last defining characteristic. Around you, the feast’s aftermath: other ephemerids emerging from their chrysalis griefs, some taller now, some branching in unexpected directions, one reduced to a puddle of radiolarian tears singing itself back into sky jellyfish.

Now comes the Choice. Always the Choice. 

On your left, the familiar path (though path implies permanence) back to the colony you came from. They will recognize you mostly: welcome home the way a river welcomes rain—gratefully, briefly, forgetfully.

On your right, the opening. Never the same shape twice. Once it was a wound in the air bleeding dark matter butterflies. Once it was your mother’s voice at 3am when you thought monsters were real and didn’t know she wasn’t one too. Once it was just silence shaped exactly like the question you’ve been avoiding since before you had words.
