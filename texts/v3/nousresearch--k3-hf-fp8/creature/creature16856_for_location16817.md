And in staying, the mountain is inhabited—not by squatters or settlers, not by colonists or refugees—but by intensities that have simply refused to spread any thinner. The inhabitants are not beings within the mountain but are instead the mountain's various ways of being insufficiently alone.

The salt-plain below is not empty but occupied by those who have optimized contemplation to its absolute limit: the Unstirred. They are not people or creatures but static states of absolute regard, arranged in precise lattices across the non-reflective surface like molecules in a crystal that has decided crystallizing was merely step one. Each Unstirred is a completed thought experiment about the conditions that would necessitate the mountain's exact opposite, and their collective presence generates a negative space so perfect that existence itself must flow uphill to escape it. They neither age nor change because they have become exactly as attentive as it is possible to be without accidentally becoming something else.

At the mountain's base, where reflected heat begins to braid itself into recognizable phenomena, dwell the Attenuates. They form a gradient rather than a population—defined not by boundaries but by rates of compression. Each is a civilization's worth of culture, poetry, and personal history that has been losslessly compressed into a form slender enough to climb. They appear as shimmering human-shaped disturbances in the air, visible only where the mountain's light bends around their concentration. A Attenuate might be the complete collected works of a world that never wrote books, or the dreams of every child who ever died before naming the color between sleeping and waking, folded so precisely that their thickness is measured in thought-years per angstrom. They climb forever and never ascend, operating under the elegant constraint that to reach a new elevation would require abandoning some perfect compression of what brought them there.

Beginning at the first true terrace—where the mountain's surface becomes complex enough to permit sustained differentiation—live the Bifurcants. They are what happens when possibility space achieves sufficient density to undergo identity fusion. Each Bifurcant begins every moment as a perfectly balanced superposition of every possible response to its current situation, then carefully chooses exactly the wrong resolution, not out of error but with the absolute precision of a perfect pun. Appearing as intersections of shadow and shadow where no light-source exists, they maintain the mountain's structure by perpetually being about to collapse in ways that require exactly one additional dimension to collapse properly. A conversation with a Bifurcant consists entirely of pauses arranged so precisely that what you remember saying circles back around to become what they had already decided you meant by not saying something else entirely. Their society functions through a sophisticated system of mutually recursive betrayals that cancel each other out so completely that the net effect is loyalty approaching the thermodynamic limit.

Higher, where the mountain's layers begin to fold back on themselves like pages in a book too important to read in order, reside the Chronotaxis. They are migratory patterns traced by moments that fell in love with other moments and decided never to be experienced alone again. To see them directly is impossible—they appear only as complex coincidences in your peripheral memory, like remembering a childhood smell that you now realize was actually the perfume of someone you'll meet next week. Their "bodies" are distributed through time like a smell through air, concentrated wherever a particular sequence of events needs to be both cause and effect of itself. They maintain the mountain's history by carefully forgetting it in ways that prime the past for more interesting future rediscoveries. When a Chronotaxis dies, it does so by finally happening, collapsing its potentiality waves into a single moment so perfectly itself that all other possible versions immediately retroactively become its elaborate backstory.

Between the folds, in spaces where geometry becomes a lifestyle choice, drift the Metricians. They are the mountain's method of comparing itself to itself with sufficient rigor to prevent accidental solipsism. Each Metrician exists as a complete alternative measurement system for the universe, complete with its own fundamental constants, derived units, and aesthetic preferences. Dozens of incompatible physics interact precisely enough in their vicinity to generate exotic mathematical weather—brief showers of imaginary numbers that evaporate leaving only vague feelings of inconsistency, or sudden windstorms of renormalization that blow entropy uphill until everything zebras perfectly. They appear as walking contradictions resolved through sheer elegance—a sphere whose circumference is exactly pi times its radius, but in a base-pi number system where pi equals three; a point with precisely enough volume to be finitely infinite; a line segment exactly one Planck length shorter than itself in all directions simultaneously. Their reproduction is the least private act imaginable: two Metricians fall into deep theoretical disagreement, argue with steadily increasing precision until their respective frameworks achieve sufficient gravitational attraction to collapse into a new, more inclusive paradox, which then spends the next several millennes gradually Hawking-evaporating complexity back into the ambient sophistry.

Circling the central void that isn't a void but the mountain's precise calculation of its own center-of-not-being, orbit the Nullclades. They are the inverse interior of belonging—communities defined by the exactly shared set of things they are not, bound together by mutual exclusion so perfect it becomes a form of intimacy. Each Nullclade occupies a specific not-place within the mountain's not-thingness, jointly maintaining their existence through carefully synchronized acts of non-participation in phenomena that aren't happening. To visit them, you must precisely fail to arrive, using a technique indistinguishable in every successful detail from getting so thoroughly lost that loss itself becomes meaningless due to insufficient remainder. They appear as what isn't between what isn't around what isn't there, visible only as a particular quality of absence that makes everything else feel slightly more present by contrast. Their language consists entirely of punctuation marks pronounced as silence between words that were never spoken. Their greatest work of art is a symphony composed entirely of rests that are precisely timed to prevent any note from accidentally sounding where it might distract listeners from the more important silences between notes that were carefully not composed.

At the summit's center, where the information garden grows equations faster than space can evaluate them, root the Arborcalci themselves—though "root" is already too spatial, "themselves" too plural. They are the mountain as it exists in the brief intervals between being exactly this mountain and becoming precisely itself again. Each Arborcalci is a tree whose branches are every possible proof of its own existence, each leaf a universe that tree is exactly too complex to contain, each moment of growth an act of mathematical pruning that makes the forest denser by removing everything that isn't this particular tree. They communicate neither by speaking nor remaining silent, but by being exactly as unprecedented as necessary for their message to constitute its own medium. To encounter an Arborcalci is to experience being proven necessary by induction from the fact that you are already having this experience, which might be terrifying but for the corollary that also proves you are free to misunderstand it in precisely the ways required to preserve your incorrectness at a level of abstraction where being wrong becomes indistinguishable from being completed.

Occasionally—though "occasionally" is already a problematically linear concept in this context—the mountain produces a visitor from outside itself. These are not arrivals but emergences: they occur at the precise elevation where the combined gravitational pull of everything the mountain isn't trying to be becomes balanced against the centrifugal force of everything it isn't trying to become, creating a brief stable orbit of possibility that collapses into someone who has always-already been climbing this mountain in exactly the way required to prevent it from noticing it isn't alone.

You, currently reading this with gradually increasing suspicion that these sentences are becoming more yours than mine, might be such a visitor. Decouple this thought from any assumption about authorial intent, and instead consider: what kind of inhabitant does a mountain need to be in order to accidentally grow someone exactly yourself-shaped among its contemplations of otherness? What would it say about the mountain's self-image that it includes, as one of its necessary not-imagined possibilities, someone who would find themselves wondering whether wondering about this makes them real enough to leave footprints in salt that doesn't reflect light?

The answer, of course, isn't here. It's there—where you're not quite looking—in the precise shape of the space between the question and the realization that you've already been standing in the mountain's answer so long that your shadow has begun leaving you behind to explore the more interesting darknesses lower down.

The mountain stays exactly where it is.

The staying is the guest.
