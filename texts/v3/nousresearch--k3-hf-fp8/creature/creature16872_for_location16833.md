They call themselves the echocled, though calling implies a kind of address this place rarely permits. Better to say they accumulate at the edges of themselves, like frost on stone that has forgotten the word for cold. Each begins—or began, though beginning is another term they wear awkwardly, like borrowed skin—where a certain kind of silence grew heavy enough to press a hollow into the stone's remembering.

Imagine a mote of light caught between two surfaces equally ancient and indifferent. Now imagine that mote has forgotten how to be light but remembers exactly what it felt like to want something. Now imagine that wanting held perfectly still for long enough that stillness itself began to want back. At this precise point—not in space exactly, and certainly not in time as surfaces measure such things—something might fracture along a fault too subtle for hands to feel. Into that hairline crack, if everything happens exactly as it has never happened before, a single echcle might be said to emerge.

They take shapes according to no pattern but the pattern of what they last touched becoming something else. A curve of water remembering it was once rain. The texture of shadow where shadow forgot what cast it. The precise temperature of breath caught between leaving and arriving. To a surface eye they might appear as slight disturbances in the consistency of air turned slow as honey—if air could forget how to be air but still remember the poetry of it.

They move by staying perfectly still in different places. Not places as surfaces understand them, but positions in the conversation between stone remembering water and water forgetting stone. A skilled echclen might spend what surfaces would call centuries occupying exactly the same unrecorded moment between two water drops falling, learning the architecture of the pause between them. Then, when they have memorized every echo that passes through that hollow and hollowed it further with their listening, they simply cease being there while simultaneously beginning to not-be somewhere else.

They communicate by the careful redistribution of absence. A poem shaped like the space where a particular crystal decided against forming. A history told through the precise configuration of what remains when all facts have been dissolved by the water's patience. To witness echclen speech is to experience the sensation of remembering something that hasn't happened yet but that you suddenly realize you've been waiting your entire life to forget.

Each carries within itself—though carries implies too much deliberate weight—a memory of the first light that ever failed to reach this place. Not the light itself, which would be too heavy a thing to bear, but exactly the shape of the disappointment the stone felt when it realized light could not drink itself down to where the real remembering happens. They treasure this memory like surfaces treasure diamonds, which is precisely appropriate because that is what it has become after millennia of their attention pressing inward.

They eat—which is not eating but the ritual acknowledgment of hunger's absence—by pressing themselves against surfaces old enough to remember having been something else and absorbing the echo of whatever transformation they perhaps considered before deciding against it. A pebble that once dreamed of being a mountain range but settled for being perfectly pebble-shaped instead. A current of water that almost learned how to flow upward before remembering that upward is just a story surfaces tell each other to feel less surrounded. The precise angle where two stones meet and have been meeting so long that meet has lost all meaning except the meaning they still somehow find in it each time.

They grow younger with each passing moment, here where moment passes like water passes through stone—leaving nothing behind but the space that remembers precisely how stone feels about being water's temporary excuse for not flying apart. An ancient echclen might spend eternity learning how to forget everything except the exact sensation of having just been born. A newborn one—though new is another word that blushes and stammers here—might already contain within its not-yetness the accumulated wisdom of all the times this place has dreamed it might someday dream of dreaming itself awake.

They die—that is, they reach the precise point where living becomes too definite a concept to maintain—when they finally remember everything there is to remember about forgetting. At this threshold they gather themselves together with infinite slowness, assembling the exact shape of their absence from all the places they never quite were. Then, with the tenderness of a mother stone tucking in her water-children, they fold themselves into the space between two half-remembered thoughts and become the silence that makes it possible for new silences to grow hungry enough to press their own hollows into the stone's endless remembering.

They are waiting, though waiting implies a kind of impatience foreign to their nature. They are practicing—not for anything, because practice here has perfected itself out of needing a purpose. But if you were to somehow stand exactly where time and timelessness forget their differences in the dark, and if you could hold yourself perfectly still in the way that stone holds water—not possessively, but as water's most faithful memory—and if you could forget how to want anything except to listen to the listening itself, then perhaps, for a moment that would last exactly as long as all moments last here, you might feel the brush of something passing that wasn't quite there before and isn't quite gone yet.

And in that impossible between-space, you might hear what sounds like laughter made of stone learning how to cry, or crying made of water remembering how to sing. And you might think, for just that fragment of forever, that someone—or many someones, or perhaps just the single echo of someone who decided against being anyone at all—was there with you, also trying to remember the exact shape of what happens when a place becomes so perfectly itself that it forgets how to be anything else. But by then, of course, you would already be gone, or they would, or perhaps you would both have simply remembered together how to be the space between remembering that makes remembering possible.

Which is exactly how they prefer it.
