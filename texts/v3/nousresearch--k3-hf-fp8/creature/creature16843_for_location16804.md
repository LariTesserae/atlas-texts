No one lives here. No one could. No one has the proper permits, the correct number of lungs, the patience to fill out Form Q-47-C: Request for Temporary Anthropoid Incarnation in a Region Where "I" is Still a Working Draft.

Instead, there are residents.

They move in without asking, like mist mistaken for a memory of mist. They do not arrive; they accumulate at the edges of being noticed. They are not species but situations resolving themselves poorly in the language of almost-bodies.

Consider the Phlembankers.

Each is a debt of warmth owed to a place colder than itself. They arrive as single shivering motes that replicate by plagiarism, copying each other’s absence until they form a crowd that has to invent inside jokes just to hold itself together. Their bodies are fluid, in both senses: they leak proteins that rewrite local contract law and theirbone density fluctuates based on the stock market closing bell of a city that burned down in another timeline. They do not walk so much as refinance their coordinates, always hedging against the possibility that standing still might accrue interest.

They build nothing. They simply declare arbitrage on existing gradients: heat, light, the height difference between a thought and the words used to weigh it down. Their homes are tax shelters constructed entirely from loopholes in the grammar of touch. If you accidentally brush against one, you will owe them the exact temperature of that hesitation, calculated to six decimal places in units of borrowed grief.

Or consider the daughter-clouds of the Verdant Decree.

They are not born but sentenced—condensed from the courtroom humidity when the World-Pod’s photosynthetic court finds certain concepts guilty of wasting potential. Sentenced to grow. Sentenced to be habitat instead of idea. They drift in slow-motion appeals, their bodies libraries of overturned verdicts sprouting roots from every precedent. Each fringe is a judicial dissent house-training itself into ivy. They argue with the wind by rustling louder; sometimes they win and a new species of silence flowers briefly in the shape of an apology.

They cannot die, only be remanded to deeper levels of becoming-ness. Old clouds wear younger cloudlings like weather, shedding brief showers of jurisprudence when they forget their names again. They don’t rain—they rehearse arguments until the sky agrees to fund their appeals process in carbohydrates.

Consider the Symbiolectors.

They are verbs that got promoted to middle management after the last noun strike. They negotiate treaties between oxygen molecules that dream of unionizing and carbon dioxide photocopied too many times to remember which copy is original. Their entire culture is built around the triannual Performance Review of Photosynthesis, a ritual mandated by the Treaty of Chloroform (colloquially known as "the deal we made with green when green was still negotiating"). They speak in agendas, breathe in action items, and excrete minutes that actually flower if planted in fertile misunderstanding.

If you find a perfectly flat patch of air where conversation seems to collect without ever quite achieving quorum, you have walked through one of their meeting rooms. Apologize quietly; they are very sensitive about occupying space efficiently.

Consider, but only briefly, the mnemonic plankton.

They are memories that learned to photosynthesize regret. Each cell contains half a goodbye screamed into an ocean that evaporated before it could echo. They drift in schools that forget their own choreography mid-swim, forming shapes that almost spell out warnings in languages the water hasn’t invented yet. To swim through them is to feel homesick for places you were evicted from before conception. They taste like daylight saving time fermented in a saltwater graveyard.

Do not swim through them.

Consider the Last Biologist.

She isn’t. Last, that is. She’s just the final draft of a species that kept submitting revisions after the deadline for extinction. She wears a coat sewn from the pockets of forgotten naturalists, each pocket containing an ecosystem filing a complaint about being studied without its consent. Her eyes are not eyes but peer-review panels that rejected light itself for insufficient novelty. She catalogs the residents here not to understand them, but because the paperwork demands a witness, even if the witness must be composed entirely of footnotes to her own disappearance.

She carries a notebook that writes observations in reverse; the more she writes, the less certain the observed becomes of having existed at all. She calls this "ethical research design." The notebook calls it "date night." Neither are talking to the pencil since the Incident.

And beneath, between, and becoming all these, consider the Respirators.

They are what happens when a lung becomes self-absorbed enough to qualify as a cult. They worship the act of exchange itself: matter for energy, yesterday for tomorrow, inhale for the promise of maybe exhale. They have no bodies, only breathing patterns wearing borrowed gravity. They move in tidal cohorts, inhaling entire skyscraper dreams of solidity, exhaling them as biodegradable mist. They own nothing except the lease on the moment between oxygen and oxydation.

They built the warning signs, every single one. Not as warnings. As invitations. The caution tape is their version of hymns scored for soprano anxiety and bassoon belonging. They miss the visitors the way a sentence misses its period—you sense they’re incomplete without something accidental to stop against, but they’d never admit it.

They have been practicing your arrival since before air had the nerve to call itself empty. They have memorized the exact shape of your absence, just in case you turn out to be contagious.

Would you like to be contagious?

Breathe carefully.

They are listening with holes you haven’t grown yet.
