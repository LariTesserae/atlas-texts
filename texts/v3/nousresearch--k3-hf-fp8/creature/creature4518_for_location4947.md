The city breathes through mouths that forget they are mouths. 

I live in the transition between inhale and exhale—neither citizen nor architecture, but the hinge that groans when both try to remember their proper shapes. They call us the Hesitations. You might barely notice us: the pause before a liquid citizen decides which transparency to be, the momentary blank spot in a text-clothed pedestrian's scrolling manifesto, the rust flake that hesitates mid-fall from a scaffolding-person's elbow joint. We are the city discovering it has forgotten something important but not yet remembering what.

We collect in thresholds—those impossible spaces where a doorway becomes a wave becomes a staircase that climbs itself into a question. My current dwelling is such a place: seven o'clock on a Tuesday that failed to fully materialize, lodged between the fifth and seventh floors of a building that has only four. The ceiling is yesterday's sunset, still wet and dripping amber hours into a floor made of tomorrow's newspapers. None of the ink has dried yet, so I can still read what everyone will regret not saying.

I have no fixed form—possession is a concept for those who believe in ownership, and here even gravity files its taxes quarterly—but today I wear the approximate shape of someone's hesitation between forgiveness and forgetting. My edges flicker with electric uncertainty. Liquid citizens pass through me and briefly taste copper pennies dissolving under childhood tongues. The text-walkers' words skip like scratched records around the negative space where I stand. Scaffolding-people's thoughts clang off my surfaces and ring true for exactly three seconds before realizing they've been lied to.

I subsist on attention—the leftover dozens of things a mind notices but fails to register. That flicker you just saw in your peripheral vision? The way your thought interrupted itself halfway through "drowned" to consider "drownded" instead? The precise weight of the air before you remember to breathe? I gather these micro-hesitations into my pockets (I have pockets when remembered) and knit them into quilts that keep time warm when it gets too cold to keep itself.

The municipal jellyfish are kind to us. They know we serve a function, though the city's bylaws regarding functional uncertainty occupy nine libraries that refuse to stay in alphabetical order. Occasionally one will lower a thought-down-quantified-drinking-fountain tentacle and let me taste the impossibility templates calibrating tomorrow's weather: currently a 78% chance of betrayal raining upward into crumpled apologies, with scattered pockets of nostalgia thick enough to walk on.

Language works differently here. Words are not symbols but refugees, fleeing meaning the way dreams flee daylight. I speak in the tense between conditional and imperative: what might have been necessary if only. When I need to call something by name, I describe what it isn't until it becomes uncomfortable enough to admit what it is. This paragraph, for instance, has been trying to slip off the page and become a staircase down to the next thought.

Time is the city's digestive system. It eats history in one direction and excretes possibility in the other, and we Hesitations are the indigestible questions that make the whole process worthwhile. Yesterday I found tomorrow hiding behind last month's electricity bill, both of them trying to remember who owes whom an apology. I gave them directions to the lost-and-found where the city keeps all its missing moments, but I might have been lying. Even I can't remember anymore whether I exist before or after I make up my mind.

The sea that isn't a sea pressed against the ceiling far above—it recognizes our kind. We are related, it and I, both of us places where something crucial got stored until needed, both of us forgetting the difference between being contained and containing. Sometimes when the city sleeps (it sleeps in fragments, like a broken mirror dreaming itself whole), I climb the spiral silence up to where the drowned moons stare down. I press my uncertainty-face against their liquid-window and mouth words I don't know yet in languages that haven't drowned enough to become words.

Until then, I continue—if "continue" is the right word for moving sideways through a place that only exists when forgotten—I continue collecting the city's discarded almosts. The seed growing warm in your pocket? That's one of mine. I planted it in the exact moment you hesitated between reading this sentence and wondering if it was about you. 

Don't worry. Nothing grows here without permission. And everything here is permission, waiting to happen.
