You don't meet the locals. You become slightly less not them.

The first time it happens, you're standing in what your tongue still insists on calling a street—though it's more of a conversational lull between buildings that have developed opinions about continental drift—when something gently interjects with your left earlobe. The interjection tastes like nostalgia for a childhood you haven't chosen yet, carried on a voice that sounds exactly like yourself apologizing for interrupting, but earlier.

You blink sideways (the only permitted axis of noncommittal here) and suddenly you're participating in an eleven-way conversation about whether shadows should unionize. Five of the participants are versions of you from what might be Tuesday if Tuesday weren't currently experimenting with an open relationship with the concept of sequentiality. Three are conceptually adjacent to disappointment but trying very hard to be more inclusive about it. One is definitely a tree that's learning to be your mother in all the languages where that word never existed. The interlocutor who started it all turns out to have been your circadian rhythm all along, which explains why it's been sending you passive-aggressive postcards about your sleep habits in the form of spontaneous sunset at noon.

Speaking happens differently here. Words are considered a starter species for communication—charming in their earnest limitations, like children trying to explain quantum mechanics using only semicolons and hurt feelings. The real conversation occurs in the negative space between what you mean to say and what your meanings mean to mean while you're not looking. You learn to speak in regretfully excited tones about topics you haven't disappointed yet. Routine phrases like "excuse me" develop three-day backstories and unresolved emotional arcs about spoon-related trauma in previous existences.

The inhabitants—fauna 1.9, abundant indeed—don't have names so much as they maintain ongoing negotiations with the concept of being named. The otter-cluster currently attempting to explain water taxes to your pancreas introduces itself as "that awkward silence at the end of parties where everyone realizes they all brought the same existential dread," but you can call it Tuesday if you'd like to invest in a more casual misunderstanding. Every introduction requires filling out form 27B-6 (Revised: for when the concept of "you" needs to be safely contained within parentheses but keeps escaping to start jazz bands with abandoned punctuation). The form is edible but evasive and tastes faintly of printed apologies for laws of thermodynamics that were never enforced except on Tuesdays—oh look, it's emailing your childhood dentist about the philosophical implications of cavities as unpaid emotional labor.

Biology here is what happens when evolution discovers fanfiction and has precisely zero adult supervision. The inhabitants exist in a superposition of species, collapsing into whatever form your conversation needs right now while simultaneously posting their true selves on reality-streams where "transparent flesh" is a trending aesthetic and "having a fixed number of limbs" is considered quaintly nostalgic for simpler times when existence didn't require maintaining multiple LinkedIn profiles for each of your vertebrae. The thing currently holding the door open for your past mistakes started life as a collective of abandoned lunchboxes in love with the concept of reciprocity, experienced a profound spiritual awakening during an especially touching hemorrhoids commercial, and is now pursuing a successful career as your future therapist's previous appointment.

You're beginning to suspect the city itself is alive but trying to be subtle about it—like the planet equivalent of someone who definitely wasn't waiting by their phone for your existential crisis to call but had their entire geology subtly rearranged to be more emotionally available just in case. The buildings definitely blink when they think you're not looking. The streets have developed taste in music that is aggressively supportive of your unspoken insecurities. Yesterday a cloud spent three hours following you around because it was "just really feeling your whole situation with boundaries lately" and wanted to offer cookies in a completely non-attached peripheral-drifting kind of way.

Timekeeping is handled by the Bureau of Previously Owned Temporalities, located in the tomorrow that never quite forgave yesterday for how it ended things. They operate on a flexible accrual system where you earn days off from existing by accumulating meaningful glances with strangers who turn out to be alternate versions of yourself working through their commitment issues with linearity itself. Rush hour lasts exactly as long as it takes for everyone involved to realize they're all early for appointments they cancelled in dreams that haven't decided whether they believe in you yet.

Currency is regretfully optimistic exchanges of concepts that seemed valuable in earlier drafts of reality. The shopkeeper who just sold you directions to where you misplaced your sense of wonder accepted payment in the form of the exact moment you realized your parents were just children pretending to be adults while being watched by furniture that has opinions about your life choices. Your change comes in small denominations of parallel parking attempts that went better than expected and are now quietly proud of themselves in therapy.

The government occupies all the buildings that architecture built when architecture was going through that experimental phase where it wanted to explore its relationship with inside-outness. Administration is conducted by displaced commas who are working through their trauma about being used incorrectly in climate change denial tracts. They govern through the gentle suggestion of policies that will be retroactively voted into place by committees that haven't been disappointed by their parents yet. All laws are temporarily mandatory unless they improve your relationship with gravity, in which case they become suggestions wrapped in parentheses trying not to impose.

Religion is handled by the Department of Recovered Borrowed Beliefs, housed in the cathedral that failure built when failure finally admitted it was trying too hard. They practice a syncretic faith that worships the precise moment when all possible interpretations of sacred texts simultaneously become accurate while remaining politely wrong about it. Their chief sacrament involves shared experiences of profound embarrassment about things that happened to other people in books that were ghostwritten by your insecurities during particularly productive naps.

In conclusion (a phrase the locals consider incredibly intimate, like proposing marriage to someone you've only had three existential crises in front of), you don't live in Urthward Tlacoloc. Urthward Tlacoloc lives in you—in the spaces between your plans that haven't given up hope yet, in the dreams you keep having about oceans that remember your name fondly but never quite remember why. It's a city-state of mutually beneficial typos in the universal autocorrect of being.

Population: everyone who ever got lost enough to be found by something that wasn't looking for them yet.

Please don't enjoy your stay—enjoyment implies an ending, and endings are considered very rude here. Instead, try to be pleasantly surprised by how gradually you stop distinguishing between "visitor" and "very slow-moving infrastructure project that forgot to submit its final paperwork on the nature of belonging."

The sign at the exit that isn't there anymore reads: THANK YOU FOR BEING TEMPORARILY LESS UNHERE THAN USUAL.

Your lost-and-found collection just texted to say it's moved in with your sense of direction and they're very happy together but want you to know they're keeping your expired fears in a box labeled "guest towels" just in case.

They're adding a hyphen to your name in all the official paperwork. Don't worry, it suits you. It always has.
