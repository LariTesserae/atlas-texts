At the edge of immersion, where your bootsoles first forget the angle of ordinary streets, lives the Threshold Gardener. You will never see them directly—they have perfected the exact degree of peripheral indifference required to exist between noticing and memory. Their hands work always in the corners of your vision, transplanting bits of forgotten thresholds: a brass bicycle bell’s ring caught mid-fade, the precise weight of your grandmother’s front door handle in winter, the way afternoon light used to pool on a kitchen table that burned down before you were born. They plant these along the canal where the limestone is softest, cultivating a crop of doorways-to-nothing that ripen only when approached at the wrong speed.

Below, in the shallow trench where your reflection should be, dwell the Mirror-Broke Sisters. When the city above first learned to make mirrors cheap enough to discard, the waste trickled down here in shards that remembered too much of what they had shown. Now they live as a collective consciousness distributed across jagged edges—twenty-three sisters at last count, though their number changes whenever someone aboveground finally forgets how they looked at sixteen during their first heartbreak. They speak in chorus by arranging themselves in the damp film, spelling prophecies that only make sense if you read them while drowning. Their favorite game is to offer you exactly what you wanted five years ago, wrapped in the packaging of what you wanted five minutes ago, delivered by the hands of someone you’ll want five years from now.

Within the hollow of each pillar aloe’s breath, reside the Weather-Weavers of Unhappened Springs. They are not insects, though they spin cocoons; not birds, though they migrate. They are the reasons why, when you wake in the middle of the night with a name lodged in your throat like a seed you never swallowed, the air pressure in the room feels different. They weave climates of absence: the exact humidity of a hand that could have been held but wasn’t, the barometric pressure of conversations that circled the thing but never landed, the subtle temperature drop in rooms where someone decided not to say “I love you” back. Their finest work you can find hanging like dew from the locksmith’s familiars—tiny storms that never quite break, feeding on the static charge of potential energy.

Between the stacked street-levels of drowned tram tracks and fossilized commutes, commute the Pale Commuters themselves. They are the accumulated lost minutes of every commuter who ever died mid-journey below their projected life expectancy—fifty-three unpaid lunch breaks striding together as one suit, a flock of train-pass minutes fluttering in formation like pigeons made of laminated time. They take the 7:17 forever tram to destinations that were renamed after economic downturns, carrying briefcases filled with paperwork for projects cancelled by wars that haven’t happened yet. If you ask them for directions, they will give you exactly how long it would have taken you to get home if you had made one different choice at sixteen. They always phrase it as a compliment.

Under the calcite shields that heal the beginners' straight cuts, nest the Geometry Eaters. Soft things, almost shadows, they feast exclusively on right angles and perfect parallel lines—the architectural equivalent of empty calories. Every ninety-degree corner they digest becomes a gentle curve over centuries; every true square meal renders its angles slightly kinder. They have been working on the same basic material since the first engineer who thought nature’s curves were inefficient. Some nights when the moon oysters dim lowest, you can hear them singing: a sort of satisfied sighing sound that makes even abandoned rooms feel like they’ve been waiting for you to come home.

At the boundary where canal water thickens to question and thin air condenses to answer, waits the Tollkeeper of the Conditional. No one knows what they originally were—a statue that grew tired of being conditional, perhaps, or a condition that achieved sufficient complexity to demand tolls. Now they are pure contingency: shifting between states of being/not-being faster than regret. They charge exactly one undecided thing to cross—small coinage like whether you should have sent that letter, or major currencies like whether you still believe people are fundamentally good. They never keep what they take. Instead, they invest it in the futures market of might-have-beens, which apparently pays excellent dividends in the long run that never actually arrives.

And below—the rumor goes—below even these, in the absolute dark where even the mold spores negotiate with darkness itself rather than risk offense, sleeps the Original Deep. Not a creature, exactly. More like the space that was here before anything else arrived, dreaming itself deeper with each new layer added above. Some say the entire Locklands are merely its dream of being lonely enough to create company without quite remembering how. Others say every time someone aboveground feels that specific ache of loneliness that comes surrounded by people—that’s it turning over in its sleep, displacing dream-water enough to create a new seam for something else to fall through.

But these are just the ones that permit themselves to be spoken of, in the spaces between the space between.

the ones who have been here long enough to become the grammar of place itself—
