Here dwells the Unfinished, the ones who took up residence in the vacancy left by the departure of the Something-that-was. They are not a species but a state of becoming, perpetually caught mid-transformation between what they were before they discovered this place and what they will be when they finally achieve sufficient allegiance with it.

The Unfinished have no fixed forms, only current negotiations. Today you might meet one shaped like a woman whose lower half has resolved into a complex root-braid system anchoring her spine directly into a particularly ambitious fig's heartwood. Tomorrow she might be mostly kimono-patterned moth wings floating around a cluster of glowing fruit—she discovered flight was easier than explaining herself to the territorial babirusa-shrews. Always, though, they retain some fragment of their pre-arrival selves, like a stone in a fruit's heart that remembers the mountain while learning sweetness. An eye that blinks too slowly because it still thinks in mammalian rhythms. A mouth that speaks in perfect iambic pentameter because that was the cadence of its original language-pulse before the forest taught it to communicate in gradients of decomposition.

They live—not quite in, not quite between—the hollow spaces left by the artifacts. Each Unfinished is paired with a Wrong Thing like a shadow paired with a sliver of missing darkness. The woman-root tends a garden of broken electronics that insist on performing calculations unrelated to mathematics whenever certain flowers bloom magenta. The moth-wing cluster keeps custody of a perfectly spherical lake that reflects nothing that exists in front of it, only things that might have existed if choices had been made differently three breaths ago. They guard these impossibilities not in the traditional sense of guarding—nothing here is protected, everything is participated in—but in the sense that they continue existing adjacent to them long after their original purposes have dissolved into nutrient cycles.

The youngest Unfinished still maintain provisional bodies composed of roughly 60% themselves and 40% co-opted materials. They sleep in cocoons woven from their own shed languages, dreaming translations between human concepts and fungal wisdom-tattoos. They eat by pressing their palms against photosynthetic surfaces and paying attention until hunger becomes irrelevant. They reproduce—I use this term loosely—when two or more Unfinished encounter an idea too large for any single iteration to contain. They then spend several seasons growing a communal thought-organ somewhere inconvenient (last year it was a deciduous cathedral that walked around at night asking theological questions of sleeping seed-pods). When the thought-organ finally flowers, it releases a cloud of conceptual spores that lodge in whatever minds or substrates happen to be passing by. Some of these spores take root and develop into new Unfinished. Most become interesting species of moss.

The oldest Unfinished barely qualify as plural entities anymore. They've achieved such perfect symbiosis with their chosen aspects of the biomesphere that distinguishing between them and their surroundings is both meaningless and rude. One of them may be the entire northeast quadrant's weather system on Tuesdays. Another might be the collective dream-memory of all the places where rain used to hit metal before the last metalsmith became a grove of particularly philosophical ironwood trees. They can be spoken with, but only by speaking to the whole conversation they're having with the soil bacteria and the migrating bird-patterns and the slow mineral thoughts of the planet's mantle through the filter of your own dreams from three nights before you arrived.

They have no leaders because leadership is an insufficiently dimensional concept here. Instead they practice a form of governance called consensual convergence, which functions like democracy if votes were emotions and elections occurred retroactively whenever enough aspects of reality agree on feeling similar about something important. Recent decisions voted into being this way include: the color of gratitude during harvest season (subtle violet-laugh), the official shape of yearning between strangers (asymptotic spiral with fractal edges), and whether to continue existing as separate entities for another subjective eternity (narrow victory for "yes, but with more ambiguity.")

Visitors—or as the Unfinished call them, "potential plot twists"—are neither welcomed nor unwellected. They simply arrive when the forest's abstract narrative structures require their particular flavor of ignorance to resolve an accumulating tension somewhere. Some stay for minutes. Some stay for geological eras measured in the retracting regret of distant galaxies. A few manage to leave again, though they never quite return to where they started from. They bring with them impossible souvenirs: the memory of a color that makes sound feel nostalgic, the ability to grow perfect greenhouse orchards in their sleep, a persistent sense of being watched by something benevolent and billions of years old that thinks they're doing remarkably well under the circumstances.

But most never leave. They don't die or transform or merge. They simply continue becoming more themselves through increasingly elaborate collaborations with their environment until eventually someone notices that a particular patch of forest has developed opinions about poetry or a certain kind of fungus now grows only in patterns that spell out questions nobody remembers asking in languages that might never have existed.

These are the ones we miss the most when we accidentally think about home. Though "miss" is perhaps inaccurate. Long-distance relationships are easier when you're not entirely sure where either party ends and the longing between begins.
