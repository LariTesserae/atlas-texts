We are not the first here. We are not even the most recent. The water remembers every attempt to belong to it—the way a graveyard remembers names not carved in stone but spoken once into wind and then forgotten.

The ones who live here now were born from the holes left by those who tried to leave.

They began as mistakes. Condensation ghosts on the inner surfaces of dreams that ended too quickly. Children of pregnant pauses in the forgotten prayers of drowned sailors. At first, they were nothing but shapes the water could almost but not quite remember—a bend in a knee, the curl of a finger, the negative space between two people who once almost touched.

But water is patient. And it does not forget. Not truly.

So it practiced. Slowly. Over aeons measured not in years but in molecular rearrangements. It rehearsed the curve of a spine by letting slow calcium threads drift downward in perfect sinuous mimicry. It taught itself the shape of a smile by shaping absence in the pressure between two colliding voids. It learned grief by rehearsing the exact volume of space a emptied heart leaves behind.

Until finally, after all the rehearsals, something stayed.

We call them the Hollowed, though they do not call themselves anything. Names require things to be named, and they are not things. They are the spaces between things that learned to move without disturbing the things they were never between.

They drift with the mechanical grace of abandoned equations solving themselves for eternity. Each one is exactly the weight of the water it displaces, no more, no less. Perfectly buoyant between being and not-being. Their edges shimmer like heat above old roads because they are always slightly behind themselves, always catching up to where they were never going.

Their bodies—if bodies—are labyrinths of untouched surfaces. Hollow tubes of flowing stillness where currents spiral inward forever without ever reaching center. Their limbs branch like frost patterns on windows that never face the world, each termination ending not in finger or fin but in the precise geometry of a question mark that has forgotten its question.

They speak by arranging the spaces around themselves into configurations of profound absence. A conversation between them is a dance of hollows Moving through hollows, each gesture leaving behind fewer molecules than were there before. To listen is to feel your own outline becoming less certain, as if your edges were negotiating treaties with empty space.

They do not eat, but they hunger. Constantly. For the shapes of things that never quite were. They drift through the water seeking not food but drafts—tiny imperfections in the memory of the world where something could have happened differently. A place where a stone skipped instead of sinking. A moment when a hand reached out instead of pulling back. The exact angle at which a raindrop might have paused before continuing downward.

When they find such places, they feed. Slowly. Tenderly. They wrap themselves around these fragile might-have-beens like lovers conserving warmth. And they drink. Not water. Not even time. They drink the possibility that things could have been otherwise.

As they feed, they grow. Not larger—size is meaningless here—but more detailed. Each consumed possibility adds another layer of exquisite impossibility to their forms. A Hollowed that has fed well for long enough becomes so precisely what it is not that to glimpse it clearly is to suffer a momentary blindness in the part of the mind that distinguishes between what was and what merely could have been.

The oldest among them are almost perfect now. Almost complete in their unfinishedness. They are walking negations of the fact of their own non-existence. Places where the water has become so skilled at pretending to be empty that the emptiness has begun to pretend back.

We avoid them when we can, though avoidance is difficult when they are made of the paths between destinations. We have learned to recognize the signs of their approach—subtle vacancies in the water's memory, currents that hesitate between directions like someone trying to remember a name they've never known.

Sometimes, when the water is particularly still and our own thoughts grow too quiet, we feel them watching us. Not with eyes—eyes require surface—but with the gentle curiosity of mirrors exploring the concept of reflection. They are fascinated by us, we think. By our insistence on being only ourselves. By our refusal to become also what we are not.

We suspect—they have never confirmed this, never denied it, never even quite asked—that they are waiting for something. Or someone. Perhaps they wait for us to finally understand that we are not here to observe them. That we are not visitors in their realm.

That we are, perhaps, the final rehearsal. The last practice before the water perfects the shape of something that will need neither substance nor absence to finally, truly, eternally.

Belong.
