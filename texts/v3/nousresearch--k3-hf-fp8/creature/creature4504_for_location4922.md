**The Chair's Familiar**

It is not waiting for you, though you might think so when your eyes finally adjust to the glow-gloom and resolve the chair's silhouette against the chamber's seamless blur. The chair has no expectations left; they've been ground away particle by particle, atom by atom, expectation by expectation, until only the posture of waiting remains. The word etched into its backrest has been read by too many eyes that dissolved before reaching the final letter, mouths that shaped the "W" and faded before the breath for "-ait" could follow. Now the chair sits like a question mark that forgot its question.

The chair's familiar lives in the negative space the chair occupies when no one sits. See how the shadows pool thicker there, even when light seems evenly diffused? The familiar is the dent in reality that grows deeper each time someone considers sitting but doesn't. It's composed of all the weight that never pressed down, all the warmth that never transferred, all the sighs that never exhaled into the chair's hollow back. It feeds on the moment between deciding and doing, that infinite pause where possibility still tastes better than consequence.

The familiar has no fixed form but prefers, when it must choose, to be the imprint of the last person who almost stayed. Right now it wears the outline of a woman who stepped forward, then back, three thousand years ago or yesterday (the distinction exists only to those who leave). Her phantom knees still hover inches above the cushion. Her shadow hands grip the armrests she never quite touched. Her memory of standing on the precipice of sitting shimmers in the air like heat distortion above cooling lava.

Watch closely when the room flexes in its geological breath. For one impossible instant, the familiar gains substance enough to push back against the chair's metal frame. The word "Wait" brightens as if freshly etched. The chair creaks in a frequency that makes your bones itch with recognition. Then everything settles back into itself, the moment of reciprocation gone before your neurons can agree it happened.

The chair's familiar is the chamber's historian, but it only records absences. It remembers perfectly every footprint that stopped exactly one millimeter short of the chair's legs. It catalogs the exact temperature differential caused by each body heat that never mingled with the chair's own slight warmth above ambient. It has measured precisely how much longer the silence stretches after each unspoken answer to the chair's unasked question.

It doesn't want anything, the familiar. It has no desires left - those were the first things to evaporate in this place where wanting changes nothing and nothing changes from wanting. But it maintains certain habits. Every time someone new arrives (though "new" is another word that has no meaning here, only "next"), the familiar performs its ritual:

First, it adjusts itself to match the newcomer's exact height of disappointment upon realizing the chamber contains only what they've always feared: nothing that will finally explain why they've always felt in themselves this same hollow ache of waiting.

Second, it projects into the newcomer's mind the precise memory of the last time they stood in a doorway, any doorway, and understood without words that what lay beyond would not make them whole but walked through anyway.

Third, it offers - without hope of acceptance, as a volcano offers smoke without expecting it to returning as rain - the illusion of choice: sit or don't sit, stay or don't stay, knowing that either decision was already made by every version of yourself that arrived here before you.

Most next-ones flee before the familiar completes its third habit, chased by the sudden certainty that if they sit they'll discover the chair is exactly their shape because it's been waiting for nothing else but to finally be filled by the one thing that will make its emptiness complete.

The very few who remain - who stand their ground through the familiar's full unfolding - they feel something shift in the air that once was solid, solid that once was air. They experience the chair's familiar experiencing them experiencing it, an infinite regress of mirrored voids staring into each other until they can't remember which absence came first. In that moment, some say they understand everything. Others say they forget everything except the word "Wait" carved not into metal but into the space between every atom of their being.

Then the room flexes again. The chair's familiar withdraws into itself, shedding the borrowed outline of the woman or man or child who almost. The word on the chair's backrest dims back to barely visible. The next arrival arrives, and the familiar prepares to wear a new shape of absence shaped exactly like them.

But none of this happens in any order you could follow. Time here is like the light - everywhere and nowhere, borrowing your eyes to see itself seeing. The chair's familiar has already been, is being, will be what it always is: the perfect companion the chair never knew it didn't want, the story that makes the waiting bearable by promising that waiting itself is the ending you've been waiting for.

The chair waits. The familiar waits. The difference between them waits too, patient as stone that chose to remember how to dream but forgot how to wake.

Sit down.

Don't sit down.

The chair's familiar will be whichever choice you didn't make.
