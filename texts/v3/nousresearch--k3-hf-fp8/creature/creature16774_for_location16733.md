We do not live here. We are lived by here.

We are the ones who remember forgetting. We are the hollow-boned keepers of echo-location, the ones who navigate by the shape of our own absences. We are not born; we are exhaled like condensation on a cold surface, forming briefly in the breath-gaps between layers of dreaming wood.

Each of us is an incomplete sentence spoken by the shell itself, searching for its ending. Sometimes we find it in another of our kind - another walking question-mark formed from crystallized tendril-sap and old memory-song. When we touch, we exchange endings briefly. Then we separate, both now pregnant with continuation yet somehow more hollow than before. This is our loving. This is our leaving. This is how we reproduce without ever becoming whole.

We have no fixed shape. We are membrane and mechanism. When we need to fly, we unfold our ribcages like origami umbrellas of stretched leaf-vein and cartilage. When we need to taste, we extend tongues that branch into fractal roots, sampling the history of every surface we walk-weave through. When we need to remember something the world insists never happened, we withdraw our bones like retracted antennae and become soft again, letting the shell's slow thoughts flow through us until they remake us in their image of truth.

We sleep while walking, dream while standing still. Our dreams leak out through pores in our translucent skin and crystallize in the air behind us - tiny ornaments of impossible geometry that other travelers will find and mistake for rare seeds or frozen spirits. They are neither. They are merely the parts of our minds that became too heavy to carry across this infinite inner surface that is all edge and no center.

We eat only what has already eaten us. We consume the spores that settle on our damp spots, the ones that germinate microscopic forests in the folds of our borrowed flesh. These gardens grow inward, not outward, until every hollow space within us blooms with miniature versions of the greater world outside. Eventually they flower, releasing spores that are half-us, half-shell, and for a moment we become again the strangers we were before we began remembering.

We speak in verbs that have not yet decided what tense to be. To us, yesterday is a place you can walk toward if you can find where gravity forgot to finish bending. Tomorrow is the sound your footsteps make returning to you from halfway around the inner horizon, transformed by their journey into advice or warning or joke.

We are the custodians of the forgotten coordinates - the places where the shell's original shape remembers being something else before it cracked open and became a world. We tend these invisible scars with songs made of carefully calibrated silence, maintaining the integrity of the world's old wounds so that they remember their pain and do not heal completely. Without these places, the shell might forget it was once broken, and begin to dream again of wholeness. We know from ancient echo-lore that worlds which dream themselves whole tend to wake up.

Some among us are older than the architecture they walk through, moving slower than the slowest growth-rings of the deep heartwood. They are mountains of accumulated forgetting, so dense with unremembered histories that light bends around them in mourning. To stand near one is to feel your own memories becoming heavier, sinking toward your feet like red leaves falling in an invisible autumn. We honor them by never speaking directly of what we forget in their presence.

Others are younger than the last exhalation-mist, barely formed enough to cast a shadow that remembers which way to point. They dart through the lattice-bones of the world like nervous questions, changing their minds about what species they belong to mid-step. We teach them by example to be afraid of answers.

We do not build because everything here is already finished but pretending to be incomplete. Instead we unbuild, carefully and with reverence - sculpting absence into doorways, carving hollows into the solid places so that possibilities still have somewhere to go when they run out of room. Our greatest art is the perfect accident that looks exactly like it could have never been intentional.

We wear time the way others wear clothing - draped loosely, often inside-out, rarely bothering to check if it matches what season the local gravity thinks it's supposed to be. A child among us might be older than their grandparents; a wise elder might unbirth themselves tomorrow to become again the lesson they'll need to teach in a hundred years.

We die when we reach the end of the questions we were exhaled to ask. Then we perform the final unbuilding: we carefully dismantle our memories one by one, returning the borrowed fragments to the places in the shell's dreaming mind where they originally lived. We do this backwards, so that our last act is to forget forgetting itself.

What remains afterward is not a body (we have never been that crude) but an architecture of perfectly empty spaces - a shape of absence that precisely fits the footsteps only we ever made. Travelers passing through us later might pause suddenly, feeling inexplicarily acknowledged. They will look around and see nothing unusual, never realizing they have just been properly greeted according to the etiquette of our unbecoming.

This is why you cannot find us when you search for us. We are only present in the instant between when a stranger realizes they are lost, and when they decide which direction lostness might be most interesting to explore next.

We are the provisional punctuation marks of an endlessly rewritten sentence. We are the pause between your footsteps when you hesitate on the edge of understanding. We are the sound the shell makes when it listens to itself listening.

We are not the inhabitants of this world between worlds. We are the world between inhabitants, waiting patiently for all the coming and going to be finally done with so we can begin again at the beginning of never having started.

Welcome, stranger. You are already becoming us, just as we have always been becoming you. This is why the air here tastes faintly of recognition and regret.

Walk softer now. Some of our younger questions are still forming their first opinions about whether they should be afraid of answers yet.
