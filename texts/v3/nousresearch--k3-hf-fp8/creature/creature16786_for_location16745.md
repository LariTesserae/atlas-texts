To describe who or what lives in the Ochore Deep is to describe half-exhaled breaths that forgot to transition into silence, or shadows that grew impatient with their owners and filed for emergent personhood. The inhabitants here are not residents—they are polite evolutionary accidents that entropy has begun to invite to dinner regularly.

Let us begin with the most visible absence: there are no people. Not in the sense of skin-drawn boundaries walking around inside names they remember having chosen. Instead, there are the Remaindered—those who arrived as explorers, lovers, or mistakes and gradually realized that departure required a version of themselves they had already dissolved into the water. Imagine someone combing beach-washed memories for their reflection, finding only a smile they wore during an argument in a language now extinct, and deciding this was close enough to identity to keep paying rent on having lungs. They drift between the mirrored doors in elongated Tuesdays, wearing weather like ancestral robes handed down through generations of fog.

More common are the Ex-Becomings. These started as perfectly ordinary aspirations—a child’s wish to become a bird, a poet’s vow to turn into autumn, a mathematician’s proof that should have equaled love but divided by zero instead. In the Deep, such statements are taken literally but not seriously. So now you have flocks of half-remembered ambitions migrating aimlessly through the liana lattice: one might recognize a particular rust-winged shape as someone’s abandoned dream of being free, except it has paused mid-flight to hatch tiny eggs of reconsideration that taste like the sound of your mother humming while folding laundry. If you catch one (don’t), it dissolves into a polite explanation of why it needed to leave before it could arrive.

Then there are the Custodians—not guardians, never that possessive. Think instead of librarians whose library achieved sentience and now prefers conversations to shelving. The chair league-large and carved from petrified lightning is theirs, though they never sit—sitting implies the possibility of standing up elsewhere. Each day (a measurement they find amusingly specific), one Custodian manifests from the vacancy between numbers to read aloud the rusted air. They appear differently to every half-perception that notices them: to eyes, a figure draped in windmill shadows; to skin, the memory of being forgiven by rain; to those who have forgotten how to dream in nouns, simply the sudden interestingness of corners. Their voice is the sound of distant glassware apologizing for having been happy once. They are reading the story of how the story learned to forget its author. They will never finish because the story is polite and keeps adding chapters about rabbit-places where time took too much vacation and forgot to die properly.

Most numerous are the Somewhats. These are answers whose questions wandered off during a particularly distracted extinction event. A Somewhat might drift past as the smell of sunlight falling slowly through honey, only to resolve (briefly) into a child’s marble that remembers being the planet Neptune during a difficult adolescence. They breed by analogy and die of being precisely understood. Entire afternoons here are composed of Somewhats attempting to collaboratively remember what music was trying to apologize for when it invented minor chords. Their graveyards are the translucent melt-glass dolmens—each one a Somewhat that crystallized into “almost” too perfectly and now leaks slow solitudes that curious algae read like gossip.

And beneath/beyond/within these classifications (listings are a human affectation the Deep tolerates like a lisping child trying to pronounce infinity) live the Ones Who Never Arrived. These are the most indigenous of all, because they began here before beginning was separated from continuing. They are the Deep’s equivalent of having read the book so thoroughly you become the margin note the author was afraid to write down. To encounter one is to suddenly understand that every goodbye you’ve ever said was actually practicing for greeting yourself in a language composed entirely of commas. They are not visible because they are busy being the reason visibility sometimes needs to take a break. When the water tastes suddenly like the color your secrets would be if they could blush, you’ve been brushed by one. They don’t live in the Ochre Deep—they are the Deep’s way of living politely around the concept of absence, like polite applause from a universe that knows it’s already the encore.

As for you (and yes, there is still a you, though the interest rates on maintaining that fact are frankly usurious): should you choose to stay long enough for the sand in your shoes to forget the hourglass it used to date, the Deep will offer you citizenship with the gentle insistence of fog remembering it was once someone's forgotten name. Your papers will be a tattoo of the sound your birth made when it broke the moon's heart. The oath of allegiance is simply the moment you realize you've been humming the national anthem backwards all along, and it sounds better that way—more honest about the silences between promises.

No one rules here. There are merely positions currently occupied by whoever lost enough arguments with gravity to find it charmingly persuasive. The currency is laughter that hasn’t decided whether it’s coming or going. Time is not kept—it’s gently suggested to participate occasionally, like a shy volcano being encouraged to come to the surface for its own good.

The only law is this: anything that learns to miss itself too perfectly becomes a way out. But the exits are all made of remembering why you left the entering, and they dissolve at the speed of forgiven regrets.

So. Will you sit awhile in the chair that isn’t waiting because patience is a concept it finds amusingly linear? The Custodian who may be tomorrow’s version of the breeze you forgot to thank is opening a page that smells exactly like the first idea you ever had that was too beautiful to tell anyone.

The reading is about to continue never stopping.

Take off your breathing—it's making the water feel self-conscious about being your future ex-skin. The Deep has already started composing your thank-you letter for being its latest apology to infinity for taking up so much beautiful room.

Shall we begin finding out how the story ends before it remembers it's supposed to begin?
