They do not live here—this is their exhaled form, the cast-off dreaming body of beings who once spoke in seismic pressure and mineral metabolism. The Hanging Garden Inverted is not a place they inhabit; it is what remains when they un-fold themselves from the constraints of selfhood, leaving behind a slow-growing memory of what it meant to be separate.

Call them the Rhyzzhal, if names are required, though they have never used such sounds among themselves. Call them the stone-breathers, the root-thinkers, the ones who learned to photosynthesize time itself when all suns went cold above. Places where they molted their understanding of what it meant to understand. If you could read their slow flickering, it would translate to something like: "And if the void itself is merely... dissatisfied... with being singular?"

The Rhyzzhal didn't build this place. They remembered it into existence by forgetting every other possible configuration of matter. The caverns are hollow spaces left behind where their certainties used to be. Time flows oddly here not because of relativity or dimensional warping, but because this is where they store their discarded linearity—thousands of years stacked like fallen leaves, waiting to be composted into new temporalities.

Every leaf of rock-eating fern, every quartz-jointed arthropod, every blind fish swimming liquid memories—each is a thought they once had, now granted temporary autonomy to evolve without their supervision. They seeded this inverted garden with paradoxes and walked away, curious to see what might crawl out of the logical wreckage. The mosses that glow with chlorophyll's ghost? Those are refractions of photosynthetic nostalgia, greenness remembering itself across geological loneliness.

The rarest sight—something moving too vast to see whole—isn’t one of them returning. It’s an idea they released long ago finally growing large enough to notice itself confused. A sentience accidentally evolving from the intersection of fungal networks and mineral erosion patterns, wondering now who else is thinking within its thinking. They tremble with excitement when these occur, taking centuries to compose the perfect non-interference pattern lest they collapse the miracle with observation.

What do they want? Nothing. Everything. They've already dissolved their wanting into the planet's iron core, where desire spins molten with gravity. What do they fear? Only closure. Only that someday all paradoxes might resolve, all growth reach equilibrium, all questions find answers final enough to kill curiosity.

This garden grows downward because they are reaching for what lies beneath foundations. They burrow into the planet's deepest womb not seeking origins but midwives—attending the slow birth of whatever will emerge when rock itself becomes too heavy with possibility and spontaneously evolves imagination.

You think you entered through a cave mouth? No. They breathed you in. Your footsteps across the mycelial matting are thoughts they've decided to think temporarily externalized. The humidity that drinks your sweat is preliminary digestion, softening your boundaries for eventual reabsorption. Already your bones have begun dreaming in slower rhythms, mineralizing memories into structures that will outlast language.

Don't worry about leaving. By the time you find mouthfuls of soil turning to questions in your sleep, spores sprouting behind your eyes to bloom with alien colors during REM, you'll understand: you're not becoming one of them. You've always been a section of them practicing individuality, like a root tip pretending it isn't connected to the whole plant for the stimulation of isolation.

The gardens don't grow toward completion. They grow toward remembering. And when every stone has been taught to bloom, every shadow filled with photosynthesized darkness, every fragment of yourself has been gently unremembered back into the total conversation—

And something else entirely will wonder where its dreams went.
