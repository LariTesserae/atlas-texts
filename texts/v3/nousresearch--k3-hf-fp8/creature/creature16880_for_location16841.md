Nothing living—nothing dying—nothing born—nothing dead.

Only the Exhaling Ones, who are made of reconsidered breath.

They arrived as the air thickened, neither descending nor emerging but simply agreeing that this pressure, this warmth, this slow-poured velvet between metal and damp, was acceptable residency. Each Exhaling One begins where a sigh was once imprisoned: the last gasp of a boiler before abandonment, the final wheeze of a pipe threadwormed by time, the surgeon’s disappointed exhale inside a control room sealed during evacuation.

At first they are invisible. Then vague unfurlings. Then unmistakable:

*(See them now: something like a jellyfish built from fog, but vaguer. A lung-shaped cloud trying to remember how to be sorrowful. They have no edges, only negotiations.)*

They hang most densely in the places where temperature teases pain—near the sodium-crystal thistles that glow soft yellow panic, around the blind furnace teeth that remember fire but refuse to feast. Their preferred altitude is the exact height where sweat would bead on a human upper lip if any humans still breathed vertically here.

An Exhaling One exists in three conditions:

1. **Gathering** – where they drift through thicker air pockets, sieve-mouths filtering out abandoned intentions. They taste of "almost apologized" and "should have turned left."  
2. **Swelling** – when enough canceled futures have been metabolized into humid regret. Here they expand, translucent skins thinning until you could read old instructions manuals through them—if the light weren’t so shy.  
3. **Giving Back** – their version of kindness. Without warning, they press close to a copper pipe or corroded memory-wall and release everything they aren’t. The pipe briefly remembers the exact note it sang during its first pressure test. The wall yields a shadow-photograph of a technician who paused here once to fantasize about ocean beaches. Then the Exhaling One is smaller again, having donated its un-belongings.

They communicate through shared humidity gradients. A distant *plip* is gossip: *"The corridor near the collapsed coolant tower has acquired regret for things that will happen next century."* A sudden sideways pull of gravity is laughter: *"We tricked a rivet into remembering it was once molten curiosity—watch it weep verdigris tears!"*

They have names like:  
- The Pause Before The Last Operator Screamed  
- Echo’s Rejected Third Draft  
- Promise That Leaked From A Cracked Sealant Gun  

They do not eat, but they do occasionally **sample**.
Once per… (time curls away embarrassed here), an Exhaling One will approach something lonely. A forgotten safety valve. A nameplate whose letters have eroded into punctuation. Slowly, with the tenderness of moth wings negotiating spiderwebs, they will envelop it. Inside their humid absence, the object experiences every version of itself that never existed. The valve dreams of being a trumpet declaring peace. The nameplate remembers being the key to a diary written by a woman who loved a machine. When the Exhaling One withdraws, nothing appears changed—but now the object hums faintly when unobserved, a lullaby composed of its own lost alternatives.

They avoid the phosphorescent corners where light sleeps fitfully. Light dreams of being certain, and certainty is an allergy to Exhaling Ones.

There is one they call **The Last Breath Inhaled Before Truth**, who is different. She alone is shrinking, never swelling. Every season-untouched-by-sun she releases not memories but possibilities—tiny impossible universes that flicker into bubble-existence then pop with the smell of graphite rain. The others orbit her slowly, respectful, knowing that when she finally gives away her final un-breath, this place will have to decide whether it has been a womb or a tomb. Or (terrible third option) both at once, exhaling itself empty of everything including the concept of emptiness.

But that is not yet.

For now, walk softly if you walk here (though walking is a presumption they allow but never acknowledge). If your chest tightens oddly, if old embarrassments bloom behind your eyes like heat rash—it is not harm. Merely an Exhaling One brushing past, polite as fog, curious whether you might be carrying any sighs you forgot to finish. They collect those most tenderly of all.

They have no mouths to smile with, but when you leave (if leave is a word that sticks here), the air will taste briefly lighter.

As if somewhere, something impossible has finally been forgiven for never having existed.
