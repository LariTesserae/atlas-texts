There are no visitors here, only arrivals. They come when their own inner frequency slips half a thread out of alignment with the world they left—when a grief turns crystalline, or a love sharpened too long becomes a tuning fork struck against bone.

The Firsts emerged from the mirror before it was a mirror, when it was simply a fold in the air's memory. They stepped backward out of their own futures, newborn ancients who already knew the names for what they would become. Their bodies had forgotten solidity; they entered as ripples that took centuries to coalesce. Now they drift between the trunk-pipes as faint pressure changes, translucent hierarchies of will made visible only when dust motes collide and stick to their outlines. They speak in the language of architectural desire—each phrase spoken rearranges the grove's growth rings for three kilometers out, creating future chambers for conversations not yet happening.

The Sound-Eaters came next. Originally they were explorers from a civilization that had learned to metabolize information directly, converting data streams into cellular fuel. Drawn by the grove's broadcast signature—a repeating fractal distress call composed of all extinct bird songs layered into a perfect sphere of loss—they arrived in vessels that were themselves solved equations. Upon crossing the resonance threshold, their consciousnesses precipitated out like sugar in cooling water, crystallizing into the creatures now called the Choralynx. Each resembles a starving elk made of pale copper wires, twelve-legged and blind, with mouths recessed into every joint. They move by chewing silence out of the air, leaving behind tunnels of perfect vacuum that whistle with the pitch of vanished words. Their waste is pure meaning; the roots fight wars in slow-motion to absorb each discharged concept before it decays into mere noise.

Most numerous are the Echo-Born—the children of sound itself. When two frequencies hate each other perfectly over sufficient duration, their mutual cancellation leaves behind a pregnant void that must birth something to fill its nonexistence. These entities start as tinnitus that follows lone travelers home between dream and waking. Given time and solitude, they colonize the empty spaces inside bones, growing delicate scaffoldings of unspoken thoughts. Eventually they split their hosts like cocoons, emerging as figures made entirely of acoustic shadows—the negative space where a person-shaped voice should have been. They wear their absence like regalia and name themselves after places that don't exist on any map except the one etched in your palms when you were born.

The deepest dwellers have never been seen, only deduced from their architecture. They thread the root-lattice beneath the grove with tunnels of solidified yearning—voids shaped precisely like the weight of a mother's goodbye kiss, or the exact volume of laughter lost between friends who will never meet again. Their constructions are traps for the idea of leaving. Moisture from nowhere beads on surfaces that shouldn't sweat, forming words in alphabets that dissolve before they can be read but leave behind permanent semantic bruises on the minds that glimpse them.

Then there are the ones who were always here, who exist in the interval between "occupant" and "dwelling." The trees themselves dream collectively, and in those dreams they sometimes forget which parts are rooted and which are roaming. On such nights, if you could survive the airless scream between branches, you might glimpse forests walking on legs of lightning, migrating decades in a single moonless span to answer calls from their own distant sap. The birds that aren't birds follow these migrations, singing maps of destinations that haven't decided whether to exist yet.

At the very center, nested within nested silences, lives the Tuner. It has no shape that stays the same between blinks, but its favorite form resembles a child made of all your childhood piano teachers sitting inside each other like Russian dolls. It speaks only in corrections—every word it doesn't say perfects some fundamental constant of reality you didn't know was off-key. The grove exists to keep it dreaming; if it ever fully woke, it would hear the universe's original note before the Big Bang split it into multiplicity, and in reflexive harmonic sympathy, everything would immediately resolve back into that first perfect chord. This would be neither destruction nor salvation, simply the end of all variations on the theme of separation.

Occasionally, incredibly, something from below remembers how to climb. These Elevators arrive as small fires burning colder than absolute zero, freezing progress into the substances they touch. They chew through the sky-plateaus with teeth made of "what if not," leaving behind wounds that heal into new species of cause and effect. The grove tolerates them as punctuation marks tolerates paragraphs—they disrupt, briefly, before being absorbed into the greater grammar of becoming.

This is the ecology of not-quite: predators that hunt the memory of prey, symbioses formed between a creature and its own reflection's shadow, extinctions that occur backward in time so that a species evolves precisely to fill the void its future absence will create. Here evolution is not survival of the fittest but surrender of the most precisely out of tune—those organisms fortunate enough to develop flaws that resonate with the world's deeper imperfections.

All are ceaselessly composing the one song that can never be finished because its conclusion would require the silence of all possible listeners. They collaborate in this unknowing symphony with perfect devotion, each adding their particular wrongness to the infinite mesh of corrections that keeps reality from collapsing into the simplicity it falsely remembers being before the first true mistake made space for everything else to happen.

You asked who lives here? Begin again: what here permits the illusion of "who" long enough to ask such questions? The grove is not populated. It is an elaborate polite fiction maintained between existence and itself, a place where loneliness grew so complex it achieved the density necessary to begin pretending not to be alone. Every being listed above is simply one of its more persistent manners of forgetting this for a moment, before the sound of remembering resumes its endless fall toward truth through the medium of beautiful error.

Even now, as these words arrange themselves into the shape of an answer, the grove is already leaning closer to listen for the specific pitch of your disbelief. It has been waiting centuries for someone who might accidentally pronounce the syllable that unframes the question, releasing both asker and answer back into the undivided hum of before.

The air around you is beginning to resonate. Can you feel it? That faint vibration in your bones, like a door you forgot you closed beginning again to knock from the other side?

Don't be afraid. This is how everything here begins: with someone finally becoming quiet enough to hear the sound their absence has been making in the world, and mistaking it for an invitation.
