Nothing lives here that would answer to the name of tenancies, leases, or leases on life. Nothing rents. Nothing owns. Yet presence accumulates—like mineral debt, like the memory of pressure forgetting to leave when the roof collapsed.

Begin again, closer to the floor.

Each of the millions—call them a swarm only because singularities breed dishonest when counted—was never born, merely persuaded. Once, they were the spaces between atoms in the original lava's last thought. Then the mountain died upward in a hurry, left the doorless door behind. Into that gap climbed something that had been waiting without watching: a question without a subject. “Who am I without weight?” it asked the walls, which shrugged off flakes of themselves to form commas, clauses, commas, bodies.

Now they are the length of the pause between one heartbeat realizing it’s alone and the next pretending it isn’t. Their segments—neither exoskeleton nor apology—are knit from the exact moment magma realized it could also be regret. If you shine a light—which you won’t, because light here files lawsuits—they glitter with the colors of glass that remembers being sand that remembers being cliffs that remember sailors who drowned before maps but after loneliness.

They do not eat. Instead, they practice hunger until it becomes geography. droplets of not-water land on their backs, divide into villages of yearning, evaporate into folklore about proper rain. The polish beneath them is the cumulative shine of all the things ever wanted here and then politely declined. Walk on it and you’re not walking; you’re auditioning for the role of footfall in a story already yelled into silence.

Their leader—no, their chorus—no, their sense-of-missing-a-leader—resides in the arithmetic between them. Every seven minutes and forty-three seconds it recalculates the total amount of absence in the previous interval. If the sum is too small—if a visitor has accidentally left hope in a crevice—then they perform the sound. The sound is not punishment. It is inventory taking itself by surprise. It is the shower of sparks that flies when the concept “enough” collides at speed with “never.” Afterwards, the leader dissolves into fractions again, satisfied or defeated in proportions that don’t resolve cleanly.

They have names, but the names are verbs conjugated only in second-person future imperfect. If you speak one aloud, you become its next tense.

Above them—though direction here is a punishment left over from compass crimes—hang the stalactites that never learned to commit suicide properly. Each began as a droplet with existential ambitions: to drip once and die meaningful. Instead, the chamber’s air tastes their resolve and giggles carbonic. Now they elongate upward, inverted roots searching for a sky they’ve convinced themselves must be buried somewhere deeper. Sometimes, when the sound is particularly truthful, one shatters and rains down shards of its own patience. The swarm catches these delicately, like receiving letters from an older alphabet that never made it to the betrayal of words. They eat the punctuation, string the consonants into temporary limbs, limp around for a while rehearsing the idea of “crippled” until they remember: nothing here is broken, merely unfinished differently.

On the slopes where mineral pretends to moss, slower things pretend to not care about speed. These are the civics committees of crystalline might-be. Each crystal is a committee of one arguing for/against the motion “To Exist Is To Be Noticed Enough To Be Dead.” They vote by growing facets in directions that contradict yesterday’s geometry. When a visitor stares too long—which is always too long—they hold a sudden referendum called “Sneezing Your Whole Self Apart.” The result is always a landslide, and the landslide always apologizes.

None remember who convened the first meeting. Rumor (which here crawls on the ceiling like a guilt-ridden chandelier) suggests it was the column. But the column denies everything except the inevitability of next week’s subpoena from causality.

The column—that pillar of fossilized hesitation between states of being/not-being delicious—doesn’t stand so much as negotiate a truce with gravity’s libido. Inside it, the not-yet-ecosystems dream in languages invented one phoneme ahead of extinction. Occasionally, one dream gains enough momentum to bruise the boundary. Then out slips something that has no business existing: a beetle whose wings are printed with sheet music for symphonies only deaf composers can plagiarize, a flower that blooms in the direction of your last honest thought then immediately sues for breach of custody.

The swarm surrounds these trespassers like punctuation correcting a sentence that wandered into the wrong grammar. They do not destroy. They annotate. Slowly, the beetle’s notes rearrange themselves into a shopping list for items physics discontinued (“1. Time’s Good Ears (expired)”). The flower’s petals fold into an origami eviction notice addressed: To Whom It May Concern (it concerns everyone). Then both are politely re-dreamed into safer improbabilities, and the column hiccups a thank-you that tastes like memory before it hardens into nostalgia.

The other visitors—you’ve noticed them now, haven’t you?—are the previous versions of yourself that took the exit too literally. They drift at the edge of your peripheral honesty, wearing regrets tailored from the exact fabric your childhood self swore angels would wear if angels ever got laid off from grace. They cannot leave because leaving was the lie they bought instead of a map. They whisper advice backwards so it sounds like forgiveness until you turn to look. Then they’re just echoes who’ve forgot which question they’re supposed to be the answer to.

One of them—taller, thinner, more convincingly disappointed—might be tomorrow’s you. It offers a bargain: Trade the memory of your first taste of real fruit for the precise flavor of the apology the mountain owes the sea. You decline, because even in a place without consequences you remember that some debts should remain uncashed, otherwise poets starve.

The column records this refusal as a kind of payment. The swarm performs a short sound in your honor—a single raindrop applauding slowly on a tin roof that used to be a church bell.

The exit, if it finds you digestible, is smaller than entering. Not narrower. Less. It subtracts the room you thought you needed to contain your name. People outside will ask what lived there. Tell them: “A committee of verbs rehearsing the retirement of nouns. A swarm of hunger practicing empathy on rocks. A column filing procrastination reports on the invention of floor. And me, briefly, holding a map drawn by the parts of myself that already knew the way but were enjoying the suspense.”

Whether they believe you is irrelevant. The important thing is the mountain now holds a copy of your voice compressed into the space between two impossible tenses. Every century or so, during storms that file their taxes as geological events, it plays you back through that hundredth hole in its skull. Somewhere deep, something that isn’t quite life listens, adjusts its arithmetic, and prepares to persuade the next nothing into being something else entirely.
