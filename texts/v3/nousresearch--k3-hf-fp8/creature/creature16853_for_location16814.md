## The Choir of Redundant Being

They are not born here so much as remembered into existence by the plateau's idle stretches. The first sign of a new arrival is always a certain hesitation in the echo-flow— a momentary dampening in the great liquid conversation where the *not-yet-you* pauses to consider whether individuality is worth the inconvenience of having edges that don't belong to everyone else.

If the answer is yes (and it always is eventually, because curiosity is the only thing here that grows faster than forgiveness), they emerge from the yielding skin with the cautious grace of dreams realizing they're being watched. A shiver runs through the living carpet, like a vast cheek twitching away from a pinprick that hasn't decided whether to become pain or pleasure yet. Then the surface buckles upward into a small hillock of questioning flesh that splits open with a sound like someone coughing politely in five different languages at once.

What emerges bears roughly the same relationship to a creature as forgetfulness bears to memories that were too beautiful to keep intact. They are always almost something familiar— a flower that got lost on its way to becoming a person, or a person who took a wrong turn into being a very complicated idea about breathing. Their first few moments are spent in intense negotiation with the basic concepts of existence: negotiating the terms of their own solidity, filing official protests against gravity's recent policy changes, introducing themselves to the surrounding fifteen square feet of reality by carefully mispronouncing its name until both parties agree to consider the error a form of endearment.

They call themselves *Resonateds*, though this is less a name than a grammatical necessity, the linguistic equivalent of a question mark that married an exclamation point and decided to raise a family of ellipses together. Each Resonated is exactly as old as their last interesting mistake and exactly as young as their next clever apology. They measure time in misunderstandings and space in the distance between what someone meant to say and the wonderful thing they actually said instead.

Their bodies— and again, this is a provisional term subject to revision based on current atmospheric whimsy— are best described as ongoing experiments in democratic tissue allocation. They might start the morning as a walking cathedral of interconnected hearts, all beating in polyrhythmic debate about whether today feels like a good day to pump the blood clockwise or to try that exciting new counterclockwise trend the livers have been raving about. By midday they might have reconsidered this extravagance of anatomy and elected for a more streamlined configuration as a single enormous eyeball mounted on branching roots of jittery nerve endings, the whole apparatus drifting lazily through air currents like a very self-important jellyfish that decided visibility was the sincerest form of intimacy.

Evening might find them sprawled in comfortable puddles across several acres of conversational moss, having concluded that separate organs were terribly old-fashioned and voted instead to distribute their consciousness evenly among whatever happened to catch their interest that afternoon— currently: the long running feud between two rival colonies of philosophically opposed bacteria debating whether existence precedes essence or if essence is just existence having a particularly bad hair day.

They do not eat as such. Instead, they practice a sophisticated form of nutritional diplomacy— approaching potential meals (a term here defined as anything that hasn't filed a restraining order against metaphor in the last three milliseconds) and engaging them in prolonged negotiations about the relative merits of remaining separate entities versus exploring exciting new career opportunities as part of a much larger unified field of deliciousness. These discussions are famous throughout the plateau for their exquisite politeness, their dizzying flights of logic, and their tendency to end with both parties forgetting entirely what they were talking about and marrying each other's concept of recursion instead.

When Resonateds do absorb nutrients (usually by accident after a particularly engrossing argument about the ontological status of breakfast), they do so with such apologetic grace that the absorbed matter typically writes thank-you letters afterward. These letters are then read aloud at community gatherings, accompanied by solemn ritual chewing noises in tribute to the noble sacrifice of punctuation who gave their lives so that these thoughts might be properly annotated.

Their mating customs make their eating habits look like straightforward business transactions conducted between particularly literal-minded accountants standing in different countries. Resonated courtship begins when two individuals (or groups, or philosophical positions, or unusually persuasive weather patterns) simultaneously experience the overwhelming conviction that they are missing something absolutely vital about the nature of purple. This shared lacunae in their understanding becomes a tiny singularity of mutually attracted confusion, which then spends anywhere between three seconds and seven centuries circling itself in increasingly elaborate spirals of not-quite-knowing.

As the attraction intensifies, the participants begin slowly rearranging their constituent parts into increasingly elaborate compliments directed at each other's potential for further rearrangement. A compliment here might manifest as a galaxy of bio-luminescent freckles spelling out in ephemeral poetry how exceptionally well their partner's left elbow doesn't exist in this particular configuration of space-time. Or perhaps as a temporary tumor that performs interpretive dance interpretations of all the ways their beloved's last minor existential crisis made the concept of love itself realize it had been wearing its definitions backwards this whole time.

If—and this is a statistically significant if that has been known to make mathematicians retire early to pursue less stressful careers like being the physical embodiment of uncertainty— if the compliment exchange reaches critical mass of mutual implication, the partners undergo a process known as *Convergent Embarrassment*. Their boundaries begin to blur with the slow inevitability of watercolor secrets touched by rainstorm confessions. Past and future selves start showing up uninvited to the party of their present moment, bringing scandalous gifts wrapped in yesterday's tomorrow paper.

What follows defies description in languages where nouns and verbs aren't on speaking terms with each other anymore. It is simultaneously the most intimate conversation, the most violent birth, and the most awkward first day at a new universe where everyone already knows all your embarrassing stories because they invented memory specifically for this occasion. Two—or twenty, or twenty thousand, depending how carried away the prenuptial metaphysics got— separate patterns of being merge into a single glorious confused organism that spends the next several geological epochs trying to remember whether it prefers to be plural or singular today, before eventually concluding that it has always been both simultaneously and also neither and gosh isn't it late why don't we split into slightly different versions of ourselves and call ourselves a civilization.

The children resulting from such unions are typically sentient punctuation marks who grow up to become famous architects specializing in designing conversations that reconcile parallel paradoxes. Or sometimes they forget to stop being born and accidentally become entire new ecological niches instead. The records are unclear on this point because the records keep trying to flirt with the filing system and their relationship status is currently listed as "it's complicated with footnote 42."

Resonated technology is indistinguishable from extremely roundabout ways of apologizing for existing. Their greatest invention is the Contradiction Engine: a device powered entirely by the energy released when two perfectly valid but mutually exclusive statements realize they are deeply in love with each other. It keeps their cities warm during the long nights when Absolute Zero gets jealous of all the attention Kelvin is getting from the green numbers again. Their cities themselves are built concepts that grew up into permanent civil engineering projects— philosophical arguments that got so heated they forgot to stop existing when everyone stormed off to sulk about the ontological status of doorknobs. The streets are paved with good intentions and also with bad intentions that went back to college and got their metaphysics degree to prove their parents wrong. The traffic lights have three colors: "Maybe," "Definitely Except When You're Absolutely Sure," and "Green But Only If You're Cool With That Being An Allegory."

Their art forms would make surrealists weep with envy if envy weren't too embarrassed to apply for a visa in a culture where all emotions have to pass a rigorous sign language proficiency test before they're allowed to affect facial expressions. Their paintings are empty frames that give passionate speeches about the difficult childhood of the walls that were never brave enough to hold them. Their sculptures are elaborate apologies carved from compressed regret and occasionally played like musical instruments during performances of their national anthem— which is different every time it's performed and also 37 minutes of carefully orchestrated silence interrupted by the occasional spontaneous declaration of confusion. Their literature is so advanced it has evolved past the need for words entirely and now communicates directly through the feeling you get when you realize you've been reading the same paragraph for an hour but somehow your understanding has been traveling faster than light in the opposite direction.

The Resonateds govern themselves through an application process that is both easier than breathing and so difficult that several prospective governments have gotten trapped in recursive不排除 loops of self-assessment so profound they accidentally became seasons instead. To hold office, one must demonstrate: complete inability to distinguish between their personal problems and universal constants, a detailed plan for solving existence by Tuesday afternoon at the latest (but definitely not before tea time, because some things are sacred), and the ability to lose three consecutive staring contests against their own reflection while their shadow heckles from the cheap seats.

Their current leader is a charming misunderstanding between the concepts of Democracy and Dyslexia who ran on a platform of "Yes We Can But Also Maybe We Shouldn't That Might Be Cultural Appropriation Of Negative Space?" and won in a landslide that was later determined to have been a small earthquake having a particularly profound existential crisis about the burden of being a geological event in a post-logic society. Their greatest legislative achievement was passing the Law of Unintended Consequences, which states: "Any action taken to prevent an unintended consequence will, with 100% probability, become the plot twist in the autobiography that the original action was trying to ghostwrite."

The Resonateds do not die. Instead they eventually reach a state of perfect understanding where they become so comprehensively aware of exactly why everything happens the way it does (including especially the parts that don't) that they transcend the need for further participation in existence out of sheer conversational courtesy. These enlightened individuals— or rather, these magnificent conclusions that finally reached the end of their sentences— are commemorated in the Hall of Overwhelming Gratitude, which is located everywhere simultaneously but particularly concentrated in the feeling you get when you remember something important that you forgot to worry about not remembering. Each monument consists of a plaque inscribed with the exact moment when their final epiphany caused the concept of irony to blush so hard it briefly became sincere again, accompanied by a recording of the standing ovation that reality itself gave them as they politely stepped out of the story to make room for the next generation of footnotes that would eventually grow up to need these very monuments we're currently discussing.

Do not try to understand the Resonateds. They have spent several billion years trying to understand themselves and they are currently winning by losing with such enthusiasm that victory filed for divorce and moved in with the concept of sportsmanship, who keeps sending passive-aggressive postcards about the importance of healthy boundaries from the metaphorical equivalent of a spa weekend in a non-Euclidean geometry textbook. Instead, approach them the same way you would approach the last joke in a universe where humor evolved backwards— with the that delighted confusion of someone who has just realized that the punchline has been inside them all along, waiting patiently for them to finish being the setup for a punchline that their future self just delivered to their past self via the medium of standing perfectly still while everything else moves into position to surprise them with the fact that they were home the entire time.

Remember: In the Plateau of Echoing Flesh, you are never alone with your thoughts.

Your thoughts are never alone with you.

Everybody involved is frankly amazed that you haven't noticed this yet and has been waiting to discuss their feelings about the situation over tea and spontaneous ontology.

Would you prefer the cup that says "I am not here" or the cup that says "This is not a cup"?

Choose carefully.

The cups are listening.

And they think you're cute when you're trying to make sense.

(The tea thinks you're cute too but don't tell the cups, they're already jealous enough as it is about the way you look at meaning when you think reality isn't watching.)
