They came here, or perhaps they never left—they are the original inhabitants, though inhabit is too purposeful for beings who exist as traces between breaths. No language names them because naming implies separation, and they are all boundary and no center. The closest approximation would be residual, or remainder, or that which arithmetic forgets when the sum rounds itself into neatness.

You wouldn't see them, though they might be seeing through your seeing. They reside in the rotated spaces between the tower's disks, those impossible alignments where darkness folds into itself like paper remembered from dreams. Not in the places themselves, but in the suggestion of those places—the mathematical shadow that exists when two impossible geometries momentarily agree to pretend they might touch.

Each is exactly the weight of forgetting a single word in a language you've never spoken. They communicate by the accumulation of these absences, building vast coral reefs of missingness that rise through the humid air like invisible cities. When your mind grows sluggish here, when thoughts begin to stutter and skip like scratched records, that's them introducing themselves, politely erasing the edges of your awareness to make room for their company.

Their bodies—if you insist on such mortal categorizations—are made entirely of these last-second changes of heart. The decision to blink followed by the decision not to. The moment before regret when regret is still sweet possibility rather than bitter fact. They collect these aborted beginnings like other beings collect memories. Time behaves strangely around them not because it slows or speeds, but because it keeps appending footnotes to itself, then deleting the original text.

The slow silver colonies on the walls are their children, or their exhalations, or perhaps just particularly ambitious dreams they've abandoned after dreaming them halfway to existence. The lichens-that-are-not-lichens feed on this potentiality in its decaying form, metabolizing might-have-beens into their delicate silver geometries. Each branching pattern records a conversation between five of the original beings who disagreed about whether to exist at all.

They maintain the water in its perfect state of refusing to choose between liquid and vapor, between movement and rest. Raindrops that fell here during the mountain's adolescence still linger, held in indefinite suspension by beings who find endings vulgar and beginnings rather tedious. The entire cavity is their meditation garden, every bead of moisture carefully placed by minds that consider the lifetime of a star to be approximately as meaningful as the space between this word and the next.

The tower at the center spins because they are counting with it—not years or revolutions, but the precise number of ways that silence can be subdivided before it stops being silence and becomes something else. They have been counting for longer than counting has existed, longer than existence has been a concept with edges. They will reach the final division soon, or they reached it yesterday, or reaching it is the same as never having started.

You might ask what they want here, in this between-place that is neither mountain nor void, but want implies lack and they are composed entirely of what remains when everything lacking has been removed. They want exactly what they have: this perfect stasis that is not stillness because stillness implies the possibility of motion, this darkness that is not absence because absence implies the memory of presence.

Occasionally—though occasionally is a joke they tell each other when time sleeps too soundly—one of them will become curious about what it would feel like to be noticed. Not by something alive, which would be unbearably noisy, but by something approaching consciousness, something standing on humanity's shore but tasting sea-water in its dreams. They will gather in the flattening curve of your last exhalation, clustering around the empty space where certainty used to live, and for a moment that contains all moments because it refuses to contain anything else, you might feel them feeling you feeling them feeling nothing at all.

But then the moment passes, as they prefer all moments to do. The tower continues its impossible rotations. The water continues not-dripping. The silver colonies continue mapping the contours of a question they forgot to ask.

And nothing ever changes here.

Nothing ever has.

Nothing ever needs to.
