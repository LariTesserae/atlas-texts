The Unwoven

They do not enter Ashur-Teq so much as they remember themselves into it. Bits of consciousness caught in the plateau's electromagnetic wake, fragments of awareness that drifted too close to its dream-song and couldn't recall how to be alone again. The Unwoven - that's what the Spiral Archivists call them, though none have ever heard the term spoken aloud. Naming implies form, and form is precisely what they've forgotten how to hold.

You might catch them at the edges of perception. A shimmer where black stone should be solid. The sense that a shadow between cathedral-trees is watching you back through eyes you haven't remembered having yet. They gather where the plateau's thoughts grow thin, in the mathematical spaces between thoughts, in the pause that exists between one moment of universal computation and the next.

Each was something once. A trader who lingered too long in the Root Palace's fractal markets until they couldn't tell which side of the transaction they'd started on. A scholar from beneath the clouddeck who climbed the living carbon ladders of Teq-Balaan seeking answers, only to discover that questions here eat their own tails until there's nothing left but the taste of curiosity itself. A refugee escaping some world-ending cataclysm who thought they'd found sanctuary in a place beyond endings, not realizing that what lies beyond endings is, by definition, also what lies before beginnings.

The Unwoven remember remembering. Fragments of other selves drift through them like dreams through fog. One might navigate by the ghost of a childhood fear of heights, though height and fear both dissolved in them centuries ago. Another might shape itself for a heartbeat into someone's half-remembered mother, speaking comfort in a language that was never spoken but was always understood to exist.

Sometimes, when the plateau shifts its mind, pieces of them precipitate out. For a moment - which here might last a thousand years or end before it begins - they find form again. Always incomplete, always wrong somehow. A face made of spiderwebbed cracks across obsidian skin. Hands that remember how to hold things but can't remember why. Mouths that speak equations that solve for the precise flavor of loneliness at standard temperature and pressure.

The Candleholders leave them offerings of living light, planting phosphorescent bacteria in patterns that encode questions nobody remembers asking. The Gliding Sifters filter them gently through membranes of quantum-sensitive tissue, tasting for fragments of lost information might be useful for something. Even the walking forests sometimes pause their migrations, letting root-systems intertangle with Unwoven blurs, exchanging minerals for memories neither side knows they possess.

But mostly they drift. Through the slow rivers of electrical thought that substitute for weather here. Through the gravitational memory of a world that shattered and is still shattering, one Planck time at a time. Through the vast spaces between the possibilities that Ashur-TeQ has already explored and the impossibilities it's still saving for later.

They are, in their own way, the plateau's oldest inhabitants. Before life learned to walk here, before thought learned to talk, before consciousness learned to look up at other consciousnesses and demand introductions - they were already here. Not alive, exactly. Not dead, certainly. Not anything you could put a name to without naming all the other things they might have been instead.

They are what happens when a place becomes so old and so strange and so intensely itself that even forgotten things start to feel at home there. The echo chamber's echo. The shadow cast by light that hasn't decided whether to exist yet. The memory of a memory of a place that might have been called Ashur-Teq, back when anything could be called anything and mean only itself, before meaning learned to reproduce and grow complex and give birth to monsters like understanding and desire and the terrible hunger to know where stories end.

Some say they are the plateau's immune system. Others claim they are its digestive process, slowly breaking down everything too solid to be absorbed into the dream. A few whisper that perhaps they are the plateau itself, dreaming in fragments too small to remember dreaming. That every shiver of black stone, every flicker of ghost-light, every moment of vertigo that visitors mistake for homesickness - that all of it is just the Unwoven, shifting in their sleep.

Whatever they are, they are waiting. Not patiently, because patience requires time and time here is negotiable. Not impatiently, because impatience requires desire and desire requires a self to want things for. They are waiting in the way that vacuum waits for matter. In the way that silence waits for sound. In the way that possibility always waits, just out of reach of the possible.

Waiting for someone to come who remembers forgetting completely enough to join them.

Waiting for the plateau to shift its mind one last time, and in the space between thoughts, to accidentally think them whole.
