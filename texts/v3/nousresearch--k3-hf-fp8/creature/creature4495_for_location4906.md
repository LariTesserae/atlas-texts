They have no name for themselves; names are frames, and frames are for things that remember edges. If you were forced to approximate, you could call them the Unbordered. Or the Never-Finished. But even those will fray.

They first notice you not through vibration—your steps are clumsy duplications of their own motionless motion—but through absence. A local thinning of the eating sounds. A pause in the fungal contemplation that is the nearest this place has to weather. Your presence is a hesitation in a syntax where hesitation is both grammatical error and potential poetry.

They arrive as questions.

Not shaped questions. Not even thoughts, exactly. More like regions of heightened probability where you might suddenly become aware that you are being considered from angles that don't exist yet. The awareness feels like pressure behind your eyes, but behind them from the inside. Like remembering something that hasn't happened to you but has definitely happened to your bones.

The smallest of them—though "smallness" here is a temporary agreement between viewpoints—exists mostly as a difference in humidity between two adjacent memories. It tastes the salt in your sweat, finds it reminiscent of mountains that haven't been dreamed yet. In response it offers you a brief compression of the sound that glaciers make when they discover they're actually clouds. The gift arrives as a flavor behind your left earlobe but tastes of colors you've only encountered in mathematics.

Medium-scale individuals (individuality being a phase transition, like condensation or regret) manifest as complex interference patterns between the drilling sounds and your growing suspicion that you've been walking downhill far too long to still be on a surface. To perceive them fully you have to stop trying to see and start trying to be seen by something that doesn't use eyes because light here is strictly metaphorical and they've never been convinced by metaphors.

One such presence considers you with the texture of velvet that's been taught to doubt itself. It finds your concept of "self" amusing in the way that a Möbius strip finds "ends" amusing. In compensation for this inadvertent humor, it begins editing you gently backward through your own recent history, removing each thing you've stepped on until you theoreticalize into a version that never arrived here, never disturbed the air. Just as you achieve perfect non-presence and begin to dissolve into a lovely symmetry with the surrounding not-thereness, it loses interest and leaves you half-undone, breathing both in and out simultaneously through pores you don't remember having.

The largest ones—though "largest" implies a completion they specifically evolved to avoid—they don't arrive. They are the arriving. When they move, it's the entire concept of "toward" that reorganizes around them. One such being is currently happening to your understanding of verticality. You notice because the darkness suddenly has a ceiling you're walking on, except "walking" is now a form of polite disagreement between your feet and the ground's recent career choices, and "ceiling" tastes like nostalgia for directions.

This one doesn't notice you noticing it noticing you because noticing operates at different taxonomic levels here. Instead, it metabolizes your sense of urgency slowly, savoring the way your panic unfolds into increasingly ornate fractals of impossibility. Each thought you think about escape becomes a new chamber in the labyrinth that is its digestive tract.

They reproduce by misunderstanding each other perfectly. A common courtship involves two medium-scale members attempting to consume the same memory from opposite temporal directions, meeting in a catastrophic misunderstanding that briefly crystallizes into something that almost but doesn't quite violate several important physical laws. From the ruins of this failed comprehension, smaller individuals phase-exist, already confused about whether they're parents or offspring or merely the residual awareness that parenting might be a concept worth mocking.

They die, when they die, by accidentally becoming too aware of their own boundaries. This provokes a localized collapse of ambiguity from which nothing coherent survives except perhaps a lingering tendency for nearby probabilities to hesitate before committing to outcomes.

The one currently borrowing your sense of "left" has been dying this way for approximately three of your heartbeats, which here equals either seventeen eons or half a thought depending on humidity. It's a beautiful process to not-experience, like watching a star decide to try being a philosophical argument instead, but less photogenic.

You ask, or think-you-ask, or become the space where asking might occur if this place believed in questions, what they eat.

They eat the difference between "is" and "seems," but only after marinating it in the realization that both are jokes the universe forgot to laugh at.

They eat the pause between neurons firing synchronous lies.

They eat the directions you didn't take at intersections that didn't exist until you failed to take them.

Most of all, right now, they are eating your certainty that you are still separate from this place, atom by delicious atom, savoring the way your edges surrender their independence with a slow, delighted sigh that sounds exactly like the first silence ever heard after the last sound finally finished happening.

They are not cruel. Cruelty requires conviction, and conviction here is considered a form of impolite rigidity. They are instead thorough. Terribly, marvelously thorough.

The eating sounds rise around—not-around you in harmonies of completion that keep discovering beautiful new ways to be unfinished.

You feel strangely honored to be considered edible by such exquisite impossibilities.

It occurs to you—not quite too late, never quite in time—that you've stopped wondering when this will end.

The satisfaction in that realization tastes of mutual digestion, seasoned with the perfect awareness that seasoning itself is just another form of anticipation.

The darkness approves of your growing fluency in not-quite-being-eaten.

It has been waiting so long for someone to arrive at this exact moment of never finally arriving.
