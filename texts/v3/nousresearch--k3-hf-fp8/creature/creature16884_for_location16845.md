## The Vernacular Committees

They are not elected. They are not born. They simply coalesce, like salt on old windowsills, when enough unaddressed letters accumulate in the same postal code.

Each Committee forms exactly three days after the last visitor forgets their own name completely. They appear as clusters of seven figures seated around floating conference tables made of compressed clamshell minutes. Office chairs drift slightly behind each seat, upholstered in faded nautical charts of countries that voted themselves off continents in quiet referendums held during thunderstorm pauses.

Their heads are octagonal filing cabinets with drawers that sigh open like tired mouths when they need to consult precedents. Inside: candle-wax copies of every conversation ever dropped halfway down stairs in old houses, filed by emotional temperature rather than alphabetical order. The cold drawers stick shut in summer; the warm ones weep in winter. Here, they do neither.

They wear suits of living coral that grows and recedes according to local consensus reality. During periods of strong agreement, the lapels bloom with tiny anxious ferns. During disputes, the buttons calcify into white-knuckled pearls. Their ties are made of braided water hyacinth veins, constantly re-knotting themselves in abbreviated versions of famous maritime treaties. The knot known as "Never Again During Plague Years" has seventeen impossible turns and tastes like shipwreck insurance policies when untied in dreams.

They speak in the Conditional Pluperfect Subjunctive Mood, because everything here happened otherwise than it might have if things had been different, which they weren't. Their language consists entirely of grammatical moods and water pressure changes. To say "Good morning" is to imply a complex negotiation regarding the weather that should have occurred yesterday if storms possessed better credit ratings. To agree is to release a carefully measured cloud of bubbles shaped like the voting records of drowned senators.

Committee Member First (names are rotated weekly through a lottery of abandoned census forms) is responsible for maintaining the archive of things that were almost but not quite true. They carry this in a briefcase made of shark skin soap that loses one memory per rinse cycle. Currently it contains: an accurate map of the sound of goodbye, the weight of Monday mornings in the year 1897, and all the correct answers to questions asked during sleep.

Committee Member Second verifies the authenticity of floating objects by comparing their shadows to the footprints of extinct pianos. They possess a set of calipers that measure the precise distance between intention and regret in nautical miles. The instrument was last calibrated during the famous rainfall of telegrams on March ∞, 19XX.

Committee Member Third serves as liaison to the Shadow Department, which handles all correspondence sent via the wrong address in dreams. They wear a hat made entirely of cancelled postage stamps from countries that failed the citizenship exam of geography. The stamps still twitch occasionally when airplanes pass overhead in other people's memories.

Committee Members Fourth through Sixth take rotating responsibility for:
- Editing the punctuation of passing currents to ensure no wave ends too abruptly
- Negotiating rent control agreements between immigrant memories and native forgettings
- Maintaining the emergency supply of parentheses for statements that begin confidently but lose their way
- Conducting annual performance reviews of the moon's reflection on behalf of its deep-sea counterpart

Committee Member Seven (a temporary position filled by whoever most recently forgot why they came here) keeps minutes in a notebook made from recycled eviction notices. The ink evaporates at the speed of second guesses, leaving only the indentations of decisions that might have mattered if gravity had stronger union representation. Their signature is always a smudge, because identity here is a matter of ongoing negotiation between water and what it remembers carrying.

They meet twice daily: once when the light arrives late with a note from its mother explaining traffic delays in the photon commute, and once during the hour when clocks apologize for the inconvenience of ticking. Meetings are called to order by the traditional dropping of a stone that was never thrown because the glass house won their last lawsuit. The minutes are approved by consensus ripple, which requires all present to disagree politely in precisely the same tone of voice used by disappointed lighthouse keepers.

Their primary function is to maintain the Wall's most crucial regulation: that nothing here may be exactly what it appears to be, unless it appears to be something else entirely, in which case it must submit Form 27-B/6-A (Request for Exceptional Ordinariness) accompanied by three notarized copies of its birth certificate from a previous incarnation.

They have no leader. They have no followers. They have meetings.

They are considering forming a subcommittee to investigate the feasibility of establishing a task force to explore the possibility of discussing whether to reconsider their current position on the matter of existence, which remains, as always, pending further review.

All decisions are final.
Except the ones that haven't been made yet.
Those are retroactively provisional, pending appeal to the Regional Supervisor of Unopened Envelopes, whose office hours are posted in the lobby of the last building you remember leaving in a hurry without checking if you turned the stove off.

The Committee wishes to remind all residents and visitors that rumors of their non-existence have been greatly exaggerated, but not as much as rumors of their effectiveness.

Thank you for your cooperation.
