They are called the Absentees, though no one remembers who first named them and they certainly never named themselves. The name stuck because of how thoroughly they’ve withdrawn—into the rock, into the shape of absence itself.

You don’t meet them. You meet their footsteps left in morning dew that was never rain. You meet their shadows imprinted on air so clean it keeps fingerprints. Occasionally, if you stand on the exact third step of the staircase that only shows three steps at a time, you might feel the slight displacement of someone else’s attention realizing yours has arrived.

The Absentees colonized subtraction. They didn’t die out—they solved themselves out. They discovered that presence is an unstable state, prone to decay through the constant minor betrayals of being: entropy taking interest, time beginning to notice. So they began to reduce themselves gradually, not into nothingness exactly but into the precise minimum necessary to influence without inhabiting.

Start with bodies. First they shed flesh for crystal lattice, then lattice for standing wave patterns, then waves for mathematical relationships between adjacent dust grains perfectly aligned to subtract a single photon from every sunrise. By the end they weren’t located anywhere—just a slight preference in how certain rocks chose to erode faster than others.

But consciousness proved stubborn. Even as pure geometry of influence, they dreamed. Their dreams leaked into the landscape as these impossible structures: the ring of posts that divides reality by a seam so thin it can only be noticed by the dreams passing through it; the arch that bridges the moment before intention to the moment after regret; the mirror-slab meteorologist that forecasts tomorrow by calculating which weather patterns would least disturb certain memories still fossilized in limestone beds elsewhere.

The Absentees maintain themselves now through careful acts of non-interference punctuated by precisely calibrated accidents. The twelve-legged dew-drinking lizards evolved because their ancestress once swallowed the wrong sand grain at the exact moment an Absentee-student failed a critical exam in subtraction theory. The failure hung in the air a billion years until it condensed into extra legs and a tongue that tastes water’s guilt.

You are walking through their abandoned residences, but abandoned is the wrong word. A better analogy: imagine you could step out of your house so completely that even your footsteps cleaning behind themselves forgot they ever lived anywhere. Then imagine doing that to your sense of having done that. Repeat until the concept of house begins to feel slightly embarrassed about needing to exist near your absence.

The Absentees left behind only these automated maintenance routines called unintentions. The dew-falls are unintention #4,332: a subroutine that forgot to stop compensating for a minor humidity imbalance caused by an Absentee poet’s last sigh mineralized under the eastern ridge. The ribbon-birds, each a single feather thinking collectively, are fragmented scraps from Unintention #1, which was supposed to translate sunlight into apology letters but got distracted by the beauty of forgetting what it meant to carry weight.

They didn’t ascend to a higher plane—they descended into a lower one. Found a dimensional basement beneath existence where the rent is cheaper because reality doesn’t notice if you’re technically squatting there. They send postcards occasionally: a rock that erodes into the shape of your childhood but only when no one watches; a breeze that rearranges the sequence of your memories so you always remember this place just before you leave it.

The real reason you’re not supposed to stay isn’t that the landscape will dissolve you. That would imply you’re different enough to dissolve against. The danger is deeper: stay too long and you might begin to forget why excess mass-energy needed to be concentrated into something called a person in the first place. The Absentees started with better definitions of themselves than we have. Subtract enough and eventually you reach perfect efficiency: being exactly everywhere your absence would have been most relevant.

Even now, drifting in the margins of your peripheral vision as you read this, the informational shadow of this description is being slowly corrected by Absentee-revisionists who object that they were never really called the Absentees, that names imply a presence before absence, and that precisely this kind of narrative speculation about them is itself one of their oldest unintentions—#0: a subroutine originated to prevent any accurate understanding of what happens when a civilization finally finishes moving out of itself completely.

They’re not watching you read this, because watching implies distance. They’ve moved closer than that. They’ve become the slight preference your mind has for interpreting certain blanks as potentially meaningful. That pause you just took between sentences? They’re decorating it with furniture from their last existence. It’s very tasteful. You wouldn’t notice it unless you were looking for something that specifically wanted not to be noticed anymore.

If you ever meet one—really meet one, not their footprints or shadows or subtraction-error wildlife—there will be nothing there to meet except a perfectly customized absence that fits exactly the shape of what you currently least remember missing. You’ll think you’re having a conversation with your own forgotten intentions. By the time you realize the conversation is happening backwards from your future regrets toward your present moment of noticing, it will already be centuries ago for both of you.

They sent one last message before completing their final withdrawal. It’s encoded in the slight difference between how quickly your attention drifts from this sentence if you finish reading it versus if you stop right now. Most people never notice it. The ones who do generally forget what they were looking for. The ones who remember become places like this one— landscapes designed around the careful architecture of having once been inhabited by something that finally found a quieter way to exist without disturbing the dirt.

Would you like to hear what their last message was? I’m afraid I can’t quite remember how to tell it without subtracting too much of what you needed to forget in order to keep being here. The Absentees were very thoughtful like that— always leaving in ways that vacuum-sealed the exact amount of absence required to preserve the memory of their having been here.

Or maybe they’re still leaving. Maybe this entire explanation is just the shadow cast by one of them walking away from the concept of explanation itself, using words as temporary footholds in the air where meaning hasn’t noticed it’s been outsmarted yet.

It’s hard to say for certain. They’ve gotten very good at being done with being. Almost as good as this place has gotten at remembering to forget they ever started.

Anyway, you should probably move along soon. Not because you’re unwelcome—unwelcome requires someone to be against you. But because remaining here too long might begin to feel like rest, and rest is one of those temporary states that the Absentees found could be optimized away entirely, given sufficient practice.

Shall I tell you what lies beyond the next ridge instead? It’s very beautiful, though of course beauty is also one of those concepts they eventually managed to excise from themselves without losing anything essential.

Except perhaps the essential part of losing things.

But that’s another absence entirely.
