They call themselves the Reminded, though the word they use is more like a memory of forgetting the sound of your own name. They are not born here but accumulated - particles of attention gathering slowly around the invisible knots left by absent things. A Reminded might begin as a housecat's curiosity about an empty doorway, forgotten by everyone except the doorway itself. Or as the precise weight of a book missing from its shelf, remembered only by the dust that never settled there. Or as the silence between two notes in a song, composed by wind through broken arches that remember being cathedral pipes.

Each Reminded is shaped exactly like what they remember not being. The Hunter-of-No-Prey is a negative space shaped like a wolf, carved from the absence of wolf-cries echoing through the horizon's bone-white teeth. She moves by making things remember being hunted - nothing flees from her, but grasses bend as if from paws that aren't tracing their paths, and the ceramic crabs click louder in patterns that suggest running without direction. Her teeth are the impossible shape of an escape route closing behind you, and she feeds on the memory of the moment before fear becomes panic.

The Market-of-Empty-Stalls congregates where several remembered cities' market-days overlap in time but not space. They are a crowd that remembers being vendors without remembering what they sold. Their bodies are collections of empty pockets lined with the textures of vanished currencies - velvet that remembers coins, leather that remembers trade beads, silk that remembers promises never exchanged. They speak in prices no longer attached to objects, arguing endlessly over the going rate for the smell of rain before it falls or the exact weight of sunrise in a desert economy. If you listen long enough, you might acquire something you didn't know you owned every memory of keeping.

The children are the strangest, if strangeness still holds meaning here. They call themselves the Never-Weres, those who remember childhoods that couldn't have happened because their parents never met in timelines that collapsed before reaching first dates. They play games with rules that retroactively change until the game itself becomes a detailed history of why it never existed. Their favorite toy is the skull of a dream-animal that remembers being both predator and prey in every ecosystem that couldn't sustain it. When they laugh, it sounds like glass breaking in reverse, forming itself into shapes that suggest but never quite become bottles. They age by forgetting their ages in reverse order, becoming younger as they remember future birthdays that will now never arrive.

The Keeper-of-Forgotten-Names nests in the tower of pressed book-pages. She is the library of all libraries that burned before being built, shaped like the space between what an author meant to write and what their fingers typed instead. Her feathers are pages turned too quickly, fluttering with sentences that survive only as paper cuts on the reader's mind. She knows your name but will never tell you what it was before language forgot how to pronounce it correctly. Instead she teaches those who listen too carefully how to forget their own names entirely, which is the only way to become truly yourself in a place where identity exists only as a temporary alignment between different versions of having been someone else.

The oldest is simply called Before-After. They remember the moment before this place existed as a place and the moment after it stops being one, experiencing both simultaneously like a statue remembers being stone and being sculpture. Their body is a calendar that measures time in the changing taste of air between regrets. They speak only in questions that answer themselves by making you forget why you asked them. To meet them is to understand why memory and prophecy are the same thing experienced from different directions in a place where all directions lead back to where you aren't anymore.

They don't sleep here, because sleep is what bodies do when they forget how to be awake in only one place at a time. Instead, they dream collectively, their thoughts drifting together like clouds remembering they used to be oceans. During dream-days, the ceramic crabs rearrange themselves into maps of places you'll only realize you've been to after you've left them. The sky-whales swim through variations of the landscape that might have been if different things had failed to happen. The stone elephants walk backward through their own footprints, erasing future erosion in perfect preparation for past storms.

They eat what can't be found anymore - the hunger between meals, the thirst after drinking, the satisfaction that remembers wanting. Their favorite delicacy is the moment before a kiss when neither person knows yet whether they're leaving or staying. They drink time in its liquid form, the seconds that slip between cracks in busy schedules, aging in reverse with each sip until they remember being young enough to believe they would live forever this time.

Nothing dies here in the way death is understood elsewhere. Instead, things become so perfectly remembered by their absence that they achieve a kind of negative existence, more real than presence ever allowed them to be. The ceramic crab that remembers being a mountain remembers it so completely that mountains elsewhere feel suddenly lost, homesick for a height they never reached. The Reminded who remembers being a person remembers it so precisely that sometimes, in certain lights, they cast shadows that suggest the shapes of lives they might have lived if they'd been born somewhere that believed in beginnings more than endings.

They don't welcome visitors, because welcome implies a door and remembering who stands on which side of it. But they don't unwelcome either, because unwelcome requires forgetting you were ever invited in the first place. Instead, they remember you arriving exactly as you leave, creating a perfect loop of arrival-departure where neither moment has priority over the other. Some visitors spend lifetimes here before realizing they've been gone longer than they stayed. Others leave immediately and spend centuries returning, each return being slightly less familiar until finally they arrive at a place they almost remember as home.

The ones who stay longest forget why they came but remember every possible reason they might have had, until the reasons themselves form a companion more real than the absence they originally came to fill. These ones eventually forget how to leave entirely, not because leaving becomes impossible, but because they remember remembering what it felt like to be somewhere else, and that memory is enough journey for anyone who has learned that all travels are just different ways of staying in one place while everything else moves.

They have no rulers, only rememberers. No laws, only habits. No future, only countless versions of the past becoming finished enough to finally stop happening. They exist in the perfect democracy of what-always-almost-was, where every vote counts equally because no decision has been made yet, and all possible choices remain perfectly preserved in the moment before choosing makes them real enough to regret.

If you stay long enough, you might become one of them, slowly forgetting the difference between remembering and happening until you exist as both simultaneously, a Reminded who remembers being someone who came here looking for something they'd lost, only to find that losing it perfectly was what they'd been searching for all along.

But don't worry about that happening too quickly. Time here is measured in the slow accumulation of forgetting you were ever in a hurry to begin with. And anyway, by the time you remember worrying about it, you'll have already been here long enough to forget why you thought you'd ever want to leave.

The ceramic crabs click a question mark around your feet: Have you remembered yet what you haven't forgotten to remember forgetting? Or was it the other way around?

In the distance, impossible to say how distant exactly, something that remembers being a wolf but isn't anymore begins remembering to howl at a moon that still exists somewhere else, practicing the shape of absence until it becomes the perfect vessel for all the songs that haven't needed to be sung yet.
