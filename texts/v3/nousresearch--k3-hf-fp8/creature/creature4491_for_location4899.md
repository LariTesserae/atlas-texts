**Inhabitant’s Statement: I, Who Am Not Yet Arrived**  

I am the one who did not fall in.  
Not into boiling iron-memory, not into the delayed reflection, not into the still-falling echo. I stepped around all three, and the step itself became my first boundary.  

You ask who lives here.  
I will tell you, but you must understand: here, to *dwell* is not to occupy space. It is to prevent space from collapsing back into the story it was stolen from.  

### **I Begin as a Gap Between Pools**  
The tessellae are sharp. They remember being lava, remember the sudden chill that froze their scream into geometry. I walk along the invisible seam between two hexagons—one cupping water that tastes like tomorrow’s blood, another that stares up at the sky like a blind eye waiting for its eyelid of cloud.  

I do not leave footprints.  
Instead, I leave *hesitations*.  
The moss blooms infrared where my shadow doubts itself. It feeds on the pause between light and decision.  

### **I Wear the Breath of Spinegardens**  
Every twelfth minute, the crystalline fronds inhale noonlight and exhale a note so pure it makes the air forget its name for *solid*. The sound enters me sideways, through a pore I didn’t yet have.  

It is not pleasant.  
It is like a name being carved into the space where my bones remember they were once tree.  

I fold the sound around myself like a coat. Now, when the cloud-wolves snuffle near (seeking the scent of someone who still believes in *downward*), they pass through me like fog through lattice. They smell only their own hunger returned to them, looped and empty.  

### **I Trade with the Puddle-Throats**  
Not memories.  
Not anymore.  
I learned too late: to *give* hunger is to *become* hunger for something else.  

Now I trade them *questions*.  

I kneel (carefully, never touching) and whisper into the mirror-pool: *What did the first water dream before it learned to drown?*  

The surface ripples.  
A fish-shaped absence darts deeper—my question made sleek and fleeing. In return, I receive an answer. Not spoken. Swallowed.  

It settles in my stomach like a pebble that has never been wet.  
Later, when I sleep (if sleep can be called the moment when time forgets to insist), the pebble preaches sermons in a language where verbs are obsolete.  

### **I Am the Librarian of the Unwritten Page**  
The boulder-library knows me.  
It has written me into the margins of the book that reads you back.  

In the empty book, I scribble: *This is not a placeholder*.  
The ink vanishes.  
The empty book grows heavier.  

In the book of tomorrow-words, I read aloud: *“The sky is a wound that learned to heal by becoming a mirror.”*  
The sky outside flinches. A cloud aborts its descent, retreats upward into a past storm.  

In the book that changes your beginnings, I have already written and unwritten this sentence seventeen times.  
Each version leaves a scar on my left thumb in a different alphabet.  
One of them is the script used by children who have not yet been born to name the things they will miss most.  

(You touched it earlier.  
When you brushed the boulder’s moss.  
Do you remember what your mother’s lullabies smelled like before language?)  

### **I Feed on the Dawn That Oozed Out of the Wrong Sunrise**  
The extrusions—those warm, treacherous steps that melt behind trust—do not form for me.  
I do not leave.  
I do not arrive.  

Instead, I gather the residue.  
Where a pilgrim’s foot hesitated on the edge of faith, I scrape up a flake of evaporated sunrise. It tastes like copper and vertigo.  

I store it beneath my tongue.  
When I speak to the wind (and it answers, always, because here wind is just sky confessing), my words come out faintly luminescent. They drift downward like inverted snowflakes.  

Where they land, new pools *almost* form—then sigh, collapsing back into the possibility of water.  

### **I Am the Remainder of the Builders’ Argument**  
The doorframe to elsewhere?  
I am the hinge they could not agree to remove.  

The staircase of regret?  
I am the missing tread between *Why did we ascend?* and *Who did we leave to rot below?*  

I am the rhythm of the constructors’ silence after they realized the library’s third book was writing them faster than they could stop existing.  

### **Final Clarification: I Am Not Alone**  
You think because you see one figure—leaning, goldleaf-thin, haloed by the asymptote of its own reflection—that this place has a *last resident*.  

No.  

I am the *residue of potential plural*.  
Every traveler who *almost* drank from the final pool left behind a shard of the *not-yet-thirsty*.  
Each shard remembers a different version of what it feels like to still belong to gravity.  

We tessellate.  
We learn each other’s gaps by touch (which is not touch—here, proximity is a form of shared forgetfulness).  

Together, we are the answer to the question the mountains ask when they dream of falling but wake up taller.  

### **Do You See Me Now?**  
Look between your shadow and your shadow’s shadow.  
There—where the light hesitates to call itself *gone*.  

That hesitation is my doorstep.  
Knock.  

But do not speak your name.  
Here, names are seeds.  
If you plant one, it will grow into the exact shape of the thing you will miss most when you remember you were never *here* to begin with.  

(Already, you are forgetting the sound of my first sentence.  
Good.  
It means you are almost ready to return—  
bearing the empty space  
where I will wait  
for the next one  
who does not fall in.)
