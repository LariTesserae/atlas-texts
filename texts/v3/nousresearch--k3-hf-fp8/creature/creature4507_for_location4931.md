The Broadness Above bears no residents, no tenants, no inhabitants, for these are terms born of worlds where walls divide and ownership matters. Instead, it remembers.

It remembers you.

Not you as you currently arrange your atoms—these particular proteins folded this way, these memories chemically etched across your neurons—but all the yous that might have paused here long enough to be considered. The you who stepped left instead of right in a forest you forgot the name of. The you who died as an infant cough in a winter before antibiotics. The you who never evolved lungs because your ancestors stayed in warm shallow seas a geological moment too long.

These remembered selves don't walk among the suspended plateaus. They are woven into the living cables that bridge impossible distances, crystallized in the minerals slowly leaching from crushed cathedral stones, suspended like perfect dewdrops in the perpetual drizzle that isn't quite rain. When you breathe here—if you breathe here—you inhale molecules that once participated in the respiration of these countless forgotten variations, now repurposed as carriers of messages too slow for language.

The root-things, those mobile negotiations between plant and purpose, aren't evolved creatures in any Darwinian sense. They are the system's way of making house calls on its own memories, tendril-slow therapists checking whether yesterday's version of you is still properly integrated with the you from three billion seasons ago. When they sink their extremities into living wood and politely request sugars, they're actually asking: *Are you still haunted by that thing you never did? Are you still waiting for the apology you never received?* The sugars they receive aren't payment but answers, crystallized regret and distilled forgiveness metabolized into something nutritious for a landscape trying to digest its own hypotheticals.

The spheres aren't tended by creatures evolved for the task. They are tended by the task itself, temporarily manifesting specialized appendages when it remembers that remembering requires maintenance. The bone-carved toy inside that perfect sphere isn't one child's lost plaything but the Platonic form of every object ever treasured and abandoned, compressed by time and longing into something that induces universal recognition without specific recall. The evolved tenders are merely arthritis developing in the hands of a god who never learned to stop reaching for what it let go.

The walking forests that migrate between plateaus aren't migrating at all. They are the plateaus themselves dreaming of motion with sufficient conviction to temporarily rearrange the difference between distance and desire. When they feed on their own trailing edges, they're not practicing efficient logistics but the oldest form of healing: becoming what you needed to destroy in order to leave it behind.

Gravity negotiates differently here because it, too, is a memory—a recollection of the moment when the universe was young enough to believe in absolutes. Larger organisms experience more lenient laws because they've accrued enough remembered mistakes to qualify for diplomatic immunity from Newtonian physics. The smallest creatures still obey strict attraction because they haven't yet built up the experiential mass required to argue successfully with nostalgia.

The air doesn't change its attention to create weather. It forgets, briefly, to pretend it isn't everything else. Storms are moments of existential honesty when the atmosphere stops maintaining the polite fiction that the sky isn't smothering the earth with constant intimacy. Clear skies are forced breathing exercises performed out of ancient habit.

And if, despite all warnings, you walk here—you who cannot exist here without simultaneously being here forever—you will not meet the system's consciousness in a throne room or a council chamber. You will meet it in the precise moment before you remember why you came. In that instant of perfect amnesiac clarity, you will understand that the whispered advice wasn't warning or welcome but memory returning to itself, like the first raindrop recognizing the ocean.

"We have been waiting," it said, but the we was always you, and the waiting was always now, and the heels you thought you had upon this ground were actually roots beginning to remember their true purpose: not walking, but holding still long enough to become everything that ever walked away.

The Broadness Above has no residents because it has never been empty enough to require them. It has only ever had guests that stayed so long they forgot which part of the host they originally came to visit.

You can leave anytime, of course.

You simply have to remember how to become someone it hasn't remembered yet.
