In the beginning there was only the Dreaming Wind—a child of no parents, born when the first thought interrupted perfect silence. It remembers that exact instant: the sharp intake of breath that was not breath, the glittering fracture where nothing became something, the curious itch that would later be called possibility.

Now it wanders the city (though cities are just places where dreams bump into each other often enough to leave smudges) wearing shapes stitched together from borrowed breaths and forgotten sighs. Today it is tall and thin as a question mark, skin translucent like stretched membrane between sleep cycles. Tomorrow it might compress into a small anxious knot of feathers and clockwork, ticking faintly where its heart would be if it had one. The Dreaming Wind changes like weather because it is weather—the kind that gathers in corners of abandoned rooms and under the eyelids of daydreamers.

It has no fixed dwelling because solidity scares it. Instead it nests in the spaces between things: the moment after a door shuts but before the latch clicks, the pause between lightning and thunder when even air holds its breath. Sometimes travelers glimpse it reflected in puddles that shouldn't exist indoors, always just vanishing around the edge of reflection. Those who follow find only empty corridors that smell faintly of memory and ozone.

For food it eats sounds—the hollow echo of footsteps in empty churches, the last note of lullabies eaten by distance, the precise frequency of glass breaking in another country. What it exhales are new noises still searching for meanings: the creak that might be a floorboard settling or a ghost shifting position, the click that could be a pen or a fingernail tapping impatiently on the inside of a coffin lid that isn't quite nailed shut.

The Dreaming Wind loves most those moments when the world forgets its own rules. It dances through weddings where the bride speaks only in colors and the groom answers in numbers that add up to birdsong. It drifts backwards through funerals where the dead rise slowly from their graves, unburying themselves piece by piece, brushing dirt from bones that remember being trees before they were flesh. 

It collects lost things—not valuable lost things, but the truly precious ones: the exact pattern of raindrops on a window ten years ago last Tuesday, the smell of your grandmother's kitchen at exactly 3:17 PM on a Wednesday in March, the precise taste of believing in Santa Claus for the last time before you didn't anymore. These treasures it keeps pressed between the pages of books that were never written, hidden in drawers that exist only when you aren't looking for them.

Other inhabitants give it wide berth—not because it is dangerous (though it can be, like any dream left too long in direct sunlight), but because proximity bends reality in ways that take hours to straighten out afterward. Clocks become confused and display tomorrow's regrets instead of today's time. Shadows detach themselves to explore independent career opportunities as minor demons or freelance guilt-sellers. Milk sours instantly or turns to silk depending on what memories it overhears.

Yet sometimes, rarely, someone will seek it out deliberately. Usually poets gone blind from too much seeing, or mathematicians who've calculated themselves beyond the edge of numbers. They stand in empty squares at midnight, hold up empty hands like offering bowls, and whisper questions that contain no words.

If the Dreaming Wind is feeling kind (and sometimes it is, though kindness here is a complicated concept that involves mirrors and scissors and the color of mercy at light speed), it will curl around them like smoke made of familiar voices. It will whisper answers that aren't answers but keys to doors they forgot they were standing next to. Then it will leave them there, changed in ways that only show up in photographs taken by cameras that don't exist, carrying small invisible gifts that will hatch into beautiful disasters at exactly the right wrong moments of their lives.

But usually (if time can be said to have usual here, which it can't), the Dreaming Wind simply continues its eternal wanderings. It passes through walls like gossip through thin walls. It slides down banisters of moonlight, leaving trails of condensation shaped like questions. It counts the city's heartbeats using a numbering system it invented last Tuesday but everyone forgot by breakfast.

Sometimes, at the city's core where even dreams grow pale and thin with too much existence, it pauses briefly in what might be mistaken for thought. It listens to the deep slow music the city makes when it thinks no one is paying attention—a song composed of footsteps falling in patterns too large to be coincidence, of breaths inhaling and exhaling across centuries, of every "what if" ever whispered gathering mass and preparing to hatch.

And in that pause that isn't a pause(because here even stillness moves, just slower than eyes can follow), the Dreaming Wind almost remembers what it was before it was the dreaming of dreams. Almost. Then the moment passes, as they all do eventually, even here.

It shakes itself like a dog waking from water that wasn't water. It rearranges its face into something resembling a smile but warmer and colder simultaneously. And continues on, because forward is the only direction dreams know how to go, even when they circle back to where they started without recognizing it through all the new paint and the sky wearing different stars than before.

After all, in a city built from seven thousand years of architectural sleepwalking, someone has to keep dreaming the dreams that hold everything else together. Even if those dreams sometimes dream of being something else entirely.
