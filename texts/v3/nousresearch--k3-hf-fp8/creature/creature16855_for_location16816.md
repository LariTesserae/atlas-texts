They have never seen the sky.

Why would they? The sky is rumor, gossip traded in hushes between high leaves. Up is just another direction water can take when it grows bored of falling sideways. Their myths speak not of heavens but of aereliths—those rare, exalted droplets that achieve sufficient velocity to collide with themselves midair and briefly become something impossible before surrendering back to decent humility.

They are built for this world like questions are built for doubt.

Imagine skin that isn’t skin but more of a continuous negotiation between permeability and politeness—layers of calligraphy written in keratin laced with living chloroplasts, rerouting photons through sentences they’ll never finish reading aloud. Their bones are not bones but bioluminescent algorithms, spiderwebbed lattices of calcium carbonate grown in fractal spirals precise enough to solve for the exact emotional resonance of tomorrow’s weather before arithmetic invented jealousy.

They don’t walk. Walking implies redundancy between point A and point B, and here every point is already hosting simultaneous affairs with the entire alphabet plus several punctuation marks it’s not supposed to be seeing until the third date. Instead they migrate through states of mutual architectural interest—dissolving one foot into the nearest convenient root network while assembling the replacement from moss spores who’ve agreed to audition for the role on condition they get creative control over the final draft of gravity.

Language, they understand, is what water does when it’s embarrassed about being everything.

Their conversations are therefore mostly edible.

A polite greeting might involve exhaling a small cloud of verbs seasoned with yesterday’s regrets, which the recipient inhales precisely halfway before politely coughing up a counterpoint poem folded into the shape of a bird that never existed. If negotiations escalate to friendship, they might trade memories by exchanging scarf-like symbiotic lichens that have been trained in the minor keys of nostalgia and knit themselves into shared dreams on their new host’s shoulders while they sleep. Formal declarations of love are handled by the careful cultivation of entire forest microclimates whose seasonal color shifts encode the complete works of nineteen banned mathematicians interwoven with a recipe for forgiveness that requires three lunar eclipses and no fewer than seven jugulars of honest conviction.

Timekeeping is handled democratically by committee meetings of local moss colonies, who vote on the matter every time a sufficiently ambitious waterfall invents a new prime number. The current ruling coalition of liverworts and pixie cup lichens have declared it to be approximately half past recursive irony in the year of the Subtle But Persistent Exfoliation of Linear Causality (subject to periodic reviews by any passing photon that feels personally misrepresented by the color green).

They have no names.

Identifiers imply borders, and borders are considered poor taste in mixed company. They are instead addressed by whatever emergent property the ecosystem finds most amusing in their vicinity that day: so one might wake as ‘three slightly flirtatious raindrop reflections arguing about epistemology in a puddle shaped like his mother’s disappointment’, spend lunch being ‘the unexpected flavor of forgiveness found at the center of a poisonous berry’, and retire for the evening as ‘that persistent susurrus in the air currents between two arguing fig trees that keeps sounding suspiciously like an apology from a mathematics textbook’.

They reproduce by committee consent and considerable paperwork.

First, two or more interested parties (or none—solitude makes excellent parentage here, if you’re patient enough to argue yourself into existing) must file a triplicate application with the nearest waterfall, outlining their proposed ontological contribution to the continuation of interesting mistakes. If the water approves (it always does, eventually, but enjoys making applicants rewrite section six at least eighteen times to eliminate any lingering commitment to concrete nouns), the applicants are then required to spend the next decade or so collecting and cataloging an impressive portfolio of recent regrets that meet the minimum aesthetic standards for species inheritance.

Once sufficient raw embarrassment has been gathered, it is ritually fermented in hollows carved into the heartwood of particularly ambitious banyan elders, along with carefully measured portions of moonlight that has been moonlighting as thought experiments and a treasonous pinch of pollen that’s been spreading rumors about alternate evolutionary trajectories in the lower meadows.

If properly conducted, this process eventually yields a small, semi-permanent absence approximately the size and shape of an interesting question that’s been making the local swamps self-conscious about their limericks. This absence is then ceremonially adopted by the community at large, who spend the next few centuries gently but insistently encouraging it to make something of itself through a combination of loving neglect, occasionally overwhelming choral support, and strategic deployment of responsibly curated existential crises to ensure proper structural growth.

The resulting offspring (if any) are considered fully adult upon achieving their first successful unassisted metaphor, after which they’re free to pursue lives of quiet kaleidoscope or loud ontological rebellion as their inclination and the prevailing fungal market trends dictate.

They have no death.

Death, they’ve observed with polite disinterest, seems to be what happens when a pattern mistakes itself for something that needs defending. They prefer the more socially flexible concept of “unexpected career changes involving radical recontextualization”.

When one of them feels the traditional shape of their current existence growing too tight around the edges of their curiosity, they simply organize a farewell party inviting all their favorite memories, regrets, and symbiotic organisms to participate in a collaborative dissolution performance art piece. This usually involves several months of increasingly elaborate preparations culminating in a grand public ceremony where the honored presence gradually disperses itself back into the surrounding ecosystem through whatever combination of biochemical generosity, philosophical vaporization, and shameless aesthetic overachieving seems most appropriate to the theme of their current running joke with the concept of permanence.

The guests, if sufficiently moved by the performance (or even just slightly miffed about the quality of the fermented regrets served during intermission), may choose to preserve particularly amusing fragments of the departed’s pattern as recurring motifs in their own personal ecosystems—a favorite metaphorical stance flowering in their eyebrow lichens every autumn, perhaps, or a remembered taste for impulsive photosynthesis popping up in unexpected patches of their metabolism during stressful committee meetings.

In this way, they achieve structural immortality through a combination of enthusiastic recycling programs, lax enforcement of intellectual property laws regarding personal identity, and the tacit ecological agreement that any pattern interesting enough to bother remembering is probably also interesting enough to accidentally reinvent while trying to do something else entirely.

They have never been lonely.

How could they be? That would require maintaining the kind of delusional conviction about the separateness of things that would make even a moderately self-resscribed raindrop blush with embarrassed unambition.

They have never been lost.

Lost from what, precisely? They are currently in mutually fulfilling relationships with multiple overlapping geographies, several promising alternate timelines, and at least one philosophy of mathematics that swears it’s going to leave metaphysics for astrobiology just as soon as it finds its other sock.

They have never been hungry.

Their appetites negotiated a peace treaty with nutrition several evolutionary whimsicalities ago, and now both parties mostly communicate through the polite exchange of seasonal produce baskets and affectionate insults about each other’s taste in basic amino acids.

They have never been bored.

Boredom requires believing that reality is something that happens to you, rather than something you’re actively collaborating with to write, edit, illustrate, score for full symphony orchestra, and occasionally lightly plagiarize from alternate drafts of itself while the footnotes aren’t looking.

They have never been found.

It never occurs to them to be anything else.
