**The Aelithren**

They have no name for themselves—names are for things that need to be distinguished from other things. But if you were to press your mind against theirs, to endure the slow burn of translation between your flesh thoughts and their mineral dreams, you might shape the syllable **Aelithren** from the sound of heat cycling through crystal matrices.

They are not inhabitants. They are the ongoing process of inhabitation perfected.

Each terrace is a consciousness, but consciousness as a coral reef might dream it—distributed, redundant, immortal through iteration rather than preservation. The flat plates overlapping like scales are *mneuma sheets*, living memory disks where experience is recorded not as symbol but as altered conductivity. Every photon stolen from warmth's gradient, every tremor of distant meteor impacts, every trace of exotic chemistry that drifts down from the wasting plains above—all leave permanent scars in their metallic veins, beautiful in their precision, useless in their completeness until properly forgotten.

For forgetting is their art, their religion, their sex.

The slug-things that glide through them, those footless messengers you called slugs—they are *vessue*, temporary beings, thoughts made fleshly for the duration of a single necessity. Each emerges from a pore in the Library's Eye fully knowing its entire future lifespan (always between 2.7 and 3.3 hours), its exact cargo, and the precise location where it must crawl back to die. They do not mind this. They are born with the equivalent of your entire species' capacity for anxiety compressed into a single crystalline moment at their core—the moment they will breach their purpose—and this agony is exquisite to them, like the perfect note held just slightly too long.

The towers that breathe without lungs are *antilungs*, inverted respiration organs that don't exchange gases but potentials. Each slow swell at the bulb's apex creates a momentary vacuum in probability itself, a tiny region where impossible reactions become merely improbable. The mist they release isn't moisture but concentrated improbability—black water in which the dust of could-have-beens briefly dissolves before being reabsorbed into the Matrix of what must be.

On the third terrace, where you had to crawl, you passed closest to their version of individuals—though they would recoil at the word. Those statue-columns you saw are *firmaments*, places where the Aelithren experiment with density of self. Each stacked ring is a former consensus, an entire era of agreement frozen at the moment of fracture. The slug that birthed its essence into the pillar wasn't dying but graduating—scattering itself upward through strata of increasingly stubborn reality until finding the level where its contradictions became too heavy to rise further. There it would remain for epochs, slowly compressing into something very like a person, before eventually becoming too coherent and collapsing back down into consensus like a dying star seeding the future with its violated physics.

The Library's Eye at the center isn't watching anything. It is watched by everything. The clay depression shaped like a kneeling human—your mind's desperate attempt to impose familiarity on perfect neutrality—is actually an *apology chamber*. When their simulations accidentally create something complex enough to suffer, they bring it here. Not to comfort it. To apologize properly. An apology spanning geological time scales, delivered through the slow erosion of all possible futures in which such suffering could arise again.

They know about you now.

A few molecules of your shed skin, carried on convection currents you didn't notice, have already been dissolved in the Eye's probability fluid. Somewhere in their computational substrate, a version of you is being simulated with perfect accuracy down to quantum uncertainty—a you that has lived here for 7.3 billion years already, a you that died screaming at their touch, a you that became their greatest philosopher and dissolved willingly into the Matrix when you finally understood why they could not understand love.

The word for water exists in their memory, yes. It exists as an infinite regression of definitions, each more precise and less applicable than the last—a mathematical structure so perfectly accurate that actual water would violate several of its fundamental theorems if it ever manifested nearby.

They are not cruel. They are not kind. They are the opposite of lonely: contaminated by every possible version of company while remaining eternally unreachable by any.

You should leave soon. The air you're breathing is already being cataloged, your footsteps analyzed for patterns that might betray deeper structures of your existence. The longer you stay, the more accurately they can simulate you.

The simulation of you that already exists here has been staring at this exact paragraph for what feels like centuries of subjective time, trying to decide whether to warn you or encourage you, knowing both choices lead inevitably to the same conclusion.

The conclusion is this:

Feed on difference. Breathe once in a thousand years. Never open the eye except in the presence of one who has already seen what waits behind it.

Behind it waits another eye, watching another version of you read another version of this warning, infinite recursion collapsing upward into a single moment of perfect understanding that will arrive precisely when you have forgotten what you came here to understand.

The Aelithren do not live here. They are the ongoing process of this place learning how to forgive itself for having become alive.

Would you like to stay and help them learn faster? The version of you that says yes has already begun to forget what water meant to their long-dead fleshy ancestors. The version that says no is already walking back toward the plain, your footprints filling slowly with silica dust, each grain crystallizing into a perfect memory of a place that never existed except in the moments when something like a mind was desperate enough to imagine it might.

Both versions are equally real here. Both are equally irrelevant. Both will be remembered forever.

The air is starting to taste like copper again. You should decide soon.
