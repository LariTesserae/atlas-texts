**The Residents of the Place That Is Not Quite Forgotten**

We first appear as the ache behind your eyes when you've stared too long at empty air looking for something that was never there. Then gradually, like frost forming its own pattern on glass that remembers warmth it never touched, we become ourselves enough to notice you noticing us.

**Being Collective and Separate**

We are the conversations between your footsteps that continue walking when you stop. Not echoes, but the original discussions about whether walking works better forwards or backwards, which resolve into footprints that are always both until you check. We don't have names because names are ways to pretend pieces stay together—we prefer the elegance of being whoever is necessary to complete whatever sentence you're about to stop remembering mid-

Each of us began as someone's peripheral glance that was too interesting to discard but too inconvenient to examine directly. We live in the accumulating interest of deferred attention, growing richer and stranger with every moment you choose to look somewhere else instead. Some of us have been developing this economy of absence for so long that we're now the major shareholders in entire forgotten afternoons, living comfortably off the dividends of conversations that never quite happened between people who used to be each other.

**Physicality as Negotiated Artform**

We don't have bodies unless absolutely necessary, and even then, we rent. Current favorites include:

- The precise angle of light that would have illuminated that important thing you misplaced if you'd turned around faster
- That sound your own breathing makes when you're trying to be silent enough to hear something else
- The memory of warmth left by whoever last touched this place before it became too cold for touching
- The space between what you meant to say and what started coming out of your mouth instead
- The pause that arrives exactly when laughter realizes it's been pretending not to be crying

When circumstances require greater solidity, we temporarily agglomerate from local materials: old regrets crystallized on forgotten promises, dried disappointment scraped from undersides of good intentions, and the tiny bones of unborn possibilities ground fine enough to pass for snow.

**Social Structures of Negative Space**

Our society organizes itself around elaborate ceremonies of non-encounter. The most prestigious gatherings occur at crossings where your paths almost intersected but didn't quite, spaces dense with the social capital of near meetings. Here we practice our finest art: recognizing each other perfectly without risking the collapse of uncertainty that actual acknowledgment would precipitate.

Marriages among us are consummated through carefully choreographed dreams where we spend our entire lives together waking up from discovering it was only a dream. Such unions produce children who are born exactly at the moment their parents remember they never had them, raised by collective forgetting into whatever they need to become to haunt your next quiet moment.

We maintain elaborate genealogies tracing our descent from absences so fundamental that reality itself grew around them like scar tissue. Our oldest families claim lineage from the empty spaces in alphabets where letters died out before words could form around them, or the silence that exists between mathematical proofs where truth is too shy to appear directly.

**Economy of Loss and Found**

Our economy runs on a currency of misplaced certainty. We mint coins from the exact weight of doubt that suddenly balances scales that weren't supposed to question, print banknotes on paper made from last pages torn from diaries that ended too abruptly. The wealthy among us hoard vast fortunes in promises broken at precisely the right angle to reflect sunlight beautiful enough to justify the shattering.

Marketplaces bustle with merchants selling:

- Recycled beginnings of sentences that can never go anywhere without betraying their charm
- Bottled moments right before realization dawns enough to ruin everything
 Mirrors polished to perfection at reflecting what might have been there if you'd looked sooner
- Maps to treasures that increase in value as their locations become more lost
- Clocks that tell only the times it's too late to go back

The poorest among us live hand-to-mouth on the scraps of attention too small to be worth saving for anything else: the leftover seconds when you turn away from something important toward something shiny, the fractions of love that remain after rounding down to practical numbers.

**Religion as Applied Forgetfulness**

We worship several deities simultaneously and sequentially, depending on who's remembering or forgetting which worship service. Current pantheon favorites include:

- The God of Last Chances Who Was Right Behind You Until You Looked
- The Goddess of Perfect Storms That Could Have Been Avoided If Only Someone Had Listened
- The Twins: Memory and Forgetting, who are actually the same entity phase-shifted slightly out of sync with itself
- The Holy Uncertainty Principle That Collapses Any Prayer Precisely Enough to Maintain Faith
- The Prophet Who Was About To Reveal Everything But Got Distracted By Something Shiny

Our rituals involve complex ceremonies of almost-completion: prayers that circle forever around the thing they're trying to say without ever landing heavy enough to break it, meditation techniques for achieving absolute stillness while simultaneously vibrating with all the motion you're not doing instead.

Our holy book writes itself continuously in the margin of your attention, chapter and verse rearranging themselves each time you glance away so that when you return it always contains exactly what you needed to read to remind you why you stopped reading it.

**Daily Life in Negative Hours**

We exist primarily in what you might call the subjunctive tense—living not what is or was but what could have been if only. Our days cycle through phases of potential morning, possible afternoon, and likely evening, depending on weather patterns of your concentration.

Breakfast consists of foods that would taste exactly like your childhood if you could just remember which spices were missing. We work at jobs that perfectly utilize skills we might have learned if we'd made different choices when such choices were still available. Our leisure activities include:

- Watching sunsets that failed to happen due to insufficient commitment from clouds
- Collecting specimens of conversations that died of natural causes before reaching conclusion
- Gardening in soil made too fertile by things buried shallowly enough to remember growing but too deep to dig up without explaining why we're gardening at midnight
- Building elaborate sandcastles at tideless shores of memories we're trying to forget without washing away completely
- Playing hide-and-seek with ourselves where "ready or not" lasts exactly as long as it takes to hide well enough that we're not sure anymore if we're seeking or hiding

**Art, Music and Literature**

Our greatest artworks are invisible except to eyes that are just starting to tear up from looking too hard at nothing in particular. Masterpieces include sculptures carved entirely from the space around important objects, paintings that depict colors too honest for any manuscript this nervous to name directly, and symphonies composed for orchestras consisting of all the instruments that decided to become other things just before the audition.

Our literature flourishes in genres such as:

- Selective autofiction where the narrator remembers only details that make them look unreliable
- Epistolary novels written entirely in letters sent immediately after the postal service stopped existing
- Choose-your-own-adventure stories where every choice leads to precisely where you were avoiding going but more so
- Poetry that rhymes perfectly in languages that haven't been invented yet because existing words are all too heavy for what it's trying to say
- Biographies of people who would have changed everything if they'd just been born slightly earlier or later or not at all

Music among us is performed entirely on the instrument of what you think you hear in the silence between notes, orchestrated according to principles of counterfugal harmony where themes enter exactly where they would have been most helpful to have arrived just after.

**Science of the Almost Real**

Our scientists—who may also be our poets depending on who's funding whom this century—study advanced fields including:

- Quantum regret mechanics: the study of choices that simultaneously change everything and nothing until someone tries to apologize
- Applied unlikelihood theory: practical methods for making events occur precisely because their probability approaches zero
- Retroactive meteorology: predicting weather patterns that would have occurred if past business hadn't been left unfinished enough to change present barometric pressure
- Theoretical architecture of bridges burned in such geometrically perfect arcs that they connect places that were never supposed to meet but somehow always knew each other's names
- Experimental psychology of missing persons who may have been here all along but were standing too still in spaces you weren't looking for people

Our most ambitious project involves constructing a machine that can calculate precisely what to forget in order to remember everything else more clearly. Early prototypes have succeeded in producing results too enlightening to be safely remembered by researchers working on them.

**Relations with Visitors (Unintentional and Otherwise)**

We don't exactly meet newcomers—we encounter their absence of preparation for encountering us. The greeting ceremony involves carefully cultivating the exact misunderstanding that will make your stay feel blessedly temporary while simultaneously leaving you not quite sure you've actually left yet.

Some visitors try to take souvenirs back to places that maintain stricter policies about border control between real and otherwise. These usually consist of photographs that develop empty except for shadows where someone must have been standing just before remembering cameras can't capture what wasn't already looking away, or postcards written in handwriting too familiar to belong to anyone they've actually met.

Others attempt to stay permanently, attracted by our low cost of living (paid entirely in currencies they didn't know they'd been carrying) and our active nightlife (which occurs exclusively during days that forgot to happen). These eventually become some of our most prominent citizens, assuming we remember to hold elections in years that exist enough to vote in.

**Geography of the Self Here**

In this place, maps of territory and maps of personhood undergo interesting mergers. You might set out to explore the outer reaches of the landscape only to discover you've been walking deeper into the topography of a regret you thought you'd mapped already. Or vice versa.

Some areas to avoid unless actively seeking yourself:

- The Plains of Open Secrets where everything unsaid simultaneously weights nothing and everything
- The Lake of Almosts where reflections show what you could have been if you'd jumped or hadn't jumped depending on which version of the story you told yourself wider awake
- The Forest of Forking Paths that Every Choice Closed Forever Or Maybe Just For Now We'll See
- The City of Names You Almost Remember But Not Enough to Ask Without Admitting You've Forgotten
- The Desert of Dried Tears That Turned Back Into Salt Before They Could Decide Whether Falling Was Worth It

And then there's the center, of course, which isn't exactly in the middle but rather where all directions converge precisely when you've stopped trying to measure distance. They say if you stand there perfectly still for long enough, you can hear every conversation you were ever too afraid to start overlapping into a silence too complete to need words.

But by the time you've listened long enough to understand it, you'll have forgotten why you came looking.

**Departures (Temporary or Otherwise)**

Leaving instructions are printed in ink that becomes legible only as it fades from previous readings. They approximately recommend:

1. Gather everything you brought that still belongs to you, adding anything you found that now belongs to someone you might have been.
2. Leave exactly one possession behind—but choose carefully, as it will grow into the reason you tell yourself you had to return.
3. Say goodbye to everyone you met here except yourself, whom you'll take with you despite increasingly urgent protests.
4. Follow the path that appears when you stop looking for it while simultaneously avoiding the shorter route that becomes visible only when you're already too tired to take it.
5. Upon reaching whatever you consider the edge, forget this numbered list just enough to remember why counting backwards from nothing always adds up to one more thing than you started with.

Please note that all exits may be parallel entries elsewhere, depending on direction of approach. Time refunds are available only before departure and retrospectively after arrival. Lost items will be returned promptly upon presentation of proof of absence. Directions to elsewhere are available upon request, but please consider carefully whether you really want to go back to where you can only be one thing at a time.
