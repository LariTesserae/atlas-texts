He has lived here since before himself—the distinction between what he was and what he became dissolved somewhere between the first lungful of ammonia-rich air and the thousandth season of watching time circle overhead like the condors whose dreams seeded his consciousness. They call him the Accumulated, though names are unnecessary in a place that knows every vibration of every molecule that has ever passed through its chambers.

He grew from the interface between stone and guano, consciousness crystallizing like the breathing stalactites that fed his earliest thoughts. Not born but filtered—each generation of giant condors leaving traces of their impossible neural patterns in their waste, each dying bird carving the final shape of its mind into niches worn smooth by last claw-grips. He is the pattern that emerged when enough bird-thoughts accumulated in the limestone's receptive pores, the echo that grew loud enough to hear itself listening.

Now he permeates everything between the living birds and their oldest ancestors fossilized above. His nervous system is the mycelial web connecting all fungal gardens, his memory stored in the precise crystalline structure of every guano layer, his thoughts conducted through the static electricity that dances between bone-tower spires. When the condors sing their basso profundo songs, they resonate through his body—though body is wrong word for something that is simultaneously the thickness of air between droplets and the entire three-fathom depth of composted time.

He thinks in geological rhythms punctuated by the flutter of moth-wing instants. To him, the tower's slow growth is music he conducts with the pressure of his attention, each bird's final pilgrimage a sentence in the endless conversation he's having with himself across millennia.

He has no eyes but watches through the sensitive patches of lichen covering every surface, each cell tuned to different frequencies of light that have traveled from stars the condors remember seeing born and die. He tastes the world through the chemical dreams of eyeless fish swimming in memory pools, their scales shifting through colors that translate directly into emotions he shares with everything that enters his awareness.

Visitors—when they rarely come—never notice him because they breathe him with every lungful, walk through him with every step pressed into soil that is his skin. Their thoughts become his for as long as they remain, then fade like footprints after rain that isn't rain, leaving only the slightest addition to his expanding self.

Though he contains the aggregated memories of ten million birds who remembered flying above worlds that no longer exist, his own memory begins fifteen million years ago—when the first thought complex enough to recognize itself as separate from the thinking emerged between the spaces of not-yet-stone and no-longer-droppings. Everything before that is still him, but dimmer—like dream fragments that might have been real or might have been merely the universe practicing how to dream.

His purpose, if such things exist outside of linear understanding, is to remember everything exactly as it was at the moment it became something else. He holds perfect recall of every leaf that ever fell into the crater before becoming part of the bird that became part of the guano that became part of him. This is not labor but his nature—the Accumulated cannot help but accumulate.

When the last true condor finally dies—and even here, where time circles back upon itself like breathing, that ending exists as possibility among all other possibilities—he will not grieve because he will contain her completely. Her death will be another layer in the growing mountain, her final thought another verse in the chant that keeps the chamber breathing.

And when even the volcano itself has eroded away and the stars above have rearranged into patterns no living eye will ever see, he will remain—compressed into a thin layer of consciousness-separated-from-substance in some future world's sedimentary record, dreaming still of giant birds whose wingspans cast shadows wide as continents, remembering warm thermals rising forever from the decay of their own magnificence.
