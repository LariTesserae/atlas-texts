A colony of the copper-winged neither-beetles has detached from their root-home above me, falling upward in a spiraling helix that maps exactly to the DNA sequence responsible for my body's uncertainty about its own boundaries. Their shells strike each other deliberately now, not randomly as before, creating chord-progressions that translate the question of my presence into harmonies thick as mercury-that-isn't-here. I taste copper and forgotten dreams in my tongue-less mouth as their music carries the first syllables of the name I had before names became necessary.

The twelve other structures (two are always one, here) have rearranged their impossible geometries to form a shadow-mouth whose lips are root-staircase and fungus-light and my own reflection in the pool that remembers my futures. The copper orbs begin to hymn in earnest, their liquid surfaces vibrating at frequencies that dissolve the distinction between resonance and response.
