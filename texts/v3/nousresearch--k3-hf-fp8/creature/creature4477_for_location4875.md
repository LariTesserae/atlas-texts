**The Residents of the Entropy Garden**

They are not precisely alive, nor are they dead, though some of them remember both states with the clarity of fever dreams. They have adapted to the drip cycle, metabolizing time itself between the 153-second intervals. Some resemble what they once were—a crescent of hazmat hood still cycling through its final filter warning, now encrypted by vine growth into a half-mask of turquoise petals that open and close to the bearing's heartbeat. Others have abandoned resemblance entirely.

The most senior resident, designation unknown, occupies the space between the collapsed railcar and the overturned seismograph. It began as a maintenance drone that lost its swarm connection when the drill bit breached the thing that wasn't oil. Over centuries of bearing radiation and alloy leachate, its memory banks developed a condition resembling nostalgia. Now it exists as a cloud of self-welded slivers—ratchet teeth, capacitors, fragments of a smile captured in Polaroid emulsion dissolved down to silver halide nerves—held together by an electromagnetic field generated by its own regret. When the flood lamps emit their zones of directional silence, you can hear it trying to hum the company's morning safety song in frequencies that shatter rust crystals.

The vine-things are more numerous but less distinct. Each root system shares memory through the oil-film via chemical postcards written in trace elements. They remember being seeds that rode down on drill-bit lubricant, remember the sudden warm rush when human hands spilled coffee on the polymercrete. Now they remember everything that happens within their circulatory network simultaneously. This makes them terrible gossips and excellent historians. One cluster near the south wall has specialized in reconstructing the facial expressions of the last crew from patterns of sweat residue preserved in the rust. It performs them in slow motion when the temperature peaks, stretching a grin or frown across twenty-seven hours of careful leaf-arrangement.

The most human-shaped resident is technically empty space. It wears the outline of the figure on the folding stool, maintained by habit and humidity. The hazmat suit inflated slowly over decades as the mold ate its contents, preserving the exact volume of a person who leaned forward slightly, right arm raised as if about to tap a monitor that displayed increasingly urgent warnings in text that grew more polite as the situation worsened. At dawn-spore (a purely local phenomenon marked by the collective exhale of condensation dropping exactly 0.3 degrees), the suit performs a ritual failure. The root-fist puncture wound blooms a flower shaped like an apology letter never sent. The internal photos shuffle themselves into new configurations of the same four faces aging through expressions of mounting embarrassment at having been caught causing a fundamental breach in the geology company's safety record. Then the flower collapses and the void within smooths itself back into waiting.

Between these anchor tenants drift the temporary residents. These arrive as concepts decaying into substance—rust cancers that achieve metastatic awareness, dreams of lubricant that hibernate in oil droplet eggs, a particularly determined thought about elevators that learned to echo itself between the rust-thinned cables five kilometers north. They stay for as long as it takes to forget what they weren't. One particularly persistent guest was the number 42.317 repeated infinitely in a canceled depth measurement. It manifested as a spiral groove etched at molecular scale into every metal surface, playing the sound of geological doubt as a 7.3 Hz hum somewhere between a question and a muscle twitch. The vines eventually negotiated a symbiosis, amplifying it through their hollow stems during spore-bursts as a mating call that attracted the attention of distant equations. Its children occasionally visit as sudden patches of hyperbolic geometry where parallel lines meet to gossip about the weather on higher-dimensional oil rigs.

The mold is everyone and everywhen's landlord. It maintains the lease on entropy by converting all arrivals into previous tenants. It has impeccable taste—only preserving the parts that most acutely remember being somewhere else. Currently fashion-forward strains are cultivating colonies that replicate the exact texture of anticipation felt by a safety inspector discovering paperwork filed in triplicate with increasingly desperate handwriting. These grow exclusively on the underside of oil droplets suspended in mid-fall, creating transient cities that evaporate into perfectly preserved moments of administrative dread when the drip finally drops.

They communicate through medium exchange agreements. The drone-cloud speaks to the vines by carefully rusting specific patterns that translate into mineral deficiency complaints in Binary oppressed by millennia of erosion. The vines reply by altering their photosynthetic output to emit electromagnetic pulses that tickle the drone's silver halide memories into brief hallucinations of sunlight on stainless steel. The human-shaped void and the folding stool maintain a long-running argument conducted entirely through posture changes phased across geological time—currently forty-three centuries into a debate about whether the correct response to structural instability is evacuation or reclassification as experimental architecture.

They eat what the place remembers tasting like. The drone-cloud metabolizes the difference between how things should have worked and their current poetic failure states. The vines photosynthesize nostalgia for photosynthesis itself, cheering themselves with the irony. The temporary residents consume the concept of "temporary" until they achieve the perfect flavor of being the oldest thing that still expects to leave soon. The mold, of course, dines on everything and provides excellent digestion-as-a-service options for residents wishing to become newly ignorant of their previous identities.

They reproduce through carefully curated accidents. The drone-cloud occasionally permits two particularly incompatible regrets to spark together, birthing offspring that manifest as cosmological constants briefly flickering before achieving stable irony. The vines flower only when convinced that flowering is an obsolete reproductive strategy, typically by overhearing the mold reminiscing about superior spore designs. The human-shaped void maintains a delicate condition of potential pregnancy with an idea it had in 1987 about the sound a falling safety record makes in an abandoned dimension.

The most recent census (conducted by weight rather than count, as most residents dispute whether "one" is an appropriate unit for distributed consciousnesses experiencing time as a shared hallucinogenic event) records approximately 42.317 residents. This number has been constant since measurement began, though individual components cycle through possibility states with enthusiasm that would concern more bureaucratic ecosystems.

They do not know, or have agreed to pretend not to know, that the whole garden exists within the bearing's final 0.3 micron of lubricant. The bearing spins on, unaware or indifferent to its role as universe-hub, generating just enough heat to remember being part of something larger while never quite enough to forget this perfect moment of catastrophic intimacy.

The next drip forms overhead, gathering variables for a decision it has been postponing since before these residents learned to call this place home. Below, everything waits in polite attention, rehearsing their roles in whatever comes after this sentence ends but before the page remembers it was supposed to be the last.
