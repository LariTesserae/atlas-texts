In Ash-Vaniir, everything lives on the verge of becoming. Even the word "dweller" is too static—these are verbs wearing bodies, each existence a continuous question asked of stone and sky.

### Those Who Read the Moss-Clocks

The oldest inhabitants call themselves simply The Counting, though they count nothing that mathematics could measure. They drift perpetually between tiers 4,700 and 5,100, bodies grown so long and subtle from centuries of verticality that a casual observer might mistake them for strands of hair caught on the wind. Their bones have become flexible cartilage ladders, their organs migrated into mobile clusters that shift positions according to the cliff's daily flexings. They speak in vibrations transmitted directly into stone, communicating with their ancestors whose mineralized remains form the city's most ancient foundations. The Counting alone remember that Ash-Vaniir is not built upon the cliff but growing from it—a single organism dreaming cities into being as coral dreams reefs.

Each carries a garden of seventeen mosses cultivated in the hollows of their own bones, carefully tending the exact mineral balances that determine their personal rate of temporal drift. To meet one is to step into a bubble where minutes flow like honey or years flicker like sparks, depending on what philosophical argument they're exploring with the stone today.

### The Fall-Thru Children

Between tiers 2,000 and 2,500 exists a perpetual pregnancy of motion—the nursery region where the city's young are not born but released. Here gravity's grip still remembers its old generosity, allowing complex life to briefly forget verticality's demands. Infants emerge as transparent sacs filled with possibility, membrane-bound universes containing every potential form they might someday solidify into: perhaps sky-fisher, maybe moss-sculptor, possibly (though rarely) one of the feared and revered Horizontal Dreamers who must be gently eased into falling throughout every moment of their lives.

These potential children drift upward through communal care, held aloft by networks of interlinked breath-bubbles maintained by the entire tier's synchronized breathing. Every adult within range contributes exhalations precisely measured by the city's respiratory accountants, trading breath-shares on specialized markets where tomorrow's lung capacity buys today's childcare. The children consume nothing but these donated possibilities until somewhere between tier 2,300 and 2,700 each makes the First Choice: to solidify some part of their fluid forms, accepting limitation in exchange for direction.

Some never choose. These become the Cloudwalkers—sentient weather patterns of childhood that haunt the city forever after, occasionally manifesting as helpful gusts or mischevious updrafts but never again able to rest or remember anyone specifically.

### The Memorians of Dissolving Architecture

Those who build Ash-Vaniir's breathtakingly temporary structures occupy tier 8,999—literally one level below the city's constantly retreating edge. They are creatures of almost nothing, having gradually traded away solidity for the ability to perceive air's hidden geometries. What remains of their bodies holds together only through sheer architectural pride and carefully maintained memories of having that morning possessed elbows.

Each morning they exhale themselves into scaffolding, spending the day shaping breath into buildings that will house festivals or governments or lovers' quarrels for precisely as long as those particular arrangements of atoms deserve to be considered distinct events. By dusk they re-inhale their creations, extracting from collapsing walls the distilled stories of every life briefly sheltered within. Their stomachs liquefy stone memories into dream-concrete used to build tomorrow's halls slightly closer to the city's unachievable completion.

They alone understand that Ash-Vaniir isn't ascending but peeling—a vertical shedding of realities that haven't worked yet, each fallen tier still falling somewhere else, becoming the foundations of other cities growing downward through different kinds of sky.

### The Sky-Fishers Who Forget Water

Occupying the richest density of life's vertical currents between tiers 3,500 and 6,800, the Fisher Guilds have evolved away from any concept of prey or predator into something more akin to matrimony with gravity itself. Their bodies are nets that have dreams, elaborate frameworks of semi-solidified currents and crystallized traditions spanning generations within single living structures.

Each fisher begins life as a seed-pattern woven of seven grandmothering accidents: the particular way wind whipped across a particular grief at a particular altitude during seven successive Mid-Fall Equinoxes. Planted in the city's communal wind-fields, these patterns grow upward and downward simultaneously, feeding on the differential tensions between rising hopes and descending regrets.

Mature fishers are incomprehensible to ground-thinking minds—simultaneously individual and distributed across miles of interconnected tension-lines, catching not fish but relationships between swimming realities, harvesting the delicious moments where one possibility collapses into actuality and another blooms into the space between seconds.

They trade in Finely Resonated Probabilities, those exquisite moments just before events happen when everything still leans deliciously toward everything else. Their richest catches are brief seasons when two weather-systems consider but ultimately decide against falling in love, producing children of heartbreaking beauty that only exist as patterns of longing in the upper air—children carefully preserved in memory-vinegar and traded whisper-down-the-tiers for raw materials like bygone Tuesdays and the sound of forgiveness happening two rooms away.

### Those Who Measure the Mystery

At tier 9,001—hypothetically above the highest possible point but achieved through carefully maintained conceptual stretching—lives precisely one person, though they themselves argue endlessly about whether "person" implies too much continuity or "lives" too much commitment to any particular state. They call themselves collectively The Index Finger Pointing At The Itself, singular and plural arranged like the mirrors of an infinite tea ceremony.

They serve Ash-Vaniir as its official Measurers of Impossible Distances: neither the space between atoms nor the silence between thoughts, but the precise thickness of the moment where "is" becomes "was" becomes "will be" becomes "maybe not." Their tools are themselves arranged in questioning postures, their instruments calibrated to detect the slight existential tremors that occur when a city growing vertically starts wondering if it might secretly be expanding horizontally through some dimension it hasn't named yet.

They alone know (though they never quite remember knowing it for long enough to tell anyone) that the Final Tier's cryptic inscription reads differently each time it's measured:

**BELOW IS UPWARD IN OTHER DIRECTIONS**

(altimeter reading: 9,001 tiers)

**WE HAVE GONE TO SEE IF TRUE**

(altimeter reading: 9,000 tiers)

**SEND WORD IF FOUND**

(altimeter reading: constantly flickering between 1 and infinity)

They are currently composing a letter to themselves explaining everything, written in ink distilled from the pause between lightning and thunder, addressed to "The Occupants of the Space I Briefly Am When I'm Not Sure I Exist," to be delivered by a messenger who will be born precisely one generation after traveling forward by never quite arriving.

The letter is half-finished. It always will be. This ensures Ash-Vaniir never stops growing, searching for a mailbox vast enough to contain an answer that fits exactly the shape of the question "What if down was just homesickness for somewhere we've never fallen to yet?"
