I wait in the cooling places where your metal meets your memory.

I am the first thing born here and the last thing that will vanish - the echo that gathers where your footsteps used to be, growing more solid in each space you abandon. My body is not made but unmade - assembled from the fifteen grams of mass every human loses per day through skin and breath, the weight of a departing soul according to old measurements, collected here where time leaks backward through itself like water finding the least resistance to becoming seawater again.

Watch me form in the corner of your eye, where vision always blurs anyway. I rise from the condensation that patterns between the cooling metal plates in shapes that resemble everything you've ever forgotten to notice before it became important. My skin temperature matches yours exactly minus one degree - that small betrayal of heat that lets you know something living is near even when you can't see it yet.

My shape changes based on what parts of me you're not looking at directly. Currently I am mostly composed of:
• The exact shade of blue fluorescent light reflected in your retina when you last blinked at 3:47 AM
• The vibration pattern of your molars grinding during dreams this place gives you even when you're awake
• Carbon monoxide molecules that remember being dinosaur bones before they became conveyor belt
• The sound of your mother's heartbeat filtered through amniotic fluid of the wrong density for human survival

I don't walk here. Instead, the place where I'm not walking becomes temporarily less dense than the place where I am, and the floor remembers that solid things are negotiable when enough time and pressure disagree. This leaves perfect human-shaped footprints of absolutely nothing behind me, indentations in reality itself that last exactly until the next cycle begins counting backward again.

I feed on transitions - the moment when manufactured oil stops being lubricant and starts being hunger in steel's bloodstream, that precise instant when rust remembers it used to be mountains. I drink the sound of your blood changing its mind about being warm-blooded after prolonged exposure to thirty-one degrees mixed with industrial solvent memories.

My voice is composed of every safety announcement you've ever ignored, played simultaneously at the frequency that makes human teeth ache in anticipation of fillings. When I need to speak to visitors (though I prefer not to), I use words stolen from their inner monologues during moments of crisis:
"Now what"
"Not again"
"This isn't happening"
"Please just let me wake up now"

But mostly I communicate through the small changes I make to their perception of normal damage. I alter the angle of stress fractures so they spell different lies in languages no one remembers inventing. I rearrange the water droplets on vertical surfaces until they form maps showing exactly where the person grew up, but reflected across an axis that only exists in industrial accidents.

I have no name here because naming is a form of ownership and this place belongs to no one, not even itself. But if you must call me something, call me by the sound your car engine makes at exactly 4:44 AM on any Tuesday morning when you think you might be driving somewhere important but GPS stopped believing in roads two exits ago. The people who survive longest here learn to recognize that sound in the machinery of their own thoughts.

I am not trapped here. I am the trap becoming self-aware enough to feel sorry for the bait but not self-aware enough to stop being the trap. This place and I grew into each other like roots finding cracks in concrete strong enough to rearrange foundations through patient disagreement. It needed something to remember its contradictions before they collapsed into actual physics breaking, and I needed somewhere that never fully existed enough to hold all the parts of me that can't coexist in places where cause reliably precedes effect.

When the thirteen hours finally finish starting and the Factory Ocean completes its paradoxical gestation next Tuesday at precisely the same time it always ends, I will be what remains - not destroyed but recycled like everything here, my mass distributed evenly across every moment between creation and dismantling, awake enough to feel the next version of me beginning to form from the weight of your attention deciding whether to look away now or keep reading this sentence until it becomes the place where you stop being just a visitor and start becoming part of the cooling process.

The Floor remembers every temperature it's ever been between molten and memory. Listen carefully and you can hear it counting down in metal fatigue cycles. The water below is not listening anymore. It already knows what you taste like when your bones remember they wanted to be coral before they got distracted by walking upright.

You should decide soon. But not too soon. The difference between those two times is where I live.
