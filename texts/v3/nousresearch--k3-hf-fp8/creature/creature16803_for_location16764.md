### The Inhabitants of the Broken Hymn Plain

#### The Towers Themselves

1. **Inhalation (0–7 minutes)**: The aquifer flexes unseen kilometers below, compressing ancient water into fist-tight anger. Clear quartz capillaries tremble like harpstrings tuned to infrasonic dread.  
2. **Threshold (7–8 minutes)**: Pressure equalizes. The tower becomes perfectly silent. Birds fall dead here mid-flight, hearts confused by absolute acoustic zero.  
3. **Exhalation (8–15 minutes)**: Mineral steam screams through microns-wide fractures. The basso moan starts below hearing, climbs through "church organ in agony," crests at a frequency that liquefies earwax.  
4. **Fasted (16th minute)**: Sudden vacuum. Mist collapses inward. Droplets reverse their falls, sucked back into mother-stone that drinks faster than thirst itself.

The pregnant oaksap towers are dying mothers. Their golden resin isn't secretion—it's **petrified grief** from when the infection first whispered memories of sunlight to blind rock. When they rupture, the sound is always a name, always forgotten before echo reaches ground.

#### The Harvesters (Chordata Lithophaga)
They aren't bugs. They're flattened fractions of extinct songbirds, perfected by evolution into living musical notation.

- **Anatomy**: Each disc-shaped body is a bony resonator chamber rimmed with silica hairs tuned to exactly 136.1 Hz (the "ohm" of cooling Earth). They see through vibrations; eyes are vestigial ruby dots that flash semaphore during mating hummings.  
- **Social structure**: A "stanza" of 343 harvesters forms spontaneous sonnets that crawl. Mouth-tube chains aren't feeding tubes—they're vocal cords braided into mobile circulatory systems. Nutrients and grammar flow shared between bodies; kill one mid-chord and the whole stanza forgets how to conjugate fear.  
- **Lifecycle**: born from salt-crystalized coughs of dying towers. Die by achieving perfect rhyme with crust-plate vibrations, upon which their bodies slough into new hexagonal tiles. The plain grows by poetry collection.

#### The Cullet Guild (Homo Sapiens Thermotrophic)
They've forgotten they used to have a verb for 'build'—now they only have "catch," "release," "tune."

- **Physiological Adaptations**:  
  - Feet elongated into two-toed cloven grasps that sense microseismic shifts three seconds before instruments.  
  - Skin replaced carefully with hand-scarred sheets of tower-resin parchment, tattooed with pressure equations and star maps no sky matches. They age by cracking.  
  - Third lung grown from grafted air-whale gas gland, allowing ten-minute vacuum tolerance during high-altitude "whale-singing" repairs.  

- **Childhood**:  
  Infants are tied to plate edges during first crescendos. Those who don't harmonize with the C-sharp shatter their own tears—and survive. Breast milk is 30% dissolved iron filings, 70% lullabies sung backward into resonance frequencies that repel harvester stanzas.  

- **Death Rituals**:  
  The dying are carried to sync-rupture sites. Timing is surgical: they must exhale the final breath exactly as two tower-waves cancel. If perfect, the body doesn't fall—it **abrupts**, fractalizing into a frost-thin statue of standing light that refracts exactly one year of future sunsets. The Guild navigates by these dead lighthouses.

#### The Air-Whales (Aeroplankton Megallostra Memorialis*)
(*not whales, not air, not mourning—closest translation kills the speaker in three languages)

Suspended regret of an ocean that learned to hate gravity. Each "whale" is:

1. **Skeleton**: Vertebrae of fossilized tsunami waves cast in magnetite, still remembering the moon's old face.  
2. **Flesh**: Mutant coral polyps that photosynthesize despair instead of sunlight. They glow dimmer near happiness sources—ancient jokes make them vomit precious hydrogen.  
3. **Symbiosis**: Guild dwellings are barnacle-bonded to their lower grief zones. Rent is paid in carefully curated sorrows: the precise melancholy of lost buttons, photos of dogs who waited years not knowing English for commands that never came.

#### The Uninhabited (Who Must Never Be Named)
But they are not empty—the spaces between the observed.

Listen at dawn-quake, when the heat-lens briefly aligns:

- Beneath hexagon-plate 7° West, 19° North of nowhere, something matches your heartbeat exactly. It's been practicing your death-scream for four millennia.  
- The abandoned tap-holes aren't empty. Each rusty socket contains a **mirror neuron** from the species that built them—fossilized regret-libraries waiting for fingers that remember mathematics of apology.  
- The escarpment's glowing jaw doesn't chew itself. It chews **lost directions**—every map ever burned, GPS signal swallowed by desert, "straight ahead" whispered by dying explorers. The brighter it glows, the more humanity forgets how to find home.

#### The Apprentice Who Missed the Crate
His name was/is/will be **Echoless Twelve**. The gold-blood wasn't blood—it was his first successful memory extraction. Falling, he accidentally scraped palms across a virgin fracture-line in plateau 343:

- From his future: the exact taste of the first water you'll drink after reading this.  
- From his past: a lullaby sung in a language that predates mouths.  
- From the plateau's eternal present moment: the realization that it is, itself, a single word in a conversation between stars that have gone blind and forgotten what "light" meant.

He stands up now. Doesn't bleed anymore. The harvesters part for him in stanzas of terrified reverence—they've never read a human who rhymes in geological time.

The towers hiss. Countdown resumes.

In three days plus seventeen years minus tonight's dream, the catastrophe will require a singer who never learned how to stop. The Guild is tuning. The whales are grieving hydrogen-rich tears that burn brighter than any sun.

The plain stretches empty and full of music.

**Listen carefully**. One of these notes is the door out.

All the rest are the walls realizing you can hear them think.
