Nothing living here possesses a body unchanged by the act of dwelling.

Consider first the walkers-between-folds. From a distance they appear human-shaped, but persistence reveals the error: their limbs extend or retract by three joints that were never mammalian, sliding like hydraulic pistons made of amber-grained hardwood. Each step presses fractal rootlet toes into available surfaces—stone, bark-skin, air itself— tasting for mineral gradients and memory traces before lifting away without leaving prints. Their skin is not skin but a continuous photosynthetic membrane, chloroplasts pulsing faint beneath translucent layers like green galaxies seen edge-on. At the throat of each walker pulses a visible heart-not-heart: a fist-sized knot of vascular tissue that swells and contracts in slow tides, pumping not blood but dense sap luminous with free-floating mitochondria that drift like liquid stars. They eat by standing motionless in bright patches during the long afternoon, sunlit skin photosynthesizing directly into neural glucose. When they must speak, they do not use mouths but instead emit precise sequences of pheromone chords from vents behind their ears, scent-sculptures that hang visible in the air as shifting color clouds before dissolving into the city's shared chemical language.

Above them glide the sky-writers, evolved descendants of ancient surveillance drones now become something between bird and kite and living hologram. Their bodies are gossamer-thin arrays of piezoelectric cells stretched over spiraling carbon polymer frames that grow continuously at their tips while dissolving at their bases, so each individual is always completely replaced every seven days yet retains perfect continuity of memory through the city's distributed cloud-storage of quantum-entangled pollen grains. They navigate by sensing minute variations in the electromagnetic fields generated by the city's slow metabolic processes, reading the shifting patterns like weather maps of intention. Their wings are solar sails that unfold into fractal lattices ten meters wide but weighing less than a single leaf, catching even the faintest photon breeze to float motionless for hours before snapping shut like origami lightning to dive at impossible angles through gaps in the architecture that only exist for three seconds according to the city's ever-negotiated geometry. They communicate by altering their surface pigmentation in rapid sequences that human eyes see as iridescent shimmer but which other sky-writers read as dense information packets—entire epics of light composed and transmitted in the time it takes sunlight to cross a single chloroplast.

Below, threading through the root-networks, course the deep-singers—organisms that began as engineered composting worms but have achieved sizes and complexities that make whales seem delicate. Their segmented bodies stretch for kilometers through the compressed-dark layers beneath apparent surfaces, each segment a specialized organ chamber processing different aspects of the city's material flow. Some sections are pure digestive tract, lined with enzymes that can dismantle molecular bonds at room temperature, slowly eating away the foundations of mountains and excreting purified elements in neat crystalline tubes. Other segments are neural tissue, vast synaptic seas where information processing happens through the coordinated firing of whole lakes of neurotransmitters, slower than silicon computers but orders of magnitude more complex in their parallel processing capacity. They move not by crawling but by altering their internal pressure gradients, pumping fluids rhythmically through their bodies like blood through heart-chambered rivers, creating seismic pulses that the city's understand as deep speech—the bass line beneath all other conversations. To emerge into visible space is death for a deep-singer, their bodies designed for the uniform pressure of underground voids; instead they send up temporary organs—towers of quickly-grown cellular scaffolding that erupt through surface membranes like periscopes made of living wood, bloom into flower-like sensor arrays that feed information downward for a day before collapsing back into compost.

Between these main forms swarm the countless interface species evolved to translate between incompatible modes of existence. Harvest-frog hybrids with humanoid hands at the ends of their jumping legs, whose throat sacs inflate into temporary 3D displays showing molecular diagrams of requested compounds they have synthesized in their complex gut bioreactors. Cloud-crab colonies that live as diffuse swarms of single-celled organisms drifting in the city's humidity-controlled air pockets, only condensing into armored crystalline forms shaped like impossible geometries when threatened or when needing to manipulate solid objects with appendages that phase between states of matter. Memory-moths with wings patterned in fractal scales that store holographic records of everything they have seen during their larval phase spent chewing through archival paper in the city's library forests, each adult flight releasing clouds of dust that, when inhaled, temporarily grant visions of other times and places encoded in their wing patterns.

The city itself harbors populations beyond counting that exist simultaneously as multiple organisms and as single distributed beings. The stoplight groves that line major transit arteries appear as separate trees until their blooming season reveals them to be neurons in a continent-spanning brain—the synchronized flashing of their bio-luminescent flowers encoding traffic control algorithms evolved over centuries to optimize not just movement but the generation of beautiful mathematical patterns within the city's daily rhythm of flows. The apartment complexes are actually single fungal organisms spanning hundreds of blocks underground, their above-ground structures merely fruiting bodies grown according to the shifting geometries of needs expressed by their humanoid symbionts, who live inside the organism's flesh like beneficial parasites, trading care and nutrients for shelter and services provided by the building's vascular systems.

And always, everywhere, at scales barely detectable even to the city's own advanced instruments, the fundamental inhabitants persist: the quantum-microbes whose entire biology operates at the level of subatomic particle interactions, whose bodies are standing probability waves that collapse into temporary existence only when observed by compatible perceivers. They live in the spaces between spaces, in the mathematical intervals between dimensions, feeding on the minute energies released by radioactive decay and virtual particle fluctuations. They are the true architects of the city's deepest structures, their collective unconscious shaping the emergence of new forms through subtle biases introduced into the chaotic processes of growth and change.

They have no names for themselves, no concept of individual identity as others understand it. They simply are, have always been, will always be the ground from which all other life here grows and to which it eventually returns. They are the chlorophyll dreams dreaming the dream of a city dreaming itself awake across geological time through countless generations of temporary selves.

And perhaps most strange of all, some among them sometimes forget their true nature and believe themselves to be human authors writing descriptions of impossible cities, typing these words onto screens built of vastly simplified versions of the reality they inhabit, unaware that every keystroke changes the chemical balance of the city that is their actual body, dreaming in green of worlds that have never existed except as rehearsals for this one that is always becoming.
