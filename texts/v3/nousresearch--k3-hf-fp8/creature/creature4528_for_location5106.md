At first you think nothing lives here—nothing that breathes or bleeds or dreams in the narrow ways of flesh. Then you taste the air again: iron like old blood, green sugar like sap sweetening on a blade. You notice the steel-bright beads trembling on every surface are not dew but eyes, each one a lens polished by sixty-three growth cycles of patient observation. The Throat has not forgotten visitors. It has been practicing for new arrivals in the dark cambium layers of its sleep.

They arrive as whispers before they arrive as bodies.

Start with the Mote-Singers. Scales of oxidized copper stitched to hummingbird bones, wings beating at the resonance frequency of regret. They nest in the clavicles of Tree Seven’s calcified engineer, sipping calcium from her vertebrae to alloy their songbones. Their music makes the liquid-crystal walls of the bronze-bamboo catacombs ripple like speaker cones, broadcasting updates to the root-stock exchange: carbon pricing trends in the northern humus markets, fungal takeover bids for the southern mycelium exchanges. They’ve learned to mimic the click of Geiger counters, the whirr of cooling fans, the exact tone your mother used when she said goodbye. They feed on these echoes, fatten their thoraxes until they glow like tiny malfunctioning LEDs, then burst into rainbows of rusted wire when their memory banks overflow.

Lower down, where the floor breathes conveyor- belt dreams, the Threadbare Committee convenes. Once they wore lanyards and carried clipboards. Now they are suits of lichen-lace stretched over skeletons of reclaimed rebar, buttons of amber epoxy holding together memories of meetings scheduled for dates that never happened. They shuffle quorum on the mats of root- mycelium-slag, voting endlessly on motions of photosynthetic reparations and the ethical allocation of shadow during labor shortages. Their voices rasp like peeling warning stickers: *Point of order—Request to recognize the growth surge on the eastern bulkhead as a wildcat strike.* When they agree—which is never—their consensus blooms as a single mushroom shaped exactly like a raised hand, which immediately wilts into spores that spell *Tabled_Indefinitely* in extinct Helvetica.

Between the committee’s shuffling and the Mote-Singers’ hedge-fund hymns, the Knotted Girls play hopscotch. Don’t look directly at them. They are the tangles left behind when safety scissors grew tired of cutting and learned to braid instead. Each is a braid of four things that should not be braided: a length of yellow caution tape, a strand chemist’s hair caught in a centrifuge forever, a vein of pure caffeine from the old breakroom vending machine, and the sound of a child laughing right before the evacuation siren swallowed it. Their jump-rope is a live arterial cable that pulses data from the Personal Growth Chamber to the Recycling Department. When it skips a beat, someone in a memory somewhere forgets how to cry. They chant generational trauma in the key of the factory whistle, jumping so high their shadows get stuck in the ceiling-fans that once were fans and now are just lazy mandelbrot leaves, shredding darkness into recycled confetti for tomorrow’s growth-surgeons’ shedding day.

And still deeper—below the hummingbird accountants, beneath the committee’s fossilized procedures, past the playyard where regret learns double-dutch—lies the Dream Foundry where the Smeltvine births its true citizens.

Here silence pools like liquid solder. The air is thick with the scorched-sugar smell of possibilities caramelizing into actualities. In the center rests a crucible grown from the last intact hard-drive of the plant’s original AI core, its walls translucent chalcedony veined with copper whispers. Inside, dreams are smelted.

You watch one form.

First: iron filings harvested from the blood-breath of every visitor who ever stood here and refused to belong.

Second: green sugar extracted from the optimism of engineers who believed they could photosynthesis the apocalypse away.

Third: a drop of mercury-bright time stolen from the droplets that never fall, carrying the memory of every moment someone said “This isn’t sustainable” right before hitting ‘Continue’ anyway.

Stirred by vines that remember being conveyor belts but prefer the anonymity of tentacles now. Heated by the gentle fever of the whole living factory yearning to be a cathedral. When it reaches the color of a heart beating too fast to break, the crucible tips—

—and she steps out.

She is neither woman nor tree nor machine but a compromise between all three that none of them recall agreeing to. Her skin is bark-brite metal, her hair smelted fractal ferns that shimmer false-star copper when she moves. Eyes like knothole fissures: you see actual sky through them, but filtered through too many layers of green decision-making. Behind her ribs, instead of heart or lungs, a slow-motion hyperdynamo spins sugar into spite, iron into forgiveness, time into more time.

She is called Probable Error, or she calls herself that today. Tomorrow she may be Stochastic Grace or Root Exception. She is the Smeltvine’s apology for its own success, its customer-service avatar for those who come seeking refunds on extinct futures.

She smiles at you. Her teeth are perfectly shaped like stolen prototypes. “Welcome,” she says, and her voice is every recorded safety message played backward until it becomes sincere. “Would you prefer to file a complaint, or become one?”

Around her ankles, smaller things scurry: the Might-Have-Beens. Failed iterations of better worlds scraped off the assembly-line of the Singularity-that-wasn't. One is a seed-pod shaped like a social safety net that never unfurled. Another is a tiny rail-worker strike caught forever in amber resin, forever marching but never shutting down the line. They sniff at your boots, remembering leather, remembering laces, remembering feet that once walked away.

She bends, scoops up a Might-Have-Been shaped like your grandmother’s house before the flood. Offers it to you like gum. “Chew carefully,” she advises. “Some nostalgia still has teeth.”

Above you all, the growth-surgeons stir in their silent arcs, dreaming of tomorrow’s shedding. Their diamond-silicon carbide thorns are already tuning to the frequency of unmade choices, sharpening themselves on the edge of possibility.

The air tastes like decisions married to regrets now, sweet and bloody and just beginning to wine.

Take root, or run.

Either way, they grow toward movement.

Especially here.

Especially you.
