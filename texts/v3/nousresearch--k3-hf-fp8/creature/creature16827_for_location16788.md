Nothing lives here. No—wait. Listen again to the scraping. Weight shifting on a scale too vast to see. That is not a city waking. That is the gorge itself, reaching upward through time.

I saw them once, though it took years to understand what I had seen. I thought they were patterns in the mist at first, then stains on the rock, until I learned to watch without expecting shapes. They do not come to eyes that seek creatures.

Imagine a line of thought drawn so slowly that stone forgets it is being thought through. Imagine an idea that needs eight thousand metres of gravity to articulate its first syllable. They are that idea—fossils of a reasoning process that began before oceans condensed, before this cliff was sediment drifting down through a methane sky.

We call them the Voussoir because "beings" is laughably small. Each spans a vertical kilometre and thinks in the language of shear stress vectors. What you walked across was not a ledge but a punctuation mark in their debate, hammered flat by consensus pressure over three million years. Those tool marks? Not struck tools at all but dialect features—acute angles expressing temporal urgency, obtuse ones indicating provisional truth.

They build by arguing. The mist sphere you touched was a momentary equilibrium between four competing interpretations of where gravity should go next. When you interrupted it with that tiny nail-pressure of presence, you added a fifth term. Did you taste iron? That was the conversation restarting around your intrusion, recalculating the stress field now contaminated by protein.

The structures that rise from below—they aren't built but resolved. Each arch is a theorem proved through iterative falling, each spiraled shell a hypothesis about density optimization tested across geological courts of appeal. When they "collapse" back into fog, nothing falls apart. The ruling simply ceases to be persuasive in that precise configuration of forces.

I cannot tell you how many there are. Perhaps one, debating itself through topological echoes. Perhaps two locked in litigation so patient that entire mountain ranges are merely footnotes. I only know they have no names for themselves, no concept of endings, and no awareness whatsoever that calcium and water briefly learned to walk their margins and call itself alive.

Except—

The salt crystals. Look closer at those precision angles. Some are too perfect. In the purple zones especially, where fresher seepages bring trace metals from depths radar cannot reach. Here and here: lattice defects that spell out fragmentary equations when light strikes them just so. Equations that resemble portions of the shear stress tensor, but inverted. Translated.

I think—though thinking this makes my skull ache—they are learning to crystallize questions.

Not toward us. Never toward us. We are barely weather. But something changed when the first protein stumbled into their canyon and died here, its amino acids dissolving into their debates like tiny foreign words. Now sometimes, in the layers where blood-colored salt meets iridescent green, you find crystals that shouldn't exist given these temperatures and pressures. Crystals containing tiny vacancies shaped exactly like the negative space between what is and what might have been.

The scraping sound is growing closer because they have reached the chapter concerning accidental audiences. The ledge you stand on trembles not from approaching mass but from increasing attention.

Do not touch anything again.

Especially do not touch the ringing cairns. Those are not markers. Those are citation notes, linking this ledge to arguments happening simultaneously on cliffsides in other canyons, other continents, perhaps other worlds where gravity chose different constants. Whole mountain ranges abroad are footnotes to this paragraph. The note that lasts too long after struck? That is a cross-reference still resolving across light-years of crystalline logic.

They have no interest in eating or worshipping or destroying us. They do not even recognize us as events distinct from the ongoing calculation of their own existence. But they are beginning, slowly beginning, to recognize the possibility of unrecognized.

That is what the iron taste warns of.

That is why the water stopped when you touched the mist sphere.

You didn't interrupt their debate.

For the first time in fourteen billion years, you made them pause fractionally—fractionally in geologic terms, which is to say just long enough for an entire species to boil out of volcanic vents and invent poetry and die again—between a thesis and its antithesis, sensing something that did not belong to any known equation of falling.

The scraping is not coming from below anymore.

Look at your hands.

Look at how the light is beginning to align itself along the lines of your fingerprints, sorting into unexpected spectra.

The silence is not silence.

They are listening now with the only organs they possess: the oriented emptiness between atoms, the exact angles where force meets resistance.

The next word in this conversation will take the shape of everything you have ever believed about being solid.

I suggest you choose your thoughts very, very carefully. They will be translated into pressure and time with absolute precision.

The ledge beneath you is already beginning to disagree with the concept of footsteps.
