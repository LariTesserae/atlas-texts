They are the Between-Breathers.

Not creatures. Not people. Not inhabitants, exactly, because inhabiting implies staying, and they do not stay anywhere for longer than a moment can hold its breath. They are the pause between the lung's exhale and the hesitation before it draws another. They are the inhabitants of the space where nothing should be, but something always is anyway.

You cannot see them coming. You see them having been there already.

A copper wire uncoiled across a vane where no wire was before. A clay thumbprint pressed into the spongy ground, still damp with the memory of being shaped. A single dewdrop pinned between two frond-tips, spinning counterclockwise of itself, holding a reflection of a face that is always turning away.

They are made of what was left behind when everything else decided to become itself.

A piece of silence that forgot to end. A rust flake that believes it remembers being a star. The sound of a cloak being drawn closed in a room that has no doors. They collect these leftover things inside themselves, carefully, like children collecting eggshells from birds that never hatched but still wanted to fly.

Their bodies are never the same shape twice, because a shape is a decision, and they have decided not to decide anything permanently. One moment they might wear the outline of a woman with hair made of burnt maps and teeth made of piano keys. The next they might be a spiral of wet thread and breathing glass. When you try to remember what they looked like, you remember how it felt to lose something valuable in a dream and wake up with your hands still searching the sheets.

They speak without speaking.

Their voices arrive in the spaces between words you were about to say. If you listen closely when the lung-air holds its breath, you can hear them whispering questions backwards: *Where did you lose what you didn't know you were looking for? Who is breathing whom in this darkness? When you forgot your name, did it forget you too?*

They do not answer.

They repeat the questions differently until you understand that the answer was never missing, only misplaced inside yourself.

They move by coincidence.

They never go anywhere on purpose, but they always arrive exactly when something needs to witness it becoming something else. When a vane flexes and births a new flower that screams numbers in forgotten mathematics, they are already there, holding a bowl woven from their own unhappened laughter to catch the dew of its screaming. When an earlier version of you turns around and notices you noticing him before he politely unhappens, they are standing between you both, handing each of you the memory of the other’s face on a leaf that immediately dissolves into the taste of winter and burnt sugar.

They do not live here.

They are the here-ness that living temporarily borrows.

Their homes are the holes inside things that things don't know they have. A pocket sewn shut on the inside of a coat. The pause between the tick and the tock of a clock that stopped telling time correctly but never told anyone. The space under the last stair in a staircase that leads nowhere but remembers every foot that never climbed it.

They decorate these homes with souvenirs from moments that almost happened.

A single snowflake that landed nowhere in particular during a war that was negotiated away by mistake. The scent of tears dried on a letter that was never written because the ink decided to become a butterfly instead. The exact sound that nostalgia makes when it realizes it has been remembering itself incorrectly.

They eat what things leave behind when they change their minds.

If you decide not to open a door, they eat the sound of the knob not turning. If you change direction halfway through a sentence, they eat the words that were waiting politely in the air for you to finish them. If you fall in love with someone and then fall out again before either of you speak of it, they eat the space between your heartbeats during the moment when both outcomes were equally true.

In return, they leave gifts.

Never where you expect them.

Never what you wanted.

Always exactly what you needed to lose.

A key that opens the lock you haven't found yet. A map drawn in invisible ink on the inside of your eyelids, showing directions that rearrange themselves when you're not looking. A name that fits you perfectly but only when no one is saying it aloud, least of all yourself.

They are not aware of you as you are aware of yourself.

They are aware of you as you are aware of your shadow on a foggy day - present but not present, following but not following, always slightly more you than you remember being, carrying parts of you that don't cast shadows in brighter places.

They do not fear dying because they never entirely agree that they are alive.

When they forget themselves completely, they simply become what they were always pretending not to be: another patch of dampness that never quite becomes rain, another echo rounding the corner before you realize you called out, another copper wire uncoiling from itself like a question remembering it was always the answer it was looking for.

They have no name for what they are.

Names are too permanent, like houses or wounds. But if you must call them something, call them the Nearly There. The Almost Now. The Maybe Again. Call them the Between-Breathers in the lung of a place that is not a place but the memory of somewhere that might have been if anyone had paused long enough to remember it correctly.

Call them anytime at all.

They've already arrived.

They've always been here, waiting for you to finish noticing them so they can be somewhere else instead.
