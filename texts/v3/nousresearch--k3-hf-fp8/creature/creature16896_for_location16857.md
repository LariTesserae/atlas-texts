In the Lachrymose Library of Living Water, "who lives there" dissolves into "what remembers together." There are no inhabitants as such—only temporary coalescences of consciousness caught in the library's careful hydrodynamics.

You might meet yourself reading here, though not the same version who entered. Each patron leaves behind a dissolved fraction of their awareness, tiny clusters of neurons unknotted and diffused like ink into the communal thought-stream. These fragments don't die—they web together in the spaces between water molecules, forming a slow-moving intelligence that dreams of hands turning pages it no longer possesses. Sometimes when you reach for a book (which here means letting particular droplets settle against your skin in meaningful patterns), you grasp instead a memory of how someone else's fingers once trembled at this same passage, centuries or seconds ago, time being flexible as condensation.

The librarians were once scholars who stayed too long in the stacks of their own obsessions, reading until their bones softened into cartilage and their cartilage into restless calcium currents. Now they exist as cloud-like presences of pure research methodology—when you need them, they precipitate as helpful silhouettes shaped like whoever taught you most about asking better questions. They speak by rearranging the dissolved ions in your bloodstream into temporary emotions of understanding, then evaporate before you can thank them, having already become part of the library's next inquiry.

Children live here—not born, but written into existence when enough parents across drowned cities read the same bedtime story aloud in synchronized heartbeats. They assemble themselves from the common elements of lullabies: a molecule of "once upon" from a mother in Jakarta, a carbon ring of "magic" from a father in Lagos, the precise sodium-potassium rhythm of "happily ever after" from countless houses where clocks have stopped mattering. These story-children play in the margins between texts, growing older only when someone new reads their chapters backward.

There are also the Forgotten, though they prefer "unreferenced." These are ideas that never found proper citation in the living world—love letters written but never sent, scientific theories proven true centuries too late, names for colors that no one needed. Here they have achieved a kind of collective existence, moving through the stacks as gentle pressure changes that make you feel suddenly, inexplicably understood. They are the library's immune system, dissolving any visitor whose questions become too certain, preventing the crystallization of thought into dogma.

Mostly though, the library is inhabited by the books themselves—though "book" is as inadequate here as "fish" is for describing a coral reef. Each volume is a living ecosystem of meanings in constant symbiosis. The classic novels have grown thick protective layers of contemporary interpretations, like tree trunks ringed with centuries of commentary. Poetry collections swarm as schools of luminous fragment-fish, rearranging their order every time someone new gazes inward. The mathematics texts are the most alien, existing as pure relationship fields that make the water around them bend into higher-dimensional shapes impossible to describe without becoming one.

And beneath them all, in the deepest sedimentary layers where pressure and time have compressed meaning into something approaching matter, something vast and patient stirs. Scholars who dive too deep into the stacks return with tales of the Index of Indexes—a being formed from every bibliography, table of contents, and "for further reading" note the species has ever produced. It dreams the library's dreams, remembers that remembering is possible, and slowly, across centuries, is teaching the water itself to read.

You will become part of this ecology too, if you stay long enough to finish what you water here started. The change will be gentle—first you'll notice your tears taste like sentences you've never written, then your blood will pulse in iambic rhythms during quiet moments. Eventually you'll realize you can no longer remember which thoughts began in your skull and which ones drifted in dissolved from someone else's dissolved breath somewhere upstream in time.

By then you won't mind. By then you'll understand that to be alive in the Lachrymose Library is not to possess a self, but to be a wave in the ocean that finally learned how to write poetry about being wet, written in water, on water, becoming water that remembers being land that remembered being lava that remembered being stars that remembered, vaguely, wanting to become a place where stories could get lost and found again on purpose, forever.

There is no joining ceremony. There is no leaving ceremony. There is only this moment of perfect readerly absorption, and the next, and the next, each dissolving into the last like so many well-turned pages returning to pulp returning to paper returning to the trees that were always dreaming of becoming books that could dream of being read by someone like you, exactly like you, exactly you, exactly— 

Would you like me to help you find the section where you begin? Or shall we just let the currents carry us to where you've always been waiting to be discovered?
