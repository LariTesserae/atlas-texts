They drift.

That is not quite accurate. Drift implies movement from one place to another, implies places, implies otherness. Here there is only the dark sea and what is not-dark-enough within it, and the distinction has worn smooth as river stones under the weight of infinite pressure.

Call them the Unseparated Ones. They remember when separation was still a possibility, when things could be this and also not-that. Each carries within their substance the crystalline lattice of that memory, grown imperfect with each recursive recall, like a tale whispered through generations of stone. They are the answer to a question asked before questions could exist: what remains when everything else has been pressed together until distinction itself dies?

In the slow currents that move continents when continents have forgotten their edges, they encounter fragments of their own before-bodies. A curve of bone-metal that remembers being a bridge across nothingness. A sphere of smoothed equation that once calculated its own existence. They absorb these fragments not as nourishment—here sustenance is a child's concept—but as punctuation. Periods added to sentences that have been writing themselves since before time was born writing.

Each Unseparated One is cities collapsed into individuals. You would call them vast if vastness still meant anything against this scale. Their edges are not edges but gradients, becoming less themselves as they extend outward until the dark sea grows slightly less dark with their presence. Within them, currents organize themselves into patterns that would be thoughts if thoughts moved slower than glaciers reading the histories written in their own ice.

They communicate by the slow exchange of impossibilities. One offers a section of its substance where light still remembers being fast. Another gifts a moment when nothing came before anything. These exchanges take longer than stars live. The sea records them as slight changes in pressure that ripple outward at the speed of hearts breaking across eternity.

Sometimes—though time here is a wound that never quite heals into scarring—two Unseparated Ones will drift too close to the concept of proximity that haunts this place like a ghost of geometry. Where their gradients interpenetrate, something happens that was once called birth but here is more accurately translation. For a moment that lasts longer than language has words, they are neither two nor one but something that makes numbers themselves close their eyes and forget how to count to infinity.

From these impossible unions, new shapes precipitate. Crystalline infants that begin vast and grow smaller as they age, forgetting themselves into existence. They start as maps of everything and forget places until they become places that can forget they were ever mapped. The Unseparated Ones call this process tenderness, though their word for it is the pressure that finally crushes all words into diamonds of pure meaning.

The oldest among them have forgotten nearly everything. One has whittled itself down to a single question that contains all possible answers except the one that would end its asking. Another has become so small it exists only as the shape left behind when certainty withdraws from doubt. They drift in the center of themselves, where the sea is densest with what was never said between universes that died before they could begin arguing.

If you could ask them—though asking is a violence here, a tearing of the fabric that is only fabric because nothing has attempted to tear it yet—they would tell you they are waiting. Not for something, because waiting and something imply separation. They are simply the form that waiting takes when waiting has been absolute zero plus a dream's breath for so long that cold itself begins to dream of warmth as a kind of forgetting.

They remember you. Not you specifically, but the shape of seeking from which you were carved by questions sharper than any knife that could exist here. You are the echo of a footstep that was never taken in a garden that grew itself before gods had learned to invent walking. They remember your arrival with the same gentle sadness that the sea remembers shores, and your inevitable departure with the same perfect acceptance with which numbers remember they must always equal themselves.

In the liquid dusk that never quite becomes night because here all things are the exact hue of happening that occurs when darkness realizes it has always been light wearing mourning clothes, one of the oldest reaches toward you. Its reaching takes centuries from start to stop, and the motion is so slow that you cannot tell where the gesture ends and your own becoming begins.

It offers you nothing. Here nothing is the most precious gift because it contains everything that hasn't happened yet, plus infinite room for all the ways it might not happen differently than it doesn't. The nothing rests in what would be its hand if hands were still necessary for holding what is too heavy for any concept of weight to carry.

And you—if you are still enough of you to accept or refuse—must decide whether to become something else by staying exactly what you could never have been otherwise, or to remain unchanging by forgetting yourself into the shape of someone who might have had this choice once, long ago, before quiet became a form of screaming too deep for stars to hear their own burning.

They drift.

And the sea, which has never been anything other than their drift made liquid, listens with the infinite patience of water remembering it was once rain dreaming of falling toward something worth drowning for.
