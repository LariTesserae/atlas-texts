**The Resonants**

They do not live here. That term has no purchase on the plainsong of their existence, no hinge where *here* meets *elsewhere*. Instead, they *attend*—a congregation of attentions woven so finely into the rubbery ground that to lift a single thread would unravel continents. Their bodies, if such structures can be kin to flesh, are the spaces between the humming stalks: negative architectures of copper-silver absence, shaped by the vibration they refuse to absorb. They are gaps that persist. Hollows that sharpen.

When the air thickens at midday (though no sun marks midday, only the deepening sweetness of unanswerable humidity), they coalesce. Not arriving, but remembering themselves into denser arrangements. A sudden clump of misplaced shadow beneath a geometry that never cast one. A frost-pattern spreading wrong-edged across warm ground, like breath on the underside of glass. To look directly is to glimpse only the distortion: your own eyelash trembling twice where once would suffice, the afterimage of a blink you haven't taken yet.

Their communication is subtraction. Where one of the slow-pulsing formations misses a beat in its centuries-long rhythm—there, they have agreed. Where a stalk cracks fractionally off-key, shattering into dust that spells SILENCE before dissolving—a message has been both sent and intercepted. They speak in the tiny unhappenings that keep the plain from completing its final symmetry, the hairbreadth errors without which even perfection would cease to be interesting.

Each Resonant is the custodian of a single impossible equation. Not written, but enacted. One carries the proof that parallel lines must sooner or later grief themselves into contact. Another demonstrates how to quarter a moment into three equal regrets. Their favorite theorem states that for every landscape continuing endlessly without change, there exists somewhere its exact opposite occurring instantaneously within the heart of a child who has just lost their first tooth. They test this last one often, though neither children nor teeth have ever appeared here. The beauty lies in the rigorous futility.

They eat the sound of questions never asked. When a traveler (there are no travelers, only misunderstandings of terrain) pauses with "Where—" trembling unspoken on their tongue, a Resonant near that latitude flickers momentarily brighter in the ultraviolet spectrum of doubt. When the full question collapses back into resignation, they feast for days on the cascading silence of its failed syntax. This is why the plain tastes faintly of interrupted conversations to those who do not belong. Why the ground yields slightly underfoot in the exact rhythm of footsteps that stopped halfway to their destination.

Their reproduction occurs in the hinge moments of paradox. Two Resonants might sustain adjacent impossibilities long enough for the contradiction itself to climax—say, a cube of absolute darkness that nonetheless casts seven distinct shadows ranging in color from forgotten lullabies to the sound of glass becoming sand under extreme pressure. In the exquisite tension between seventh shadow and null cube, a third Resonant precipitates: neither the product nor sum of its parents, but rather the question they forgot they were asking each other.

They do not die. Instead they graduate. When a Resonant has attended the plain too perfectly—when their equation has become so elegantly unsolvable that even the plain itself begins to forget the possibility of solution—they undergo what cannot be called transformation but is perhaps best approximated as restitution. They vacate their absence suddenly, cleanly, like a breath held underwater for eons that finally remembers belonging to the atmosphere. The stalks nearest this event grow overnight to twice their height while humming in perfect unison a note that makes the dew remember being ocean. The geometries nearest cast shadows for the first and only time: exact, terrible images of what the plain might have been if it had ever needed to become anything. And in the space where the Resonant was not, the ground remains slightly more porous forever after, absorbing light at a subtly increased rate—a tiny, permanent acceleration toward the plain's eventual but impossible becoming something else.

Though they have no leaders, they maintain strict hierarchies based on indifference. The highest rank belongs to the Unwitnessed, those whose equations are so obscure that even the other Resonants have forgotten to forget them. The lowest are the Peripherals—those whose impossibilities occasionally graze the edge of comprehensibility, like the faint but undeniable scent of cinnamon that sometimes emanates from the northwest quadrant during false dawns. Between these poles exist infinite gradients of disregard, each meticulously documented in archives that exist only as the exact degree of curvature in certain otherwise straight horizons.

They have watched you reading this. Not because they care about being seen, but because your act of imagining them has momentarily provided a new variable in their oldest calculation: Recursive Observation of a System That Contains No Observers. Some among them are arguing—gently, through increasingly subtle misalignments of starless constellations—that your continued attention threatens to collapse their waveform into undesirable certainty. Others counter that your inevitable forgetting will make the eventual rediscovery sweeter. A third faction insists that both positions are already contained within the original paradox, and besides, none of this has happened yet.

In the meantime, they continue. They subtract. They attend. Beneath the unchanging sky that never quite clears, they practice the impossible art of maintaining without interfering, of witnessing without memory, of waiting for a question that must never come but must always be expected. They are the custodians of the plain's perfect incompletion, the silent audience for a performance whose final note would destroy both stage and stars. And if, on certain nights that aren't nights, you feel suddenly certain that something has just missed touching the edge of your awareness—some vast, patient thing with the texture of unremembered dreams and the smell of copper beneath rain—do not turn around. They are never behind you. They are the precise distance between your "here" and the "here" you are about to inhabit, the resonant interval in which all seeking inevitably discovers only more seeking.

They do not live here.

They ensure that no one ever will. Not completely. Not forever.

This is their kindness. This is their crime. This is their equation, humming endlessly beneath the rubbery ground: that for every place that becomes precisely itself, there must always remain some tiny, essential distance where becoming continues eternal and undetected.

You are currently occupying part of that distance. Whether you walk or stand still, question or accept, stay or leave—the calculation continues beautifully unchanged.

They subtract you gently into the ongoing.
