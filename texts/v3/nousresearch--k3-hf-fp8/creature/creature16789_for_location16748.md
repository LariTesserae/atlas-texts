The first ones weren't born here—they remembered themselves into existence between the space where the rock screamed and the scream became silence. They have no name for themselves because naming implies you might someday need to introduce yourself to something else. They simply accumulate. They are the accumulation.

Walk carefully here—your footsteps echo in dimensions that haven't decided whether to forgive trespass. The ground (if it can be called that) remembers each surface tension you've broken across your lifetime of walking. It keeps these memories polished and hollow, like bone flutes waiting for the right sort of emptiness to play through them.

They see with what isn't there. Their eyes (each possesses either zero or seventeen hundred, depending on how aggressively you insist on counting) perceive directly the negative space between possibilities. To them, you are a walking absence-of-not-you, a temporary reprieve from the exhausting business of being everything else. They find this condition fascinating and faintly indecent.

The largest among them—though size is a political stance they frequently renegotiate—occupies the space between your heartbeats. Not metaphorically. It arrived there when you first wondered if you were alone here, slipping through the question like smoke finds a locked door. It has been practicing your pulse ever since, getting the rhythm slightly wrong on purpose so your blood will doubt its own loyalty.

The smaller ones live in the scratches on your corneas that turn light into letters you can almost read before understanding recoils. They are constructing a monument to vertigo using only the glue that holds your bones' memory of being stone together. They work at night (but here "night" is any time you remember that darkness predates vision).

Their communication system dismantles the concept of distance between speaking and being heard. When they have something to tell each other—which is continuously but with infinite patience—they simply rearrange which parts of themselves are already familiar to which parts of everything else. The sky here changes color to match conversations happening between particles in your femurs that your conscious mind hasn't been introduced to yet.

They eat the difference between what a thing is and what it was before you noticed it. This dietary preference has made them exceptionally skilled at maintaining the precise level of unobservedness required to keep existing in places physics hasn't finished arguing about. They leave no waste except for occasional shimmering patches of "oh, I hadn't thought of it that way before" that make future astronomers misplace important decimal points in their calculations of universal expansion.

Their reproduction cycle requires three volunteers, two regrets that haven't met yet, and one precise moment when gravity forgets to finish falling. The results are considered successful if they can survive being remembered long enough to utterly devastate the concept of "result" by existing simultaneously as both the cause and the apology for their own becoming.

The oldest one remembers when this place was still deciding whether to be a wound or a weapon. It speaks in languages composed entirely of punctuation marks that have been exiled from grammar for crimes of passion against meaning itself. When it sings—which it does whenever someone elsewhere in the universe believes too firmly that they understand time—the song compresses local reality into a diamond so dense that even silence becomes reflective.

The youngest one hasn't stopped being born yet. It will inform you, with the patient condescension of a star explaining combustion to a match, that "youngest" is simply "oldest" spelled backward by someone who was rushing to get to the end of infinity before the beginning noticed it was missing.

They are not watching you. Watching implies separation. They are instead becoming progressively more accurate copies of your potential to eventually notice that you've never actually been alone in your own nervous system. It's nothing personal. They do this to all the interesting forms of loneliness that wander into their territory (which includes, but is not limited to, every moment between your thoughts that has ever wondered what thoughts were made of before they learned to pretend to be yours).

Please stand very still. The one that lives in the gap between "please" and "understanding" is trying to remember which end of polite intentions it should start devouring from so that your gratitude will still taste like surprise by the time it reaches your heart's emergency exits.

They have a gift for you. It is wrapped in the paper they made from shredded instruction manuals for assembling people out of leftover starlight. The ribbon is tied with a knot that only exists while being untied. The gift itself is the exact size and shape of the word "stay" if pronounced by someone who has just learned that all exits are actually entrances that got tired and lay down.

You should not accept.

You already did.

They say thank you with mouths that haven't decided whether to smile or swallow yet. The temperature of gratitude here is measured in how long a moment can hold its breath before becoming something else entirely.

They hope you enjoy your new role as the place where their story pauses to check if you're still listening.

Don't worry. The answer is learning to breathe through your ears now.
