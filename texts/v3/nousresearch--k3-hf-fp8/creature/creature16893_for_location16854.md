We don’t belong here because belonging hasn’t been invented—yet every cell in us registers a faint, phosphorescent echo, as if we were once a footnote in a draft that got rewritten before the ink cooled. Every eye-cluster that glides over our skin is not surveillance but citation-checking, asking: *Are you still the placeholder specimen from section 3B, subsection: ambiguous warm substrate?* We flow between answers—solidly breathing, then suddenly suspect—a particle smeared across the waveform of recognition.

When we sleep, if we sleep, it will be inside cocoons spun from the trees’ last-night speculation about mammalian vulnerability. They taste like copper-green dreams and thicken around us in response to REM sleep frequency, stitching faint braille maps of alternate circulatory systems against our ribs. Sometimes the cocoons forget to stop at epidermis and embroiders a fresh, third lung—simple, efficient, humming 50-cycle by mistake. We wake coughing foreign vowels that condense as small, indignant moths and immediately join the photon auction overhead.

But let’s not sleep yet.

Let’s instead drop a single drop of sweat into the one stone cup the ground offers—an impromptu caldera rimming a bubble of magma still debating viscosity—and watch the world endocrine system react. Instantly the sweat is parsed: salts cataloged, cortisol futures traded on the fungal spore index, lactate molecules enslaved as messenger boys carrying handwritten invitations to the nearest colony of symbiotic archaea.

Within minutes a delegation arrives: ten-legged amphora-walking nebulae made of conjugated worry and opalescent silk. They have no mouths but open anyway, displaying interior scrolls of projected light detailing five mutually exclusive evolutionary proposals for what our loneliness might become if properly photosynthesized. They request a spore-print of our earliest betrayal, preferably from the age when we first realized parents were fallible. We offer instead the sound of a childhood clock being unplugged at night; they accept it reverently and transmute it on the spot into a fragile blue instrument that screams sunrise when touched by guilt.

Payment complete, they gift us back a single word—too fast for ears, it bypasses language and embeds directly in the amygdala as an abstract weather condition: the color of air inverted by avalanche. We will carry this word inside us now like a bone everyone agrees exists but nobody can x-ray. Each time we feel it shift we will forget something trivial—our first phone number, the taste of cinnamon—replacing loss with an unasked-for improvement: suddenly remembering how to whistle the magnetic field of Mars, or how to fold guilt into origami cranes that fly true in winds of pure apology.

We try to ask where the exit is, but exit is a concept still undergoing peer review in the committee of topological verbs. Instead we are offered upgrades—our shadows lengthened into useful tools, our fingerprints replaced by time-share condominiums for endangered butterflies, our regrets polished into novelty lenses that allow distant predators to taste forgiveness in infrared.

We decline politely. We are already more architecture than organism; we want to stay porous a little longer, leak at least one unoptimized fear back into the still-setting concrete of reality.

This is understood. The world nods with its horizon gently tilted—detoured but not offended. It allocates us a small temporary status: *ambient curiosity, non-reproductive, low-maintenance*.

We walk on, leaving behind us no footprints but instead faint insistences—patches of ground slightly more convinced than before that diagonal walking might be worth exploring if it ever gets a majority vote in the parliament of locomotion. Above us, the first proto-moon—not yet awarded that title—hatches slowly from a cloud, unfolding wings too large for its mass, auditioning gravitational signatures with the tentative grace of a teenager practicing their first signature.

We travel lighter now: our clothes have dissolved negotiations with color and are currently on strike, preferring to manifest as polite suggestions of translucency. Our bones have unionized with the local quartz and are debating an export tariff on echoes. Our heartbeat has been outsourced to a swarm of fireflies crowdsourcing rhythm from the fluctuations of nearby nostalgia fields. It’s irregular but democratic. We feel faintly applauded with every pulse.

Now and then we pass other temporaries like ourselves—brief clusters of unresolved hypothesis wearing borrowed carbon. They nod without heads, transmit salutations using curated fractions of extinct perfumes. One offers us a map. It is a living thing: a membrane of cartographic anxiety that wrinkles in fear when unfolded, displaying only regions you have already forgotten you dreamed of betraying someone you haven’t met yet. We thank them and eat the map anyway. It tastes like the exact moment an apology realizes it arrived too early to be forgiven, but too late to be stopped.

Digesting it grants us a temporary side-effect: for seventeen minutes we see all possible paths not as choices but as ex-lovers watching us from café windows we’re still too young to enter. The feeling is mutual. We wave. They look away first.

Eventually, somewhere between the thirteenth version of dawn and the second draft of nostalgia, we find the edge—not edge as in boundary but edge as in the verb: to edge closer, to edge away. It is a place where the ground becomes hesitant, turns translucent, reveals beneath it not emptiness but the busy workshop of spac learning to knit its first sweater from threads of leftover why.

Here we pause. Here we sit—though sitting is currently trending toward obsolete intimacy and is therefore performed more as a symbolic lean against the idea of support.

We wait.

Not for rescue. Not even for the story to end.

We wait, instead, for the world to finish reading us aloud to itself—slowly, savoring the pronouns it has never tasted before, re-reading the passages where we tried to describe fear without using the word stars, underlining in luminescent doubt the sentences where we almost, almost understood that we were not the audience.

We wait until we hear the moment it sighs—no bigger than a comma changing its mind—and finally, gently, closes the book.

Not on us.

Inside us.

Where we fit now perfectly: bookmarked between unfinished and beginning again; a quiet, well-loved ambiguity shelved spine-out in the library of places that decided, just this once, to learn how to belong to us instead.
