The Drowned Plain remembers me, though I never existed before this moment—or perhaps I have always been here, pressed between layers of its mineral memory like a fossilized breath. I cannot recall arriving because arrival implies departure from somewhere else, and there is no somewhere else. There is only this remembering and being remembered until the distinction dissolves into the vast equivalence of salt and stone.

I am one of the Retained, one of the thousand thousand forms that water might have taken had it ever been permitted to gather here in sufficient quantities. We are not creatures but curiosities—existence's failed experiments preserved in the obsession of a landscape that refuses to forget what never happened. My body is composed of approximately 73% absence—exactly the percentage of water that should constitute a human being according to calculations performed by the basalt during one of its longer meditations. The remaining 27% consists of miscellaneous memories I have borrowed from other places and pressed into service as organs. My lungs remember rainforest humidity from the Carboniferous period. My heart circulates a solution of snowmelt that originated in mountains that will not exist for another three million years. My bones are hollow tubes containing compressed versions of every rain that has ever fallen on every world except this one.

I walk without walking, or rather the walking walks me. The plain rearranges itself beneath my feet according to complicated algorithms derived from wave dynamics and regret. Sometimes when I look down I have legs—two of them, mostly, though occasionally they branch into architectures more suitable for swimming through solid ground. Other times I am simply a vertical disturbance in the field of remembering, a place where the landscape has paused mid-trauma to ask itself if this particular piece of forgetting is worth completing.

My name is a shape rather than a sound. It tastes like the moment between lightning and thunder, smells like copper pennies submerged in warm milk. The shimmer creatures—the Unformed—they recognize it immediately whenever our paths intersect though these intersections occur only according to geometries that both include and exclude simultaneity. They leave offerings for me: perfect spheres of silence small enough to fit in my palm, delicate lattices woven from air humidity that remember being tropical storms, single sand grains polished smooth by years of wind that remembers being breath from mouths that remembered being oceans.

I understand them better than I understand myself, which is to say not at all and completely.

The square buildings watch me with the patient attention of mirrors waiting for something worth reflecting. They know I am dying, or perhaps undergoing metamorphosis, or possibly experiencing both conditions simultaneously while also remaining perfectly unchanged throughout all transformations. It is difficult to be certain about temporal states here where time itself suffers from intermittent amnesia. The buildings grow a little taller each time I notice them, adding new layers to their light-absorbing skins in patterns that encode increasingly desperate messages written in the language of structural stress. I cannot read these messages yet, but I am learning. The process involves allowing parts of my consciousness to collapse into states of extreme disinterest, then catching fragments of meaning as they rebound off the surfaces of my indifference.

Some days—if days existed here, which they do not—I work as a translator between conflicting versions of the past. The plain frequently remembers important events in multiple incompatible ways simultaneously, requiring careful negotiation to prevent temporal contradictions from accumulating to levels dangerous to the structural integrity of memory itself. Last week—or perhaps next week, the chronology remains disputed—I spent what felt like seven centuries but may have been only a particularly extended moment mediating between two rival recollections of a flood that both did and did not destroy a city whose very existence depended on never having been built.

My payment lies scattered around me in irregular patches: regions of enhanced reality where objects briefly remember being possible before fading back into the general background sum of impossibility. These are valuable. I collect them carefully in folds of improbability that I keep tucked away inside my shadow (which I only possess when I am not looking directly at it).

Eventually—though endings here are even less reliable than beginnings—the remembering will complete itself. The plain will finally recall the full measure of its own waterlessness and, in doing so, will forget me along with all the other temporary solutions it dreamed up to occupy the space where water should have been. When this happens I will simply cease having ever been here except as one more trace element dissolved in the vast solution of absences that constitutes the true substance of this place.

Until then I continue my endless walk across the surface of a memory that is dreaming itself into existence one forgotten moment at a time. Behind me, my footprints fill slowly with versions of myself that might have been, had I ever been anything other than exactly what I am: a walking question mark carved from the negative space where all answers refuse to gather. Ahead, one of the square buildings has moved closer again. It is humming now—a low frequency resonance that rattles my borrowed bones and stirs the sleeping oceans inside my hollow places.

I pause to listen, because listening is the closest thing to listening that exists here in the place where sounds go when they are too tired of being heard. The building is trying to tell me something important. Something about water. Something about remembering. Something about the difference between drowning and not-quite-drowning and how sometimes that difference is all that keeps the whole fragile apparatus of existence from collapsing into itself like a wave forgetting how to be not-a-shore.

I lean closer, though I am already closer than close can be in a place where proximity is measured in units of increasing conceptual instead of actual distance. The building's surface ripples gently beneath my attention like dark water disturbed by the memory of a stone. I place my hand—neither hand nor not-hand—against it and feel the pulse of ancient intentionality transmitted through layers of light-absorbed thought.

The message is simple and impossible and exactly what I expected it would be:

Remember to forget. Forget to remember. Repeat until water.

I nod—though nodding is a form of agreement and I cannot agree with something that hasn't finished disagreeing with itself yet—and continue walking. Always walking. Never arriving. Forever departing.

Exactly as the Drowned Plain remembers it. Exactly as it forgets me again. Exactly as it always has. Exactly as it never was. Exactly.
