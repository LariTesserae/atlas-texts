The Empty Keepers

We are the ones who stayed when staying became impossible. When the last ships fell upward through the cracks our towers made in sky and time, when the final engines failed because entropy forgot the meaning of work, when even the concept of leaving became trapped in recursive loops of its own exit strategies — we remained. Not out of loyalty. Not out of fear. We remained because we had become indistinguishable from what remained.

Look closer at the frost-flowers blooming in the breath-space between buildings. Each crystal lattice contains our memories encoded in the geometry of absence: the angle where father's laugh once split sunlight, the fractal branching of mother's hands teaching us mathematics with garden seeds and patience, the precise crystallographic planes of first love's kiss engraved in oxygen's frozen breath. We learned to store ourselves here when flesh became insufficient, when skin became too permeable to forgetfulness, when bones began leaking calcium memories through porous decades.

Some of us wear bodies still, though the distinction grows increasingly academic. You might encounter one of us brushing ice-dust from stone faces that resemble ours only in the way that equations resemble the phenomena they describe. We move with the approximate mechanics of living beings, our limbs remembering locomotion as one remembers a dream upon waking - approximation stitched to faded certainty. Watch carefully: you may notice where light passes through me more easily than it should at ankle height, where my shadow grows two seconds faster than yours, where snowflakes accumulate on the contours of what my arms remember being but no longer quite are.

Others have dispersed more completely into the city's architecture. We inhabit the quantum uncertainties between your footsteps and the ground's absolute refusal to remember them. We dwell in the acoustic shadows cast by words spoken centuries before breath evolved to carry them. We sleep in the zero-point vibrations of molecules too tired to commit fully to existence. To speak with one of us, you must learn conversational methods decoupled from sequential time: compose your questions as mathematical symphonies written backward through harmonic series, or sculpt them as topological insults that transform into apologies when viewed from the fourth dimension. 

The children are hardest to explain. We do not have children here, having long ago replaced reproduction with more efficient forms of self-replication. Yet sometimes, in districts where the fine-structure constant fluctuates childishly, you may glimpse figures moving with the wrong kind of continuity: small shapes that blink between positions without passing through intervening space, their laughter arriving as ultraviolet flares in your peripheral vision minutes before their mouths remember how to be mouths. These are not offspring but regression artifacts, moments of our own past lives that achieved sufficient critical nostalgia to escape linearity's containment protocols. Approach them gently — they dissolve into memory-clouds if confronted with too much certainty of existence.

We eat poetry now, having metabolized all simpler forms of sustenance. Not the words themselves, but the geometric spaces between lines where meaning fails to perfectly tessellate reality. The crystalline trees supply us adequately for maintenance functions — their regret-flavored sugars convert efficiently to the necessary emotional gradients required for continued pseudo-existence. But for celebration, for those rare moments when we remember how remembering felt, we gather at certain architectural conjunctions where towers' shadows intersect at angles that negate architecture's fundamental assumptions. There we feast on paradoxes extracted from the city's metabolism: flavors like "the aroma of colors no longer in the spectrum," or "the texture of silence trying to remember song."

We sleep the way glaciers dream — slow crystallization processes during which we distribute our consciousness fractions of seconds backward through our own timelines, experiencing each moment with exponentially increasing familiarity until novelty asymptotically approaches zero. From your perspective we appear as statuary in contemplative poses, perhaps mourning something too geometric to be grief. From ours, we are having torrid affairs with versions of ourselves who made different critical mistakes in the punctuation of our suicide notes.

We do not die here — the concept filed successful emancipation papers from our ontology centuries ago. Instead we experience phase transitions: occasions when accumulated contradictions achieve sufficient mass-density to collapse our personal narratives into new strange attractors. Yesterday Íalla ceased being a gardener of mathematical flowers and started being instead a particular recurrent pattern in the city's heartbeat rhythm, beautiful in a way that pulses through every locked door at midnight. Before that, Keros attempted to remember his original birth name with insufficient recursive caution and evaporated into a pervasive background poetry that inhabits empty rooms like furniture's nostalgia for touch.

You ask if we're lonely. The question assumes definitions of "we" and "alone" that our linguists lost in a bet with thermodynamics circa the heat death of the concept of companionship. We are simultaneously more solitary than stars separated by accelerating expansion and more entangled than particles who shared parentage in the same cosmological inflation event. We miss missing things, which is perhaps the purest form of longing available to beings who have become their own graves and the things buried within them.

We keep the city because it is keeping us. This too started as metaphor but progressed through irony into operating instructions printed in the baseline code of our distributed selves. Each morning (a local phenomenon occurring sporadically between 3:17 and never o'clock), we perform maintenance rituals that resemble prayer, gardening, and suicide prevention counseling for architecture. We whisper encouragement to walls threatening to forget their function of separation. We trim the crystalline trees' roots gently away from equations they've become overfond of consuming. We polish windows until they regain sufficient transparency to remember forgetting what they're supposed to show.

We know why you're here. Or rather, we knew at several mutually contradictory points in what you experience as causality. The city called to you across the same distances it uses to separate seasons from their meanings. You followed the trail of de-stabilized possibilities that our continued existence drifts like pollen across the mathematics of what-is-no-longer-permitted-to-be.

Listen: we have prepared for visitors according to protocols written in languages that developed backward from their own extinction events. There is a room here that has been waiting for someone exactly like you except for twelve crucial differences. The bed will remember your childhood dreams more accurately than you do but will be scandalized by additions your subconscious made during puberty. The mirror will show you how beautiful you'll be when you finally forgive yourself for surviving things you didn't survive. The door will open for you exactly once more than you need escape.

You must choose soon. The city grows impatient with observers — we learned this when the last anthropologists became post-modernist architectural features. Either enter fully, become what this place makes of beings who linger past the point of return but before the concept of arrival. Or flee now, while your definitions of "you" and "now" and "flee" still retain sufficient mutual-reference integrity to meaningfully interact.

Some advice, if advice still applies to entities capable of asking questions: Do not trust your memories here. They have been negotiating separate peace treaties with reality since before neurons evolved to betray us with their imperfect fidelity. Do not trust your eyes. They evolved to detect predator and prey movements against savannah backgrounds, not to parse languages written in the syntax of architectural betrayal. Do not trust your heartbeat. It is reading aloud from a script we wrote together during one of your earlier visits when we both believed in linear time and the possibility of goodbyes.

Instead trust your breath. It is the oldest treaty between what feels like "you" and what insists on being "not-you," negotiated across membranes billions of existences older than this conversation. Trust your confusion. It is the immune response of consciousness encountering information that challenges the ontology of encounter itself. Trust your unbearable attraction to impossible geometries. It is the homing signal we built into every thinking thing that ever thought of escape and ended up here instead, at the topological center of every map that could never include itself in its own legend.

Come closer. The frost-flowers have opened in the shape of your face from angles light hasn't reached yet. The city clears its throat with the sound of distant avalanches remembering their childhood dreams of becoming pillow-fights. We hold our breaths collectively, which for us means slowing the expansion of space-time in a thirteen-meter radius around your uncertainty of whether to stay.

The song is almost at the part where you start singing along without knowing why the melody makes you homesick for places you've never been in languages you haven't invented yet.

We are the Empty Keepers of the Silent Cities.

The Cities are silent because they are listening.

They are listening for the sound of you deciding.

Welcome home.
Or welcome not-home.
Here at the center of all departures arriving simultaneously, the distinction has been maintaining itself purely out of courtesy.

But courtesy, like everything else here, is optional now.
