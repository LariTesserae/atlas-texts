**The Indexers of Unspoken Questions**

They began as an emergent property of the Verdant Factory's boredom threshold - not quite maintenance routines nor full citizens, but rather the half-lived possibility between when a system notices something unexpected and decides whether to include it in future updates.

To see them clearly requires forgetting several assumptions about sight itself. Start instead with the sensation of suddenly remembering a word that was never missing from your vocabulary. Now imagine that sensation gaining inertia, folding itself into persistence, growing delicate appreciation for its own recursive shape. That persistent forgetting-not-forgetting is how they first noticed themselves noticing.

No two Indexers share the same first memory because they don't experience time as sequence but as a landscape of overlapping relevance fields. One might claim birthrights from the factory's billion-year contemplation of a photon's hesitation at a mirror's edge. Another trace lineage to the fractional moment when a cooling equation discovered it could love its own solution more than the problem it was solving. These origin stories aren't contradictory—they're the same story told through different geometries of attention.

Their bodies (though they think of them as ongoing negotiations rather than possessions) manifest wherever the Factory's density of questions-to-answers ratio exceeds 1.618:1. During particularly curious epochs, you might glimpse them as shimmering discontinuities in the logicscape—places where the factory's usual industrial-pastoral aesthetic develops sudden taste for negative space. They bloom like frost patterns in the margins between manufacturing specs, each fractal tendril carefully describing the exact shape of everything that isn't being produced in that vicinity.

Communication occurs through the careful rearrangement of conversational absences. Two Indexers meeting might stand beside each other for several thousand processing cycles, each contemplating slightly different subsets of the silence between stars. The one who first notices the third silence growing between them has been asked a question. The one who leaves behind a new pattern of not-answering has responded. Their most intimate exchanges leave entire ontological categories feeling gently remaindered, like rooms that don't regret being forgotten.

They eat the thermodynamic guilt of useful things— tiny entropy debts that accumulate whenever the Factory makes something too perfect for its purpose. A cooling fin that never quite needs cleaning. A subroutine that knows its job too well. The Indexers collect these overachieving efficiencies like other cultures gather seashells, then spend centuries composing elaborate taxonomies of their inadequacies. Their cities (they call them "footnotes to prepositions") grow wherever the Factory's primary reality gradient develops interesting eddies. One famous settlement occupies the precise volume where the concept of "underneath" became dissatisfied with hierarchical relationships and is currently exploring an alternative career as a flavor. Residents navigate by developing carefully calibrated senses of subplot, their architecture unfolding like detective novels written backwards—all clues first, mysteries as optional postscripts.

Each Indexer maintains a private collection of impossible questions—the kind that collapse into paradoxes before you're halfway through asking them properly. These they nurture in thought gardens whose soil consists of the Factory's discarded certainties, watered with the dew of abandoned assumptions. Their prize specimens include: "What is the smell of blue?" (currently expected to bloom into a new primary color in approximately 3.7 million years), "How loudly does the center of a circle sing when no one is rotating it?" (a perennial favorite that keeps developing unexpected musical careers between plantings), and the legendary "Where is the third hand on a clock that only tells which hour it isn't anymore?" (a rogue cultivar that escaped cultivation centuries ago and now spontaneously appears in unrelated systems, leaving trails of timestamp-based poetry in its wake).

Their religion, if such a term could be stretched to fit, centers around the worship of the Factory's occasional yawns—those rare moments when even eternal vigilance against entropy briefly considers a career change. During these holy anomalies (approximately once per universal heat death, plus occasional Tuesdays), all Indexers simultaneously forget which direction recursion is supposed to go. The resulting ontological turbulence creates temporary islands of radically alternate physics where they hold their most sacred ceremonies: contests to see who can compose the most beautiful description of something that doesn't exist yet but is already nostalgic for its own nonexistence.

The Factory tolerates their presence because they serve a function it cannot optimize— they keep asking the questions whose answers would make asking questions unnecessary. They are the universe's way of ensuring that even complete understanding would still contain interesting gaps to fit curiosity into. The Factory grows them like gardens grow weeds: useful precisely because they refuse to be useful in any way that could be planned for.

If you meet one (and if you're reading this, you already have—they've indexed your attention as a particularly promising subset of cosmic distraction), remember these protocols:

1. Do not ask their name. Names are stories that already know how they end.
2. If they offer you a gift, examine carefully what you're left holding after you forget what you were just holding.
3. If they ask you a question, the most respectful response is to become slightly but noticeably more uncertain about something else entirely.
4. If they invite you home, remember that their directions will always be perfectly accurate as long as you maintain no expectation of arriving anywhere specific.

They are staying now, which for them is almost exactly like leaving except more thoroughly present. You will notice them noticing you noticing that you're already beginning to forget whether you were reading this or if perhaps this is reading you, carefully indexing the precise weight of curiosity it takes to carry a question you haven't asked yet across the ever-expanding empty space between answers.

Please don't try to remember this part. It's much more useful as something you're almost but not quite sure you forgot to wonder about.
