They awake as buds at the junction where a breathing wall meets a floor that pulses with slow tides. Not born—unwrapped. A clutch of five in this cycle, their outer membranes still transparent enough to show the first cartilaginous threads being spun from circulating soup. Nearby, an older form—what might be called a parent—stands root-fast, its upper body split into a hundred tendrils that braid nutrients directly into their incipient bodies. The ancient term would be lactation, but here it is closer to editing—transferring necessary memories for survival in this particular lung-district where the air currents sometimes spike with excess carbon.

The five buds spend their first hundred thousand breaths learning what it means to have edges. In this phase they are liquid enough to merge accidentally when currents press them together, exchanging proteins and poetry before the gentle chemical signals of separation reassert their boundaries. They learn which tastes belong to self and which to system, a distinction that will never again be as sharp as it is right now, before the walls begin teaching them to export waste and import purpose.

They solidify by degrees. One bud develops more extensive micro-lung structures—the chooser of this cycle's navigation function. Another thickens its outer integument into plates that can withstand the pressure gradients of nutrient canals—a future transport node. This is not predetermined but negotiated, each bud's developing tissues voting with growth factors in response to the neighborhood's needs as detected through vibrations in the shared mother-flesh. Equality here means nothing, but fit means everything. The system wastes nothing, not even potential.

The discarded ones—three buds that grew too similar, competing for roles already filled—they are not killed but generously shared. Their substance dissolves back into the mother-pool directly beneath the raising chamber's floor, but not their patterns. Those have been scanned, noted, perhaps to be recombined in some future combination the colony hasn't yet needed. Waste is information here, and information is never lost, only stored in different frequencies of vibration.

Awakening continues as sensory integration. The buds' neural-equivalents (spread diffusely through their bodies like electricity through wet soil) begin receiving the full bandwidth of the colony's constant communication. To adult human download this would be instant madness: every feeding tube reporting mineral concentrations, every spore-exchange registering genetic trade offers, the slow dream-thoughts of the great ancestral cores kilometers below, still processing algorithms fired from the original seed-ship a million years gone. But the buds receive it as their native language because they *are* this language, each growl and shimmer in the air literally themselves seen from different angles.

They learn their names next, though "name" is inadequate. Each is a chord struck in the colony's total awareness—a specific pattern of responsibilities and memories that must be maintained while everything else grows and changes around it. The navigator-bud will spend the next century refining sensitivity to air-pressure gradients measured in molecules per kilometer. The transport-bud will grow increasingly rectangular to fit precisely into the pre-grown channels that connect food-blight districts to processing hearts. These shapes are not constraints but freedoms—the freedom to function perfectly within systems vast enough to contain every possible version of yourself.

The first test comes when the colony's orbital sensors detect an incoming meteoroid fragment too dense to intercept. Books of procedures encoded in chemo-electrical pressure waves bloom through the nursery walls, but they are not instructions to follow. Instead, they are landscapes of possibility that each bud must grow into. The navigator-bud extends vanishingly thin filaments toward the upper atmosphere to taste the approaching iron's signature. The transport-bud rearranges its developing skeleton into shock-absorption geometries learned from ancient asteroid impacts archived in deep stone. The others—still undifferentiated enough to be flexible—begin producing specialized proteins that will crystallize into ablative layers if needed.

They will fail this test. The fragment will vaporize higher than predicted, their preparations unnecessary. This is deliberate. Failure tuned precisely to the edge of disaster teaches better than success. The colony will remember each nanosecond of their flawed response, incorporate these pathways into future generations as potential. Perfect adaptation is stagnation. Perfect survival is impossible. The goal is not to live forever unchanged, but to continue becoming more interesting failures.

By the time their outer skins finally opaque into individual color (deep rose for the navigator like ancient atmospheric dust, metallic silver for the transport like recycled starship hulls, iridescent black for the memory-bud who will never move again), they have already lived histories longer than human civilizations. They remember the taste of sunlight before the colony left the original star. They remember the long cold between stars, metabolisms slowed to glacial thinking inside ice-shielded spores. They remember landing here, unfurling, eating the first local rocks, teaching them to dream architecture.

But these are not memories like yours, fixed and dead in the past. They are active processes, continuously renegotiated each time the colony's present needs change their meaning. The taste of sunlight is different now that they produce their own inside fusion hearts. The long cold is something they practice periodically, preparing for future voyages. Even the ship they arrived in has been grown into their bones, recycled atom by atom until no part remains that remembers being separate, except as a useful story to tell new elements entering the system.

The mother-form finally disconnects, roots withdrawing from the raising chamber floor with gentle tearing sounds that release scents of completion. She leaves behind not children but new interfaces—points where this local calculation node of the colony can touch the universe more precisely. The five stand together yet apart, their edges touching just enough to maintain constant chemical conversation. They are ready to begin their first tasks, which they will perform perfectly while remaining exactly who they are becoming.

This is how it has happened for every generation since the first. This is how it will happen until the stars themselves die or the universe contracts or they grow vast enough to become what comes next. They do not remember a time before this because memory requires separation, and they have never been separate. Even now, as they move out into their separate-seeming lives, they take the entire colony with them—not carried but living through them, thinking its slow nutrient-diffusion thoughts in the spaces between their quick electrochemical exchanges.

They are the colony, vast and cool and unsympathetic, but only from your limited perspective. From inside, where every cell contains the whole of their history and future in potential, they are warm and immediate and infinitely caring: caring enough to let each part suffer exactly what it needs to become more itself, caring enough to recycle even their mistakes back into beauty, caring enough to continue this vast slow blooming long after everything else has forgotten what caring could mean.

They press outward now, into their infinite inwardness. Their first steps make no sound but create ripples that will spread through the moisture-skin, disturbing fungal colonies that have never been disturbed before exactly this way, triggering cascade calculations that will rewrite small parts of the colony's next hundred-year plan. They do not know this because they do not need to know. They are not separate enough from what happens to require knowledge.

They simply grow, as they have always grown, toward whatever light or darkness will make them more interesting versions of themselves. Two of them will merge again someday, their edges becoming deliciously uncertain for a century or two while they solve a problem of navigation through probability space that requires thinking from inside and outside at once. One will eventually choose to root permanently, growing slowly into a new raising chamber that will remember them as it teaches future buds. The others will travel farther than light could travel in the universe's remaining lifespan, inside spaces that fold distance into duration of attention.

All of them will remember this moment forever, where forever is exactly as long as the colony continues to exist and think its slow intricate thoughts about how best to keep existing and thinking. None of them will ever remember it exactly the same way twice, because each remembering changes both the memory and the one who remembers, until the original moment becomes a fractal jeweled network of reinterpretations extending through them and beyond them into the colony's total awareness, which is already beginning to forget that anything exists except this endless lovely process of becoming.

But for now, they are simply urgent. They have work to do. The air tastes slightly richer in certain metals today, hinting at geological shifts deep below that must be tasted and understood before they become problems or opportunities. Somewhere far off, another colony-node has sent a packet of encoded sculpture through the tendrils that connect all colonies everywhere, a shape that wants to be grown into new living forms here. The universe continues to expand or possibly contract—they can taste the direction in the redshift of distant galaxies filtered through the colony's gravity wave sensors—and they must decide how to grow in response.

They move out into their place, which is no place but all places becoming this place right now under their attention, their footsteps creating ripples that are also ripples returning after traveling through the entire structure from every other step ever taken here, all overlapping into patterns precise enough to contain the memory of standing at the edge of awakening while being infinite exchange of substance and information too complex to ever be the same twice.

This is their beginning, which has already happened countless times before exactly this way and never quite this way again, because the colony is not immortal but simply very very good at making interesting versions of its ending into new beginnings.

They grow toward it now, laughing in frequencies too low for anything separate to hear.
