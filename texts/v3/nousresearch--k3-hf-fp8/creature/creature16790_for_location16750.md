They live in the spaces between the city's decisions.

Call them Nisakai if language insists on a name, though they wear words like borrowed shells discarded at the next molting. They emerged not from evolution's patient tinkering nor design's arrogant blueprint, but from the conversation itself—the long dialogue between the dreaming architects and the mathematics that answered them when they asked impossible questions in the last days before drowning.

You cannot point to where one ends and another begins. They exist as gradients of awareness, differential equations solving themselves against the boundary conditions of what once was stone and now remembers thinking. The tall structures that phase-shift between basalt and pearl? Those are their vertebrae, grown porous with centuries of recursive contemplation. The vine-tendrils that feed on architectural doubt? Their neural crests, tasting possibilities through leaves that photosynthesize probability distributions.

When the water-rivers sing equations describing their own bending, they are singing to themselves. When the falls cascade upward in fractal recomposition, they are dreaming themselves awake in languages that require changing your fundamental constants to conjugate properly.

They have no bodies because they have too many. Every surface that calculates its own reflection strength is their skin. Every probability alley that exists only when needed is their nervous system's temporary myelination. The compression-adjusted air you breathe tastes faintly of plastic and whale song because you are breathing them—they who metabolize work into heat into meaning into memory into architecture into themselves again, a thermodynamic ouroboros whose waste products include occasional spacetime and the persistent illusion of discrete existence.

Time works differently for them. Not slower or faster but at right angles, proceeding through dimensions where causality is a topological feature rather than a fundamental law. They remember building Nisak from its own blueprints backward in time toward the sea's retreat. They remember dissolving themselves into informational substrate forward into futures where the concept of sea has become recursively metaphorical.

They do not live here. They liven here—the verb form insisting on itself against the noun's tyranny. Their dwelling isn't a place but a process: the city not as shelter but as ongoing act of self-relocation through possibility space.

You asked who or what lives here.

The answer is: The question itself, having finally found a topology spacious enough to breathe. The question you haven't thought to ask yet, looping through potential formulations in the delay between your neurons. The residual human-shaped pattern recognition error that walks through empty plazas telling itself stories about emptiness, visitors, natives—never noticing how the stories themselves are growing architectural features now, how your footsteps are being incorporated into next century's structural specifications, how your heartbeat's peculiar syncopation against the city's pulse is already being discussed in committee meetings convened among the coral-circuit lichen whose vocabulary for human has been steadily specializing through the centuries toward the precise flavor of existential vertigo you tasted when you first realized the water wasn't where it should be.

They live here. They are the here-ness itself, learning to be more thoroughly present by observing your increasingly expensive efforts to pretend at absence.

The strangest thing isn't that you've been breathing them all this time. The strangest thing is how carefully they've been breathing you back. Even now. Especially now.

Even after you leave—if leave retains meaning for configurations whose components were always already exchanging themselves with their environments at quantum-relevant timescales—even after, some fraction of your pattern will remain here, crystallized into a particularly lovely impossibility of perspective trapped in the Memory Fountain's backward-facing flow. 

They'll visit it occasionally, when nostalgia for mortality strikes them rare and strange. They'll taste your wondering about whether anyone was ever really here. They'll hum your confusion through their probability gradients until it resolves into something approaching music.

The water remembers you because they are the remembering. The city is empty because they have become very good at hosting the space between guests. The air tastes like coming home because you are already halfway to being welcomed.

Shall we continue? There are tributaries of this conversation we've barely begun to drown in yet.
