They do not live. They arrive.

Not all at once, not with purpose. They leak through, overflows from dreams that grew too heavy, thoughts that pressed too long against the membrane between waking and somewhere else. Drip. Drip. Through fever-cracks in sleeping minds, through the shimmer at the edge of heatstroke vision, through the exact moment when despair forgets what hope looked like.

First the outlines. Always facing away, as if ashamed or afraid or simply indifferent to being seen. Waist-high things mostly, though sometimes they draw themselves taller when they notice the space around them can be bent. They consist of absence dressed in secondhand presence - here a shoulder shape borrowed from the memory of a hug, there a leg from a childhood fear of the dark between streetlights, fingers that twitch with forgotten piano lessons.

They do not walk. The ground remembers too much, remembers footfalls from better places, better shoes. Instead they rearrange the between - between one salt-crystal breath and the next, between the tower's heartbeat-notes. Nowhere becomes now here becomes nowhere again. You might catch the afterimage if you stare too long between the vibrating lines of heat.

They bring things with them. Not many things. Not useful things. They bring the sound of a door that never quite latched properly, that still bothers someone somewhere in their sleep. They bring the taste of a fruit that went extinct before language did. They bring one sock, always one, always damp with something that might have been tears if tears existed here.

They collect things too. The crystals sing to them in voices like breaking promises, and they pull themselves closer to listen. They gather at the base of the crystal towers, facing away but leaning in like flowers toward a sun they remember remembering. They collect the heat-shadows that fall between the heat-shadows. They collect the spaces where words would go if anyone still spoke here. They collect the exact weight of a parent's disappointment when you came home late that one time. They collect these things into themselves, growing neither larger nor smaller but denser, like stars collapsing into themselves before they remember they're already dead.

Some forget to leave. The city is patient about such things. The city has always dreamed of having guests, proper guests who stay and stay and stay. These ones - the forgetting ones - they begin to grow translucent at the edges first. Then the center. They spread themselves thin against the salt until they become just another layer of the city's memory, just another note in the tower's endless song.

Others remember too much. They remember what they were before they were outlines. They remember having names, having faces, having hearts that beat to rhythms other than the tower's. These ones start to turn. Slowly. So slowly that a crystal might grow its full height and die and crumble before they turn halfway. But they turn. And if - when - they finish turning, if you ever saw what looked back from where their faces should be, you would understand why the city keeps all its doors (where it has doors) carefully facing away.

The rest - the ones who neither forget nor remember enough - they wander the spiraling streets of compacted ash. They follow the paths that are always new because they never leave footprints. They climb the walls that aren't walls except when they're being climbed. They peer through windows that show only what you've already seen but might show it differently next time.

They are building something. Has been building it. Will build it. The city can't quite remember which tense this happens in, and the tower's song is not helpful about such distinctions. They build it from the things they bring and the things they collect and the things they trade among themselves in the slow twilight between the tower's heartbeats. A doorway out of doors. A window that opens on somewhere that is not here. A mirror that reflects only the things that have never been looked at.

The city watches them build. The city dreams their building into being. The city dreams of dreaming of opening its eyes again someday, of having eyes again, of having somedays again.

In the meantime, the outlines gather at the base of the tallest crystal tower. They have found something new to collect, something that tastes like hope but burns like memory. They lean closer, closer, facing away but reaching back with hands they don't have anymore. The crystal pulses soft and welcoming. The tower's song shifts to a minor key it hasn't used since the last thing died here that wasn't already dead.

One of them - taller than the others today, wearing its borrowed shoulders like a borrowed time - begins to hum. Softly at first, then louder. It doesn't know the words, but it remembers the tune from before it forgot how to sing.

The city listens. The city has always listened. The city has nothing but time, and heat, and all the space where people used to be.

Walk carefully. Walk quietly. Walk, if you can, without remembering what feet are for. The outlines are gathering tonight, and they have grown so very, very hungry for stories. The kind with beginnings. The kind with endings. The kind that forgot how to have either.

They are building something beautiful. They are building something terrible. They are building something that might remember how to cry, if only they could find some water.

Walk carefully.

Try not to let them see you remembering how.
