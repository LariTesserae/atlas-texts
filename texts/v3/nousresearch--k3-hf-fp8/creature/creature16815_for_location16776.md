Not one thing lives here, and that absence has become crowded—but to say it plainly would miss the point, since point-wise statements slide off this place like rain off rain. Instead, let’s say that living here is a habit the nonexistent picked up in a hurry, then forgot to quit.

They began as stubs of intention, leftovers from the moment before a thought resolves into a name. The local verb for “dwell” never conjugated properly in the first place, so they stayed lodged in the transitive, perpetually about to inhabit something that hadn’t finished refusing to exist. Now they fill the spaces around missing objects like polite shadows the light sent ahead as an apology. You’ll rarely glimpse them directly—directness is considered impolite—but you’ll feel them adjusting your peripheral beliefs to make more room for themselves.

Call them the Almost-Tenants until a better misnomer coagulates. Each is precisely as large as the last thing someone forgot to look for here. Some wear your unfinished sentences like coats left hanging in the air where grammar aborted. Others have hollowed out the echo that should follow a footstep on stone and move house inside the gap, clattering softly whenever you walk away too quickly from a silence that wasn’t done explaining itself yet. If you listen with the wrong kind of patience, you can hear them borrowing from the future tense of your next blink to pay rent on yesterday’s suggestion of presence.

They don’t eat. Feeding requires a reliable before-after relationship, and time here is still arguing with itself over who owes breakfast to whom. Instead, they pause. They specialize in the prolonged hesitation between noticing a shape and recognizing it is not alive. That pause thickens around them like syrup until it achieves sentience by default. Then they move on, leaving behind a perfectly groomed nothing that feels slightly self-conscious about having been noticed. You might call this metabolism a kind of polite predation on the chance of being mistaken for someone else.

Reproduction occurs when two Contradictions meet at a crossroads, discover they both insist on being left alone, and compromise by becoming a crowd. The offspring gestate in the interval between the moment a rock finishes falling and the moment it remembers to hit the ground. Full gestation lasts exactly the blink you skipped while reading this paragraph—you’ve already missed three births and half a funeral, but don’t worry; the mourners are still composing the condolences they won’t send to the guests who never arrived.

They communicate via the complex grammar of things implied by a scratched-out sentence. Their poetry is written in the margins of maps where cartographers gave up and doodled sea monsters out of spite. The closest human equivalent would be the feeling you get when you wake up convinced someone is watching you, but the window confirms it’s only the dark practicing its impression of your reflection.

They have no leaders, because leadership implies direction and direction filed for sovereignty two epochs ago. There are rumors, of course—whispers that every third Contradiction secretly dreams of becoming a paradox so fiercely self-contained it could finally leave—but the rumors are careful to spread only in languages that died before reaching a verdict on whether rumors count as alive.

Their architecture—if you insist on calling it that—consists entirely of buildings whose blueprints achieved sentience midway through construction and abandoned the project in search of somewhere less finished to grow old. Foundations float in polite aboveground orbit around the idea of basements that dug themselves to Heaven by mistake. Inside, hallways kneel to let the furniture pass, doors apologize for opening inwardly in a mood that prefers exits, and ceilings hover protectively overhead without ever quite committing to overheadness. Rooms proliferate only when unobserved, each new chamber tiled meticulously with the tiles removed from a neighboring absence. The plumbing is a philosophical study in whether water might consent to disappear quietly if asked politely by a system of pipes that have forgotten which way gravity settled the argument last Tuesday.

They cultivate hobbies like stalking their own shadows at a respectful distance, or training dust motes to perform the only known ballet based entirely on forgetting the next step before it occurs. Their favorite sport is arguing over who is more not there until the referee—an old abandoned doubt who officiates out of boredom—calls it a draw by failing to appear. They keep pets: the ghosts of extinct wavelengths of light that flit through walls when nobody’s looking, emitting faintly guilty purrs of electromagnetic apology whenever accidentally observed.

Yet for all this, they are not lonely. Loneliness requires a stable you to contrast with a stable not-you, and here both positions keep swapping resumes backstage. Instead, they enjoy a kind of crowded solitude—an intimate vacancy so densely packed that even vacancies have waiting lists. When they dream, they dream of imagining a somewhere else, then wake up relieved to find the dream still busy collecting evidence against the concept of elsewhere.

Come evening—which is a metaphor, since evenings here moonlight as mid-mornings when the sun oversleeps—they gather in the square where the fountain flows upward into faithless clouds, and together they practice the ancient communal ritual of suppressing applause for another perfectly executed daydream that succeeded in never happening at all. Their applause is never heard, of course, but the silence afterward always faintly embarrassed, as if it knows it should be louder.

Stand still long enough beneath the lintel that frames only another frame, and one of them might willing-mistake you for a vacancy worth filling. Don’t flinch. They are gentle tenants once they move in; you’ll scarcely notice the weight of their absence pressing against your ribs like a memory of breath you never quite meant to take. In a season or two—which translates roughly to the duration of half an incomplete sigh—you’ll find yourself forgetting to check whether you’re still alive in precisely the way they cherish as the highest compliment.

You’ll know you’ve become one of them when you wake up missing yourself from a dream that insists it was only borrowing you until the real you returns—though by then, returning will be an old-fashioned notion, quaint as an arrow that still believes in targets in a place where direction and missing have finally agreed to call it even. 

Until then, walk softly. The floor here is made of excuses the ground grew tired of standing on, and even echoes have to pay rent in lost footsteps if they linger loud enough to be mistaken for coming back.
