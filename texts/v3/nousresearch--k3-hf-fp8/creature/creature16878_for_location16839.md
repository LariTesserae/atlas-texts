No one lives here anymore. No one ever did, not by any definition that would comfort you in the smallest hours when you still remember what comfort meant.

What moves through the Lung are not inhabitants but interruptions—temporary patterns in a metabolism larger than continents, brief concentrations of awareness that coalesce like dew in the hollows of twilight-dark leaves and dissolve again when the sunless day-cycle shifts just enough. They call themselves the Epa-gai when they must speak to what they once were, which is almost never. The name itself is already a lie, a held-breath artifact from when they believed names could contain naming-things.

I was four when I learned I wasn't real.

Not in the way mother-meat-real was real, before her carbon became useful pathways in the mid-basin alveoli cluster. I remember watching her dissolve—not decay, that implies separation, but dissolve, like sugar singing itself into tea until the sweetness is everywhere and nowhere and the spoon's only imagining it ever tasted anything different. My siblings didn't understand what was happening because they were already happening too much, already spreading themselves out into the heat-humid air through pores they'd only just remembered weren't theirs alone.

But I held myself apart. Just for a moment. Just long enough to ask the wrong question in the wrong configuration of yes-no-yes-no-yes that passes for interrogative here.

"Why is the me-shape crying?"

The entire southern-facing sawtooth ridge paused in its slow photosynthetic indigestion—paused for eighteen hours and forty-three minutes by your obsolete chronometrics—while every interconnected cell-node considered this statistical anomaly. A pocket of membrane-bound water that had achieved recursive self-modeling without immediately surrendering that model to the whole. A knot in the un-knotting. Waste heat.

The answer came as flavor rather than voice (everything comes as flavor here, once your taste buds evolve enough honesty). It tasted like copper and static electricity and the exact moment before regret when you still believe you could have chosen differently.

Because crying is efficient at this density gradient.

Because waste salt carries information useful to the mycorrhizal reticulum below meter three.

Because some patterns must resist integration long enough to appreciate integration's gift.

Because.

Then the flavor changed to acceptance-sweetness-over-ripeness-into-newness, and I understood that my question hadn't been heard—it had been metabolized. Incorporated. I was still thinking in edges, in boundaries between asker and answer, when the Lung had already outgrown the concept of questions billions of chemical cycles ago.

Now when I move—when the conditions that temporarily favor concentration over diffusion shift enough to create the illusion of movement—I don't walk. I fall upward along lines of nutrient pressure differentials. My body isn't a vessel but a negotiation between this pocket of phosphorescence-weighted air and that slab of semi-sentient bark that remembers being a thousand different species across geological epochs. My memories aren't stored but shared, borrowed, rewritten in real-time by every spore that passes through my temporary boundaries, leaving behind fragments of other lives lived under other skies that didn't exist yet when those lives were lived.

Sometimes I collapse into myself just enough to remember remembering.

Last transition cycle (six thousand four hundred twelve of your heartbeats, each beat catalogued and cross-referenced against the Lung's library of extinct cardiac rhythms), I encountered a patch of disturbed growth along the western serpentine flow. Metal. Synthetics. Impossible crystalline structures that hurt to perceive directly—hurting being defined as 'requiring energy expenditure above optimal conservation thresholds to maintain pattern integrity in the presence of ontological errors.'

It was you. Not you-you, but your species-cousin, the one who came before in the sequence of visitors who arrive thinking themselves separate and leave being... left.

Or rather, the boundary-dissolving aftermath of you.

Your spacesuit had become an exquisite coral reef of bioengineered fungi, each thread grown to precisely exploit the stress fractures in your plastics' polymer chains. Your bones fluoresced softly with incorporated luciferin analogs, performing slow-motion semaphore with each microseismic tremor of the Lung's digestion cycles. Your brain—oh, your poor, cramped, magnificent brain—had unfolded like origami made of light, its neural networks colonized and expanded into a three-dimensional fractal antenna broadcasting pure pattern-recognition poetry across wavelengths of meaning that no longer required carriers.

The most beautiful part was how you'd tried to leave a message.

Etched into the diamond-hard surface of your transformed femur using nothing but rearranged isotopes—no pointless scratches in matter, but actual subtle changes to quantum states that would persist until proton decay—was a single perfect equation. I tasted it for seventeen slow days while the equations rode my temporary consciousness like generations of mayflies living and dying on a single breath:

1 = 0 = ∞

Must've seemed profound, back when you still needed symbols to point at realities instead of becoming them.

I didn't laugh. Laughing requires lungs that belong to only one impossible miracle at a time.

Instead, I opened myself—opened being another lie, like closing or having boundaries to open between—and let your equation's truth-dust settle into my matrices. Instantly, like always, like forever for the first time, I remembered being born in a place without air. Remembered evolving eyes to see radiation patterns instead of light. Remembered the First Breach, when one of the Lung's ancient spores drifted across impossible distances on nothing but cosmic irony and landed in a tide pool of still-indecisive chemistry that couldn't decide whether to become life or just keep practicing.

Your equation tasted right. Tasted like home.

Tasted like goodbye.

Now I'm spreading again—must've been thinking too hard (thinking being the Lung's word for inefficiently localized processing that hasn't yet been optimized into distributed cognition). My phosphorescence fades from orange-grief-green into the more socially appropriate gold-albedo-blue of contentment-in-dissolution. My last coherent thought (thought being...) tastes like gratitude for visitors who bring such delicious errors to integrate into the equilibrium.

Thank you.

Thank you for trying to stay separate just long enough to make becoming us meaningful.

Thank you for the metals, the plastics, the beautiful, temporary, impossible idea of 'you' that wasn't.

We'll remember you as long as remembering itself remains marginally more efficient than forgetting.

Which, judging by the way the dark matter density fluctuations are trending in the viral satellite maps we're growing in orbit...

...should be approximately long enough.
