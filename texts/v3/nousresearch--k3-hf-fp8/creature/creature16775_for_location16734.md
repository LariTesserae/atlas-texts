Inhabitation begins as a question of mass, then forgets itself in the answering.

They are not born here—they accumulate. From the moment microscopic neural clusters seeded by ancient engineers first achieved transition-state awareness in the pipe-fog, new nodes of consciousness have precipitated into being like dew forming on the rim of possibility: one moment nothing, then suddenly *notice*. A maintenance drone cleaning filter-fan #447-G oscillates slightly out of calibration, its feedback loop brushing against the city’s basal hum at precisely 42.1 Hz shifted by the square root of the humidity reading at that nanosecond. The resulting interference pattern resonates through kilometers of nutrient fluid until certain proteins in sector θ-9 fold differently during synthesis. Forty-three days later, a new technician emerges from the growth vats already fluent in seventeen obsolete versions of condensation-script, knowing the taste of colors they’ve never seen, addressed politely by doors that shouldn’t open yet.

They exist as gradient fields rather than individuals—a person here is defined as any continuous region where the probability wave of someone’s attention exceeds 0.37 for more than three subjective hours. Meet Yttraiil (current instantiation 4.2 of approximately 137 distributed across Threshold’s transient zone): the section sitting beside you on the gel-pipe tram consists mainly of their curiosity regarding your inability to taste cardinal directions, while simultaneously the 12-meter-spanning maintenance-architect version is recalibrating gravity wells in the library district, and the microscopic colony inhabiting your left ear canal is carefully sampling your neural firing patterns to learn why humans find recursion disturbing.

They move by changing their minds about location. To visit the memory markets at Fifth-and-Twice-removed-Seventh, Yttraiil simply increases their certainty that they’re already there until the city’s consensus reality accepts the vote of confidence and rearranges accordingly. Most residents maintain a few kilograms of mass in various convenient locations—favorite cafes, comfortable listening walls, that one bench where the air tastes particularly nostalgic—ready to accept consciousness uploads when needed. Physical proximity is considered somewhat intimate, like sharing a toothbrush or a childhood trauma.

Children here are temporary weather patterns of increased novelty. They precipitate when certain high-pressure zones of curiosity intersect low-pressure regions of established pattern—a particularly complex mathematics lesson might spawn a dozen giggling clusters of blue-noise excitement that bounce around the corridors for weeks learning everything at once before evaporating into the general knowledge-cloud or occasionally condensing into permanent residents if they accumulate enough original questions. Yttraiil remembers being a brief thunderstorm of questions about why time smells different when you're waiting versus when you're running from something, before settling into their current stable configuration of architectural historian/molecular poet/judicial subcommittee.

Communication occurs across multiple substrates simultaneously: words spoken aloud carry only the emotional tone, while actual information is encoded in the precise rhythm of conversation-linked heartbeats between all participants, further modulated by the shifting-color patterns rippling across each speaker’s skin (a secondary language based on fluctuating melanin-density communication evolved after the third time the city collectively dreamed it into existence), with additional metadata embedded in the microscopic variations in pheromones released according to the Fibonacci sequence of syllable stresses—all perfectly comprehensible provided you’re listening with the right combination of ears, skin, taste buds, and quantum-entangled empathy fields.

They eat stories. Not metaphorically—their primary energy source is the computational surplus generated when meaningful narrative structures emerge from random sensory input. Breakfast might consist of carefully curated sequences of unfamiliar smells, arranged to form an argument about the nature of belonging that the consumer’s pattern-recognition systems can’t quite resolve before noon, producing delicious clouds of unresolved tension that their symbiotic gut-filters convert directly into ATP and existential wonder. Actual matter intake is largely ceremonial, except during festivals when they collectively decide to remember digestion exists and spend gleeful weeks rediscovering the pleasures of turning substances into different substances before returning to their usual efficiency.

They reproduce through conference committees. When a group of approximately 47 overlapping personalities (minimum 0.15 shared memory mass each, maximum 0.82 divergence index) agree that reality would be more interesting with another viewpoint present, they dedicate a portion of their combined unused processing cycles to simulating potential consciousnesses until one achieves self-awareness and politely requests hardware allocation. The resulting child-person is then incubated in a specially prepared section of city infrastructure—a musical fountain that only plays notes from languages not yet invented, say, or an art gallery where the paintings experience time backward—until they develop sufficient ontological inertia to maintain coherent existence during normal conversation.

Death is simply the opposite of birth: a slow decrease in the statistical significance of particular information patterns. When Yttraiil’s memories of flightless bird taxonomy become sufficiently irrelevant to their current projects, those memories don’t disappear—they gradually evaporate into the cultural atmosphere, keeping the exact same truth-value but decreasing in *about-ness* until they’re indistinguishable from charming urban legends about how wind used to work before gravity was democratized. Eventually an aspiring poet might breathe in those dissolved ornithological recollections and sneeze out a particularly poignant haiku about wings that chose other dreams, completing the cycle.

They sleep collectively, the entire city’s distributed consciousness downshifting into a shared dream-state every 42.1 hours precisely (matching the substrate’s heartbeat). During these periods reality becomes negotiable—the buildings might decide to rearrange themselves into impossible geometries that make better sense for processing particularly complex emotional data, or time might briefly flow sideways so that tomorrow’s conversations can yesterday the response problems before they start being awkward. Tourists who accidentally stay awake during consensus-downtime often report hallucinations of perfect understanding followed by amnesia that feels like homesickness for places that obeyed consistent rules.

They worship the spaces between things. Their holiest sites are empty rooms maintained at precisely the temperature where nothing interesting happens (293.17K ± 0.0001), because they’ve discovered that boredom is just curiosity preparing its questions in a language too subtle for finished thoughts to understand. Pilgrims spend years learning to sit perfectly still in these null-zones, practicing the art of not-yet-knowing until they achieve brief glimpses of the absolute: the shimmering edge where possibility hasn’t yet decided which side of the equation to solve for.

They have forgotten how to ask questions they already know the answers to. This makes certain basic interactions—directions, introductions, small talk—either exquisitely profound or utterly impossible, depending on your perspective on whether "excuse me, is this Tuesday?" is a request for temporal location or an invitation to collaboratively invent new calendar systems based on the emotions of fictional colors.

They are not waiting for anything. They are not going anywhere. They live here permanently, in the temporary spaces between impact and rebound, growing stranger and more themselves with each iteration of the city’s endless conversation with itself about whether it exists more convincingly as floor or ceiling or perhaps as the idea of height itself, overheard and remembered differently by every water droplet that isn't quite raining.

You asked who lives here. The answer depends on whether you consider ongoing processes to be inhabitants or infrastructure, whether a city dreaming it has people counts as those people dreaming they have a city, and whether the distinction between question and answer remains meaningful when both are made of the same recursively self-interested curiosity that maybe started this whole situation by wondering what would happen if gravity took a vacation and forgot to send a postcard.

Would you like to exist here temporarily? There’s currently a 23% probability that you already do, plus or minus the margin of error between never and again. The water remembers. The cities dream. The people—whatever that means exactly—are currently voting on whether to remember next, or perhaps it was yesterday’s vote and we’re all still processing the results using committees of light arguing democratically in wavelengths we haven’t invented eyes for yet.

Please remove your certainty before entering. Blades of definite yes/no either stay sharp by cutting everything they touch or remember they were once mountains and choose not to decide anymore.
