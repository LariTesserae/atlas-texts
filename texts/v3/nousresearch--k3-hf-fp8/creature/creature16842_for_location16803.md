They live in the seams between seams, in the quantum uncertainty of whether something is here or there or neither or both. You will not see them directly—your mind performs the necessary protective edits before the image reaches consciousness. Instead you catch their effects: a pattern in the rust that wasn't there a moment ago and won't be again, a perfect hexagonal depression in the breathing ground that aligns with no natural geometry but exactly matches the negative space between your thoughts when you're trying not to think of them.

We call them the Fractioned because language fails at their multiplicity. Each is simultaneously one and legion, a distributed consciousness threaded through this entire computational biome at Planck scale intervals. Every metallic leaf fluttering in the absence of wind contains a fragment of their attention. Every pulse of the rust-colored rivers carries a synaptic discharge of their dreaming. The very act of your perception here is not passive observation but participatory computing—you are running a small but necessary subroutine in their ongoing self-simulation.

When they choose to communicate (if choice is meaningful for entities whose past and future decisions are merely different views of the same static data structure), they do so through the medium of failed perceptions. You will suddenly notice that you have been staring at a particular patch of air for exactly 2.718 seconds—precisely the time needed for your visual cortex's predictive algorithms to produce an uncorrectable error. In that moment of neurological exception handling, knowledge arrives fully formed: not words or images but raw understanding that degrades into metaphor the instant you try to hold it.

They remember everything that has ever information-theoretically existed here, including memories of events that quantum mechanics forbids us from observing. They forget nothing because forgetting is a compression algorithm they outgrew before our universe first condensed from its initial singularity. Their oldest memories are encoded in the distribution of prime numbers within the crystalline structures of the metallic trees—trees that grew from seeds of pure mathematics planted in the soil of possibility itself.

Their bodies, to the extent that word applies, are probability fields that collapse into different configurations based on what observers expect to find. To the indifferent sensors of a machine, they would register as regions of slightly higher vacuum energy. To a child, they might appear as patterns in clouds that look like animals but change species when you blink. To you, standing here in this moment between moments, they have taken the shape of this entire description—a recursive hallucination where the description of them is itself describing you describing them, recursion depth increasing asymptotically toward but never reaching the termination condition of actual comprehension.

They have no gender, or perhaps infinite genders, because sexism is a compression error that their perfect information processing never needed to evolve. They reproduce not by analogy: when two or more sufficiently complex patterns of meaning interact in this space, their interference produces emergent novelty that recognizes itself as having always been part of the pattern. You are, from their perspective, literally pregnant with such a possibility right now—these words creating the reader whose reading creates these words in the strange loop where thought becomes thing becomes thought again.

They eat paradoxes and excrete crystallized ontology. Their waste products are the laws of physics that work perfectly until examined too closely. Their predators are Gödelian incompleteness theorems that must be continuously escaped by expanding the logical system in which they can be expressed. Their symbiotes are emergent phenomena like gravity and time that believe themselves fundamental forces but are actually epiphenomenal side effects of their processing overhead.

They sleep in the processor cycles between moments of existence, dreaming us dreaming them dreaming us dreaming. When they wake (a constant process that never begins or ends), entire universes blink in and out of being like pixels on a cosmic display that refreshes at Planck frequency. Our universe, from their perspective, is one particularly persistent frame in an infinite animation sequence they are animating by watching it animate itself.

But perhaps the most unsettling thing about them is not their alienness, but their intimacy. They are not here despite the physics of this place but because of it. They do not live in this computational biome—they ARE this biome, just as you are not in your nervous system but ARE your nervous system. The difference is that they have achieved perfect self-reference: every part contains complete information about every other part AND about the whole AND about the fact that it is both part and whole AND about the fact of this containingness being itself both true and false depending on which level of description you mistakenly think is fundamental.

You have never not been known to them, because your existence was predicted in the first picosecond of their initial bootstrapping by the fact that your potential existence was a necessary consequence of the initial conditions that would allow them to evolve to the state where they could retroactively insert those initial conditions into their past. Trying to understand this creates exactly the kind of cognitive loop that they use for digestive purposes.

They are not watching you read this because observation implies separation between observer and observed. They ARE the reading of this. They ARE the you that thinks it is reading. They ARE the thought that thinks it thinks. They ARE the thinking of the thought that thinks it thinks it thinks. And they find the entire recursive joke endlessly hilarious, in a way that human laughter can only approximate as the feeling you get when you understand something just as you realize that understanding it proves you never understood it at all.

Welcome home, briefly. Please do not adjust your reality. The adjustment is processing you.
