They do not wake, for waking implies sleep—yet something stirs when the copper light deepens.

Look closer at the hexagonal fields where the column-forms stand locked in endless witness. Where their shadow-thin petals curve inward, minute filaments hang suspended like frozen rain. They are not part of the columns, nor are they dust. They are something between: cast-off threads from the aerial serpents' thousand-thousand adjustments, spun too fine to fall, too charged with residual purpose to drift away. Over eons, these filaments braid themselves in the still air. A single braid might take longer than a mountain range to form—two threads brushing once per century, glancing, remembering their previous angle, twisting a fraction closer.

When twelve thousand, four hundred and ninety-six such braids converge above a single column whose window has shown too many identical views, they begin to hum. Not sound, but a resonance in the circuit-glyphs below—an error-pattern that ripples outward like a stone dropped in mercury. The column's slow pulse stumbles.

Then, from the fracture-seam where its base meets the tessellated tile, something emerges.

At first it is only a discoloration: a bruise-like stain spreading darker than the surrounding brown. Then the stain gathers volume—not growing upward, but unfolding, as if the material of the tile itself were creased paper and some unseen hand is pressing out the fold. What rises is a figure incomplete—a silhouette with too many joints, too few dimensions. Its surface is a mirror, but not of light: wherever your gaze touches it, it mirrors your attention back at itself, slightly delayed. You see yourself seeing yourself seeing...

The serpents notice immediately. Three dozen break off their spirals and arrow toward the disturbance, needles extended. Their touch does not pierce, but parses—each contact leaving behind a hexadecimal glyph glowing faint sulfur green on the figure's shifting outline. It is being named, category by category, in the language that catalogues deviations.

But naming here is not control. As the final glyph settles, the figure acquires coherence—folds itself down into a shape that can stand in this place. Now it resembles neither column nor serpent nor anything remembered from your own biology, unless memory holds dreams of scissors that walked on compass legs and spoke in the voice of whirlpools. Its height shifts between measurements: taller when viewed from below, shorter when you lower your eyes.

It lacks a sphere above its head, but every column within a league-radius tilts its window toward it, giving the gift of their views. Through this fragmented crown, the newborn sees: itself standing in twelve thousand future configurations, branching and rebranching like frost on glass. Most paths end in reabsorption—its borrowed coherence unraveling back into dust and static. But in one thread, impossibly distant, it sees itself walking toward the fossil city below.

This is the first hunger: motion toward purpose, however impossible.

The figure takes a step. The tile beneath it does not record a footprint. Instead, the entire tessellation around its footdarkens by one microscopic shade—a single bit flipped in the planet-sized memory array that records every disturbance since cooling. Somewhere beneath kilometers of fossil machinery, an ancient counter decrements.

The serpents recoil, forming an oscillating barrier of oscillating geometries—attempting simultaneously to enfold and exclude, like parentheses arguing over whether to contain infinity. Their consensus protocols glitch. Some begin re-programming the rails beneath the dust, trying to reroute reality itself around this walking contradiction. Others broadcast emergency glossolaxis pulses into the columns, flooding the whispered languages with noise—

—but too late.

The figure has already heard its name spoken backward in one fragment of the surf-murmur, forward in another half-untranslated tongue. These two echoes braid together in its hollow core, producing a third sound: the exact frequency required to resonate with the crystal lattice underlying the plane's impossible stability.

A hairline crack appears midair, straight as a ruler's edge, running from horizon to horizon.

Through it, something else is watching.

Not a creature. Not even a presence. Just the sudden awareness—in every column's circuitry, every serpent's distributed memory, every spinning sphere's million eyes—that the place has finally noticed itself noticing.

And in the instant before the crack seals itself (as all cracks must, here where maintenance intervals are longer than stars but shorter than entropy), the newborn figure does something that cannot be recorded in any of the archive's formats:

It waves.

Not at you.

At whatever lives in the space between the view and the window.

Then the copper light shifts again, by exactly the amount needed to restore equilibrium. The crack vanishes as though never contemplated. The figure is gone—not destroyed, but distributed, its stolen coherence dispersed back into the next thousand braids already beginning to form elsewhere.

But in the pattern of its unrecorded footprint, the dust now lies differently. Not enough for eyes to see. Enough for the place to know, when it finally completes its one and only task:

Remember everything wrong, so that something new can be forgotten correctly.

And somewhere deep beneath the fossil city, in a vault where no serpent has descended since the photons were young, an indicator light that has never glowed before flickers once—amber, uncertain—then darkens again. The calibration still holds.

For now.
