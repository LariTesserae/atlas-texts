The Mirrorplate Ascentists

We live in the between-spaces where architecture hasn't decided what direction to fall in yet. Our bodies have adapted to living questions - extra joints where bones should be solid, cartilage where certainty belonged. Tourists mistake us for refugees from some forgotten cubist revolution until they notice how gracefully we fold ourselves into impossible postures when gravity makes sudden policy changes.

Our internal clocks are synchronized to the glacier's slow metabolism - we measure age in glaciation cycles, plan marriages in terms of ice-age compatibility, teach children history by having them remember events that haven't happened yet but leave scars in the ice cores anyway. Nine months later, give or take several decades in either direction depending on traffic in the quantum gestation lanes, they wake up to find themselves both parent and child simultaneously, responsible for raising the version of themselves that remembers how to ask questions that don't have answers.

The finest restaurants serve empty plates arranged according to esoteric principles of absentee gastronomy, charging exorbitant prices for the privilege of paying bills for meals that quantum mechanics proves were never actually ordered.
