## Preparing for the Chamber of First Rain

### First, Understand: You Cannot Prepare

This is the central paradox. Those who enter prepared leave unchanged. Those who enter broken open may survive the transformation. Pack nothing you cannot bear to see reflected back infinite times—a wedding ring becomes a galaxy of questions, a photograph becomes a graveyard of possibilities. Come empty-handed by necessity, not virtue.

### The Clothing Ritual

Wear only what has been washed in your own tears. The water here recognizes chemical familiars—salt grief, adrenalized fear, the copper tang of secret shame. Synthetic fibers will dissolve slowly, politely, leaving you clothed in drifting polymers that never quite touch skin. Wool is honest enough to simply shrink until it binds you like a second, tighter body. Cotton is best; it remembers being plant-life and may be forgiven faster.

Layer for grief, not weather: the temperature will match your blood's betrayal exactly. Too warm when you remember love, too cool when you forget why you came. Most travelers find themselves stripping or dressing again and again, performing an endless striptease for mirrors that cannot look away but never quite see.

### The Water Diet

Begin three days prior: drink only what you have collected yourself—breastmilk if you still remember how to cry properly, morning dew from poison ivy leaves if you're brave enough to need this place, your lover's tears but only those shed while dreaming of someone else. The rain here is a connoisseur of borrowed water. It will taste the plastic bottle you drank from last week, the desalination plant that birthed your convenience, the cloud-seeding crimes of thirsty nations. And it will judge you wanting.

Practice drowning in bathtub inches. Learn to drink through your skin. The water will enter you regardless—through pores, through tear ducts, through that old piercing you thought healed over, through the space between atoms where the body's borders pretend to be real. Better to welcome it like a lover than fight it like a thief.

### Memory Editing (Optional, Recommended Against)

Write down everything you think you know about:
- How stone should behave
- The proper direction of falling
- When it is acceptable to start building something you'll never finish
- The difference between being lost and being chosen
- Why mirrors lie and why we forgive them

Read this list aloud to running water, then eat the paper. If it tastes like apologies, you may still turn back.

### The Mirror Exercise

Spend one hour each night before glass you have never slept beside. Stare at your reflection until it blinks first. When it does (it always does), memorize exactly what it looks like guilty—how the pupils dilate with relief, which muscle betrays the smile's lie, where the first wrinkle of panic appears at the corner of your pretend composure. You will need this knowledge later when the mirrors multiply.

Important: Do this naked. Clothes are a contract between sight and modesty that none of us actually signed.

### Packing the Non-existent Bag

Bring:
- A name you have never answered to
- The sound of your mother's heartbeat as she decided whether to keep you
- One doubt sharp enough to cut through certainty, small enough to swallow whole
- A map drawn by someone who has never seen the sky
- The exact weight of your childhood's biggest secret in uncarved stone
- A tongue that remembers the taste of before

Do not bring:
- Waterproof anything (the concept will offend the rain)
- Your phone (all calls will be answered by previous versions of yourself)
- Shoes (the floor needs to know what kind of animal walks on it)
- Timekeeping devices (see above re: phone, previous versions)
- Hope that looks like escape
- Fear that tastes like staying

### The Arrival Fast

Eat nothing that casts a shadow for twenty-four hours beforehand. Drink only light filtered through leaves that have never heard traffic. If you must breathe, try to remember how trees do it—in steady exchanges of sacrifice and gift, no trace gases left behind to betray your species.

When the hunger becomes a voice that knows your real name, answer it honestly. This will hurt less than when the chamber asks later.

### Final Preparation

Find a stone that fits perfectly in your palm, one that has been broken and smoothed by water that never asked permission. Hold it until your temperature matches its exactly. Then, at the precise moment when night realizes it has become morning again but hasn't told the stars yet, throw it directly up into the air.

If it never comes down, proceed.

If it hits you in the face, proceed anyway—some invitations require bruising.

If it hovers, vibrating between falling and not-falling, stay exactly where you are. You are already there. The chamber has begun building itself around your hesitation.

### Upon Entry (Remembered Later, Instructions Never Followed)

There will be a moment—after the first raindrop enters your ear like a secret whispered through centuries, before the last mirror shows you standing in a room that has always been missing its ceiling—when you will understand why all the builders left their tools unfinished. It is the same reason the floor is still raw, the walls undecided, the water rising but not drowning.

In that moment, if you still remember what hands are for, pick up the nearest abandoned thing. Feel the weight of all the possibilities it refused to become. Then, very carefully, set it back down exactly where it isn't supposed to be.

This is the only prayer that works here: the sacred geometry of deliberate mistakes in a world that hasn't yet learned to call them wrong.

### Post-Visit Notes (To Be Read Before Departure)

1. The water will not fully leave you. Some nights your dreams will flood unexpectedly. Condense these carefully—they make excellent ink for writing letters to previous selves.

2. Mirrors will flirt with you shamelessly afterward. Refuse their invitations politely but firmly. They never want you, only the version of you that believed their reflections.

3. Plants left in your care will develop unreasonable expectations about rainfall and emotional availability. Love them anyway, but never apologize for normal weather.

4. You will lose the ability to participate in conversations about "finished" things—houses, relationships, explanations, deaths. This is normal. Seek companions who find comfort in ellipses.

5. The stone you didn't throw will wait exactly where you left it. Sometimes it will call your name in rainfall patterns. Answer only if you're ready to build something you can't live inside.

6. There are no souvenirs worth bringing back except this: memory now tastes like future rain on unmade stone, and sometimes, when you're very still and very lucky, you can hear the sound of a planet learning to be gentle with its trespassers.

7. You should leave now.

8. The water is warm.

9. The mirrors are bright.

10. The rain remembers what you was before you started using verbs.

11. You should—

12. But the doorway—

13. Shh. Listen.

14. The rice is flowering.

15. Stay.

16. Learn.

17. Enough. 

(These instructions will self-destruct upon completion. You will know you have finished when there is nothing left of you that still believes in endings.)
