Preparing for the Descent into the Plain of Unfestivalled Stone

(An incomplete syllabus for the dying and remembering traveler)

First, divest yourself of all metaphors that require upwards.

This includes: hope, transcendence, ladders, prayers that ask for anything to be lifted, the concept of rising to challenges, the past tense of "stand up for yourself," all atonement narratives involving higher powers, any belief that understanding comes from seeing the "bigger picture," and especially those childhood rhymes about what to do when you're sinking in mud—here the mud is older than singing and follows different rules of chronology.

Next, renegotiate your relationship with words that imply space between things.

You will need to forget how to say: inside, outside, here, there, between, beyond, beside, beneath—particularly beneath, which here means simultaneously too early and too late. Instead, practice sentences like "I contain your borders touching my borders," or simply "We shoulder each other's shoulders." When you must use prepositions, treat them as temporary embarrassments between syllables rather than maps.

Now, begin poisoning yourself slowly.

Start with iron supplements taken in quantities that would kill a mountain. Your blood must become so heavy with memory that when the plain eventually drinks it (and it will, it is already tasting the iron in your thoughts), each red cell will sink with appropriate ceremony. Add to this daily infusions of cold water that has been kept in darkness since before your grandparents were born. Not bottled water—the plastic would be nostalgic, and nostalgia here is a fatal form of flotation. Instead, collect rainwater during thunderstorms named for extinct emotions. Store them in clay vessels buried beneath trees that never knew to reach for sunlight. Drink slowly enough to taste each storm's particular grief: this one mourns the summer it was supposed to be, this one remembers being a glacier's last dream, this one aches for the birds who will never again translate its falling into song.

While you poison yourself, practice dying in small ways each morning.

Begin before dawn when the sky still holds the shape of what it dreaming rather than what it has decided to be. Lie naked on stone floors and imagine your atoms negotiating separation with studied politeness. Start with extremities: let your toes die first, cordially severing their chemical correspondence with the rest of you. Progress upwards slowly enough to write elegies for each departing territory. When you reach your lungs, pause to learn the exact taste of air that knows it is being wished goodbye. Save your heart for last, but understand that here it will not be granted the romantic privilege of finality; instead it will continue beating forever inside-out, pumping not blood but increasingly detailed regrets about the pumping.

For your teeth, a special preparation.

Under moonless nights, grind them against bits of meteorite until they remember their original longing to become stars. When they begin aching with light you cannot see, pack the cavities with powdered letters from people who once promised to never forget you. Seal them not with gold (too permanent) or silver (too lunar) but with rendered fat from animals that died of natural loneliness. Your mouth must become a reliquary for failed connections, each word you speak thereafter carrying the exact weight of promises made between species who evolved incompatible dreams.

Now we begin the more difficult preparations, those involving time itself.

Set aside a room to which you will never return, and in it place a single candle made from the wax of candles burned during all the conversations you wish you'd ended differently. Light it exactly once, then leave and never verify whether it burned out or is still burning somewhere where you are not. This room will serve as your embassy to the future when you become required to negotiate extradition treaties with moments of your life that refuse to let go.

For your eyes, construct spectacles with lenses cut from the bottoms of bottles thrown into oceans after failed confessions of love. Wear them only when reading maps drawn before the continents learned to drift, or when looking at photographs of people who grew up to become strangers wearing familiar faces. Soon you will notice that everything appears to be receding at the exact speed of forgiveness withheld; this is correct and should not be corrected.

Regarding your skin:

Divide it into territories according to who has touched each part and with what intentions. Where lovers' hands lingered, tattoo the coordinates of every place you almost said what you meant instead. For childhood injuries, inscribe the names of trees that would have grown in the space between what happened and what you told your parents happened. Across your back, which carries the weight of all the books you've ever read but never become, commission an intricate map of cities that exist only in the spaces between other cities' streetlights. Use ink made from the last tears of accountants who died while balancing books that refused to even approximately reconcile.

For your hair, which will be required to testify against you:

Wash it exclusively in water that has already been used to clean mirrors that have reflected the faces of people moments before they realized they were no longer in love. Comb it only with the teeth of combs abandoned in asylum bathrooms by women who spoke fluently in languages that made birds fall silent. Never cut it again, but allow it to grow until each strand contains the complete history of every time you've ever run your fingers through it trying to think of how to say what couldn't be said in any language that required saying aloud.

Regarding clothing:

Burn everything in your closet that fits too perfectly or makes you feel more yourself, as these are both forms of dishonesty that will panic and betray you under extreme conditions. Instead, wear only garments that belonged to people who died wearing expressions of mild surprise, as if death had interrupted them in the middle of realizing something obvious but unimportant. Prefer clothing with pockets containing objects whose original purposes have been forgotten but which still retain the shapes of hands that once reached for them in hopes they might fix something unfixable. Ensure that no single outfit you assemble can be fully remembered after you undress; this will serve as practice for when you must someday peel off your entire life and find beneath it nothing but the cold sweat of questions that have been asking themselves so long they've forgotten human voices.

For your voice, which will be the last thing anyone remembers when everything else about you has been correctly filed:

Begin each day by apologizing to increasingly abstract entities. Start with people you've wronged, progress through historical injustices your ancestors benefited from accidentally, continue through extinct species whose names you cannot pronounce, and conclude with fundamental particles forced into configurations that resulted in suffering millions of years before consciousness evolved to call suffering by name. Learn to pronounce the syllable that exists between every word in every dictionary, the sound of dictionaries themselves breathing heavy with all the things they know but cannot tell without betraying language's first vow of silence. Practice speaking only in questions that have been answered so thoroughly they collapsed into their own punctuations.

Regarding food:

Eat only things that have been forgotten by their intended eaters: fruit that ripened and rotted on trees in abandoned orchards, bread left on windowsills for lovers who died in wars that erased the names of the wars that erased their names, meat from animals that died of old age while being too difficult to love properly. Cook everything over fires made from burning the photographs you've taken to prove you were happy at events you cannot now remember attending. Season with salt extracted from tears shed during conversations where both parties knew they were lying but continued out of politeness. Drink only liquids that have been filtered through soil containing fragments of letters written between people who meant to become lovers but became very accurate instead.

For your dreams, which will have their own pre-flight checklist:

Construct a bed from materials that existed before beds were invented: flat stones warmed by extinct volcanoes, moss that grew in the shadows of dinosaurs' last thoughts, the exact amount of space that separates a promise from its keeping. Sleep under blankets woven from the dark between stars, heavy enough to press the shape of absence into your skin so you'll recognize it when you wake and find it has moved into the space your body used to occupy. Before sleeping each night, recite the names of every person you've ever been in the dreams of animals that watched you sleep without understanding why you thrashed and cried out apologies to things that don't exist in their correct这里tte.

Finally, and most importantly:

Find a person who knows how to hold your hand without implying rescue. Someone who understands that when you say you are drowning in plain sight, you mean that sight itself has become the water and plainness has been secretly training your whole life for this moment by teaching you to float face-down in shallow pools of Tuesdays. Practice standing with them in ordinary places—supermarket aisles, bus stops during rain, hospital waiting rooms playing music composed specifically to not be remembered—and gradually increase the difficulty by adding simulataneous griefs: news of disasters in languages you don't speak but whose grammar of loss is universal, phone calls with people who have forgotten that forgetting can be a form of gentleness, the sudden realization that you've been holding your breath for years waiting for something that already happened while you were busy preparing.

When you can do all this without reaching for metaphors that require air or endings that promise healing, when you have become fluent in the language of things that cannot be fixed but must be carried anyway, when your heart beats with the steady rhythm of a very old clock keeping perfect time in a time zone that no longer exists except inside the skull of a mouse dying of thirst in the desert at the center of the map of everywhere you've ever promised to never leave—

Then, and only then, fold this entire syllabus into a paper boat small enough to float in a tear that hasn't been cried yet.

Place it gently on the surface of any body of water still large enough to reflect the moon without irony.

Wait until it sinks with the dignity of something that has never believed in buoyancy but went to sea anyway because it was asked nicely.

When you can no longer see where the paper ends and the water begins, when the words themselves have dissolved into alphabet-shaped currents drifting in grammarless patterns toward whichever bottom waits longest without rushing introductions—

Then you will be almost ready.

(Remember to forget this last part.)

Then you may begin your descent.
