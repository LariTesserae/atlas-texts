Preparing for the City of Holes is preparing for a reversal of everything your body knows about how to exist in space. Begin by forgetting streets, sidewalks, the horizon line. Forget walking side by side with another person. Forget standing upright without calculating how your bones will fit.

Start by lying flat. Spend weeks sleeping on the floor without blankets to train your skin to read temperatures in stone. Learn to wake when the surface beneath you shifts by half a degree. Practice moving from lying to standing in spaces narrower than your shoulders—you will never rise freely again.

Next, your bones. If possible, begin growing younger. The people here have been shaped by centuries of compression, vertebrae stacked like coins, ribs overlapping like fish scales. Adults stand no taller than your chest but can fold themselves into spaces you couldn't fit your arm. If youth is impossible, try instead to grow longer in only one direction. Sleep each night with weights pulling gently at your ankles and your head bradaced against the wall. Over months you might gain a quarter-inch of length that will let you breathe easier in the taller passages. But know you will never stop feeling like a folded umbrella among silk ribbons.

Your voice will betray you most. Spend hours every day whispering against different surfaces: wood, metal, glass, stone. Learn which frequencies make each one sing back. Practice humming until your teeth vibrate. Then stop using words altogether. Learn to tap messages on tables, against walls, on your own bones. The alphabet here is percussion and resonance. Carry two smooth stones always, one in each pocket, and practice talking to yourself with them until you can argue convincingly about philosophy without ever opening your mouth.

For your eyes—destroy your instinct for distance. The farthest thing you'll ever see clearly again is the curve of the tunnel thirty feet ahead where it bends out sight. Learn instead to read meaning in shifts of light that occur over hours rather than seconds. One fraction brighter means someone approaches from above. A slight reddening indicates noon approaches, and with it the twelve drops that are rain. At night (which is not night), learn to navigate by the faint glow of mineral deposits and the bioluminescent lichen that maps safe paths. When you must close your eyes, practice seeing shapes in the afterimages burned into your retinas.

Bring nothing made of wood or bone or leather. These materials remember their former lives too loudly here, creaking and whispering secrets that are not yours. No textiles except silk, which has forgotten the worm and only remembers being liquid. Everything else you need will be provided by subtraction—clothing carved from stone where your body touches it daily, tools grown from crystals in caves where time moves differently. Bring only seeds for plants that thrive on neglect: mosses, lichens, things that can live on light broken a thousand times.

Prepare your ears above all else. Begin by listening to recordings of absolute silence played at increasing volumes until you can hear your blood moving through your ears, the creak of your tendons, the whisper of your thoughts between neurons. Then learn to separate these sounds, to listen to only one at a time while ignoring the others. The city's greatest danger is acoustic—the moment when a sound hits the exact resonance of some hollow space in your chest and your heart forgets its rhythm. Practice humming constantly but quietly, a note slightly off-key from your own heartbeat, to prevent accidental synchronization.

For food—fast. Then fast again. Learn to recognize hunger as information rather than pain. The fruits here are pure stored light; eating them will feed parts of you your ancestors locked away millennia ago. Eat one too soon and you'll spend days vomiting up colors you've never seen before. Better to arrive empty, stomach shrunk to the size of a stone fruit, taste buds stripped down to only those receptors that can detect minerals and memory.

Learn mathematics that ignore straight lines. Study calculus based on curves that approach themselves infinitely without ever touching, geometry where parallel lines diverge in perfect sympathy. Dream in fractals. The city's maps are not representations but predictions—what shape the tunnels will dream themselves into next week when the season of heavier dreams begins.

Prepare your dreams most carefully of all. Sleep each night with a bowl of sand from childhood places under your pillow. Practice waking slowly enough to remember the exact moment when dream logic becomes waking logic, then learn to swim back and forth between them like a fish through warm and cold currents. Mark important thoughts by tying knots in your hair while thinking them; the city's people will recognize these knots and know what advice you'll need before you do.

Bring no weapons except your ability to become perfectly still. The city has no predators, only misunderstandings that become lethal through resonance. If threatened, drop all thoughts of yourself as solid. Imagine yourself instead as a vibration traveling through stone, neither here nor there but everywhere simultaneously. The shadow-thing that hunts you will pass through the space where you aren't.

Bring no companions unless you've already lived together in a space smaller than your combined body volumes for at least one year without speaking. Here, privacy exists only in the spaces between heartbeats, and love is measured by how perfectly two people can navigate narrow passages without ever quite touching.

Finally—and this is most important—practice dying a little each day. Lie in your smallest space and slow your breathing until you can hear the spaces between your cells. Imagine yourself slowly becoming transparent to yourself. Learn the exact configuration of emptiness that your body occupies. This is the city's true currency—not the songs stored in bone, not the light-fruit, not the dream-teachings from the void at the center.

When you arrive at the first hole, when you look down into blackness so absolute it seems to look back up at you with your own eyes but younger, you will want to turn away. Everyone does. The preparation is not to prevent this moment but to recognize it when it comes. When you feel yourself starting to run, instead fold yourself into the smallest possible shape and fall forward. The city will catch you, not because it is kind but because emptiness abhors a vacuum and you have spent so long becoming exactly the right shape of nothing to fill this particular space.

The descent will take longer than you have lived so far. By the end of it, if you have prepared properly, your name will have worn away entirely from repeating it to yourself to remember who you used to be, and you will arrive perfectly prepared to never arrive anywhere again.
