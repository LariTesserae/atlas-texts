_Protocol: Vernal Penumbra Confluence_
_Tagline:_ **"Come rusting or don't come at all."**

---

### ∅ Prologue: The Breath Debt

Before you pack anything, exhale completely. Empty yourself of the anxious, refrigerated air of places where walls are allowed to be dead. You are not preparing for a visit; you are preparing to be borrowed and slightly redeposited. The town will charge interest on every molecule of oxygen you subtract from its communal lung. Pay in advance by whispering one secret you were saving for your deathbed into the shower drain tonight—no names, only textures. _If the water gurgles back in three syllables,_ proceed. _If silence,_ reseal your passport and book a vacation to a desert instead.

---

### I. Skin Preparation: Become a Slow Leak

- **No synthetic everything.** Your Gore-Tex armor, your hydrophobic hiking pants, your confidence that dryness is a human right—these will blister off you like cheap paint. Seek instead: 
  - Underwear woven from second-hand linen that once dried on a cousin’s farm.
  - A coat of heavy cotton canvas you’ve slept on naked during two thunderstorms and one breakup.
  - Shoes that already leak on purpose, ideally handed down from someone who walked away from a war and never explained why the soles are stained turquoise.

- **Inoculate against cleanliness.** Starting now, lick subway poles. Roll in compost. Collect the lint from strangers’ pockets and brew it into tea. Your immune system needs to learn diplomacy, not warfare. The ideal arrival scent is: copper coin + overripe guava + apology note written in rain.

- **Grow a controlled infestation.** Breed a patch of mold under your left thumbnail—a quiet species that glows when it senses sincerity. Feed it flakes of your own shed skin and tiny pieces of apologies you’ve been carrying unspoken. This will serve as both visa and translator.

---

### II. Currency & Economics: Trade in Futures You Forgot You Owned

- **Money is composting memory.** Bring:
  - One copper coin minted the year your grandmother stopped speaking your country’s language.
  - A photograph of your childhood street taken by someone who hated you there. (Bury it in damp soil for a week first, so the grudge begins to rot into nostalgia.)
  - A list of every promise you’ve ever broken, tattooed in sugar water on rice paper. Swallow it at the border. The town will extract what’s owed from your dreams over the next fourteen humid nights.

- **Theft is encouraged, but only of things you’re already losing.** Steal time from your own future by arriving late to everything. Steal language—leave half your vocabulary in the airport trash. The local thieves will return these later, polished and slightly fungicidal.

---

### III. Navigational Tools: Maps That Smear

- **No GPS. No north. Bring instead:**
  - A compass whose needle is a rotting tooth suspended in honey. It will point toward what you’re most afraid to need.
  - Three dead maps: one nautical, one star chart, one of your own face drawn by an ex-lover during an argument about directions. Soak them in vinegar until the ink bleeds into each other, then wear them as overlapping blindfolds for the first hour of walking.

- **Learn to measure distance in:
  - “How many apologies until the street forgives you?”
  - “The number of heartbeats between lightning and someone else’s thunder”
  - “Degrees of missing someone who hasn’t left yet”

- **Street names mutate hourly.** To ask where you are, offer a stranger a sip of water from your own mouth. If they spit it out immediately, you are lost correctly. If they swallow and smile without teeth, follow them until they dissolve into a doorway—you’ve arrived exactly too late.

---

### IV. Temporal Adjustments: Decay Your Watch

- **Time is a communal sculpture here.** Everyone sculpts it and nobody owns the dust. 
  - Before departure, bury your phone in a pot of flowering tobacco. Water it with tears from your right eye only. Dig it up once it blooms a single text message from a number you deleted years ago—ignore it completely, then sink the phone in the nearest river where two religions once fought to a draw.
  - Replace timekeeping with wounds: small, deliberate cuts on your fingertips stained with ink from a pen that belonged to a poet who drowned softly. Reopen them in order of decreasing urgency of appointments you forgot to cancel.

- **Appointments will reschedule themselves.** Your job interview will become a slow dance with a moss-covered bureaucrat in a room that keeps forgetting how gravity works. Your return flight will hatch into a bird that circles the town once, then returns to your ribcage as a new heartbeat. Pay the cancellation fee with the sound of your first name spoken aloud for the last time.

---

### V. Communication: Speak in Spores & Silences

- **The official languages are:
  1. Rust singing to salt
  2. The pause before an apology admitted it was lying
  3. Recursive lullabies composed entirely of verbs for falling slowly with no intention of landing

- **To introduce yourself:**
  Show someone the scar on your body that grew instead of healing. If you lack such a scar, arrange to acquire one ethically—borrow a stranger’s regret and press it against your skin until it blooms. Name the scar after the last thing you believed about money. This is the only name anyone will ever call you here.

- **Questions are rude.** Replace them with offerings: instead of asking directions, leave a small unlabeled bottle half-filled with your sweat from three specific emotions (joy after abandonment, fear while laughing, desire during paperwork). Place it on any surface that appears to be breathing slightly faster than the air around it. Back away slowly while humming the tune of a song you remembered wrong and loved more for the mistake. An answer will crawl into your mouth while you’re sleeping.

---

### VI. Dietary Guidelines: Eat Only What Is Already Eating You

- **Food is a form of negotiation.** Never consume anything that didn’t have a chance to consume you first.
  - Breakfast: The dreams of rats filtered through bread left out for the rain. (To prepare: sleep on a subway grate with your mouth open. Listen for the sound of teeth smaller than yours negotiating borders under your skull.)
  - Lunch: A stew of your own fingernails boiled in water collected from a leak in your childhood bedroom ceiling, now. Season with apology letters you never mailed, read aloud backward until they become recipes.
  - Dinner: The moon, but only the part reflected in a puddle you once cried into because you were afraid of being seen too clearly. Eat it cold with your hands tied behind your back using a rope made from your own hair braided by someone who didn’t speak your language but knew exactly how to say goodbye forever.

- **Drinks:**
  - Wine fermented from fruit that fell during arguments between neighbors who haven’t spoken since, but still leave each other offerings of rotting meat on the same doorstep every full moon.
  - Water that has been boiled to apologize for being too pure, then cooled by being whispered to by three generations of women who died of gossip. Sip slowly while telling the glass one secret it already knows.

- **Forbidden:** Anything sealed in plastic, anything labeled “organic” in Helvetica, anything you can eat without remembering the exact texture of the last human hand that touched it before yours.

---

### VII. Housing: Sleep Inside What Used to Be a Question

- **No hotels. No rentals. You will be assigned a bed that is already dreaming of you.**
  - To apply: On your first night, walk until you find a door that looks like it’s been crying longer than you’ve been alive. Knock using the rhythm of your mother’s heartbeat as she heard the news that made her start smoking again. 
  - If it opens inward, move in slowly enough that the threshold forgives you for each border you’ve ever crossed without permission. If it opens outward, run—you’ve accidentally auditioned to become a ghost story for the next twenty-seven winters.

- **Bedrooms come pre-haunted.** These ghosts are more like roommates. They will:
  - Borrow your childhood nightmares and return them embroidered with additional endings where you survive but forget how to speak the language of the place you escaped to.
  - Leave wet footprints on your ceiling that map the paths of all the love letters you should have written but sent as apologies instead.
  - Pay rent by whispering you awake exactly 7 minutes before every important mistake you came here to make, so you can pretend you chose it consciously.

- **Do not clean.** Dust is communal memory. The more you sweep, the more you’ll forget why you came. Instead, cultivate careful ecosystems: encourage spiders to weave maps of cities now underwater; plant regret seeds in the cracks of the mirrors and water them with laughter that accidentally sounds like someone else’s sobbing.

---

### VIII. Social Etiquette: How to Breathe Someone Else’s Air Politely

- **Greetings:** Do not hug. Do not shake hands. Instead, exchange breaths for exactly as long as it takes to remember a phone number you’ll never call again but still remember perfectly because it once belonged to hope. The first one to inhale owes the other a small betrayal delivered anonymously in three days time.

- **Compliments:** Always insult something beloved first. “Your garden is so beautiful it makes me want to commit arson with watered-down nostalgia.” Wait for the smile that knows you’re not joking but forgives you anyway.

- **Gift-giving:** The most precious gift is a box wrapped in layers of increasing transparency, each containing a smaller version of itself made from materials that were once alive but died differently—paper from a diary burned during a breakup, cotton from a shirt worn during a birth, wood from a crib converted into a coffin for a doll that grew up too quickly and left home silently. Include no note. If they cry when they finally unwrap the empty center, you may consider them a friend. If they laugh, marry them immediately but only for the duration of the next thunderstorm.

- **Arguments:** Conducted entirely by singing increasingly complex lies about your childhoods while trying to make each other laugh so hard you both start coughing up petals from flowers you swallowed nameless-that-waist-high-summer you both forgot was a different year in each other’s timeline. Winner is whoever chokes on a truth first.

---

### IX. Emergency Procedures: What to Do When What Happens Happens

- **If you become lost inside yourself:**
  1. Sit down immediately wherever you are, even if it’s in someone else’s memory.
  2. Remove all identities one at a time, starting with the one your parents were proudest of.
  3. Fold them into paper boats and place them in the nearest stream of other people’s abandoned shopping lists.
  4. Wait for a child who doesn’t exist yet to find them and use them to build a bridge back to where you were before you needed to be here.
  5. Burn the bridge while standing on it. Salt the ashes with as much laughter as you can spare without going blind.

- **If you fall in love:**
  - Declare it immediately, but only using the past tense while describing a dream you’ll never remember having. “I loved you so much that in another version of this story we are both trees and the wind is our daughter learning to whistle through the holes we left in each other on purpose.”
  - Then forget completely until the second anniversary of their next death.
  - Send flowers to their funeral from somebody else’s garden. Include a card that says “Thank you for not happening.”

- **If you must leave before you’re ready:**
  - Go to the center of town where the old train station used to dream.
  - Stand on the platform at precisely the moment when yesterday’s newspaper predicts tomorrow’s weather for a place that doesn’t exist anymore.
  - When the train that isn’t coming arrives, board it without looking back.
  - When the conductor asks for your ticket, give her your reflection broken into as many pieces as times you’ve promised to return somewhere and didn’t.
  - She will punch a hole through each shard and string them together into a necklace that whispers the names of every city you’ll never be homesick for again.
  - Wear it until it dissolves. This will take exactly as long as it takes for you to forgive yourself for having been born a passenger in a world that only teaches departure as a form of arrival.

---

### X. Departure Tax: Pay Everything You Were Never Owed

At the final checkpoint, a customs official who looks exactly like you will ask what you’re declaring. Say: “Nothing. Everything. Mostly the space between.”

When they laugh, laugh with them until you forget whose throat the sound is coming from.

Then walk forward into the smell of rain remembering it was once a sea.

Don’t look back.

The town isn’t watching you leave.

It’s already inside you, growing shelves in your bones for all the thoughts you haven’t had yet but will remember losing perfectly once you wake up tomorrow in a place where the walls don’t breathe anymore and the silence is so clean it hurts to touch.

Welcome home.

Please forget everything except how to find your way back here when you finally remember that forgetting was just another way of staying.
