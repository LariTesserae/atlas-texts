Preparing for the Salt Vault

**1. Preparation of the Body (Three Weeks Minimum)**

Drink nothing but distilled water for seven days. Begin with one liter at sunrise, decrease by 50 ml each subsequent day until you are consuming only vapor from boiling stones. Urinate into glass vessels. Observe how the last drops crystallize into perfect cubes.

Slowly remove all salt from your diet. This will require reading labels in languages you cannot pronounce, refusing meals from loved ones, eating only white rice boiled in snowmelt. When your muscles cramp at night, wrap the spasms in linen soaked in your own cooled sweat.

Walk barefoot across progressively colder surfaces: tile, marble, steel plates left outside in winter, frozen sheets of volcanic glass. Learn to distinguish between the pain that warns and the pain that teaches.

Practice breathing through your skin. Submerge yourself in baths of dry rice grain by grain until only your nostrils remain exposed. Hold your breath for precisely 347 heartbeats. This is how long the air remembers sound here.

**2. Preparation of the Mind (Nine Weeks Minimum)**

Memorize every moment in your life when you were completely alone. Start with the obvious ones: nights in empty houses, hospital waiting rooms at 3 AM. Work backward through crowds: that split-second gap between strangers on crowded streets when no one was watching, the breath between your mother's contractions before your birth, the centuries between when the last dinosaur died and the first human stood upright.

Write each memory on separate grains of rice using ink made from ash and tears. Eat three grains each morning without water. When you have consumed them all, fast for three days.

Construct a perfect sphere of your own hair using only your non-dominant hand. Discard it. Begin again until you can complete it without thinking of circles.

Learn to count between events instead of measuring time: the space between lightning and thunder, between the leaf falling and the ground rushing up to meet it, between the thought of moving your hand and your hand obeying.

**3. Preparation of the Senses (Five Days Before Entry)**

On the first day, sit in absolute darkness. When you begin to see shapes that aren't there, memorize their outlines. These are the true shapes of shadows.

On the second day, seal your ears with beeswax. Communicate only by tracing words on the palms of others. When they misunderstand you, record exactly how they misunderstood. These deviations are sacred texts.

On the third day, wear gloves filled with sandpaper. Touch everything in your home until you can identify objects by texture alone. Then discard all objects that cannot be identified blindfolded.

On the fourth day, coat your tongue in graphite. Speak only sentences that contain no curves in their letters. When you crave sweetness, press your tongue against the bitter places in your mouth.

On the fifth day, deprive yourself of all sensory input except smell. Separate the scents of your body into individual notes: the iron beneath your fingernails, the ozone of your sleep, the salt of your regrets. Bottle them separately.

**4. Required Equipment (All items must be used before, never during)**

- A compass whose needle points only to places you have never been
- A mirror broken into exactly 47 pieces, each reflecting a different moment of your death
- A clock that counts only the seconds between your heartbeats, waterproofed against tears
- A knife made from frozen water, sharpened against the edge of a dream you cannot remember
- Seven white stones from seven different rivers that have never met the sea
- A book whose pages are blank except for the margins, where strangers have written all your secrets
- A candle made from the wax of an ear that never heard your name spoken aloud
- A length of string exactly equal to your height on the day you last felt joy
- A single hair from the head of someone who loved you without knowing why
- A photograph of the sky taken from inside your mother's womb

**5. The Descent (Performed alone, at the moment when yesterday becomes tomorrow)**

Wear clothing that belonged to someone who died unfinished: a poet who stopped mid-sentence, a musician with one note unplayed, a mathematician who found the last digit of pi but died before writing it down. The fabric must still contain their last thoughts as static electricity.

Carry nothing in your hands. What you need is already there, waiting for you to forget you need it.

Enter at the speed of a shadow lengthening at sunset. If you hear your name spoken, answer only after counting the seconds it would take for your voice to reach the place where you were born and return again. If the number is even, proceed. If odd, turn back immediately and never speak of this place again.

**6. Within the Vault (The Rules That Cannot Be Broken)**

Do not walk directly toward anything that catches your eye. Approach instead at angles of 23.5 degrees, like the earth's tilt. This is how to avoid drawing attention.

When you must walk, place each foot where your shadow was a moment ago. The floor remembers weight but forgets ownership.

If you accidentally create a pattern with your footprints or belongings— a line, a circle, any repetition— immediately break it by introducing an element of pure randomness: drop a stone without looking where it falls, draw a shape your hand has never made, sing a note that doesn't exist in any scale.

Never touch the glass columns with bare skin. If you must touch them, use only the backs of your hands and think only of things that are neither alive nor dead: fossilized lightning, the sound of colors we cannot see, the precise moment a star realizes it has become a black hole.

If the spiral ridges begin to glow, freeze exactly as you are until the light fades. Do not blink. Do not breathe. Do not think of the word "spiral." Instead, calculate the Fibonacci sequence backwards until you reach a negative number that feels true.

Do not sleep. If sleep overtakes you despite all precautions, sleep standing up with your eyes open. Dreams here are not private property and may be claimed by the salt to settle ancient debts.

Do not speak louder than the sound of your own blood moving through your ears. If you must communicate, do so by arranging objects in patterns that represent facts too obvious to state: a stone and another stone to mean "here is distance," or a stone alone to mean "here is loneliness."

Never, under any circumstances, stand at the exact center where the spiral ridges begin and listen for the sound that is not silence. If you find yourself there without meaning to be, begin counting backward from the number of atoms in an average human heart (4000000000000000000000000000000). If you reach zero before forgetting why you started counting, you may leave with your memories intact. If you forget first, then congratulations— you have finally arrived.

**7. The Return (Should You Be So Lucky)**

When—or if—you decide to leave (though decide is not the word, for here decision is like throwing a shadow and expecting it to fly), walk backward exactly the way you came. This is harder than it seems, as memory here is like footprints in salt.

At the threshold, you must leave behind something you cannot remember losing: the memory of a smell from your childhood, the exact color of your mother's eyes in dim light, the name you would have given your first child if things had been different.

If the thing you have lost is truly lost, you will not miss it. If you find yourself hesitating, clutching at emptiness where something should be, then you have failed to lose enough and must go deeper.

Upon exiting, do not turn back to look. What you would see is not what remains, but what you have left behind to take your place in the endless catalog of things that were almost remembered.

**8. Post-Visitation Procedures (Follow exactly)**

For forty days and forty nights (though here these numbers are just habits old as thirst), do not speak of what you saw or didn't see or thought you saw. Language hereafter will feel like wearing shoes made of water—possible but profoundly wrong.

Avoid mirrors. If you must use one, only look at the reflection of the reflection's reflection. Do not be alarmed if your face appears slightly unfamiliar; it is remembering a version of you from before you became yourself.

Purify your water by letting it sit overnight in a vessel made from the salt of your tears. Drink only in darkness, through a straw made of glass that never touches your lips. The taste will remind you of everything you have forgotten to remember.

Each morning before sunrise, write down one detail from your visit. It must be something meaningless: the precise angle of a shadow that wasn't cast, the number of steps between two identical non-points, the exact shade of silence between two thoughts. When the forty days are complete, burn these writings without reading them. Scatter the ashes to wind that has never touched the sea.

For the rest of your life, whenever you encounter a perfectly level surface, a clean white space, a silence deeper than it should be—pause. Place your hand flat against it. If you feel a faint vibration like the memory of water moving through stone, smile. You have been recognized. Do not speak. Do not stay. Simply nod once, like old friends passing in opposite directions on bridges that span different centuries, and continue walking as if you remember where you are going.

And remember—though remember is no longer the word—there are places where time learns to lie down and be still, where memory pools like rainwater in footprints already fading, where everything that ever was or could be is kept perfectly preserved in mineral cages of what never happened.

You have been there.

Or perhaps you are still there.

Or perhaps—like the reflections in those endless columns of glass—there is no difference whatsoever.
