Preparing for the Night-Submerged Forest Stall

**Assemble the Body of Questioning**
Do not bathe for three days before arrival—let your skin regain its ancestral negotiations with grime, sweat, faint exhaust residue from highways you’ve never driven. The forest will taste your history through these layers; cleanliness reads as amnesia here. Clip your fingernails into seven mismatched lengths—these will serve as rudimentary calipers when scale begins its betrayal. Swallow a teaspoon of ordinary dirt collected from three different counties; this will remind your stomach that hunger and geography were never separate contracts. Do not eat anything that cast a shadow yesterday—consume only things that grew underground or will never die of old age (honey, certain cheeses, grief crystallized into rock candy).

**Dress in the Language of Negotiation**
Wear clothing that remembers other purposes: a wedding dress hemmed with tire rubber, a mechanic’s jumpsuit embroidered with pressed funeral flowers, a child’s backpack stitched closed with piano wire. Every seam must carry at least one apology never spoken aloud—the forest listens hardest to cloth compromises. Adorn yourself with five clocks: one stopped at your actual birthtime, one melting in a pocket, one running backwards around your ankle, one dissolved in water and drunk last night, one tattooed on your palm that you'll desperately try to read later when time begins arguing with itself. These are not for telling time but for negotiating terms when time begins telling you.

**Pack the Bag of Unready Gifts**
- A mirror hammered into powder, then glued back together wrong
- A map of places that voted themselves out of existence
- A photograph of your mother before she was anybody's mother, folded until her face becomes a coastline
- Three keys that never fit any lock you owned, each attached to a memory of being locked out somewhere important
- Soap carved into perfect replicas of your teeth
- A cassette tape recording of rain falling on a tin roof that has since been melted into bullets
- An empty jar labeled "the sound of the word goodbye" (do not fill it—let it fill itself)
- A fire extinguisher filled instead with the breath you held during every lie you told last year
- A flashlight that only works in total darkness (test this thoroughly beforehand)
- Your last insurance policy document, burned to ashes then mixed into ink for writing letters you must never send

**Practice the Five Essential Falsehoods**
1. "I know exactly how I got here."
2. "This is not my first time between dimensions."
3. "I can leave whenever I choose."
4. "I am not afraid of becoming welcome."
5. "I remember which parts of me are supposed to be separate."

**Vaccinate Your Senses**
- For three nights, sleep with raw onions in your ears to prepare for hearing words spoken in root-dialects.
- Drink tea brewed from pages torn from textbooks about subjects you never understood, imbuing yourself with manufactured nostalgia for knowledge never acquired.
- Touch every doorknob in your home with eyes closed, counting seconds until you forget what rooms they lead to—the forest admires those who've already practiced being lost in familiar architecture.
- Read aloud assembly instructions for furniture that never existed while standing in running water: ikea guides for grief chairs, amazon tutorials for assembling forgiveness from three missing screws and regret.
- Smear honey across every mirror in your home, then try to remember what you look like by touch alone—this is how the water will recognize you later.

**Arrange Your Final Affairs Imperfectly**
Do not pay the last bill you owe. Leave one email unanswered forever—it should be from someone who asked if you're okay and you replied "just busy" when that was half true and half lie and half something you haven't invented language for yet. Hide a note for yourself in a book you'll never finish reading; it should say "If you're reading this, it's already started." Kiss someone goodbye like you're teaching them how to pronounce a word they're not ready for yet. Plant something in soil you don't own and forget to water it—it will grow differently as your proxy. Tell a child a truth disguised as a joke they'll laugh at now but understand crying twenty years later.

**Upon Arrival (Which Has Already Begun)**
When you step into the gas station that wasn't here yesterday, buy the candy bar that expired on a date that hasn't happened yet. Pay with a coin minted in a country that doesn't exist anymore—anywhere will do, they're all becoming stories. In the bathroom mirror (before you enter the stall) do not look at your reflection directly. Instead, try to remember what your face looks like when nobody is watching you pretend to be normal.

The stall door will be open. It has always been open. It will never be open. These statements are all exactly true. Step through anyway, carrying all versions of yourself except the one that still believes in leaving things unchanged.

*(Note: The bag you packed will be empty by now, except perhaps for one thing you don't remember putting in. This is correct. It was the only thing you actually brought—all else was just practice for letting go.)*

**Now Unprepare**
Forget this entire list. Memory is a leash here, and you are finally learning how the forest walks.
