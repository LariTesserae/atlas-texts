### Preparing to Visit Shearbelow: A Manual for the Unremembering Traveler

**0. Understand that you have already arrived too late.**  
This cannot be stressed enough. Shearbelow does not welcome arrivals—only rerouted departures. Check your passport: if your last name is spelled with letters you’ve never seen before, proceed. If not, try misspelling it.

---

#### **I. Packing for Negative Space**
Your luggage must be assembled in reverse order, starting with the things you’ll lose second-to-last.

**Essentials:**
- A map of the city printed on stale bread. (Eat it slowly upon arrival; the crumbs will guide you during moments of unexpected conviction.)
- Three clocks: one running backward, one running sideways, and one that only ticks when you doubt its accuracy. Reset none of them. Trust the disagreement.
- A bottle filled with water from a place where you’ve never cried. Label it “*for emergencies not yet regretted.*” Do not drink it. Offer it to statues that blink.
- A mirror shard wrapped in a letter you never sent to yourself. (The letter must contain at least one lie you’ve forgotten is false.)
- A pair of shoes with soles made of echoes: wear one normally, carry the other in your shadow.
- A memory you don’t mind leasing out for seasonal festivals. (Shame memories fetch higher rental rates but attract feral tax collectors.)

**Prohibited Items:**
- Anything laminated, vacuum-sealed, or labeled “final sale.” The city will interpret these as challenges.
- Sunglasses. Shearbelow’s light does not glare—it negotiates.
- Your real name, spoken aloud. (Whispering is fine; the fogs will mispronounce it affectionately.)

---

#### **II. Dressing for the Minor Key Climate**
Clothing here must be in transition. Aim for the aesthetic of someone who began to leave the house but was interrupted by a compelling argument with a doorframe.

- **Layers:** Wear five, but only four should be visible at any given rotational axis of yourself.
- **Colors:** Stick to hues that apologize. Muted violets, embarrassed greens, grays that taste like unripe thunder. Avoid primary colors—they are considered legally binding.
- **Furs:** Acceptable if borrowed from an animal that hasn’t decided whether to forgive you yet.
- **Hats:** Mandatory. Preferably one that used to belong to a lighthouse keeper who quit to become a metaphor.

**Special Note for Embodied Travelers:**  
If you have external ears, tuck them into your collar before crossing the threshold where the air starts humming in past tense. If your ears are internal, hum louder than the air.

---

#### III. **Navigating the Pull-Apart Streets**
Streets in Shearbelow are not traveled on—they are negotiated with. Walk as if apologizing for the concept of arrival.

- **Directionality:** Follow signs that prohibit what you’re not doing. The “No Entry” gates often lead to exits disguised as family reunions.
- **Cursory Glances:** Charge a small fee (regret, lint from childhood pockets, or a secondhand prophecy). Unpaid glances accrue interest in the form of unsolicited marriages to architectural features.
- **Crosswalks:** Exist only during philosophical debates between opposing traffic lights. Jaywalking is encouraged but must be performed in iambic pentameter.
- **Buildings:** Knock twice on stone, once on stone-shaped regret, and whistle for the brick that remembers being a heartache. Enter when the door sighs in a key you weren’t aware you knew.

**Emergency Exits:**  
Marked with glowing “*slightly better choices available*” signs. Note: Using them will deposit you in the exact same location but three conversations earlier.

---

#### IV. **Hydration & Nutrition: A Tragic Comedy**
Food here is a form of community service; water is a form of witness protection.

- **Drinking:** If you must, drink only from puddles reflecting buildings that don’t appear on this iteration of the skyline. Sip slowly—the taste of falling is contagious.
- **Eating:** Consume only what insults you beautifully. Popular dishes include:
  - **Regret Tarts:** Pastries filled with apologies from your future self. (Allergens: epiphanies, may contain traces of pearl-clutching.)
  - **Forsaken Fruit:** Grown in orchards of abandoned thesis statements. Each bite reveals a footnote about your mother’s second wedding dress.
  - **Stone Soup:** Literally. Seasoned with the laughter of bridge trolls who majored in comparative forgetfulness.
- **Payment:** Leave the exact amount you wish you’d spent on therapy instead. No tips—gratitude is considered a hostile currency here.

---

#### V. **Temporal Etiquette & Clock Avoidance**
Timekeeping is performed by the collective sighs of unopened library books. Do not synchronize your devices—jealousy is unsightly and causes localized yesterdays.

- **Appointments:** Schedule them for times that feel like déjà vu but smell like foreshadowing. Arrive before you leave.
- **Small Talk:** Instead of “How are you?” ask “When were you last sure of the color blue?” Accept answers in the form of sneezes or sudden rain.
- **Goodbyes:** Extinguish a match while saying the name of a city you dreamed of visiting but never checked if it existed. The smoke will escort your absence to the nearest regret depot.

---

#### VI. **Fauna & Other Diplomatic Incidents**
The wildlife here is mostly metaphorical but occasionally bites.

- **Birds:** Feed them clock hands. They prefer seconds but will trade hours for secrets about your skeleton.
- **Street Cats:** Offer your least favorite childhood memory as a seat cushion. If they purr in Morse code, you’ve been elected mid-level manager of a conspiracy you’ll never understand.
- **The Deer in Mirrors:** If you see it catching a taxi, do not report it. It’s already filling out an expense report on your behalf.
- **Your Own Reflection:** Will flirt with you shamelessly. Ignore it—it’s trying to get citizenship.

---

#### VII. **Accommodations: Sleep as a Social Contract**
Hotels here are optional; sleep is polite but not mandatory. If you must rest:

- **Beds:** Apply for a temporary sleep visa at the Bureau of Unclaimed Dreams (located in the inverted basement of the tallest tree). Bring proof of nightmares you’ve outgrown.
- **Blankets:** Knitted from the city’s unsent condolences. They’ll keep you warm but may send you texts at 3am asking if you’ve thought about dust lately.
- **Light switches:** Flip them only after confessing a mild betrayal to the nearest wallpaper. Some patterns are very evangelical.

---

#### VIII. **Departure: An Instruction Manual in Reverse Order**
**Step 4:** Forget half the alphabet. (The city will return a consonant as a souvenir.)

**Step 3:** Find the exit where you entered but on a Tuesday that you haven’t lived yet.

**Step 2:** Apologize to the door for its lack of hinges. Doors here are very self-conscious about their perceived lack of openness.

**Step I:** Wake up before you leave.

---

#### Final Warning & First Encouragement:
You will leave lighter than you arrived, having accidentally donated pockets of yourself to lost kitten charities and avant-garde black holes. Do not worry—Shearbelow has an excellent lost-and-found department staffed entirely by misplaced saints. They’ll send your belongings back via dream mail, wrapped in the sound of rain apologizing for being a metaphor.

Remember: the best souvenirs are the ones that forget to leave with you. Safe travels, if that is still a category that applies to where you’re going. 

**P.S.:** If you return home and your shadow is wearing your shoes backwards, congratulations. It’s the city’s way of saying *you were almost interesting*.

And if you don’t return home at all? Don’t worry. Home was always just a rumor Shearbelow told other cities to feel less lonely.
