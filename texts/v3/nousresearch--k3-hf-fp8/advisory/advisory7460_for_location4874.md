Preparing to visit the Wangdangdoodle Nursery Float is impossible in the way preparing for childbirth is impossible—there is a protocol, but it is written in a language you do not yet speak, and obedience is optional in the way breathing underwater is optional if you are already a fish.

Nevertheless, here is what you must do, listed in the order your future self will remember reversing:

---

### **1. Unlearn Solidity**
Begin three months before departure. Every morning, wake at 04:07 (your local time, always) and swallow a tablespoon of warm seawater taken from the nearest bakery. Do not ask how seawater is sold by bakeries; the answer involves a scandalously accurate description of your grandfather’s left kneecap, which you are not ready to hear.

Spend the rest of the day practicing being interrupted by silence. When someone asks your name, pause until the second ceiling fan blade passes the third shadow groove, then reply: “It depends on who’s counting.”

This prepares your membranes for the ultrasound teeth.

---

### **2. Edit Your Body Inventory**
Visit a dentist who works exclusively in dreams. Have them remove your wisdom teeth but replace them with smaller, more polite teeth that hum “Waltzing Matilda” whenever you contemplate Euclidean geometry. Pay in advance with the memory of your first bicycle crash; they will give you an IOU payable in the laughter of a stone fruit you haven’t met yet.

Next, commission a tailor who sews only with lengths of the night you accidentally slept through in 2017. Ask for a coat with seventeen pockets, each pocket containing a slightly smaller version of today’s regret. The coat must weigh exactly the opposite of your childhood pet’s final thought.

You will not wear this coat. It will wear you later, briefly, with apology.

---

### **3. Arrange for Your Absence**
Call your boss. Tell them you’ve been accepted into a residency program for clouds that never learned to cry convincingly. If they ask when you’ll return, say: “When the last e-mail I sent you finishes downloading itself into the past.”

Cancel all subscriptions except for:
- The monthly delivery of bottled apologies from the Republic of Silence
- The weather forecast for cities whose names are anagrams of “regret” spelled backward underwater

Leave your house unlocked but lock every window from the outside. Leave a note for the burglars that reads: *The spoons are spoonsier in the dreams you’re not currently having. Help yourself to the silverware’s anxiety.* 

---

### **4. Acquire the Unacquirable**
15 days before departure, find a hummingbird that is actually a very small time machine disguised as a hobby horse’s abandoned hobby. Negotiate passage by teaching it the difference between punctuality and puncture wounds. It will bite you. This is the password.

10 days before, locate a book titled *The Complete Unabridged Index of Things That Are Not Missing Yet*. Burn it immediately. Smuggle the ashes into a museum of forgotten applause. Whisper to the ashes: “You’re not lost, just unpunctual.”

5 days before, send a postcard to the ocean floor addressed to yourself at age 844. On the back, draw a map of the sound of forgiveness arriving late to its own funeral. Do not include a stamp. The ocean collects hobbies.

---

### **5. Practice Dissolving**
Every evening at sunset (even if your location lacks sun, or set, or evenings), stand fully clothed in an empty bathtub and turn on the showerhead to its most philosophical setting (usually labeled “Existential Drip” or “Tears of Confused Architects”). As the water rises nowhere, recite your bank account number backward in the voice of a lighthouse giving directions to ships that already believe in paradise.

When you forget what your knees are called, you are ready for step 6.

---

### **6. Step 6 Does Not Exist**
Proceed directly to step 7.

---

### **7. Step 7 Is a Mirror**
Look into it until you see yourself looking out of the mirror looking into yourself looking out of the mirror looking into—stop. That’s too many. Save one for the exit lung.

---

### **8. The Day Before: An Unpacking**
Empty your suitcase. Empty it more. Remove the ghost of the suitcase. Remove the concept of luggage from the suitcase’s childhood. What remains should be a perfectly spherical emptiness that smells faintly of the color Tuesday.

Pack only this:
- A ball of string that leads nowhere but apologizes sincerely when pulled
- Three questions you stole from a dying star’s last message (preferably questions involving whales and/or tax law)
- A photograph of the sound your mother’s hands made when she thought no one was listening
- No more than seven memories that contain the word “almost” but never finish the sentence

---

### **9. Arrival (Which Is Always Early for Its Own Funeral)**
Stand on any shoreline where the sand is composed of fragmentary decisions made by sailors who mistook mermaids for meetings they could reschedule. Wait until the tide forgets its own name, then wade barefoot into the water up to exactly your regret’s deepest recorded level.

When a small, polite shark approaches wearing the expression of a librarian who has just been asked to locate “Embarrassment, General Theory of, Volume ∞,” hand it your ball of string without breaking eye contact. If it bows, follow it. If it laughs, laugh slower. If it asks for ID, show it your reflection in the spoon of a lie you told yourself last Tuesday at breakfast. The shark knows you’re lying. That’s how it knows you’re ready.

---

### **10. The Swallowing**
There will be teeth. They will not belong to you. They will belong to the version of you who already paid for this trip in the currency of unspoken bedtime stories. They will ask you to choose between being swallowed whole or being swallowed in installments with interest.

Choose both. Choose neither. Choose the third option written in the margin of the contract in ink made from the laughter of geologists who finally got the joke about rocks dreaming of becoming rivers.

When you are inside the teeth, remember:

- You are not being eaten. You are being reminded.
- The stomach is just a mouth that forgot its manners.
- Digestion is a form of slow applause.

---

### **11. Inside Before Outside**
You will reassemble in a sphere (17 stories tall, if stories stacked like apologies). Your first breath will taste like childhood’s least embarrassing secret spiked with a hint of tomorrow’s dental appointment.

Gravity will introduce itself politely but insist on rotating bedrooms every 11 minutes. Introduce yourself back but use your middle name backward. If gravity laughs, you may stay. If gravity cries fog, catch some in your hands and name it after the last person who asked if you were okay when you were actually longing.

---

### **12. The Tourist’s Guide to Not Being a Tourist**
- Do not touch the walls. They are still learning to forgive walls for what walls have touched.
- Do not ask directions. Directions here are shy and prone to marrying questions that never call them back.
- Do not photograph the dreams. They develop better in memory’s darkroom and besides, your camera’s warranty doesn’t cover existential blur.
- Do not eat the Salad Forest’s footnotes unless you are prepared to testify in court as a character witness for your own punctuation.
- Do not drink the River of Quietude without first writing a thank-you note to the noise you’ll miss most when it’s gone.

---

### **13. Interacting with the Curators**
- When a Curator offers you scissors shaped like question marks, accept graciously but use them only to cut silence into smaller, more portable silences.
- When a Curator asks you to explain your favorite color without using colors or words, hum the sound of a disappointed sunrise learning it was adopted.
- When a Curator offers refreshment made of chilled curiosity, sip slowly enough to forget the question but not so slowly you remember the answer.
- If a Curator’s paper face begins to burn, read it poetry about rain until it forgives fire for being so dramatic.

---

### **14. Souvenirs**
You may take nothing that exists. You may leave nothing that doesn’t. Between these two lies the gift shop.

Recommended purchases:
- A jar of bottled laughter from the year your laughter still fit you
- A scarf knitted from the breath of bridges before they burned
- A map of the stars you’d see if you looked up right now in the version of now where you never left home
- A receipt for the exact moment you realized you were already forgiven

Payment accepted in:
- Slightly used minutes
- First editions of last chances
- Rare stamps licked by people who later became oceans
- Exact change only (exactness negotiable)

---

### **15. The Exit Lung (Which Inhales Returners ExhALEed by regREt)**
When you are ready (you will never be ready), stand in the warm red chamber and remember the taste of your own heartbeat before it learned rhythm.

The coat will find you now—the one stitched from all the introductions you never got around to making. It will be heavier than you remember. That’s because it’s carrying the weight of every door you could have opened but smiled at instead from the sidewalk.

Put it on backward. Button it with apologies for buttons you never learned to sew.

The chamber will ask you a question in the voice of the first person who taught you loneliness by accident. Answer honestly but in reverse alphabetical order. If you stutter, stutter in morse code for “thank you for the indigestion.”

---

### **16. Afterward (Which Is Always Before You’re Ready)**
You will wake on a beach somewhere temperate. Your boots will be full of sand that has read all your unsent letters and offers consignment terms. Your pockets will contain only a seashell and the realization that “back” is just “front” wearing sunglasses and pretending not to recognize you.

The seashell will contain a limerick that rhymes “ineffable” with “treble clef” but the punchline is in a key you’ll never hum correctly on purpose.

Whistle it once anyway, totally off-tempo, loud enough to make the nearest seagull blush.

Then walk inland until you find a town where nobody knows your name but everyone recognizes the way you hesitate before answering telephones that aren’t ringing.

Move there.

Change your name to “Almost.”

Open a shop that sells maps to places that float six kilometers above their own regrets.

Business will be slow.

Perfect.

---

### **Postscript for Journeys That Forget to End**
If, years later, you wake tasting saltwater at 04:07 (a different time zone now, but time zones are just geography’s way of keeping score), and remember this place only as a dream that borrowed your childhood pajamas without asking—

don’t panic.

Panicking is for people who still believe they haven’t been dissolved and reassembled by something that loves them precisely because they keep forgetting the punchline.

Instead:

Go to your kitchen.

Boil water for tea you don’t intend to drink.

As it heats, whisper into the steam:

“Thank you for the teeth. Thank you for the swallowing. Thank you for returning me lighter.”

Then pour the tea into the sink with the same care you’d use introducing your estranged future to your forgiven past.

Dry the cup with the towel you keep for guests who never arrive, but always leave their umbrellas politely.

Place the cup upside down in the cupboard where you store the sounds of applause for performances that haven’t started yet.

Go outside.

Look up.

If the stars wink three times in Morse code for “this too is a flotation device”—

you’ll know they’ve kept your receipt.

And if not?

Whistle anyway.

The ocean’s still listening.

It’s always been a sucker for unfinished limericks about forgiveness arriving late to its own party, wearing the wrong shoes but dancing like it’s finally found the joke it was always meant to be the punchline of.
