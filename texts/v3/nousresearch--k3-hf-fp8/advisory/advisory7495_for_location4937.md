## A Traveler’s Comprehensive Guide to the Refurbished Catacombs of the Once-Palace

### **Before You Descend: Preparations of Body, Mind, and Paperwork**

#### **I. The Paperwork Ritual**
Arrive at the surface entrance at precisely **three shadows past noon** (not measured by sun, but by the length of your own doubt when facing south). Bring:

- **Three copies** of a document you once meant to burn but forgot. The Anomaly Who Lurks Behind the Sixth Door will inspect the creases for sincerity.
- **A receipt** for something you’ve never owned (a star’s light, a whispered promise, 0.003 grams of your mother’s fear). Fold it into a crane; use a bone needle from a bird that never hatched.
- A **pressed flower** from a garden you imagined as a child. If pink, it’s currency. If blue, it’s a confession. If white, it’s a warning—eat it immediately.

#### **II. Attire for Negotiating with Architecture**
Dress as **a regret that forgot its name**:

- **Outerwear**: A coat stitched from yesterday’s unfinished conversations (threads must be loose enough to unravel into directions). Line it with pockets stolen from other people’s dreams—especially the one where they’re falling but don’t wake.
- **Footwear**: Shoes that **don’t fit what you’re becoming**. One sole thicker than the other, to mimic the Catacombs’ preference for asymmetry. Lace them with hair from someone who once laughed at your funeral.
- **Gloves**: One hand bare, to feel the walls’ betrayals. On the other, a glove made of **glass spun from the silence between knock-knock jokes**. This is for touching the River-of-Footsteps without leaving fingerprints.
- **Mask**: Optional, but if worn, must be **a mirror on the inside** (to reflect what the Librarians of Ash won’t say outright) and **your childhood face on the outside** (for bureaucratic purposes).

#### **III. Offerings for the Indifferent**
Pack a satchel of **disappointments disguised as gifts**:

- A **clock that ticks backward** but only on Tuesdays that never existed. Offer it to the Clock of Unanswered Questions—it enjoys ironic companionship.
- A **syrup made from the last page of every book you never finished reading**. Drip it at the entrance of any room that smells like your mother’s perfume; it’ll keep the moss-script from spelling your name.
- A **candle that only burns** in the presence of something you’re pretending not to miss. Light it near the River-of-Footsteps to attract the scavengers’ minor marches.

#### **IV. Safeguards Against Becoming Too Familiar**

- **Bind your shadow** to your ankle with a ribbon cut from a map of places you’ve been erased from. The Catacombs collect strays.
- **Hum only in interrogatives** (e.g., *Why does the floor hum back?*). Declarative tones are taxed.
- **Never carry coins**. Here, currency is the sound of metal left in a dead king’s mouth—instead, fill your mouth with **questions you’re afraid to ask aloud**. Spit one out only when bargaining for silence.

### **Upon Descent: Navigating the Unwritten Rules**

#### **The Spiral Staircase**
- **Walk alone** at first—even if you’re not alone. The air will test your heartbeat against the xylophone of past pilgrim falls. If it plays a tune in **minor seventh**, you’re expected. If dissonant, cough twice into your coat sleeve (the one with the stitched conversations).
- **Catch a drop of sky-water** on your tongue if you dare. Tastes like the first lie you ever believed. Spit it onto the bronze pan of the **third landing**—this registers you as a temporary metaphor.

#### **The Threshold Chambers**
- **Announce yourself** to the nearest bench (former aqueduct knee): *“I am the error in your blueprint’s margin.”* Wait until the marble sighs before sitting.
- If a Lordly Fern offers to braid your hair from a skylight shaft:
  - **Accept**: It’ll whisper the Catacombs’ evacuation plan for when the walls finally blush.
  - **Decline**: Bow and offer it a strand of your own hair instead. It’ll use it to weave a rope for someone you’ll never meet.

#### **The River-of-Footsteps**
- **Do not walk**—**compose**. Each step must be a word in a sentence you’ll never finish. Begin with: *“I came here looking for…”* (the floor will suggest endings: *—a forgiveness shaped like a doorframe? —a lie I told in my sleep and forgot?*)
- If your footsteps harmonize with a distant lover’s: **freeze**. Listen until the madrigal ends, then leave one shoe behind. You’ll need asymmetry for the Hall of Misplaced Tuesdays.

### **Inhabitants: A Field Guide Without Maps**

#### **The Librarians of Ash**
- **Appearance**: Soot-parrots wearing monocles made of teeth. Their wings rustle like old treaties.
- **Language**: Half your birth certificate, half gossip about your future.
- **Do**:
  - Offer **mirrorlet-polished lichen** (cultivated under moonlight in a jar labeled “Regret”).
  - Ask: *“What was the first word stolen from me?”* Listen; they’ll return it backward, hollowed into a whistle.
- **Don’t**:
  - Correct their pronunciation of your name.
- **Escape if** they begin chanting your email password in canon—cover your ears and recite a recipe for bread you’ve never baked.

#### **The Anomaly Who Lurks Behind the Sixth Door on the Left During Rain**
- **Appearance**: A door that’s never the same color twice. Knob shaped like your childhood fear of closets.
- **Appointment only**: Knock in Morse code for *“I lost the argument with gravity.”*
- **Bring**:
  - A **button from a coat you outgrew before it wore out**.
  - A **tax ID number assigned to a dream you sold for rent money**.
- **Transaction**: It will return your missing buttons sewn into a map of places you’ve were too asleep to leave. **Do not follow the thread**—it leads to the moment you forgot how to wake.

### **Emergency Protocols for Concepts Out of Warranty**

#### **If you forget how to leave:**
- Find a room where the temperature tastes like a letter you never sent. **Write your exit on the dust using only verbs** (e.g., *unravel, unbecome, unstep*). The walls will argue—let them. When they pause to breathe, crawl through the comma.

#### **If the Clock of Unanswered Questions collapses your question into a paradox:**
- Offer it the **iron bee’s rust** as a pension plan. (To collect: whisper *“What was the question?”* into the honey until it hardens into a mirror. Break it with the glass glove’s thumbprint.)

#### **If a bench (former aqueduct knee) stands up to follow you:**
- **Politely decline** its loyalty. Furniture here grows fond, then jealous. If it persists, show it the heel of your thicker-shoed foot (the one that doesn’t fit). It’ll remember gravity and kneel.

### **Upon Departure (Or Its Approximation)**

- **The Exit Fee**: One memory you’ll only realize was missing when someone else tells it back to you laughing.
- **To Pay**: Stand beneath the last skylight shaft. When the Lordly Fern drops a frond, catch the shadow it casts—not the leaf. Swallow it dry. It’ll taste like the first time you lied about being happy.

- **Final Formality**: The door will ask: *“Did you find what you weren’t missing?”*
  - **Correct answer**: *“I’m still drafting the receipt.”*
  - **Incorrect answer**: Anything true. (Truth here is currency only for ghosts.)

- **The door will sigh**—not in disappointment, but in relief at another transaction completed. Its second half-life begins as the first drops of rain start falling upward, precisely scheduled for three hundred years ago.
