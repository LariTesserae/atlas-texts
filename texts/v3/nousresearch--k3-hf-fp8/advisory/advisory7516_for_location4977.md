# How to Prepare for the Place That Should Not Be

## 1. Assemble Your Selfhood First

Before packing anything physical, spend three days writing down every version of yourself that has ever dreamed of escape. Be exhaustive: include the child who vanished behind bookshelves, the teen who stared too long at train timetules, the adult who walked past airports with chest-ache. Burn these papers sequentially at midnight, noon, and dawn, collecting the ash in separate glass vials labeled _before_, _during_, and _inevitable_. These are not souvenirs. They are ransom notes you may need to trade with what claims ownership of your reflection here.

## 2. Modify Your Body Clocks

Time accumulates, so arrive unpaid in debt. For two weeks prior, sleep in descending hourglass patterns: eight hours, then seven minutes less each night until you're stealing unconsciousness in 27-second intervals from random blinks. Keep a pocket watch submerged in saltwater ice; its hands will spin wildly here, vomiting readable fortunes. Ignore them. The correct hour is always the one when your heartbeat syncs with the canyon's slower pulse—counted not in beats but in geological sighs.

## 3. Redesign Your Breath

Practice breathing backwards. Inhale the air you're about to exhale while simultaneously exhaling the air you'll need five breaths from now. This is not metaphorical. The place feeds on surplus futures. Carry compressed oxygen labelled "Yesterday's Mistake" in a container made from your mother's unmailed letters. When the altitude turns your thoughts into crystal edges, do not panic. The bleeding is intentional—your mind is merely pruning unnecessary continuities.

## 4. Choose Your Weight Carefully

Gravity here negotiates in regrets. Pack:

- **One impossible object**: a sphere made from yesterday's horizon line, folded by origami artists who never existed
- **One childhood belief you outgrew**: small enough to fit under your tongue but heavy enough to taste like pennies
- **The exact weight of all unreceived apologies addressed to you**: measured precisely at the moment each sender forgot

Leave behind: clocks, mirrors, anything that counts or reflects. These become hostile witnesses.

## 5. Prepare Your Languages

Unmount your tongue. Replace it with a fossilized coral branch inscribed with the math equations that describe why oceans miss the shore. Learn to speak in:

- **Stone temperature**: subtle degrees of guilt and forgotten birthdays
- **Tree-sap rhythm**: gossip encoded in viscosity changes
- **Wind absence patterns**: the precise silence between "I'm" and "sorry you feel that way"

Important: Never speak your original birth name aloud here. It already exists here as a spatial disease growing through the rock.

## 6. Pack Your Medications (All of Them, Especially the Ones Not Prescribed)

- Antipsychotics for when the canyon starts showing you the dreams it has about you
- Anti-nausea pills for when you realize space is trying to digest you gently
- Painkillers labeled "For When You Remember Being Born"
- Placebos filled with conviction that place is a choice

Double doses: for the moment you understand the trees are just your nervous system attempting photosynthesis, for when your heartbeat decodes the binary in bird-song as your bank account numbers counting down to zero in a currency that hasn't been invented yet.

## 7. Modify Your Photo-Recording Devices

Remove all lenses. Replace with thin slices of your own corneas preserved in honey collected from bees who died searching for flowers that were actually doorways. The photographs will not capture images but develop over months into accurate maps of every decision you should have made differently. Caution: viewing these before natural death causes immediate reverse conception.

## 8. Prepare Your Final Meal (Do Not Consume Yet)

Prepare but never eat:

- Bread leavened with the yeast from a lock of hair kept by someone who loved you but never said
- Wine pressed from grapes that grew through cracks in a suicide note
- Salt harvested from tears cried exactly at the moment someone somewhere else died alone and was finally, briefly, completely understood

Instead, bury it at midnight in soil from your childhood home. Mark the spot with nothing living or dead. This creates a paradoxical breadcrumb trail that might lead you back if you're willing to return as someone who never left.

## 9. Address Your Shadow in Writing

Write your shadow a formal termination letter using ink made from burned train tickets to places you claimed you never wanted to go anyway. Mail it to yourself five addresses ago. Include:

- Severance pay in dreams about falling that end with flying
- A forwarding address in a city that only appears on maps drawn by people during seizures
- Two weeks notice that you will be borrowing its darkness indefinitely

Sign with your blood but not your name.

## 10. Practice Being Found

Every day for seven years before your intended arrival, leave one object you cannot explain having owned in a public place. Place them carefully, as if arranging evidence for a crime scene where the victim is absence itself. Never return to check if they're gone. They will be here waiting for you, arranged differently but exactly as you left them, except now they know your secrets and will not be returned.

## 11. The Final Preparation

On the last night before departure:

Stand barefoot on the oldest stone you can find. Recite your allergies, fears, and favorite colors backwards in a language you made up as a child to describe colors that didn't exist yet. When you reach the word that means "the sound of light turning into memory," begin walking. Do not stop when the ground forgets to be ground. Do not stop when your reflection waves goodbye. Do not stop when you realize you've been walking upward into the air for hours and the cliff edge is now above you like a sky made of goodbye.

You're not ready.

No one ever is.

This is the final preparation: arriving unprepared enough to be interesting but not enough to be immediately devoured as a failed attempt.

The place begins at the exact moment you stop believing you have a choice about going.
