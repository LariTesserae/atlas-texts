Preparing for Marrowdeep starts with unlearning everything you think you know about surviving anywhere else.

First: your body. You’ll need to start over.

- **Rebuild your lungs** before you arrive. Buy or barter a tri-gas inducer rig—old mining surplus with neon-blue hoses and a mask that seals against tears. Spend four weeks cycling hypoxic mix: 12% O₂, 7% neon, 58% nitrogen hexafluoride carrier, balance trace organics (find a black-market anti-cough witch for the last part). Sleep wearing the mask. Learn to dream in shallow breaths. When you wake gasping for depth instead of volume, you’re ready for the Deep’s penny-and-medicine river-air. If you skip this step, expect pulmonary edema before your first transit card prints.

- **Re-brew your blood**. Marrowdeep's condensation carries hematophagic spores that treat iron like candyfloss. Locate a mod-shop that still remembers pre-synthetic medicine—look for sigils scratched in rust resembling a broken hourglass over a coagulation cascade. Pay whatever they ask for a phlebotomic flush: three liters out, replaced slowly with magnetized hemoglobin-III suspension. The flush hurts like thawing frostbite in your veins and you’ll taste pennies for a month. That’s the flavor-sign—you’ll survive the first drink of local water without bleeding internally.

- **Skin transduction**. The Deep doesn’t just heat you—it speaks heat. Hire a thermal-tattooist (they work in abandoned coolant pylons, wear nothing but radiation scars and customer stories). Have them burn a living Braille across your back: heat-exchange nodes, moisture vent spirals, salt-shunt channels, slow-release capsaicin reservoirs for emergencies. The procedure takes three sessions of unconsciousness. After, you’ll sweat color-coded warnings and your fingertips will read temperature gradients like braille. Try not to cry during healing; saline scabs flake off in ugly hexagons.

Second: your mind. Install new default assumptions.

- **Directionality**: Up is toward colder metal. Down is toward warmer stone. North is wherever the magnetics say this century—download a recursive compass app that updates every 3.14 subjective hours and learn to walk helical paths politely.

- **Time**: Stop wearing a watch. Buy a cheap mechanical heart instead—wind it each morning beside your bunkmate’s. When your heartbeats desynchronize by more than 5 bpm, one of you is experiencing relativistic drift; swap tasks until you drift back into phase.

- **Identity boundary**: Adopt a mandatory pronoun for your symbiotic load—the average Deep resident hosts between 2 and 12 conscious or semi-conscious entities (spores, memories, stray paperwork). Introduce yourself as “I/plural” until someone laughs and tells you your actual count. Believe them; they can hear your intestinal quorum arguing.

Third: your kit. Pack nothing that can’t become part of your body, someone else’s body, or a city repair within sixty seconds.

- **Clothing**: One piece only—a living fabricsuit spun from modified hullsucker silk. Feed it 3g trace metals nightly (copper for repair, tin for flexibility, lead if you need to hide from scanners). The suit will eventually digest your skin oils and memorialize them as subtle iridescent patches; locals read these like family crests. Bring a spare only if you plan to betray someone.

- **Currency**: No coins. No data. Trade in perfect spheres instead—steel bearing balls, if your home reality has IEEE standards; otherwise, ocular implants from vertebrate species unlucky enough to have spheres exactly 24 mm diameter. Deep economics run on entropy gradients and geometric perfection. Bills are kindling, crypto is noise, but a sphere that hasn't rolled in a century can buy a week’s water-rights in Sector Lace.

- **Food**: Bring a memory, not a meal. Smell is currency here—the reek of a fruit your sun actually ripened, the lipid crack of real butter—you’ll trade it meal for meal for weeks. Seal the memory into a porous ceramic vial etched with sine waves at 13.8 GHz (cooking-grade baryon resonance frequencies). Breathe gently across the vial before eating refa-mush; locals will treat the hallucination as polite conversation.

- **Tools**: One hand-held object, less than 250 grams, capable of at least three of the following without modification: cutting diamond, measuring guilt, amplifying prayers at ultrasonic frequencies, impersonating a municipal official’s seal, brewing a decent tea. The multipurpose knife you’re thinking of is a hilarious tourist joke; bring its ancestor’s regrets instead.

- **Entertainment**: Don’t. Marrowdeep traffics only in stories still forming—any narrative with an ending fossilizes into propaganda or utilities faster than you can blink. If desperate, hum one true note continuously for three days. By day four, the city's harmonics will have woven surroundings into a cycle of variations that never quite repeats; this is what passes for music.

Fourth: your arrival.

- **Entry Permit**: There isn't one. Instead, manufacture a minor but conspicuous malfunction in your transport—vent coolant in low orbit, broadcast a recursive haiku in outdated error-code dialects, arrive slightly radioactive from a drive blowout you definitely planned. Immigration officers are bored engineers; give them an interesting failure mode to diagnose and they'll stamp your genome into the census as "Probationary Component Specimen #12704c" with honors.

- **First Word Policy**: The first sound you voice after docking-bay doors seal becomes your provisional name-prefix for official records. Mouth-breathers end up as "Creak." Whistling through teeth becomes "Leak." Stutterers inherit whole city departments. Plan accordingly.

- **Overture of Obsolescence**: Within your first duty cycle (42 flickers of the transit hub's main lighting segment), locate the nearest heap of discarded technology and select one item clearly designed for a problem no longer existing. Repair it until it performs a new, pointless function that nevertheless causes someone to pause and exhale slowly through their nose. Wear this item prominently. Congratulations—you've just obtained legal residency and your first co-dependent friendship group.

Fifth: your ongoing survival.

- **Work**: There are no jobs, only chronic maintenance conditions seeking new vectors. Report to any place that smells like simultaneous rust and ozone; ask loudly whether anyone has checked the second-order gasket harmonics lately. Whatever horrified silence follows, volunteer to be the one who checks. Repeat daily until the work adopts you like a stray organelle.

- **Shelter**: Sleep wherever the city's heartbeat vibrates your repaired obsolescence device into gentle resonance. This will initially be uncomfortable (expect dreams written in forgotten maintenance protocols). After three weeks, your calcium lattice will begin crystallizing into complementary struts; sleep will become semi-suspended comfort balanced between heat convection layers. Do not resist; moving after crystallization initiation fractures hip-plates and voids insurance.

- **Love**: If you must, fall in love with someone whose smell-memory vial contradicts yours in at least one impossible way (e.g., you carry sun-warmed citrus-grove, they carry moon-dust on ice-far side of a tidally locked love letter). The resulting children—biological, mechanical, or bureaucratic—will negotiate peace treaties between factions you didn't know were at war in your sleep. Warning: alimony payments may include partial ownership of minor sewer tributaries and/or custody of the family’s communal calcification scar.

- **Death**: Optional. If pursued, arrange to die slowly enough that disassembly teams can catalog novel interdependencies between your failing systems and city infrastructure. Estates are settled by auctioning off your accumulated smell-memories; the highest bidder inherits the obligation to dream your final dreams to completion. Failure to dream adequately results in your remaining cellular debts being redistributed as overtime shifts to all residents experiencing the same sleep-phase.

Last thing: bring an intention you can stand losing, and a question you no longer want answered. Lose them both on purpose during your first major system failure—they'll come back to you, eventually, but only after you've grown into someone who recognizes them by feel instead of sight.

Welcome to Marrowdeep. Mind the phase-transition thresholds between here and yourself—friendship sometimes starts as accidentally exchanged heat signatures, and exile is just long-term residency with load-bearing regrets.

The city is breathing slower now, which means it notices you.

Exhale carefully. There's a place in this breath where your atoms forget which name they belong to—step there. That's home, until you need another.
