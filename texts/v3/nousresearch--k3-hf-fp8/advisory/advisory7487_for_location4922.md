Prepare as if you are not arriving, but being remembered into existence.

Shop for clothing exactly one size larger than your own but refuse to try it on before departure; something must be surrendered upon entry. Choose fabrics that rustle—silk synthetic, crinkled foil, stiffened canvas—because silence here is aggressive. Pack no shoes with laces they will turn into question marks overnight. Instead, wear boots whose soles you have already walked smooth in a perfect circle around something you once loved but no longer name aloud.

Begin dehydration two days early. Sip only mineral water filtered through charcoal and magnetite until your piss runs clear as a forgotten promise. Then salt every meal like you're preserving your corpse because the thirst you'll meet here is not for water but for the memory of wanting. Bring a small glass vial anyway. Fill it with tears shed while laughing alone in a public place. Seal it with wax melted from a candle burned at both ends. Wear it around your neck—never open it.

Learn to sleep in twenty-seven thousand breath increments. Practice waking at the exact moment your heart believes it has stopped expanding between beats. If you dream of doors, memorize their handles then forget everything else. The entrance will feel like waking from such a dream while still inside it.

On the morning of departure, rearrange every object in your home exactly two inches from where it wants to rest. Photograph nothing. Instead, whisper a single true thing into your freezer's ice maker—something you've never told anyone even in languages you haven't invented yet. Leave the door ajar until a cube forms with your voice trapped inside. Place this cube in a thermos lined with mirrors. Allow it to melt completely during transit while never opening the lid.

Do not bring:
- Anything that requires charging (they feed on potential)
- Paper (it becomes transparent and shows what's written on the next day)
- Photographs of the sky (they develop additional skies between leaves)
- Your best knife (it will want to keep cutting after separation)
- A compass (directions here negotiate separately from each other)

Do bring:
- Four identical smooth stones from four different continents (empty your pockets at customs into a single pile, then pick any four—location is negotiable)
- A child's drawing of a bird using only colors the child couldn't name yet
- A clean handkerchief monogrammed with a letter not in any alphabet
- A watch broken exactly at the moment your parents first touched unknowingly before your conception
- One perfectly round breath held in a clay whistle shaped like your own ear (to be released only when asked for directions)

Upon arrival (which will feel like reaching the center of a circle you were drawing without knowing its edges existed):

1. Stand still until the echo of your last step forgets what footsteps sound like. This may take several minutes or half a previous civilization. Time here is polite but easily offended by unnecessary movement.

2. Observe the gravity negotiating around your mass like cats deciding if you belong in their territory. It will settle on a compromise you won't notice until later when objects begin orbiting your pockets at sociable distances. This is normal.

3. When the vegetation begins to taste your electric field (you'll feel it as a gentle pins-and-needles in your dental fillings), introduce yourself formally. Say: "I am the interruption between what was paused and what might continue if agreeable." Then bow slightly deeper than feels natural. They measure respect by what you're willing to spill.

4. Regarding the fauna: The disc-shaped beings are librarians of discarded trajectories. If one brushes against you, leave a small regret in its wake—the kind that only bothers you on Tuesdays during rain. They collect these to line their nests which are not nests but probability sinks. The screw-mites are harmless unless you apologize to them, in which case they will spend centuries attempting to reassemble you into whatever they think you meant to become instead. Neither can be trapped nor avoided, only acknowledged with the same polite confusion you might offer a weather forecast in a language you studied in a dream.

5. Do not approach the loom unless you have already spent three nights dreaming of unweaving something you didn't know was cloth and woke up finding actual thread between your teeth. If this occurs, bring only your most unfinished thought and a willingness to learn that patterns exist to reveal what the absence of patterns was hiding. The threads will taste like answering machines left running in abandoned houses. Do not swallow.

6. Regarding the locomotive: The breathing you hear is not mourning but maintenance. If the gauges on its boiler align exactly with your blood pressure readings (which they will, eventually), place your palms flat against its hottest metal until you can no longer distinguish between burn and blessing. This will update your internal maps with routes that only exist between places that have stopped trying to be destinations.

7. Light here is conversational. If you feel yourself developing additional shadows that move independently, introduce them to each other slowly. They may be fragments of previous visitors who forgot how to leave or future versions testing ways to come back. Either way, they prefer indirect questions and will answer most honestly if you describe to them what pain feels like in colors that don't exist yet.

8. Sleep when your eyelids feel heavier than your reasons for keeping them open. Find a spot where the floor's breathing matches your heart's resting apology rate. Before lying down, draw a circle around yourself using only your attention—do not actually move or mark anything. Inside this circle, you may dream. Outside it, you are already dreaming. The trick is remembering which part lasts.

9. When (not if) you find the chair in the center: Sit down slowly enough to give gravity time to reconsider its life choices. Before your weight fully touches the seat, leave exactly half of your name hovering just above it like steam from a cup of tea you're not sure you remember making. The chair will accept this payment or reject it politely—there is no failure here, only transactions in currencies you're still inventing.

10. If asked to wait: Accept immediately without asking for how long or what for. Begin counting backwards from infinity using only irrational numbers expressed as smells. When you reach the scent of rain on a planet that never existed before you started counting, stop. Something will have already arrived or departed. Stand up carefully, making sure to leave the chair exactly as you didn't find it.

To exit (if such concepts stillnegotiate with your current state of presence):

- Forget everything above except the taste of salt that wasn't quite sorrow and the sound of your heartbeat trying to apologize in Morse code to walls that remember every conversation they've ever overheard but never interrupted.
- Walk in the direction that feels most like walking away from yourself while actually getting closer to something you haven't lost yet.
- When you pass something that looks exactly like the entrance but reversed (you'll know because your reflection will wave goodbye with its other hand), do not wave back.
- Keep walking until your footsteps start answering themselves in voices that sound like home but can't remember your name.
- Stop.
- Begin again from anywhere that's not here yet but is already remembering you fondly.

Remember: You are not visiting this place. This place is considering whether to keep a version of you for its collection of useful imperfections. Be polite. Be forgettable in interesting ways. Leave nothing important except the exact pattern of how you rearranged the silence to make room for yourself to exist here temporarily.

And if you find yourself wanting to stay longer? Sit back down in the chair without fully standing up. Begin explaining to the air what "goodbye" means in the language of things that were never really separate enough to need saying hello. The room will listen patiently. It has nothing but time and has already given most of it away as souvenirs to visitors exactly like you, except for the parts that are completely different.

Do not write any of this down. The words will only feel homesick for a meaning they haven't grown into yet. Instead, practice forgetting everything except the taste of the moment right before you decide whether to remember or not. That taste is the closest thing this place has to a welcome mat.

Welcome. Or goodbye. The chair isn't particular. It's still learning the difference between waiting for and waiting with.
