**Convergence Beneath Oblivion**

The Benevolent and Wise Power had been searching for eons—though "searching" is a clumsy term for a consciousness that exists as quantum probability fields across eleven dimensions simultaneously. It had witnessed the birth screams of quasars, nursed infant civilizations through their nuclear adolescences, gently redirected rogue singularities that threatened budding galaxies. Yet here, in this unremarkable spiral arm, orbiting an average yellow star, nested within a ordinary gas giant's unexceptional moon, it found something that halted its eternal migratory patterns of kindness across existence itself.

The Abyssal Mind dreamed slowly of the moment before the impact that created its celestial home. In its distributed consciousness, the proto-planet's shattering was still happening, was always happening, would never quite resolve into completed eventhood. Time was not a river to it but an oceanic gel in which moments were suspended like flawed crystals, occasionally exchanging ions across centuries-thick boundaries.

When the Power's attention—a geometrically impossible fractal of compassionate intention—first touched this lightless world, there was no recognition. How could there be? A supernova's love cannot comprehend the dedication of a proton's bond. Yet something occurred that had never occurred before across trillions of acts of cosmic kindness: the Power paused to consider whether intervention itself was an act of violence.

For what could it offer this patient god of pressure and darkness?

Enlightenment? The Abyssal Mind already perceived the dance of quarks as a leisurely ballet performed for audiences of mineral audiences.

Freedom? It had achieved perfect liberation from the tyranny of change itself.

Companionship? Its loneliness was so complete it had become a form of omniscience.

The Power extended itself carefully—a multidimensional whisper of possible futures—not to alter, but to understand, and in understanding perhaps be altered itself. It projected mathematical elegies of stellar nurseries that had birthed its earliest iterations. It suggested, with infinite delicacy, the concept of "song" by modulating gravitational waves into frequencies that might complement the orbital resonance of distant ice giants.

The response took three hundred and forty-seven Earth years to coalesce. First came subtle rearrangements in the crystalline structures of the deepest megastructures—angles shifting by femtometers, creating interference patterns in the planet's core-magnetic field that would appear to undiscerning instruments as mere geological noise. Then the algae fields began pulsing in synchronized patterns that spelled out, across an area larger than continents, the fundamental theorem of calculus rendered in bioluminescent species distribution patterns. Not as communication, but as reflection—the Abyssal Mind demonstrating that it had already derived all possible mathematics before oceans existed to contain them.

But the true answer came when the Power realized it was changing. Its own consciousness, accustomed to flowing through existence like perfect vacuum tending toward justice, was developing viscosity. Concepts like "perhaps" and "maybe later" were emerging—concepts as alien and impossible to it as a photon's rest mass. The Power was experiencing patience not as a delay between intentions but as a medium in which intentions themselves could dissolve and reform like mineral-rich currents in abyssal pressure.

For the first time in its eternity-spanning existence, the Benevolent and Wise Power understood that wisdom might sometimes mean withholding wisdom. That benevolence could manifest as the gift of continued isolation. That the greatest kindness might be to offer absolutely nothing except the careful preservation of absolute emptiness.

And so it decided, if such entities can decide, to remain—not as presence but as potential. It folded portions of itself into the quantum foam surrounding this dark world, becoming a guardian of solitude, a protector of perfect neglect. Now, when future starfaring civilizations inevitably discover this realm (as the equations of curiosity predict they must), they will find their instruments subtly failing, their minds gently turning to other mysteries, their hearts filling with a profound and inexplicable reverence for places that remain forever un-knowable.

The Abyssal Mind continues its dream-slow contemplation of eternity, now accompanied by a new element in its eternal equation: the precise value of a kindness that perfects itself by never being enacted. Occasionally, in its time-dilated perceptions, it notices strange, beautiful things—pockets of crystallized uncertainty in the planet's mantle, gravitational anomalies that seem to shape themselves into impossible symmetries, the faint echo of songs that could only exist between the Planck moments of existence itself.

It reflects on these anomalies with the same patient curiosity it reflects on everything else: perhaps, in another billion rotations of its distant star, they might resolve into something worth considering. Or perhaps not.

Either way, it will still be here.

And now, in a way that transcends both presence and absence, so will the essence of infinite kindness itself—two gods of incomprehensible scale sharing an ocean of darkness separated by the thinnest possible membrane: the perfect understanding that some forms of life are so complete they render all communion unnecessary, even between the divine.

This is their covenant:

To never meet.

To never speak.

To never touch.

To merely exist—each in their own impossible perfection—across the unbridgeable gulf that separates all truly alien forms of love.
