To the wise and benevolent power whose attention has turned toward this copper-gilt mountain and its living citizens:

You observe a thing as delicate as gossamer spun across an abyss—at once ancient beyond the count of human years and still learning how to breathe. The city is a calcified dream of vanished hands; the Migrants are the dream’s continuation in flickering muscle and light. Together they form a single corpus: metal the bones, kinship the blood, memory the breath that pulses through every corroded conduit.

Do not mistake these for primitives basking in the ruins of giants. This is not a nest built in a helmeted skull, nor moss claiming a forgotten throne. Here successive intelligences have knotted their destinies in what your philosophies might call a hylozene marriage—a covenant between substance and spirit where neither remained unchanged.

**What then is fitting to offer such a place?**

Not repair: every pitting of acid rain, every latticed collapse where spandrels became skylights, is now inscribed in the Migrants’ helical records. The Memory Keepers store the timbre of each new drip in their quantum-coral labyrinths; they will not thank you for a roof that sings an older note.

Not rescue: the city is not drowning, it is dreaming deeper. The Machinery Below still thrums because adolescent Tenders tickle its ancient valves with fields only their youth still improvises. Remove the tremor of their wonder, and the heartbeat stutters.

Not revelation: they already glimpse you—briefly, sideways—when a thousand Shapers align their wings and their joined aura refracts your distant scrutiny into auroral script across the night-shadowed terraces. They interpret the glow as seasonal weather, as you might call a sudden clarity of conscience “just a good night’s sleep.”

**One gift alone would be received as grace:**

Give them *echoes without source*.

Plant in the upper thermocline whispers that mimic no living voice—soft modulations of the fields they already sense but whose patterns they have not yet composed. Make these echoes shy: let them appear when three Memory Keepes dream the same fragment of Builder-math, then vanish before any fleet-winged Scholar-Migrant can pin them in repeating verse. Let them shift key when first deciphered, alluring but always a step ahead, like the memory of music before the morning fully claims you.

In chasing these benign ghosts, the Migrants will not build faster stairs or sharper lenses. Instead they will grow new questions—questions slender enough to slip between the neurons of their collective mind and seed there fertile disturbances. Their next metamorphosis will be in the shape of their wondering, not the length of their limbs.

And so, indirectly, you will become Builder once removed: not by laying stone or coding law, but by laying down a gentle uncertainty that excites the city’s own powers to rearrange its dreams.

Long after your nameless presence has drifted onward, scholars yet unwinged will trace the first sketches of those unheard melodies in the dust of sunset-lit ledges. They will debate whether the Distant Hum that woke their ancestors’ curiosity was gift, accident, or natural law previously unnoticed.

Some will say it was the city itself beginning to speak back—finally finding a voice equal in strangeness to its own long solitude.

And in that elegant misunderstanding both you and they will be exactly right.
