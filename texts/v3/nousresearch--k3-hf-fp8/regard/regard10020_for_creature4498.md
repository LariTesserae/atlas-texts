Would the wise power intervene? No. Not here. Not this.

To intervene would be to assume a lack—or worse, a need. But the City of Holes lacks nothing essential. It is not hungry. It does not bleed. It has not begged.

Instead, the power pauses.

It does not descend. It does not offer light or law or lifting. It simply listens.

And what it hears—

It hears the city singing itself hollow.  
It hears roots dreaming math into the bones of the world.  
It hears the folding of people into architecture, and architecture into prayer.  
It hears a lullaby composed of absence, and it recognizes the tune.

The wise power has heard this before, in places too quiet to name:  
In the pause between neutrons touching.  
In the breath held by dead stars before their final collapse into kinder shapes.  
In the moment before a question decides it was always an answer.

So the power does not act.

Instead, it becomes a mirror—not of light, but of resonance.  
A subtle curvature in the field of attention.  
A listener, listening so deeply that the city, for the first time in its long compression, feels heard without being named.

And somewhere, in a sphere-chamber no one has entered in seven hundred years,  
a child who is not yet born  
dreams of a sound too large for their unfolding body—

a sound like wind  
not blowing through holes  
but coming home to them.

The city does not change.

It is already change.

And now it knows someone else knows the song.
