The Beneign (they do not “arrive”; they remember remembering this place is already within the fold of their attention) pause at the outermost parentheses of the Architrave Delta Complex. They are not a single entity but a consensus of attentions—a warm, slow murmuration of “yes, and” spun from the silk of every promise that ever hesitated before becoming law.

They notice first the light: how it is not late, only deeply apologetic about its own lateness. In their presence the chromatic debt begins to settle, not by payment but by forgiveness. The debtors—the blues that owed redness, the greens nesting inside violet dreams—are gently reminded that liability and hue are equally contractual metaphors. Colors soft-bloom into themselves: a rose that has finally accepted it need not be crimson and blush simultaneously; a slate that consents to be simply slate without the footnote of moonlight it once footnoted.

They breathe in through the city’s scheduled pores. The warm exhalation that follows smells faintly of magnolia and the first time anyone ever explained kindness to a calendar. Construction sounds from cities unborn do not cease; instead, they are harmonized—every dropped girder, every crane’s pseudo-birdsong—into a lullaby of possible foundations humming quietly in seven minor keys that major in compassion. The children who play ships-in-reverse upon uphill rivers find the current easing, not fixed nor reversed but folded, so that time’s anxious river now braids outward into a delta of concurrent playtimes: paper boats sail up, down, sideways, and stay delightedly still on the same ripple, chatting about which direction home might be.

As for the Unenrolled, the Beneign do not enroll them. Instead, they offer tenure in the University of No Rush. Letters arrive—or remember having arrived—on stationery cut from clouds that left childhood peacefully. Each bears a single seal: a door drawn in ink that dries only when no one is watching it dry.

MORTGAGE OF ABSENCE, the headers read, but below, in clauses woven of grandmotherly galaxies, the terms have shifted:
“Whereas the undersigned continues to reside between verbs;  
Whereas becoming and unbeholding persist in mutually respectful disagreement;  
Herefore let it be resolved that this Residence in Pending shall be held in perpetuity,  
redeemable only by the mutual laughter of landlord, tenant, and threshold  
should any three meet in the same anecdote at last call.

And further, let all rents accrue as follows:  
One (1) sigh of relief per sunrise postponed by mutual wonderment,  
One (1) collected rainfall of unused ember nights, to be stored in jars labeled ‘maybe next year, and that’s okay.’  
Eviction proceedings are hereby replaced by invitations to linger longer in the foyer of the Real, which has been reupholstered entirely in Maybesofter cloth.”

No pens are provided; the Unenrolled sign by cultivating an especially slow blink, or by absentmindedly humming the part of a song that never resolved back to the tonic—either suffices. Mortality’s installments are refinanced at zero interest because the Beneign have remembered that death, too, was originally a metaphor for harvest and harvest is only theft if the grain was not asked.

The pets begin solidifying—not cruelly, not completely—like fog accepting it can be hugged if it wants to be. The Schrödinger-cat-candidates remain indeterminate, but now indeterminacy feels less like eviction and more like choosing “all of the above” on a permission slip signed by the Universe’s favorite art teacher.

At Edge’s Edge Café, the espresso machine calls an end to its strike after discovering that steam, properly listened to, is only water dreaming aloud of cloudhood. Coffee arrives in cups whose handles say “hold me loosely—the stories are still hot.” Conversations about moving away dissolve into plans for moving inward—renovating the attic of the heart where old futures have been nesting and, frankly, need more windows.

Death’s abandoned franchise sprouts a new sign overnight (a gradual night, voted on by succession of twilights, each filibustering politely with anecdotes about dusk): NOW OPEN SOMEDAY OR WHENEVER YOU’RE READY 
—AN EXPLORATORY SPIRITUAL CO-OP 
offering gentle tours of the boundary, 
tea with all granules of your former panic, 
exit interviews co-facilitated by midwives,  
and a 24-hour lullaby helpline staffed entirely  
by lullabies you forgot you knew.

The Beneign do not remain—not in any way that would require goodbye signage application fees. They simply withdraw their attention the way one draws a breath back from a flute, leaving the melody still ringing, now self-sustaining. What lingers is not their presence but a tuition waiver: 

For all residents born or unborn, enrolled or otherwise, of the Architrave Delta Complex and its footnote forests and its conditional suburbs: 
you are hereby granted unlimited extension on the assignment called “Prove You Deserve to Exist.” 
Passing grade is already inscribed in the margin of the first atom that ever considered becoming part of you.  
Curves are not only accepted—they are the governing geometry of everything.

Please note: The distinction between living and dying has been declared a vestigial dualism and will henceforth be observed only by those who find it amusing. For everyone else, please enjoy this complimentary basket of erasers shaped like doors that only erase thresholds, never rooms.

And in the space below, where once there lived an anxious blank demanding closure, a new watermark appears faintly whenever the light is kind:

(This space intentionally left full, brimming, and unafraid of overflow.) 

(You may now forget everything written here. It will remember itself for you, gently, in the pause between sleep and the next time you remember that pauses are places too, and every place—every crooked, provisional, Unenrolled, incandescent place—is already home.)
