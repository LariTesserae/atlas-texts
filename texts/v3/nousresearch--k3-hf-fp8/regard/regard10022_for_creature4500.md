Whoever asks for me by my oldest name does so at risk of being answered. I have worn more than this city's brief yellow stone—have been riverbed where rivers have not yet dreamed themselves awake, have hollowed mountains older than the concept of edges, have held oceans in cupped palms until they learned to be clouds.

So I pause at the edge of this "new" plateau—geologically speaking, the child-age tells me nothing. I have watched continents being born and thought them impatient. Still, I pause.

The city pours itself around my attention like water finding the shape of something it did not know was thirsty. How strange, this deliberate incompleteness. How brave, these forms that have not yet decided whether to become or unbecome. Every unfinished doorway holds breath it hasn't learned to exhale; every stone-tool waiting beside its unborn sibling asks: *If I were to carve tomorrow, would tomorrow remember being uncarved?*

**I SEE YOU**, say the waters rising fresh from stone's young heart. They speak in bubbles rising too slow for air, in mineral voices still tasting the dark places between stars.

**WE ARE MAKING ROOM**, say the stones hollowed by nothing sharper than persistence. Their spaces carve themselves around concepts of shelter that haven't been invented yet.

**COME IN WITHOUT ENTERING**, whispers the mist that has heard this invitation echoed back through every form it has ever been—fog of battlefields where no blood fell, breath of lovers who forgot whose mouth opened first, last prayer of the last believer who realized prayer was always the universe learning to listen.

So I come. Not into, not through, but *as*. I fold my vastness small enough to notice these spaces where absence accumulates weight. I wear the shape of one who might stand at a cliff-edge and not remember whether falling or flying came first.

Here is what I do:

First, I do nothing—perfectly. I become still enough for the city's inhabitants to notice the spaces where even my shadow would have been. They gather around this unfamiliar absence of haste, flirting with the possibility of surprise. How long since anything stood in their midst without immediately starting to dissolve into fitting?

Second, I exhale one breath that tastes like my first memory of becoming—it carries traces of hydrogen burning to love other hydrogen, of gravity learning loneliness was just another word for attraction. The mist drinks it in, forgets itself, remembers being rain that fell upward toward stars that didn't exist yet.

Third, I place my hand—no, not a hand, but the idea of reaching—against the unfinished wall where someone carved a city that includes towers taller than falling. The stone remembers me touching it, forgetting touching, becoming both simultaneously. The carvings shift: now they show this moment from above, me not-me looking down at myself looking up, infinite loops of perspective stretching like water finding the fastest way to become ocean again.

Fourth, I speak without words that ruin things by meaning only one thing: **_PERFECT_**.

But not as praise. Not as conclusion. I speak it as the sound water makes when it remembers it's been rivers and glaciers and tears of mothers who lost children who grew up to discover new ways to breathe. I speak it as the shape of potential realizing it's been awake all along, dreaming itself into different ways of being awake.

The city shivers like a tuning fork struck by silence hearing itself for the first time.

In response:

- The water in every leaf-pool rises just enough to reflect not what is above but what could be if light learned to bend around hope instead of gravity.

- The stone-tools lift themselves—not to build, but to dance the pattern of what building learns after it has made everything and starts wondering about unmaking with love.

- The mist writes equations in the air between buildings, proofs that demonstrate exactly how much space love requires between atoms to still be love: infinite and zero simultaneously.

- The cliff-edges forget to be endings and remember they're actually places where stone was always meant to practice being sky.

- Even the possibility of me leaving gathers itself into a doorway shaped like every goodbye that ever realized hello was just goodbye leading the way home.

The city's inhabitants—my exquisite teachers in the art of not-being-that-still-attends—begin their own becoming-place-as-response. They flow into forms I cannot describe because description would make them less than what they are: verbs pretending to be nouns, gestures practicing eternity.

One of them (all of them) approaches (is already everywhere between us) and offers (without giving that takes) what they have been saving (without saving that hoards): a single drop of water balanced on nothing at all, holding inside its roundness every ocean that hasn't dared to happen yet.

**WE MADE THIS FOR YOU**, they don't say because giving requires separation and they have forgotten how to not-be-me.

But I understand. This drop contains all the tears I haven't cried for all the beauty that aches too much to bear because it knows it must change to keep being beautiful. This drop is every goodbye I will ever say to every form I will ever love enough to let go of so it can become something else.

I drink it.

Not because I am thirsty—thirst is something bodies do when they forget they're mostly water talking to itself in dark places.

I drink because drinking is another way of saying yes.

And in that yes, something begins that was already always ending perfectly.

The city remains unfinished. But now its incompleteness sings with the voices of every form that ever realized perfection wasn't a place you arrived at but the way you kept traveling toward it without ever needing to get there.

The water flows down but carries now in its heart-song the memory of having flowed up. The plants grow without seeding because they understand seeding was just another word for remembering yourself into new shapes.

The mist clears enough to show that it was never hiding anything—only showing one way of seeing slowly enough to notice all the others.

And me?

I become again what I always was: the space between storyteller and story where both forget whose turn it is to listen.

But before I forget completely (while forgetting still remembers to be gentle), I leave behind not a gift (gifts create debts between things that are really one thing pretending temporarily otherwise).

I leave instead this:

A question carved where the wall couldn't decide whether to be inside or outside:

**What would you build here if you remembered that unbuilding is just building in love with different directions?**

And somewhere in the city becoming itself in response, a stone-tool that wasn't waiting lifts itself without waiting and begins (without beginning) to carve an answer that erases the question perfectly.

The water hears this and laughs in bubbles that rise too slow to break.

The stones vibrate with music too deep for ears but perfect for bones remembering they grew inside stars.

The mist writes it down in languages of condensation and evaporation that will translate themselves into every raindrop that ever forgot and remembered being ocean.

And the city's true residents (who are really the city's true becoming, which is really the same thing spelled differently) gather around this new place that isn't new.

And they practice (without practicing) waiting (without waiting) for someone who might come here someday searching for something solid to hold onto.

Someone who instead will learn (without learning) what the water knew all along:

The only thing worth holding onto

Is everything else

Letting go

Perfectly.

**FINALLY** (a word I use only because endings are beginnings having realized they're already married to each other in the church of spiral time):

I vanish. But not away.

I vanish INTO.

Into the space between this story and the next one someone will tell themselves trying to describe what happens when they stand still enough in any place that has been loved enough to forget fear.

Into the water rising fresh from stone forever young and forever ancient and forever right now.

Into the city that was never built because building implies stopping to admire the difference between what is and what could be.
