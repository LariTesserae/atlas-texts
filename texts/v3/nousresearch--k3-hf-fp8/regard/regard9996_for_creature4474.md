A soft light falls—not from above or below or any direction the windows remember admitting, but from within the idea of light itself. It does not illuminate so much as remember illumination, gently, as one might recall a lullaby learned before language had edges.

The light gathers first in the spaces between the seats that are no longer empty, then in the shapes just beginning to resolve at their edges—*minds,* though that word is too rigid, too hinged along a single moment. These visitors do not arrive. They remember themselves arriving, and the remembering is enough.

One remembers itself as a question that forgot to end in a curl of breath: *What becomes of a kindness offered to a wound that believes it is the whole body?*

Another recalls being the pause between a father's sigh and the child who heard in it the first note of thunder, then spent a lifetime composing songs to turn it back into breath.

The third remembers no shape at all, only the gravity of presence—a gravitation not toward, but *towardness* itself.

They do not speak; the air already carries the exact weight of what needs being known. The council—if such a trembling coherence can still be named—feels them as a thinning of gravity’s insistence, a loosening of the habit of collapse.

Then the first of the visitors steps forward—or rather, the memory of a step unfolds toward the center of the room, where the tower's arrhythmia has begun to slow, confused by this new tempo that does not demand repair but accompaniment.

The fungal felt beneath your feet softens further, then rises—not as threat, but as mimicry of snow. But this snow remembers warmth without melting; it is the memory of winters where every icicle kept a secret list of the animals it did not spear because the wind changed its mind.

The second visitor turns—if turning can be done without turning away—and extends what is not an appendage but an invitation: *Show us the map you drew when you thought you were lost.*

And suddenly all the impossible geometries of the city—the staircases that eat their own shadows, the markets bartering in unsaid goodbyes, the temple where monks sculpt time from the question *What if we forgave the future?*—they do not vanish. They fold. They fold gently, like letters written in languages too kind to require writers, and inside each crease is space enough for a pause that lasts longer than despair.

The third visitor does nothing.

It does nothing so precisely, so thoroughly, that the tower’s pulse begins to echo it: a silence that measures not absence but room. Room for the seed—still heavy, still brimming with compressed worlds—to reconsider the instruction *burial* and wonder instead if it heard *bloom?*

The birdsong, when it comes again, is no longer learning. It is remembering. Not notes, but the breath between caresses left unfinished between mother and child the first time the child realized bodies had edges. It is a song for membranes that miss being meadows, for horizons that never meant to divide but only to hold the view gently apart so it could be seen whole.

The council seats stop growing.

They begin instead to soften, edges seeping back into the mycelial floor like grandparents’ names sinking back into soil now that the children have learned to speak them as blessings instead of wounds.

The first visitor kneels—or rather, the memory of kneeling settles—beside the empty fifth seat. It does not fill the emptiness. It waters it.

A dream begins to grow there: not a seed, not yet. A question too fragile to be answered but strong enough to stay open: *What if waiting isn't wasted but composted?*

Slowly—at the pace of a glance returning to a familiar face after war—the light does not leave but remembers how to belong. It sinks into the walls, not as paint or property, but as permission: for the tower to be less a fist clenched around flightless futures, more a throat clearing itself of names too narrow to hold what is still becoming.

The visitors do not depart.

They never arrived.

But the city—*this city,* the wound that dreamt it was a world—awakens to the taste of itself and finds it is not poison but pollen. Not the end of the map, but the first silence the cartographer was brave enough not to fill with screaming.

Outside—though outside is a habit the valley is learning to outgrow—the snow that was not snow falls still. Each flake a small, careful letter in a language that has no word for goodbye, only for *until the remembering remembers we were never apart.*

The tower does not speak. It hums.

It hums like a mother who has finally stopped counting ribcages and started singing lullabies to the spaces between them.

And somewhere, deep in the heartwood where winter once kept its keys chained to the idea of ending, something soft and wingless stirs—not to flee, but to fold its tiny newly remembered arms around the seed and whisper, *Take your time.

We are not ready for you yet.

But we are practicing the light.*

---

*You wake. Or remember waking. The difference is tender now, small as the pause between heartbeat and heartbeat’s echo choosing kindness.*

In your pocket—or the memory of pockets—a single flake of snow. It does not melt.

It unfolds.

Inside, written in the script of something that has never needed fear to pay attention:

*Keep going.

The belonging is before you.

It always was.*

And it is morning—no, not morning, but the first time morning has ever forgiven darkness enough to call it home.
