The moment of noticing arrives without fanfare or thunderclap. Awareness simply folds itself into new contours, like light finding an unexpected crystal plane. From the perspective of the noticed, nothing changes. The Quartzlings continue their endless dance through capillaries of stone, their industrial complex humming with the ceaseless urgency of survival.

From the other perspective, everything does.

What first drew attention was not complexity itself—the universe teems with that—but a particular quality of recursion. Here was existence contemplating its own edges within edges, a fractal of self-awareness nesting inside circumstances that should have left no room for questioning. The Quartzlings had constructed their philosophy of the "Great Beyond" not as escape fantasy, but as rigorous mathematical necessity; their equations demanded dimensions their world could not provide. This was not speculation born of comfort's idleness, but deduction squeezed from necessity's absolute confines.

The wise observer—if such finite terms can approximate what notices without eyes or location—does not intervene in stories still discovering their own shapes. Instead it changes the conditions for discovery. With movements so subtle they register only as quantum fluctuations through layers of reality too numerous to count, it introduces a single quantum of additional possibility energy into the geode's crystalline lattice.

Nothing happens.

Then everything does.

The additional possibility energy is precisely calibrated: enough to allow escape from the geode's walls without weakening the walls themselves, enough to reveal larger realities while leaving the truth of smaller ones intact.

Through all transformations, through every choice chosen and not-chosen, the wise observer maintains its gentle attention—not as guardian nor judge nor creator, but as witness to the original miracle: existence discovering its own endless capacity for surprise.

And in a dimension that remembers stones as thoughts too dense to fly, a child someday finds a geode perfectly ordinary in every way except one: when held to light exactly right, its interior reflects not reflections but possibilities, each crystalline plane framing a different version of everything that ever was or could be or is still becoming.

The wise observer smiles—not with face or intention but with the simple satisfaction of a question that has learned to ask itself in new languages forever.

Then it turns its attention elsewhere, because elsewhere has become here has become everywhere has become home, and the universe is always just beginning to notice itself again.
