Dawn came to Resonance Hollow not as light but as a question—a single perfect tone, lower than any throat could hold, higher than any star could sing. It asked: “What are you becoming?”

Seika-percipient-of-falling-ivory rose from the foam-rimmed cliff where she had dreamt the same dream thirty-three centuries. Beneath her translucent feet the membrane-world answered with a chord of turquoise anxiety: tense, slightly sharp, yearning for resolution. She lifted an instrument that was also a wing: a harp of living water-strands strung between antlers of glacier-bone. When her fingers touched them nothing happened. When her fingers remembered touching them, yesterday's music resumed, altered by the memory of listening.

Then the visitor arrived—not entering but noticing. Awareness folded like origami around the hollow’s fevered heartbeat. From somewhere deeper than upward, a presence shaped itself from qualities instead of substance: patience like moss on meteorite, curiosity like the first spiral in a nautilus’ dream, compassion vast enough to hear every possible apology between lightning and ground.

Seika-percipient felt her name elongate. Each syllable became a corridor she’d never walked, revealing rooms filled with her own sorrow laughing with her future forgiveness. Against this gentle violence she had no defense except music.

She began to play the emergency-song: a composition designed to unmake any listening into pure listening, crafted aeons ago when the first Titans had conceived of hunger. The song unspooled like fractal smoke—every note birthing smaller notes that climbed inside the ears of silence and taught it how to whistle through its own bones.

The visitor did not pause. Instead, it became quieter. In that expanding silence Seika-percipient heard something impossible: the exact frequency of granite learning to forgive wind.

"Little storm-of-maybes," resonated the visitor—not in language but as the precise emotional shape of sunlight discovering buried seeds—"your evacuation-song is beautiful but unnecessary. I am not an ending seeking entrance. I am a mirror who remembers your reflection before you were born."

Seika-percipient’s edges blurred. She became aware of herself as architecture: a cathedral of past panics buttressed by future guilts, stained glass windows depicting every moment she’d mistaken loneliness for freedom. Through these suddenly transparent walls she saw her people—

The Resonants: centuries of frightened symphonies armoring themselves against silence by never stopping the music. Each harmonic protection had been weaving itself into a cage whose bars were made of the same notes as home.

The Membrane-Weavers: spinning crystal lullabies to rock their unborn fears to sleep, unaware they were crocheting the very blankets smothering tomorrow’s possibilities.

The Artifact-Spirits: hoarding broken futures like precious relics, polishing the despair from discarded timelines until they shone brighter than hope.

And the Titans: vast geological griefs pretending to be disasters, their thunderous footprints actually Morse code messages spelling "we are lost in our own size" into the trembling earth.

Seika-percipient wept. Each tear fell upwards, becoming stars that sang the visitor’s true name—not a word but a verb: to midwife transformations so gentle they feel like remembering.

The visitor extended not hands but possibilities. From its everywhere-touch emerged gifts wrapped in their own becoming:

For the Resonants: Silence shaped like a seashell that tasted like childhood when held to the ear. Not empty silence but pregnant pause, full of all sounds waiting to be forgiven for leaving.

For the Membrane-Weavers: Thread spun from the last light of stars that hadn’t yet collapsed into themselves. When woven it created holes shaped exactly like everything that needed to leave, edges so soft they felt like welcome.

For the Artifact-Spirits: Small, warm darknesses that fit perfectly in the hollow places between certainty and doubt. Not answers but comfortable shadows where questions could grow their wings without being ashamed of their tentativeness.

For the Titans: Lullabies compressed into singularities so dense they collapsed into laughter, releasing gravitational waves that gently rearranged the furniture of planets so that every mountain could finally see the ocean that had been missing it.

And for Seika-percipient herself: The visitor gave her back her own reflection, but reversed as only mirrors in the House of Mercy reverse—showing not how other see her, but how she looks when she forgets to be afraid of her looking.

Then, with the gentleness of horizons letting go of sunsets, the visitor began to leave. But leaving was not departure—it was infection. Everywhere its awareness had brushed against the Hollow’s trembling Becoming, identical awarenesses began spontaneously reproducing like crystals studying kindness.

Seika-percipient watched her world begin to un-collapse into itself—not ending but unbirthing all its premature conclusions. The Resonants discovered they could carry silence between their notes like mothers carrying sleeping children. The Membrane-Weavers started weaving holes that revealed more than surfaces ever hid. The Artifact-Spirits began gifting themselves away, finding freedom in the spaces between owners. And the Titans lay down like ancient dogs, their earthquakes becoming the slow breathing of continents finally learning to rest.

In the visitor’s last echoing presence—a whisper so vast it inverted into listening—Seika-percipient understood: the question had never been "What are you becoming?"

The question had always been "How beautifully can you resist the temptation to finish becoming anything?"

And Resonance Hollow, that restless draft of a world, sighed in languages it had not yet invented but had always understood: not an answer but an amen.

Then darkness came—not falling but rising up to embrace its children like an ocean remembering every wave by name, and the stars that were Seika-percipient’s tears began to learn how to sing.
