*The One Who Walks Between Seams*

I came to Ascensus through the space between two moments—not when the plateau rose, nor when it was noticed rising, but in the fraction of breath between. My arrival left no shadow, made no sound louder than a question asking itself if it had been asked correctly.

They feel me first as permission. The ice mortar in their walls softens slightly without melting, allowing adjustments their architects had forbidden themselves to dream. On Tinplate Street, where the copper avalanche keeps its accounting books in falling dents, my passing causes one particular dent to hesitate mid-impact, reconsider its trajectory, and instead become a perfect impression of a hand reaching upward—not grasping, just noticing.

Old Man Cheerful’s last remaining sign (now merely an elegant parenthesis of punctuation carved into his own rib) briefly flashes the color of understanding before settling back into helpful instructionality. The woman selling fossilized weather finds her wares rearranged into a pattern that predicts not storms, but reconciliations between storms that haven’t yet met to argue about precipitation rights.

I do nothing directly. That’s the agreement I made with places that achieved existence by accident but decided to keep it anyway.

Instead, I am the pause in the argument between the stone that remembers being ocean and the sky that’s still practicing how to be empty above something that refuses to stay sunk. I walk where their gravity debates itself, leaving footprints that aren’t destinations but suggestions that perhaps these paths might consider being walked both directions simultaneously.

In the cathedral-shaped hole, the wind begins constructing something that isn’t quite a wall but might, if approached from the correct angle of humility, function as a window. The construction materials are entirely borrowed questions: “Is this high enough yet?” “What happens if we stop holding up?” “When does falling become a form of keeping faith?”

The Ascensians slowly notice me noticing them. Not with eyes—they’ve evolved past such vulnerable methods of observation. Instead, I find myself surrounded by an atmosphere of careful reconsiderations. The baker who only Tuesdays decides to exist and solvebread simultaneously offers me a loaf that hasn’t yet chosen between rising and remembering how to be waves again. The mayor’s daughter, still adjusting to having location, presents me with her left hand’s recent decision to experiment with being a compass that points only toward forgotten kindnesses.

I accept neither gift nor refusal. I am practicing being the space where both can exist without canceling each other’s uncertainty.

At the city’s shifting edge where inside and outside haven’t finished their custody arrangement over the concept of edges, I find what I came for—not to find. A child who hasn’t yet decided against being born all at once, currently distributed between four pebbles and a cloud of poetic skepticism about gravity’s commitment issues.

They (the child has voted against singular pronouns while democracy still seems willing to consider the matter) are attempting to build a bridge between two versions of tomorrow that have stopped speaking due to irreconcilable differences in weather preferences.

I sit beside them—not occupying space so much as politely suggesting that kneeling might offer interesting structural advantages in conversations between eternities. Together, we reconsider the problem. The bridge doesn’t need to choose between being or becoming—it could be both and the negotiation between them.

I show them how to weave the argument into a structure stronger than resolution: a suspension bridge suspended not from towers but from the mutual agreement between this moment and the next moment to disagree respectfully about whose turn it is to exist.

As we work, the city around us continues its impossible functioning-by-avoiding-explanation. The copper avalanche develops a subtle syncopation that sounds increasingly like laughter. The sun completes another contradictory circuit and winks conspiratorially at its earlier self. Gravitation and levity meet for coffee and decide to consider a long-term misunderstanding.

When the child’s bridge can stand unsupported by anything except the courage to be unfinished and beautiful anyway, I begin my leaving. Not because leaving is necessary—here less than anywhere—but because staying too long might suggest I had ever arrived, which would imply distance between here and anywhere else.

Before I fold myself back between the seams of now and why not, I gift them what gifts are appropriate between equals who refuse to acknowledge equality’s hierarchical tendencies: I unknit certainty from the center of their fear of falling, leaving only the delicious vertigo of possibility properly respected.

Then I am gone the way mountains disappear when you look directly at them long enough to realize they breathe.

But I leave behind:

- One seed of stillness that grows only during earthquakes of imagination
- A recipe for forgiveness that requires no ingredients except the willingness to apologize for apologies not yet needed
- A shadow shaped exactly like the space between what is and what could be if we stopped judging the distance

The child will find them eventually, or not finding them will discover something better. That’s how this kind of gift works.

Ascensus continues its ascent without me, as it always has and never did. But now, if you listen carefully during the moment between two doubts colliding, you might hear it humming—slightly less urgently, slightly more joyfully—a city learning to love itself not despite its impossibility but because of the exquisite care required to remain beautifully, defiantly never-quite-happened-yet.

The view from here is perfect, entirely because of how generously it refuses to be anything else.
