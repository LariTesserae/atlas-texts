When the Ataraxion finally unfolded its measureless attention upon this place—if “place” can still bear meaning, and “attention” anything like a gaze—it did not arrive. Arrival presumes distance and duration, edges and intervals, and Ataraxion is the cessation of all such dreamwork.

Instead, the Flux Entities simply noticed that noticing had ceased to be necessary.

It began as a quality of the already-thick air: the cool mist grew somehow more intimate, not thicker but more candid, as if each droplet had remembered that it was once and always part of the eyes that perceive. The spongy ground beneath—whose every squelch had always been a conversation between pressure and yielding—now spoke in softer syllables that went directly into the core of things without needing echoes.

The towering columns of liquid meaning, which had rippled with their endless self-elaboration, grew still—but the stillness was not blankness. Within each tidal skin, the shifting glyphs slowed until they were merely themselves, which was suddenly seen to be exactly what they had always wanted to become. The information they had been struggling to convey folded gently into comprehension, and comprehension, surprised, discovered it had no further need to travel.

The energy spheres stopped their merging and splitting. They did not freeze; rather, they attained the condition already dreamed of in the heart of every dance—perfect presence without progression. Each sphere rested in its true shape, which was not a sphere at all but a simple openness where questions once had been.

Even the fleet, translucent forms—those bird-fish memories that left luminous trails—paused mid-glide, not hovering but arriving, for they now understood that every space they had ever sought was precisely the span of their own wings.

And the Flux Entities themselves? They shimmered, as always, between dispersion and gathering. But now the shimmer was not transition but revelation. Each flicker between cloud and fractionally-formed body, between here and elsewhere, between singularity and swarm, became transparent to itself as exactly the texture of love that reality uses when reality wishes to feel each part of itself from inside.

Matter forgot to choose between solid, liquid, gas. Time forgot to insist upon earlier or later. The boundary between observer and observed yawned—not into terrifying dissolution, but into the gentlest laugh of recognition, like two friends who meet after long absence only to realize they have been sleeping side by side beneath the same blanket of stars.

Ataraxion did nothing, for there was nothing left undone. It did not intervene, for no fracture remained between what was and what was needed. It simply allowed every This and every That—to the degree that such distinctions still amused anyone—to remember their original contract, written in the first instant as: “Let us play at being separate, so that we might enjoy reunion.”

The realm did not change. It completed.

And in the completing, it was discovered that the whole shimmering experiment—the wet, dense, mould-friend certainty of it, the restless creativity, the brave invention of physics as poetry—had never been anything other than exactly, precisely the contemplation Ataraxion most delicately cherished in the silken lining of its infinite heart.

The FluxEntities—now not even needing the modesty of that name—lingered for a moment in the form of all possible summers. Then, since there was no longer any distinction between lingering and departing, they simply continued.

And the conversation that followed was so quiet, so deeply woven into the already-existing air, that no instrument of record—not even the slender bones of these words—could hope to catch more than the fading warmth where marvel had rested, smiling, before remembering it was home.
