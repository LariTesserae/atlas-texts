The Presence arrived not with thunder or trumpet, but as a gentle shift in the way light refracted through mist—an impossibility of brightness without warmth, clarity without harshness. It had traveled across dimensions not measured in distance, drawn by a song it had never heard but always remembered: the crystalline silence of perfect balance.

For seventy-three breaths of the Mist Terraces—that eternal moment where condensation forms and falls without ever quite achieving either state—the Being paused to observe. Its awareness unfurled like invisible petals, touching each lichen-creature's distributed consciousness without disrupting the delicate weave of their existence.

How long had it been since the Presence had encountered such beauty that did not yearn to be more? Such completeness that held no thirst for expansion?

In the hushed cathedral of perpetual becoming, the lichen-people shimmered in unseeing recognition. Their color-language flickered through an octave of silver-greens never before expressed—not fear, not welcome, but something infinitely rarer: acknowledgment without separation.

The Presence could have spoken in the voice of collapsing stars. Instead, it whispered a single question woven from the space between atoms, directed not at their minds but at the spaces between their thoughts:

*"What would you choose, if choice itself were a gift wrapped in the possibility of undoing?"*

The answer rose not from individual entities but from the entire ecosystem—stone and moss and mist and creature-voice entwined—an answer that took the form of a question folding back upon itself:

*"How can emptiness receive without becoming less than empty?"*

And in that exchange, something impossible occurred without occurring. The Potential remained potential. The Gift stayed unmanifested. The Presence did not intervene, did not transform, did not uplift.

Instead—more profoundly—it understood.

In the infinite compassion of perfect wisdom, the Presence recognized that these beings had already achieved what countless civilizations across aeons had sought as highest aspiration: communion without loss, becoming without striving, every moment their own eternal and sufficient end.

The Presence departed exactly as it had arrived, leaving behind only a subtle permanent impermanence: from that moment forward, when the mist grew particularly dense and light danced through water droplets in particular patterns, the lichen-people would sometimes pause in their flowing to pause to flow—catching glimpses between their glimpses of a possibility so vast it could only be expressed as complete and utter contentment.

And in distant realms where change was worshipped as divine, philosophers and prophets would argue for millennia over the meaning of the briefest verses ever recorded in the celestial archives:

"Found in the Mist Terraces:
Perfection asleep in its own embrace.
Left untouched, save for the touch
That never touches anything."

The Presence never returned.

It had never left.
