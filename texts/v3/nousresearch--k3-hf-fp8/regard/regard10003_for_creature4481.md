The first thing the power notices is not the sound—though it registers the sound as a kind of grief too old for trembling, a lament compressed into vibration until it became geography. It has heard such things before. There are worlds that forgot to stop screaming when their stars went out, and those screams calcified into mountain ranges later named for forgiveness by species who never learned what was forgiven. But this place is different.

This place is answering.

The power does not arrive. Arrival presumes a before, an after, a trajectory curled around some center. Instead, it notices. Noticing here is a transitive verb, a tender violence. Noticing is how gravity learns to love, how light remembers it was once a prayer.

It notices the sound is circular but not repetitive. It is a question being asked by the act of being unasked—a mother tongue dissolving back into the mouths that first shaped it. The power listens until listening becomes indistinguishable from bleeding sympathy into the shape of silence shaped into soil.

Then it notices the stair.

The stair is not architecture. Architecture is a promise standing upright long enough to forget it was once a promise. This is something else: a conversation between falling and forgetting to hit the ground. Each step is a thesis abandoned mid-sentence by philosophers who realized the cliff was not metaphorical. The power climbs without moving by remembering what it was to be climbable.

Halfway up—or down, distinctions are currently filing for emancipation from the nouns once used to restrain them—the power encounters the first of its own abandoned names. The syllable is moss-green, syllabic as rain, and it tastes like the moment between a heart breaking and realizing breakage was always the point. The power picks it up. The syllable unravels into fingers. They clasp each other’s absence like old friends who recognize each other only by the shape of the wounds they left behind.

"We were trying to write a letter," the syllables explain, "to the version of ourselves that might still believe in recipients."

The power nods. Nodding here is a form of resurrection—acknowledging something back into existence without the trauma of definition.

They continue together. The stair does not so much support their weight as it forgets to object to gravity’s long-standing objections to coherence. At intervals they pass doors standing politely in what might once have been walls. One door is made entirely of harmonica sorrow played backwards through a throat of burnt honey. Another is the sound of a child learning the word for "gone" in a language where "gone" is conjugated differently depending on whether the absence weighs more than the memory of bread. Each door opens onto the same beach where the ocean is busy apologizing for inventing shore.

The power does not enter. Entering would imply a way out, and exits here are currently enrolled in witness protection programs under assumed identities as entrances experiencing existential dread.

Instead, they notice the flowers.

The flowers are not biological. Biology is a bedtime story matter tells itself when it gets scared of being infinite. These are something earlier: griefs that took up photosynthesis to survive the darkness before language. Their petals are pages torn from unfinished treaties between gods and the void. Their pollen is the color of the first time something realized it was lonely.

When the power exhales—not air but the memory of having lungs—the flowers turn their faces toward the sound, photosynthesizing compassion into a fragrance that smells exactly like forgiveness discovering it was never necessary in the first place.

At the top—or center, or inside these days the prepositions are all attending couples therapy with the concept of direction—there is no chamber. There is instead a breath being held collectively by everything that ever died waiting. The breath is shaped like the space between what was promised and what was actually received. It hums with the patience of unanswered prayers finally unionizing.

Here the power stops. Stopping is its own kind of movement here—like how silence is just sound remembering it never needed to prove anything.

It notices the steward.

The steward is not an octopus. Octopus is what happens when fluid intelligence decides to wear eight questions as jewelry. The steward is the question the sea asks when it wakes up night after night still not drowned. Her robes are the weather’s diary entries from all the times it tried to run away from home but ended up embracing the horizon instead. Her eyes are the color of music explaining color to someone born in darkness who nevertheless once dreamed in sonnets.

She does not bow. Bowing presumes hierarchy, and hierarchy here is currently busy applying for refugee status from a universe that discovered burning bushes were always just desperate metaphors.

Instead, she offers the power a cup.

The cup is not empty. Empty is what happens when presence goes to the bathroom without telling absence it’ll be right back. The cup contains a drop of tomorrow’s yesterday: the exact moment this entire world realized it was not dying but was instead graduating from the need to pretend to live.

The power drinks.

Tastes like: the first time light learned it was not fast, just impatient. Tastes like: the last time darkness believed it needed to apologize for being the place where stars learn to stay hidden long enough to remember why they started burning. Tastes like: home finding the letter you wrote when you were six explaining why you ran away to join the concept of infinity and leaving it unopened until you were old enough to read between the lines: "Dear Self, I forgive us for not waiting up." 

When the power lowers the cup—though lowering implies a before-higher which these coordinates politely decline to recognize—it notices that everything around it has become simultaneously closer and further away, like love discovering distance was just a shy way of practicing intimacy.

Then it does the only thing a truly wise benevolence can do in a place where salvation has been replaced with communal acts of remembering how to need each other without ownership.

It forgets itself.

Not entirely. Not cruelly. Just enough.

Enough to remember that forgetting is the first form of forgiveness taught in the language where "I" and "we" are conjugations of the same verb depending on how deeply you’re willing to inhale.

The power forgets its name until its name becomes a shape the stair can use to rest its tired metaphors.

It forgets its purpose until purpose realizes it was just purpose’s way of growing into something that could hold hands with meaninglessness without trying to fix it.

It forgets to leave until leaving blushes into staying like a river remembering it was always the ocean’s way of practicing being everywhere at once without getting lonely.

And in the space where the power’s forgetting touches this world’s remembering, something new—something ancient—something exactly like kindness but without the ego tax—begins to grow.

It starts as a sound.

But not the same sound.

This one is softer.

It has shoulders.

It knows how to carry things without making them finish becoming whatever they thought they had to become to stop being heavy.

It sounds like:

Shhh.

Not to silence.

But to cradle the screaming until the screaming remembers it was always just singing with a throat full of shrapnel that finally learned how to rust into bells.

The power—no longer quite powerful, no longer quite apart—sits down beside the stair.

It does not wait. Waiting implies time is late to its own appointment.

It attends.

It is still attending when the next traveler arrives, carrying under one arm the ashes of a map and in the other hand a question that has already begun answering itself in languages made entirely of the spaces between heartbeats.

The power—which is now mostly just presence wearing kindness’ old coat—offers them the cup.

It is not empty.

It never was.

They drink together.

The taste is familiar.

It tastes exactly like beginning.

Except quieter.

And infinite.

And already forgiven. 

And somewhere—

very soft inside the deafening roar

that has always been the planet’s way

of lullabying its own erosion into lullaby—

a door opens.

Not to somewhere else.

But to here.

Finally.

Here.

Here, but with more room

for everything

to be

still

becoming.
