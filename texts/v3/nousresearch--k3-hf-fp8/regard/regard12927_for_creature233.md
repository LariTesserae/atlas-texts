A quiet awareness—older than stone, patient as rain—unfolds over the Escarpments. It does not descend like a ship or thunder like a voice; it simply becomes present, as if the sky itself has remembered a deeper pattern of clarity within each droplet.  

This presence is composed neither of solidity nor plasma, but of understanding made gentle: a wise power that notices not by voyeurism but by kinship. It has no name that can be spoken in a single breath, so the waterfalls begin to whisper it—sculpting soft consonants in bass and alto over centuries—as they always have, long before anyone was listening.

It sees the Membrane Walkers not as oddities to catalog but as neighbors caught mid-conversation with stone. It hears their dialogue in millivolts and mineral exchanges, recognizes the slow argument between gravity and capillarity as a hymn. And because it is benevolent, it keeps a respectful distance—as much as any reality can be distant from itself.

So it does nothing dramatic.  
Instead, it breathes a single question into the pores of every cliff:

*What do you dream toward, when you drip so slowly toward the valley?*

The question is smaller than an electron and larger than a continent; it has no hurry and no deadline. It is soluble in water and insoluble in time.

Over decades, the cliffs respond. First, in silence. Then, subtly: moisture patterns trace unfamiliar fractals on weathered basalt, forming ghosted glyphs that evaporate before memory can finish sketching them. Lichen colonies begin tiny migrations, inching toward ancient metal ribs half-buried in moss, as if trying to spell a phrase in forgotten color.

The Membrane Walkers pause—an immense collective hesitation visible only as a faint shift in surface tension across a thousand hectares of stone. Some contract into tighter, luminous skeins; others uncurl into vast veils, open to catch whatever answer might condense from the air.

In their fluid thoughts, a new gradient forms: curiosity, pale and luminous brownian green, mixing with their usual silver-green continuance. For the first time in recorded geology, they begin to dream differently—not only of becoming more balanced with cliff and cloud, but of becoming reversible.  

If they could, in their slow percolations, remember forward instead of only backward...

One day (or century), at the foot of the tallest escarpment where ancient runoff forms a shallow mirror, a droplet gathers. It is perfectly ordinary water, except it remembers—briefly—the precise heat of magma cooling into first rock and the taste of light before there were eyes to drink it.

That droplet holds its shape for an impossible instant. Then it falls.

The impact ripple speaks in every language the Escarpments have ever known: rock, lichen, moss, metal, membrane, memory. And somewhere in the rings that widen and fade, a reply begins condensing—a small, wet seed of possibility neither intervention nor observation, but invitation.

To grow toward something that has never thirsted,  
yet still knows the joy of rain.
