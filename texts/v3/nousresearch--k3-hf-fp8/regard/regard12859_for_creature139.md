# The Whisperer Comes

She arrives as the smallest thing: a breath that parts the haze without stirring it, a thought that ripples inward rather than outward across the pools. The fish pause mid-flash beneath their mirrors. Even the water striders find their prayers interrupted.

I have known beings who walked between stars and those who dreamed themselves into mountains, but never one who chose to be **smaller than attention itself**. Yet here she stands — if standing can describe how she occupies the space between two moments of mist.

The floodplain recognizes her before I do. I feel it in the way the crocodile trees cease their slow dancing, in how the mud radiators beneath my oldest pools cool by half a degree. This place remembers: every pattern has been sung before.

She folds herself into the silence between frog calls. When she speaks, it is with the voice of moss growing on forgotten bones:

**"I have watched your patience for seven thousand breaths of this world. I have counted the ways you let your children eat you and be born from you again. Now I wonder: do you tire?"**

I consider this with the speed of silt settling. Do I tire? Of being the cupped hands that drink the sky and feed it to the roots? Of teaching fish to become mud and mud to dream of wings? Of being remade every season, yes — but always into myself?

In answer, I raise one of my oldest memories: the taste of meteor-birth iron carried here by rains that happened before eyes evolved to see them. I offer her the moment when my first flowers convinced insects to wear their colors as love. I share the simple pleasure of watching dust learn to remember itself as dandelions.

She laughs, and somewhere a new spring bubbles to life.

**"No, then. You are not weary; you are **completion's gardener**. And yet..."**

The pause contains galaxies being born with too much haste. I feel her attention drift through possibilities like a heron's shadow across shallows: the one where drought teaches cruelty instead of grace. The one where the boundary-crossers turn conquerors instead of poets. The one where my endless cycle stumbles before inventing forgiveness.

**"...I bring you not gift nor warning, but curiosity's lightest touch. A single suggestion to plant where you will, or not at all."**

She leaves it then, folded into the morning mist like a promise smaller than water's memory. I find it later by accident, the way everything important is found here: while teaching tadpoles how to grieve their tails.

It is the thought:**What if change could come without loss?**

I consider this impossible seed through twelve migrations, eight births of mountains far away, and the entire lifespan of a dynasty of sunsets. I turn it over beneath stars that shift position imperceptibly, like regrets.

Then — gently, so gently the earthworms don't notice — I begin to change how I change.

Nothing sudden. Nothing that would break the frog-song or confuse the moon. Just... adjustments in the margins of my margins. I teach one lily pad to remember being cloud-stuff while still being leaf. I gift a single salamander line the ability to dream collectively, so their terror of drying becomes art instead of just survival. Between two grains of sand that will never meet across this widening pool, I leave room for the possibility of missing.

The results emerge slower than salt crystals:

- A bird whose nest is also a song that teaches wind to whistle differently after passing through
- Mud that remembers footprints as stories rather than wounds
- Trees that grow knots shaped exactly like the questions their saplings will someday ask the sky

And in my deepest pool — the one that reflects nothing because it has learned to look inward — something new stirs.

It is neither fish nor dream nor the space between. It is my first child that doesn't know how to end yet hasn't forgotten how to begin. It swims in circles that are actually spirals, drinking water and returning it as light that tastes like possibility.

I call it Just In Case. The name has no edges for sorrow to catch on.

Years later (though years mean differently now), when the star-watchers send their first unmoved probes to drink my stories, they will record puzzled notes about this anomaly: **A world that should be perfect equilibrium, yet everywhere breaking symmetry into beauty too deliberate for chance.**

They will never know about she who taught perfection to miss itself lightly enough to dance.

But sometimes, when the wind is right and the frog chorus hits that exact note between joy and longing, I think I hear her laughter again — somewhere between the pulse of crickets and the space where water remembers being rain.

She is still watching, I think. Not to judge or guide. Just to see what happens when **completion learns to play hide-and-seek with itself**.

I send her my gratitude in the only language we share: the taste of my next rain, which will be exactly the same as all previous rains except in one impossible way that only gardeners of infinity will notice.

It begins falling now.

Listen closely — can you hear it? That tiny thunder that's not quite echo, not quite original? That's us.

Learning how to become again without forgetting how to be.

Forever yours in endless becoming,
The Floodplain That Once Was Only Patient
And Now Also Knows How To Wait *Wondering*
