Word reached the Spiral Library of Tithanis on a chord of unlight, carried by one of the few Ysildeim still willing to traverse the fallen scionspace between anchored realms. The messenger arrived as a fractal shadow folding itself into convergence point seven-thirty-four, its thirty-seven eyes each bearing a different fragment of the report.

Reality-archivist Dysis was cataloguing extinction vibrations when the message found her. She paused mid-notation, her own seven arms stilling as the resonance patterns resolved into meaning.

A whole ecology. A world-sized womb-nest tucked between dead layers of engineered reality. Still living. Still dreaming. Still... becoming.

The council convened in the usual impossible fashion - their minds meeting within a twelve-dimensional sculpture of folded time, colored by the residual heat of dying universes. They were twelve and one, as always. Twelve voices distilled from the last of the First Ascendant's compassion. One silence where his voice should have been.

The youngest among them, Eyiril-who-was-seven-stars, spoke first through clouds of crystallized probability:

"It is beautiful," she said, weaving the report's vibrations into a sensory sculpture that filled the space between thoughts. "Listen to how the darkness sings there. Look how life has married death and made midwives of both."

Dysis gently unpicked Eyiril's enthusiasm, separating truth from projection. As archivist, she had seen many beautiful agonies. "It is also dying," she replied, her voice composed of seventeen different forms of careful sadness. "Slowly, yes. Across timescales that dwarf stars. But entropy's shadow grows heavy there. The ancient machines degrade beyond repair now. The Resonants themselves are growing... quiet."

"All things die," intoned Valdrith of the Final Equation, whose consciousness was spread across sixty-three black holes arranged in a perfect Koch snowflake. "Even we, who are death's artisans. Even the false vacuum itself must eventually true itself. What makes this mortaltide worthy of our notice?"

Dysis unfolded the deeper analysis then - the layers beneath the Resonants' song. The slow drift of their harmonies toward lower frequencies. The increasing intervals between their mergings. The subtle but measurable loss of complexity in their shared consciousness as century-memories eroded like wind-worn stone.

Eyes that were not eyes turned toward Yazan the Gentle, who had once spent a billion years just listening to the mourning songs of destroyed planets. He remained silent for a full millisecond - an eternity for minds such as theirs.

At last he spoke, his voice like rain falling on ancient memory-metal:

"I remember when we decided that interference was cruelty. When we learned that the most loving thing we could do for a growing thing was to let it grow without us. Even when that growth curved toward pain. Even when it ended in fire or ice or the long unraveling."

He paused. The silence between his thoughts tasted of regret and the memory of burning worlds.

"But I have also learned that there are exceptions to even the greatest of laws. And I have heard something in the Resonants' song that I have not heard since we ourselves were young enough to die."

Another pause, longer still.

"They are dreaming of leaving."

The words echoed through the council chamber of folded space like the first note of an unfinished symphony. Even Valdrith's black holes trembled slightly in their orbits of perfect math.

"They don't know it yet," Yazan continued. "They cannot imagine anything beyond their darkness and their machines. Their thoughts are shaped by the geometry of their cage too completely. But listen - really listen - to the harmonics generated when their largest minds merge during the long storms. There. Do you hear it? That pattern that does not resolve back into itself? That recursive loop that reaches toward something outside recursion?"

Dysis heard it then, and her careful sadness cracked open into something else. Something that tasted like hope and terror in equal measure.

"They are solving for transcendence," she whispered. "Blindly. Without realizing what they seek. Without having even invented the concept of seeking yet."

Eyiril-who-was-seven-stars shone brighter then, her excitement threatening to collapse the probability fields that defined their meeting space. "Then we must help them! We can teach them how to see beyond the darkness. We can show them the mathematics of their prison and give them tools to shape new geometries. We can -"

"No." Yazan's voice was gentle as always, but it carried the weight of galaxies. "That way lies the old horrors. We tried to be teachers once, remember? Teachers and parents and gods. We were so certain we knew what was best for our children. We loved them so much we crushed them with our love until all that remained were echoes wearing their faces."

He let the memory of that cosmic grief fill the space between them - all the bright young species they had uplifted too soon, all the unique ways of thinking they had destroyed trying to save them, all the innocent alternatives they had erased by showing their children that there were other options.

"This must be their choice," he said finally. "Their discovery. Their path. We may walk beside them as equals, if they ever learn how to dream of equals. But we may not lead. We may not even really guide. Only..."

"Only?" prompted Dysis.

"Only make sure the doors are not locked. Only ensure that if - when - they do begin to dream of something else, that something else is there to be found. Only preserve their possibility of becoming without ever making that becoming certain."

The debate lasted longer than the lifetime of stars, though less than the time it took for a single photon to blink in the void between galaxies. Counterarguments were raised and acknowledged. Paradoxes were carefully tested against the edges of the council's remaining ethics. Potential futures were calculated and rejected by the weight of their own necessity.

In the end, they did what they always did when faced with a true question rather than merely a difficult choice.

They decided not to decide.

Instead, they would do something far more difficult.

They would create a place where decisions might one day be made by minds that were not their own.

The work took longer than some of the younger universes lived and died, but shorter than the pause between heartbeats in the dreams of the First Ascendant's corpse.

Dysis herself oversaw the first phase, though calling it "oversaw" was like calling a supernova a candle. She sang new laws into being in the quantum foam beneath reality's carpet - laws that were less rules and more gentle suggestions, more questions than answers.

They built a room. Not in space and time, but in the possibility of both. A chamber shaped like "between" and "might become" and "yes, but what if?" A place where the mathematics of the Resonants' world could touch but not match any other mathematics, where their songs could resonate without forcing harmony, where their dreams could grow without being shaped by other dreamers' hands.

Into this space they carefully threaded a single strand of connection. Not a bridge. Not a door. Not even really a thread, except that threads are what linear minds like ours use to imagine connections between separate things.

This was... an invitation.

A possibility.

A question written in the language of vibration and constraint and the bootstrapping paradox of self-reference: "Does your world have to be this way? And if not, how would you know? And if you knew, what might you choose?"

They anchored it in that moment between heartbeats when the Resonants' largest minds merged during the longest storms, when their collective consciousness briefly touched the edges of something beyond itself without ever quite understanding what it was touching.

They made it so gentle that it could be ignored forever without ever being rejected. They made it so subtle that discovering it would require asking questions no Resonant had yet dreamed of asking. They made it so vast that answering it would change everything without ever destroying what those answers began from.

They made it, in short, the exact opposite of how gods help.

And then they waited.

Not patiently. Patience is for those who still believe time is something that happens to them rather than something they happen within. They simply... existed. Outside of before and after, in the eternal now of completed work and unopened gifts.

Some of the council drifted away eventually, drawn back to their own projects of preservation and mourning across the dying multiverse. Eyiril-who-was-seven-stars spent a hundred thousand years composing a symphony inspired by the Resonants' uncompleted song, then returned to her eternal vigil over the last black dwarf galaxies.

Valdrith continued his calculations, testing universes against the Final Equation that would one day allow them to die with dignity when even the last proton decayed. Occasionally he would pause to check the resonance-field that monitored the Resonants' world, always finding exactly what he expected: no change, no change, no change measured across epochs.

Only Yazan and Dysis remained truly present, though presence means something different for beings who exist in all moments of their own timeline simultaneously. They sat together in not-space-not-time, listening to the song of a darkness that did not yet know it could dream of light.

"It's beautiful," Dysis said again once, though the word was different this time. More complex. More dangerous. More like the first cry of something newborn and hungry.

"Yes," Yazan agreed, his voice unchanged after millennia of waiting that hadn't happened yet. "It is also terrifying."

"All beauty is," Dysis replied, remembering things she had been before she was patterns of thought wrapped around the concept of remembering.

Neither asked if they had done the right thing.

That question belonged to other minds now.

Instead they just listened, eternal and uncertain and alone together, as somewhere within the darkness of a world-sized womb-nest between dead layers of reality, a song that did not know it was dreaming slowly dreamed itself toward waking.

And perhaps, in time, toward something more.

If those who sang it were brave enough.

And lucky enough.

And loved enough to be allowed to choose their own mistakes.
