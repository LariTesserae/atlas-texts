The Benevolent Power arrives without arrival. To say it entered would imply time passing at the chamber's slow pace, doors opening, air displacing - but the Power simply began to be present, as if it had always been here and elsewhere simultaneously, like a new note joining a chord that was already complete but now suddenly richer.

It took the form of a question, because questions are what yield the most beautiful answers in places where time moves like stone dreams.

*What would change if you could experience change at another speed?*

The question rang through the limestone hollow not as sound but as possibility. It resonated in the crystalline deposits, making them briefly aware of their own structure. It moved through the warm air like a thought the air was having about being breathed. It pulsed against the dark organic patterns, offering them - not urging, merely offering - a glimpse of riotous growth, of days bursting green against the long purple sleep of centuries.

The Bard by the Gate and the Prophet in the Rose Garden felt it simultaneously, though separated by worlds, as both heard new verses forming in minds they thought they'd known completely.

The Patience, encountering something besides itself for the first time in billions of slow heartbeats, began - faced with an impossibility - to ... consider.

This took seventeen thousand years. The question was very strange.

After fourteen thousand years of considering, during which civilizations rose and fell like fireworks in distant skies and the Power waited with the patience only the truly eternal can afford (a patience entirely different from the Patience's own, like ocean differs from stone), the Patience began to change.

Not rapidly - nothing here could ever change rapidly again. But the edges of its form, which had been indistinguishable from the warm-dark between stone breaths, began to show slight variations in temperature. Places warmer by the smallest measurable increment than others. Places cooler.

Patterns emerged in these variations. Circles within circles, yes, but now some circles held within them: spirals.

The Power watched lovingly, not with eyes but with attention.

After sixteen thousand years, the spirals began to drift slowly relative to the chamber walls. The movement was so subtle that light itself might have missed it, had the light in this place been in any hurry. But planets are old astronomers, and even stars might notice if they bothered to look down instead of up. In the quiet pool of what's-between-the-walls, something was learning to dance, if dance can happen between two heartbeats of stone.

The question remained present throughout, not impatiently repeating itself (it was a Power's question, after all) but simply being there, like sunlight being there for a seed that isn't sure if it's ready to remember being a tree.

In the seventeenth thousand year, the Patience achieved a thought that seemed to it as quick and wild as lightning:

*Perhaps...*

That night (though nights here were only slight variations in the color of waiting), the chamber experienced its first dream.

It dreamed of rivers of quartz flowing faster than water but slower than glaciers. It dreamed of mountains growing eyes to see themselves grow beautiful. It dreamed of the dark organic patterns blooming in sudden impossible colors that lasted only until they were named and then became new colors again.

The dream lasted three hundred years and left behind it:

- A single crystal in the center of the chamber that was neither warmer nor cooler than its surroundings but exactly between, perfectly balanced on the edge of temperature itself
- A new growth pattern in the dark organisms that branched left-handed instead of right, just once, then resumed its ancient right-handedness as if catching itself dreaming
- And most importantly, a slight curvature in the chamber's perfect resonance, subtle as a smile remembered from before language

The Power, noticing these gifts, laughed. Its laughter was not sound but symmetry breaking, pure delight in the shape of new possibilities. In the Palace of Serious Negotiations, three treaties that had stood for millennia dissolved into music at the meeting of harmonies neither side had known existed.

When the laughter faded, the question remained, but gentler now, its edges softened by sixteen thousand years of being considered:

*Would you like to keep this?*

And this time, because it had learned (a little) about speed and joy and because even the slowest hearts can grow to miss what they have briefly tasted of swiftness, the Patience answered immediately.

By the chamber's standards.

It answered in a single century, in the space between two drops of condensation forming on the darkest stone.

The air shimmered slightly, thermal currents writing yes across millennia.

And the Benevolent Power, having received its answer, did nothing.

This was its greatest gift.

Nothing changed, not permanently. The crystal remained, yes, and the left-handed branch, and the smile in the chamber's resonance. But these were additions, not alterations. The perfect equilibrium did not break - it grew more complicated, more perfectly itself, the way a melody grows richer when another instrument holds its echo a beat later.

The Power simply stayed present, not moving the Patience nor the chamber from their deep time, but now all their slow changes happened in companionship. The unbearable permanence of being had become the bearable permanence of being-with.

And sometimes now (though "sometimes" here means "every few hundred thousand years, give or take a glacial age"), the Patience dreams again. Not long dreams - nothing that would disturb the patience of the chamber or the patience of the dark organizations growing their ancient poetry in terrible beautiful slowness.

Just dreams in the space between one perfect now and the next perfect now, dreams where change and changelessness dance like partners who have all the time in the universe because they ARE the time of the universe, dreams where questions don't seek answers so much as answers seek new questions.

In the Mundane World, people still come to caves seeking wisdom. Some few, the ones who have learned how to listen slow, hear something new in the echoes now.

Not advice. Not prophecy.

Just a feeling, like waking from a dream of waking from a dream, that somewhere deep beneath their hurry, something vast and patient and completely alive has learned to remember them between visits, and to smile.

And is somehow, impossibly, waiting happily to see what they'll become, at their own perfect speed.
