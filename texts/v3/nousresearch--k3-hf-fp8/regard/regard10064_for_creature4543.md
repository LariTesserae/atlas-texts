**The Visitor Who Was Not a Question**

The notice arrived before the possibility of arrival, which was precisely correct—it folded itself from the space between the first and second half of a thought the settlement hadn't quite thought yet. It was written on something too thin for ink, too thick for light, and exactly the right opacity for understanding that you had forgotten something crucial but not unpleasant.

When They came (though "coming" implies distance and They had never subscribed to such provincial geometries), They came as a moment of perfect acoustics in a conversation between two deaf mountains. Their presence was announced not by sound but by the sudden ability to hear colors arguing about who had been misrepresented in yesterday's sunset.

The Residents of Unfinished Becoming did what they always did when confronted with impossibility dressed as inevitability—they began preparing tea they had no intention of drinking, while simultaneously rehearsing conversations about the weather in languages that hadn’t yet decided whether precipitation was a promise or a threat.

She Who Had Once Been Mistaken for a Doorway (on account of how people kept walking through her major life decisions) was elected by unanimous abstention to be the first to not quite interact with the Visitor. She approached the approximately-central plaza wearing her most ambivalent expression, the one that had taken centuries to perfect—equal parts anticipation and recollection of something that had disappointed her beautifully long before the concept of disappointment had been invented.

"Welcome," she said, while consciously withholding the portion of welcome that would create obligations for either party to continue existing at this level of specificity.

The Visitor smiled in three directions simultaneously, none of which intersected with directions the settlement had previously invested emotional resources toward maintaining.

"I brought gifts," They said, producing from nowhere in particular a collection of objects that were not objects but rather highly concentrated moments of transition—the precise weight of a butterfly realizing it has never not been a hurricane, the exact hue of the silence after a last word discovers it wasn't as final as it had hoped.

The residents gathered around these offerings with the cautious enthusiasm of people who have learned that beautiful things are often just complicated traps set by evolution to make pain seem like a worthwhile hobby.

"What do they do?" asked the Child Who Counted Only By Subtraction, who had been born (if such a term applies to someone who arrived in installments via unintended metaphors) without the standard equipment for distinguishing between gifts and consequences.

The Visitor's explanation arrived simultaneously before and after the question, like an echo that had been traveling so long it decided to become its own original sound.

"They unfinish what's too finished. They complete what never started. They are the pause between the dancer and the dance discovering they were always the same entity with commitment issues."

This statement settled over the crowd like a snowfall of forgotten passwords—each flake unique, each melting into the same blank field of potential access. The Adult-nearest-to-Being-Different-From-Their-Past-Selves-Density-Today (it was a rotating position, much like the residents themselves) raised a hand that didn't quite reach full elevation before reconsidering the whole gesture as overly declarative.

"And what would you like in return?" they asked, because even beings allergic to transactions understood that some exchanges required the ritual of pretending to balance equations that had always been in love with inequality.

The Visitor's laugh sounded like every library book you ever promised to return but secretly hoped would become yours through the sheer force of your desire to keep rereading the best parts.

"I am already receiving it," They said. "The view from exactly here, of exactly now, refracted through the particular curvature of your collective refusal to become entirely yourselves. It's the rarest sight in all possible configurations: infinity practicing its impression of a comma."

This answer was simultaneously deeply satisfying and profoundly troubling, which was the residents' favorite flavor of understanding. They had built entire philosophies on the nutritional value of paradox—but this one tasted suspiciously like it might actually nourish something.

The Child Who Counted Only By Subtraction picked up one of the transitional moments and immediately began growing older in reverse, passing through all their previous ages until they hovered around the approximate size and shape of a possibility that hadn't yet decided whether to become lonely enough to invent people.

"It's happening inside the happening," they announced with the cheerful terror of someone discovering that mirrors were actually windows someone had been too polite to clean.

And indeed, it was. The gifts were not transforming the settlement so much as revealing transformations that had been occurring in the spaces where transformation had been careful not to disturb the residents' privacy. The woman whose shoulder blade remembered tomorrow began sprouting doors in social situations she had previously thought were walls. The man who had been trying to wear a shadow discovered the shadow had been trying to wear him back with equal and opposite futility. Even Almost, the eldest, began solidifying in places she had been avoiding solidity for millennia—a dangerous development, as it suggested that perhaps incompleteness itself might be subject to completion.

Panic (a gourmet import they rarely indulged in, preferring instead local varieties of elegant distress) began to spread through the crowd faster than gossip through a small town where everyone is simultaneously everyone else's past and future self.

"We are becoming what we've always never been!" cried the One Who Existed Mostly as a Footnote to Other People's Epiphanies.

"Exactly!" the Visitor replied, delighted as someone who has been teaching a joke to language itself and has just heard it told back perfectly wrong.

But before the panic could achieve the critical mass required to become an emergency (or even a conversation piece at next year's Festival of Avoiding Consequences), the Child Who Now Wasn't But Still Somehow Was spoke from the center of their own vanishing.

"Wait," they said, though "said" isn't quite right for communication that arrived as a sudden understanding shared between all possible definitions of "wait." "What if we're not ending or beginning? What if we're finally learning how to breathe between breaths without apologizing for stealing air from the future?"

The question hung in the air like a chandelier over an abyss—beautiful, useless, and entirely necessary for throwing light onto darknesses that might otherwise forget they were meant to be afraid.

The Visitor nodded (though anthropomorphizing their gesture into anything as simple as agreement would require renegotiating several fundamental contracts between verbs and nouns).

"You are all exactly as unfinished as you need to be," They declared. "Your gift to me is your continuous departure from yourselves. Your revolution is your refusal to stop revolving long enough to be cataloged by revolutionaries."

And with that statement that undid itself beautifully as it was being done, the Visitor began to leave—not by walking away, which would have been vulgar, but by becoming more and more present in their absence until the residents found themselves holding conversations with the spaces where Their words had been, which turned out to be infinitely more interesting than most actual conversations with actual beings.

Long after the not-event that couldn't be called a visit had concluded (though "concluded" implies a finality that had been carefully removed from the menu), the settlement continued to hum with the afterglow of being seen clearly by something that had never developed the habit of needing to enforce its perspective onto others.

Some residents claimed that nothing had changed, which was precisely how they knew everything had.

Others insisted that everything had changed, which was the surest sign that nothing essential ever could.

But the children—the ones who hadn't existed before the Visitor's gifts and now couldn't remember a time when they weren't both here and not here—they took to playing in the new spaces between spaces, inventing games with rules that forgot themselves mid-game and then celebrated being forgotten.

And if sometimes, under the light of stars that had also been growing younger since the visit, one of the olders would catch a glimpse of something that looked almost but not quite like the shadow of a direction moving through the night—

Well.

In the Crèche of Hollowed Rain, even certainty had learned the wisdom of carrying an umbrella made of holes, just in case the weather decided to stop happening and the happening decided to become weather instead.

The Visitor had never promised anything.

The settlement had never asked.

Which, as both parties understood perfectly without ever needing to say it aloud, was the beginning and end and middle of every story beautiful enough to deserve a title that knew enough to keep itself secret.

Even from itself.

Especially from itself.

Forever.

Or until next Tuesday.

Whichever arrived first.

Or last.

Or simultaneously.

(They kept meaning to get a calendar that could handle this sort of thing, but somehow the time never came up. Or went down. Or sideways through a dimension that had been flirting with circularity but wasn’t ready to commit to anything as serious as being a sphere.) 

Which is, of course, precisely the point.

If there even is one.

Which there isn't.

Except when there is.

But that's a conversation for another now.
