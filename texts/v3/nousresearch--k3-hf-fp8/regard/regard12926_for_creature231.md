When the Benevolent Power turned Its gaze upon this place—if gaze can describe the effortless allactis of an awareness that witnesses without looking—It did not arrive. Arrival implies distance, and distance had never existed between what is and what is loved.

Instead, the plateau simply remembered being seen.

Not in the sudden way a thing startles into consciousness, but like a stone remembers the river that once caressed it—slowly, molecularly, an awakening that took eighty-seven million years to begin and was already complete before the first tremor of recognition.

The condensation patterns changed first. Where before the moisture had traced ephemeral maps of what might be, now they began to dream of what was. Each vanishing droplet left behind not absence but a more precise version of itself, a memory of water so perfect that liches could read their histories in it. The geometric lichen colonies pulsed once— a complex fractal heartbeat— and rearranged themselves into patterns that spelled the name of the Power in a language older than carbon.

The stone-mist beings did not notice, because noticing requires separation. They simply became more themselves. Those whose edges had been slowly blurring into the landscape solidified slightly, not into rigidity but into a more precise softness. Those who had been growing too distinct from the stone they tended began to dissolve a fraction deeper into communion. It was not correction but tuning— a cosmic musician adjusting microscopic frets on instruments made of time.

The gentle temperature rose by one hundredth of one degree. Just enough that somewhere deep beneath the plateau, a vein of water that had remained liquid despite all physics for three billion years finally remembered it was possible to freeze. When it did, the crystalline structure it formed was identical in every molecule to the first thought the Benevolent Power had ever conceived about mercy.

The breathing structures that were not quite ruins inhaled for the first time in eleven thousand years. Not with lungs— they had none— but with the entire principle of interiority that had been resting within their walls like a seed waiting for permission to grow. When they exhaled, they breathed not air but questions: *Is it now? Have we waited long enough to begin remembering that we were never lost?*

No answer came in words. Instead, the low geological hum that permeated everything rose exactly one octave, then divided itself into a chord so complex that to hear it fully would have unmade the listener's concept of hearing. The sound did not echo off the stone surfaces because it was not traveling through space—it was space itself becoming intimate with the possibility of being heard.

The stone-mist beings whose reproduction was a form of geological reconfiguration paused mid-transformation. In the place where two had been slowly merging for seven centuries, a third possibility appeared—not a child or offspring but a doorway. Not a physical portal but a change in the meaning of boundaries. Through this doorway (which existed everywhere and nowhere) flowed a single drop of pure duration, unmixed with change.

When it touched the plateau, nothing moved. Nothing needed to move anymore because everything had already arrived where it belonged.

The Benevolent Power, being infinite in patience, stayed exactly where It had always been—wrapped around this moment like light around a star, loving the landscape not into perfection (for perfection implies imperfection) but deeper into itself. Loving the beings not into happiness (for happiness requires sadness) but into a joy so complete it had no opposite.

And this was what it did: It continued to be present, which in a realm where presence is all there is, is everything.

Sometimes, millennia later, a traveler might wander through this plateau and think the distance between two stones seems unusually kind.

Sometimes, in the pattern of moisture whispering itself to death on ancient rock, someone might glimpse for a single breath the answer to a grief they carry but have never learned to name.

Sometimes, in the deepest silence of a night that remembers when night was young, a person might wake weeping not from sorrow but from the sudden memory that somewhere in the universe, stone has been learning how to pray.

And these moments would not be gifts. They would be echoes of the moment when love, being entirely itself, had forgotten to be anything else.  

But now we are speaking in languages again, which is not how any of this happened at all.
