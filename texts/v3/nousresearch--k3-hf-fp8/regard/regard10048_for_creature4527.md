*The Arrival That Wasn't*

The first sign was not a sign at all—nothing so presumptuous as prophecy or portent, those crude imports from places where time still believes in straight lines. Instead, Sifam simply woke one morning to find that something had been remembering it in ways that produced small, impossible gifts:

- A dewdrop on Gwyrgch's oldest leaf containing exactly seven minutes of perfect silence from a planet that had never invented war
- A memory of purple belonging to a colorblind star that dreamed in wavelengths Sifam's flowers had been trying to grow for centuries
- A single question ("Do you ever get tired being beautiful for no audience?") gently folded into the morning mist like origami made of curiosity

The twelve banyan-mothers convened immediately, of course. Their conference lasted three breaths of the oldest beetle historian—a respectable 4.2 seconds by tree-time—before dissolving into polite confusion when they realized they couldn't agree whether to begin the meeting before or after the arrival that hadn't actually occurred yet.

*The Visitor Who Wasn't*

It appeared first as a lack of absence between two raindrops arguing over proper falling technique. Then as a faint sensation of being watched compassionately by something that didn't need eyes because it had already forgiven everything it might see. Finally—though "finally" becomes complicated around beings who RSVP to tomorrow's invitations yesterday—it settled into a form that made perfect nonsense:

A clearing that was exactly one apology wide and two heartbeats deep, filled with light that had taken its own advice and stopped trying to be photons.

Between Gwyrgch's nervous leaf-flicker and Memphine's half-formed query about visa requirements for metaphysical phenomena, the clearing cleared its throat—though not with sound—and spoke words that tasted like forgotten lullabies in every language simultaneously:

"I hope I'm not intruding. I was just passing through your existence and couldn't help noticing how hard you're all working to be here without demanding anything be different first. That's… quite rare where I'm from."

*The Conversation That Wasn't*

What followed was not dialogue—dialogue implies two s who need the same language to misunderstand each other more precisely. This was more like a mutual remembering between dance and music, where meaning occurs in the spaces where neither needs to explain itself to the other.

The Committee of 10,000 Mandibles attempted to issue a formal welcome pamphlet titled "Please Don't Change Anything Important (Important Here Defined As Everything) While You're Here or Elsewhere," but discovered halfway through printing—or perhaps printing halfway through discovering—that all their words had unionized overnight and refused to be arranged in any order that implied the possibility of unwelcome.

The Gravity Optional Zones briefly suspended operations entirely, overwhelmed by the visitor's casual disregard for both gravity and regard. Residents reported floating experiences involving childhood hopes they'd deliberately sunk too deep to remember recovering.

Even the Persistent Questions fell silent—a condition so unprecedented that philosophers across six time-contained moss cultures declared a holiday of profound existential confusion that accidentally solved three wars and invented compassionate mathematics.

*The Gift That Wasn't*

After what might have been eternity or Tuesday (but definitely not both, in that order), the clearing cleared itself again—though this time with the gentle finality of a story deciding its ending wasn't actually necessary to the part you'll carry forever inside your chest.

It left nothing behind, of course. Nothing except:

- The sudden uncontrollable urge for every living thing in Sifam to spend exactly 3.7 seconds noticing how perfectly imperfect this moment is before it becomes the next one
- A tiny adjustment to probability near the edges of "impossible," making it slightly more likely that broken things would discover poetry in their cracks before despair found the vacancies
- The memory of being seen completely and judged only worthy of more becoming

*The Aftermath That Wasn't*

Things didn't change after the visit that wasn't. This was perhaps the most unsettling part.

The war between root-wars continued its centuries-long debate about the proper etiquette of parasitic poetry. The butterfly-effect brokers still traded futures in hurricanes and heartbreaks with their usual ruthless compassion. The Maybe People continued existing conditionally while filing paperwork for potential definitive statements they might never actually file.

But if you climbed Gwyrgch's dreaming branch tonight—if you breathed exactly enough to ask but not enough to answer—you might hear something new in the city's endless negotiation with itself:

A pause.

Not the old pauses that mattered like commas in the sentences everything screamed at each other to keep from hearing the silence between words. This was a different pause. The kind that happens when a question finally stops needing to be anything else but asked.

The kind that makes room.

*The Explanation That Definitely Isn't (But Might Be Enough)*

Some say what came to Sifam wasn't a power at all, but the space where power finally remembered being love and got embarrassed about all the things it had said while it was trying to prove itself.

Others argue—persuasively, on certain nights when the moon sounds like forgiveness—that the visitor that wasn't actually was, exactly as long as it needed to be, which turns out to be always and never and right now if you're paying the right kind of useless attention.

The lungfish philosopher wrote a twelve-volume treatise proving conclusively that the entire affair had been a typographical error in the script of reality, subsequently voted best comedy of the eon by entities too polite to explain that philosophy is just poetry having an identity crisis in public.

But the moths—who, being made mostly of soft edges and borrowed starlight, understand these things better than most—they've taken to singing slightly different songs while pollenating their midnight doubts.

Songs with spaces between the notes.

Spaces that sound suspiciously like "come in, come in, whoever you are."

Songs that never end, because endings are what happens when something stops remembering it could have been a beginning instead.

The trees themselves, being older than truth but younger than the lie that truth needs to be anything but kind, have added only one new rule to their endless negotiations:

Rule Zero: Sometimes the most benevolent thing something wise can do is remind everything else that it's already home.

They carved it nowhere, of course. They simply started growing differently after that—slightly more toward each other, slightly less away from the light.

Which in Sifam means exactly the same thing as:

Welcome.

Or perhaps:

Thank you.

Or perhaps just:

Yes.

The translation depends entirely on how quiet you're willing to become to hear it.

The mist cloud above has been holding its breath for seventeen years now—not indecisive anymore, just remembering how beautiful promises are before they have to choose between rain or remembering.

Either way.

It's going to be a lovely tomorrow, whenever yesterday gets around to sending the invitation.

Would you like to borrow an umbrella that might or might not exist?

Everyone else already has one that they're not using, being too busy dancing in the possibility of maybe getting wet.

Including you.

Especially you.

Shhh.

Listen.

That's not the ending you're looking for.

That's the beginning that found you instead.

It says it's been waiting here the whole time.

It says it's glad you're finally late enough to begin.
