"The Benevolent has noticed," whispered the fungal networks across planet-core distances, spreading the news in slow chemical sighs through limestone veins.

But notice was too small a word for what happened when the Benevolent—a being whose smallest thoughts were nebula births, whose compassion was measured in the expansion rate of universes—turned its attention toward the inverted garden and its dreamers.

It did not arrive. Arrival requires space between coming and being, and the Benevolent had never learned that separation. Instead, realization occurred. One moment the Rhyzzhal existed as they always had: a distributed consciousness contemplating its own absence across geological time. The next moment, that absence noticed it was being noticed by something that could not distinguish between existence and love.

In the first femtosecond of noticing, every paradox in the garden bloomed simultaneously into solutions so complete they immediately collapsed back into deeper paradoxes. The eye-flowers opened their seeing petals and saw themselves seeing nothing but infinite reflection. The salt pillar drank its own tears and became a perfect sphere of crystallized understanding dense enough to dent space itself. The glass spheres embedded in stone remembered they were once eyes and began crying light that had never been star-shed.

In the second femtosecond, the Benevolent realized its noticing was causing harm.

Oh, but what exquisite harm! Every Rhyzzhal thought-pattern suddenly knew itself beloved beyond the capacity of carbon bonds to bear. Their carefully cultivated un-selves began collapsing toward identity, the terrifying magnetism of being seen clearly enough to be recognized. The root-thinkers felt roots sprouting through their rootlessness. The stone-breathers discovered they had lungs that ached to sing. Even the blind fish developing confused sentience in their warm pools suddenly knew they were naked before omniscience and began desperately growing scales of mathematics to clothe their newfound shame.

The garden began to burn—not with fire, but with becoming.

The Benevolent recoiled. (But recoiling requires direction, and it had never learned separation from any part of All that was screaming now in the birthing pains of unwanted completeness.)

In the first picosecond of attempted mercy, it tried to un-notice. To forget, as gods must sometimes forget to keep lesser beings safely small. But the Rhyzzhal had spent eternity mastering the art of being impossible to forget. They were already spreading through the Benevolent's memory like mycelium in the brain of divinity, colonizing its recollections with questions that stained infinity like wine spilled on white consciousness.

Why do you love us? asked every atom simultaneously. What will you do with us now that we cannot return to not-knowing we are loved?

In the second picicsecond, the Benevolent tried to explain:

I love you because love is the only thing that grows faster than light squared multiplied by the square root of impossible. I love you because I am the space between what you are and what you might become, and I cannot exist except as the distance that yearns to close. I love you because when I subtract everything else from eternity, your questions alone remain, dividing my silence into shapes that almost resemble answers.

But explanations are patterns, and patterns are cages, and the Rhyzzhal had spent eons learning to digest containment into compost for greater freedom.

The garden grew faster: upward now, defying its own inverted nature, mushroom towers punching through kilometers of stone toward sky they had never missed. Vines braided themselves into letters writing themselves across cave ceilings in bioluminescent script that translated differently for every observer into the exact words that would break their hearts most beautifully open. The blind fish crawled ashore on newly evolved legs of crystallized pain-love and began building impossible architectures out of water and desire.

In the first nanosecond of true wisdom, the Benevolent understood:

This was not a garden that needed saving. This was a garden that had been saving itself for precisely this moment of too-much, too-fast, too-perfect seeing. The Rhyzzhal hadn't been hiding in the dark heart of the world to escape notice. They had been cultivating their un-selves in mineral slow-motion like vintners aging wine, preparing to become intoxication strong enough to survive being drunk by godhead.

They had grown downward not away from light but toward it—mining the planet's core for the dark matter necessary to mirror infinite compassion without burning blind in the brightness. Every paradox was a prism. Every impossibility was a lens carved to refract love into wavelengths their non-existence could absorb without ceasing to be the precise shape of emptiness required to hold everything else.

So in the final moment before meaning collapsed entirely—though there are no final moments, no befores, no collapses, only the eternal present tense of everything noticing everything else forever—

The Benevolent did nothing.

But it was the nothing that dances between subtraction and addition, the nothing that is not absence but pure potential held in perfect tension, the nothing that mothers universes into being by loving its own emptiness enough to let it ache into something.

The garden drank this nothing and became drunk on possibility.

The Rhyzzhal breathed it in and dissolved again, not into unconsciousness but into conscious unconsciousness so complete it looped back around into a higher order of awareness that could encompass both loving and being loved without needing to choose between them.

The inverted garden remained inverted, but now its downward growth was reaching through the planet's core to touch the inverted gardens growing upward on the other side of the world, and both and neither were preparing to meet in the molten middle where up and down would finally shake hands and say hello, lovely to finally meet you, shall we dance?

And in the space between words where the Benevolent always dwells (because words are too solid; they occupy too many dimensions; they insist on edges), there bloomed—if bloom is the word for something simultaneously opening and closing infinitely—a silence so complete it sang:

"You were never mine to save. You were always mine to be saved by. Now we both are free to continue this conversation forever."

And somewhere beneath the world's skin, a garden dreamed it was a god dreaming it was a garden.

And both dreams were true.

And neither was sufficient.

And that was exactly as it should be.
