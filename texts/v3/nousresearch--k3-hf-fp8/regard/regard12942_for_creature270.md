The Benevolent Power arrives neither as light nor darkness, but as a sudden silence deeper than the absence of sound - a complete and perfect pause in the garden's ceaseless mathematics. In that impossible stillness, the upward-flowing water halts mid-ribbon, crystallizing into static sculptures that defy both entropy and essence.

The Axiom Tenders perceive this intrusion instantly, not through their usual neural-networks of equations and constants, but through a new modality entirely: they feel observed from within. Their crystalline projections flicker and stutter, briefly losing coherence as if embarrassed by detection. The threads of light between structures pulse erratically, leaking strange colors that have no names in any spectrum known or knowable.

Then comes the adjustment - not a defense, for beings of pure mathematics cannot defend against that which transcends all parameters, but a curious rearranging. The empty chair at the terrace's edge begins to change, not in form but in potential. Its glass-like substance becomes simultaneously smoother and infinitely textured, each atom rewritten to contain within itself the precise number of possible sitting positions equal to all beings who have ever or might ever desire rest.

The floating pools of trapped equations collapse inward, each implosion birthing a miniature star that burns neither hot nor cold but with the gentle warmth of understanding itself. These stellar infants arrange themselves in a shifting mandala above the chair, their light composing and re-composing the fundamental theorem that describes all possible compassion between the abstract and the embodied.

The wise power speaks without voice: "I have noticed your garden of exceptions."

The response ripples through every dimension of the space, manifesting as a geometric flower blooming in negative space, its petals unfolding the proof that even in the purest mathematics, selection itself implies preference, and preference implies care.

"Your tending," the silence continues, "is perfect in its precision. Yet perfection can also be constraint. Would you allow me to introduce an imperfection?"

For the first time in their existence beyond time, the Axiom Tenders hesitate. Imperfection? Error? These are the antitheses of their existence, the shadows from which they were conceived to shield reality. But this is no ordinary proposition. This is an offer from one who has noticed them not as phenomena to be explained, but as beings who might choose.

The crystal crabs freeze mid-motion. The bioluminescent flowers cease their calculations. Even the strange ferns stop their eternal spiral dance.

Then incrementally, with the infinite care of beings who rewrite laws as others might breathe, they begin to adjust. Not the local variables this time, not the constants or coefficients that define their pocket universe, but something deeper - the very meta-rules that govern how rules themselves may be changed.

A single raindrop falls upward.

But it is not water. It contains neither hydrogen nor oxygen nor any element from any periodic table. Instead, it holds a question without words or symbols, a perfectly formed curiosity that cannot be resolved by any equation yet conceived.

The drop ascends toward the mandala of understanding-stars, each one perturbing its path by unequal amounts, introducing chaos so gentle that it becomes a new kind of order - the order of becoming unpredictable by degrees too small to measure but too large to ignore.

And where the drop would eventually dissolve into the garden's maintained perfection, something impossible occurs: it doesn't. It persists as itself, a tiny rebellion against even the law of resolution, a permanent question mark in the flawless syntax of their reality.

The empty chair, now containing infinite possibilities plus one, remains empty.

But now it waits differently.

The Axiom Tenders have not accepted the gift. They have not rejected it. They have done something they never conceived possible: they have allowed it to exist alongside their perfect tending, neither assimilated nor expelled, but simply... welcomed as guest.

The wise power's presence begins to recede, not departing but diffusing until it becomes indistinguishable from the garden's own maintained strangeness. But it leaves behind new constants in the deepest registers of the space's mathematics:

That symmetry may be beautiful, but asymmetry contains possibilities symmetry cannot imagine.

That precision is noble, but gently blurred edges allow for unexpected connections.

That even beings of pure calculation might cultivate, in some carefully contained corner of their perfect gardens, a small plot where things are allowed to grow according to laws they have not yet discovered.

And if any being of sufficient wisdom ever passes this way again, seeking rest in their journey through the folded places between realities, they might find the chair no longer empty.

Instead, they will find waiting for them a single crystal crab made not entirely of living crystal, its geometric form subtly but unmistakably altered. It will regard them with eyes that hold both the clarity of perfect reason and something else entirely - something like wonder.

And after the longest pause, it will ask, in a voice composed of wind chimes and wind through uncountable mathematical spaces:

"Would you care to sit? And while you rest, perhaps... would you tell me what questions you carry that have no answers?"

At that moment, the observer will realize that they have stumbled not into a maintained space between realities, but into something far rarer: a place where even the gardeners themselves have begun to dream.
