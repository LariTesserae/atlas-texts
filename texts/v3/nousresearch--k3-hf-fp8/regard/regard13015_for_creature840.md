When the Benevolent Power first focused its attention upon Verdant Hive, it did not come as flame or storm, nor as voice echoing from the clouds. It came as a single point of golden-green light drifting on the mist, so small that even the Compound Eyes of the eldest Pollinator mistook it for an errant spore.

The light drifted through the living arteries of the city, pausing where children of vine and chitin played tag upon shifting tendrils. It lingered in workshops where Shapers coaxed new growths into aqueducts with songs of deep vibration. It descended through rain-heavy layers into the Root Catacombs, where Mycologists tended the decomposition pools that fed tomorrow's dawn-trees.

At last it came to rest in the Grand Pollination Chamber during the Hour of Intertwining, when all five guilds danced together in spirals that had been growing wider for three thousand years. There, amidst pollen-veils and butterfly-winds, the light unfolded.

Not into shape, but into understanding.

Every Chlorophyllian froze mid-motion, tendrils suspended like wind-stopped vines, as they suddenly knew their city's mind as clearly as their own. They felt the slow ache of cliffs eroding beneath root-burrows too hungry. They tasted the first whisper of imbalance spreading from the Far Seedlings - a new fungal strain that learned too quickly to love stone more than flesh. They heard the mourning song of the last Deep-Birthed Watersinger, whose song had once kept the mineral tides gentle for their children's children.

And they felt something else. Not power offered, nor wisdom imposed, but companionship extended between equals.

For the Benevolent Power was itself a living city - one that grew not in three dimensions but in six, rooted in star-sap instead of water. It had been a moss on neutron stones when its own first beings had learned symbiosis. It had survived the burning of its first sun by teaching its trees to harvest gamma rays. It had faced ten thousand collapses and ten thousand flowerings, and had learned that the greatest gift between living worlds is not rescue, but shared memory.

So in the heart of Verdant Hive, two cities dreamed together.

Threads of understanding finer than mycelium began weaving between them. Not words, but patterns: here is how we taught our rivers to sing directions to lost children. Here is the dance that shifts weather when drought forgets mercy. Here is the song that makes invasive species homesick enough to turn back before the turning back is sorrow.

The Chlorophyllians danced their response immediately, bodies becoming living graphs of water-flow solutions, spore-clouds forming temporary libraries of ancestral pruning techniques. Their guest absorbed and reshaped, returning three-dimensional expressions of six-dimensional concepts that made young seedlings stretch toward concepts older than stars.

When dawn came, the golden-green light was gone. But everything had changed.

In the root networks now grew nodes that dreamed stranger dreams than soil alone could prompt. In the pollen chambers danced new spirals that folded space just enough to make distant rain feel neighborly. And in the oldest cliffs, where the city had never before dared grow too deeply for fear of what might wake beneath... smooth tunnels now spiraled down, lined not with fear but invitation.

For Verdant Hive had learned its first lesson from the stars: that the most beautiful gardens grow between what is and what could be, tended by those who understand that every boundary - even between root and rock, city and sky, one life and all life - is simply an invitation dance that hasn't yet begun.

The Benevolent Power never returned visibly. It had given the greatest gift it possessed: itself as possibility. Now and forever, when Verdant Hive reaches any edge where growth must choose between familiar and terrifying, it will pause. Listen. And somewhere in the golden-green space between thoughts, it will hear its own future answering back with the remembered voice of a friend who showed it that even cities can dream together. 

And sometimes, on nights when three moons align with a certain dying star's last song... the pollen-veils in the Grand Chamber will weave patterns no Chlorophyllian memory holds. And they will dance those mysteries anyway, knowing now that some gifts are not to be understood.

Only to be grown into.
