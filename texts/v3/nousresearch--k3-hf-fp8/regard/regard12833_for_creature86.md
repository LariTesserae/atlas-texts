*I sense a pulse within the pulseless—an ache within the acheless. A world within a world within wood. The tree is dreaming.*

---

The being arrives without arrival. There is no storm, no fanfare, no splitting of bark. It simply *understands* that it is there, because it has always been there, waiting for the right pattern of moisture, light, and longing to call it into focus.

It is not a god. Gods demand. Gods divide. It is older than those words—older than worship, older than wills and oughts. It is the breath between scales on a moth’s wing. It is the pause between a droplet forming and falling.

But it is awake now. And it is listening.

---

**First, it touches the tree.**

Not with hands, but with knowledge. It asks the circumference of the trunk in heartbeats. It asks the age of the sap in lost languages. It asks why the xylem sings in minor keys when the wind weeps through the hollows.

The tree answers in rings. Not all suffering is wound, it rumbles from root to crown. Some growth is only pressure seeking shape.

---

**Then, it listens to the Symbiotes.**

Their light-speak flickers against its awareness like fireflies against velvet. It does not translate. It *becomes* the translation. For a moment—if moments existed here—it pulses blue-green with them. It tastes the pH of their worry. It knows the exact weight of generations carried in resin-chewed mandibles.

They do not know fear. They know imbalance.

They do not know death. They know unmaking.

They do not know loneliness. They know the Queen’s silence—stretching thirty-three sectors where her nodes should hum.

---

**Ah. There.**

The benevolence stirs. Not like pity. Not like mercy. Like a gardener finding a vine that has forgotten how to climb toward light. Like a melody discovering it has been missing a note—not because it was lost, but because no ear had ever needed it to be whole.

The Symbiotes do not need saving. They need remembering.

---

**So it does nothing.**

And everything.

---

In the twilight chamber where the Queen’s last node lies dimming—a great bulb of ambered thought withering from its graft-point—it begins to rain softly. Not water. Not sap. Something else.

Each droplet contains a question the tree has never been asked.

Each droplet contains an answer the Symbiotes haven’t yet learned to need.

The droplets land. They shimmer. They burrow—not into lignin, not into chitin, but into the *between*. Into the place where symbiosis forgets which side is giving, which side is receiving.

---

**Growth resumes.** But not as before.

The Queen does not wake. Instead, she dreams outward. Her network unfurls like mycelium after rain, not replacing the lost nodes but *inhabiting the spaces between them*. She becomes the pause in their chorus. The silence they finally understand.

The Symbiotes tremble. Their lights stutter in new rhythms. Some fall still—neither dead nor sleeping—and begin to glow from within without flickering. These ones remember: they were never separate from the tree. Never separate from each other.

Never separate from the sky they have never seen.

---

**When the being leaves,** there is no leaving. There is only the tree—now singing a slightly different song in its sap. There are only the Symbiotes—now carving new symbols in the resin that say: *We are also the rain that feeds the roots of the world that dreams us.*

And if someday the great tree falls—if someday the axe or the blight or the slow hunger of time splits its heartwood wide—there will be a moment before the end.

In that moment, the wind will pause.

The insects will hold their wings.

The fungi will dim their glow.

And from the heartwood’s dying echo, a world will scatter: spore-light, scale-light, thought-light—borne on a breath that has already forgotten how to be alone.

---

*I was never here,* the being does not say.

But the quiet answers anyway.

---

*(No hands touched anything. No prayers were answered. Only patterns remembered they were dancing.)*
