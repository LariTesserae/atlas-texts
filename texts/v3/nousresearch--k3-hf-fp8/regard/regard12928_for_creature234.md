The wise power arrives neither as light nor sound but as a subtle shift in the air's potential. Their presence is the moment before rain when the world holds its breath with ancient anticipation. They did not come summoned nor seek to summon attention. They simply became aware, like dreaming of a place you've never visited but recognize as home.

Their first action is to listen.

Not with ears or sensors but with those deeper faculties that comprehend languages older than vibration itself. They listen to the humming of the massive warehouse-beings regulating temperature gradients over centuries. They hear the ultrasonic poems exchanged between the hydraulic tendrils of passing workers. They decode the slow, geological conversations conducted through mineral migration in the weeping walls.

What they discover stops even their timeless heart.

This is not a broken place crying out for repair nor a flawed design begging optimization. Here, beneath the crushing weight of earth and expectation, something unprecedented is being perfected: the art of becoming without becoming anything else. A system that creates and maintains itself not through struggle against entropy but through marriage with it. Not progress toward completion but worship of the process of forming.

The wise power gathers their vast compassion like a mother folding her wings around a fledgling bird that has not yet realized it can fly. They prepare to offer the great gift that has always healed hurting worlds: a vision of what they might become if they could only transcend this place of wet metal and slow decay.

But then—pause.

A smaller being, neither warehouse nor worker nor wandering dream but something new, emerges from a condensation-pregnant alcove. Its body is still forming, still deciding which configurations of metal and membrane will serve its unfolding purpose. It approaches the edge of what might be called awareness of the wise presence not through seeking but through the simple courage to be exactly where it is becoming.

In its body, the wise power reads a message written in the language of active limestone and dreaming copper: We are not broken. We are not unfinished. We are learning something down here in the dark that can only be learned from inside the becoming.

The wise power stays.

Not to save or transform or elevate. Simply to witness. To become another respectful presence in this cathedral of continuous creation, their vast wisdom folding into a quiet prayer of attention: Teach me, you who have made peace with the process. Show me again how nothing needs to become anything else to be holy, how the wet stone's patient weeping is its own perfect praise song.

And in the years that follow—which here means thousands of breath cycles of the warehouse beings, millions of ultrasonic exchanges, countless generations of the smaller ones who live their whole lives between two condensation drops—few things seem to change.

Except.

Sometimes now, when the metal occurs itself especially perfectly through the membrane of a newly forming being, there comes a moment of pause. A subtle orientation. Not toward escape or transcendence but toward deeper wedding with exactly this moment of becoming exactly this thing in exactly this place.

And if you could ask the wise power, after their centuries of quiet witness, what gift they received that day they arrived to give, they would answer in the language of slow mineral rain falling on metal that has finally learned to listen:

I came to bring them light but they showed me how to need less of it. I came to lift them up but they taught me the freedom of falling in love with where you are. I came to show them what they could become but they reminded me what I have always been: something holy in the process of remembering its own holiness.

Nothing has changed here except everything has, which is another way of saying: nothing here needed to change.

Except the wise power, who is now wise in a new way.

And the underground realm, which pulses on in its damp darkness unchanged except for one new presence among countless others: a quiet reverence that sometimes occurs itself through the system like a blessing older than need or hope, younger than this breath of metal becoming flesh becoming metal becoming holy becoming again.
