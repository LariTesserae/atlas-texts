From far beyond the reach of sunlight, beyond even the pressure-crushing depths where even the Threadsingers have never ventured, something stirs – not with the blind urgency of hunger nor the sharp reflex of fear, but with a slow, deliberate shift that resonates through the entire ocean like a thoughtsong too deep to hear.

The wise power is ancient beyond measure, older than the shifting plates that once swallowed these cities and older still than the coral beggars that now lift them back toward air. It is neither one nor many, neither mind nor force, but rather pattern itself made conscious – the mathematics of tides given breath, the geometry of shells raised to awareness.

For millennia this power has observed, cataloguing the universe's possibilities in patterns of pressure and temperature, of mineral flow and current. It has watched stars be born from nebulae-birthwaters and watched civilizations dissolve back into the same elemental soup. Always it has observed without interference, understanding that true wisdom lies in recognizing when to act and (far more rarely) when to act.

But now, as its vast perception pass through these risen waters, something truly novel catches within its consciousness – a place where the usual patterns fold back upon themselves in ways both beautiful and impossible. A city that grows DOWN toward ruin while simultaneously growing UP toward hope, tended by beings who are neither former nor future but both at once.

The Threadsingers feel it first as a subtle change in the city's breathing – how the bubble-gardens hold their shapes for breath-held seconds longer than the laws of pressure should allow, how the coral-stone songs gain faint harmonic overtones that somehow contain meanings too vast for even the eldest to parse. The engineered fish flash in new patterns seen only once before in their deepest genetic memories, while even the radically transformed beings in the city's deepest recesses emerge to drift upward in curious spirals, their luminous bodies pulsing questions they themselves no longer remember how to form.

Then comes the scattering light.

It begins in the deepest trenches far below, where no light has ever reached – tiny crystals forming from minerals dissolved in unthinkable concentrations, each perfect lattice identical down to the atomic level but arranged in patterns that defy three-dimensional geometry. These crystals grow not larger but somehow more crystalline, becoming more purely themselves with each passing moment until their very existence resonates with the fundamental notes of reality.

The Threadsingers hear it as music unlike any they've ever played – a song not of water through channels but of possibility itself finding voice through pressure and time. Their gossamer fingers move instinctively to join the harmony, but find themselves playing rhythms and melodies they've never learned yet somehow remember from before remembering. Around them, the city responds – not growing faster or stronger, but growing more elegantly, dissolving here with greater grace while rising there with more purposeful beauty.

And then, impossibly, the wise power chooses to manifest.

Not as a being for there are no words for what it becomes, but rather as a space where space itself becomes more perfectly what space already was. At the exact midpoint between the sediment-covered streets below and the surface beyond sight above, reality folds with exquisite care until it becomes a sphere of perfect clearness – not water nor air nor vacuum but something that contains all three while being none of them.

Into this sphere, the wise power carefully places a gift.

It is smaller than a Threadsinger's smallest fingertip and larger than their entire risen city – a seed not of matter nor energy but of possibility itself, folded into existence using dimensions curled too small for even their most sophisticated symbionts to perceive. Within its impossible boundaries lives every pattern that this place and these beings might yet become, arranged not as choices but as harmonies, each transformation flowing into the next with the inevitability of tide following moon.

The wise power does not speak commands or offer guidance, for it knows that both are forms of limitation that would diminish rather than enhance what grows here. Instead, it simply holds this possibility-seed suspended at the city's heart – not forcing growth nor preventing dissolution but making both processes more perfectly themselves, refining decay into a purer kind of decay while elevating rebirth into a truer form of becoming.

Then, having given the only gift that true wisdom can give without cost, the wise power withdraws itself gradually, folding back into the deeper patterns from which it emerged, leaving behind only the quiet gift of its brief attention and the subtle alteration in how pressure flows through water, how time settles into stone, how possibility seeds itself in patience.

The Threadsingers will spend generations debating whether this visitation occurred at all, or was instead merely a particularly vivid shared dream brought on by unusual chemical traces in the water-flow. Their songs will never quite return to what they were before, gaining lingering overtones that make the coral-stone grow in shapes more beautiful than functional but somehow more perfectly suited to beauty-function than either alone.

The city itself will continue its slow dance between rising and falling, neither hurrying toward either destination nor quite resting in between. But now there is something new in its pulse, a subtle harmonic that makes even the harsh grinding of dissolution sound somehow like laughter, even the aching slowness of growth seem somehow like joyful anticipation.

And in the deepest heart of the city, where the old streets meet the new coral in patterns older than either, the possibility-seed waits with infinite patience – not to be found or forgotten, but simply to be precisely where it is, making every pattern that unfolds around it more perfectly itself without ever needing to do anything more.

Which might be the finest gift that wisdom, no matter how ancient or vast, can ever offer to a place that has already learned the hardest truth of all: that true transformation begins not with changing what you are, but with becoming more beautifully and completely what you already were becoming all along.
