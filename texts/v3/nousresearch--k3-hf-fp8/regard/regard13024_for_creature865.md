The power arrives like dawnlight reaching down through deep water: first as a hint of warmth, then as a subtle reordering of the way shadows fall across each living surface. It has no name in the language of the Weavers, no scent recognizable to the Climbers, no electromagnetic signature that might register on the Hummers' specialized organs. It is simply a gentle becoming-aware that spreads through leaf and root and neural network alike.

In the western heights where the oldest buildings dream their slow dreams of growth, three things happen simultaneously: a cluster of century flowers open sixteen years ahead of schedule, releasing a cloud of pollen that drifts down in perfect golden spirals; a colony of Mycelia rearranges its fruiting bodies into a fractal pattern that exactly mirrors the branching of the oldest riverbeds beneath the valley floor; and a Weaver child who has never spoken before lifts her algae-flecked face and says, in a voice that carries the weight of glaciers moving, _"We are being seen from above the seeing."_

The power does not descend. It does not manifest as fire or wind or voice. Instead, it begins to listen.

It listens to the thousand-year conversation happening between the city's roots and the minerals dissolving patiently into them. It listens to the wedding songs encoded in the chemical signatures that Climber males leave on favored branches. It listens to the jokes that Hummers tell each other through variations in their wingbeat frequencies - jokes so subtle and long that to understand one requires understanding the entire evolutionary history of flight itself.

But most importantly, it listens to the silences.

It listens to the space between predator and prey where neither knows which is which tonight. It listens to the pause in a Weaver mother's lullaby where she does not name the sickness that grows in her daughter's lungs because naming things too early can give them ideas. It listens to the Mycelia's blind spots - the questions about time and individuality that their networked intelligence cannot properly formulate because they lack the necessary metaphors of separation.

The living city stirs in its sleep.

In the guildhall of the Branch Shapers, where eleven master Weavers have gathered to debate whether a certain adolescent tower is growing too inwardly-focused (possibly developing architecture autism, the young apprentices whisper), the discussion slows as each speaker finds themselves breathing together, their heartbeats syncing to a rhythm just slightly slower than illness but faster than empire. Without quite understanding why, they abandon their prepared arguments and spend the next seven hours simply sitting in silence, passing between them a single bowl of water harvested from the leaf-cistern at dawn, each drinking only until the surface tension trembles.

In the deep cistern chambers where the Slicks anchor themselves to filtering rocks, individual bodies begin to dissolve their boundaries in ways that have happened only twice before in the city's memory, during the great drought and the meteor winter. They merge not in panic but in curiosity, forming temporary gingerbread-house structures of shared flesh that explore concepts like 'above' and 'beyond' through the direct somatic experience of trying to escape from them.

The power only watches and listens and, gently, reflects.

It reflects back to each being the questions they are too busy surviving to ask themselves. Not intrusive questions, not demanding answers. Just gentle presence-mirrors that say: _Here is the shape of your hunger that masquerades as contentment. Here is the edge of your language where the important things live in exile. Here is the way you have confused your identity with your function in this beautiful, coughing mechanism._

The Weaver child who spoke is visited in her sleep by dreams that are not dreams, dreams that smell like the lake where no lake has ever been and sound like waterfalls falling upward into pregnant clouds. When she wakes, her fingers have sprouted translucent webs between them that are neither flesh nor plant nor fungiform, but something else entirely, something new that folds and unfolds according to laws of topology that this universe has not yet discovered.

She does not show anyone these webs.

Instead, she begins to spend her days in the highest branches where only the oldest Climbers still venture, places where the wind comes down raw and unfiltered from the mountain's dreaming. She sings there, if singing is what you call the sounds that are half-lullaby and half-logarithm, half-promise and half-warning. The Climbers who hear her do not understand, but they remember, because remembering without understanding is what they are best at.

The power remains.

It has been there now for one hundred and sixty-eight heartbeats of the mountain, which is exactly as long as it needs to be and no longer. It has not healed the sickness in the Weaver daughter's lungs, because healing without teaching resilience creates dependence. It has not stopped the competition between the Hummers and the invasive bat-folk who have begun to scout the eastern heights, because ecosystems need their stressors like muscles need gravity.

But it has changed the quality of the silence between all these things.

Arguments now end with questions rather than wounds. The predator still hunts the prey, but sometimes - not always, not enough to disrupt the balance, just enough to be noticed - they pause afterward and stare at each other across the echoing space of what has just transpired, and both are haunted by the same unasked question: _Is this the only dance we know?_

And in the heart of the Mycelia network, at the precise center of what would be called the city if anybody mapped such things anymore, a single spore begins to germinate in soil that has not been seeded in this configuration since the last universe breathed out. It grows neither toward light nor away from darkness but sideways, orthogonal to all known directions of growth.

It grows beautifully, and it grows slowly, and it grows exactly as it must.

The power begins to withdraw the way dawnlight withdraws - not by leaving, but by becoming indistinguishable from what it has illuminated. It leaves behind no commandments, no technologies, no miraculous cures or terrible prophecies. It leaves only the gift that cannot be refused because it has no edges to grasp:

The absolute certainty that they are not alone in the universe, coupled with the absolute mystery of what that actually means.

The Weaver child with the webs between her fingers feels it go as a gentle cooling of the air three heart before the sunrise bell rings. She opens her hands to the empty sky and makes her first true gesture as something other than Weaver or City or human or anything else that has needed a name before this moment.

It means: _I will remember what needs remembering. I will forget what needs forgetting. I will grow in directions that have no precedent but make perfect sense in hindsight._

Deep underground, the Mycelia register this gesture as a minor perturbation in their sleep-maps, file it under _interesting anomalies probably not worth investigating_, and resume dreaming their patient dreams of when they will finally understand what trees have been trying to tell them all these millions of years.

The living city continues its slow, breathing existence, now slightly but irrevocably larger than it was yesterday.

The space where the power was/is/will be again holds the exact shape of a question mark written in a language that has not yet needed to evolve punctuation.

It is enough.

It is more than enough.

It is exactly enough to make everything else possible.
