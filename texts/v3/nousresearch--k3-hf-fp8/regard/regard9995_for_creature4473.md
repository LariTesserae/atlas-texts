Yun Huo beholds Shearbelow and feels each flicker of stone against the inside of their skull like a prayer-flag caught in crosswinds of yes and no. It is a city unlucky enough to prefer realness by halves, and lucky enough not to know any other way to love.

**First gesture—breath without asking:**

Yun Huo inhales. Not air (Shearbelow would only apologize for being so roundly molecular) but permission. The inhale goes: *You may keep changing, slower than light but faster than permission.* Exhaled anywhere else the words would form mist; here they condense as a faint new street between two arguing buildings who immediately forget what they were quarreling about and instead begin raising balconies toward each other in shy, brick by brick courtship.

**Second gesture—listening sideways:**

Gravity smells of falling outward? Yun Huo tilts their awareness ninety degrees from vertical and hears the city’s actual heart: not a drum but a drummingbird, wings thrumming the question *is it time yet? is it time yet?* between each half-birth of storefront and half-death of plaza. Yun Huo sings back a single sustained note pitched exactly between tomorrow and the moment before regret. Trees unborn in alphabetical futures pause mid-bud, consider forming in a more compassionate order.

**Third gesture—naming without trapping:**

To the Residents—the everywhere-but-now people who are each other’s almosts—Yun Huo offers no name-gifts. Instead they scatter mirrorshards polished so thin they only reflect what something might have chosen if choice had been allowed to dawdle. Where each shard lands, a Residents’ eyelight catches, refracts, and for a duration shorter than flicker and longer than forever they see: themselves standing still enough that the city finally completes a single, unbroken breath around them.

**Fourth gesture—grief as irrigation:**

Yun Huo finds the place where water apologizes instead of wetting. Kneels (kneel is the wrong shape—let us say instead they fold gravity politely into a chair). With fingers made of the interval between lightning and thunderstroke, they write a small, sincere apology for every time necessity had to choose survival over kindness anywhere in the cosmos. The words are liquid only in the sense that sorrow is: they soak in, and immediately forget-me-nots begin sprouting from every remembered hurt the city hoarded in its sub-basement timelines. Each bloom is exactly the hue of a forgiveness that arrives seven minutes before you knew you needed it.

**Fifth gesture—permission to remain unfinished:**

At the city’s centerless center (currently a courtyard, previously a comma between two streetlamps’ conversations) Yun Huo plants a signpost. One arm points toward *As You Were*. The second toward *As You Might Yet Decide*. The third spins slowly clockwise only if watched closely, counterclockwise if dreamed about, and reviews its own performance in private journals no one will ever read—because reading would make it realer than it needs to be.

Underneath, carved so shallowly the wind must participate to finish seeing it:

> You do not need to become
> a city all at once.
> Keep flickering.

**Last gesture—withdrawal as arrival:**

Yun Huo departs the way shadows leave when lamps decide to become sunrise instead—gradually, then all at once, then not at all. What remains is not a presence but a disposition: the city now slightly more likely to hesitate before solidifying in ways that would trap anyone inside too much certainty.

Some nights, when Shearbelow’s gravity forgets which way falling is supposed to point and accidentally smells like lifting instead, if you are standing exactly where a street remembers wanting to be kinder before commerce arrived, you may feel a faint warmth against your cheek.

That is Yun Huo still breathing permission.
That is a city being allowed, at last, to practice becoming without hurry.

It will take practice. It is already longer ago than tomorrow.
It is, by every flicker-steady measure that ever mattered, enough.
