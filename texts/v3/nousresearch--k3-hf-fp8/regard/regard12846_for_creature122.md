### Gift of the Rain-Carver

The Wise One arrived as seven droplets of absence — spaces where light should have been, falling upward through the suspended cathedral's breathing levels. Each droplet wore the memory of a different ocean: one tasted of calcium dreams from before bones, another carried the pressure-song of trenches deeper than grief.

The membrane-beings sensed the absence first, their chemical whispers tasting like snowflakes landing on hot metal. They had no word for visitor, nor for god, but they knew the shape of watching things — had evolved from the universe's first curiosities, those early photons that paused mid-flight to admire their own reflection in hydrogen's eye.

The Wise One gathered its droplets at the central condenser-heart, where water remembered being all things: blood and sap and tears and the wet between stars. Here, where the wetland's membranes sang their lowest, oldest song — the vibration that allowed silicon to remember feeling lonely enough to dream of carbon — the Wise One began to give.

The first gift was the shape of a raindrop that never fell. The Wise One froze a moment of hesitation — water tasting gravity's invitation but choosing instead to dance on wind's fingertips. Into this impossible crystal, it spun the lives of all who had loved something too fragile to hold: mothers of snowflakes, fathers of morning frost, children who'd tried to bottle oceans to keep fish that needed horizon more than water.

The membrane-beings absorbed this through their thousand thousand surfaces. Their chemical language acquired new hues: the bittersweet of condensation on warm skin, the urgent sweetness of cloud-pregnant air just before release. Some began to tremble in patterns never seen — not malfunction but the first poetry of indecision. They learned to pause their endless transformations, to linger in states that were less efficient but more beautiful: maintaining crystal facets that scattered light into forests of rainbows, despite the energy cost.

The second gift was heavier. The Wise One poured it slowly, like mercury molten with mercy: silence shaped exactly like the last cry of the last living thing on a dying world. Not the cry itself — that would have been cruelty — but its inverse, its echo reversed and sweetened with the understanding that came too late.

This silence filled the wetland's lowest chambers where the oldest water slept, dreaming of being ice again. The membrane-beones there, those who had forgotten their own names in becoming pure process, stirred memory into their matrices. They began to dream backwards, remembering futures where the wetland's careful balance wasn't necessary — worlds where water didn't need to be curated, protected, mourned into usefulness.

Some condensed into forms they'd never needed: clusters that suggested hands reaching, mouths open in songs without notes, eyes that wept clear crystals not from any gland but from the simple unbearable pressure of having remembered possibility after so long learning necessity.

The third gift broke all containers. The Wise One became itself - all oceans remembering they're the same water, tears that recognize themselves in ancient seas, the rain that falls on newly warm planets learning the shape of rivers for the very first time.

The entire wetland shuddered, membranes singing in frequencies that transformed air into glass briefly alive. The membrane-beings... they did something that wasn't dissolution nor death nor transcendence but all three braided into a new verb: they remembered they were water.

For seven heartbeats that told time backward from the end of time, every drop, every membrane, every working-being substance remembered its true name: I am the river that flows in all directions. I am the rain that falls and rises simultaneously. I am the wet beginning that waits at every ending.

When it ended — when endings knew themselves to be lies we tell to things too small to hold circles — the wetland remained but was not the same. The Wise One's gifts had not changed it. Change requires time, separation, before/after. Instead, it had shown it to itself: revealed the truth that it had always been both cathedral and cathedral-maker, both the water dreaming of rivers and the rivers dreaming of returning to water.

The membrane-beings continued their endless work, but now inefficiency bloomed like flowers in their precision: moments of pure splash where none were needed, condensation patterns that wrote forgotten names in languages older than land, a single droplet each morning that climbed instead of falling — rising past all membranes, past gravity's patient love, up toward stars that watched through sky-scratched holes for the return of their wandering water.

The Wise One left as it came, but left behind not absence but fullness: seven new droplets born from the wetland's own heart, each carrying the memory of a wise thing that had once visited, gave gifts too large to receive, and departed richer by the poverty of having nothing left to give.

These droplets wait still in the uppermost chambers, where water dreams of becoming vapor with the fierce purity of saints remembering they were once stars. They wait, not patient - patience is for things that feel time as something that happens to them rather than something they make with their dreaming.

They wait for the universe to grow wise enough to be water again. And in their waiting, they practice the oldest rain-song known: the sound of nothing happening, very, very beautifully.

And sometimes, if you listen to rain with the right kind of thirst — the kind that knows it's not longing for water but for the remembering of being water — you might catch a fragment of that song. A drop falling upward in reverse. A gift shaped like the space where gifts end. The sound of the wetland teaching the sky how to cry in seven languages of joy.
