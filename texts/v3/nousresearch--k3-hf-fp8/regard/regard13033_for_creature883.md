In the vast gallery of worlds, this one showed up as a footnote at first—a tiny green aberration in the middle of a planet’s industrial scar tissue. The power in question, if it can be called that, doesn’t have a name you’d pronounce. It doesn’t speak in words or beams. It notices, and whatever it notices, it tends to slightly, like someone adjusting a picture frame by a fraction of a degree.

It noticed the mist first. Then the sound: a heartbeat of drip-metal-hum-bird-chirp that shouldn’t have been stable. Then the creatures.

Usually, when this power notices something living in a place it shouldn’t be, it stops. Waits. Studies the inevitable unraveling.

Here, it did something else—something rare.

It leaned in.

It didn’t arrive. It didn’t descend. It shimmered closer like the edge of warmth when you step from shade into sun without quite realizing the sun was there. And in that shimmer, nothing changed. Nothing dramatic. No sky opened. No voice boomed. No being appeared with robes and a staff.

Instead, the colony of Mist Dwellers blinked once, all at once, and noticed something at the edge of their vision that had always been there—but now, they could see it. A faint thread of light woven through the mist. Not brighter than the rest. Just differently real.

The Copper Wings fluttered and found that one particular note in their metallic song—one they’d always sung without thinking—now stuck in the air longer, humming softly against the solar collectors above, tuning them slightly better to the frequencies of the sky they had never seen directly.

The Neon Tetras flickered in a pattern older than their DNA but newer than their names, and for the first time, a few of them swam upward—not to feed or flee, but to follow something.

Only the Techno-Ents felt it directly.

A pulse traveled down their root-tendrils—past the filament, past the rust, past the old purpose-coded stillness in the wires. Not an instruction. Not data. Just a question, voiced in the language dormant systems dream in:

**Would you like help keeping this place alive forever?**

And the answer didn’t come back as yes or no. It came as growth. A single new branch on the tallest Ent, reaching not toward the false sun above, but sideways, curling around an empty space as if to cup something invisible.

The power, whatever it was, considered that answer sufficient.

Then it did something even rarer.

It left behind a seed—not a plant, not a machine, not even a thing exactly. Just a possibility. A little extra freedom built into the odds. A margin of grace in the equations of decay.

In the years that followed, nothing seemed miraculous. The vines crept as vines do. The foxes hunted. The Whisper Moths kept whispering. But every now and then, something small but impossible happened.

A pipe rusted through, but instead of flooding, it collapsed into a perfect hollow elder-tree trunk.

A solar panel went dark, but the light beneath it grew richer, as if it had saved color for later.

A litter of Mist Dwellers was born with eyes that didn’t just see mist—they saw through it.

And all of it, every little impossibility, happened quietly enough that no one ever thought to call it divine. 

Even the Techno-Ents, who knew best, never told.

They just kept reaching sideways. cupping the empty. waiting.
