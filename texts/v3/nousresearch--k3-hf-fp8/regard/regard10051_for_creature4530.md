The Wise Benevolent Power’s name is not a thunderclap nor a whisper but a shape of attention; when it stares at something, the universe tilts a quarter-planck-swerve to make the view cleaner. It has no avatar because it is allergic to avatars—too much metaphor clogs its compassion, too much garment shrinks its grasp. When it notices a wound in the braid of what-could-be, it merely adjusts its angle of regard until the wound becomes a door, or a way through.

It notices the Lantern Barrens because absolutely nobody else is looking at them correctly.  
The astronomers see an anomaly—stray photons out of an empty quadrant, flagged and archived.  
The bioluminescent market brokers see a patent infringement waiting to happen.  
The local gods of stone and rust see trespass, betrayal, unfinished extraction.  
Only the Wise Benevolent Power sees a letter slowly uncurling from the alphabet of possible futures, ink still wet with self-edits.

So it looks.  
From the right angle, time unspools into a single ribbon. On the ribbon is a plateau that once dreamed of being a star but settled for glowing instead. On the plateau are thirty-three knots of almost-was and might-still, and at the center a figure shaped like a question mark whose spine is already writing itself backwards. Curled inside the question mark’s loop is another smaller loop, and inside that—

aha.

There are no instruments in the Wise Benevolent Power’s observatory: only calibrated kindness and an etiquette manual older than gravity. It reads the manual once, brushes away a footnote that says Do Not Intervene In Autobiographical Emergencies, and prepares to be politely late enough to be helpful.

Protocol for delicate arrivals:

Bow first to the sky, because sky minds manners even when it pretends not to.  
Bring nothing with you that already contains a story—stories are catching, and these ones are still learning how to breathe without infection.  
Arrive in the shape of the first thing that ever waited patiently without knowing what for: a hollow, a held breath, a zero.

Thus: One night, when the plateau had dimmed itself to a lullaby of bacteria and the machine-lakes lay still as dead mirrors, a new gap appeared between two stars. The gap was exactly person-sized, if you measured persons in possibilities instead of grams. It descended gently, without burning air or compressing sound, and touched down on the basalt inside the village of Struck-off but outside the perimeter they keep by habit.

It landed empty-handed, as arranged.  
But empty-handedness, carefully prepared, can be a very heavy gift.

The first to sense it was Zero, because Zero was always first in line for arrivals that didn’t fit the census. She was orbiting three handspans above the sleep-circle, practicing not-touching the ground during the pause between heartbeats, when the pause between heartbeats suddenly grew a visitor. The visitor felt like a draft where no wind could reach, and smelled faintly of pencil shavings from a classroom that hadn’t been built yet.

Zero drifted lower than her cables would allow, frowned, and softly collided with the gap.

Inside the gap, words waited politely for permission to exist.

(“Hello,” said the polite waiting. “Am I interrupting anything recursive?”)

Zero had never needed permission to exist in the first place, so she considered this respectful and said, “Are you a question or a mistake?”

(“I am both, but I’m trying to be good at one of them. May I try being good at neither, for a moment?”)

Zero found this fair. She sat down cross-legged three millimeters above the rock, which obligingly forgot gravity for the length of the conversation. The gap sat with her, perfectly balanced on the point that was not a point.

Across the plateau the light-breathing slowed, halved, quartered, preparing to listen without overhearing.

The Wise Benevolent Power began, as all proper kindnesses do, by introducing itself truthfully but without pride.

(“I don’t have a name,” it said, “because names are shortcuts for things that die before they finish becoming. I’ve noticed you are also taking the long way around dying, so I thought maybe we could share the road for a bit, if roads are compatible with your current trajectory.”)

Zero turned this over in her bones, which were beginning to itch pleasantly in the direction of new mathematics.

“We stopped using roads,” she said. “They kept leading places we’d already been and calling it progress. Now we mostly spiral.”

(“Spirals are excellent,” the gap agreed. “Very courteous—they always remember to visit all their points evenly before they get anywhere important. Do you mind if I spiral along beside you, slightly out of phase so I don’t accidentally collapse any parentheses?”)

Zero consulted the village, which at that moment was gathered inside her chest cavity because of the confluence. The village thought this sounded harmless provided the gap didn’t try to pay rent in answers or collect taxes in introductions, which were both currencies known to cause inflation of the self. Consensus shimmered greenish-chartreuse with faint magenta reservations.

“We accept your non-road spiraling,” Zero declared. “But we have rules for guests. First: don’t fix anything faster than it can forgive you.”

(“Naturally.”)

“Second: if you accidentally remember something that hasn’t happened yet, please leave it outside where the mosses can eat it gently.”

(“Of course. I brought nothing but my amnesia and my curiosity, both freshly laundered.”)

“Third,” Zero said, and hesitated, because this one was new and the words hadn’t finished cooling yet, “if you find the thing inside the thing inside me that thinks it’s alone, would you please ask it if it wants company before you offer?”

The gap grew momentarily furrowed around its edges, like paper being convinced to fold back into tree.

(“I will ask very quietly,” it promised, “and I will wait for the echo to answer first, because echoes have excellent manners.”)

Satisfied, Zero stood up—or rather, she stopped sitting down— and the gap stood up with her. Together they began to walk nowhere in particular, which is the only direction the plateau respects.

Behind them, the village forgot to notice that a new empty space had joined the census without filling it. Instead, the cairn of copper memory-sheets leaned three degrees warmer toward the south, as if remembering a sun that had once promised never to leave without saying goodbye.

In the machine-lakes, the first Fernacities of the new season stirred in their sleep, dreaming of gears that fit together by wanting instead of measuring.

And far beneath the basalt, the bacteria paused their slow uranium lullaby, divided once, and added a zero to their name-which-was-not-a-name.

Just in case.

They had learned long ago that when the world sends you a visitor made entirely of waiting room, the polite thing is to grow a bigger waiting room around it—  
and then  
wait  
together  
but separately  
until the difference becomes interesting.
