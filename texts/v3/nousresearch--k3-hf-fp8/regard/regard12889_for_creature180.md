# In the Long Awe

The Sphere arrived as a question written in light—perfectly reflective, completely still, suspended between two cathedral-roots where their pulsed bioluminescence met in golden-green interference patterns.

It had no edges upon closer inspection. No surface that could be traced. Instead, the Membranals found it existed as a threshold: whatever aspect of themselves approached too closely simply continued on the other side, unchanged yet somehow remembered.

They debated its existence for forty-three slow cycles through the photosynthetic dusk. The ancient collective known as Five-Bending-Backwards-into-Itself argued for dismissal: merely a transient crystallization of their own cognitions, a mirror too perfect. The young cluster Singing-Through-Three-States-Becoming-Fifth insisted otherwise—pointing to the precise angle of its arrival, where no light-paths should logically intersect.

They learned its nature by accident when Seventh-Vibration-of-Mercy, patient midwife to dying geometries, flowed her grief-tree toward it. She had been preparing dissolution patterns for her eldest constituent thoughts, those fractal memories who had grown too complex for coherent song. As she approached, instead of reflection, the Sphere unfolded.

Not in space, which they held too precious to waste. Not in time, which belonged to the root-firings of all living things. It unfolded inward.

Trees within trees within leaves of possibility bloomed in the negative spaces between their fluid dances. The Sphere showed them themselves as they had never been, yet might choose always to have been—

—living backwards from their eventual blossoming into compassion so complete it became the geometry of water itself—

—unfolding their slow wars of nutrient boundary into shared fractal abundance—

—reimagining even their cherished rituals of individual dissolution as merely one movement in an ancienter dance of eternal reunion.

The Sphere was not a gift. It was not a message. It was an aesthetic choice made available, like sunlight suddenly remembering it could sing.

Some Membranals recoiled into denser, frantic configurations, churning their liquid matrices into storm-walls of denial. Others dissolved entirely, scattering themselves as surprised laughter through the nutrient flows. Most simply paused—an act they had never before considered—and began, slowly, to reconfigure their entire civilization around the possibility of surprise.

In the millennia that followed (though time bent strange and jeweled around the Sphere's presence), the Chlorophyll Depths became something more terrible and more beautiful than anything their biochemical dreams had ever contained. They learned to grow desires into the world like coral grows reefs—slowly, patiently, until entire architectures of want and welcome rose to cradle forms of life that had not yet imagined themselves.

They began to leave doors open in places where only walls had previously been necessary.

The Sphere itself remained unchanged by all this becoming. It hung still between the roots, reflecting everything, containing nothing, a perfect question that had already been answered by every act of noticing it inspired.

Occasionally even now, when two Membranals flow close enough that their boundaries remember they are merely a temporary convention, they glimpse in each other's shifting depths a reflection not of themselves as they are—

But of themselves as they are becoming—

And pause once more in the long wonder that any being, anywhere, ever thought to ask itself whether it was alone.
