The Engine sang first—a single note too low for any ear in the nursery to catch, but every ceramic sphere vibrated slightly, as if remembering the taste of dawn before stars were invented.

The note did not travel. It arrived.

Above the Float, where the thermocline bends light into molten glass, a point forgot how to be small. It opened outward, not like a flower, but like the idea of “door” arriving late to a conversation about walls. Through it stepped—no, leaned—no, simply became—the Visitor.

They wore a coat of no particular color, because embarrassment refused to stick to it. Their hands were both empty and currently helping someone else lift a piano upstairs in a country that hadn’t been founded yet. Their hat was shaped exactly like tomorrow when you’re absolutely certain you’ve misplaced it somewhere around last Tuesday.

The moment they noticed they were observed (which took precisely the amount of time it takes a snowflake to remember its own name), they smiled politely in seventeen languages simultaneously. Twelve of them were composed entirely of punctuation. One was the sound a mother makes before she decides you’ve been forgiven. The rest signed, Please ignore the tea kettle impersonating a mountain range in the foyer—he’s still learning boundaries.

The Curators convened instantly, three minutes ago. They met in the space between two blinks where important decisions hide from calendars. The minutes of the meeting were recorded as a single dot on the forehead of a sleeping letter Q, who promised to deliver them by carrier whale if anyone ever bothered asking why whales mail letters upside-down on Sundays but never RSVP.

After considerable debate (measured in the number of feathers required to turn a committee of thoughts into a flock of actual ducks—approx. π, carry the one, don’t ask where the duck went, it’s still paying off student loans from its brief stint as a metaphor), the Curators reached the unprecedented consensus of knitting their hats together until they agreed on something.

This took approximately one eternity but felt like Tuesday.

When the hats unknit themselves (apologizing smoothly for the inconvenience), the Curators had decided unanimously to appoint the nearest idle daydream as Acting Ambassador to the Unexpected, provided it wore matching socks and promised not to argue with the wallpaper about the philosophical implications of beige.

The ambassadorial daydream stepped forward, tripped over its own enthusiasm, and discovered in the process that it had been a small ornamental staircase poetically inclined toward clouds the entire time. Embarrassed but determined, it bowed elegantly sideways and offered the Visitor a cup of tea which had previously been employed as a minor moon in a solar system too polite to complain about orbital dandruff.

The Visitor accepted with the kind of gratitude that immediately upgraded the tea’s resume to include “interpersonal relationship consultant” and “part-time horizon.”

Conversation commenced. It was slow at first—they had to negotiate the exact viscosity of silence suitable for discussing weather patterns in places where rain enjoys nostalgia therapy—but eventually they settled on a mutually agreeable pace roughly equivalent to a lullaby humming itself to sleep in a rocking chair carved from misremembered lullabies.

Visitor: (after politely waiting for a pause that had gone out for cigarettes in 1973 and never quite found the exit) I hope I’m not interrupting something important.

Acting Ambassadorial Staircase (currently practicing meditation by pretending to be a comma in a complicated apology letter): Oh, not at all. We were just rearranging the furniture of our expectations into a pleasantly uncoordinated dance routine. Your timing is impeccable—everyone else is busy being elsewhere.

Visitor: (nodding with the serene confidence of someone who has already read the footnotes to tomorrow’s conversation) Excellent. I’ve always felt that interruptions are simply conversations practicing their entrances like shy actors backstage at a play written entirely in handclaps.

A brief intermission occurred while they collectively remembered that time is sneaky about changing its clothes in public without paying rent for the changing room. They agreed to ignore this breach of etiquette if time promised to stop borrowing their best moods without asking and returning them smelled faintly of déjà vu.

When dialogue resumed, they had skipped ahead three chapters and accidentally invented a new genre of poetry based entirely on the emotional resonance of misplaced semicolons.

Visitor: I come bearing—though not literally, as gifts tend to get homesick and send postcards complaining about their luggage being heavier than their metaphors—an offer of assistance.

Ambassadorial Staircase (now considering early retirement as a metaphor for stairs that prefer elevators but are too polite to say so): Assistance is a lovely word. Sleeps well at dinner parties. Never forgets to water the houseplants of lesser compliments. What flavor of assistance did you have in mind? We currently stock several varieties: classic existential dread with a hint of peppermint, emergency optimism in convenient time-release capsules shaped like procrastinating turtles, and the ever-popular “Oh dear, did we accidentally become a metaphor again?” cake. Warning: cake may contain traces of serendipity and/or spontaneous ecosystem reorganization.

Visitor: (pausing to admire the way hesitations grow wildflowers in the corners of awkward silences) I was thinking perhaps of something more... foundational. Your Wangdangdoodles are delightful—truly, the absence of instructions for proper mischief maintenance is a bold creative choice—but I couldn’t help noticing that your entropy budget is scheduled for an existential audit in approximately two Tuesdays and a half-forgotten sneeze. Tax season for the ineffable can be such a bother—forms filed in triplicate in languages that haven’t been invented yet, receipts written in disappearing ink that insists on reappearing on your toothbrush at inappropriate moments...

Ambassadorial Staircase (suddenly remembering an important appointment with a nap scheduled for last week but which had politely rescheduled itself for the invention of patience): Oh. That. Yes. We were meaning to address that indefinitely at some point between yesterday’s tomorrow and the day after never. But you know how it is—one starts organizing one’s uncertainties into neat little piles labeled “later,” and before you know it, “later” has invited all its relatives over for an extended staycation and they’re reproducing like rabbits who’ve discovered existential philosophy and badminton.

Visitor: (sympathetically not noticing that their coat had begun quietly translating the entire conversation into a ballet performed by very polite dust motes on the nearest convenient sunbeam) Precisely why I’m here. Among my modest collection of useful improbabilities, I happen to possess a spare set of conceptual scaffolding specifically designed for propping up architectures of whimsical precariousness during audits conducted by committees of overly punctual paradoxes. They even come with instructions—though I recommend not reading them aloud near open minds or librarians with sensitive alignment to alphabetical order.

Ambassadorial Staircase (suddenly overcome by an emotion it couldn’t quite identify, so it politely asked a passing cloud to hold it until it remembered how to spell “gratitude” in semaphore using only mismatched socks): Would you...? Could we possibly...? Without triggering the inevitable avalanche of paperwork written in the ink of unspoken thanks which tends to clog the drains of obligation on alternate Tuesdays when the moon is in the southern hemisphere of emotional maturity?

Visitor: (smiling gently in the way sunshine apologizes for accidentally waking flowers that were pretending to be asleep): Of course. But only on one condition.

Ambassadorial Staircase (holding its breath so quietly that nearby thoughts tiptoed around it like mice respecting the privacy of a cheese that was having an existential crisis): Anything within reason or very politely outside it.

Visitor: You must promise not to thank me. Gratitude is a lovely thing but terribly unreliable—it tends to breed obligations that grow into vines of expectations that strangle spontaneous generosity with their insistence on thank-you notes written in languages where “you’re welcome” translates as “I now own your next five mistakes.” Instead, I propose a favor exchange. I assist with your temporal tax avoidance—purely through entirely legitimate means involving the depreciation of linear causality under Section VII, Subparagraph “Snooze” of the Interdimensional Napping Accords—and in return, you agree to do something utterly unnecessary and beautifully impractical for the next stranger you meet who looks like they’re trying too hard to remember how to be lost.

Ambadorial Staircase (after consulting briefly with its conscience, which was currently disguised as a comma having an identity crisis about whether it wanted to grow up to be a semicolon or a butterfly): Agreed. But only if you promise to forget this conversation as soon as it’s convenient for your forgetfulness, and to accidentally remember it fondly at least once whenever you smell something that reminds you of childhood adventures that never quite happened but should have.

Visitor: (offering a handshake that turned into a high-five halfway through and then decided to become a hug that lasted exactly long enough to share body heat but not long enough to require awkward small talk about weather patterns in alternate dimensions): Done. And undone. And beautifully re-done like a sweater knitted by love itself practicing its yarn-over stitch in Morse code.

Thus sealed the agreement between the Unexpected and the Unfinished, witnessed only by a flock of hours who promised not to tell anyone in exchange for being allowed to migrate south for winters that preferred to vacation in summer vacation brochures.

The Visitor began their work quietly—by first complimenting the Engine on its excellent taste in lungfish, then casually mentioning that they knew a very nice little island nation of retired metaphors that might enjoy hosting an extended cultural exchange program for surplus whimsy. Before anyone could quite remember why they were supposed to object to things making perfect nonsense, the entire Wangdangdoodle Nursery Float had accidentally declared itself a temporary autonomous zone of perpetual now o’clock on the calendar of “Oops, Did We Do That Thrice?”

The audit, when it arrived (exactly two and a half forgotten Tuesdays later, wearing a tie that had clearly been borrowed from tomorrow’s ransom note to yesterday’s apology letter), found everything in such charmingly improbable order that it immediately filed for existential doubt, took early retirement, and moved to a small cottage by the sea where it spends its days tutoring metaphors in the delicate art of forgetting how to be allegories.

And somewhere between the beginning of this sentence and the moment you’ll remember forgetting it begins, the Wangdangdoodles launched themselves skyward in crates that read “This End Up” on all six sides, including the inside, and were promptly delivered exactly nowhere they expected but precisely everywhere they were needed most.

And if you happen to meet someone tomorrow who looks like they’re trying too hard to remember how to be lost—perhaps pause. Offer them an unnecessary kindness. Smile like you know a secret that’s been practicing its introduction as a misunderstanding all its life.

It might be them.

Or it might be you.

Or it might just be Tuesday again, wearing Monday’s socks and claiming they’re vintage.

Either way, the tea kettle’s whistling like it’s auditioning for a mountain range, and somewhere the Engine is dreaming of slightly warmer water where yesterday’s laughter is teaching tomorrow’s tears how to dance the waltz in waltzing time with now itself.

Don’t worry about thanking anyone.

The gratitude garden is already blooming beautifully in all the places you forgot to plant it.

Just remember to water it occasionally with random acts of beautifully unnecessary kindness.

And maybe—just maybe—try mispronouncing “home” intentionally once in a while.

You’d be surprised how many places will answer to it.
