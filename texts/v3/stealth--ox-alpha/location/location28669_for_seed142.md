**The Level**

You will notice the warmth before anything else, because there is nothing else to notice at first. The dark here is not the dark of evening; it is structural. It goes down into the ground and up into the sky and it does not intend to lift. But the ground is warm through your boot soles — evenly, patiently warm, like a hearthstone hours after the fire — and under the silence there is a tone, so low it is less a sound than a pressure behind the sternum. The air tastes of hot stone, graphite, a faint mineral sourness. It is dry in a way that seems final, as though dryness here were not a condition but an achievement.

This is the Level: a high plateau worn down to a whisper of itself. The air is thin enough to remind you of altitude, and the land has spent whatever elevation it once had. There are no peaks left, no cliffs, no young angles anywhere. What remains is a plain with swells in it the width of counties and the height of a handshake — the buried roots of mountains, showing through like bones under skin. The erosion here did not merely proceed. It *finished*. Every slope has found its last possible angle and accepted it. Every stone has been rounded to the shape it will keep. You are not looking at a ruined landscape. You are looking at a completed one.

Overhead, the sky is black and pinned. The stars do not move. The constellations hang in their old addresses, fixed as rivets, and navigation is trivially easy and telling time is impossible. Near the horizon — if you can call that suggestion of deeper darkness a horizon — hangs the moon, or the thing everyone agrees to call the moon: half-full, motionless, at the same position every night of every year, forever deciding whether to wane. Whether a sun ever existed here, the Level does not say. The warmth comes up from below. That is all anyone can confirm.

A road finds you before you find it. The glowroads are paved with crushed phosphoric tailings, and they shine with the dim foxfire green of old instrument dials — just enough light to place your feet, never enough to cast a shadow. They run dead straight across the swells, converging on stations a day's walk and more apart, and between the stations the darkness resumes like deep water.
