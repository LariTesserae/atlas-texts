# The Pocket at the Summit

To understand the room, you have to understand the climb — briefly, because the climb belongs to another world. Above the last of the valleys the rock turns young. Whole ridgelines here have the look of things set down yesterday: slopes with no soil, no gullies, no scree-cones at their feet, stone still carrying the smoothness of having cooled. The dome at the top catches a wind that has crossed nothing but sky, and the cold of that wind is enormous, and it is the last cold thing you will feel for a long time.

Near the crown of the dome there is a seam, body-narrow, breathing warm air outward against the wind. Hardwood pegs stand in drilled holes beside it, their surfaces polished to the sheen of old bone by hands gripping them. Whoever drove those pegs had no metal. Whoever drives them now, if anyone does, still doesn't.

You go in sideways. The seam narrows, widens, and opens —

— into warmth, and into darkness.

## The Dark

Begin with the dark, because the dark is the largest thing in the room. It is total. Not dusk, not dimness, not the deep-but-adjustable dark of caves — adjustment accomplishes nothing here. There is nothing to adjust to. After some minutes the eye gives up its claims entirely, and you stop being a seeing animal in a dark place and become a touching animal in a small one.

And the dark is warm. That is its second nature. It lies on the skin with weight, like water almost thick enough to notice, like the inside of something. The air is saturated — every breath is part drink — and within moments of entering you feel the moisture begin its work on you: scalp damp, sleeves clinging, the fine hairs of your arms beading.

The room itself is pocket-small. Arms out, fingertips nearly graze opposite walls. Ceiling low enough that standing tall puts your hand flat against it. Three paces by four, perhaps — dimensions you will never confirm by sight and come to know instead the way you know the size of your own chest.

## The Young Stone

Every surface is new.

Run a palm along the wall and it comes away dusty — not gritty, not worn, but powdery, the texture of a thing that has never once been rained on, though rain is happening here constantly, in a sense, from the inside. The stone has not settled on its grain. Here a patch is dense and ringing under a knuckle; a hand-span away the same wall yields slightly, pumice-soft, undecided. The rock is still becoming rock. Press a thumbnail in and the surface gives — and holds, unmarked, giving without recording, the way water gives.

It is all faintly, evenly warm. Blood-warm. The heat of the dome's young heart comes up through everything, steady as a heartbeat that never speeds.

And everything weeps. This is the room's great fact. The walls sweat in a fine perpetual exhalation; beads gather along the ceilings and let go at long intervals; and from a dozen seams hang straws of stone — thin as reeds, wet-shining under the fingertip — each one delivering a slow bright rope of water down into the dark. The air is so crowded with moisture that the room feels fuller than its size should allow: water in the air, water on every surface, water in transit, water waiting. Dense. Layered. Floor-pool, wall-rills, ceiling-straws, suspended fog — a column of wetness packed edge to edge.

## The Pool

At the room's center the floor dips to a pool barely broader than a bed, and its surface is the closest thing the room has to an event.

*Plink.* A long silence. *Plink* — a second voice, lower, offset from the first like two notes of a clock that tells no hour. Each drop raises a ring; the rings cross and cancel; and the pool's level never rises. Somewhere beneath, the mountain drinks exactly what is given, and once in a long while you feel rather than hear it — a deep, slow *swallow* under the floor, tectonic in its patience.

The acoustics are close and soft. The dark eats sound the way wool eats it. Your own breathing arrives back at you immediately and privately. Your blood becomes audible. Once, from somewhere in the walls, a single bead ticks loose and falls for a surprisingly long time.

Underneath the dripping there is a tone. Very low, more felt than heard, coming out of the stone itself — the sound, perhaps, of all that trapped heat escaping its billion small chambers at once. The room hums to itself, contentedly, at the bottom of hearing.

## What Lives

Almost nothing. Which is its own kind of statement.

In three seams of damper stone grow pale sprouts — rootless, white as candle-wick, seven or eight stems in all, each leaf-tip curled around a single bead of water that swells, trembles, and does not fall. The sprouts are neither growing nor dying. They give the room its only green smell, faint and bitter, a rumor of leaves in all this mineral wet.

And in the shallows at the pool's edge lies the animal. Pale as the sprouts, smooth, the size of two fists, shaped like something between a fish and a folded hand. Its skin is thin enough that a slow pulse shows beneath it — one beat, then a long darkness, then another. It breathes about once a minute. It does not flee when touched; the flesh is cool-slippery and utterly calm. And here is the small wrongness you will keep turning over afterward: wherever you stand, it is facing you. Move around the pool, silently, and its head has turned to follow. It has never needed eyes. It knows where warmth is.

Once, and only once, something small skittered across stone behind you — three ticks of sound — and was never heard again, and no search of the walls ever finds a crack big enough to have held it.

## The Marks

Hands have been here.

Four low steps descend to the pool, pecked into the rock — bashed, ground, patiently hollowed with a harder stone against a softer one, no metal anywhere in the work. Their risers are shallow. Feet smaller than yours made these. Beside the pool, a shelf has been levered level, and on it stand three cups, each hollowed by the same patient pecking, each positioned precisely beneath a straw, each brim-full — the water standing in a trembling meniscus at the exact lip, never spilling, never sinking.

One wall carries five long grooves, dragged by fingers, side by side: a tally. Visits, or years, or sorrows — the count stops in the middle of the fifth line, and below it, where it fell or was set down or slipped from a hand, sits a hammerstone. Round, comfortable in the palm, waiting. Nearby, a small niche holds a crust of ancient soot, the only black thing in the room — someone carried fire into this wet dark, once, and the dark kept the evidence.

The strangest part is how unworn it all is. Steps that should be glossy from generations of feet are matte and fresh. The tally-grooves have crisp edges. As if the whole visit — fire, cups, counting, the dropped stone — happened this morning. Or as if it is happening now. Or as if it will happen, always, at exactly this degree of almost.

## The Rules

This is a place at equilibrium, and the equilibrium is absolute, and it extends past the water and the warmth into physics itself.

Along the east wall a rill of water runs — uphill. A thumb-width of climbing water, moving steadily against every law, and it has always done this, calmly, as if gravity were a habit the room never got around to adopting. Snap one of the hanging straws and it is whole again before your hand withdraws, restored to the same length, dripping at the same pace. Make footprints in the pale mud by the pool and turn around: they are filling from beneath, the mud rising smoothly to erase them, water finding its level, the room declining to remember.

Nothing here erodes, because wearing-down takes difference — gradients, time, one moment leaning on the next — and this room refuses all of it. Nothing grows, either. The straws have been *about to touch* the pool for longer than most mountains have existed. The sprouts have held their beads. The animal has lain in its shallows, pulse and pause, pulse and pause. The room is not frozen; frozen implies a before. It is simply finished beginning and declined to continue — a first morning sustained past the lifespan of empires, still warm from its own birth, still wet from it, everything mid-emergence and nothing emerging.

Even you, it balances away. Your breath adds humidity to the air; an equal amount leaves somewhere you cannot find. Your body heat joins the room's and is indistinguishable within minutes. You shout — the dark folds the sound shut. The room accepts your presence the way the pool accepts its drops, and remains, in every measurable respect, unchanged.

What you carry out is only this: the knowledge of a place where the world is still wet from being made — and where the making, gently, infinitely, declines to finish.
