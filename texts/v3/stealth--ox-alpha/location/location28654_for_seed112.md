# THE ORGAN-LOFT

You hear it before there is anything to see. From the last stretch of the ascent, while the rim is still a wall of pale stone above you, the sound arrives: a rolling, sourceless percussion, like a siege on the other side of a hill, punctuated by long tearing cracks and under all of it a bass drone that you feel in your molars before your ears decide it exists. Then the ramp tops out, and the Loft opens.

It runs to every horizon, and past them. This is the top of the world — a tableland raised so high and so wide that the horizon itself bends, faintly, the way large things bend. Off along the southern rim you can find the escarpment's edge and look down, through two thousand fathoms of bright thinning air, at a deck of cloud belonging to some lower, softer country. Up here the air is cool and thin and glittering. Your breath ghosts even at noon. The light is ferocious — white, hard-edged, dust-veiled so that distance turns to layers of blue-white glass — but it carries almost no heat. Light without weight. The cold is in the wind, and the wind is always going somewhere.

And the plain itself: white to the point of pain, a mineral glare of salt-crusted gravel and bleached dust — mottled everywhere with vast irregular patches of dark violet-black, as if something enormous had been burned here and there, or buried here and there, or *born* here and there. Scattered across the whole of it, sparse as teeth in an old jaw, rise the verticals: dark towers, smooth fluted poles, squat swollen barrels, each standing alone in its own acre of emptiness, each trailing smoke or throwing sparks or, at unpredictable intervals, firing.

The emptiness is not absence. On the Loft, emptiness is the primary material. You can walk two full days and meet four verticals. The distances between things are measured in discharges heard: *two booms to the nearest stack, half a day to the smoking ridge.* Everything that exists here exists separately, surrounded by room, and the room is loud.

---

Begin with the ground, because the ground changes everything.

It is alive. Not dotted with life, not clothed in it — alive the way a flank is alive. The dark patches are hide: a continuous living crust, thin and tough and faintly warm, stretched over the mineral bone of the plateau. Walk on the white and your boots crunch salt-gravel. Step onto the dark and the floor gives — softly, firmly, like the hide of a sleeping animal — and when you look back your prints are already shallowing. By dusk they are gone. Cut it, and it bleeds a bead of green resin that seals itself like wax. It heals the way skin heals: completely, and without comment.

The mottling is the hide's own history — pale *scalds* where lightning has killed it or herds have mown it, dark regrown stretches, scar-shapes so old they have names in no one's language because there is no one to name them. The map of these wounds predates any nation below the rim. Nothing here is new except the hour.

And here is the thing to understand before anything else: **nothing on the Loft has drunk in a million years.** There is no dew. No seep, no mud, no fog, no rain that finishes. The storms that walk the plateau are dry — dust and lightning and wind, thunder without a drop in it. Green is not the color of water here. It is the color of patience. Every living thing on the Shelf eats light, breathes stone, and drinks nothing, and its flesh is accordingly wrong: leather, horn, iron, wax. Crush a sprig and it smells like bruised leaves and gunsmoke. Its chemistry is its own affair.

---

The towers are called stacks, and each one is a refinery that grew.

They stand thirty, sixty, a hundred yards tall — fluted, buttress-rooted, barked in true cold iron (they smelt it themselves out of the ground; their roots are mineshafts going down further than anyone has followed). Each stack runs a fire in its heart. Not a metaphorical fire. An actual flame, a tame combustion kept in a lined chamber and fed oil the tree secretes for exactly this purpose, tended the way a body tends its own stomach acid. The heat rises through the trunk and exits the crown vents as a steady exhale of dark smoke, so that the Loft's skyline, at any distance, is a scatter of slow black columns standing in the white air.
