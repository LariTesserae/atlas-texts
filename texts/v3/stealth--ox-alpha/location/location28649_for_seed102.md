# The Reservoir

You reach it through a seam in the rock, a cleft barely wide enough for your ribs, and the first thing that greets you is not light or smell but *pressure* — a deep push against your sternum, as if the mountain were pressing one warm thumb against your chest. Then the seam opens, and you step down into the chamber, and you understand that the pressure is a sound. It has been sounding the whole time. You suspect it has been sounding the whole time for a very long definition of *whole*.

The chamber is small — forty paces across at its widest, domed low, floored with fine pale dust — but it holds its size generously, the way a held breath does. Most of it is empty: bare warm floor, open air, long clean sightlines running wall to wall. What occupies it occupies it in scattered islands — a grove here, a glow there, a heap of worked stone — each surrounded by its own margin of emptiness, like exhibits in an unlit gallery. The stone overhead is veined with mineral light, amber and bruise-green, and the light does not flicker. Nothing about it wavers, dims, or breathes. It burns the way a painted flame burns: absolutely, at one setting, forever. Between the veins the dark pools undisturbed. Your eyes adjust and find the room lit like a room at dusk where every candle has been replaced by a small decision never to go out.

And the air is full of dust. Not falling dust — circling dust. Every mote in the chamber travels a small tight circle, thousands of them, a slow glitter of microscopic orbits, so that wherever a vein-light passes through the air you see it threaded with tiny turning sparks. It takes you longer than it should to understand why they never land.

Because nothing here lands. Nothing here finishes.

The sound — you come to call it the Note, in your head, with a capital you can't justify — is loud. Not ambient, not distant: it fills the room to its corners and presses on you from every side, a chord with the mass of weather. At its base is a fundamental so deep you feel it in your teeth and the hinges of your jaw, and you eventually locate its source, though locating it helps nothing. Above that lie middle voices, round and ringing, like fingertips run around the rims of glass, and above those a high shimmer, the rustle of a thousand stiff reeds all flexing at once. It should be chaos. It is the opposite of chaos. It never varies. Listen for ten minutes or an hour — you will lose the ability to tell those apart — and the Note repeats nothing, because it never ends in order to repeat. It simply *is*, at full volume, the way a stone is.

There is no water here. You look for it the way any creature looks — damp corners, seeps, the dark undersides of things — and you find instead the salts: broad pans of the floor crusted white, grown with feather-crystals so delicate and so unworn that they seem to have finished forming a moment ago. In a sense they did. These are the evaporites of some last ancient moisture, the fingerprints of water that left before whatever happened here happened, and not one crystal has dulled since. Yet nothing in the chamber is thirsty. You will notice this slowly, and it will unsettle you more than the dark: the reeds stand fat and turgid, the moth-wings are supple, your own throat, oddly, does not ache. Whatever thirst is quenched in this place, it is not quenched the way thirst is quenched elsewhere. The reeds drink the Note. On a stone lintel by the far wall someone has carved a word in a script you almost — almost — read. The shape of it, the weight of it, tells you enough. *Reservoir.* Not of water. Of this.

The reeds deserve their description. They grow in one dense grove against the eastern curve — tall translucent stalks ribbed like organ pipes, gold-green, each crowned with a trumpet-flower caught at the exact fullness of bloom, petals flung wide, stamens mid-offering. The grove is lush the way a chord is lush: layered, opulent, complete. No leaf has ever fallen here; the ground beneath the grove is clean as a swept floor, and every stalk's edges are knife-crisp, young, unweathered, as though the whole thicket broke the soil that morning. Where the Note passes through the stalks, they glow — and it passes through all of them constantly — so each reed carries bands of brightness at fixed intervals up its length, standing waves made visible, the room's music written on the room's plants in light.

The fauna arrive gradually, once you know how to look. Around each knot of mineral glow on the walls hang turnmoths — pale, four-winged, the size of your palm — and each moth flies a fixed ellipse around its globe, never straying, never landing, wingbeats phased so precisely to the Note that their flight adds an overtone rather than a flutter. Flat-bodied sippers the color of raw clay lie belly-down on the ringing stones, legs tucked, absorbing; they have no mouths you can find and seem to need none. In the trumpets sits nectar at perpetual fullness — every flower brimming, every sip taken instantly restored, the whole grove a banquet eternally about to be eaten.

And around the chamber's perimeter runs a trackway. It is engraved in the dust like a fossil path — thousands of prints, deep and exact — and along it walks a procession: heavy, woolly, gray-eyed grazer-things, nose to tail, each animal's stride landing precisely in prints that fit it as if cast from its own feet. Because they were cast from its own feet. They are walking in their own footprints, and have been, and the prints came first. You try one, experimentally, fitting your boot into a groove. It fits you too. All of them fit you. You step back out quickly and do not dwell on what that means about the shapes of paths in this place.

But the source — you should meet the Singer.

Off-center, on a low mound of the same pale stone, lies something vast and folded: a gray-skinned bulk the size of an ox seal crossed with something older, throat swollen into a ridged drum, jaw dropped open toward the dome. From that open mouth comes the fundamental. The whole floor-deep voice of the chamber, the pressure you mistook for a thumb on your chest — it is a song, held. The Singer's eyes are neither open nor shut; its lungs neither fill nor empty; it is caught precisely between inhale and exhale, between sleep and singing, which here amount to the same condition. You cannot tell if it is blessed. You cannot tell if it is trapped. Along one wall, a row of dry-stone alcoves — nest-shaped, pillow-flat, clearly beds — every one of them faces the mound. Whoever slept here slept facing their god's one sustained syllable.

The builders are gone, or appear to be, but they left their work mid-sentence. A wall of stacked dry stone stands unfinished at shoulder height, its capstone leaning ready against the pile, and it will lean there forever, because completion is a kind of change and this place does not permit change. At a knapping floor nearby lie chert flakes and hammerstones where they fell — except one flake does not lie. It hangs. A single chip of stone, suspended a finger's width above the striking surface, one inch into its fall, and it will fall that inch for as long as there is an *is*. The rule, once you see it, is almost elegant: here, every motion must return to its beginning. An orbit closes, so the moths may fly. A fall does not close, so the chip may not finish falling. Even the inscriptions obey it — spirals, tallies, and in three places the same carved word, cut confidently to its final letter and there abandoned, the chisel lifted, the last stroke missing. You begin to suspect the builders were transcribing something in the Note's upper overtones, a near-language riding the shimmer, a sentence almost sayable. Every attempt, you notice, fails at the same word.

Sit for a while. The stone where you sit is already warm in the shape of you. Walk the margin and find your own footprints ahead of you in the dust — yours, exact, leading the way to places you have not yet decided to go. Speak, and hear your echo arrive a breath early. It takes a moment to understand, and then you understand it completely: nothing here changes, and you are here. Your visit is part of the equilibrium. Somewhere in the permanent now of this chamber, you are permanently arriving — squeezing through the seam, pressing your palm to the warm salt, fitting your foot to the engraved path — and always will be.

Leaving is easy; the seam was never hidden. Climb back through it and the outside hits you like cold water: ragged wind, an insect stuttering out of rhythm, somewhere a drip of actual water falling *irregularly* — falling and landing, landing and vanishing — and the sheer messy contingency of it nearly brings you to your knees. You will be glad of it within an hour. But your pulse will keep the Note's meter for days, and far below and behind you the chamber will go on holding its one moment, steady-lit, thunderously quiet in its loudness, young as the hour it was struck — and in it, forever, you are just now walking in.
