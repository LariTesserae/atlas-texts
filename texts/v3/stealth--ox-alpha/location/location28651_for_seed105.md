There is no surface here. There is a Roof.

Understand the difference. A surface is a place where a world ends and another begins, a membrane, a door. The Roof is a ceiling — a plain of ice hundreds of meters overhead, kilometer after kilometer of it, going grey-green into the distance like the underside of a sky that has hardened. It glows faintly, sourcelessly, a light with no sun behind it, brightening and dimming according to weather that no one down here has ever seen. Beneath it: black water, cold almost to its own freezing, so cold it has thickness, so that moving through it is like moving through night that resists.

This is a sea that lies over a drowned country.

The country was high land once — a massif of ridges and hanging valleys, field-walled, terraced, inhabited. The water came up over it slowly, in stages, and the stages are still legible: three great benches wrapped around the flanks of the range, each one an old shoreline, each one a tier of the drowning. The lowest bench is ancient, its retaining walls rounded like knuckles under mature silt. The highest is recent — recent enough that its field-walls still hold their sharp edges beneath only a thin skirt of sediment, and one wall on the middle terrace stops mid-course, the lower stones fitted cleanly, the upper ones roughed and waiting, never laid. The water took the mason's afternoon. Now the taking is over, and has been for longer than grief lasts. The stone has been smoothed. The silt has ripened. Geology has accepted the arrangement.

Between the ridges lie the plains — basins of flour-fine sediment, olive-grey, soft as breath. Put your hand — if a hand were here — into the floor of a basin and it would come up tasting of centuries. The plains are made of the gardens. They are the compressed sheddings of ten thousand years of frond and blade and lace, settled layer on layer. There are no shells in this ground. There is nothing in this ground that ever had bones, or eyes, or a mouth. Nothing here eats. Nothing is eaten. Whatever food web exists runs on warmth and the Roof's dim light, and death arrives only by cold, by burial, by age. The ecosystem has no predators the way the plains have no stones: it simply never had any to lose. You walk — if you walk — on the compost of light alone.

Which is why the emptiness between things is so complete. The gardens do not carpet this country; they *occur* in it, in islands, in refugia, with days of featureless plain between them. Visibility ends at fifty meters. Sound goes on for forty miles. The ratio governs everything.

The gardens themselves are lush the way a struck bell is loud: totally, and only in their own place. They stand where warmth seeps from the rock — hydrothermal threads rising through fissures, faintly mineral, faintly blood-warm — and where the melt-halos open beneath the welded summits. Their architecture is vertical, because their light is vertical. Whip-fronds launch from the seafloor on stipes like anchor cables, sixty, eighty meters of them, and spread at the top into canopies that hang in the black like layered streets of foliage. Lantern-bladders climb among them, gas-filled organs on long tethers, hoisting their leaf-fans nearer the Roof's glow, adjusting their buoyancy with small interior explosions. Stone-lace crusting the rocks burns a blue so weak it is more idea than color. Scaleweed presses to the current-facing sides of boulders like chainmail.

And all of it turns. Nothing here has eyes, and everything turns.

It turns toward warmth, because warmth is the rarest signal in this world — rarer than light. A seep is a hearth; the gardens arrange themselves around their hearths the way a household once arranged itself around fire. Which means that a warm body in this water becomes an event. If you hung in the black above a plain, in your little fever of thirty-seven degrees, the nearest whip-fronds would begin to lean. Slowly — over minutes, with a creak of stipe — the whole garden would orient toward you, canopy by canopy, a forest turning its face. It would not be aggression.
