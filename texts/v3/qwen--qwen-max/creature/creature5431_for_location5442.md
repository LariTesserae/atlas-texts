In this ancient, constructed landscape, the inhabitants are few and far between, yet those that do exist are deeply intertwined with the environment, having evolved to embody its harsh, unyielding nature. The most prominent of these creatures are the **Dustwalkers**.

### Dustwalkers

**Physical Description:**
Dustwalkers are a species of hardy, bipedal beings, standing at an average height of 1.5 to 2 meters. Their bodies are sleek and streamlined, with a chitinous exoskeleton that ranges in color from a deep, earthy brown to a dusty, almost silvery gray. This exoskeleton is segmented, allowing for a range of motion, and is punctuated by a series of small, thorny protrusions that serve as both armor and a means of defense. Their limbs are long and slender, with sharp, claw-like digits that are perfect for gripping the rough, unpolished stones and loose, gritty soil. Their heads are small and elongated, with large, black, multifaceted eyes that are capable of seeing in the harsh, unfiltered sunlight. Their mouthparts are simple and efficient, designed for both eating and conserving water.

**Behavior and Adaptations:**
Dustwalkers are highly adapted to their environment, both physically and behaviorally. They are primarily nocturnal, emerging from their burrows when the heat of the day wanes, and retreating back into the earth as the sun rises. Their burrows are a marvel of engineering, spiraling deep into the ground, with multiple chambers for resting, storing water, and even small, makeshift nests for their young. The burrows are lined with a substance secreted from their exoskeleton, which helps to stabilize the loose soil and prevent collapse.

**Survival Strategies:**
Survival for the Dustwalkers is a constant struggle, but they have developed a range of strategies to cope with the relentless conditions. They have a highly efficient system for water conservation, with their bodies capable of reabsorbing nearly all the water from their waste. When the rare, gentle rains come, they collect and store water in specialized, expandable pouches within their bodies. They also have a keen sense of smell, which allows them to detect the faintest traces of water, even from great distances.

**Diet and Foraging:**
Dustwalkers are omnivorous, though their diet is primarily composed of the hardy, thorny plants that dot the landscape. They are adept at breaking through the tough, leathery leaves and extracting the precious water and nutrients. They also feed on small, hardy insects and other invertebrates that manage to survive in the harsh conditions. Their sharp, claw-like digits are perfect for digging and extracting their food, and their simple, efficient mouthparts allow them to process and store the water and nutrients they need.

**Social Structure:**
Dustwalkers are generally solitary, but they do form loose, temporary communities, especially during the rare, life-giving rains. These communities are based on a shared water source, and the Dustwalkers will work together to collect and store as much water as possible. They communicate through a series of low, rumbling sounds, which can travel through the ground and air, allowing them to coordinate their efforts and warn each other of potential dangers. Despite their solitary nature, they are capable of forming strong, albeit temporary, bonds with one another, particularly in times of need.

### Other Inhabitants

While the Dustwalkers are the most prominent inhabitants, there are other, smaller, and more elusive creatures that also call this landscape home.

**Thornbacks:**
Thornbacks are small, hardy insects, about the size of a human hand. They have a tough, thorny exoskeleton and are capable of surviving in the harshest conditions. They feed on the thorny plants and are often found in the same areas as the Dustwalkers, though they are more elusive and rarely seen. Their exoskeletons are a deep, almost iridescent green, and they have a unique ability to go for long periods without water, surviving on the moisture they extract from the plants.

**Grit-Snakes:**
Grit-Snakes are small, burrowing serpents, about a meter in length. They have a rough, sandpapery skin that helps them move through the loose, gritty soil. They feed on small insects and other invertebrates, and their slender bodies are perfectly adapted for moving through the narrow, winding burrows of the landscape. They are rarely seen, and their presence is often only indicated by the faint, almost imperceptible shift of the loose soil as they move beneath the surface.

### Conclusion

In this vast, constructed landscape, the Dustwalkers and their smaller, more elusive counterparts form a delicate, interdependent web of life. They are a testament to the resilience and adaptability of life, even in the harshest and most unforgiving of environments. The landscape, with its raw, unpolished geology and relentless heat, is a place of constant, slow change, and the inhabitants, with their unique adaptations and behaviors, are inextricably linked to the very fabric of the world they inhabit.
