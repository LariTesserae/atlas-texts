The landscape is a vast, high-elevation plateau, a place that seems to be in a state of perpetual, embryonic formation. The atmosphere is warm and humid, with a constant mist rising from the ground, condensing, and then evaporating again, creating a near-perpetual cycle of moisture. The plants are not just a part of the landscape; they are the landscape itself, a living, breathing, and ever-growing entity.

The water is not just a liquid; it is a fundamental part of the ecosystem, a constant presence that shapes and molds the landscape.

The sound is the breathing of the living landscape, the constant, rhythmic pulse of the plants as they grow, breathe, and interact with the water and the air. The sound is a constant, unchanging presence, a reminder that this place is in a state of perpetual equilibrium, a frozen equilibrium where nothing changes, yet everything is in constant motion.

They are mostly insects, with gossamer wings that allow them to float through the air, and they are often covered in a fine, iridescent fuzz that helps them to blend into the misty, colorful environment. The animals are not just part of the ecosystem; they are part of the living, breathing entity that is the landscape itself, moving through the air and the plants with a grace and fluidity that seems almost otherworldly.

The light is normal, but it is never direct. The sun, if it is there at all, is always obscured by the mist, and the light filters through the air, giving everything a soft, diffused glow. The light is not just a source of illumination; it is a part of the landscape itself, a constant, unchanging presence that shapes and molds the environment.

The rules of this world are simple, yet profound. The landscape is a living, breathing entity, a single, continuous, and ever-growing organism. Everything in this place is part of this organism, from the plants to the water to the few animals that exist. The landscape is in a state of perpetual equilibrium, a frozen equilibrium where nothing changes, yet everything is in constant motion. The plants grow, the water flows, the air moves, and the animals move, but it is all part of a single, unchanging, and ever-present cycle. The landscape is a living, breathing, and ever-growing entity, a place of constant, unchanging, and eternal equilibrium.
