The atmosphere is heavy with the scent of salt and the earth, a mix of the sea and the land, creating a sense of both familiarity and strangeness.

The landscape feels like it is in a state of becoming, a place where the forces of nature are still at work, shaping and reshaping the land.

The plateaus and outcrops are connected by a network of submerged ridges and shallow channels, creating a multi-feature landscape that invites exploration. The environment is dense, with the plateaus and outcrops often close together, and the vegetation growing in thick, layered clusters. The density is not just in the physical space but also in the sense of presence, a layered, high-saturation world where every element is in close, near-touching proximity.

The settlements are small, with simple, hand-crafted structures built from the local rock and wood, often partially submerged in the water. The buildings are low, with rounded, organic shapes, blending seamlessly into the landscape. The infrastructure is minimal, with simple paths and walkways connecting the structures, and small, hand-crafted tools and implements scattered about. The technology is primitive, with early tool use and simple metal or stone tools, but there is a sense of ingenuity and resourcefulness in the way the people have adapted to the environment. The light is dim, a soft, diffused glow that filters through the thick, dense clouds above. The dim light adds to the sense of strangeness and otherworldliness, creating a world that is both familiar and alien, a place where the boundaries between the natural and the artificial are blurred.

These creatures are adapted to the half-submerged, dense, and dimly lit world, with streamlined, almost ethereal forms that move with a slow, graceful fluidity.
