The plants, though sparse, are adapted to the conditions, their roots digging deep into the cracks of the ancient stone, and their leaves and stems a deep, rich green. These plants, with their thick, waxy leaves and hardy, fibrous structures, have evolved to thrive in the intermittent moisture and occasional rain. Pockets of water, formed by the slow, steady condensation of the cold air, dot the landscape, creating small, isolated oases of life.

The vastness of the landscape is such that it seems to have its own weather, with distant clouds and shifting mists adding to the sense of a place that is self-contained and self-sustaining. The sky, though often overcast, is punctuated by occasional breaks in the clouds, allowing the normal light to filter through, casting a gentle, even glow across the landscape.

The structures, though primitive, are impressive in their scale and the effort that went into their construction. The settlements are few and far between, with much space between them, giving a sense of sparseness and isolation. The buildings, often in a state of disrepair, are layered with the patina of time, their walls and roofs covered in a layer of moss and lichen, blending seamlessly into the natural environment.
