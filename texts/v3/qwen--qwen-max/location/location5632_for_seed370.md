The place is a sealed, self-contained ecosystem, with no gradients or changing conditions. The absence of life is palpable, and the only sounds are the constant, deafening roar of the environment itself. The noise is so overwhelming that it seems to fill every corner of the place, a constant, unyielding presence.

The crystalline structures that line the walls and floors are not just architectural; they are the primary, perhaps the only, presence. These crystals are the result of a pre-metal, primitive technology that has long since become a part of the environment. The crystalline forms are a testament to the hand-tools and simple fabrication techniques that were used to shape and mold them. The structures are fully-occupied, with no room between presences. The place is packed, with the crystalline forms interwoven and interlocked, creating a dense, intricate network that fills every available space.

The rules of this world are simple but profound. Everything is made; there is no natural substrate, no organic life, and no water. The place is a constructed environment, a sealed, self-contained system that has reached a stable, equilibrium state. The energy that courses through the crystals is a primitive, pre-life form, a kind of mineral-based vitality that has no analog in the more familiar, organic world. The place is a testament to a time when technology, though primitive, was used to create something that has endured for eons, a place that is both ancient and timeless.

The faint, internal luminescence of the crystals provides the only illumination, casting a soft, blue-tinged glow that is just enough to see by, but not enough to fully illuminate. The light seems to be a part of the crystals themselves, an inherent property that has long since become a natural part of the environment. The dim light adds to the sense of a place that is both ancient and unchanging, a place where time has no meaning, and the only constant is the unending, deafening sound.
