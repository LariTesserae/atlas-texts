An ordinary human multiverse traveler should prepare for this place not as if visiting a desert, a cave, or an abandoned city, but as if entering a sealed thermal instrument whose pipes are streets, whose garden is mineral and sulfur-bound, whose weather is breath, and whose silence is not silence at all.

The central fact is this: you are a wet, warm-blooded, air-breathing animal entering a dry, hot, mineral, non-animal world. The hollow does not need you. It does not provide for you. It will not poison you with obvious malice, but it can harm you through heat, dust, gas, spores, pressure changes, brittle structures, acoustic stress, and your own mistake. More importantly, your presence can harm it. Water, breath, microbes, vibration, footprints, heat, and accidental contamination are all intrusive here.

So the preparation should be governed by one principle:

**Bring your own biosphere, keep it sealed, move slowly, listen carefully, and leave no trace except temporary, reversible observation.**

---

## 1. Understand What You Are Entering

Before packing anything, understand the nature of the place.

It is:

- **Hidden**: no surface indication except a faint pressure change when the ground exhales.
- **Entered through a narrow fissure**: claustrophobic, possibly hot, possibly subject to airflow pulses.
- **Enclosed**: a basin beneath or below a broad mineral plain, not open to normal sky.
- **Dim**: weak brownish light filtered through mineral crust or old shielding.
- **Hot**: not necessarily flame-hot, but constant, pressing, and capable of burning you through contact with vent-near stone or metal.
- **Absolutely dry**: no water in any ordinary form.
- **Built**: streets, terraces, plazas, canals, towers, vents, stairs, walls, rooms.
- **Empty of animal life**: no insects, birds, tracks, bones, eyes, predators, scavengers, or even small rustlings.
- **Filled with dry alien vegetation**: sulfur-ferns, wax-thread moss, glass canes, bell-pods, ember-bulbs, and other heat/mineral-based growth.
- **Acoustically active**: the city itself hums, moans, ticks, rings, cracks, and amplifies thermal cycles.
- **Slowly active**: not still, but moving through heat tides, spore release, dust shifts, plant growth, vent pulses, and stone settling.

You are not going to a dead ruin. You are going to a living, breathing, dry, hot, architectural ecology with no animals. You are the animal intrusion.

---

## 2. Decide Whether You Should Enter at All

An ordinary human should not enter unless they are prepared for:

- Confined-space travel.
- Hot-environment life support.
- Respiratory protection.
- Low-light navigation.
- Acoustic stress.
- Complete self-reliance.
- Strict contamination discipline.

Do not enter if you have unresolved issues with:

- Claustrophobia.
- Heat intolerance.
- Respiratory illness.
- Heart conditions.
- Panic disorders.
- Severe sinus or ear pressure problems.
- Hearing sensitivity.
- Dehydration risk.
- Any dependence on immediate external rescue.

This is not a place for casual sightseeing. It is a place for careful, reverent, instrument-grade visitation.

If possible, make your first visit a brief threshold visit only: observe the fissure, measure air, record sound, watch one full thermal cycle, and leave. Do not descend until you understand the rhythm of the place.

---

## 3. The Prime Rule: You Must Carry and Contain All Water

The hollow has no water.

That means:

- Do not expect drinking water.
- Do not expect condensation.
- Do not expect dew.
- Do not expect ice.
- Do not expect local fluids to be safe.
- Do not expect plant fluids to be drinkable.
- Do not expect “dry” to mean merely arid. It means water is absent from the local chemistry.
