**Preparing to Visit the Chime-City Under the Salt**

The Chime-City is not hostile, but it is profoundly incompatible with ordinary human life. An ordinary human traveler is mostly water, accidental noise, soft clutter, and heat-seeking clumsiness. In this place, those qualities are not merely inconvenient. They are pollution.

To visit the Chime-City safely, you must prepare by becoming less: less wet, less soft, less noisy, less bulky, less likely to interrupt the chord. The city does not need you. It needs heat, mineral, vibration, and empty resonant volume. Your task is to pass through without disturbing that balance.

The simplest summary is this:

> **Seal your water. Protect your ears. Move slowly. Leave nothing. Add nothing. Become as smooth, dry, and brief as possible.**

Below is a full preparation guide for an ordinary human multiverse traveler.

---

## 1. Understand the Three Laws Before Packing Anything

### First Law: Sound must pass

The city is an instrument. Its streets, plazas, terraces, bridges, reed-beds, seed-pods, and empty halls are all part of an acoustic system. Emptiness is not absence; it is functional space. Anything that blocks, absorbs, muffles, or redirects sound improperly can weaken the chord, starve the plants of vibration, interrupt seed dispersal, or create dead air.

As a visitor, you are already an obstruction. Your body absorbs sound. Your suit may reflect or scatter it. Your gear may rattle, squeak, hum, or flap. You cannot become invisible to the city, but you can reduce your interference.

Prepare by favoring hard, smooth, rounded, non-shedding equipment. Avoid soft external surfaces, loose straps, fluttering cloth, dangling tools, large flat panels, or anything that creates an acoustic shadow larger than necessary.

### Second Law: Dryness is life

There is no water here. There is also no tolerance for water.

Your breath contains moisture. Your sweat contains moisture. Your urine, tears, saliva, blood, and the humidity inside your mask are all potential contamination. A single drop falling on tuned salt, dry reeds, resinous plants, or crystalline filament could be a small catastrophe: swelling, softening, staining, rotting, detuning, or silencing.

You must bring water because you are human, but you must keep it absolutely sealed. Your body must become a closed vessel. If you cannot guarantee that no external moisture will escape from you, you should not enter.

### Third Law: Stillness is dangerous

The city lives because it sounds. Heat moves air. Air moves reeds. Reeds open. Seeds burst. Pollen travels. The walls tick. The bridges tense. The chamber breathes through vibration.

Stillness is not peaceful here. It is stagnation.

You should not stand motionless for long, especially in doorways, vents, narrow throats, seed-paths, or air channels. You should not wedge yourself into niches, lie across plazas, or become a plug in the city’s circulation. Move slowly, smoothly, and with awareness of the sound around you. If the chord changes because of you, you are doing something wrong.

## 2. Decide Whether You Should Enter at All

The Chime-City is not a place for casual tourism. It rewards preparation and punishes casual human habits.

Do not enter if you have any condition that makes sealed-suit travel, extreme dryness, intense noise, heat stress, or narrow descent unsafe.

You should avoid the city if you have:

- A current cold, cough, sinus infection, or excess mucus.
- An ear infection, ruptured eardrum, severe tinnitus, or vestibular disorder.
- Severe claustrophobia or panic disorder.
- Uncontrolled asthma or respiratory sensitivity.
- Heart problems, heat intolerance, or poor hydration discipline.
- Open, weeping wounds that cannot be fully sealed.
- Inability to manage urine, sweat, or saliva inside a sealed system.
- Any dependence on exposed liquid cooling, open drinking, or frequent mask removal.

An ordinary human should treat the Chime-City as a specialized environment, not a scenic stop. If your multiverse travel gear includes improvised seals, leaky hydration systems, noisy servos, venting respirators, or soft bulky survival equipment, you are not ready.

The city does not care about your curiosity. It will not soften itself for you.

## 3. Choose the Right Time to Visit

The city changes with heat and light. The same chamber can be merely overwhelming at dawn and physically punishing at midday.

### Best time for a first visit: dawn to early morning

Morning is gentler. The reeds begin to open, the air currents are strengthening, and the chord is rising but not yet at full pressure. The light is bright but less fierce. The temperature is more manageable. This is the safest time for an ordinary traveler to enter.

### Most dangerous time: midday

At midday, the selenite roof blazes, the walls heat, air shafts draw strongly, the reeds whistle, the seed-vessels burst, and the full chord becomes deafening. This is the city at its most alive and most beautiful, but also at its most hazardous. Sound pressure may be high enough to cause nausea, disorientation, tooth pain, rib discomfort, panic, or balance failure. Heat stress inside a sealed suit becomes serious.

Do not enter at midday on a first visit.

### Afternoon: dust and seed hazards

Afternoon brings gusts, dust whirls, pollen movement, and seed dispersal. Filters may clog more quickly. Visibility can become strange. The air may carry particulate clouds. If your respirator is not robust, avoid this period.

### Evening: brittle and percussive

As the chamber cools, the sound becomes more cracked and percussive. Stone may tick or snap suddenly. The city remains noisy, but the nature of the noise changes. It is less a continuous bloom than a series of sharp reports and shorter tones. This can be startling. Evening visits are possible for experienced travelers, but sudden acoustic shocks can unsettle the inner ear.

### Night: not recommended

The description of the city suggests that sound is its life. If heat falls too low and the chord weakens too much, the chamber may become stagnant. An ordinary traveler should not enter at night unless there is a very specific, well-supported reason.

## 4. Train Before You Go

You do not need extraordinary powers, but you do need practiced competence.

### Train in sealed arid conditions

Before visiting, spend several hours in a hot, dry environment while wearing your full sealed system. You should be able to:

- Breathe without venting moisture.
- Drink from a sealed tube without spilling.
- Manage saliva without opening your mask.
- Move without overheating dangerously.
- Remain calm when your breath sounds loud inside your helmet.
- Keep all sweat contained.
- Avoid touching your face or seals.

If you cannot do this in a controlled environment, do not attempt the Chime-City.

### Train with intense sound

Practice being in loud spaces while wearing hearing protection. Industrial halls, engine rooms, rehearsal spaces, resonant stone chambers, or acoustic testing facilities can help. Learn to recognize the difference between sound you hear with your ears and sound you feel through your chest, teeth, feet, and skull.

The Chime-City will not become quiet just because you wear earplugs. You must learn not to panic when the noise enters your bones.

Practice communicating without speech. Learn simple hand signals, rope-tug codes, and touch signals. Practice giving and receiving “stop,” “follow,” “danger,” “retreat,” “check seal,” and “I am okay” while gloved and visually restricted.

### Train for the fissure descent

The entrance is a narrow seam barely wide enough for a body. You must be comfortable in tight, abrasive, vertical spaces.

Practice:

- Slow rope descent.
- Moving through narrow slots while wearing a sealed suit.
- Keeping gear from snagging.
- Controlling your breathing when your chest is compressed.
- Not thrashing or twisting suddenly.
- Equalizing pressure in ears and mask if pressure changes occur.

If you panic in tight spaces, the fissure will be dangerous even before the sound begins.

### Train your movement

Inside the city, movement should be smooth, deliberate, and economical. Do not run. Do not leap. Do not stomp. Do not flail. Practice walking with a low, rolling step on polished stone. Practice turning your body side-on to reduce your profile in narrow acoustic channels. Practice kneeling with hard knee plates rather than soft pads.

You are not trying to be silent. The city is already loud. You are trying to avoid adding irregular noise or blocking sound.

## 5. The Traveler’s Body: Sealed, Dry, and Contained

Your first and most important piece of equipment is your suit. It must treat your body as a hazardous material.

### Full-body sealed arid suit

Use a full-body suit with no external venting. It should be:

- Smooth and rounded.
- Pale, reflective, or bone-white.
- Resistant to salt abrasion.
- Non-shedding.
- Non-flapping.
- Free of loose external fabric.
- Free of soft external foam, fleece, wool, or fibrous insulation.
- Sealed at wrists, ankles, neck, and mask interface.
- Capable of containing sweat, urine, and exhaled moisture.

The outer surface should be hard or semi-rigid where possible. Think of yourself as wearing a polished shell, not a soft explorer’s outfit. A smooth resin-coated textile, waxed and burnished fiber, thin articulated plates of bone-like composite, or carefully fitted metal scales may be appropriate if they do not rattle or scrape.

Avoid anything that sheds lint, hair, fibers, or microfragments. The city already has enough particulate life.

### Internal moisture management

Your suit will need an internal absorbent or condensate-capture layer. Sweat must be drawn away from the skin and retained inside sealed reservoirs. Exhaled moisture must be captured by a rebreather or condenser system.

Do not rely on evaporative cooling. Evaporative cooling releases water vapor, which is unacceptable here.

Your suit should include:

- Internal sweat wicking.
- Sealed urine collection.
- Sealed hydration bladder.
- Internal drinking tube with a reliable bite valve.
- Emergency spit or condensate pouch if necessary.
- No external relief valves that vent moist air.

If you must urinate, do so into the sealed system. If you vomit, bleed, cry, or drool, it must remain contained. That sounds unpleasant, but the alternative is contaminating the city.

### Hydration

You must bring water, but it must never be exposed.

Use an internal bladder with a locking bite valve. Test it for leaks under heat and pressure before entry. Do not carry open canteens, water bottles, hydration packs with external tubes, or squeeze pouches that can burst.

For short visits, carry only what you need. A sealed system can fail, and every liter of water inside the city is a potential disaster. Prehydrate before entry, but avoid overhydrating to the point where sweat management becomes difficult.

Bring sealed electrolyte tablets if your system allows you to take them without opening the suit. Do not plan to eat or drink openly inside the city.

### Waste

For a short visit, empty your body immediately before suiting. If the visit may be long, your suit must have a reliable sealed waste system.

No urine, feces, vomit, saliva, or other bodily fluid may leave the suit. This is not merely hygiene; it is ecological protection.

## 6. Breathing: No Moisture May Leave Your Face

The air in the Chime-City is warm, dry, bright, and full of fine particulate matter: salt dust, pollen, seed fragments, resin particles, and possibly microscopic crystalline filaments. You should not breathe it unfiltered.

But ordinary filtration is not enough, because exhaled breath contains water vapor.

### Preferred breathing system

Use a sealed full-face visor or mask connected to a closed-circuit rebreather or dry-gas loop. The system should:

- Filter incoming air.
- Scrub carbon dioxide.
- Capture exhaled moisture.
- Provide dry oxygen or dry air.
- Not vent humid gas into the environment.
- Use desiccant or condenser traps that remain sealed.
- Have a backup loop or emergency supply.

A simple dust mask is unacceptable. It lets your breath escape into the city and does not protect your eyes.

### Filter protection

Pollen and dust may clog filters quickly, especially during seed burst or afternoon gusts. Carry spare filters in sealed hard cases. Filters should be protected from impact and should not have exposed fibrous surfaces that shed into the chamber.

If your vision fogs, do not open the mask. Use a dry purge system, double-pane visor, or internal anti-fog method that does not release liquid or vapor.

### Do not bring wetted masks, wet wipes, or damp filters

Anything “moistened for comfort” is forbidden.

## 7. Hearing and Vibration Protection

This is the part most travelers will underestimate.

The Chime-City is not merely loud. It is structural. The sound begins as pressure in the chest. It rises into the bones. It is heard through the soles of the feet, the ribs, the teeth, and the skull. Ordinary ear protection will not make the city quiet. It can only reduce the damage.

### Ear protection

Use layered protection:

- Custom-fitted earplugs with high attenuation.
- Hard-shell over-ear muffs.
- A helmet or hood that reduces peak pressure without creating a large acoustic shadow.
- If available, active noise control tuned for low-frequency pressure, but never rely on electronics alone.

Do not seek total silence. Total isolation can be disorienting in a place where vibration carries spatial information. You want to reduce damaging peaks while retaining enough orientation to move safely.

### Bone-conduction and body vibration

Earplugs cannot stop sound that enters through the skeleton. You may feel the chord in your teeth, jaw, sternum, spine, and inner ears. A soft bite guard may reduce tooth discomfort. A well-fitted helmet may reduce skull resonance. But the only true protection against full-body exposure is limiting duration and avoiding the loudest phases.

If you feel nausea, panic, pressure pain, or sudden vertigo, leave. These are not signs of cowardice. They are signs that your body is being overstressed.

### Communication

Speech will be erased or swallowed. Do not plan to talk.

Use:

- Prearranged hand signals.
- Rope-tug codes.
- Touch signals on shoulder, arm, or helmet.
- Visual markers visible through glare.
- Dim, shielded, non-flashing lights only if absolutely necessary.

Do not use speakers, buzzers, whistles, clickers, or sonic signaling devices. Do not tap on the city’s walls to communicate unless you understand exactly what you are doing, and even then, do not do it.

## 8. Thermal Protection: The Problem of a Wet Creature in a Dry Oven

The air is warm, sometimes near or above skin temperature. The ceiling is blazing. The walls reflect light. Your sealed suit prevents normal evaporative cooling. This creates a serious heat risk.

### Enter cool

Pre-cool your body before entry using sealed methods only. Do not wet your skin. Do not use evaporative vests. Do not carry exposed ice.

Use sealed phase-change packs or internal cooling loops if available. Make sure any condensation remains inside the suit and is captured. If your cooling system can leak, do not use it.

### Reflective exterior

Your suit should reflect light. Pale, burnished surfaces are better than dark cloth. A smooth brow shade or helmet visor can reduce direct glare from the selenite roof, but do not wear a broad floppy hat or cloak. Those can flap, shed, and block sound.

### Limit exertion

Move slowly. Do not climb unnecessarily. Do not rush. Do not carry more weight than you need. The city is not large; the danger is intensity, not distance.

If your core temperature rises, exit. Do not open your suit to cool down. Opening the suit would release moisture and contaminate the city.

## 9. Eye and Skin Protection

The light is fierce. Salt reflects it. The roof scatters white-gold rays. Shadows are sharp and blue-white. Depth perception may become strange.

### Eye protection

Use a sealed visor with:

- UV protection.
- Glare reduction.
- Anti-fog capability.
- Scratch resistance.
- A secure seal against dust and pollen.

Do not look directly at the blazing roof. Do not rub your eyes. If dust enters your eye, do not remove your mask inside the city. Exit first.

### Skin protection

No skin should be exposed. The dry air will desiccate lips, nostrils, eyes, and skin quickly. Salt dust may abrade. Crystalline filaments may be sharp. Resin from damaged plants may harden rapidly.

Wear sealed gloves. Use thin but tough gloves if you need dexterity. Avoid metal claws or hard scraping tools on the fingertips.

Use lip protection inside the mask. Do not apply wet balms externally. Do not use sprays, lotions, or perfumes that can shed vapor or scent into the chamber.

## 10. Clothing and Gear Materials: Hard, Smooth, Pale, Silent

The city’s aesthetic is not decorative; it is acoustic. Your equipment should follow the same logic.

### Favor

- Smooth resin-coated surfaces.
- Polished bone-like composites.
- Thin copper or bronze elements if they do not ring.
- Wax-sealed fibers.
- Hard shells.
- Rounded edges.
- Flush-mounted storage.
- Pale colors: white, bone, pale gold, dull salt-grey.
- Quiet closures.
- Internal padding only.

### Avoid

- Soft cloth exteriors.
- Wool, fleece, felt, or foam exposed to the air.
- Paper.
- Untreated leather that sheds fibers.
- Flapping straps.
- Loose chains.
- Bells, chimes, or decorative metal.
- Plastic sheets that crackle.
- Velcro or noisy fasteners.
- Squeaking joints.
- External speakers or fans.
- Large flat boards or panels.
- Bulky backpacks that make you too wide for the fissure.

Test your kit by moving in silence before you go. If your gear rattles, sings, creaks, or whispers, fix it or leave it behind.

## 11. Packing Principles for the Chime-City

### Carry as little as possible

The city requires emptiness. Your gear is a violation of that emptiness. Bring only what keeps you alive and contained.

### Keep everything internal or flush-mounted

Nothing should hang. Nothing should swing. Nothing should catch on the fissure or brush against resonance-vines.

### Use rigid, smooth containers

If you must carry spare filters, repair materials, medical supplies, or recording tools, place them in hard, sealed, rounded cases. Pad the inside if necessary, but keep the outside smooth.

### No loose objects

A dropped object is not merely litter. It may block a vent, detune a niche, strike a seed-vessel, injure a beetle, or absorb sound in a place where sound must pass.

If you drop something, retrieve it carefully. If it falls into a sensitive vent or plant thicket, do not cause greater damage by digging. Use a slender, smooth tool if possible. If retrieval would harm the city more than the object, you have made a serious mistake. In most cases, prevention is the only acceptable policy.

## 14. Rope Descent: Entering Through the Seam

The entrance is a narrow fissure in the salt crust. From the surface, the city is hidden. You must descend by a braided yellow reed rope.

### Respect the reed rope

The rope may be simple, but it belongs to the place. Inspect it without damaging it. Look for brittleness, fraying, or resin buildup. Do not wet it. Do not cut it. Do not tie heavy knots directly into it if you can avoid doing so.

If you use a backup line, make it thin, strong, pale, and non-shedding. Pad any contact points against the salt. Do not anchor to living reeds, copper clamps, carved tuning pins, or fragile alabaster edges.

### Gear profile matters

The fissure is barely wide enough for a body. If your tanks, cooling packs, or cases make you too broad, you will become stuck. That is dangerous for you and potentially damaging to the seam.

Use conformal equipment. Keep tanks low and close. Avoid wide shoulder frames. Practice moving through a mock fissure before arrival.

### Descend slowly

The fissure may breathe with warm dry air. Pressure may shift as the chamber inhales and exhales through the seam. Descend slowly. Equalize your ears and mask as needed. Keep your limbs close. Do not let your gear scrape the walls.

At the bottom, do not rush into the city. Pause in the threshold if there is space. Check your seals, your breathing, your hearing protection, your buddy, and your composure.

The transition from silent salt-field to roaring chamber can be shocking even when expected. Give yourself a moment to become part of the sound without fighting it.

## 15. Entering the City: First Minutes

When you first stand inside the pocket hollow, you will likely feel several things at once:

- The brightness is intense.
- The sound is physical.
- The air feels warm through the suit.
- The emptiness feels deliberate.
- The architecture seems to be listening.

Do not immediately walk toward the center. Do not look up at the blazing roof. Do not speak. Do not spread your arms. Do not unpack.

Instead:

1. Check your seals.
2. Check your breathing system.
3. Check your hearing protection.
4. Check your hydration valve.
5. Check your buddy.
6. Observe the dust and reed movement.
7. Identify air paths.
8. Identify seed-vessels and their likely dispersal lines.
9. Choose a route that avoids central sound lanes.
10. Move slowly.

The first minutes are for calibration, not exploration.

## 16. Moving Through the City

### Stay aware of air paths

Air moves through hidden shafts, carved throats, bridge membranes, reed thickets, and vented walls. You can often identify these paths by:

- Dust streamlines.
- Trembling reeds.
- Faint polish on stone.
- Oriented leaves.
- Seed fragments gathered in lines.
- Changes in pressure against your suit.
- Small ticking or whistling intensities.

Do not stand in these paths. Do not block them with your body, pack, or rope. If you see a clear line of moving dust across a plaza, that is not a decorative pattern. It is circulation.

### Move side-on in narrow acoustic spaces

In narrow streets, doorways, or processional avenues, turn your body side-on when possible. Present a smaller obstruction. Keep arms close. Do not stop unless necessary.

### Do not linger in doorways

Doorways may be shaped to prevent dead air. If you stand in one, you may become a plug. Move through smoothly.

### Avoid the central plaza during seed burst

The broad plazas are likely part of the city’s dispersal system. At the loudest part of the day, seed-vessels may snap and release pale seeds and pollen clouds into moving air. This is beautiful, but you should not be standing in the middle of it.

Observe from the edge if possible. Keep your visor protected. Keep filters clear. Do not chase seeds. Do not collect them.

### Do not touch plants

Do not cut, bend, pluck, or scrape the vegetation.

- Wax-leaved scrub may be hard and resinous.
- Hollow reeds are acoustic elements.
- Resonance-vines may tighten when disturbed.
- Seed-vessels may burst under particular frequencies.
- Crystalline filament may be brittle or sharp.

If a leaf turns toward you, it is responding to vibration, not greeting you. Step back. Do not encourage it. Do not feed it. Do not make sustained sound to attract it.

If you accidentally damage a plant and a bead of amber resin appears, do not touch it with bare hands, do not add water, and do not try to “heal” it. Leave it. If you have caused significant damage, withdraw carefully and do not make the situation worse.

### Do not disturb fauna

The pale beetles and translucent moths are rare and deliberate. They are part of the city’s mechanism. Do not touch, catch, chase, or block them.

If a moth lands on your suit or visor, remain calm. It may be filtering vibration or light in some way you do not understand. Move slowly away if you can, or wait until it leaves. Do not swat.

### Do not sit, camp, or lie down

The city is not a resting place. Sitting on the polished floor makes you an obstruction and may put you in a seed-path or air channel. Camping would introduce clutter, warmth, moisture, and prolonged acoustic shadow.

If you must pause, choose a broad, open step or ledge where you are not blocking a vent, doorway, niche, or plant bed. Angle your body. Keep the pause short. Then move or leave.

## 17. The Problem of Speaking

You will feel an urge to speak. This is normal. Humans use speech to reassure themselves, coordinate, and mark presence.

Do not speak inside the city unless absolutely necessary, and even then understand that your voice will be absorbed into the chord or erased by it. Speech is not useful here. It may also add an irregular biological frequency to a tuned environment.

If you must communicate, use touch or prearranged visual signals.

If you are overwhelmed and want to cry out, recognize the urge as a stress response. Breathe inside your mask. Check your vitals. If the pressure becomes unbearable, leave.

## 18. Managing Sound Phases

The city changes through the day. Prepare for each phase.

### Morning: low hum and opening reeds

The sound is lower, more breath-like. Reeds loosen. Air begins to move. This is the best time for observation.

Your main hazards here are underestimating the environment and moving too quickly. The city may seem almost gentle. It is not.

### Midday: full chord and seed burst

The sound becomes a layered, deafening bloom. Light blazes. Walls tick. Seed-vessels snap. Dust trembles. This is the city’s peak activity.

Only enter during this phase if you are experienced, protected, and brief. Do not stand in seed lanes. Do not look up at the roof. Do not try to endure the sound as a test of will.

### Afternoon: gusts and dust whirls

Air currents may become gusty. Pollen and dust may form miniature storms. Filters may clog. Visibility may shift.

Keep your visor clean. Avoid open plaza centers if dust is moving strongly. Protect your breathing system.

### Evening: cooling cracks and brittle percussion

As the chamber contracts, the sound becomes sharper. Stone may crack suddenly. Reeds may produce shorter notes. The city may feel more brittle and less continuous.

Be alert for sudden acoustic shocks. Do not let your guard down because the continuous roar has lessened.

## 19. Do Not Try to Tune the City

Some travelers, especially musicians, engineers, acousticians, and architects, may feel the temptation to improve, adjust, or answer the city.

Resist this completely.

Do not:

- Move tuning pins.
- Adjust copper clamps.
- Shift stone blocks.
- Clear “obstructions” that may be intentional.
- Re-align reeds.
- Open seed-pods manually.
- Tap walls to test resonance.
- Place objects in empty niches.
- Remove dust that seems strategically placed.
- Add your own instruments.
- Hum, sing, whistle, or play recordings.
- Use sound-canceling fields or silence spells if your multiverse technology provides them.

The city has found its balance over long ages. Your intuition about what sounds “right” is not authority here.

## 20. Fire and Spark Discipline

The city is dry in an extreme sense. Dry reeds, papery seed-vessels, resin, pollen, and aged fiber create a serious fire risk.

Do not bring open flame. Do not smoke. Do not use combustion tools. Do not create sparks. Avoid electronics that arc or overheat. If your equipment uses batteries, they must be sealed, stable, and silent.

Pollen clouds can be flammable in some worlds. Resin can burn. Dry reed can ignite. Do not test this.

If you see smoke or smell burning inside your suit, exit immediately. Do not attempt to fight fire with water, because you should not have exposed water. Do not attempt to smother fire with soft cloth that sheds fibers. Withdraw and let the city’s own dryness and airflow respond as they will, but do not remain inside a burning acoustic chamber.

## 21. Buddy System and Surface Support

An ordinary human should not enter alone.

### Buddy rules

- Enter with at least one equally prepared companion.
- Maintain visual or tether contact.
- Use prearranged signals.
- Check each other’s seals before descent.
- Agree on maximum duration.
- Agree on abort signals.
- Do not separate unless absolutely necessary.

Your buddy is not there to help you “conquer” the city. They are there to notice leaks, overheating, disorientation, or equipment failure before you do.

### Surface support

Someone should remain outside the fissure, protected from the salt plain’s glare, monitoring time and lines. They should have:

- Backup rope.
- Dry communication method.
- Emergency extraction plan.
- Sealed medical supplies for after exit.
- Shade and eye protection for themselves.
- Clear instructions not to enter unprepared.

Do not portal directly into the chamber if you have multiverse travel capability. Arrival inside could create pressure disruption, acoustic shock, or moisture release. Enter through the seam as the city expects.

## 22. Emergency Procedures

### If your suit leaks moisture

If you feel or know that liquid water, sweat, urine, saliva, or condensation has escaped your suit into the environment, abort immediately.

Do not stop to wipe the city dry with cloth. Do not spit. Do not try to evaporate it. Leave. If the leak is inside your suit and fully contained, you may continue only if the leak does not threaten your thermal or breathing systems. If it threatens containment, leave.

### If your breathing system fails

Switch to backup immediately. If the backup fails or you feel carbon dioxide buildup, exit.

Symptoms of CO2 stress include:

- Headache.
- Confusion.
- Panic.
- Rapid breathing.
- Dizziness.
- Flushed skin.
- Impaired judgment.

Do not remove your mask to “get fresh air.” The chamber air is dry, hot, and dusty. Exit first.

### If your hearing protection fails

If one earplug shifts or a muff seal breaks, you may suddenly experience painful sound pressure. Do not try to fix it with bare hands while standing in a seed-path or vent. Move to a less intense area if possible, signal your buddy, and exit.

If you experience sudden hearing loss, severe ear pain, vertigo, vomiting, or disorientation, leave immediately.

### If you overheat

Early signs include:

- Heavy sweating inside the suit.
- Thirst.
- Weakness.
- Nausea.
- Rapid heartbeat.
- Hot skin.
- Confusion.
- Irritability.

Do not open the suit. Exit. Use internal cooling if it is still functional. If you become confused, your buddy should initiate extraction.

### If you become disoriented by sound

Infrasound and vibration can cause dread, nausea, or the sense that the walls are moving. Focus on tactile information: your feet, your harness, your buddy’s hand, the rope or wall if safe to follow. Do not close your eyes and try to “wait it out” in the middle of an air path. Move toward the exit with deliberate steps.

### If the city’s sound weakens or stops

This is a serious warning. The city is alive because it is noisy. If the chord falters, the vegetation may close, the air may stale, and the chamber may become unsafe.

First, check whether you are blocking a vent or shaft. Move out of any narrow throat, doorway, or dust-line. If the sound does not recover, leave. Do not try to restart the city by shouting, striking stone, or creating artificial noise.

### If a seed burst occurs near you

Turn away if possible. Protect your visor. Keep your breathing filter clear. Do not inhale sharply if you suspect your seal is compromised. Do not chase seeds. Wait for the air current to pass, then move away from the dispersal line.

### If you drop something

Stop. Locate it. Retrieve it without damaging the city. If it is in a vent or plant bed, use a smooth tool and patience. If retrieval would cause greater harm, you have failed in preparation; do not make the damage worse by reckless action. In future, secure your gear better.

### If you are injured

Any injury that releases blood, serum, or moisture is an environmental emergency. Contain it inside the suit and exit. Do not let blood touch the salt, plants, or architecture.

If you fracture a limb or suffer a severe injury, your buddy should help you exit as smoothly as possible. Do not use emergency beacons that emit loud sound or vibration unless there is no other way to save your life. Survival comes first, but avoid harming the city if possible.

## 23. What to Do If You Are Overwhelmed Emotionally

The Chime-City can produce strong emotions. Travelers may feel awe, grief, loneliness, terror, or an inexplicable desire to weep. The sound can make thought feel physical. The emptiness can feel like judgment. The absence of water can feel like a missing organ.

If you feel overwhelmed:

- Do not remove your mask.
- Do not speak.
- Do not run.
- Do not seek a hidden quiet corner.
- Check your breathing.
- Check your oxygen and CO₂ status.
- Check your core temperature.
- Signal your buddy.
- Begin moving toward the exit.

The city is not speaking to you personally. It is sounding. Your nervous system may interpret that sounding as meaning. That is understandable, but do not obey the interpretation if it tells you to stay, to sing, to open your helmet, or to become part of the chord.

## 24. Departure

Leave before you are exhausted. Do not push your time to the limit.

### Ascend carefully

The fissure may feel tighter on the way out, especially if you are tired, overheated, or disoriented. Use your rope and ascent tools smoothly. Do not scrape the walls. Do not leave behind any snagged fiber, tape, or broken strap.

### Do not open your suit at the fissure

Wait until you are fully outside and away from the seam. The area around the entrance should remain dry and uncontaminated.

Move away from the fissure before opening seals, drinking, or treating medical issues.

## 25. Dry Decontamination After Exit

The city has no washing water, and you should not introduce washing water near the seam.

After exit:

1. Remain sealed until you reach a controlled area.
2. Brush or vacuum dry salt dust and pollen from your suit using sealed dry methods.
3. Inspect boots, gloves, seams, and filters for trapped seeds or fibers.
4. Place any removed particulate matter into sealed containers.
5. Drain captured sweat, urine, and condensate into sealed waste vessels.
6. Remove the suit only in a dry, contained space.
7. Do not wash the suit at the site.
8. Do not pour any liquid onto the salt plain.
9. Do not discard seeds, pollen, dust, or souvenirs.

If any seed or plant fragment has attached to your gear, do not plant it. Do not study it casually. Contain it and carry it out according to your multiverse biosecurity protocols.

## 26. Medical Aftercare

Even a successful visit can stress the body.

After leaving:

- Rest in a temperate, humid-safe environment.
- Rehydrate normally only after you are fully out of the city and away from the seam.
- Monitor hearing for ringing, muffled perception, pain, or balance problems.
- Watch for respiratory irritation from fine dust.
- Check eyes for abrasion or redness.
- Inspect skin for salt abrasion or resin contact.
- Monitor for heat illness symptoms for several hours.
- Do not immediately enter another loud or acoustically extreme environment.

If you experience persistent vertigo, hearing loss, chest discomfort, cough, or confusion, seek medical care.

## 27. Ethical Preparation: Learn to Want Nothing from the City

The hardest preparation may be psychological.

Most travelers are conditioned to collect, document, touch, taste, sample, photograph, and share. The Chime-City resists this. It offers no water to drink, no fruit to taste, no flower to pick, no stone to chip, no echo to command, no crowd to meet, no market to browse, no souvenir to take.

It asks you to witness without possessing.

Prepare yourself to see a lush place and take nothing from it. Prepare yourself to hear overwhelming sound and add nothing to it. Prepare yourself to feel alone in a place built for settlement and understand that your loneliness is not the city’s problem.

The city is not waiting for you. It is not performing for you. It is continuing itself.

## 28. A Simple First-Visit Plan for an Ordinary Traveler

If you are an ordinary human with competent but not heroic capabilities, a conservative first visit might look like this:

1. **Arrive outside the salt plain before dawn.**  
   Do not portal into the hollow. Approach on foot or by silent transport that stops well away.

2. **Suit fully before reaching the fissure.**  
   Check seals, breathing, hydration, waste, cooling, hearing protection, and communication.

3. **Inspect the reed rope.**  
   Add a backup line only if it can be done without damaging the seam.

4. **Descend slowly at first light.**  
   Pause at the bottom. Do not rush.

5. **Spend no more than thirty to sixty minutes inside.**  
   Stay near the margins of your planned route. Avoid central seed-paths. Observe the reeds opening and the chord strengthening.

6. **Leave before midday.**  
   Do not wait for the full blaze unless you are experienced and specifically prepared.

7. **Ascend carefully.**  
   Move away from the fissure before opening anything.

8. **Perform dry decontamination.**  
   Contain all dust, waste, moisture, and gear residues.

9. **Rest and assess.**  
   Do not treat the visit as complete until aftercare is done.

This is not a dramatic itinerary. That is good. The Chime-City is not a place for drama. It is a place for precision.

## 29. Final Intuition: Prepare by Becoming a Small, Smooth, Passing Object

If you ask what visiting the Chime-City would feel like, the answer is: like entering the inside of a vast dry bell that is also a garden, also a body, also a city, and also a prayer with no worshipper.

You will move from blank silence into roaring brightness. You will feel the sound in your ribs before your ears understand it. You will see plants with no thirst, streets with no crowds, niches with no statues, and light with no softness. You will be acutely aware that you are made of water in a world that has replaced water with resonance.

The correct preparation is not to conquer that feeling. It is to survive it without harming the place.

So seal your breath. Bind your sweat. Round your edges. Quiet your tools. Shorten your stay. Learn the signals. Respect the emptiness. Do not fill what is meant to remain open. Do not wet what is meant to remain dry. Do not silence what is meant to sound.

Enter as a polished stone would enter if it could choose to pass through briefly: smooth, contained, reflective, and gone.
