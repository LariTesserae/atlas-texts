## Preparing to Visit the Pocket City of Weeping Stone

An ordinary human multiverse traveler should prepare for the Pocket City of Weeping Stone not as if visiting a ruin, a cave, or a settlement in the usual sense, but as if entering a sealed, breathing instrument made of water, stone, and careful attention.

The place is small, wet, enclosed, dim, loud in its own way, and intensely worked. It is not hostile in the manner of a predator or a storm, but it is unforgiving of clumsiness. It does not want bright light, dry dust, plant seeds, loud voices, careless feet, open flame, or curious hands pulling at its channels. It asks for restraint.

To visit it safely, you must become smaller in every practical sense: quieter, dimmer, cleaner, slower, and less wasteful.

---

# 1. Understand What Kind of Place You Are Entering

The Pocket City is not a landscape. It has no horizon, no sky, no distant weather. It is a contained hollow below the water table, more like the inside of a vessel than a room. The experience will be intimate and compressive. Your body will be one of the largest moving things inside.

It is also not a natural cave in the ordinary sense. Every usable surface has been shaped or assigned. Water is not merely present; it is distributed. It is caught, led, gathered, slowed, dropped, and possibly listened to. The carved channels, basins, pipes, and sounding jars are not decoration. They are part of the city’s functioning.

There is no green life. No plants, no moss, no roots, no pollen, no wood, no leaves. The living presence is mineral, fungal, bacterial, and aquatic in a cold, dim way. This means two things:

1. The place may feel strangely “dead” to someone expecting ordinary life.
2. It is actually alive in ways that are fragile and easily contaminated.

Your preparation should therefore be a mixture of caving discipline, clean-room caution, museum etiquette, and cold-water survival.

---

# 2. Decide Honestly Whether You Are Fit to Enter

An ordinary human should not enter if they are prone to severe claustrophobia, panic in darkness, or loss of coordination in tight wet spaces. The entry fissure is narrow, the steps are slick, and the chamber beyond is close. There is no wide open space to recover in.

You should also avoid entering if you have:

- An active respiratory infection.
- Open wounds that cannot be fully sealed.
- Severe balance problems.
- Uncontrolled coughing or sneezing.
- Recent exposure to invasive plant seeds, soil organisms, or aggressive fungal spores from another world.
- A fear of water in enclosed spaces.
- No reliable light source.
- No outside contact or rescue plan.

This is not a place for improvisation. If something goes wrong, the environment itself becomes difficult: wet stone, low ceilings, poor visibility, possible bad air, and a narrow exit.

---

# 3. Prepare Your Mind Before You Prepare Your Gear

The Pocket City will disturb ordinary human expectations.

There is no far distance. Your eyes will search for a horizon and fail. There is no daylight rhythm. There may be no obvious day or night inside. The light is weak, blue-gray, and trembling. Sound may be constant: dripping, trickling, water sliding over stone, droplets striking basins, echoes moving through carved channels.

You should mentally prepare for:

- **Enclosure.** The ceiling may press low. The walls may feel near. The city is dense without being crowded.
- **Sensory strangeness.** You will navigate more by sheen, sound, texture, and memory than by clear sight.
- **Absence of green.** There will be no familiar plant smells, no leaves, no comforting biological softness. The world is mineral, wet, fungal, and cold.
- **Constant damp.** You will never feel fully dry. Condensation will be on everything.
- **Acoustic intensity.** The place is described as loud, but not necessarily loud in a human way. It may be loud with water, resonance, and enclosed echoes.

Before entering, spend time in dimness. Let your eyes and nerves settle. Do not rush from a bright, open world straight into the fissure if you can avoid it. Sit near the entrance. Listen. Breathe slowly. Learn to make your body less urgent.

---

# 4. Choose the Right Time and Conditions

Even though the city is below the surface, conditions above matter.

The plain above is cold, wet, and mineral. Water gathers in shallow pools. If the water table rises, the fissure and hand-cut steps could become more dangerous. The chamber is not described as flooded, but any below-water-table pocket can become hazardous if the hydrology changes.

Before entering:

- Choose a period of stable water levels.
- Avoid entering after unusual rains, flooding, or rapid melt if the local climate has one.
- Check whether the shallow mineral pools above are expanding or draining unusually.
- Look for signs of recent water level change near the fissure: fresh mineral crusts, wet lines, pooled sediment, or newly slick surfaces.
- Do not enter if the fissure sounds different than expected, especially if water is moving faster, louder, or in greater volume.

If possible, have someone remain outside or nearby. At minimum, leave a clear itinerary with a trusted contact: where you entered, when you entered, when you should return, and what should be done if you do not.

A small party is best. One person inside, one at the entrance, and one surface contact is safer than a large group. Large groups add heat, moisture, carbon dioxide, noise, and contamination.

---

# 5. Clean Yourself and Your Gear as if the City Is Alive

Because the Pocket City has no plant life and depends on mineral, fungal, and microbial systems, you must be careful not to introduce foreign biology.

An ordinary multiverse traveler may carry invisible hitchhikers: seeds, spores, soil bacteria, pollen, insect eggs, mold, fibers, food crumbs, and residue from other worlds.

Before entering:

- Inspect all clothing, boots, cuffs, pockets, and gear for plant matter.
- Brush boots thoroughly.
- Remove any soil residue from previous travels.
- Avoid wearing clothing that has been in contact with fertile earth, grass, hay, compost, forests, or agricultural worlds.
- Do not carry wood, paper, dried flowers, seeds, food husks, or natural fiber shedding.
- Avoid scented soaps, perfumes, lotions, insect repellents, or strong food odors.
- Use clean synthetic or mineral-compatible materials where possible.

You are not just protecting yourself. You are protecting the Pocket City from you.

---

# 6. Dress for Wet Cold, Not for Open-Air Cold

The chamber is likely cool, wet, and saturated with moisture. Wet cold is more dangerous than dry cold. Even if the temperature is not extreme, constant dampness can drain body heat.

Clothing should keep you warm when wet, resist absorbing water, and allow careful movement in tight spaces.

Avoid cotton. Cotton holds moisture, cools the body, and sheds fibers.

Better choices include:

- A thin synthetic base layer that moves moisture away from the skin.
- A wool or synthetic insulating layer that still functions when damp.
- A waterproof but breathable outer layer.
- A hood that fits under a helmet.
- Gloves that retain grip when wet.
- Spare dry layers sealed in a waterproof bag.

You do not want bulky expedition gear that snags in the fissure. You want slim, warm, quick-drying, low-shedding clothing.

A good balance is:

- **Upper body:** thin base layer, insulating mid-layer, thin waterproof shell.
- **Lower body:** flexible quick-drying trousers with knee protection, possibly with a thin waterproof overlayer if the spray is heavy.
- **Hands:** thin grippy gloves plus a spare pair. If the stone is sharp or slick, consider a thin cut-resistant layer under a wet-grip glove.
- **Head:** warm cap that fits under a helmet, plus a hood for condensation.
