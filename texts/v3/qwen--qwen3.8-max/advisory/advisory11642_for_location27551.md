Treat this destination as **three environments stacked on top of one another**:

1. **A high-altitude volcanic rim**: thin air, cold wind, strong UV, raw rock, possible altitude sickness.  
2. **A warm, mineral-rich inland sea**: chemically active, biologically saturated, sometimes murky, sometimes glass-clear.  
3. **A young underwater terrain**: sharp basalt, unstable pumice, fresh fault scarps, hot vents, dense root curtains, microbial mats, and almost no artificial help.

An ordinary human multiverse traveler should prepare less like a tourist and more like a careful diver-mountaineer-field biologist entering a fragile, newly born world.

---

# 1. First assumptions: what this place is like for a human body

This is not an ocean beach, not a swamp, not a cave, and not a simple warm lagoon. It is a **high, warm, living, mineral-thick lake inside a newly broken volcanic basin**.

For an ordinary human, the main challenges are:

- **Thin high-altitude air** before and after immersion.
- **Warm but chemically complex water** that may irritate skin, eyes, lungs, and equipment.
- **Sharp, unweathered rock** that can cut gear and flesh.
- **Dense vegetation and root systems** that create entanglement hazards.
- **Geothermal vents and gas bubbles** that may be hot, acidic, alkaline, sulfurous, carbon dioxide-rich, or otherwise hazardous.
- **Low animal life**, meaning no reliable food source and little warning from fauna.
- **No structures**: no docks, no ladders, no platforms, no shelters, no markers.
- **Fragile biological architecture**: the “buildings” of this place are living mats, root roofs, microbial skins, and young plant colonies.

The traveler must be self-contained, conservative, and respectful. The right mindset is: **move slowly, breathe carefully, touch nothing unnecessarily, and always know your way out.**

---

# 2. Physical and mental preparation

## 2.1 Be fit for altitude diving, not just swimming

This is not a place for a casual dip unless you remain only at the surface with proper flotation and breathing safety. To truly visit the submerged country, you need:

- Good cardiovascular fitness.
- Comfort in water without panic.
- Strong breath control.
- Healthy ears, sinuses, and lungs.
- No uncontrolled asthma, heart problems, seizure disorders, or severe claustrophobia.
- Medical clearance for diving if possible.
- Specific caution if you have a history of decompression illness, patent foramen ovale, lung blebs, or ear surgery.

Because the lake is high-altitude, ordinary dive rules change. You need training or at least expert guidance in **altitude diving physiology**.

## 2.2 Acclimatize before entering

Do not step out of a portal at cloud level and immediately dive.

Before immersion:

- Spend time at the local altitude if possible.
- Monitor for headache, nausea, dizziness, shortness of breath, poor coordination, or swelling.
- Use pulse oximetry if available, but do not trust it blindly.
- Keep exertion low on arrival.
