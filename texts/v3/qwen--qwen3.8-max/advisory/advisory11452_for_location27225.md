Prepare as if you are visiting three places at once: a newly flooded construction site, a shallow underwater chamber, and a loud, bright, intimate room with no shore and no ordinary natural life. The basin is not a reef, not a cave, not a swimming pool, and not a ruin softened by time. It is fresh, sharp, handmade, water-filled, and acoustically alive. An ordinary human multiverse traveler should treat it with the same care one would give to a delicate architectural interior that happens to be submerged—and with the caution one would give to a tidal machine that has not yet learned your body.

Below is a full preparation guide.

---

## 1. Understand what you are entering

The most important preparation is mental.

This is a room-sized underwater basin, built of tile, brick, timber, bronze, iron, rope, and leather. It is young. The sea has only recently entered it. The mortar is still pale. The edges are still sharp. The place has not been rounded, colonized, or softened. It is not an ecosystem in the ordinary sense. It is a constructed hollow, newly given over to water.

That means you should expect:

- **No shore, no bank, no place to stand and rest.**  
  Everything is water and built work. If you tire, you must float, tread, or exit.

- **A narrow entry throat.**  
  This is the main hazard. The basin opens only through a tile-lined passage. It may admit current, surge, and sound. It can snag gear, cloth, hair, or flesh.

- **Sharp, fresh surfaces.**  
  Hand-fired tile edges, broken chips, crisp brick corners, tool-marked wood, and iron fittings can cut or abrade.

- **Fresh lime and mortar dust.**  
  Lime can be alkaline and irritating. Pale dust may cloud when the current moves. Do not treat it as harmless silt.

- **Warm, bright, loud water.**  
  The warmth is pleasant, but the brightness and sound can be intense. The room may feel bodily, close, and overwhelming.

- **No animal life.**  
  No fish, crabs, shells, or moving creatures. There are plants in built containers, but the absence of fauna is striking. Do not expect companionship, movement, or biological reassurance. The place may feel sterile, sanctified, experimental, or simply too new.

- **A sense of recent making.**  
  The architecture declares hands. You are entering something made, not something grown. Respect that.

The right attitude is not adventurous conquest. It is careful, quiet visiting.

---

## 2. Decide whether you are qualified to enter

An ordinary human may be a multiverse traveler, but ordinary human lungs, skin, ears, and nerves still apply.

You should not enter unless you can do at least the following:

- Swim confidently in open water.
- Remain calm while submerged in confined spaces.
- Control your breathing under stress.
- Move without stirring the bottom or striking walls.
- Use a line or guide to return the way you came.
- Manage basic diving or freediving safety.
