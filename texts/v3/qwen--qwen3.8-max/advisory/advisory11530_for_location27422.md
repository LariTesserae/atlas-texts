# Preparing to Visit the High Wet Basin-City

The first thing to prepare is not your pack. It is your manner.

This is a place that does not ask for boldness, conquest, noise, or improvisation. It asks you to arrive as if you are entering a room that has already been arranged with great care: a room made of warm stone, shallow water, wet reeds, young cliffs, and silence. An ordinary human multiverse traveler should prepare for this basin-city less like an explorer and more like a careful guest, a temple attendant, or a person learning to move through a landscape where water has priority and human presence is secondary.

The central rule is simple: **do not become a disturbance.**

Everything else follows from that.

---

## 1. Understand What the Place Is Asking

Before choosing gear, clothing, food, or travel methods, sit with the character of the location.

This is a high basin, newly formed, still sharp-edged and mineral-bright, but warm and saturated. Water is everywhere, yet not violent. The city is built not against wetness but through it. Channels, weirs, cisterns, causeways, stairways, and terraces exist to give water a path. The settlement is spacious, sparse, and quiet. It has urban order without crowding. It has infrastructure without industry. It has life without animal life.

That last point is crucial.

If the basin truly has no fauna — no insects, birds, worms, fish, tracks, nests, calls, or scavengers — then a human visitor is not merely a traveler. You are an animal body entering a place that has no ordinary animal movement. Your breath, sweat, skin flakes, hair, footfalls, voice, waste, food crumbs, and microbiome all become unusually significant. You may be the most animal thing there.

So preparation must include humility.

You are not going there to fill the silence. You are going there to avoid breaking it.

---

## 2. Decide Whether You Should Visit at All

For a multiverse traveler, the first practical question is often: *Can I go?*

Here, the better question is: *Should I go?*

A fauna-free wetland city is ecologically and spiritually delicate. Even if it appears stable, its stability may depend on absence. The introduction of foreign microbes, spores, seeds, fungi, skin bacteria, food residues, or animal-derived materials could matter more than in a normal world. A single overlooked seed in a boot tread, a fragment of wool, a crumb of meat, a drop of honey, an insect egg caught in a seam — any of these could be a small catastrophe in a place where animals are absent.

If your multiverse travel can be observed remotely, send only instruments. If instruments must go, seal them. If a person must go, send one person, or at most two. A larger group becomes louder, dirtier, smellier, and harder to control.

A first visit should be short. Daylight hours only, if possible. Overnight only if you are fully prepared for waste, shelter, silence, and restraint.
