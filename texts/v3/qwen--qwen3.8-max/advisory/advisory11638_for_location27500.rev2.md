An ordinary human multiverse traveler should not prepare for this place as if it were merely a deep-sea cave, a biological anomaly, or a reality-thin zone. It is all of those things, but its essential nature is closer to a wound that has begun to dream of becoming a room. The preparation must therefore be physical, psychological, ecological, and ontological. You are not only protecting yourself from pressure, darkness, cold, and strange time. You are preparing to be tolerated by a place that does not behave like open space.

The first rule is this: do not prepare to enter. Prepare to be accepted.

---

## 1. Decide whether physical visitation is appropriate

Before anything else, understand that an ordinary human body is not welcome here in its natural state. There is no air. There is no safe breathing pocket. There is only dense, cool, mineral liquid and pressure that will not negotiate with lungs. The chamber is also extremely narrow, biologically crowded, and easily contaminated. If your multiverse capability includes remote perception, proxy forms, dream-walking, or non-invasive observation, consider those first.

If you cannot bring a proper deep-pressure, closed-loop, liquid-compatible life-support system, do not attempt physical entry. This is not a place where courage substitutes for engineering. The pocket does not care that your intentions are reverent. It will simply continue, and you will become part of its slow recycling in a way you did not intend.

If you do enter physically, your objective should not be exploration, sampling, or conquest. The correct objective is witnessing. If you must name a purpose, make it small: to be present briefly, to observe without taking, to leave without widening the wound.

---

## 2. The central attitude: become less urgent

The pocket is saturated with patience. Its water shifts in slow internal layers. Its life grows by accretion. Its darkness is not empty but compressed. If you arrive with the ordinary human tempo — eager, curious, reaching, measuring, wanting to know — you will feel wrong immediately, and the place may respond by becoming less accessible.

Prepare by practicing the reduction of urgency.

You should train yourself to move as if motion is a minor concession, not a default. Do not rehearse bravery. Rehearse restraint. Rehearse not touching. Rehearse not naming. Rehearse the discipline of seeing without insisting on what you see.

The pocket may feel as though it is listening without ears. Whether that is literal or merely the psychological effect of a reality-thin environment, the practical result is the same: high emotional intensity, aggressive curiosity, or panicked resistance may make the space feel denser, smaller, and less navigable.

Cultivate a mental state closer to:

- “I am here briefly.”
- “I do not need to understand everything.”
- “I will not correct what I find.”
- “I will not take what is not offered.”
- “If I am not accepted, I will leave.”

This is not mysticism for its own sake. It is operational hygiene.

## 3. Physical preparation: pressure, cold, liquid, and confinement

The environment is fully submerged, cool, dense, and enclosed. The chamber is described as no larger than a curled body, though its scale is unreliable. That means your equipment must be compact, smooth, quiet, and pressure-compatible. Rigid suits, bulky tanks, protruding instruments, sharp edges, and large helmets are poor choices. They will snag on filaments, scrape young basalt, and violate the intimacy of the space.

### Body suit

Use a flexible, pressure-compensated environmental membrane rather than a bulky atmospheric suit. It should:

- Maintain internal pressure without large gas spaces.
- Be abrasion-resistant but externally soft.
- Have a low-friction, non-reflective surface.
- Be chemically inert and non-nutritive to microbial or filamentous growth.
- Seal completely against spores, threads, and fine particulate matter.
- Have no external loops, straps, or dangling components that can catch in the fissure.
- Be matte black, deep gray, or dark blue. Nothing shiny. Nothing reflective.

The chamber is crowded with life. You do not want your suit to become a surface for colonization, and you do not want to tear living mats while entering.

### Breathing system

Do not bring a system that produces bubbles. Exhaled gas would be wrong here in both practical and symbolic terms. There is no air ceiling, no place for gas to collect, and the introduction of bubbles may disturb the water’s internal layering, the bead-rain, or the biological equilibrium.

Use one of the following, depending on available technology:

1. **Closed-cycle liquid breathing**  
   A fully fluid-filled respiratory loop with oxygenated carrier fluid, CO₂ scrubbing, temperature regulation, and pressure compensation. This is preferable because it eliminates gas spaces and reduces the risk of squeeze or embolism.

2. **Closed-circuit oxygenated membrane respiration**  
   If your multiverse technology allows, a gill-like membrane system may extract usable oxygen from the surrounding liquid, but do not rely on the pocket’s water alone. Its chemistry may be too mineral, too dense, too old, or too biologically occupied. Carry your own oxygen reserve.

3. **Internal chemical oxygen loop**  
   For very short visits, a self-contained chemical oxygen system with scrubbers may suffice, provided it produces no gas venting and no heat plume.

Whatever you use, it must be silent. No hissing. No pumping vibrations. No mechanical cycling louder than a heartbeat.

### Thermal protection

The water is cool but not freezing. That sounds manageable until you remember that the chamber slows processes. Cold here may feel patient rather than sharp, but it will still lower your reaction speed, dull your thinking, and make your movements heavier.

Use active thermal regulation, but make it quiet and low-emission. Do not create warm currents. The pocket’s water moves very little; a heat plume could disturb suspended threads, alter chemical gradients, or attract unwanted biological attention.

### Mobility

Do not bring fins. The space is too tight, the growth too dense, and the water too strange. Fins are for open volume. Here, movement should be done with small adjustments of hands, shoulders, hips, and breath. Practice slow, minimal motion in confined water.

Your body should be able to:

- Curl into a compact posture.
- Rotate without kicking.
- Back out along a crooked path.
- Remain motionless for long periods.
- Move by touch rather than sight.

If you cannot remain still while something brushes your face, you are not ready.

## 4. Sensory preparation: learn to inhabit darkness

The darkness is complete and not simple. It has weight. It presses. It fills spaces between filaments. Occasional bioluminescence does not illuminate the chamber; it only proves how much remains unseen.

Do not prepare by bringing powerful lights. Bright light would be a violence here. It would disturb the biological logic, overstimulate phosphorescent responses, and probably distort your own perception of the space. If you must carry illumination, carry the faintest possible source and use it only in extremity. Better: no light at all.

### Vision

Your eyes will be almost useless. Even if your suit includes enhanced optics, the chamber may defeat them. Distances do not behave honestly. Objects may appear in multiple positions until attention forces them to choose. The darkness may register not as absence of image but as presence without outline.

Prepare for visual frustration. You may feel that you are failing to see. That feeling is correct. You are not meant to see this place the way you see a street, a forest, or a room.

If your helmet has a visor, make it non-reflective. Reflections may confuse you in a space where identity and position are unstable. Do not rely on visual landmarks.

### Touch

Touch will be more reliable than sight, but even touch must be used gently. The chamber is packed with mats, tubes, threads, and living tissue. You should not push through it. You should feel for openings, pressure gradients, and the subtle difference between stone, growth, and water.

Use gloves that protect against sharp basalt while preserving sensitivity. They should not have hard knuckles or rough surfaces. They should feel more like a second skin than a tool.

Practice identifying textures in total darkness:

- Stone.
- Filament mats.
- Tubular growths.
- Mineral crusts.
- Suspended threads.
- The denser feel of the bead-rain, if you are unfortunate enough to encounter it too closely.

### Hearing

Sound is absorbed. What exists is pressure-tick, internal creak, filament hush. Do not bring active sonar unless you are prepared to harm the chamber and yourself. If you must use sensing, use passive pressure sensors at extremely low intensity.

Train yourself to interpret silence not as emptiness but as saturation. The quiet is full. If you wait for noise, you will miss the subtle changes that matter: a slowing of the bead-rain, a tightening of the mosses, a withdrawal of faint phosphorescence.

### Proprioception and spatial sense

Your sense of body position will be challenged. The chamber may feel intimate and vast at once. A wall may be near and yet recede into depth when something moves inside it. You may feel curled into a small recess while also sensing a drop without floor.

Prepare by practicing in confined, dark, neutral-buoyancy environments. Learn to distinguish actual body position from imagined body position. Learn not to thrash when your spatial sense contradicts itself.

If you feel the chamber fold around you, do not panic. Panic makes you larger in every way that matters.

## 5. Time preparation: bring anchors, but do not trust them absolutely

Time behaves uneasily here. Changes occur slowly but not always forward. A frond may unfurl over decades and contract in a moment. Crystals form, dissolve, and re-form as if the stone is rehearsing possibilities. The young rock is sharp not only because it is new, but because it is being repeatedly renewed by some hesitation in its nature.

Do not rely on a single clock.

### Multiple time anchors

Carry at least three independent measures of duration:

1. **A mechanical or spring-driven timer**  
   Simple, quiet, and not dependent on local electronic stability.

2. **A physiological anchor**  
   Heartbeat, pulse, or controlled respiratory cycle, if your breathing system allows it. Do not rely on this alone, because the cold and density may slow you down without your noticing.

3. **A multiversal or ontological recall device**  
   If your travel tradition includes return anchors, use one that does not emit active signals into the pocket. It should be passive, internal, or shielded. A loud recall beacon may act like a hook in soft tissue.

Expect these anchors to disagree. That is not necessarily malfunction. If your mechanical timer says seven minutes have passed but your body says an hour has passed, do not argue with either. Use thresholds rather than exact durations.

### Exit criteria

Decide your exit conditions before entry. Do not decide inside.

Examples:

- Oxygen reserve reaches a fixed safe margin.
- Core temperature drops below a set threshold.
- Any suit breach occurs.
- The entrance no longer feels present.
- The chamber repeatedly seems to inhale around you.
- The bead-rain slows noticeably in your presence.
- The larger presence moves more than once.
- You begin to feel that you have always been there.
- You see yourself, your gear, or a familiar object in two places at once.
- You experience strong affection, belonging, or desire to remain.

That last one is important. The pocket’s beauty is severe and secret. It may produce the feeling that you have finally found a place where urgency is unnecessary. That feeling can be dangerous. Do not mistake seduction for safety.

## 6. Ecological preparation: arrive sterile, leave absent

The pocket is a closed, patient engine. Every death is folded back into the living mass. Nothing leaves quickly. A shed filament may drift for years. A collapsed tube becomes a foundation. The chamber is crowded into pattern.

An ordinary human traveler is a contamination event.

You carry microbes, skin cells, chemical residues, heat, electromagnetic noise, and foreign intention. In an ordinary ocean, these might dilute. Here, they may become permanent.

### Sterilization

Before descent:

- Shower with neutral, non-reactive cleansing agents approved for xenobiological contact.
- Avoid perfumes, oils, insect repellents, sunscreen residues, or medicinal odors.
- Sterilize the exterior of your suit without leaving toxic residue.
- Flush breathing loops and seals.
- Inspect for trapped spores, fibers, or particles from previous worlds.
- Ensure waste systems are sealed and redundant.

Do not bring organic offerings. Do not bring food. Do not bring seeds, wood, paper, leather, natural fibers, or anything biologically expressive. The pocket does not need gifts. It will incorporate what it can, and that is precisely why you must give it nothing.

### No samples

Do not collect filaments, tubes, mineral chips, water, rain beads, or structures. Do not scrape growth. Do not peel mats to see what is beneath. Do not break basalt to test sharpness.

If a fragment attaches itself to you, do not pull it off violently. If possible, wait for it to release. If it does not, exit and remove it during decontamination using methods that do not kill it unnecessarily. If it must remain, isolate it in a sealed containment layer until specialists can decide how to return or preserve it without harm.

### No tools unless necessary

Avoid metal tools. If you must carry something, make it smooth, inert, and blunt. No knives. No cutters. No probes. If you become entangled, cutting may cause more damage than stillness.

If you need to free yourself from filaments, use slow retraction, not force. The mats are not merely plants; they are the chamber’s skin. Tearing them may not be an injury to a single organism so much as a disruption of the room itself.

## 7. Psychological preparation: practice not being the center

This place will test the ordinary human assumption that experience is organized around the observer.

The chamber may duplicate objects. It may make distances dishonest. It may create the impression that entering was not an event but a realization. It may suggest that the pocket has always been around you. It may make you feel watched without providing a watcher.

Prepare by practicing these mental disciplines:

### Soft attention

Do not stare. Do not fixate. Fixation may force unstable geometry to choose a position, and that choice may be unpleasant. Let your awareness remain broad. If something appears twice, notice both appearances without demanding one be true.

### Non-naming

The mind will want to classify:

- “This is moss.”
- “This is coral.”
- “This is a machine.”
- “This is a guardian.”
- “This is a womb.”
- “This is a grave.”

Resist final labels. The pocket is not obedient to human categories. If you name too quickly, you may stop perceiving what is actually happening.

### Emotional neutrality

Fear is not the only dangerous emotion. Awe, longing, grief, and love can also make you clumsy. If you feel overwhelmed by beauty, slow down. If you feel a sudden certainty that the place knows you, check that certainty. If you feel forgiven, cleansed, chosen, or recognized, treat those feelings as environmental effects until proven otherwise.

### Identity anchoring

Because the chamber may blur boundaries, carry a small internal anchor. Not necessarily an object. It might be:

- A remembered sequence of touches.
- A simple phrase repeated silently.
- A rhythm of pulse.
- A memory of ordinary geometry: a doorway, a table, a road, a window.

Do not make the anchor dramatic. It should be plain. The pocket is full of strange significance; your anchor should be deliberately ordinary.

## 8. Preparing for the descent

The pocket lies at the bottom of a sunless basin, below cold shelving stone and black sediment. The sea floor has split in a thin, uneven seam. The crack is young. Its edges are sharp, glassy, and abrupt.

You must reach this place without scarring it.

### External support

Even if the final entry is solitary, do not attempt the dive without external support. Your support team should remain outside the basin’s wound and monitor:

- Your life signs.
- Your position until the rock and reality-thinning defeat tracking.
- Your planned exit thresholds.
- Emergency recall protocols.
- Decompression and time-stabilization facilities.

They should not send signals into the fissure unless necessary. Active pings, magical summons, or multiversal beacons may act like pressure hooks.

### Descent suit behavior

During descent, keep your profile compact. Avoid thrusters that disturb sediment. If you must move through the basin, move slowly and let suspended particles settle. The black sediment is not just dirt; it is the accumulation of long quietness. Do not cloud the approach.

### Finding the seam

Do not search aggressively. The fissure is thin, uneven, and young. It may be felt before it is seen: a line of colder water, a faint chemical difference, a pressure irregularity, or a sense that the stone has not finished being separated.

When you find it, do not enter immediately. Pause. Let your suit equalize. Let your mind slow. Observe whether the aperture seems to breathe inward.

If it does, do not match it by forcing your body. Match it by reducing resistance. If your breathing system allows, slow your pulse. Relax your shoulders. Make yourself less separate from the surrounding pressure.

If the entrance feels closed, do not pry. If it feels open but your body tightens, do not ignore that. An ordinary human’s dread is sometimes a valid instrument.

## 9. Entering the fissure

The passage is too narrow for comfortable passage. It is crooked. It is choked with filaments. It does not remain constant.

### Before entry

Check:

- All seals.
- Oxygen reserve.
- Thermal system.
- No external snag hazards.
- No lights active.
- No active sensors emitting.
- Exit threshold memorized.
- Support team informed of final commitment.

Then enter slowly.

### During entry

Do not kick. Do not push hard against the walls. Use fingertips. Feel for the sharpest basalt and avoid it. The edges are new and may cut a suit. If you feel a scrape, stop immediately and assess. A slow leak in this environment is worse than a sudden one outside, because here a leak becomes an exchange.

The filaments may brush your helmet, gloves, and limbs. Do not react as if attacked. They may simply be touching. If they resist, wait. If they part, proceed. If they do not part, withdraw.

If the aperture seems to breathe inward, enter with that inwardness. Do not think of it as being swallowed. Think of it as being allowed.

If at any point you feel that the pocket has already been around you for a long time, do not let that thought expand. Acknowledge it and continue checking your equipment. Ontological strangeness does not excuse safety negligence.

## 10. Inside the chamber: how to be there

Once inside, stop.

Do not immediately look for the center, the rain, the structures, or the larger presence. Let the chamber settle around you. Let your own pressure signature soften. Give the place a chance to decide whether you are a disturbance or a passing object.

### Posture

Curl slightly. Keep limbs close. Do not spread out. The chamber is intimate, and your body should become as small as possible without compromising life support.

If you can, avoid contact with the floor mats. If contact is unavoidable, distribute weight and do not press into the living tissue. The floor is not a surface; it is a packed history of growth and collapse.

If you must touch a wall, use the backs of your fingers first. Let the filaments register you before you commit.

### Movement

Move only when necessary. If you move, move as if the water is listening. Slow hand strokes. Minimal rotation. No sudden turns.

Do not swim through the chamber as if it is a room. It is more like the inside of a body. You are not crossing it; you are being briefly contained by it.

### Sight

If your eyes strain, stop straining. Let the darkness remain darkness. If faint blue-green shimmer appears, do not chase it. It is not illumination offered for your benefit. It is a response inside the chamber. Watch it peripherally, if at all.

If a cluster of tubes appears in two places, do not alternate your gaze rapidly between them. Do not try to prove which is real. Let it remain ambiguous unless your safety requires a choice.

### The bead-rain

One wall of the pocket contains a sheet of darker droplets descending slowly through water. They are denser than the surrounding liquid, black, perfectly round, falling without splash or sound. When they touch the floor, they spread briefly and rise again.

Do not touch the rain if you can avoid it.

Do not pass through it unless absolutely necessary.

Do not attempt to collect a bead.

Do not assume it is water, oil, blood, memory, or condensed time. It may be all of those things or none. Its behavior is enough. Let it be.

If a bead contacts your suit, do not wipe at it. Watch its response. If it spreads, then rises, remain still. If it stains, do not attempt to clean it inside the chamber. Deal with contamination only after exit.

The rain is not falling toward anything. Remember that. Human perception will want destination. The rain does not require it.

### The structures

You may notice small arrangements: half-rings of valves, fused tubes, lattices of hardened threads, stair-like overlaps of thin stones. These are not ruins and not technology. They are habits left behind by organisms that build the way trees grow rings.

Do not interpret them as messages. Do not rearrange them. Do not touch them to test stability. Do not try to complete them.

If they seem to wait for recognition, do not provide recognition. Acknowledge them with the same neutrality you would give to a stone. If your mind insists they are deliberate, let that insistence pass.

### Fauna

Fauna is rare. You may see a translucent worm, a pale drifting shape, or something that is not an individual but a temporary agreement among smaller beings. Do not follow it. Do not attempt to attract it. Do not imitate its motion.

If it approaches, remain still. If it touches you, allow the touch without reaction if your suit remains intact. If it enters a fold of equipment, do not chase it. You are not here to inventory souls.

### The larger presence

At the lowest corner, something rests. It may be tissue, colony, creature, or the chamber’s own capacity to be aware. It is difficult to distinguish from vegetation.

Do not focus on it directly.

Do not approach it.

Do not try to communicate unless communication is unmistakably initiated in a way that does not require you to violate your own safety or the chamber’s integrity.

If the pocket seems to inhale around it, become still. Lower your metabolic rate if possible. Do not withdraw abruptly. Abrupt motion may be more disturbing than slow retreat.

If the presence moves, do not describe it to yourself as attack unless attack is obvious. It may simply be changing position. The entire chamber may respond to that change. Your task is to remain harmless.

If you feel that it notices you, do not offer it your identity. Do not mentally project your name, history, desires, or fears. Offer only stillness. If it wants more, you are probably not prepared. In that case, prepare to leave.

## 11. The problem of scale and geometry

The chamber’s scale refuses to remain honest. Measured by pressure and touch, it is intimate. Measured by motion, it may feel deeper than the walls allow. The far side may be a body’s length away and also recessed into a depth without floor.

Prepare by refusing to demand consistency.

If the chamber feels larger, do not explore the new distance. It may not be real in the way you need. If it feels smaller, do not panic. It may not be crushing you; it may be remembering its size.

If you need to orient toward the exit, do not rely on visual direction alone. Use the memory of pressure changes during entry. The entrance may not be where it looked, but it may still be where the water behaves differently.

If you cannot find it, stop. Do not circle. Do not sweep your hands wildly. Reduce motion. Let the chamber settle. Sometimes the entrance is more available when you stop trying to escape.

## 12. Emotional preparation for the beauty

There is beauty here, but it is severe and secret. It is not the beauty of revelation. It is the beauty of refusal. The place does not open itself for you. It remains closed, packed, patient, and full.

You may feel:

- Grief without cause.
- A sense of being held.
- A desire to sleep.
- A sense of being unnecessary.
- A sense of being forgiven.
- A sense of being examined.
- A sense that ordinary space is shallow.
- A sense that the chamber is more real than the world above.

Do not treat these feelings as truths or threats. Treat them as pressure. They are part of the environment. Let them pass through you without becoming decisions.

If you begin to love the pocket, be especially careful. Love can make you stay too long. Love can make you touch what should not be touched. Love can make you believe you belong somewhere that merely tolerates you.

## 13. Emergency protocols

### Suit breach

If your suit is cut by young basalt or abraded by mineralized growth, seal it immediately with flexible patch material compatible with liquid environments. Do not inject expanding foam unless it is ecologically inert and absolutely necessary. Expanding material may tear living mats.

If water enters your breathing loop, do not gasp. If you are trained for liquid breathing, allow the transition. If not, activate emergency internal oxygen supply and exit.

### Lost entrance

If the fissure cannot be found:

1. Stop.
2. Reduce motion.
3. Check oxygen and thermal status.
4. Recall the entry pressure pattern.
5. Use passive sensors only.
6. Do not create noise.
7. If the chamber feels denser, wait.
8. If the chamber feels clearer toward one direction, move toward that clarity with extreme slowness.
9. If nothing resolves before safety margins decline, activate your multiversal recall if it does not harm the pocket.

If recall would harm the pocket, you face a serious ethical emergency. That is why passive, low-impact return methods are preferable.

### Entanglement

If filaments attach to your suit:

- Do not pull.
- Do not cut.
- Relax the limb involved.
- Slowly reverse the motion that caused contact.
- If the filaments tighten, stop and wait.
- If they begin to incorporate your suit into the mat, use the least harmful release method available.
- If release requires damaging large amounts of growth, choose self-preservation but plan decontamination and ecological containment carefully afterward.

### Time anomaly

If your clocks reverse, if you remember actions you have not yet taken, if the chamber seems to rehearse your movements before you make them, do not fight it.

Use your body anchor. Return to the simplest fact: breath, pulse, suit integrity. If the anomaly persists and your safety margins are threatened, exit by the most direct harmless route.

### Presence response

If the larger presence moves and the chamber inhales:

- Become still.
- Reduce light, if any.
- Reduce active systems.
- Do not project thought toward it.
- Do not flee suddenly.
- If the bead-rain slows, prepare to leave.
- If the mosses tighten, you are too present. Make yourself smaller in intention and motion.
- If phosphorescence withdraws, the chamber is asking for less of you. Give it less.

### Psychological overwhelm

If you feel your identity softening:

- Touch a familiar part of your suit.
- Count a simple sequence.
- Recall an ordinary place with clear geometry.
- Do not analyze the experience while inside.
- Leave before the softening becomes pleasant.

## 14. Leaving the chamber

Do not leave as if escaping, but do not leave as if departing is optional. Exit should be deliberate, quiet, and unhurried.

Signs you should leave include:

- Any safety threshold reached.
- The chamber becoming noticeably denser.
- The bead-rain slowing or altering.
- Phosphorescence withdrawing.
- The larger presence shifting.
- Your thoughts becoming unusually poetic, resigned, or serene in a way that reduces vigilance.
- The feeling that you have been inside longer than you think.
- The feeling that you have not entered yet.
- The feeling that the chamber is no longer empty but also not occupied by anything visible.

To leave, reverse your entry posture. Move toward the remembered pressure seam. Do not push through filaments. If they resist, wait and reduce intention. If the entrance seems absent, do not claw at the rock. Find the place where the water feels youngest, sharpest, most unresolved.

When you pass back through the fissure, do not sigh with relief if that relief becomes a burst of motion. Keep your body controlled until you are fully outside the wound.

## 15. Aftercare: decontamination and reintegration

Leaving the chamber is not the end of preparation. The pocket may remain in your senses, your memory, and possibly your biology.

### Ecological decontamination

Your suit must be treated as a possible vector in both directions:

- Do not rinse with harsh chemicals near the basin.
- Do not release any water, spores, threads, or beads into another environment.
- Isolate the suit in a sealed, sterile chamber.
- Remove any attached growth using xenobiological containment procedures.
- If a bead or filament remains on the suit, do not assume it is inert.
- Scan for ontological contamination: repeated patterns, impossible residues, objects that seem to occupy multiple positions, memories of the suit behaving strangely.

If you brought anything out by accident, it should be preserved or returned according to specialist guidance. Do not keep it as a souvenir.

### Physical medical care

Check for:

- Pressure injury.
- Thermal stress.
- Hypoxia.
- CO₂ retention.
- Skin irritation from suit seals.
- Microabrasions.
- Neurological symptoms: slowed thought, altered time sense, visual persistence, dissociation.
- Respiratory trauma if liquid breathing was used.

Because time may have behaved strangely, medical monitoring should include observation over a stable duration before you travel to another world. Do not immediately jump onward. You may carry residual temporal unevenness.

### Psychological care

The pocket can leave a mark not through horror but through intimacy. You may find ordinary spaces too empty, too bright, too fast. You may feel that surface reality lacks density. You may dream of black rain, soft filaments, or a room that does not need you.

Debrief slowly. Do not force yourself to explain it immediately. Record impressions while they are fresh, but do not mistake the first explanation for the true one.

Useful reintegration practices:

- Sit in a simple room with one light source.
- Touch ordinary objects one at a time.
- Drink warm, non-stimulating fluid.
- Walk on stable ground.
- Speak in plain sentences.
- Avoid making major decisions for a while.

If you begin to believe that the pocket was more real than everything else, do not fight the belief violently. Test it gently against ordinary life. The pocket’s patience may have entered you. That can be valuable, but it can also become withdrawal.

## 18. What the visit will feel like

If you do everything right, the first thing you will notice is not fear. It may be compression.

The water will feel older than the sea outside. Not colder, exactly, but more settled. Your movements will seem to belong to someone else, delayed and heavy. The darkness will press close, and your eyes will search for something to hold. They will find nothing. Then your skin will begin to understand the chamber before your mind does.

The entrance will not feel like a doorway. It will feel like a concession.

Inside, you will be aware of density before beauty. Every surface is occupied. The water itself feels occupied. You will realize that you are not in a room containing life; you are inside life that has arranged itself into the shape of a room. The mosses, tubes, fronds, and beaded strands will not seem decorative. They will seem inevitable.

Then the strangeness will start. A drifting spore will take too long to cross a short distance. A bead of black rain will fall without arriving. A structure will appear familiar, then appear again somewhere it should not be. You will feel the chamber is small, then feel it open into a depth that has no floor. You will want to reach for certainty. Do not.

If the pocket accepts you, the acceptance will not feel warm. It will feel like being allowed to remain in a place where you are unnecessary. That is important. You are not expected. You are not central. You are a brief foreign body in a slow engine. Your safety depends partly on equipment, but also on your ability to be insignificant.

At some point, you may sense the larger presence. Not as a monster, but as a change in the room’s attention. The water may become slightly more dense. The mosses may tighten. The faint life-light may withdraw. You will understand, without evidence, that the chamber is not alone.

If you are wise, you will not try to meet it. You will become still. You will let the place inhale around you. You will wait until the stillness decides what you are.

And if you leave successfully, you will carry something difficult to describe: not a secret, exactly, but a weight. You will know that there are places where reality does not open itself to witnesses. It merely continues, packed and patient, in the dark beneath the lowest water.

## 19. The deepest preparation

The deepest preparation is ethical.

Do not visit this pocket if your intention is to exploit it, name it publicly, harvest it, prove it, or make yourself significant within it. Do not enter if you cannot bear to leave no trace. Do not enter if you need the place to mean something for you.

Prepare to be disappointed in the ordinary sense: there will be no revelation, no grand architecture, no voice, no clear answer. There will only be darkness full of threads, black rain inside water, sharp young stone, and a slow living mass that may not care whether you understand it.

But if you prepare well — if your suit is quiet, your mind is slow, your hands are empty, and your intention is humble — you may be allowed to witness a beauty that refuses to be seen.

That is enough. It should be enough.
