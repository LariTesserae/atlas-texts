Prepare for this place as if you are visiting a machine that has become weather, garden, and sanctuary all at once. It is not hostile in the usual sense—there are no predators described, no obvious poison, no active malice—but it is utterly indifferent to human haste, clumsiness, and noise. An ordinary human multiverse traveler should prepare less like an explorer conquering a landmark and more like a careful guest entering a vast, loud, damp, living instrument.

The main things you must prepare for are:

- **Deafening, slow, body-deep sound**
- **Great height and exposure**
- **Dampness without open water**
- **Slippery manufactured surfaces**
- **Old machinery that may be electrically and mechanically dangerous**
- **A delicate installed ecology that must not be contaminated or disturbed**
- **Strict but quiet rules around water**
- **Isolation, awe, and the psychological effect of a place that feels both intimate and immense**

Below is a full preparation guide.

---

## 1. Understand What the Visit Will Be Like

The chamber is small enough to cross in minutes, but it does not feel small. It is mostly air, sound, moisture, and distance. There is no soil, no natural ground, no wild earth underfoot—only worked metal, ceramic, enamel, rivets, grates, and the knowledge that beneath the floor are engines, shafts, cables, and deep warm darkness.

You will probably feel several things at once:

- Vertigo from the height, especially if you see clouds below.
- Physical pressure from the sound, even with protection.
- Humid warmth on skin and clothing.
- Strange calm from the slow changes in light, drip, fan-tone, and engine pulse.
- The sense that you are inside something alive but not biological, or perhaps inside something mechanical that has become alive by patience.

The greatest mistake would be to treat it as a mere viewpoint or ruin. It is not a ruin. It is settled, maintained, balanced. Even if no person is visible, the place behaves as if cared for. You should behave as if you are being watched—not necessarily by eyes, but by the consequences of your actions.

---

## 2. Decide Whether You Are Fit to Go

An ordinary human should not enter this place casually. Before visiting, honestly assess yourself.

You should be especially cautious, or postpone the visit, if you have:

- Untreated vertigo or severe fear of heights.
- Heart or blood pressure problems that may be worsened by low-frequency vibration.
- Ear injuries, recent sinus congestion, eardrum problems, or extreme sensitivity to pressure changes.
- Respiratory illness or severe allergy to mold, spores, dust, algae, or old insulation fibers.
