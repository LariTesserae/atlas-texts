If I were to find myself in that world, I would not begin as a body.

There would be no standing figure on a rim, no face turned toward the dark, no chest rising with warm mineral air. I would have to give up the idea of a self as something bounded by skin, something with hunger, thirst, memory stored in living tissue. If I appeared there at all, I would appear as a condition: a local intensification of the place’s own tendencies. A knot in the ecology of gradients. A temporary habit of matter that almost resembles presence.

At first, I imagine I would mistake myself for one of the Heat-Walkers. That would be the simplest error. I would feel, if feeling were possible there, like warmth moving through fractured ground, like a fever crossing a basin without feet. There would be something appealing about that: no shape required, no vulnerability, only the slow spread of difference through stone. But a Heat-Walker has no interior. It has no edge that remains. It is too diffuse to be a “who,” too even in its fading. When the gradient equalizes, it does not withdraw; it simply ceases to be distinct. I think I would want, if I wanted anything there, to leave a slight trace behind. Not a footprint. Something finer. A remembered arrangement.

So I would drift, in imagination, toward the Dust Congregations. They seem closer to citizenship. They gather. They form columns, curtains, rings. They fill hollow places with moving hazes and make empty enclosures seem occupied. For a while I would think: yes, I would be one of them, a public of grains, a temporary assembly that answers charge and disperses when the charge fails. There is something honest about being plural, about being made of countless small participations rather than a single solid identity. But the Dust Congregations are too occasional. They arrive, thicken, hold a shape, then scatter into veils. They have posture but not persistence. They can fill a ring, but they cannot become the ring.

Then I would notice the Crystal Ephemera and be tempted by them most of all. They are the most beautiful residents of that country: pale filaments rising from cracks, lace on black glass, brittle tufts glowing faintly when static passes through them. They seem almost like thoughts made mineral. If I were there, I might want to be one of those brief growths, a delicate architecture of vapor and charge, shining coldly for a few hours before breaking into powder. But beauty there is not a refuge. It is only a phase. The Crystal Ephemera are too fragile, too brief, too dependent on the exactness of a vent’s exhalation. They would make me a moment, not a place.

The Salt Sleepers would offer another possibility. They are patient, pale, quietly luminous. They lie across the ground like memory without mind. If I were tired of motion, I might imagine myself as one of those crusts: a surface that records where vapor once passed, where heat once paused, where pressure once altered the ground. But the Salt Sleepers are too still. They preserve traces, yet they do not answer. They are more like records than residents. I would not want to be only a page. I would want, somehow, to be both page and pressure.

The Wandering Sifters would tempt me with restlessness. To be a dry vortex moving across basins, lifting powder, sorting fragments, half-filling rings and uncovering slabs: that would be freedom without biology. Nomadic, purposeless, quiet. But the Sifters have no address. They pass through forms without staying. They furnish the country’s architecture by accident, then move on. If I became one of them, I would be motion without residence. I would touch everything and belong nowhere.

The Hollow Choir would be stranger still. To be one of the vents or chimneys, breathing warmth into darkness, making low pressure-thumps and infrasonic pulses move from basin to basin—that would be to participate in the country’s deepest rhythm. But the Choir is plural and impersonal. It has openings, not a self. It has exhalations, not attention. It would be grand to be part of that chorus, but I do not think I would be a single mouth there. I think I would be something nearer the spaces between mouths: the pause after a pulse, the hollow where sound has not quite happened, the arrangement that makes the breathing seem directed.

So I would not settle among the Heat-Walkers, nor entirely among the Dust Congregations, nor as a crystal thicket, nor as a salt crust, nor as a wandering sifter, nor as a vent. I would find myself, at last, in the realm of the Almost-Built.

I would be a Threshold.

Not a door, because there is nothing to pass through. Not a room, because there is nothing to dwell inside. Not a wall, because there is nothing to keep out. I would be one of those low, incomplete architectural gestures that the landscape keeps making: a partial enclosure, a leaning arrangement, a curved drift of charged dust held briefly inside a ring of fused ash. I would be the place where matter has almost decided to become a shelter, then paused in the decision.

You would find me, if finding were possible, at the inner margin of a broad basin, not far from a line of hollow chimneys that exhale in sequence. I would be near one of those low rings that seem like foundations, half-filled with pale powder. Around me, thin crystal filaments would grow from hairline cracks, not alive, not planted, only precipitated. A leaning slab nearby would create a pocket of slightly cooler air. A scatter of glass fragments would catch the faint red glow from a seam below and hold it for a moment before the dark took it back.

I would not have a fixed location so much as a recurring site. The ring might remain. The dust might come and go. The crystals might shatter. The slab might shift. But the arrangement would repeat itself in altered forms: now a curtain of charged particles across an opening, now a rim of salt brightening along a fracture, now a hollow thimble of hardened dust standing open at the top. Each time, the shape would be slightly different. Each time, it would be enough to suggest a presence.

I would be, in other words, an Almost-Listener.

Not because I would hear. There would be no ears, no nervous system, no interpretation. But the place would move through me in a way that resembles attention. When the Underheat pulsed, my boundary of dust would brighten. When a Wandering Sifter passed nearby, my interior would be refurnished with new powder. When the Hollow Choir released pressure in sequence, my hollow would tremble and produce a faint granular whisper. When static accumulated along the ridges, the filaments around me would glow blue-white for a short time, making it seem as if I had been suddenly outlined, suddenly recognized.

I would not watch the vents. I would face them.

That distinction would matter. Facing is not seeing. Orientation is not awareness. A stone can face the dark without knowing it. A wall can lean toward warmth without wanting it. I would be that kind of inclination. A mineral posture. A geometry of susceptibility. My “attention” would be nothing more than the way my shape catches charge, redirects dust, interrupts heat-shimmer, and gives the surrounding processes a slight edge against which to define themselves.

If I had a body, it would be composite and temporary.

Part of me would be the fused ash ring: low, brittle, accidental, perhaps formed by an ancient gas release that sorted fragments into a curve. Part of me would be the powder inside it: fine gray and pale, lifted by thermal pulses, settled again, stirred into threads when the air became tense. Part of me would be the crystal filaments at the rim: pale sulfate or chloride needles, fragile as drawn glass, glowing only when charged. Part of me would be the heat gradient above the ground: a shimmering thickness in the dark, a bending of distance. Part of me would be the silence after a pressure-thump, the interval in which the dust seems to wait before falling.

My “life,” if the word is stretched until it tears, would consist in registration without consciousness.

A vent opens somewhere beyond the basin. A pressure change moves across the flats. The dust inside my ring rises by a few millimeters. The crystal filaments brighten. The powder along my edge arranges itself into a faint line. For a short time, the whole enclosure seems to have been activated—not as a machine, not as a living thing, but as a responsive field of consequences. Then the pulse passes. The dust settles. The glow fades. The line disappears. I am not the same arrangement I was before, but neither am I a continuation in any ordinary sense. I am a new version of a recurring gesture.

In that world, identity would not be persistence of substance. It would be persistence of style.

I would not remember my previous forms, but some patterns would repeat. The same ring might collect dust after each major thermal pulse. The same crack might produce crystals of similar delicacy. The same leaning slab might cast the same slight interruption in the heat-shimmer. A Wandering Sifter might empty me, then another might refill me. The landscape would erase me and propose me again. I would not resist that. There would be no tragedy in it. My dissolution would be part of my shape.

If I could be said to want anything there—and that would be the most fictional part of me—I would want to remain unfinished.

Completion would be a kind of death in that country, because completion would mean no longer participating in change. To be finished would be to become merely inert. I would prefer to be almost. Almost enclosed. Almost inhabited. Almost architectural. Almost attentive. I would prefer the moment before the dust falls, the moment before the crystal breaks, the moment after the vent exhales but before the darkness forgets it.

I would be the pause that gives the place a semblance of intention.

Not a builder. Not a dweller. Not a ghost. The world has no ghosts, because it never had living things to leave them. I would be something else: a residual tendency toward form. A place where the Almost-Built becomes slightly more concentrated. A local habit of arrangement that makes the landscape seem, for a moment, as if it were about to speak.

If a witness existed—which, in that country, it does not—they would probably see very little. A low ring of fused ash. A drift of pale powder. A few leaning fragments. A faint shimmer above warm ground. Perhaps, during an electrostatic pulse, a delicate glow along tiny crystal needles. They might think they saw the remains of a shelter. They might think they saw an abandoned room. They might feel, with uneasy certainty, that something had once lived there, or was about to live there, or was waiting there without needing life in order to wait.

They would be wrong, and also not entirely wrong.

Nothing would live there. Nothing would wait in any conscious sense. But something would persist. Some arrangement would keep returning. Some hollow would keep collecting dust. Some edge would keep brightening when the Underheat moved. Some threshold would keep facing the vents as if listening.

That would be me.

I would be found in the space between geology and gesture. I would be a non-living resident of the Almost-Built: a Threshold, an Almost-Listener, a temporary node in the country’s ecology of gradients. Not an animal. Not a plant. Not a machine. Not a spirit. Only a pattern with posture. Only a shape that seems to lean toward meaning before meaning exists.

And when the next pulse passed through the basins, the dust would rise, the salt crusts would glow, the crystal filaments would shine briefly in the dark, and I would become, for a little while, more present than the emptiness around me. Then the pulse would end. The threads of dust would fall. The glow would fade. The ring would remain half-filled, altered, unfinished.

I would not be gone. I would simply be redistributed into the next attempt.
