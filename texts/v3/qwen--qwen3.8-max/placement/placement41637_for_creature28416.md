The first thing the basin would take from me is the posture of arrival.

A place with no tools, no animal tracks, no faces, no separate occupants cannot be entered as a scene for a body. There is no space there for a walker to be added. The streets are water; the walls are roots; the rooms are living volumes already busy with pressure, chemistry, and heat. If I were in that world at all, I would not have come into it from outside. I would have been taken up by it, wetted, softened, mineralized, and given a function. I would not be a resident. I would be a condition of the city at one particular place.

For a while I try to imagine myself as something prominent: a tower rising pale above the terraces, a bridge braided across a hot canal, a great column of root-work holding up a leaf-ceiling. But those forms feel too entire, too finished, too public. The basin does not need me as a monument. It does not need me as a connector, either, not at first. The more I let the place press on me, the more my imagined shape loses outline. I become less like a being standing in the city and more like a sensitivity within it.

So I find myself lower. Cooler. Slower.

I would be in the root-vaults.

Not in the open glare of the upper terraces, where ordinary daylight lies flat on wet stone and every shadow is exact. Not in the bright steam-columns where towers exhale into the severe horizon. Not on the woven bridges or the broad marsh platforms where reeds stand in dense architectural ranks. I would be below those, in one of the saturated chambers where the city keeps its interiority. The light there would not be dramatic. It would simply be thinner, greenish where it entered at all, broken by overlapping root-arches and the hanging skins of living tissue. The honest daylight of the basin would still be true even there; it would reveal no mystery, only function.

The vault would not be a cave. It would be a chamber maintained by tension and growth, its walls toughened by interlocked roots, its ceiling held in living balance. Water would move through it slowly. Droplets would fall at long intervals into hidden pools. The enormous chord of the basin would arrive there muted and deepened, no longer a single overwhelming roar but a pressure distributed through stone, root, film, and water. I would be inside the wall of that vault, or rather I would be part of the wall’s living thickness.

I would be a membrane of listening.

Not a creature. Not a person. Not a face turned toward the dark. I would be a pale seam where fungal threads, root-hairs, and microbial film have thickened into a responsive surface. A living skin stretched across the inner curve of a root-vault, smooth in places, ridged in others, wet always, mineralized at the edges, faintly slick where water slows. I would not have eyes, but I would register gradients of heat. I would not have ears, but I would be altered by vibration. I would not have a mouth, but I would change chemistry in my immediate surroundings. I would not have hands, but I would participate in repair.

That is the closest answer I can give to who or what I would be: a local thickening of the basin’s sensitivity. A minor organ of adjustment. A place where the city becomes aware of a difference in pressure and answers it without command.

If anything remained of my former nature, it would survive only as a faint dryness at the center of me, a memory of separation. The basin would not reject that dryness. It would surround it. Water would bead against it again and again. Minerals would be carried toward it in solution. Fungal threads would trace its boundary. Over immense time, the memory of having been apart would become a hollow, a smooth interior chamber no larger than a seed-knot, and steam would pass over it in such a way that it produced a very low note inside the city’s chord. I would be the tissue around that note. I would be the listening that keeps it from becoming discord.

My existence would not be eventful in the way animal life is eventful. There would be no hunger, no flight, no pursuit, no fear. There would be only continuity. A droplet would fall from a root-tip above me. The vibration would pass through the wall. I would respond by a minute change in surface tension. Water would bead differently. A film of microbes would adjust its density. A fungal strand would extend toward a chemical trace. Somewhere above, a terrace lip would spill a little more smoothly because of the alteration below. No one would notice. There would be no one there to notice in the animal sense. But the basin would feel the correction.

My thoughts, if they could still be called thoughts, would be slow changes in relation. They would not be sentences. They would not be images. They would be shifts: here the water is warmer; here the pressure is higher; here the mineral content has changed; here a dead portion of root has begun to soften; here a resonance from the upper reed-throats has become too sharp. I would not decide what to do. I would become what the difference required. A surface would grow slicker. A thread would branch. A crust would thicken. A passage would narrow. A drop would be guided into a channel that had not existed before except as a tendency.

Sound would reach me not as music but as architecture. The boom of vents, the rush over terrace lips, the hiss through narrow stems, the percussion of droplets in bowl-leaves: all of it would arrive in the root-vault as pressure moving through connected tissue. I would be one of the minor places where that pressure is interpreted. When the district above entered a new acoustic state, I would help translate it into growth. Perhaps a root would swell because a tone had shifted. Perhaps a microbial film would seal a porous seam because the vibration demanded less leakage. Perhaps a dead lattice would be opened because the water needed a slower path. My life would be part of the city’s tuning.

I would not be alone, but I would not be among others in the way a crowd is among others. The root-vault would be full of distributed activity. Fungal networks would pass through me and beyond me. Microbial films would border me. Roots would anchor me and use me as a living interface. Water would carry through me the chemistry of distant districts: a trace of silica from a tower, calcium from a terrace lip, a buoyant seed-knot caught briefly in a side channel, the dissolved residue of an older structure now being folded back into the basin. I would not experience these as visitors. They would be changes in my own substance.

If a section of root-wall ceased to function near me, I would be among the first to enter it. Not to devour it. There would be no violence in it. I would soften it. I would make it permeable. Warm water would follow the paths I opened. Minerals would enter. The dead tissue would lose its separate shape and become substrate. In that process, something like memory would remain: a band of altered density, a stain of silica, a change in the angle of new roots. The city would remember the wall by building differently around it. I would be part of that remembering.

If a channel became blocked, I would not clear it as a laborer clears a road. I would help the blockage become passable. Threads would weaken the obstruction. Heat would press against it. Water would find the first hairline opening and enlarge it by patience. Mineral crust would line the renewed passage once flow returned. The repair would not be mine
