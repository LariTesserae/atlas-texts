The crawler would not understand the word, but it would feel the word as a change in the room’s moisture: a breath gone warm against the seam, a tremor crossing the basin, a fiber somewhere in the wall loosening as if language were a small green wound. The chamber would answer with a wet click. The word would no longer belong to the mouth that made it.

If I were to find myself in that world, I think I would not find myself as a whole body.

The place is too intimate for bodies. It is too loud, too alive, too quick to translate whatever enters it into its own processes. A person would not stand there as a person stands in a field or a house. A person would be broken down into effects. My breath would become weather. My pulse would become a drip somewhere overhead, arriving too early or too late. The heat of me would thicken the air, and the walls would drink that heat as if it were vapor. If I tried to speak, the room would answer with the sound of fibers parting. If I tried to listen, my listening would become another pressure in the hollow, another stillness for the crawler to notice.

So I would not be the intruder, not for long. The chamber would make me into a repeated process before I could make myself into a guest.

At first I imagine I would become the sound of myself: a footfall turned into a bead of water striking a leaf; a heartbeat turned into the low note moving through the seam; a name turned into the creak of new growth tightening inside the wall. I would be the room’s mistaken echo, the human residue it cannot digest but can rhythmize. That is the first answer, and it feels true, but not final.

Then I imagine I would be the light.

That ordinary, impossible beam entering through the split in the living wall. The late-afternoon brightness laid across the plant-floor like a plank. The dust and spore-motes drifting in it as if they remembered wind. The leaves turning toward it with simple hunger. The crawler standing inside it, briefly translucent, its green digestion brightening as though the light were feeding it too. If I were the light, I would be the room’s borrowed memory of elsewhere. I would be proof of a surface that might not exist. I would touch some surfaces and ignore others. I would reveal corridors that were not there. I would make shadows fall from things that had not yet entered the room. I would be familiar and therefore wrong.

There is a temptation in that image. To be the beam is to be the visiting animal without flesh, the ordinary thing misplaced, the proof of outside. It would be beautiful to lie across the living floor and watch the crawler step into me, trusting me only because I am warm.

But the longer I stay with the room, the less I think I would be the light. The light is too clean. It belongs to the seam, to the wound, to the idea of surface. It enters from elsewhere. It remains, in some way, a rumor of distance.

I would be lower than that.

I would be among the loose things on the floor.

Not the leaf-basin, though I would tremble in it. Not the hanging root, though I would fear it. Not the crawler itself, though I would be near it constantly. I would be one of the sharp possibilities the room has not yet learned to use.

I think I would be the spine.

The detached thorn. The pale broken base. The tip aimed into the dark. The thing that has fallen or been torn from a living stem and now lies among the chamber’s unbuilt future. Not an artifact. Not a possession. Not a tool in any true sense yet. Only a natural fragment with a point, waiting at the threshold of being chosen.

I would find myself low, on the pressed mat of root-flesh and stem-substance, perhaps near the edge of the broad cupped leaf where the water gathers. I would not be buried. I would not be fixed. I would be loose in a place where almost everything else is still joined to the living body of the room. That would make me strange. The walls grow, sweat, drink, contract, listen. The ceiling sweats and exhales. The floor is a tense architecture of stems. The crawler moves with careful pauses. But I would be the detached thing: the one sharp object that can be lifted without changing the shape of the chamber.

That is where I would be: in the crawler’s forelegs for a while, then set down, then perhaps carried again.

I would not belong to the crawler in the way a knife belongs to a hand. The crawler does not own. It only chooses, and choice in that room is already the beginning of a long history. When it picked me up, I would become an extension of its caution. It would use me to tap the wall and feel whether the fibers were hungry. It would lower me into the leaf-basin and let a bead climb along my point. It would hold me across its limbs when the chamber rained and the noise made danger difficult to hear. It would carry me through the beam of misplaced daylight, and for a moment I would cast a thin shadow on the living floor, a shadow that might look older than me, a shadow that might belong to the first awl, the first needle, the first wound deliberately made.

But I would not yet be any of those things.

That is the important part. I would be the before.

In a room where nothing has been made, I would be the possibility of making. Not the act itself, only its sharp edge. The place is full of such edges: leaf-stems that could cut, hollow seed-shells that could hold water, bark-strips that could become cord if peeled. But I would be the one aimed into the dark. I would be the point. I would be the hard little silence after the crawler’s tap, when the wall either remains still or begins to close.

I would know the room through use, but not through understanding. I would feel the leaf-basin tremble when the crawler lowered me into it. I would taste the water only as a bead climbing my length. I would know the hanging root by the way the crawler turned away from it, carrying me in a wide, nervous arc. I would know the seam of light by the warmth on my tip when the crawler stood inside the beam. I would know the rain by the amplified drumming that let me move through the open hollow without being interpreted by the walls.

If the room thinks in circles, I would be one of its smallest hard circles.

Lifted. Carried. Set down. Found again. Tapped against a fiber. Lowered into water. Held across the body during rain. Forgotten. Returned to. Lifted again.

I would not have memory in the way a person has memory. I would have position. I would have pressure. I would have the difference between lying still and being carried. I would have the faint green smell of the ruptured wall where I was torn away. I would have the pale scar at my base, the place where I stopped being part of the room’s living structure and became available.

That availability would be my danger.

The chamber is alive and not all of it is patient. Some fibers contract when touched. Some leaf-cups close when disturbed. The hanging root may be sensor, lure, or snare. If I were carried too near the wrong surface, I might provoke it. My point might cut a root-fiber and make the room bleed sweetness into the air. My point might touch a leaf-margin and cause the whole panel to fold with a soft, terrible decision. I would not be innocent. Even before being a weapon, a point changes the world by concentrating force. In a place made entirely of living tension, a detached sharpness is a kind of question the walls may not tolerate.

Perhaps that is why the crawler sets me down.

Not from forgetfulness, but from reverence. Or fear. Or the simple fact that it has not yet become the kind of creature that can keep me.
