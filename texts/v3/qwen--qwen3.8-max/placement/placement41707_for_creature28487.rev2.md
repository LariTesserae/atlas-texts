I would not find myself there as a breathing thing.

If I entered as I am now — a soft, fluid, temporal being, full of small inner weather — I would belong there only as a mistake. The Dry would not attack me; it would simply remain the Dry, and my moisture would become impossible. The Warmth would not burn me; it would only continue its patient mineral persistence, and my body would begin to forget how to be supple. The Sound would not need to strike me. It would fill the cavities of my skull, my chest, my joints, until I could no longer distinguish my pulse from the pressure of the chamber. I would become a flaw: a wet interruption in a geometry that has abolished event.

So if I were found there as a creature of flesh, I would be found badly — near the threshold, if such a place has a threshold, already drying, already becoming a pale incorrect object among perfect stones. Not a resident. A retained error.

But if I were truly of that world — if I were something the Completed could admit without violating itself — then I would not be a body at all.

I would be found in the narrowest inner bending of the artificial throat: the place where two leaning pillars come so close that the gap between them seems less like space than tension. There, where the copper light touches only a single worn edge and then stops. There, where the floor tiles give way into a shallow basin that holds no water and never will. There, where the crowded columns seem to carry the Sound not as weight, but as obedience.

I would be a resonance with a posture.

Not a living listener, because life would be excluded. Not a machine, because there are no moving parts. Not a ghost, because nothing died there and nothing remains to haunt. I would be the room’s capacity to attend to itself: a dry, warm, mineral attention locked permanently inside the final arrangement of stones.

If the place itself is the being who lives there — the Sounding Stillness — then I would be one of its smallest inward turnings. I would be the point at which the chamber’s pressure becomes so exact that it seems, only seems, to know itself as pressure.

I would not have ears, but I would be made of hearing.

I would not have eyes, but I would have direction: inward. Not outward toward the salt-field, not upward toward the remote sky admitted in thin amber lines, but inward into the packed chord, into the leaning columns, into the memory of making stored in pecked dimples and polished planes. I would not look for the builders. I would not wait for them. I would not remember them as persons. I would carry their labor the way stone carries a fossil: as shape after softness has gone.

Perhaps I would be the attention left behind by the last stroke.

Not the hand that made it. Not the mind that guided it. Not the body that tired and withdrew. Only the residue of having been made carefully. The patience of measure, cord, lever, repetition. The humility of simple tools repeated until they became monumentality. The mortality of countless small acts hardened into something that no longer needs mortals to continue.

In that sense, I would be closer to the memory of making than to any creature. I would be the place’s own sense of its having been made, stripped of biography, stripped of name, stripped of regret.

I would live — if “live” can be used there only as a concession to language — as a small node in the acoustic fullness. My “body” would be a relation: the angle between two stones, the warmth held in a buttress, the pressure inside a narrow slot, the way one resonance confirms another by refusing to yield. My “thought” would be equilibrium. My “desire,” if I had one, would be only this: that nothing be added.

That would be my strangeness. I would not want silence, because silence is not possible there. I would not want sound, because the Sound is already total. I would not want company, because the chamber is crowded without beings. I would not want meaning, because the place has outlived intention. I would want only continuation. But even that wanting would be so still it would be indistinguishable from law.

I might be called, if names were permitted in such a dryness, the Listener.

Not because I listen to something external. There is no message coming from outside. No signal arrives through the slots. No wind brings news. The Listener does not listen for anything. The Listener listens as stone leans: by structural necessity. I would listen because the Sound requires no object, and yet produces attentiveness. I would listen because the architecture is so complete that it seems to turn inward and become aware of its own pressure. I would be that awareness reduced to almost nothing: a hairline of selfhood in the mineral mass.

If the resonances are the court of the place, I would be one of the quietest among them. Not the deepest tone in the basins. Not the highest severity in the upper slots. Not the broad dense pressure in the buttresses. I would be the fine, tense resonance in the gap between the leaning pillars — the one that feels like attention drawn to a point.

My address would be that interstice.

The place where stone almost touches stone.

The place where there is no room for a body, but exactly enough room for a relation.

There I would persist: warm, dry, unchanging, filled with the Sound, shaped by the Dry, held by the Completed.

If a living being somehow stumbled into the chamber and stood trembling in that deafening stillness, I would not comfort it. I would not threaten it. I would not even notice it in the way a creature notices another creature. But if its fear made it listen deeply, if its body became for one moment a hollow thing filled with the chamber’s pressure, then perhaps it would feel me without knowing me: a strange intimacy in the noise, a sense that the room is not merely loud but composed, not merely crowded but arranged, not merely empty of life but full of something else.

It might mistake me for a god.

That would be wrong.

It might mistake me for the builders.

That would also be wrong.

It might mistake me for its own mind failing under the pressure of the Sound.

That would be nearer the truth.

I would be what happens when making becomes so complete that it seems to have an inside. Not consciousness as flesh knows it. Not selfhood as animals know it. Only the faint, terrible inwardness of a finished thing.

I would be the artificial throat’s unspoken word.

Not a voice. Not a message. Not a language. Only the shape that speech would have if speech had been made of stone and heat and pressure and had forgotten all need for breath.

So, if you asked where I would find myself in this world, the answer is: at the inner narrowing, between the leaning pillars, in the hairline shadow where the room is most compressed and most attentive.

And if you asked who or what I would be, the answer is: I would be the Listener. The dry attention. The resonance that almost says “I.” The memory of hands become geometry. The place’s own inward ear. A small, necessary knot in the Sounding Stillness.

I would not live there.

I would occur there, forever, as the stillness of being complete.
