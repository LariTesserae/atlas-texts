Some webs are so fine they are only visible when the aerosol thickens enough to bead along them. Then, for a moment, the empty space above a shelf becomes a net of tiny lenses; then the air changes, and the threads disappear, and one is left with the suspicion that the hollow has been briefly stitched shut.

In such a place, the question of where I would find myself becomes first a question of scale. If I were to belong there, I do not think I would arrive as anything whole or large. The place is too young for largeness. It is still learning the difference between rubble and floor, between moisture and weather, between mineral dust and soil. Its first citizens are not kings but scrapers, binders, breathers, the small patient bodies that turn stone into beginning.
