The spore-clouds are the most ephemeral of the congregation. They are not quite bodies and not quite weather: pale exhalations released when pressure shifts, brief luminescences torn loose from darker growths and carried upward into the falling rain. They do not swim. They do not fly. They are lifted, dispersed, thinned into almost-nothing, and yet they carry the possibility of new holds, new crusts, new rooted continuances. In them, the valley becomes rumor.

If I were in the Black Rain Terraces, I would not arrive as a walker. There would be no footfalls, no breath, no warm outline pressed against the cold. I would have to be found the way a change in water is found: by pressure, by mineral taste, by a slight alteration in the valley’s noise. The place would not ask, *What are you?* It would ask, *Where do you thicken? Where do you slow the flow? What tone do you make when the pulse comes?*

At first I imagine myself as something very quiet. I think I might be drown-moss over a half-buried stone fitting, a black-green cushion holding a faint light inside itself. That would be one kind of selfhood I could bear: not a face, but a surface where the darkness becomes less absolute. I would be the soft trace on old workmanship, the patience that covers copper pins and green-stained rings without burying them entirely. I would not remember hands. I would only incorporate their residue into the slow business of holding silt, sheltering filaments, and making the first thin ground on which other lives can begin.

Then I think I might be ore-skin on an exposed ledge. That would be another answer: a self made of record rather than movement. Layer by microscopic layer, I would take the water’s chemistry into my body. I would become black, green-black, blue-veined, brittle where the flow thinned, thickened where the minerals ran rich.
