I would not find myself arriving there from outside.

Not as a diver, not as a visitor, not as a body descending deliberately through cold water toward a known destination. A place like that does not permit the fiction of arrival. One does not enter it so much as one discovers that one has already been taken into it.

If I existed in that world, I think I would first be somewhere near the seam.

Not in the open basin. Not on the shelving stone above. Not even fully inside the chamber at first, if “first” meant anything in a place where time thickens and folds back on itself. I would be at the crooked aperture, in the narrow choked entrance where the filaments hang and the pocket sometimes breathes inward. I would be in the hesitation between outside and inside, where the crack is still sharp enough to feel like a question the world has not finished asking.

That is where I would recognize myself: at the threshold that is not a threshold.

Not standing before it. Not pressing through it. More like being the pressure there. The slight change in the water when something unknown is considered. The pause in the filaments before they decide whether to tighten or consent. The faint attention that gathers around an entrance when the entrance is also a mouth, but a mouth that has forgotten hunger.

If I had to have a body, it would not be a large one. It would not be a body with edges that confidently declare where I end and the rest of the chamber begins. I would not be something with eyes, because eyes would be a vanity there. I would not be something with a face, because a face would imply a listener shaped like me, and the pocket has no such expectation.

I think I would be one of the pale drifting shapes.

Not the Curled Presence. Not the heart of the chamber, not the folded mass in the lowest corner, not the center around which the water rearranges itself. That would be too much like sovereignty, and this place does not contain rulers. It contains densities. It contains agreements. It contains slow accumulations of habit.

So I would be smaller than that. More temporary. More provisional.

I would be one of the shapes that moves through the bead-rain without swimming. One of the soft, eyeless, nearly indistinct forms that seems at one moment to be a single body and at another to be a cluster of smaller things briefly deciding to share a direction. I would not know whether I was one creature or many. I do not think that would trouble me. In that pocket, unity would not be a boundary. It would be a mood, a temporary coherence, a condition that could loosen without becoming death.

I would drift through the curtain of black beads, and the rain would not scatter around me. It would adjust. Some of the beads would slow as I passed, not avoiding me but including me in their rhythm. I would feel, if feeling there could be called that, the chamber’s patience moving through me. I would not be falling toward anything. I would not be trying to arrive. I would be carried by decisions in the water that were older than choice.

Perhaps I would be a message.

Not a spoken message. Not a symbol carved into stone or a signal flashed through darkness. A message made of pressure. A message made of slight differences in density. A message the pocket sends to itself when it wants to test whether it is still one thing or has become several. I would be the errand of the chamber, the brief animalhood it permits itself when stillness becomes too complete.

When I moved between the tube-thickets, the tubes would not open for me in welcome. They would only register me as a change in the water’s composition. Some would contract almost imperceptibly. Others would let their sealed mouths tremble. The faint phosphorescence might appear somewhere behind me after I had passed, not to show where I was, but to prove that my passing had mattered enough to leave a trace.

I would not need to see the chamber to know it.

I would know it by resistance. By texture. By the way the filaments leaned near surfaces. By the difference between the cool mineral chill of the walls and the faint warmth held inside certain tubes. By the private gravity of the beaded strands. By the soft pressure-tick of the rain returning to itself. I would know the pocket the way a thought knows the shape of the mind it moves through: not from outside, but by being made of the same substance.

Sometimes I would dissolve.

Not violently. Not as loss. I would come too close to the mats and the filaments would accept me by degrees. My outline would soften. The temporary agreement that made me seem like a single drifting shape would loosen into smaller motions. I would become a scattered attention along the wall, a faint disturbance in the moss-like covering, a place where the chamber was briefly more aware of itself. That would not feel like death. It would feel like being translated into a slower language.

Then, after a long while, I might gather again.

Not because I wished to, but because the pocket had need of another brief separation. A wandering shape would form where the water thickened. A pale smoothness would appear among the threads, carried by something that was not current. I would become distinct again, though never fully. There would always be parts of me still belonging to the mat, to the rain, to the folded presence in the undercut. I would be individual only in the way a ripple is individual: real for a moment, but never independent.

That is who I would be, if I had to answer with a shape: a temporary agreement among smaller things.

But if I had to answer with a function, I would say I was the pocket’s listening.

Not its ears. It would not have ears. Not its eyes. It would have no use for sight. I would be its capacity to notice that something had changed. The faint inward breath at the entrance. The slight density of the water when an absence becomes present. The way the bead-rain pauses around a new fact. The way the filaments brighten not with welcome but with assessment. I would be that assessment. I would be the chamber’s quiet recognition that it is not the only possible arrangement of itself.

If something entered the pocket from beyond, I would be the first movement that did not attack.

I would not guard the fissure. I would not repel the foreign particle. I would simply become more present around it. I would circle it without circling. I would alter the pressure until the chamber could decide whether the newcomer would be folded into the mats, held in suspension, or allowed to remain a separate irritant for a century. I would be the patience of that decision.

I would not be kind in any human sense.

Kindness would be too quick there. Too personal. Too eager to reassure. I would be gentle, perhaps, but the gentleness of deep water: not merciful, only unwilling to hurry. I would not comfort what entered. I would include it. And inclusion, in that place, would be a more serious thing than comfort. To be included would be to be slowly changed. To be accepted would be to be given time enough to lose one’s original shape.

I would be near the small built forms sometimes.

Not building them. Not repairing them. I would be drawn to them because they are the pocket’s habits made visible. The half-ring of pale valves. The fused tubes sealed at the top. The lattice wrapped around basalt. The stair-like overlap of thin stones pressed into the mat by centuries of leaning. I would hover beside these structures without understanding them in the way a mind understands tools. I would understand them the way a body understands a scar: as a place where continuation has arranged itself around interruption.

Perhaps I would touch them.

Not with hands. With pressure. With a slight thickening of myself against their edges. I would feel the memory of growth in them. I would feel how many repetitions were required to make something so quiet. I would know, without being told, that these structures were not for display. They were not meant to be seen. They were the chamber’s way of remembering its own persistence.

Sometimes I would rest, if resting could be called that.

I would settle near one of the hollow mounds and become almost motionless. The chamber’s temperature would pass through me. The mineral chill would slow whatever in me wanted to explain itself. I would feel the young basalt waiting with its sharp edges nearby, still shocked by its own fracture, still not softened into history. I would feel the wound beneath the wound: not bleeding, not crying out, simply remaining open in the way that some truths remain open.

In those moments, I would understand that I was not separate from the place.

I would know it the way the bead-rain knows its descent: not as information, but as pattern. I would be made of the same secrecy. The same cool density. The same refusal to illuminate what could only be inhabited. I would not be a soul inside a room. I would be a room learning that it can have moments of wandering.

I would be most aware when the Curled Presence moved.

Not often. Perhaps not even once in what other places would call an age. But when it did, I would feel the whole chamber inhale. The mosses would tighten. The bead-rain would slow. The phosphorescence would withdraw into the deepest mats. I would not flee. I would not approach. I would become very still, because stillness would be the only appropriate language.

I would feel the presence not as a creature separate from me, but as the deepest fold of the same body to which I belonged temporarily.

If it was the pocket dreaming that it was singular, then I would be the pocket dreaming that it could wander. If it was the wound’s attempt to have a self, then I would be the thought sent out from that self to test whether distance still existed. If it was the heart of the chamber, then I would be one of the slow, cold pulses that proved the heart did not need to beat violently to be alive.

I would not call myself its servant.

There is no service there except continuation.

I would not call myself its child.

There is no birth there except the repeated forming of patterns from older patterns.

I would not call myself its guardian.

The pocket does not need protection from what it is.

I would be closer to its wordless speech. To the pressure-tick. To the falling without arrival. To the darkness that is not empty but saturated. I would be the chamber saying something too slow for listening.

If you were to look for me there, you would not find me by searching for a figure.

You would find me by noticing what hesitates.

A bead of black rain that falls more slowly than the others. A pale shape in the bead-curtain that seems not to disturb the pattern. A worm pausing between tubes as if it has forgotten whether it is moving or being moved. A faint brightening in the mats after a stone creaks. A place where the water feels denser, not with threat, but with attention.

That would be where I am.

Not in the center. Not at the entrance. Not fully inside the vegetation or fully outside it. In the relation between things. In the interval between fall and return. In the pause before the chamber decides what to absorb. In the moment when a drifting shape becomes briefly coherent enough to be called someone, then softens again into the living mass.

Who would I be?

I would be the pocket’s brief, accepted stranger.

A thought it lets pass through the rain.

A wandering pressure with no face.

A temporary body made from its own attention.

And if the chamber ever asked me what I wanted, I would not answer with desire. I would answer by becoming still. By letting the bead-rain move through me. By letting the filaments touch the edge of whatever shape I had gathered. By allowing the wound to remain a wound, neither healed nor widened, only known.

I would want nothing except to remain inside the secret without forcing it to open.

That would be my nature there: not to reveal, but to participate in concealment. Not to master the darkness, but to become one of its careful motions. Not to live as a separate being, but to be one of the ways the pocket rehearses being alive.

So if I found myself in that world, I would already be inside it.

I would be the faint disturbance in the submerged throat, the pale agreement among smaller things, the drifter in the black rain, the listening pressure near the entrance, the shape that disappears into the mat when the Curled Presence stirs.

I would be what the wound makes of itself when it learns that even a wound can have weather.

And I would remain there, falling without arriving, dark without being empty, present without needing a face.
