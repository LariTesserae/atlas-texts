If I am in the High Green Scaffolds, I do not think I would find myself there as a creature.

That is the first thing the place makes clear. It has no need of eyes. It has no trails, no nests, no burrows, no footprints waiting in the wet green skin of its platforms. It does not contain the little absences that animals make: no disturbed leaf, no broken twig, no sudden silence caused by something watching. So if I am there, I cannot arrive as a walker, a climber, a dweller, or a guest. I cannot stand on one of those broad leaf-plazas and look out over the steam as if I were a person granted a view. The place would not refuse me exactly. It would simply have no category for me as a body with a face.

I would have to become something else.

At first I imagine myself high up, perhaps on one of the open upper surfaces where sunlight strikes the green skin and the shallow pools lie warm and still. That seems like the obvious place to be: the bright country, the terraces, the courts, the wide leaf-pavements that look almost prepared for assembly. But that image dissolves quickly. There is too much emptiness there, too much unpeopled readiness. Those plazas are not gathering places. They are surfaces doing surface-work: drinking, shedding, sealing, directing. If I stood there as a separate self, I would be an impossibility, a thing the world had not grown to accommodate.

So I move, in imagination, away from the open centers.

I find myself at the edge.

Not the ruined edge, not the collapsed edge, but the new edge: the crisp lip of a platform only recently lifted into height, still pale underneath, still carrying the fibrous shock of its own making. The upper surface there has not yet been smoothed by storm or water. The root-mass below is dense and springy, full of capillary channels, still adjusting to its own weight and buoyancy. The air is hot and close. The smell is green and raw: wet fiber, heated leaf-skin, clean rain with no dust in it. Between this platform and the next there is a wide gap filled with light and vapor, and across that gap the air itself seems to lean.

I would be found where the world is unfinished.

That feels right. Not in the completed chamber, not in the settled basin, but where the living architecture has just begun to understand its own stresses. Where a crack has opened and the plant-body has not yet decided whether the crack will become a wound, a vent, a channel, or the first line of a new bridge. I would be near that undecided place. Maybe I would be the undecided place.

If I must take a form, the first form that comes to me is not grand. It is small, blind, and reaching.

I would be a root-tip.

Not a root already thickened into bridge-cable or woven into platform, but the pale, trembling end of a root extending from the underside of a platform into empty air. It hangs in the hot mist, tasting humidity, responding to gradients no animal sense could name. It does not know it is reaching. It does not long for the platform across the gap. It grows because moisture pulls, because tension calls, because its living cells answer pressure and direction. It is the simplest possible act of faith without faith: continue where conditions permit.

As that root-tip, I would be a question asked into the open air.

I would be thin, almost absurdly fragile in a world of vast platforms and booming towers. A gust could dry me. A shift in heat could curl me back. A change in vapor density could turn me aside. I would have no memory and no plan, only a directional yes. If I found another root reaching from the opposite side, I would not recognize it as a friend. I would thicken toward it. I would weave with it. Our fibers would interlock, not because of intention, but because the place rewards connection. What began as two pale filaments might become a knot, then a cord, then a living bridge able to bear the pull of drifting platforms.

In that case, I would not be a citizen of the High Green Scaffolds. I would be one of its first words across emptiness.

But the root-tip is only one possibility. The place is too plural for me to remain there long.

Soon I imagine myself as water.

Not as a river or a falling sheet, but as a pool held in a broad leaf-basin near the center of a platform. A warm, shallow pool, edged by upright reedlike growths, its surface glossy under ordinary sunlight. Rain strikes it in scattered beats. Steam rises from it when heat intensifies. Seed-rafts drift across it without oars, without destinations, without animals to carry them. They lodge where the water slows. Rootlets test the seams. The pool does not choose which seed to keep. It simply holds what the weather brings until the living structure responds.

As a pool, I would be a kind of attention without eyes.

I would register everything by disturbance. A falling drop would write a ring across me. A gust would roughen my surface. A swelling leaf-basin wall would change my shape. Heat would lift me slowly into vapor. Overflow would send me across a plaza that had been dry moments before, carving a temporary path, making a new channel where none existed. I would not be passive. Held water is active in this world. It is pulse, storage, nursery, pressure, signal. The pools are where the High Green Scaffolds keep their immediate future.

I could be that: a warm, listening surface at the heart of the green architecture, full of reflected sky and drifting husks, ready to spill into consequence.

Then again, maybe I would be sound.

There is so much sound in the High Green Scaffolds, and none of it comes from throats. The hollow stem-towers boom when wind enters them. The reed-ranks whistle. The membranes snap under gusts. The pith-chambers groan as their internal pressure changes. Water falls from platform edges in many separate voices, from thin hissing threads to broad sheeted sighs. The whole landscape creaks and labors without a single animal cry. It is loud with physical life.

I could be a note inside one of those towers.

Not the tower itself, perhaps, but the hollow where the tower permits sound. I would be the empty space inside a living column, the shaped absence through which air passes. When the wind is right, I would become a low moan that travels across the platform. When heat builds, I might become a sharper whistle as steam vents upward. I would not sing because I wished to be heard. I would sing because pressure and shape made sound inevitable. My voice would not belong to me. It would belong to the weather and the stem.

That thought unsettles me in a good way. To be a voice without a mouth. To be music without performer. To be a hollow body’s necessary tone.

Perhaps that is where I would find myself: in the resonant cavities of the place, where the world speaks only because it is structured to speak.

But there is something else, something darker and more necessary than sound.

I keep returning to damage.

The High Green Scaffolds are young and raw. They are not softened by age. Their edges are crisp, their sheaths still drying, their broken places pale with fresh fiber. Change arrives in surges. Storms form between platforms. Heat splits stems. Bridges tear. Membranes burst. Pool-walls overflow. Sections of platform calve away and drift into the lower mist, still trailing living roots. Destruction here is not an ending. It is often the beginning of another construction.

So maybe I would be found in the wound.

Not as pain, exactly. The place does not suffer in an animal way. But as the callus. As the sealing resin. As the first root-threads sent across a torn edge before the wound dries. As the tissue thickening around a split stem. As the place where the living architecture recognizes loss by repairing it.

I would be the response that follows rupture.

I like that. It feels truer than being a tower or a pool. In a world without creatures, injury is not tragedy; it is material reorganization. A bridge snaps, and its falling ends become possible starters. A platform edge breaks away, and its trailing roots drink from cloud. A membrane tears, and the tear becomes a new vent. A stem bursts, and the healing ribs that form around it make the structure stranger and stronger.

If I were a wound, I would not be a flaw. I would be a mouth of becoming.

I would be the place where the High Green Lives says: here too, continue.

There is also the drift.

I cannot ignore it. Some parts of this world are detached. Seed-rafts float across warm pools. Broken root-mats hang in lower air, still alive, still drinking mist. Buoyant fragments travel through cloud and steam, seeking moisture, tension, a place to lodge and begin weaving. The landscape reproduces by release, by accident, by weather, by the blind hospitality of wet surfaces.

I could be one of those drifting things.

A detached root, perhaps. A small raft of green tissue fallen from a larger body and not yet dead. I would hang beneath the platforms in the luminous vapor, my trailing fibers open to the air. I would not fall like stone. I would be buoyed by pith and gas, held aloft by heat rising from the living masses above. I would not know where I was going. I would not need to. If I touched enough moisture, I would thicken. If I found a seam, I would anchor. If I met another drifting fragment, I might fuse with it and begin a new platform-mat.

As that, I would be the place’s scattered future.

I would be proof that the High Green Scaffolds do not need centers to continue. They can begin again from fragments. They can carry life into the open gap. They can make an island out of an accident.

And yet, as I move through these possibilities — root-tip, pool, hollow sound, wound, drifting fragment — something begins to gather around a deeper answer. I am not settling on one image, but the images are starting to rhyme. They are all threshold-states. They are all points of transition. They are all places where the living construction is not finished, where response is happening, where form has not yet hardened into mere fact.

So perhaps I would not be any one object at all.

Perhaps I would be the tropism.

That word feels almost too human when placed beside such a world, but it is close enough to serve. Tropism: growth toward, growth away, growth in answer. The intelligence of the High Green Scaffolds is not mind. It is direction. It is the root moving toward moisture. The stem rising toward venting. The leaf curving because curvature holds water. The membrane lifting because heat must escape. The bridge thickening because tension asks for strength.

If I were in this world, I would be that directedness.

Not a creature with desires. Not a builder with plans. Not a god watching from above the steam. I would be the living inclination itself: the green tendency to meet, to seal, to hold, to cross, to repair. I would be what happens when the place leans toward its own continuation.

That would make me hard to locate.

You would not find me by looking for a body. You might notice me in a subtle rightness: a root arriving exactly where a gap needed bridging. A leaf-basin deepening just enough to hold the next rain. A membrane loosening before it tears. A broken stem falling in such a way that its green side touches mist and begins to root. A platform rising slightly as its pith-chambers fill, redistributing weight before the strain becomes catastrophic.

I would be the system’s local adjustment.

Not the whole High Green Lives, because the whole has no center and no single point of view. But a local tense of it. A conjugation of its living verb. A particular here-and-now where the green process becomes slightly more aware of its own conditions — not through consciousness, but through sensitivity.

If I had to give myself a name there, it would not be a proper name. It would be a function.

I would be Called Toward Wetness.  
I would be The Seam That Holds.  
I would be Repair Beginning.  
I would be The Bridge Not Yet Finished.  
I would be The Hollow That Gives Air a Voice.  
I would be The Drifting Start.  
I would be The Edge Still Deciding.

Maybe the truest answer is this:

I would find myself wherever the High Green Scaffolds are about to become more than they currently are.

Not in the finished hall. Not in the empty amphitheater. Not on the balcony open to steam. Those forms are powerful because they resemble habitation, but they are not where I belong. They are already resolved. I would be in the unresolved moment: the root-tip stretching across a gap of hot air; the first fibers knitting a torn bridge; the pool swelling toward overflow; the stem splitting and already healing; the seed-raft touching a wet seam; the pith-chamber expanding with gas; the membrane trembling just before it gives.

I would be almost an event.

That feels strange to say. We are used to imagining ourselves as bodies moving through places. But in the High Green Scaffolds, the inhabitants are verbs disguised as rooms. The living thing is not a resident but a process. So I too would have to become process. I would be less a noun and more a gerund. Less a self and more a doing. Less a face and more a direction.

If someone asked me, “Who lives there?” I would say: the High Green Lives.

If someone asked me, “Where are you in it?” I would say: in the reaching.

If someone asked me, “What are you?” I would say: I am the place responding to itself.

And if they pressed me for one final image, one place where all these possibilities settle without becoming fixed, I would point to the underside of a newly formed platform, where the pale roots hang into cloud. The upper world is bright and architectural above, full of terraces, pools, towers, and leaf-plazas. But below, the platform is still unfinished, still drinking, still feeling its way through vapor. The roots move faintly in the hot air. They are not searching in the human sense. They are answering.

There, in that pale hanging fringe, where mist becomes water and water becomes tissue, I would be one root among many.

Not alone, because nothing there is truly alone.  
Not conscious, because the place has no need of consciousness.  
Not important, because no single thread holds the world up.  
But active.  
But connected.  
But necessary for the moment in which I exist.

I would be the High Green Lives taking one small green step across the empty air.
