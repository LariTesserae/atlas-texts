I would not find myself there as a traveler. The hollow would not tolerate a traveler.

A traveler implies a road, a destination, a body set against weather with the intention of crossing something. That place would refuse such a body gently, the way water refuses a sharp edge: not by violence, but by wearing away the need for sharpness. If I existed in that world, I would exist there only because I had already begun to thin into it.

At first, I might be mistaken for weather.

A small thickening of mist at the lowest part of the rim, where the hollow draws air inward. A shadow that seemed to lean too attentively over the bowl. A place in the vapor that did not disperse when it should have. If I had to call that a self, it would be a self made of hesitation: not yet a creature, but a possibility the place was considering.

But the hollow is not a place for hovering. It gathers things downward. It teaches gravity without force. So, as I continued to exist there, I would feel myself drawn toward the lower terraces, toward the black central stone, toward the water that passes over it in a thin continuous sheet.

Not onto the stone itself.

That place belongs to the dweller, or rather the dweller belongs to it. I would be just below, in the shallow basin where the water slows after touching the stone. There, where the sound is fullest, I would begin to hold a shape.
