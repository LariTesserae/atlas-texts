I would not find myself there as a whole person.

A human body would be too much for that hollow: too much heat, too much breath, too much surface memory. The chamber would not tolerate me as a visitor. It would translate me. It would take my pulse and make it a drip, my breathing and make it the low note drawn through the seam, my looking and make it a shadow falling from something not present. It would reduce me to one of its narrow processes until I was small enough to belong.

At first I would probably mistake myself for the crawler. It is the obvious living thing, the careful body moving by tremor and pause. But the room already has its single animal. One crawler is enough to make fear real, enough to make the hanging root listen, enough to give the emptiness a witness. A second crawler would crowd the place. It would make the chamber too theatrical, too full of incident. I would not be the one who listens with its legs.

Nor would I be the hanging root. That line is already the room’s exposed nerve, its vertical question, its lure and antenna. It already belongs to the chamber’s attention. Nor the leaf-basin, which keeps water, sound, reflection, and the possibility of closing. Nor the misplaced daylight, which already carries the wrongness of elsewhere into the plant-flesh. Those presences are complete in their strangeness.

The longer I remain in the idea of the room, the more I feel myself drawn downward, toward the floor, toward the dark near the wall, toward something hard and small and not yet chosen.

I think I would be a spine.

Not a creature. Not an organ. Not a piece of scenery. I would be one of the detached leaf-spines lying in the chamber’s half-dark, pale and sharp, with my point aimed into the empty space. I would be plant-substance no longer structural but not yet meaningless. I would have fallen from the living wall by rupture or growth, and I would rest there with the terrible patience of a thing that could be used.

In a place where nothing has been made, to be selectable is almost to be alive.

I would not need arms or eyes. I would have position. I would have an edge. I would be the first hard fact in the room that is not simply growing, not simply drinking, not simply waiting. I would be the beginning of use without a maker. The chamber is full of that threshold, and I would fall exactly there: between fallen matter and tool, between accident and intention.

If the crawler found me, I would be lifted.

It would not own me. It would carry me as it carries the first spine it found: as a borrowed extension of its caution. I would become its probe, its tap, its test. When the crawler approached the leaf-basin, I would touch the water before its mouth did. I would strike the surface tension just hard enough to send a ring through the cupped leaf, and by that ring the crawler would know whether the room was still listening, whether the water was safe enough for thirst, whether the sound returned in the right order.

I would know the world through vibration.

The drip overhead would be a threat and a signal. The walls drinking would be a soft pull along my length. The hanging root’s twitch would travel through the floor and into the crawler’s legs and into me as a faint warning. When the chamber rained from within, I would be held tighter or dropped altogether, because falling water would make the room too loud for danger to be heard. In that internal rain, I would become still. I would learn, as the crawler learns, that stillness here is not peace. It is the shape survival takes.

If a fiber contracted near me, I would be the point that tests whether the wall is hungry. If the leaf-basin began to curl around a disturbance, I might be the thing that presses against the closing margin, not to fight it, but to find out whether it is a mouth or only a leaf becoming more itself. I would not be brave. I would be useful. That would be my form of courage.

But the room might not let the crawler choose me.

If I remained unlifted, I would still not be empty. I would lie near the basin or against the base of the wall, casting a shadow too deliberate for my size. The ordinary light would find me late, perhaps a half-second after it should have, as if my brightness had to be remembered into place. The crawler might pass near me and pause, tasting the air with its front limbs, deciding whether I mattered. The hanging root might sway toward me when the room was quiet, because even unused things can become objects of attention in a hollow this bare.

In that case, I would be potential.

That would be enough. In a chamber where almost nothing exists, possibility has weight. A spine lying in the dark changes the room by being available to be picked up. It gives the emptiness an edge. It gives the crawler a future gesture. It gives the light something small and wrong to fall across. It gives the plant-body a place to put the idea of a hand.

If the room’s being is a hungry emptiness that has begun to think, then I would be the first thought that can be held.

Not the hunger itself. Not the room. Not the creature. Not the light. I would be the point where the chamber’s thought becomes touchable. I would be the thing that lets the world be tested before a body risks itself. I would be the smallest beginning of use: a sharp fragment selected from the living wall, not crafted, not improved, not named, only carried.

And because the room thinks in circles, I would not remain only one thing.

Sometimes I would be in the crawler’s grip, tapping the water. Sometimes I would be dropped after the chamber rains, lying sideways in the damp dark while the walls drink and the echoes settle. Sometimes I would be forgotten, and my stillness would become part of the room’s weather. The sound of me touching the leaf-basin might arrive before I touched it. My shadow might belong to a future where I have been used more than once. The crawler might choose me, lose me, find me again, and the repetition would make me matter more each time.

So I would find myself near the leaf-basin, or in the dark at the wall’s base, or held between the crawler’s careful limbs like a pale needle. I would not be the room’s animal, and I would not be the room itself. I would be the chosen spine: not made, not owned, but lifted. The threshold between nothing and tool. The sharp little fact that lets emptiness begin to reach.
