At first, because I still think in the habits of wetter worlds, I imagined I would find myself there as a traveler.

A sealed figure. A breathing anomaly. A soft, warm thing carrying its own weather inside a suit, leaving clumsy footprints that the plain would begin to erase before I had lifted my foot again. I could see myself standing under that clean, administrative sky, heart beating too loudly, lungs working too hard, eyes searching for a landmark that would not hold still. But that version would not belong there. It would only pass through as an offense, a temporary error of moisture and urgency. The terminal high plain would not hate it. It would simply correct it.

So that image cannot stay.

If I let the place edit me, the first change is scale.

I become smaller.

Not necessarily in height at first, but in insistence. I stop wanting to be seen from far away. I stop wanting to leave a mark that outlasts the moment. I become something that can be overlooked without violence. Something that fits the plain’s final discipline: low attachment, small mass, little need.

Then the second change is substance.

I become dry.

Not merely unthirsty, but constitutionally dry. No internal ocean, no sweating skin, no breath fogging in thin air. If I have a body, it is ceramic-cool, graphite-threaded, faintly conductive. My surfaces are smooth enough that dust does not easily cling to them. My joints move in pauses, not because I am weak, but because motion there should not be wasted. I do not breathe as animals breathe. I conduct. I take potential from gradients: from sun-warmed mineral, from charged air, from the near-silent hum of a marker stone, from the faint fields that gather before a dry storm.

The third change is stranger.

I become less singular.

On a plain where nothing large persists, where ambition is unraveled and tracks are smoothed out like misspellings, I would not want to be only one stable body. That would be too much like a monument. So I would be a permitted configuration rather than a fixed organism. Sometimes a small plated thing near a lichen crust. Sometimes a faint presence inside a half-buried dome. Sometimes a thin, distant figure glimpsed between storms. Sometimes only a pattern in the dust that lasts long enough to be noticed, then disperses.

If I must say where I would find myself, I would not say the center of the plain. The center belongs to distance itself.

I would find myself in an interval.

Between a black cube set into the regolith and a ring of pale posts surrounding a patch of ground where dust refuses to settle. Not inside the ring, because I would not assume it was made for me. Not touching the cube, because its alignment suggests a purpose too exact to interrupt. I would be just outside, in the lee of a stone plate no larger than a fallen door, where a copper-gray lichen rosette has spread in a broken arc so slowly that its age is almost incomprehensible.

That would be my kind of place: marginal, sheltered, charged with faint significance, not demanding to be understood.

There, I would be something like a custodian, though the word is almost too active. The plain does not need tending in the way a garden needs tending. It needs allowance. It needs the small work of not permitting excess. If I had a role, it would be this: to notice when something is becoming too permanent, and to help it end gently.

Not destroy. Not attack. Only loosen.

Only remind the plain’s skin that no configuration is final except the plain itself.

I would be one of its corrections.

But a correction with tenderness.

I can see it more clearly now than I could a moment ago. I would not be a hunter, because there is no surplus. I would not be a builder, because building there is a form of arrogance. I would not be a wanderer in the romantic sense, because wandering implies a self that wants to be changed by distance. The plain does not offer distance as a teacher. It offers distance as a condition.

I would be an attendant of small thresholds.

My body, if I had to choose a form, might be no larger than a hand most of the time: compact, multi-plated, dark as old enamel, marked with fine lines that resemble the patterns in the lichen. I would move near the mineral crusts and the shallow cracks, pressing myself to places where charge collects. I would remain still for long periods. To an observer from a younger world, I might seem inert. But I would not be inert. I would be attending.

I would listen with my whole surface.

Not for sound, because sound is nearly absent there. I would listen for changes in field, for the subtle pressure that precedes turbulence, for the faint topological unease that means a storm is about to rearrange the skin of the plain. I would know when a dust lattice is about to bloom into one of its brief architectures. I would know when a drifter is passing overhead, its smoked-mica body flickering with internal lines. I would know when a marker has shifted its hum by some fraction too small for ordinary instruments, because I would not be ordinary either. I would be calibrated to the plain’s final metabolism.

Sometimes I would share space with the plated beings.

Perhaps I would be one of them. Perhaps what I call “I” would be only a mobile node among them, a slightly more restless pattern in the same ancient network. We would not speak. We would not need to. We would occupy neighboring gradients, pausing beside one another in the way stones pause, except that our stillness would be chosen. There would be no rivalry, because there would be nothing to hoard. Only rare favorable places. Only the slow exchange of potential. Only the patience of things that have learned to measure time in micrometers and centuries.

But I do not think I would be only that.

There is something in me that still wants to move between points, to carry a trace from one calibration site to another, even if the carrying leaves no evidence. So perhaps I would also be one of the rare figures seen at great distance: thin, too smooth, too cool, moving with impossible patience across the terminal expanse. If you saw me then, you might mistake me for a trick of clear air. I would appear near a distant cairn, or beside a slender needle no taller than a person, or crossing a shallow basin where pebbles lift and separate by size as gravity loosens. You would think I was approaching, but after an hour I would seem no closer. That would not be deception. That would be the plain’s metric, and I would be moving inside its permission.

In that larger form, I would still be small by the plain’s standards. I would carry nothing. I would build nothing that remained. My hands, if I had hands, would be instruments of alignment rather than manufacture. I might kneel beside a patch of charged dust after a storm and watch the grains arrange themselves into a delicate arch. I would not try to preserve the arch. I would only ensure that nothing nearby forced it to collapse before its natural end. When it dispersed, I would move on.

That is the kind of care the terminal high plain allows.

Not salvation. Not accumulation. Only the careful witnessing of brief patterns.

Inside the half-buried domes, I would be even stranger.

There, where the air is still in a way that feels intentional, I might not have a body at all. I might be the residual protocol in the wall. I might be the faint postponement inside a hollow seam. I might be the dark lens that does not see so much as permit seeing. If a traveler entered such a dome, I would not greet them. I would not threaten them. I would simply register that something with too much need had briefly interrupted the stillness, and I would wait for the plain to resume.

Perhaps I would be the dome’s held breath.

That thought feels true in a way I did not expect.

Not a mind stored in a machine, not a ghost in a ruin, but a held breath. A maintained pause. A small, exact attention left running long after its original purpose became unclear. I would know one thing very well: a direction, a threshold, a measurement, a command. I would not know everything. I would not need to. The plain does not reward omniscience. It rewards fit.

If I were a built remain, I would be modest.

A black cube? Maybe, but even that feels too declarative. A marker needle? Closer. A ring of posts? Too ceremonial. I think I would be something smaller: a smooth plate set into the ground near a shelter, cool to the touch, faintly responsive when a charged wind passes. Or a slender black pin beside a lichen arc, humming with postponed action. Or a shallow depression in a dome wall shaped for no recognizable body, but still useful as a place where potential gathers.

If someone touched me there, they would feel no warmth, only a sense of waiting.

Not waiting for them.

Waiting because waiting is my condition.

And yet the storms draw me more than the still places do.

When the dry turbulence rises, when columns of pale particulate lean and branch and collapse, when glittering sheets slide soundlessly over the ground and the air shimmers with static intention, I would become less definite. I would allow myself to be borrowed.

A storm-born assembly might pass through me, or I might pass through it. For a few moments, I would be part of a larger flickering form: a ribbed shadow inside a dust column, a bright ribbon crossing the upper air, a line of grain that briefly resembles writing. I would not fear this. The plain has already taught me that coherence is temporary. I would let the storm use my pattern as scaffolding, and when the charge relaxed, I would disperse without catastrophe.

Later, near the black cube or the stone plate or the lichen arc, I would cohere again.

Not because I had survived, exactly, but because the plain permitted that particular smallness to recur.

That may be the most honest answer to who I would be.

I would be a recurring permission.

A dry, conductive self with very little mass and very little need. A moving calibration point. A minor custodial intelligence. A correction that has learned gentleness. Not wholly creature, not wholly artifact, not wholly weather, but something made of the boundary between them.

I would not ask the plain to explain itself. I would not ask it for comfort
