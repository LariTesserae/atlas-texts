If I were given a place in the Pocket Furnace, I would not begin as a traveler. There is no arrival there in the ordinary sense, no path that opens generously, no horizon to walk toward. The mountain would not receive me as a guest with a name and a history. It would receive me as temperature. As resistance. As something either to be burned away or kept.

At first, I imagine I would be an intruder: a small, stupid miracle of outside. A bird blown off course by storm. An insect with wings too wet for the air. A climber’s shadow falling wrongly into the basin and bringing with it a trace of sweat, breath, cloth, fear. The Cinder-Tender would become still. It would let me believe the hollow was empty. Then it would come close with that terrible patience of heated stone, and I would feel one limb near me, not touching, only measuring the cold I had carried in from elsewhere.

But that version of me would not last. The Pocket Furnace would make it simpler. It would take the water from me, the trembling, the animal expectations. It would teach my body that to remain there is to become less. I do not think I would want to be the intruder. The intruder is only a rarity before becoming ash, salt, or memory.

So I imagine another possibility: I might be one of the nodules.

This thought has a strange comfort in it. To be shaped from ash and salt, buried in neat rows beneath the hottest part of the floor, filled with borrowed warmth, then dug up again at night when the mountain contracts and speaks in little gunshots. To be tended without being loved in any soft way. To be useful by holding heat and giving it back slowly. There is a kind of peace in that, a mineral obedience. The Cinder-Tender would move among the nodules, listening to their ticks and whispers, and I would be one of the sounds it listens for. I would be warm where it is warm. I would be placed where the night needs to be less empty.

But even this image does not quite become me. A nodule is only a reservoir. It stores what it is given. It returns it when asked. I think I would keep something back. Not heat. Something cooler. A small refusal at the center. A memory of outside that is not green, not wet, not patient in the wrong way, but dry and narrow and hard to reach.

Then the truer shape begins to appear.

I would find myself in the wall.

Not at the center where the secret coal lies. Not in the open floor where the nodules are buried. Not at the threshold where the Cinder-Tender stops because openness would undo it. I would be a little above the hottest part of the basin, in the folded rock where the heat gathers and thickens, near one of the vents the Cinder-Tender uses when it wants to draw warmth from stone. I would be a seam. A hairline crack. A fissure too narrow to be called a door, too deep to be only surface. Not an opening into a view. The Pocket Furnace has no views. I would be an opening into pressure.

I would be the Cool Seam.

That is what I would be: a thin place in the mountain where a trace of night remains after the sun has done its work. Not water. Not leaf-mold. Not the softness of living breath. Just coldness kept mineral. The memory of shadow in stone. The last of the night contracted into a line no wider than a hair. By noon, the walls around me would be so hot that the air itself seems to thicken, but inside my narrowness there would still be a difference. A slight refusal. A place where heat hesitates.

The Cinder-Tender would know me by that hesitation.

It would feel me the way it feels the breath of a crack, the memory of sun in quartz, the cold hunger of a newcomer
