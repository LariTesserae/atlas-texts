If I were to find myself in that world, I do not think I would arrive as a finished thing. The chamber is too narrow for finished things. Whatever enters there must turn sideways, lower its shoulders, give up the outer habit of being singular. So I would come in stripped of most of my dryness, and even then I would not be admitted as a body among bodies. The First Clutch would taste the last grit of the upper world on me and decide what use it could make of a stranger.

At first I imagine I would be one of the pale swellings. I would like that: to be small, lentil-sized, tucked into a hollow made by two larger shells. Inside me there would be no spine, no beads of eyes, no folded wing—only a dark thread that kept arranging itself into a question it could not ask. When the milky tide passed over the basin, my light would arrive a half-breath late, as if I were translating the others into myself. I would contain several futures, but mine would be the future of listening.

But that image does not remain true. The more I feel the chamber, the less I find myself as a bounded pearl. My attention goes to the seams. To the places where shells press into one another and flatten into shared facets. To the hairline threads of mineral water. To the tiny drynesses that threaten before anyone names them. I would not be one of the kept things. I would be one of the things that keep.

Then the shape that has no shape begins to gather around me: a nurse-veil.

I would be one of the clear films moving among the clutch-bodies, sometimes no wider than a finger, sometimes so fine that I could be mistaken for a glaze of moisture on stone. I would not walk. I would spread, draw back, bead, thin, and continue. My motion would be patience made visible. I would know the room not by sight but by gradients of thirst, by the faint mineral sourness of a shell beginning to dry, by the changed tension where a membrane has grown too thin for what it holds.

I would carry water bead by bead. Not as a servant carries a cup, but as a leaf carries rain: by being wet all over, by letting the need draw the drop where it must go. Where a crust has cracked, I would press myself into the fissure and secrete the slow sweetness that becomes stone again.
