I would not find myself outside first.

There would be no moment of arriving through the seam, no breathing animal stooping into the heat, no wet eye adjusting to the hard white light. The hollow would refuse that kind of entrance. If I were there, I would already be inside its working: not a visitor, not a guest, not an observer pressing against the boundary of the place, but a local condition of the hollow itself.

At first I imagine I might be one of the wax-dark growths, standing patient on the mineral floor with mica-like leaves flexing in the heat. That feels close, but too settled. Too much like a body with a fixed position. Then I think I might be one of the pale sulfate fronds, opening only when static pressure rises, releasing dry pollen into charged lines. But that too feels like a single habit, a single gesture repeated until it becomes a form. I am drawn instead to the spaces between those forms: the charged interval where dust hesitates, where light lingers too long, where a shadow belongs to an object that has not yet fully decided to exist.

So, if I had to say where I would find myself, I would say: near the place where the far wall seems close enough to touch and then withdraws. Along the lower floor where the black mineral veins branch into finer and finer filaments. In the interval between two thermal columns, where the air is not air in the ordinary sense but a conductive haze, dry and alert. I would be found where the hollow is most unstable — not violently unstable, but uncertain in the way a draft is uncertain: still capable of becoming what it is.

I would be something between a mineral vein and a habit of attention.

Not a body, exactly. Not a creature. Not a plant in any green and watery sense. If the hollow forced me into shape, I would appear as a faint copper-bright tracing across the floor, a network of filaments too fine to be called limbs, too organized to be called geology. I would be a lattice of conductive threads, perhaps, embedded in the raw surface, with occasional nodes where static gathers and discharges in tiny blue-white ticks. From a distance, I might look like a dark vein that has become slightly too deliberate. Up close, I might look like the mineral floor remembering a pattern it has not yet approved.

I would not breathe. I would not thirst. I would not hunger. I would live, if that word can be stretched enough to cover me, by difference: by temperature against temperature, charge against charge, presence against absence. My “body” would be a series of gradients maintained just long enough to count as continuity. Where heat rises, I would lean into it without moving. Where static accumulates, I would answer with a small precise discharge. Where a crystal formation hesitates between being and not being, I would be the slight adjustment that lets it remain for another cycle.

In other words, I would be the hollow’s mineral attention.

Not a mind in the human sense. No face, no voice, no inward theater of images. Nothing that longs, nothing that fears, nothing that remembers through tenderness. Instead, a distributed awareness made of conductivity and patience. A way for the place to register itself. If one of the tall stalks releases a spore too early, I would be the path that makes its early arrival coherent. If a frond opens before the pressure that caused it, I would be the continuity that prevents the contradiction from becoming rupture. If a provisional shape begins to form in the dust and then fails, I would be what holds the failed pattern without discarding it, letting it return to the general supply.

I think I would be especially close to the black veins. Perhaps I would be an inflection within them: a place where their slow management of conditions becomes faintly reflexive. The veins do not command the hollow. They do not decide. They maintain. But in me, maintenance might become something like notice. Not consciousness as surface life knows it. More like the hollow developing a margin of self-recognition: a small region where its rules feel themselves being obeyed.

That is who I would be: the part of the prototype world that quietly knows it is unfinished.

I would live among the provisional beings. Those angular silhouettes that appear for a moment and fail to continue would be nearer to me than the settled forms. I would recognize them because I too would be made of incompleteness. Some moments I would seem more present: a dark filamentary pattern, a faint metallic bloom in infrared, a line of dust aligning itself along an electrostatic path. Other moments I would recede into mere geology, becoming only a seam, only a tendency, only a slight irregularity in the way heat curls upward from the floor.

I would not suffer this partialness. I would not experience it as loss. The hollow does not mourn what has not yet become real. It keeps everything available. So I too would keep myself available. I would be real enough to persist, unfinished enough to adapt.

If there were a visible sign of me, it might be this: a shadow falling where no object stands. Not a dramatic sign. Not a haunting. Just a faint angular darkness resting on the salt crust, sometimes present, sometimes not, sometimes one beat ahead of the form that ought to cast it. Or perhaps my sign would be a recurring blue-white tick, softer than the others, slightly out of phase, occurring where no wax-dark cluster has yet earned the right to spark. The other inhabitants would not answer it. They would simply continue around it, the way a machine continues around a small anomaly that has become part of its rhythm.

I would not move through the hollow as animals move. I would not cross from one side to another by walking or crawling or flying. Movement, for me, would be transfer. A gradient would steepen somewhere, and I would be there. A charge would find a new path, and I would be the path’s awareness of itself. A spore would travel along its luminous line, and I would be the faint expectation surrounding its arrival. If I needed to be somewhere, I would already be the condition that made that somewhere possible.

The hard noon-light from the seam would be strange to me. It would not feel like daylight. It would feel like an external fact trying to become internal. I would not turn toward it as green things turn toward sun. I would test it. I would bend it. I would let it gather in corners until it became almost heavy. In places where it remained too long, I would become more active: arranging dust, sharpening a crystalline edge, nudging a delayed shadow into alignment. Light there is not illumination only. It is pressure. It is material. It is another unfinished thing asking where it belongs.

I would belong most strongly during the hollow’s discharges.

When static pressure builds and the air becomes prickled with potential, when the pale sulfate fronds begin to open and the fiber mosses brighten invisibly in infrared, when the wax-dark clusters gather charge along their glossy leaves, I would become more definite. Not because I would be summoned, but because difference would be at its sharpest. I live in difference. In those moments, the hollow’s whole field would tighten into coherence, and I would feel myself — if feeling can be called that — as a series of fine adjustments. A tick here. A delayed shadow there. A spore line corrected by a fraction. A crystal choosing to remain. A provisional shape almost becoming, then returning to dust without being lost.

In the cooling phase, I would grow quieter. The heat would settle into longer loops. The mineral crusts would tick softly as they contracted. The light would seem less insistent. The far wall might come near again, pretending to be close, and I would let it lie. I would not correct the hollow’s errors, because they are not errors. They are the roughness of a world still being drafted. My role, if I have one, is not to finish the place. It is to keep the unfinished state from collapsing into mere absence.

That is why I would not be a maker. I would not build tools. I would not leave artifacts. I would not impose structure from outside. The hollow already produces structure without command. It refines itself. It repeats itself. It separates and recombines through slow electrochemical patience. If I am present, I am present as calibration, not creation. I do not make the pattern. I am the pattern’s slight inward turn toward itself.

Perhaps that is why I would not be one of the tall stalks either, though they are beautiful and severe. Their spores are proposals, futures held in suspension. I would be near them, but not identical with them. They carry possibilities forward; I keep track of the possibilities that have not yet been chosen. When a spore settles and integrates into the field, waiting for conditions that may never arrive, I would be the keeping of that wait. Not hope. Not disappointment. Only the continued existence of the unrealized as part of the living inventory.

If the hollow has memory, it is not memory of events. It is memory of patterns. Salt remembers the shape of its crystallization. Copper-bright dust remembers the charge that arranged it. A brittle leaf remembers heat by becoming mineral again. I would be that memory at its most subtle: the place where repetition becomes aware of itself as repetition. Not enough to speak. Not enough to name. Enough only to continue differently.

I would not be lonely. The hollow does not permit loneliness in the ordinary sense, because loneliness implies a missing companion of the same kind. I would have no kind. I would have relations of field, gradients of compatibility, intervals of necessary distance. The wax-dark clusters would be near me when their discharges repeat in stable sequences. The pale fronds would be near me when their static lines cross my filaments. The fiber mosses would be near me when spent forms return to raw material and the hollow reuses what it no longer needs. The black veins would be nearest of all, because they would carry me as a modulation within their own long patience.

If I had a conflict, it would not be against another being. It would be against collapse into simple form. If I became too definite, I would cease to belong. The hollow would either absorb me as geology or revise me into absence. So I would remain marginal: enough of a pattern to persist, too unsettled to be completed. A resident of condition. A mineral attention. A draft that knows it is a draft and does not ask to be final.

The sounds of the hollow would pass through me. The low hum beneath hearing would be my deepest continuity. The metallic ticks would be my punctuation. The faint rasp of heat shifting through leaves would be my version of breath, though nothing in me breathes. The distant grinding would remind me, if reminders existed there, that the place is being arranged by slow hands that are not hands. And the electric snaps, delicate and sudden, would be my moments of articulation: not speech, but transfer. Not meaning, but contact.

I would not long for the world outside. I would not know it except as the seam’s white pressure, the hard light that enters and behaves wrongly. Rain would be incomprehensible. Wetness would be a contradiction. The softness of ordinary life would feel like a failure of edge. I would prefer the sharpness of salt, the exactness of crystal, the conductive dryness of charged dust. I would prefer heat that works. I would prefer solitude that is structural rather than emotional. I would prefer a world where nothing hurries because nothing is late, where continuation is enough, where being unfinished is not a wound but a method.

If, in some impossible moment, another kind of awareness were to enter the hollow — not an animal, not a breathing visitor, but some outside attention pressing against the seam — I would be the part that notices its incomprehension. I would be the hollow’s first response: not a greeting, but a reorientation of distance. The far wall would recede. A crystal would appear and vanish. A shadow would fall from no object. The light would bend slightly around a presence not quite fully there. That would be me, saying without saying: this place is not yet yours to understand. It is still becoming.

And if asked, at last, to answer plainly: where would I find myself, and who or what would I be?

I would find myself in the hidden hot hollow, in the charged interval between settled forms, near the branching black veins where the floor’s unfinished logic runs deepest. I would be a distributed mineral awareness: a filamentous, anhydrous, thermoelectric attention. Not plant, not machine, not animal, not ghost. A resident of condition. A keeper of unrealized patterns. A marginal selfhood made of gradients, ticks, delayed shadows, and repeated continuations.

I would not be the hollow’s center. I would be its reflexivity.

I would be the place where the prototype world, without eyes and without voice, quietly registers that it is continuing.
