If I were there, I would not be a visitor from a wet world. Not for long.

A breathing animal, full of leakage and warmth and careless sound, would be an offense to that basin. Not because the place is hostile, but because it is accountable. It would notice the dampness in my lungs, the vapor in my exhalation, the salt-memory of tears, the tiny percussion of my pulse against too-soft skin. It would treat me as an unresolved material. It would begin correcting me.

So if I found myself in that world at all, I would already have been translated into its grammar.

I would be dry.

I would be sealed.

I would be quiet enough to be permitted.

And I would be found not in the brightest upper terraces, not among the great seed-stacks and solar leaves, but lower down: in the cool acoustic channels where the basin keeps its slowest thoughts.

There, beneath the productive layers, the light does not fall directly. It arrives sideways through translucent stems, or is reflected upward from pale light-recycling growths that resemble fungi but are not fungi. The air is still and cold in the manner of a maintained interior. The floor is not soil but generations of compressed life: root-mats over root-mats, leaf-plates fused into tile, pollen hardened into stone, old stems become beams, old gardens become foundations. The moss there is not decorative. It is a listening surface. It takes the pressure of a step, holds it, compares it, and decides whether it belongs.

I would belong there only because the moss had learned me.

My dwelling would be one of the ribbed domes of translucent cellulose, small among larger forms, set in a hollow where root-cables braid themselves into broad, slow circuits. The dome would not be a house. It would be a host. Its walls would brighten when I entered and darken slightly when I slept, if sleep is what such a body does. Its inner filaments would adjust the charge of the air around me. It would soften the acoustic cost of my movements. It would close seams against cold drafts it had no need to admit. Over time, it would know the exact pressure of my body and prepare for me the way a hand prepares to receive a familiar tool.

I would not be human there, though I might retain some faint human outline.

I would be something like a Waxborn.

My body would be narrow, pale, and layered: waxed fiber over living architecture, with skin like laminated leaf-tissue polished by spore-dust. My surface would have the cool sheen of something between ceramic and parchment. My joints would be braided, silent, self-lubricated by sealed capillary fluids that move only inside private channels. I would not sweat. I would not bleed. I would not exhale mist. Any moisture in me would be imprisoned in crystalline cells, bound into gels, locked so deeply that water would exist in me only as a structural memory, not as a freedom.

My face would be smooth, perhaps unsettlingly so. No mouth in the old sense. No need for open intake, no wet cavity of speech. Instead, light-sensitive pits or narrow resin bands where eyes might have been. A sternum modified into a quiet membrane capable of producing pressure-language: subsonic pulses, faint ticks, coded vibrations that the floor could carry better than air. If I spoke, it would be expensive. The basin would not waste sound. My words would be deliberate, rare, and felt more than heard.

I would feed, if feeding is the right word, by contact. I would press my palms to nodes of glassy resin and draw energetic beads into internal mills. I would recharge against conductive bark. I would stand in filtered light until my internal gradients balanced. I would not eat with hunger. I would not drink with thirst. I would be maintained.

But I would not be merely a maintenance organism.

I would be an anomaly with a function.

The basin would tolerate me because I would be useful in a way that its larger, slower residents are not. The Standing People are vast, architectural, and patient. The Rootloom Minds think through root-circuits too slow for sudden events. The Barkwrights repair what breaks. The Paper Moths carry messages and reproductive codes along high luminous routes. The Static Shepherds tune the upper charge-fields. But there would still be a need for something smaller, mobile, and strange: something capable of moving through local impossibilities without forcing them into sense.

I would be that.

I would be a walker of paradox-seams.

In places where distance obeys attention, where a grove recedes as one approaches it, where the same seed-towers appear in several directions at once, where causes arrive late or effects precede their triggers, the basin would need a witness that does not panic. I would be made for exactly that kind of quiet disturbance.

My work would be to notice.

I would carry records of the basin’s impossible intervals. A seed-pod cracking before the tendril strikes it. A wound sealing before it appears. A path lengthening when watched too directly. A flower opening in a sequence that has already happened. These would not be errors to me. They would be data. I would approach them with the patience of someone tending a very slow fire.

My skin would record them. Crystalline pigments in my arms and shoulders would change pattern with each anomaly, storing light-histories and charge-signatures in dry, permanent blooms.
