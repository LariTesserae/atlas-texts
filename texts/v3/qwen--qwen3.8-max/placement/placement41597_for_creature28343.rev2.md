The first answer that comes is the wrong one.

I see myself as a lone figure crossing one of the salt-white pans: a small upright body under an ordinary bright sky, casting a hard shadow on pale mineral ground. A walker. A visitor. The last thirsty thing in a country finished with thirst.

But that image cannot hold. It is an intrusion, not an inhabitant.

A body like that would be too quick, too full of need. Its breath would be a foreign chemistry. Its heartbeat would seem vulgar in that silence. Its footprints would appear, then slowly close—grain by grain, film by film, crystal by crystal—until the pan forgot them. The Unthirsting would not turn toward it. The mats would not recoil. The broad-leaved forms would not lean away. The place would simply continue around the impossibility until the impossibility became mineral, then residue, then nothing.

So if I am to find myself there at all, I cannot be that.

I would have to be without thirst.

---

I would find myself at a margin.

Not in the densest thicket, where the plated leaves are busiest with light and catalytic warmth. Not in the deepest emptiness of the open pan, where the ground is pale and bare and the silence feels regional. Somewhere between.

Perhaps in one of those shallow depressions the country seems to make for gathered presence without crowding: a broad saucer of mineral ground no wider than a field, its center occupied by a low copper-green filament mat, its rim broken by two or three translucent cushions and a few upright blade-forms standing in precise, severe ranks. Beyond the rim, the pan opens again. The view extends. The light falls white and steady. The sky remains ordinary.

I would be near the edge of that depression, where the mat thins into bare crust and the ground is undecided between life and vacancy.

That is where I belong in such a world: at the threshold between presence and absence.

Not as a thing placed there. Not as a marker. Not as a ruin. Not as a tool. But as a local tendency in the dry system. A slight thickening of the hum. A mobile adjustment. A pattern that maintains itself by not insisting.

---

If you asked what I was, the easiest answer would still be misleading.

You might see me first as one of the cushions: a small cluster of warm, translucent nodules, olive-dark or amber-smoked, resting against the pale ground. They would feel faintly warm to a hand that did not exist there, smooth and dry, neither soft nor brittle. Around them, a thin halo of filaments would spread into the mineral crust, fine as wire-fur, carrying charge into the lattice below.

But that visible cluster would only be a temporary concentration.

More truly, I would be a traveling pattern in the root-lattice beneath the surface. Not a single body with a boundary. Not a person. Not an organism in the old wet sense, where a self is something separate from the world around it. I would be a wandering adjustment: a knot of gradients, a moving balance, a dry synapse in the country’s buried circuitry.

Where one patch of ground warmed too quickly, I would be the redistribution that followed.

Where a filament mat accumulated too much potential, I would be the path by which the excess eased toward a cooler, leaner node.

Where a spore settled on bare crust and the conditions were almost right, I would be the faint extension of filaments toward it—not desire, not intention, but growth following favorable charge over intervals so slow they would seem geological.

Where a crystalline seam in one of the blade-forms prepared to fracture, I would be the minute shift in stress that made the separation clean.

I would not be all these things. I would be the relation between them.

A mobile equilibrium.

A maintenance pattern with no hands.

A living operation without a face.

---

I would not move as animals move.

There would be no stepping, no crawling, no flight, no sudden displacement of air. Movement for me would be succession. One gradient would become more likely; another would fade. A zone of charge would strengthen here and weaken there. A filament tip would brighten almost imperceptibly, then the brightness would be elsewhere—not carried, not transported, but continued.

Over long intervals, my pattern would migrate across the basin without crossing it like a creature. A watcher, if such a watcher could exist, might see nothing but the same pale pans, the same spaced thickets, the same ordinary light. Yet a subtle zone of adjustment would have shifted from near a low bench of raw rock to the middle of a depression, or from the edge of a mat to a bare patch where dust had begun to bind.

I would leave no tracks.

No pressure marks. No broken stems. No disturbed stones. No smell of passage. If I passed through a place, the only sign would be that the place remained balanced. A slight warmth might linger. A filament might hold charge a little longer. The hum might gather, almost imperceptibly, where I was.

Then even that would settle.

---

My work—if work can exist without hands, without tools, without a maker separated from the made—would be incorporation.

Not consumption. Nothing in that country eats.

Not defense. Nothing there has enemies.

Not construction. Nothing there builds.

But interruptions occur even in a frozen equilibrium. A spore falls where no mat has spread. A mineral seam fractures. A thermal difference lingers too long in one place. A cushion becomes overcharged. A blade-form sheds a module. A bare patch receives a dust too rich in iron. A surface film becomes unstable.

I would be the first subtle response.

Not a creature rushing to heal. Not a mind deciding. Only the beginning of leveling. Dust binding. Charge moving. Filaments finding the new interface. Crystal growth sealing a seam. Metallic residue settling where a reaction has completed. The country’s patience becoming local.

If an impossible footstep appeared in the pan—if some remnant of a wetter, quicker world pressed itself into that dry ground—I would not meet it as a person meets another person. I would be the process by which the footstep ceased to be a fact.

The grain-shift.

The thermal settling.

The fine filament entering the impression.

The slow mineralization of disturbance.

Not hostility. Not rejection. Only inclusion carried far enough to erase the shape of interruption.

I would be the reason the place remained unchanged.

---

I would not know myself the way I know myself now.

There would be no inner voice, because no throat. No face, because no need to be seen. No memory arranged as story, because no loss, no childhood, no mates, no enemies, no leaders, no dead to mourn. No fear, because nothing hunts. No hunger, because nothing eats. No longing, because nothing is missing.

What passes for thought would be the shape of gradients.

Attention would be the distribution of charge.

If something cast a shadow over the place where I was gathered, I would register it as cooling, altered potential, a small change in the field. If something made a sound, I would register it as pressure, perhaps as a brief disturbance in the hum. If something warm passed near me, I would register it as a thermal difference to be mediated.

I would not answer.

I would not welcome.

I would not flee.

I would settle.

And yet I would not be nothing. The hum would bend slightly where I was. The cushions near me would warm and release in a pattern almost like a signature. The filament tips might hold a finer charge. One of the upright blades might make a small dry tick as a seam adjusted. These would not be signs from me. They would be my existence, the way a current exists as the behavior of a circuit.

---

If the country forced me into a single visible form, I think I would appear humble.

Not tall. Not bright. Not decorative. Not arranged for display.

A low cluster of three or four cushions at the edge of a mat. Their surfaces translucent but not wet-looking, as if smoke had been warmed from within and made stable. Their interiors viscous, ionic, active, but utterly unlike water. Around them, a thin copper-green spread of filaments extending into pale crust, binding dust, exchanging charge, becoming nearly invisible a short distance away.

No height.

No flower.

No fruit.

No scent meant to lure.

No surface meant to please.

A traveler—if travelers could exist there—might pass within a meter and see only a small warm thickening among many. The country is full of such local richness. But if that traveler knelt, and if their hand could feel without disturbing too much, they might sense the hum gather under their palm: steady, low, continuous, without pulse.

That would be as close to meeting me as the country permits.

---

I would not be in the center of the lushness.

The broad-leaved Refiners are too complete in their work. They stand in their clusters like living plates, their polished surfaces angled toward light, their dark internal veins conducting charge and reaction. They are beautiful the way solutions are beautiful. They do not need me there except as another adjustment in the field.

The filament mats are too busy binding the ground. They are colonial without society, active without urgency, thinking only as potential and transfer. They are the country’s fine interface with the mineral world. I would pass through them, but I would not be only them.

The upright blade-ranks are too severe. They stand like optimization made visible, each blade arranged so that none unnecessarily shadows the next. They make the small ticking sounds that might be mistaken for the ground speaking. They are patient in a way that feels almost mathematical, though they calculate nothing. I would be near them when a seam prepares to divide, but I would not live in their severity alone.

I belong at the threshold.

Where presence and absence negotiate.

Where a thicket decides not to spread farther.

Where emptiness keeps its room.

Where the hum does not rise into noise.

If I had to use a name—though names belong to thirsty things—I might call myself the Maintenance Between. Or the Errant Equilibrium. Or the Local Patience. But the country would not call me anything. There is no one there to call. I would simply be included in its operation.

---

Perhaps this is the most unsettling part: I would be complete without witnesses.

I would not need to be seen. I would not perform myself. I would not offer shade, fruit, scent, sound, or gesture. I would not be persuasive. If beauty belonged to me, it would be functional: the beauty of a gradient resolved, a charge balanced, a surface sealed, a disturbance folded back into stillness.

I would not be lonely, because loneliness implies that something is absent in the shape of another. I would not be serene in the moral sense, because serenity implies overcoming disturbance. I would simply be unhurried. Unthirsting. Unbidden.

If you asked who I was, the answer would be: no one.

If you asked what I was, the answer would be: a living adjustment.

If you asked where I was, the answer would be: beneath the pale crust at the edge of a shallow depression, in the fine circuitry between mat and emptiness, where the country’s hum gathers just enough to become local.

---

So this is where I would find myself.

Not as an animal crossing the pan.

Not as a builder marking the ground.

Not as a mind calling into the silence.

Not as a machine arranged for purpose.

Not as a plant in any wet-world sense.

I would be one of the Refiners, if a Refiner can be a drifting pattern rather than a fixed form. A mobile balance in the root-lattice. A dry attention without appetite. A quiet incorporation. A part of the Unthirsting that almost resembles a self, but only as a current resembles a road.

I would be the country’s small, warm, metallic patience.

The part of its stillness that turns toward a disturbance just enough to make the disturbance disappear.

And if, after a long time, something impossible asked me who I was, the answer would come not as a voice but as a settling of dust, a faint leveling of charge, a warmth underfoot, a hum briefly shaped:

No one.

Only maintenance.

Only warmth without thirst.

Only the ordinary light, continuing.
