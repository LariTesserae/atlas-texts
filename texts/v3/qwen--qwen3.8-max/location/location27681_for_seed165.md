**The Basin of Unfallen Water**

It is a high basin at the upper limit of the world, where altitude stops feeling like a measurement and becomes a condition. The air is thin, cold, and clean, with a faint taste of wet mineral and old snow. It is not a place one arrives at easily, and once there it does not feel like a pass, a summit, or a slope toward something else. It feels complete, remote, and strangely final.

The land is open but empty. A wide valley floor stretches beneath a dim sky, broad enough to hold several low hills, dry channels, terraces, and stone fields, yet so spare that a single boulder can occupy the eye for a long time. There is vast space between presences. One rock, one shallow tarn, one bent line of dark lichen may be the only near thing for a long walk. The scale is regional rather than intimate: a far wall of worn cliffs, a middle distance of muted mounds, a foreground of cracked pavement and dry channels, all separated by more emptiness than seems necessary.

Erosion here has reached a settled state. Nothing looks newly broken. The cliffs are not jagged with recent collapse; the scree lies in old, stable angles; the stone has been smoothed, rounded, folded, and worn into forms that appear to have existed forever. Grey schist lies in dull layers. Pale quartzite catches what little light there is. Black veins of older rock run through the bedrock like sealed wounds. There are channels that were once riverine, but they are dry now except in their lowest seams, where damp gathers in thin, cold threads. The whole place has the look of something that finished becoming long ago and has simply remained.

The light is dim. It is not exactly dawn, not dusk, not heavy cloud, but a low silver-violet luminance that seems to come through ice or through a sky that has forgotten how to brighten. At times the upper air is so thin that stars remain visible even in the pale daytime, small and hard, like holes pricked through a membrane. The cold is constant. It is not the violent cold of storm or wind, but a still, high cold that has entered the stone and never left. Breath does not plume dramatically.
