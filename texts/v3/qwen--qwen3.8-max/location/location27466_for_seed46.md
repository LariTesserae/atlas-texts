It is a hidden hot hollow, high in a dry mineral upland, a pocket no larger than a room folded into the raw skin of unfinished terrain. Outside, there is only thin altitude, pale dust, and the sense of a world still waiting to be shaped; inside, the place is sealed, intimate, and uncomfortably alive with heat.

Ordinary light enters through a narrow seam or seems to have been trapped there from the beginning: white, hard, unromantic noon-light. It falls on surfaces that have not yet been weathered into gentleness. The ground is new and angular — dry salts, metallic oxides, brittle silica, copper-bright dust, black mineral veins, and crystalline crusts that look as though they were extruded moments ago. There is no erosion here in the usual sense. Nothing has been rounded by rain or softened by time. Every edge is embryonic, sharp, provisional. The place feels less discovered than emitted, as if the landscape is being printed slowly from somewhere beneath it.

There is no water. Not in pools, not in mist, not in dampness, not in the memory of wetness. The chemistry of the hollow is anhydrous and mineral. What would be moisture elsewhere becomes here a fine conductive haze, a dry ionic breath, a shimmer of charged particles suspended in the hot air. The atmosphere is clear but thick with heat, and the light behaves slightly wrongly: it remains too long on surfaces, gathers in corners, or bends around objects that are not quite fully present.

The hollow is hot, but not with the simple heat of sun-baked stone. It is a working heat. The pocket contains its own slow engines, though no engine has been built. There are no structures, no tools, no artifacts, no walls laid by intention. Instead, industrial process exists as a condition of the place. Thermal columns rise from the mineral floor and pass through coiled growths that flex with the patience of pistons. Static charges accumulate on filamentous leaves and discharge in tiny blue-white ticks, welding dust into crusts. Certain mineral seams refine themselves in slow electrochemical gradients, producing repeated crystalline parts with the regularity of manufacture. The landscape is not inhabited by machines; it behaves like machinery. Production, separation, refinement, repetition — all occur without makers.

Vegetation is lush in form, though sparse in number. The plants are not green in any simple way. Some are wax-dark and heat-glossy; others are pale as sulfate, tipped with copper rust or blackened at the edges where their metabolism runs hot. They grow in isolated clusters, leaving broad stretches of bare mineral floor between them. Their leaves may be layered like mica, flexible only when warm. Their roots are not roots in the ordinary sense but conductive filaments threading the raw geology, drawing charge from temperature differences rather than water from soil. There are frond-like forms that open only when static pressure is high, low mosses of metallic fiber that bloom in infrared, and tall sparse stalks crowned with dry luminous spores. They are abundant in complexity but few in body: a small population of strange beings spaced carefully across the chamber.

No animal life exists here. No insect, no crawling thing, no bird, no breathing visitor. The plants reproduce without fauna, using thermal updrafts, crystal fracture, and electrostatic transfer. Pollen, if it can be called that, moves in precise lines between charged points, sometimes arriving before the flower that releases it. Decay is not rot but re-mineralization: spent leaves stiffen, become brittle, then break into raw material that the hollow reuses. The ecology is closed, patient, and industrial in its recycling.

The sound is mostly quiet. There is a low hum beneath hearing, felt in the teeth and soles of the feet. Occasionally a soft metallic tick marks the cooling of a mineral crust. Leaves rasp faintly when heat shifts. A distant grinding suggests geology being arranged by slow hands, though there are no hands. Sometimes there is a brief electric snap, almost delicate, followed by silence so complete it seems to press inward.

The place is governed by rules that do not fully obey the world beyond its seam. Distance is unreliable. The far wall may seem close enough to touch, then recede with each step. Certain plants appear once, yet cast several shadows from different moments. A crystal formation may be present in one glance and absent in the next, not because it vanished but because it has not yet decided whether it belongs to this hour. The light can illuminate an object without casting its proper shadow. Heat flows in loops that seem intentional. The hollow is a pocket of reality under construction, and its unfinished nature makes it unstable.

This is not a ruin. There is nothing to ruin. It is more like a first draft of a place, or a prototype world left running quietly by itself. Its lushness is not abundant life in the familiar sense but concentrated invention: a few vigorous forms emerging from mineral emptiness. Its industrial character is not pollution or labor but process made visible — heat moving work through matter, repetition becoming structure, energy organizing matter without command.

The changes are slow. A new leaf may take days to uncoil. A salt ridge may rise by fractions of a grain. Static patterns repeat until they become habit. The hollow breathes in long thermal cycles: warming, charging, discharging, cooling, settling, then beginning again. Nothing hurries. Nothing is completed. The place waits inside itself, hot and hidden, lush without wetness, mechanical without machines, real only insofar as it continues to emerge.
