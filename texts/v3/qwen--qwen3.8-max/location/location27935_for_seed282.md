Beneath the surface, in a service pocket tucked under the underside of a larger industrial world, there is a landscape no bigger than a low room. It is hidden in the way that essential things are often hidden: behind flush panels, between conduit and cooling lines, beneath a floor that hums faintly with power elsewhere. It is not a cave. Nothing in it is natural in the sense of being untouched. Every wall, plate, seam, and shadow has been made.

The chamber is built from fabrication, not excavation. Its shell is layered steel, ceramic sleeves, leaded cable runs, riveted access panels, and black glass inspection ports. The ceiling is a close canopy of pipes, chilled coils, sensor stalks, wire bundles, and hanging chains beaded with moisture. The floor is a patchwork of grated walkways, shallow pans, sloped metal plates, ceramic gutters, and recessed trays. Even the dust here is manufactured: fine metal filings, lime bloom, glass powder, salt residue, polymer flecks, and shredded insulation caught in damp corners. There is no original soil, no bedrock, no substratum that predates the building. Whatever ground exists has been produced
