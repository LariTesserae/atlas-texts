**What Lives in the Court of Sweating Stone**

At first, the basin refuses the question.

If you ask what lives there, the first answer is the answer of the high air and the bare rock: nothing that can be hunted, nothing that can be gathered, nothing that nests, burrows, sprouts, feeds, bleeds, or calls. No green thing. No feather. No shell. No track in the grit. No egg hidden in a crevice. No root threading the stone. The Court of Sweating Stone is closed to ordinary life. It belongs to cold, light, mineral water, and time.

But that is only the first answer.

The second answer is stranger and more crowded. The basin is not empty. It is inhabited by lives that do not require flesh, by presences that do not move as bodies move, by beings whose births are condensation, whose breathing is freezing and sublimation, whose memory is salt, whose voices are ticks of thermal stress and the tiny ring of metal touched by falling ice. What lives there are not creatures in the warm-blooded sense. They are slow inhabitants, mineral citizens, atmospheric pilgrims, and residual intentions. They are lives measured not in heartbeats but in beads of water.

If one must name them, they are the Congregation of Pillars, the Bead-Born, the Salt-Scribes, the Frost-Lens Sleepers, the Copper-Bound, the Fitted Ones, and, taken together, the Court itself.

### The Congregation of Pillars
