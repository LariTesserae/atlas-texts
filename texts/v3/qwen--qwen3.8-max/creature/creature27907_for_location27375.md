Others open only when the dark is loud: pale flower-throats lifted toward the moving air, ear-shaped leaves turning to catch the night draught, and seed bells whose inner membranes tremble until a fine gold powder shakes loose. In the hollow, night does not bring silence. It lowers the key.

What lives there is not numerous. It is concentrated, sealed, and careful. Every body is a vessel. Every movement spends something precious. The hollow supports life the way a struck bell supports sound: not by abundance, but by exactness.

The first inhabitants are the plants, and they are the true majority. They do not grow because the place is gentle; they grow because the place is focused. Light, vibration, and mineral dust gather in certain terraces and courtyards, and there life knots itself tightly. Elsewhere the surfaces remain bare, bright, and swept clean by wind.

The crystalline groundcover is alive, though it could be mistaken for frost or mineral bloom. It is a living crust of sealed cells bound into salt plates, so dry that it seems brittle, yet it repairs itself grain by grain. It holds the floor together, slows the dust, and gives the beetles their first grazing. When something crosses it, it makes a tiny rasping sound, like sand remembering how to speak.
