What lives there are not born, and they do not arrive.

They are not ghosts, because ghosts require a past and a leftover. There is no leftover in the basin. They are not machines, because machines imply parts forced into service. There is no servitude there. They are not plants merely, though the green crowded ranks may first persuade you otherwise. The life of the place is broader than the distinction between plant and animal, and rarer than either.

At first the basin seems inhabited by its vegetation alone: the crystalline mats, the translucent fans, the salt stalks, the broad fronds arranged in exact crowns. But after a while you understand that some of the green forms are not scenery. They are residents that have taken verticality as their nature. The tall trunk-like presences with pale faceted surfaces and leaf-scaled skins are not growing toward light. They are standing in the way that a proof stands. Their surfaces accept the ordinary light and return it without waste. The tiny leaf-scales are not organs of feeding. They are attention made minute and repeatable. Each one is a small correctness. Together they make the standing ones aware, if awareness can exist without curiosity.

The standing ones are not numerous, but they are not solitary. They occur where the forest is densest, their trunks near-touching, their crowns overlapping in the dark terraced intervals. They do not lean. They do not sway. They are not restrained from motion; they simply have no motion to withhold. Their life is not a process moving through them. It is a state in which they participate. If you look at one long enough, you may begin to suspect that it is not a body but a place where the basin has become locally self-aware. Its shape is not anatomy. It is position with dignity.

Lower among the roots are the folded ones.

They are the shapes your eye catches at the edge of recognition: a knot of pale limbs arranged against a trunk, a low form resting in the angle between crystal blades, something whose joints are bent but not relaxed. They resemble animals only in the sense that they suggest repose. Their limbs are folded with such precision that they seem closed instruments rather than legs or arms. They have no flanks that rise and fall. They have no mouths turned toward food. They do not sleep, because sleep would be a change of state, and they are already complete. When you see one, you do not see a creature waiting. You see a creature whose waiting has been solved away.

The folded ones seem to keep the lower order of the basin. The shining root-lattices braid through the salt crust around them, and where their bodies touch the ground the patterns remain exact. They are not tending the roots. They are part of the roots’ argument. Their stillness is a kind of maintenance without labor. If they have hands, those hands are not for grasping. They are more like termini, endpoints where a body’s geometry can meet another geometry without altering it. If they have faces, those faces are not expressive. They are polished planes that hold the surrounding world in miniature and return it truthfully.

There are also the faceted heads, if heads is what they are.

They appear among the stems and on the upper terraces, sometimes set upon no visible body, sometimes implied by the way light gathers into a sharp, many-sided form. They do not turn. Yet when you look again, a different face of them is present, as if the basin has chosen which angle to permit. Each facet contains a reflection of the country, but no two reflections agree perfectly. One shows the salt plain as it is. Another shows it close when it is far. Another shows a nearby thicket with a shadow that has no source. The faceted heads are not looking at the basin. They are the basin considering itself from several incompatible positions at once.

Their eye-like surfaces are among the most unsettling details of the place. They are not eyes because there is no lens of liquid, no pupil contracting, no muscle turning them. They are surfaces that reflect with too much accuracy. To be seen by one is not to be watched. It is to be included. Your figure appears in it cleanly, without distortion, but the reflection seems to belong there more naturally than your body does. For a moment you may feel that the image in the facet is the real visitor, and you are only the disturbance that brought it.

The suspended particles in the warm air are alive in another way, if they can be called alive at all. They hang without drifting, glittering without motion. They may be reproductive units, but there is no reproduction here in the sense of succession. They are not seeds waiting for rain, because there is no rain and no waiting. They are possibilities held at the exact point of remaining possible. Some seem almost like unborn inhabitants: not infants, not embryos, but unresolved forms. The basin does not need them to become anything. Their completeness lies in their suspension. They are a population of maybes, each one balanced so perfectly that it never has to fall.

There are lives stranger than bodies.

In the crowded places where the stems stand close and the spaces between them darken, something occupies the intervals. It is not a creature with mass. It is more like a thickness in relation. The darkness between two trunks may be deeper than the trunks permit. A branch may cast two shadows, one correct and one plausible, and the plausible shadow may be the living part. These interval beings do not move because they are not located in the ordinary way. They exist wherever the basin’s geometry has a subtle redundancy: a duplicated outline, a reflection arriving too soon, a distance that refuses to remain single. They are not parasites. They are the place’s small self-differences. They live in the gap between what is seen and what is permitted to be seen.

None of these inhabitants feed. None drink. None breathe in a way that changes the air. The basin’s warmth passes through them without becoming heat. The light enters them and is accounted for. If they exchange anything, it is not food or waste but pattern: a constraint passed from one form to another, a charge balanced without current, a correction so slight that it registers only as the faint crystalline tick felt in the bones. That tick may be their entire society. It is not communication as message. It is communication as confirmation: one term touching another and being exactly canceled.
