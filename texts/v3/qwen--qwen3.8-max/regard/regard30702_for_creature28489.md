What a benevolent and wise power would do is not what a frightened or impatient power would do.

It would not arrive as rescue.

Rescue, in the lower world, often means taking a thing out of itself. It means correcting what is strange, softening what is severe, filling what is hollow, making the unbearable ordinary as quickly as possible. But wisdom knows that some places are not mistakes, even when they are dangerous. Some beauties are made from deprivation. Some lives persist not despite absence but through it. Some music can exist only where flesh has become thin enough to let the wind pass through.

So the first act of the benevolent power would be restraint.

It would not flood the basin with rain. It would not send green things climbing over the pale stones. It would not warm the air until the crystal blossoms melted into common water. It would not still the storm, because the storm
