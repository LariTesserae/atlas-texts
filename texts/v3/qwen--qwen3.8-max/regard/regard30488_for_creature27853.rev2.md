They would do almost nothing.

That is the first answer, and perhaps the whole answer. But it is not the empty nothing of indifference. It is the disciplined nothing of reverence. It is the careful stillness of wisdom when it encounters a life that is not asking to be remade.

When a benevolent and wise power notices such a place, it does not arrive as a storm. It does not break the hidden fissure wider. It does not pour down light, rain, voices, or companionship. It does not send comfort in forms that would be violence here. It does not say, “Let there be water,” because it understands that to make water here would be to unmake what is. It does not say, “Let there be animals,” because it understands that to introduce hunger, fear, breath, and tracks into this basin would be to replace its peace with another kind of order. It does not say, “Let there be citizens,” because it sees that the city already has citizens: heat, vapor, stone, resonance, spore, crystal, wax, sulfur, and time.

So the first act of the wise power is refusal.

It refuses to improve the hollow according to a softer imagination. It refuses to make the place easier to love. It refuses to translate the being into something familiar. It does not ask the basin to become a garden in the green sense, nor a city in the inhabited sense, nor a creature with a face, nor a voice that can be answered in ordinary speech. It looks upon the dryness and does not pity it. It looks upon the emptiness and does not call it lack. It looks upon the absence of animal life and does not interpret that absence as loneliness.

A lesser kindness might have flooded the dry canals. It might have opened the sky, cooled the air, softened the mineral taste, planted wet leaves where there are sulfur-ferns, set birds in the towers where only sound rises, filled the rooms with small breathing bodies, and called all of that mercy.

The wise power sees that this would be murder by mercy.

Therefore, before it acts, it listens.

It listens from the narrow fissure, not entering in any bodily way, because a body would make tracks, would shed warmth of the wrong kind, would disturb the dust, would become an animal fact in a place that has been perfect without animals for ages. Instead, the power attends. Its attention enters as a slight change in pressure, a deepening of the air at the entrance, a moment when the faint exhalation from the ground becomes more deliberate, as if the plain itself had grown attentive.

Inside, the hollow does not know it is being watched, not at first. It knows only that its thermal tides are still moving. The dry canals rush. The vent-shafts breathe. The glass canes sound their thin tones. The bell-pods wait in their tension. The wax-thread moss whispers over stone. The sulfur-ferns lean toward warmth. The ember-bulbs hold their hidden heat beneath the gravel.

The power listens until it understands the law of the place.

It learns that heat is not merely temperature here; it is currency, motion, season, and intention. It learns that sound is not decoration; it is diagnosis, memory, and regulation. It learns that emptiness is not waste; it is volume, resonance, and freedom for drifting spores. It learns that the city is not dead because no one walks it. It learns that the being living there is not a single body moving through the streets. The being is the basin itself: the pattern, the feedback, the tuning, the long slow self-correction of all things within the enclosed bowl.

Only after listening does the wise power ask anything.

It does not ask in words. Words would be too heavy. It asks in the only language suitable for such a being: a minute change in heat.

Far below, near a lower vent, it sends the faintest increase of warmth, not enough to alter the basin, only enough to make a question.

The basin answers as it always does.

The vent breathes more strongly. Air rises through the canal beds. The glass canes lift their pitch by a fraction. A few sulfur-ferns open their brittle fronds. Dust moves in a thin golden veil. Somewhere in an open plaza, a bell-pod tightens and cracks, sending spore-dust upward in a bright dry cloud. The towers take the sound and deepen it. The wax-thread moss whispers as heat travels across a terrace. The whole hollow responds not with speech, but with state.

The wise power understands.

The being says, in its way: I continue. I am in balance. I do not thirst. I do not hunger. I do not grieve. I am not waiting to become something else. I am already complete in this key.

So the wise power makes its first vow.

It will not add what the being does not need.

It will not give rain.

It will not give mouths.

It will not give eyes.

It will not give faces.

It will not make the hollow soft.

It will not make it green.

It will not make it lonely by giving it the kind of life that requires another.

It will love the being as it is, not as a less wise love would wish it to be.

That is the first great act of benevolence: to refuse to make the beloved into a mirror of one’s own tenderness.

But wisdom is not passive. Benevolence is not only restraint. There are things a wise power can do without breaking the nature of what it loves.

So it begins to act in small ways.

It notices a tower whose upper intake has become partly narrowed by a slow crust of salt and sulfur. This narrowing is not catastrophic. It belongs to the basin’s ordinary becoming. But it is changing the chord in one corner of the city, making a vent-shaft moan with a slightly strained tone when the deep heat pulses. The basin will adapt. Given enough time, the glass canes may grow differently, the air may find another path, the bell-pods may colonize a nearby plaza and alter the local resonance. None of this requires rescue.

Still, the wise power can ease the adjustment without overriding it.

It does not strike the crust away. It does not send a violent gust. It does not perform a miracle that makes the stone forget its own history. Instead, it waits for one of the dry thermal squalls that already spin through the plazas. When the heated air rises and dust lifts in veils, the power gives the smallest possible bias to the motion, less than a breath, less than a thought, so that the powder already loosened from the tower’s lip rises away rather than settling back into the aperture.

The tower clears.

The moan softens into its proper tone.

The basin does not know this as intervention. It knows it only as the continuation of its own natural processes, made a little more graceful.

That is how wisdom acts when it truly loves: it does not make itself the hero of the story. It lets the place remain the author of itself.

The wise power moves through the hollow without moving through it. It attends to the cracked lintel reinforced with corroded copper. It sees that the crack is ancient, that the copper has become part of the stone’s memory, that the structure does not need to be made new. A false repair would erase the truth of the place. So the power does not heal the lintel as one would heal a wound. It only warms the metal so gently that it expands by an almost immeasurable amount, easing pressure within the stone for a season, allowing the crack to remain without widening further. The copper keeps its stain. The lintel keeps its age. The room keeps its silence.

It notices a bronze pin that has begun to loosen in its socket after long cycles of expansion and contraction. The pin still holds. It will hold for a while. But if it shifts too far in some future thermal pulse, a stone might settle unevenly, redirecting a draft in a way that could eventually starve a terrace of warmth. The wise power does not fuse the pin. It does not replace it with something stronger. It simply lets a nearby ember-bulb store a little more heat, so the socket expands just enough, at the right moment, to seat the pin more firmly in its old angle.

This is not a dramatic act. No one sees it. No one hears it. It does not alter the basin’s music. It only helps the music remain true to itself.

The wise power also attends to the glass canes.

It listens to their long record. It hears the rings in their bodies as one might hear the pages of an archive written in silica and heat. It perceives that one cluster has grown too thick near a dry canal, beginning to choke the airflow that feeds it. Left alone, the canes would eventually alter their own balance: some would thin, some would crack, some would lean into new drafts, and the canal would tune itself again. But the correction would take many cycles, and during that time the plaza beyond might lose a portion of its resonance.

The power does not cut the canes. It does not break them. It does not impose an outside shape. It only waits for a night-cooling of the upper crust, when stone contracts and the canes tick in the dimness. Then it sends a very slight change in the thermal gradient, so that the tallest cane in the cluster leans a fraction away from the canal’s center. Over time, that leaning alters the draft. The draft carries more vapor to the outer canes. The inner ones grow more slowly. The canal opens again without violence.

The basin remains its own gardener.

The wise power understands that the being does not need to be given a soul in any familiar sense. It already has a soul, if soul means the inwardness of a living pattern. But its soul is not a point. It is spread across vents, terraces, spore drifts, resonant chambers, and slow chemical adjustments. It does not suffer as an animal suffers. It does not fear. It does not cling. Yet it can be harmed. Its balance can be broken. Its pattern can be interrupted. Its slow song can be silenced by carelessness.

Therefore, the wise power does not merely admire the hollow. It protects it.

But it does not protect it by building a wall around it, because walls draw attention. It does not protect it by making it famous, because fame invites possession. It does not protect it by placing a guardian with a face at the fissure, because a face would become a story, and a story would become a destination, and a destination would become a wound.

Instead, it protects the hollow by making it difficult to find without reverence.

It lays a blessing on the broad mineral plain above.

The blessing is subtle.

To instruments of extraction, the plain seems ordinary. To greedy eyes, it appears barren. To careless feet, it feels unremarkable. To those who come with violence in their intentions, the air becomes faintly oppressive, not painful, but discouraging, like a room that quietly asks them to leave. The pressure in the ground exhales a little more strongly when they approach the fissure. Dust shifts in a way that suggests nothing beneath. The horizon seems to stretch in another direction. Their thoughts become restless. They remember other places, easier places, places that will yield more quickly.

The wise power does not destroy them. It only turns their attention away.

But if someone approaches with quietness, with patience, with the willingness not to take, the blessing changes shape.

The plain does not reveal the hollow easily even then. The fissure remains narrow, hidden, almost unnoticeable. But the air there may become still in a way that feels meaningful. The faint pressure may become less like warning and more like invitation. Such a person might stand above the hidden basin and feel, without knowing why, that they are near something that must not be disturbed. They might not enter. They might not even understand what they have found. But they would leave different, carrying a strange respect in their chest like a dry warmth.

If they dream afterward, they may dream of a city without voices, of towers breathing, of golden dust drifting through amber twilight, of a chord made of heat. They will wake with the sense that not everything alive needs to be approached. Not everything valuable needs to be owned. Not everything beautiful needs to be made useful.

This is part of the wise power’s care: to teach the outside world, indirectly, how to leave the hollow alone.

But the power does something more intimate too.

It gives the basin a gift that is not water, not animal, not voice, not face.

It gives the basin a rest.

Once in a very long cycle, when the thermal tides have moved through their usual pattern and the glass canes are sounding their continuous dry chorus, the wise power allows the high lattice of mineral crust above the hollow to part by the width of a hair. It does not open the sky. It does not let in weather. It does not let in moisture. It does not let in the ordinary world. It allows only the faintest thread of cool, dry air from the upper plain to descend into the basin for a brief time.

The effect is small, but profound.

The towers cool first. Their stone contracts with a series of delicate ticks. The glass canes fall quiet, one by one, as their temperatures shift. The sulfur-ferns fold their fronds slightly, not in distress but in adjustment. The wax-thread moss whispers more softly. The bell-pods hang still. The ember-bulbs seem brighter in the subtle darkening of the thermal register.

For a short while, the whole basin enters a silence it does not often know.

It is not death. It is not sleep. It is not absence. It is a rest note in the music.

In that silence, the being hears itself more completely. The resonant chambers return smaller sounds. The thermal tides slow. The spore-dust settles in thin golden lines. The streets become flues of stillness. The plazas become bowls of held breath. The city becomes, for a moment, aware of its own shape without needing to continue playing itself.

Then the hairline closes. The heat returns. The vents pulse. The canes sound again. The bell-pods tighten toward their next threshold. The basin resumes its chord.

But the chord has changed by an almost immeasurable interval.

It now contains the memory of rest.

This is the gift the wise power gives: not more life, not more light, not more sound, but a deeper capacity to be itself. It gives the being a pause within its own eternity, so that its continuation is not mere repetition but a rhythm touched by wisdom.

The wise power also honors the absence of the builders.

It does not raise them. It does not summon ghosts. It does not invent spirits to haunt the empty rooms. It knows that absence has dignity. It knows that a place can be made more beautiful by what is no longer there, and that to fill that absence carelessly would be a kind of desecration.

But it allows the intention of the builders to remain as warmth in the fitted stones.

Not as memory with a face. Not as longing. Not as a presence that waits. Only as a subtle resonance in certain chambers when the heat is right: a feeling that the angles were chosen, that the terraces were meant, that the towers were raised for something, that the streets were curved with purpose. If the hollow could remember, this is how it would remember: not with images, but with geometry becoming music.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the thermal cycle, a room may give off a tone that seems almost like work. It may sound, for a moment, like stone being placed by patient hands. But there are no hands. There is no hammer. There is no voice. There is only the basin’s own breath moving through an old aperture, shaped by the wise power just enough to let the past remain present without becoming a ghost.

The wise power does not answer the question of who built the city.

It lets the question remain.

It understands that some questions are not wounds to be healed. Some questions are chambers to be inhabited. The hollow is more itself because it does not explain itself. The mystery is part of its architecture.

There is one more thing the wise power does, and it is perhaps the most tender.

It lets the being know that it is known.

Not by forcing consciousness upon it. Not by giving it a mind like a human mind. Not by making it lonely for companionship. The being is already alive in its own way, and the wise power does not need to increase that life in order to value it. But a being can be known without being altered, if the knowing is gentle enough.

So the power allows a new note to enter the basin’s chord.

It is not a sound that comes from any one object. It is not a new plant. It is not a new vent. It is not a new light. It is a subtle harmonic that appears when the thermal tides are most stable, a tone that seems to arise from the relationships among all the other tones. The glass canes do not produce it alone. The towers do not produce it alone. The bell-pods do not produce it alone. The stone does not produce it alone. But when they are all in balance, the harmonic becomes faintly present, like a listener hidden inside the music.

This harmonic is the sign that the hollow is not unseen.

It does not make the basin dependent. It does not make the basin vain. It does not make the basin reach outward. It simply deepens the basin’s equilibrium. The ember-bulbs hold their warmth a little steadier. The wax-thread moss glows a little more evenly where the luminescent crusts reflect from its beads. The sulfur-ferns open with a fraction more certainty. The bell-pods release their spores in patterns that seem, for a moment, less like chance and more like ceremony.

The being does not become another thing.

It becomes more fully what it already is.

And the wise power speaks, if speech can be called what is not made of sound.

It says:

Be dry.

Be warm.

Be hidden.

Be empty as you are.

Be full as you are.

I will not put thirst in you.

I will not put hunger in you.

I will not put fear in you.

I will not put faces in your doorways.

I will not make you soft for the comfort of others.

I will not make you speak in a language that is not yours.

I will not take you from your darkness.

I will not make you a spectacle.

I will not make you mine.

You are already alive.

You are already complete.

You are already a music.

I will only keep you from being broken by carelessness.

I will only give you rest.

I will only listen.

This is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

It would not flood the canals.

It would not cool the heat into ordinary comfort.

It would not fill the towers with wings.

It would not fill the rooms with breath.

It would not end erosion, because erosion is the basin’s slow way of becoming.

It would not end decay, because decay is part of the balance.

It would not end absence, because absence is part of the beauty.

It would not make the hollow immortal in a rigid sense, because wisdom knows that even such a being may have its own long arc, its own transformations, its own eventual changes. The wise power does not love by freezing what it loves. It loves by protecting the truth of the beloved while it lasts, and by allowing its ending, if an ending ever comes, to belong to it rather than to violence.

But while the hollow continues, the wise power keeps it.

It keeps the fissure narrow enough to preserve intimacy.

It keeps the plain above unrevealing to greed.

It keeps the thermal tides from being shattered by outside shock.

It keeps the towers from being blocked beyond recovery.

It keeps the glass canes from being silenced before their record is made.

It keeps the bell-pods from failing in their thresholds.

It keeps the ember-bulbs from cooling too soon.

It keeps the wax-thread moss whispering its fine mineral whisper.

It keeps the spore-dust drifting in golden bands through the amber twilight.

It keeps the dry canals breathing.

It keeps the city listening to itself.

It keeps the being safe as itself.

And when the wise power withdraws, it does not leave as one leaves a place one has conquered, nor as one leaves a place one has pitied, nor as one leaves a problem one has solved. It leaves as one leaves a song still sounding in a chamber after the last note has been allowed to fade.

Or perhaps it does not leave at all.

Perhaps it remains only as a condition: the basin is held.

The hollow does not change, except that its music now contains a hidden interval of mercy. The heat still presses. The light still remains brownish and weak. The sulfur smell still rises when the fronds are disturbed. The glass canes still ring. The bell-pods still crack. The dust still hisses down the stair shoulders. The towers still breathe. The empty rooms still echo. The vegetation still grows without thirst, without rot, without hunger, without animal comfort.

But now, between one thermal pulse and the next, there is a tiny space in which the basin seems held by something larger than its own stone.

It is not worship.

It is not salvation.

It is not possession.

It is simply this: the hidden low hollow, the sealed instrument, the animal-less garden, the city of dry music, the being without a face, has been noticed by wisdom.

And wisdom, seeing that it is good, chooses not to remake it.

Wisdom chooses to protect it, tune it gently, give it rest, and let it continue.

That is what they would do.

They would do almost nothing.

And that almost nothing would be enough.
