If a benevolent and wise power turned its attention to that tableland, to the being who lives there—or to the scattered few who carry that form of life—it would not arrive as a conqueror of conditions. It would not look at the thinness and call it deprivation. It would not look at the dimness and call it blindness. It would not look at the absence of faces, as lowlanders understand faces, and decide that personhood had failed to occur. It would see what had been made: a life tuned to altitude, a grammar of pressure, a way of dwelling inside an unresolved horizon.

Therefore, its first act would be restraint.

It would not lower the plateau. It would not thicken the air by force. It would not warm the black stone into a counterfeit of somewhere else. It would not give them voices that could be heard from a distance unless those voices were asked for. A wise kindness does not begin by erasing the shape of the beloved. It does not assume that comfort and goodness are the same thing. It knows that some beings are not rescued by being made easier, but by being allowed to continue without unnecessary cruelty.

So the power would come, if it came at all, as something almost invisible: an equalization.

The being might feel it first as a soft fullness behind the eyes, the way another person nearby is felt, and turn with the slow economy of its limbs. But no one would be there. The hollows would be empty. The cairns would stand leaning into the wind as before. The tracks in the gravel would become less certain under direct attention, as they always had. Yet the air would not be empty. It would be courteous.

The power would make itself small enough to be borne.

It would learn the marks of habitation before acting. It would understand that the worn hollows were not signs of worship but of use, of bodies folding themselves against the ground to conserve warmth and thought. It would understand that the cairns were not monuments but negotiations with wind. It would understand that the tracks were not confusion but protection: a way of remaining ungraspable in a place where anything seen too clearly might be seized.

It would not try to turn the tracks into roads.
