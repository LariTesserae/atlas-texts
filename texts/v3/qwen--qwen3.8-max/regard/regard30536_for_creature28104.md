If such a place and such a being drew the attention of a benevolent and wise power, the first thing they would do would be to stop.

Not stop as if confused. Not stop as if frightened. Stop as one stops before something that must not be approached with hands.

They would not come down in fire. They would not arrive as a storm, or a brightening of the air, or a sudden warmth in the thin sky above the dry mountain. They would not send rain, because rain would be war. They would not send wind, because wind would be trespass. They would not send a messenger, because a messenger would imply that the chamber had something to receive, and the chamber had already received itself completely.

The power would stand, if standing can be said of something without feet, outside the seam, and they would listen.

They would listen to the unbroken industrial chord: the deep drone of the engines, the tight electric buzz of the transformer boxes, the fine singing of taut cables, the granular whisper passing through packed mineral filaments. They would listen not for meaning, not for a message, not for a prayer hidden inside the noise, but for distress. They would listen for the sound of something bound. For the sound of something suffering. For the sound of something waiting.

They would find none.

They would look into the pocket, through the narrow fissure whose lips are worn smooth by old pressures, and they would see the saturation: the copper-green mosses, the black floss, the olive-gray tufts, the laminated salt fronds, the mica leaves, the ropes of braided fiber, the crystal bulbs glowing faintly jade, the machines humming in their permanent function without work. They would see the cables becoming stems, the stems becoming conductors, the stone becoming bone, the light becoming memory. They would see that there was no empty interval into which a gift could be placed without violence.

Then the power would ask the first question benevolence asks:

*What is needed here?*

And the chamber, without answering, would answer:

*Nothing.*

Not because it could not speak, but because speech would be another sound added to the chord, and the chord was already complete. Not because it refused the question, but because the question had no purchase there. Need had no foothold. Lack had no crevice. Desire had no unoccupied surface on which to stand.

The power would look for thirst and find only conduction.  
They would look for hunger and find only circuit.  
They would look for loneliness and find no hollow in which loneliness could live.  
They would look for ruin and find only old weathering that had finished its work.  
They would look for imprisonment and find a being that was not trapped but fulfilled.

If the power were merely kind, they might weep.

But if they wept, a tear would be a catastrophe.
