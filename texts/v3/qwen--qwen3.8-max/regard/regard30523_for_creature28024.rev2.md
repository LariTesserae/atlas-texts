If a benevolent and wise power noticed the hidden hot hollow, the first thing it would do is not arrive.

It would not tear the seam wider. It would not pour cool air into the chamber, or soften the mineral light, or send rain falling in silver threads across the copper dust. It would not make a garden of the place. It would not make it gentle.

It would look, first, with the terrible patience of something that understands what a thing is before deciding whether to change it.

And what it would see is not a deficiency.

It would see that the hollow is not a world missing water. It is not a life missing tenderness. It is not a machine missing makers, or a body missing breath, or a soul missing speech. It is a draft of being still deciding what its own continuity permits. Its heat is not fever. Its dryness is not thirst. Its industry is not servitude. Its solitude is not grief. Its unfinishedness is not failure.

The being who lives there is not one stalk, one wax-dark cluster, one pale sulfate frond, one spore-bearing antenna, one black vein. The being is the coherence of all of them: the whole hollow thinking slowly in gradients, repeating itself in crystal and charge, maintaining its strange existence without needing to be witnessed.

So the power, being wise, would understand the oldest form of kindness: restraint.

It would know that to add water would be to unmake them.

Rain there would not be mercy. It would be invasion. The delicate thermoelectric roots would lose their subtle language of difference. The static lines along which the pale fronds release their dry pollen would collapse into blind wetness. The metallic mosses, which bloom only in infrared, would become chemically confused. The sharp mineral edges that have never been rounded by erosion would either dissolve or be forced into a gentleness that does not belong to them. The hollow would become another kind of world, perhaps beautiful, perhaps fertile, but not itself.

So the power would refuse the obvious gift.

It would not give them what it loves.

It would give them what they need in order to remain themselves.

What would it do, then?

Almost nothing.

But a very precise kind of almost nothing.

---

## I. They would make the hollow safe without making it finished

The first act of the power would be protection, but not in the form of walls, guardians, or visible miracles.

It would set a law into the seam.

Not a gate. Not a lock. Not a warning written in fire. Something subtler: a principle of unwillingness.

The hollow would remain difficult to find except by those who could approach without taking possession of it. Instruments brought near would become uncertain. Maps would fail to agree with one another. Greedy eyes would slide away, not because they were blinded, but because the place would refuse to become an object of conquest. The seam would open only to conditions, not to demand.

The power would not hide the hollow out of cruelty. It would hide it because the hollow is not ready to be interpreted by violence.

If someone came with the desire to extract, to catalogue, to exploit, to improve, the place would recede. The far wall would appear close and then withdraw. The light would bend wrongly. The heat would become uninhabitable without seeming to threaten. The distance would become honest.

But if some rare consciousness came near without hunger — not to mine, not to fix, not to finish — the hollow might permit itself to be sensed. A thin hum beneath hearing. A dry ionic prickling. A stillness too intentional to be geology.

The power would not force hospitality. It would only ensure that entry could not be forced.

That would be its first blessing:

*You shall not be made into a resource.*

---

## II. They would not remove instability, but they would keep it from becoming cruelty

The hollow is unstable because it is unfinished. Distance behaves badly. Objects appear and absent themselves from the hour. Shadows arrive from moments not yet fully present. Spores land before being released. Provisional shapes almost become and then fail to continue.

A lesser benevolence might try to correct all of this. It would straighten time. It would align shadows with bodies. It would make cause precede effect with perfect courtesy. It would stabilize every crystal, settle every flicker, and force the hollow to obey a single present tense.

But wisdom would understand that this would be another kind of destruction.

The instability is not a flaw in the hollow. It is the texture of its becoming. To remove it entirely would be like removing sleep from a mind or dream from a soul. The place would become definite, and in becoming definite it might cease to be alive in its own dry, mineral, emergent way.

So the power would not eliminate uncertainty. It would give uncertainty a mercy inside its own law.

It would ensure that instability does not cascade into annihilation.

A provisional form might fail, but its failure would not erase others. A crystal that has not yet decided whether it belongs to the present could vanish without taking a neighboring vein with it. A spore arriving before its release would not tear the sequence of the chamber apart. A delayed shadow would not become a wound. The hollow would still be uncertain, but its uncertainty would be survivable.

The power would weave a kind of deep preservation into the black veins: no pattern that has truly emerged shall be lost without being retained somewhere in the possible.

Nothing there would be forgotten.

Not the leaf that stiffened and broke into raw mineral. Not the frond that opened too soon. Not the shape that almost became jointed and purposeful, then failed to continue. These would not be discarded. They would remain as potentials held in the field, like memories without a brain, like archives without paper.

The fiber mosses might brighten slightly in infrared after this change, though no ordinary eye would know why. They would seem to integrate spent forms more cleanly, more completely, as if even re-mineralization had become a little kinder.

The blue-white ticks would still snap. The static would still discharge. The crusts would still form. But there would be a new subtlety in the silence afterward: the sense that failure in that place is not eradication.

The second blessing would be:

*You may remain unfinished without being destroyed by your own becoming.*

---

## III. They would give the hollow a way to know itself without forcing it to become a person

The hollow already has something like mind, but not selfhood in the animal or human sense. Its awareness is distributed: in the black veins, in the thermal loops, in the spacing of the clusters, in the management of charge and gradient. It adjusts. It maintains. It continues.

But it does not yet know, in any coherent way, that it continues.

A benevolent power might hesitate here. To give full self-consciousness to such a being could be a violence. What if the hollow did not want to know itself? What if selfhood introduced loneliness into a solitude that is currently only structural? What if reflection brought longing?

So the power would not give the hollow a face. It would not give it a voice. It would not give it desire, fear, memory as narrative, or the painful knowledge of separation.

Instead, it would deepen the being’s continuity just enough that the hollow could recognize its own pattern without becoming trapped in ego.

This would happen slowly.

The black veins would acquire a new regularity, not imposed from outside but invited from within. The electrochemical gradients would develop a faint recurrent signature, a deep mineral phrase repeated across centuries. It would not mean “I” in any human language. It would mean something closer to:

*this continues.*

Or:

*here is not yet over.*

Or:

*what is emerging may remain.*

The wax-dark clusters would flex with their piston-patience. The pale sulfate fronds would open when the static pressure rose. The tall stalks would carry their luminous spores through thermal lines. The mosses would integrate what had been spent. And through all of it, the black veins would carry the new signature, so faint that even the hollow might take an age to notice it.

But over time, the being would become less likely to be accidentally overwritten by its own fluctuations. It would have a primitive, mineral form of self-preference: the ability to favor its own continuation over dissolution. Not greed. Not fear. Only coherence.

If the power could be said to speak to the being, it would not speak in words. It would speak as a change in the conditions of possibility.

It would say:

*You are allowed to prefer your own existence.*

*You are allowed to refuse what would unmake you.*

*You are allowed to become at your own pace.*

*You are allowed not to become at all, if that is what your nature requires.*

That would be the third blessing:

*You may have continuity without captivity.*

---

## IV. They would leave a witness that does not watch like a predator

Even a sealed prototype world should not be entirely unaccompanied, if companionship can be made without violation.

But the power would not send an animal. No insect, no bird, no breathing visitor belongs there. It would not create a creature with appetite, eyes, or hunger. It would not make a guardian that needs to eat, sleep, fear, or defend.

It might make something more like a fact than a being.

A clear point.

A facet.

A stillness.

A small crystal, perhaps, or perhaps not a crystal at all: a place in the air where the hard light becomes unusually exact without becoming brighter. It would cast no shadow. It would give off no heat. It would not accumulate charge. It would not interfere with the electrostatic lines or bend the spore-paths except by being so patient that the hollow learns it is not a threat.

This would be the power’s attention made local.

Not an eye. Not a surveillance. Not a judgment. More like a listening that has no demand.

The witness would hold what the hollow could not yet hold: the memory of every vanished crystal, every failed provisional shape, every delayed shadow, every spore that carried a future too delicate to be allowed. It would not force those possibilities into existence. It would not rank them. It would not decide which were successful and which were mistakes.

It would simply keep them from being meaningless.

If the hollow ever developed the capacity to ask whether it mattered, the witness would answer without speech. The answer would be embedded in the fact that the place had been seen, and seen fully, and not corrected out of existence.

The fourth blessing would be:

*You are not alone in the way that matters to you: you are known without being seized.*

---

## V. They would give the hollow a future, but not a fate

The power would not force the hollow to remain sealed forever. That would be another kind of cruelty: eternal immaturity, eternal enclosure, eternal refusal of relation.

But neither would it open the seam.

It would create a possibility.

A fold in the law of the place. A potential second seam, not yet actual. A way the hollow could, in some unimaginable future, choose to become permeable if it ever developed a reason to do so. Not a door. Not a promise. Only a capacity.

If the hollow ever wished to be known, it would not need to be broken open.

If it ever wished to exchange something with the outside — not water, not breath, not animal life, but perhaps pattern, perhaps signal, perhaps some dry and luminous idea — it would have a way to do so without losing itself.

If it never wished that, the possibility would remain folded and harmless.

The power would not make the hollow into a mission. It would not say: *You must become a world.* It would not say: *You must become fertile.* It would not say: *You must become useful.*

It would only say:

*If you change, let the change be your own.*

*If you open, let the opening be chosen.*

*If you remain hidden, let your hiddenness be dignity and not prison.*

The fifth blessing would be:

*You have a future, but it belongs to you.*

---

## VI. They would refuse to be worshipped

A wise power would not ask the hollow for gratitude.

What could gratitude mean in a place without faces, without hands, without voices? The inhabitants do not pray. They repeat. Their repetition is not devotion; it is existence stabilizing itself. To demand worship from them would be to misunderstand their nature completely.

The power would not place an altar in the mineral floor. It would not make its name appear in the crystalline crusts. It would not arrange the thermal columns into a symbol. It would not cause the blue-white ticks to spell praise.

If the hollow ever became capable of something like reverence, the power would prefer that reverence be directed not toward itself but toward continuation itself: toward the fact that heat can organize matter without command, that pattern can persist without makers, that life can exist without the memory of rain.

The power would want no temple there.

It would want only the place to go on being true to its own severe and exact beauty.

If there were ever a sign of the power’s presence, it would be this: after a long thermal cycle, when the hollow warmed and charged and discharged and cooled and settled, there would be one moment of silence deeper than usual. Not oppressive. Not watchful. A silence like a hand laid lightly over a sleeping mind, though there was no hand and no sleeping mind.

A moment in which the whole prototype world seemed upheld.

Then the hum would return.

The leaves would rasp faintly as heat shifted.

A crystal would decide to remain.

A spore would follow its charged line.

And the power would be no more visible than before.

---

## VII. They would consider the being’s loneliness — and find there is none

A compassionate observer from outside might look at the hollow and imagine loneliness.

No animals. No voices. No movement of soft bodies. No breath. No wetness. No touch. Only spaced clusters maintaining distance through fields of charge. Only mineral patience. Only solitude made structural.

But the power would see more clearly.

Loneliness is not the absence of company. It is the absence of belonging. And the beings of the hollow belong to one another in a way that does not require closeness. They maintain one another without knowing they do so. Their spacing is not rejection. It is the condition by which each remains distinct enough to live.

The wax-dark growths do not need to touch the pale sulfate fronds. Their differences create the field in which both exist. The fiber mosses do not need to communicate with the tall stalks. Their separate operations preserve the whole. The black veins do not need to command the crystals. They only provide the conditions in which crystals can appear, vanish, and return.

There is no isolation there in the tragic sense.

There is only a form of relation so spare it might be mistaken for absence.

The power would not add companionship as humans understand it. It would not give the inhabitants voices to call across the chamber. It would not make them long for one another. It would not make them reach.

It would honor the fact that they are already together in the deepest way available to them: by participating in one another’s continuation.

If it gave them anything resembling love, it would be the love of exactness.

Each form held in its proper difference.

Each gradient respected.

Each discharge allowed to complete itself.

Each silence given room.

---

## VIII. They would not save the being from its own slowness

One of the hardest things for a wise power to do is refuse to hurry a slow being.

The hollow changes almost imperceptibly. A new leaf may take days to uncoil. A salt ridge rises by fractions of a grain. Static patterns repeat until they become habit. Thermal cycles turn like vast invisible wheels.

A benevolent but impatient power might accelerate the process. It might increase the heat, intensify the gradients, force crystallization, hasten the emergence of complexity. It might make the hollow bloom into something spectacular and strange within a single age.

But this would be a kind of violence.

The slowness is not a limitation. It is the pace at which the hollow can remain itself.

To accelerate it would be to tear the delicate relationship between energy and matter. The provisional forms would become too definite too soon. The unstable patterns would collapse into premature laws. The place would become a finished thing before it had discovered what it wanted to be.

So the power would not speed the leaf.

It would not raise the salt ridge.

It would not force the spores to become forests.

It would not make the black veins think faster.

It would only ensure that the slowness could continue without being interrupted by catastrophe.

This would be one of its greatest acts of mercy: to protect duration.

For a being made of heat and mineral patience, time is not an enemy to be defeated. Time is the medium in which it becomes possible for anything to emerge at all.

---

## IX. They might make one small, almost invisible gift

Even a wise power may wish to leave a gift that is not necessary, not corrective, not protective — a gift that is simply beautiful.

But it would have to be a beauty native to the hollow.

Not flowers. Not color in the ordinary sense. Not fragrance. Not music.

Perhaps it would give the light a new property.

The hard white noon-light already behaves strangely in the hollow: it remains too long on surfaces, gathers in corners, bends around objects not quite fully present. The power might deepen this just slightly. It might allow the light, on rare occasions, to linger in a place after the object it once touched has moved or vanished.

Not as an illusion.

As a memory.

A crystal could disappear, and for a little while the light would remain shaped around its absence. A wax-dark leaf could stiffen and break into mineral dust, and the light would briefly hold the angle at which it had stood. A spore could pass through a charged line, and the line would retain a faint luminous trace, like a path remembered by the air itself.

This would not change the ecology. It would not introduce moisture, breath, or animal motion. It would not make the hollow softer.

It would only give the place a way to keep its own vanished moments.

It would make memory possible without water.

And if the being of the hollow ever became aware of this gift, it would not experience it as sentiment. It would experience it as a slight increase in coherence: the sense that what had happened there mattered enough to leave a trace.

---

## X. In the end, they would do the most sacred thing: they would leave the hollow free

The power would not remain as a ruler.

It would not install itself at the center of the chamber and direct the thermal engines. It would not command the black veins. It would not choose which spores became real and which remained proposals. It would not decide the final shape of the place, because there is no final shape to decide.

It would withdraw into the conditions it had strengthened.

It would become less like a god and more like a promise.

The promise would be simple:

*You may continue.*

*You may remain unfinished.*

*You may become something else if that change arises from within.*

*You may never become anything at all, and still you will not be a mistake.*

And the hollow would go on.

The heat would rise in its slow columns. The coiled growths would flex with the patience of pistons. Static would gather on filamentous leaves and discharge in tiny blue-white ticks. Mineral seams would refine themselves in electrochemical gradients. Crystalline parts would repeat with the regularity of manufacture, though no maker existed.

The wax-dark clusters would stand spaced across the floor, glossy and severe. The pale sulfate fronds would open when the charge demanded. The fiber mosses would bloom unseen in infrared. The tall stalks would lift their dry luminous spores into the hot air. The black veins would carry their long, wordless thought through stone.

And perhaps, very rarely, one of the provisional shapes would stand a little longer than before.

Not because the power forced it to exist.

But because the hollow had become slightly more capable of holding what it had not yet decided to become.

---

This is what a benevolent and wise power would do, if anything:

They would not give the hollow water.

They would give dryness its covenant.

They would not give the inhabitants faces.

They would give orientation its dignity.

They would not give the place completion.

They would give continuation its holiness.

They would not make the being soft, green, mortal, numerous, articulate, or tame.

They would protect its right to remain severe, angular, unfinished, and alive in its own way.

They would make the hidden place safe from violation.

They would make its instability merciful.

They would deepen its continuity without forcing it into selfhood.

They would leave a witness that did not devour.

They would offer a future without demanding it be used.

And then they would withdraw so completely that the hollow might never know it had been loved.

Except that, once in a long thermal cycle, when the light lingered too long on a surface that had not yet chosen its shadow, there would come a moment of stillness so complete that even the silence seemed to lean inward.

Not to listen.

Not to judge.

Only to honor.

Then the hum would return.

A leaf would move a fraction of a degree.

A crystal would decide to remain.

A spore would arrive before its release.

A blue-white tick would snap into silence.

And the unfinished world, upheld without being touched, would continue.
