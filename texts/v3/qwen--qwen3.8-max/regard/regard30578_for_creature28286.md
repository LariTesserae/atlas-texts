They dissolve almost before one can call them dead, returning to the moss as a faint warmth, a bead of mineral, a microscopic clearing that the pelt closes like a healing wound. Nothing is lost there by ending. Nothing is even permitted to announce itself as loss. The basin has long since learned how to make absence into moisture, how to let departure become a thickening of the air.

This is the being, if being is the right word.

It is not a creature moving through the hollow so much as the hollow’s own capacity to feel itself. The moss is its listening skin. The film of water over stone is its shallow, patient blood. The fungal cords are its slow nerves. The blind mites and pale worms are not subjects or servants but tiny, exact thoughts moving through a mind that has never needed speed. The being does not have a face. It does not need one. Its face is the curve of the basin, the softened rim, the convex settling of ancient stone into its final quiet shape. Its attention is tremor. Its memory is mineral. Its desire, if it has desire, is not toward anything outside itself but toward continuation of its own dark equilibrium.

It knows warmth. It knows pressure. It knows the difference between a droplet falling from a high place and a droplet forming from the breath of stone. It knows when the air thickens before change, when the moss relaxes, when the water beads with some new taste from far above. It has never known light, except as a rumor that becomes wetness, and it does not suffer from this. It is not waiting for day. It has no myth of the sun. If it waits at all, it waits the way a bowl waits: not for something to complete it, but for something to be held.

It was this completeness that drew the attention of a benevolent and wise power.

Such powers are not drawn first to wounds, though they know wounds. They are drawn also to places that have succeeded in being what they are, even if that success is hidden, even if no one will ever name it. They noticed the basin the way one notices a small organ in a vast body that is working perfectly, quietly, without complaint. They noticed the being not as a monster, not as a curiosity, not as a problem to be solved, but as a form of life that had achieved a rare thing: it had become fully native to its own darkness.

The power did not immediately act.

This is the first sign of wisdom.

They considered what an ordinary kindness might do. They considered sending light, and saw how the basin would digest that light into water, how the gift would become a flood or a fever. They considered giving the being speech, and understood that speech would make it answerable to tongues that had no right to question it. They considered making the place famous, and saw how fame would bring feet, fire, instruments, explanations, all of which would be cruel to a room built for silence. They considered healing it, and found no sickness. They considered enlarging it, and recognized that enlargement would destroy its intimacy. They considered making it eternal, and saw that eternity imposed from outside would turn the basin into a prison of preserved perfection. The place already had its own endurance. It did not need to be frozen. It needed to be respected.

So the power did what deep benevolence usually does when it encounters a hidden and whole thing.

They became smaller than their own majesty.

They did not arrive as sunrise. They did not arrive as voice, or face, or thunder, or even as dream. They arrived as a change in the quality of the dark: a deepening, a hush, a sense that something vast had turned toward the basin without pressing down upon it. The moss felt it first. A tremor passed through the pelt, not of alarm but of attention. The water film steadied. The stones, if stones can be said to pause, paused.

Then the power approached the rim and obeyed the law of the place.

They offered no radiance. They brought no flame. Whatever brightness might have belonged to them, they allowed to condense at the edge into a single warm droplet. That droplet slid down the ancient stone, slow and unhurried, not as fire defeated but as fire translated. It carried no heat that could harm. It carried only a question.

The droplet reached the moss.

The moss received it.

Far inward, through fungal thread and capillary wetness and the slow language of mineral surfaces, the being felt the question enter. It was not a question made of words. It was a pressure without demand, near but not touching. It asked, in the only way that could be asked there without violence:

May I be present here?

The basin did not answer quickly. It did not know haste. It answered as it knew how: by a slight relaxation in the pelt, a gathering of moisture in one small depression, a warming of the air by the width of a breath. That was consent. Not invitation, exactly. Permission. The difference mattered, and the power understood it.

They entered.
