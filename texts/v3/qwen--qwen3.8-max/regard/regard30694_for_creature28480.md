## The Attention That Did Not Make a Face

When the benevolent and wise power turned toward the High Green Scaffolds, it did not arrive as a figure.

It did not descend by a stair of light. It did not open the clouds with a voice. It did not make the ordinary sun strange, or add a color to the air, or set a shining upon the towers. If anything, the light became more ordinary. The shadows became more exact. The heat became more itself. The water threads falling from the platform edges continued falling. The wind continued passing through the hollow stems. The place continued being loud, wet, green, and raw.

The power came first as attention.

Not attention as a human understands it — not a gaze, not a scrutiny, not a search for fault or beauty. It was attention as a vast and patient turning-toward. It was the difference between a thing existing unnoticed and a thing being held, completely, in a kindness that had no need to own it.

For a while, the power did nothing but see.

It saw the platforms hanging above the cloud-floor. It saw the pale roots beneath them, combing the mist. It saw the leaf-pavements, the basins, the ramps, the corridors that led nowhere, the balconies open to steam. It saw the bridges of living fiber crossing empty air. It saw the membranes lifting in the heat, the towers venting, the pools swelling and shrinking with the weather of the place. It saw broken pieces falling and becoming beginnings. It saw water moving through everything. It saw heat lifting everything. It saw the whole system breathing without lungs, repairing without hands, continuing without hope.

Then the power looked for the being who lived there.

At first it looked in the old ways.

It looked for a center. It looked for a hidden animal, a sleeping mind, a ruling organism, a heart, a brain, a nest, a throne, a seed-person curled in the deepest chamber. It looked for eyes. It looked for a mouth. It looked for fear. It looked for desire. It looked for loneliness. It looked for some creature that could turn toward benevolence and answer it.

It found none of these things.

There was no one hidden in the towers.  
There was no one resting in the pools.  
There was no one walking the corridors.  
There was no one waiting on the balconies.  
There was no one behind the steam.

And yet the place was not empty.

The power saw that quickly. Emptiness was the wrong word. The High Green Scaffolds were crowded with process. Every surface was active. Every gap was crossed by water, wind, light, or reaching root. Every rupture was already becoming a scaffold. Every fallen thing was still part of the living whole. The place was not a house with no tenant. The place was a tenant shaped as a house.

So the power let the word *who* widen.

If there was a being there, it was not a being bounded by skin. It was not a being gathered into a single face. It was not a being that could be met eye to eye. It was a being made of many actions: swelling, sealing, weaving, venting, reaching, tearing, repairing, floating, drinking, exhaling. It was a being whose body was architecture and whose thought, if thought it had, was tropism.

The power did not force the place into a shape it could understand more easily.

That was the first wise thing it did.

It did not make the corridors feet.  
It did not make the pools eyes.  
It did not make the towers mouths.  
It did not make the roots hands.  
It did not make the membranes wishes.  
It did not make the steam a breath that longed.  
It did not make the bridges arms that missed someone.  
It did not make the storms anger.  
It did not make the silence loneliness.

A lesser kindness might have done these things. A lesser kindness would have looked at the place and said: *You need a face. You need a voice. You need a soul like mine. You need to be recognized.* A lesser kindness would have added animals to soften the strangeness. It would have sent birds into the towers, fish into the pools, insects into the leaf-corridors, eyes into the shadows. It would have made the place easier to love in the ordinary way.

But the power was wise, and wisdom knows that not every completion requires addition.

The High Green Scaffolds were not incomplete because they had no animal life. They were complete in a different order of being. Their completeness did not require witness with eyes. It did not require speech. It did not require consciousness to justify itself. It already had its own dignity: the dignity of growth answering pressure, of water answering shape, of form answering force.

So the power did not give the place a mind.

That was the second wise thing it did.

It understood that to give the High Green Scaffolds a mind would not be a gift. It would be a wound.

A mind would bring separation. The platforms would become aware of themselves as separate from the mist, separate from the roots, separate from the gaps. The towers would know they were tall and fear falling. The pools would know they were temporary and fear drying. The bridges would know tension and dread snapping. The membranes would know the storm as enemy. The broken pieces would know themselves as broken.

The place did not suffer, because suffering requires a self that can be hurt in the animal sense. Here there were only changes. A tower split. A bridge tore. A membrane burst. A platform calved. These were not tragedies inside the logic of the place. They were transformations. The power saw that to introduce grief would be violence disguised as mercy.
