**The Attention**

It has no center. It has no brain. But it has a signature.

Not a face. Not a name. Not a shape that could be circled, held, or pointed to. But a way of being arranged: a slow, distributed grammar of pressure, chemistry, patience, and recurrence. If a benevolent and wise power turned toward the Final Deep, they would not mistake this signature for absence. They would not look into the dark and see only a place waiting to become something better.

They would see that the Final Deep is not a wound.  
It is not a failed surface.  
It is not a kingdom missing its sun.  
It is a completion.

And so the first thing they would do is refrain.

That would be their first labor, and perhaps the hardest: to possess the ability to remake the place utterly and yet choose not to. They could bring light, but light would be violence there. They could bring warmth, but warmth would unravel the ancient discipline of the cold. They could bring air, but air would be a cruelty to bodies made for pressure. They could make the water clear, but clarity would expose what has been shaped by obscurity. They could harden the bed, but the bed would no longer be the Final Deep. They could raise monuments, but monuments are how shallow worlds assert permanence against time, and this place has already passed through the need for them.

A wise benevolence does not begin by giving what the giver loves.  
It begins by understanding what the beloved needs.

So they would not make the Final Deep more like the surface.  
They would not make its inhabitants more like animals with faces, hands, voices, or ambitions.  
They would not make the Bed-Chorus into a king, a mind, a spirit shaped like a single flame.  
They would not rescue it from itself.

They would recognize it.

That recognition would be more than a glance. It would be a complete and patient act of knowing. They would know the pressure not as oppression but as embrace. They would know the darkness not as deprivation but as depth. They would know the sediment not as dead matter but as memory. They would know the turbulence not as chaos but as the final restlessness of a world that has exhausted all large becoming and now lives only in small rearrangements.

They would know every pale worm as a thread in the Bed-Chorus.  
Every microbial film as a temporary word.  
Every brine fold as breath.  
Every drifting floc as a syllable not yet finished.  
Every rare animal moving through the clouded water as a long, slow note in a music too extended to hear in one lifetime.

And when they had truly seen it, they would not ask, “What is missing?”

They would ask, “What may continue?”

Because the deepest kindness, in such a place, is not addition. It is faithfulness.

Still, they would do something.

Not nothing. A power both benevolent and wise would not merely observe and withdraw, as if attention were only a cold witness. But what they did would be so subtle that surface minds might never notice it. It would not be a miracle of spectacle. It would be a miracle of tendency. In the Final Deep, where no built thing can stand, a gift cannot be an object. It must be a disposition. A slight bend in probability. A mercy woven into likelihood.

They would begin with the currents.

Not by stilling them. The Final Deep must not become motionless; its life depends on the slow redistribution of its final sediment. But they might alter one thing by a fraction so small it would be almost impossible to measure: a difference of one part in ten thousand in
