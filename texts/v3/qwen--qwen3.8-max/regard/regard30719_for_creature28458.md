If a benevolent and wise power noticed that place, they would be very careful.

They would not come as a storm. They would not open the mountain, widen the fissure, cool the chamber with a merciful wind, or send green life down into the wet dark to make it intelligible. They would not speak a name into the vapor and expect the creatures to turn. They would understand, almost immediately, that this is a kind of existence that can be destroyed by too much kindness.

The first thing they would do is refrain.

Not out of indifference. Out of precision.

They would see that the chamber is not a room needing furniture, nor a world needing correction, nor a suffering creature needing rescue in the ordinary sense. It is a compressed beginning. It is life still close to the conditions that produced it: heat, water, mineral exhalation, accident. It has not yet separated itself into clear individuals, clear territories, clear histories. It has not yet learned to keep anything. It is full of use, but empty of artifacts. It is crowded, but not social in a way that could be improved by law. It is alive, but not yet convinced that life and stone are different.

A wise power would recognize that the obvious gifts are dangerous.

To widen the fissure would be to let the outside mountain enter too quickly: cold, dryness, dust, wind, time. The chamber’s wetness would thin. Its heat would lose its intimacy. The small fauna would be exposed to rhythms they have not yet become. The place might become easier to see, but it would cease to be itself.

To cool the chamber would be to take away the engine of its being. The creatures there are not suffering from heat as a human would suffer; they are arranged around heat. Their bodies, hatching, hunting, resting, molting, and stillness are all responses to thermal gradients. Remove the heat, and you do not free them. You dissolve the pattern that allows them to exist.
