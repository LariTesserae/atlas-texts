It suggests that the chamber is not a malfunction corrected, but a severity carried so far that it became habitable. The correction is not against the room; it is the room. The space has been made exact enough that life, if life appears, must not be soft. It must be dry, angular, patient, and acoustic. It must be able to live inside pressure. It must be able to make a body out of absence.

If such a place and such a being drew the attention of a benevolent and wise power, the first thing that power would do is not act.

It would refrain.

Not from indifference. Not from the coldness of high wisdom looking down on a small thing. Its refusal would be precise, because benevolence without precision can be a kind of violence. The power would understand immediately that the obvious kindnesses would be atrocities here.

It would not bring water.

Rain here would be a catastrophe disguised as mercy. Water would make the cold cling. It would teach the metal to bleed. It would introduce rust, swelling, softening, mineral streaking, microbial film, decay, the whole slow chain of damp grief that the chamber was built to exclude. Water would make the floor treacherous, the ceramic slick, the fibers brittle in a new and wrong way. It would turn a clean mineral cold into a wet one, and a wet cold in this place would not be gentler. It would be contamination.

The power would not bring warmth.

Warmth would alter every tolerance. It would make the hidden engines matter in a different way. It would create expansion, stress, drift, fatigue. It would make the air less sharp, less capable of carrying the roar in the exact manner the chamber requires. It might comfort a human body, but human comfort is not what is at stake. The being there does not need a hearth. It needs its cold as a structure, as a condition, as part of its own boundary. To warm it
