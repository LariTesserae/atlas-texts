They would not come as rain.

That is where wisdom would begin.

A lesser benevolence, seeing so much dryness, might immediately imagine water. It would dream of clouds, rivers, dew, mud, green shoots pushing through powdered porcelain. It would want to soften the light, thicken the air, fill the silence with leaves and voices, and call that salvation. But a wise power would understand at once that this lowland is not a failed wet world. It is a finished dry one. Its absence of water is not a wound to be healed but a condition to be respected. To bring rain here would be to dissolve a civilization of patience. It would be conquest wearing mercy.

So they would do nothing that could be mistaken for violence.

Their first act would be almost invisible.

They would lower their own brightness.

Not the brightness of the plain — the plain had its own hard, clean radiance, its white sky and reflective ground — but the brightness of their attention. A wise and benevolent power does not stare at a fragile world until it trembles. It does not overwhelm what it loves. So they would become quiet at the edge of the place. They would listen with the kind of patience the landscape already possessed. They would let the lowland continue being itself long enough to be understood.

And in that understanding, they would see what lived there.

Not only the motes, not only the foil lichens and wire-shrubs, not only the mirror beetles and filament-worms and seam-mites, but one being in particular — not because the others were less real, but because attention, when it is kind, often rests where it can be intimate.

The being was like a sheet of animated glass.

Flat, translucent, slow, it slid across the polished depressions where charged motes gathered thickest. It had no face in any familiar sense. It had no voice. It moved without sound, feeding on dense patches of airborne particles, filtering them through comb-like structures along its underside. Its passage left a faint trace, a temporary disturbance in the dust, which the plain’s electrostatic smoothing erased almost immediately.

It was not lonely in the way soft, wet creatures are lonely. It had never known company as a need. But it was solitary in a way that even silence could notice. It moved through a world full of presences and touched none of them fully. It left marks that vanished before they could be remembered.
