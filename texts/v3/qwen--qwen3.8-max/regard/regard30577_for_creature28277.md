If they did anything at all, it would be almost nothing.

That is the first thing to understand. A lesser goodness might have arrived as rescue: a breaking of the dark, a hushing of the engines, a descent of warm light, a sudden green flourishing where only rust and damp had been. A lesser goodness might have looked at that low, made place and said, *This is wrong because it is severe. This is dead because it is quiet. This is suffering because it is hard.* Then it would have tried to remake the place into something more consoling, and in doing so it would have destroyed what had taken ages to become itself.

But a benevolent and wise power would not begin by changing the world. It would begin by perceiving it.

It would not come from above, because there is no true above there. It would not enter as a figure, because the place refuses faces. It would not announce itself with radiance, because radiance there would be violence: a burning of films, a scattering of spores, a shock to the slow tissues that have learned to live by tolerances. If such a power came at all, it would come as a change too subtle to name. A valve would close one heartbeat later than usual. A current would hesitate in a corroded conduit. A note in the deep turbine chord would lose a fraction of its edge. A drip would fall through a forgotten ceramic layer and arrive less bitter. A pocket of saturated sound would, for the first time in a long age, become not merely false silence but true rest.

That would be the manner of its arrival.

Not a descent.  
A lessening.

---

The power would notice the place because the place had become complete in its own strange way.

It was not ruined. It was not waiting to be restored. It had passed beyond intention into a kind of terrible, patient geography. The machines were no longer tools. They were weather. The floor was no longer architecture. It was ground. The darkness was no longer absence. It was substance. The sound was no longer noise. It was climate. And life, sparse and surface-bound as it was, had not conquered the place. It had learned to be allowed by it.

That would draw attention.

Not because it was beautiful in the ordinary sense. Not because it called out. Not because it was pitiful. But because it was improbable. Because even there, where everything had been made and worn and flattened and saturated with sound, something still persisted. Not triumphantly. Not abundantly. Not with flower or song or motion. But as film. As crust. As thread. As dark green patience.
