**What a Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do at the High Cisterns of the First Build**

If the High Cisterns of the First Build drew the attention of a benevolent and wise power, the event would not begin with spectacle. No sky would open. No face would form in the mist. No voice would roll across the basin like thunder demanding reverence. The ordinary sky would remain ordinary. The light would still fall clear and soft over the wet architecture. The hollow stems would still breathe their warm, slow breath. The droplets would still gather on leaf teeth and tremble at the edge of falling.

The first sign would be subtler: a change in the quality of attention.

The air would seem, for a moment, more attentive. The humidity would deepen by no measurable degree, yet everything would feel more held. The sound of the basin—the drip, the plink, the hum, the glug, the resinous creak—would become not louder but more present, as though some vast listening had leaned close without pressing down. The water beads on the membranes would hang a little more perfectly, not because gravity had been altered, but because they were being witnessed in their fullness.

A wise power would not arrive here as a king. It would not descend with a retinue of angels, or a train of celestial architects, or a book of commandments. It would not bring tools. Tools would be an offense in this place, because here everything is made by growth, not manufacture. It would not bring fire, because fire would be too quick and too final. It would not bring law in the sense of prohibition carved into stone, because there is no stone. It would not bring a throne, because the basin already has its only ruler: water without crown, moving through living channels.

If such a power turned toward the High Cisterns, it would come first as listening.

And the first thing it would do, perhaps, would be nothing.

This would not be indifference. It would be the hardest form of love.

A benevolent power that lacks wisdom often wants to help by adding. It sees something strange and wishes to clarify it. It sees something quiet and wishes to animate it. It sees something complete but unfamiliar and feels the itch to improve it, finish it, explain it, populate it, consecrate it, or make it useful. A wise power knows that the deepest kindness is sometimes to keep one’s hands still. It knows that not every beauty is a problem to be solved. Not every silence is a vacancy. Not every unmoving thing is waiting for motion. Not every unborn thing needs to be born into something else.

So the power would look upon the First Build and see that it is not suffering. It is not incomplete in the way that wounds are incomplete. It is not lonely in the way that isolated minds are lonely. It is not a failed world waiting for persons to justify it. It is not a temple awaiting a god. It is not a machine awaiting an operator. It is not a garden awaiting a gardener.

It is already held by its own becoming.

Therefore the first act of the wise power would be restraint.

They would not give the basin a face. They would not place eyes in the towers or a mouth in the cisterns. They would not carve a countenance into the weeping sap-boulders, because that would teach the mind to seek a person where there is instead a pattern. They would not give the First Build a separate soul, because that would divide it from itself. They would not wake it into self-consciousness as humans understand self-consciousness, because that might introduce the pain of separation. The place does not need to say “I” to be alive. Its life is distributed, hydraulic, resonant, vegetal. It lives as a chord lives. It lives as a watershed lives. It lives as a first hour lives.

The power would not improve the sound. They would not make the dripping more musical according to some foreign taste. They would not tune the hollow stems into instruments for concert. They would not compose a hymn for the basin, because the basin already has a hymn: the loud, patient equilibrium of itself. To add a song would be to imply that the existing sound is insufficient. A wise power would know better. The sound is not performance. It is maintenance. It is the place announcing that it continues.

So they would listen.

They would listen to the water striking broad leaves. They would listen to the root-eaves plinking into pools. They would listen to the long tubes filling and emptying with their deep glugging notes. They would listen to the membranes humming where vapor passes. They would listen to the creak of resinous joints under steady tension. They would listen to the faint hydraulic sigh of the floor answering pressure. They would hear all of it not as noise but as a single sustained body of resonance, a chord made of wetness, warmth, shape, and patience.

They would hear in it something like prayer, but without petition.

Not a prayer asking for anything. Not a prayer of praise directed upward toward a higher being. Just the fact of the basin, sounding itself out into the air. The loudness as proof of life. The loudness as balance. The loudness as the place’s way of holding itself steady.

After listening, the power might bless the basin. But they would not bless it by changing it.

A wise blessing here would not be a miracle that interrupts the pattern. It would be a confirmation.

They might say, if speech were necessary:

*Blessed be the living floor, which answers weight with moisture and does not ask for a face.*

*Blessed be the cisterns, which hold without hoarding and are filled without overflowing.*

*Blessed be the hollow stems, which drink height and make capacity out of emptiness.*

*Blessed be the weeping sap-forms, which give wetness from stillness and make patience visible.*

*Blessed be the mosses and root-laces, which catch what loosens and let nothing become waste.*

*Blessed be the translucent drifters in the clear pools, who move without urgency and belong to quiet.*

*Blessed be the pale snails, which cross wet spillways without wounding them.*

*Blessed be the wingless pollinators, which perform their maintenance without spectacle.*

*Blessed be the buds that remain swollen at the threshold of opening, because they are not failing to become; they are protecting the first hour.*

*Blessed be the water, which is mason, law, breath, and memory.*

*Blessed be the sound, which is not disturbance but equilibrium.*

*Blessed be the emptiness, which is not absence but constructed pause.*

*Blessed be the First Build, which does not need to be finished in order to be whole.*

Such a blessing would not add power to the basin. It would add permission. Not permission to exist, because the basin already has that. Permission to remain exactly what it is, even in a universe full of powers that might mistake it for a resource, a curiosity, a mistake, or a stage set waiting for actors.

Then the wise power would consider the question of protection.

This is where benevolence could become dangerous if it were clumsy. A lesser power might surround the High Cisterns with a wall of light. It might station guardians at the doorways. It might make the basin impossible to find. It might hide it behind storms, or fold it into another dimension, or seal it away as a sacred relic. But such actions would carry a kind of violence. To hide the place entirely would be to deny it its own openness. To wall it off would be to turn it into a treasure. To guard it visibly would be to impose fear upon its spaciousness. The First Build is not fragile in the way a jewel is fragile. It is fragile in the way a balance is fragile: not easily broken by force alone, but easily ruined by the wrong kind of attention.

So the power would not make a fortress. They would make a condition.

They would fold into the approaches of the basin a subtle law of incompatibility: that those who come to possess it become confused by their own greed. Not struck down. Not punished. Not made to suffer. Simply confused. Their certainties would soften. Their instruments would grow heavy. Their maps would become uncertain at the edges. Their names for the place would fail to stick. Their plans would leak like poorly sealed membranes. They would feel, without knowing why, that they are being measured by moisture. They would find the humidity intolerable—not because it harms them, but because it dissolves the dryness of their intention.
