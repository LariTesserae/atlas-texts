It is at such a moment—if a moment can be said to occur in a place so reluctant toward events—that the attention of a benevolent and wise power might turn toward the Basin of Unfallen Water.

Such a power would not come as fire, or voice, or sudden light. It would not arrive in a form that demands recognition. It has seen enough worlds to know that some truths are ruined by being announced. So it would come, if it came at all, as a deepening of attention: a pressure without weight, a listening without ears, a regard so careful that it does not even disturb the stars still visible in the pale thin air.

The power would look first at the basin as a place.

It would see the wide valley floor, the worn cliffs, the old scree angles, the grey schist and pale quartzite, the black veins running through bedrock like sealed wounds. It would see the channels that were once riverine and are now only damp in their lowest seams. It would see the tarns lying under their skin of cold light. It would see that nothing here is unfinished, and nothing is trying to finish. The Basin does not long for a higher state. It is not waiting for rain, or spring, or resurrection. It has already arrived at its final grammar.

Then the power would look for the being who lives there.

At first, a lesser wisdom might imagine many beings: the mineral film darkening stone by a shade; the low, broad grazers with shells of schist; the thread-dwellers moving through capillary seams like thoughts too subtle for a mind. But the wise power would perceive the deeper fact. These are not separate lives in the way lower lands understand separateness. They are organs, habits, motions, and pauses in one vast slow being. The being who lives in the Basin is the Basin itself: the stone made patient by moisture, the moisture made wise by stone, the cold made livable by restraint, the silence made into a form of metabolism.

It is a being that feeds on not much. It eats temperature, iron, silence, the faint electricity of stars. It drinks without thirst. It moves without haste. It has no eyes because it has no need to compare itself with anything else. It has no voice because it has nothing to demand. It has no memory of falling because nothing here has fallen. Even water has been spared the biography of descent.

The power would see this, and its benevolence would stir.

This is the dangerous moment.

Benevolence, even in a wise power, wants to give. It sees endurance and wishes to reward it. It sees austerity and wishes to soften it. It sees patience and wishes to place a gift inside that patience, something bright, something unexpected, something that says, *You have been faithful; now be glad.*

Such a power might be tempted to send rain.

Not storm-rain, not violent rain, but a gentle silver falling that would fill the dry channels, swell the tarns, wash the dust from the stone, and make the Basin shimmer with reflected sky. It might think: *This place has known water only as standing, beading, gathering, seeping. Let it know water as gift. Let it hear water arriving.*

But wisdom would stay that impulse.

Rain would bring memory. Rain would bring expectation. Rain would teach the Basin to look upward. It would create a history of descent, and with descent would come the idea of above and below, giver and receiver, drought and abundance. The thread-dwellers would no longer be the thoughts of water wishing to remain water; they would become the thoughts of water remembering when it fell. The film would grow faster, then thinner, then desperate. The grazers would move with urgency. The stillness would acquire anxiety. The Basin would become a place of events, and in becoming a place of events, it would cease to be the Basin of Unfallen Water.

So the power would not send rain.

It might be tempted to warm the air.

Only a little. Only enough to loosen the cold from the stone for a few hours each day. Only enough to let the bead-basins in the film soften their rims, to let the thread-dwellers widen their channels, to let the grazers move with something less than geological deliberation. Warmth could be a kindness. Warmth could say, *You do not need to endure so severely.*

But wisdom would see that warmth here would not be kindness. It would be acceleration. It would bring decay where there is only accretion. It would bring hunger where there is only maintenance. It would make the stone give up its old mineral breath and replace it with the busyness of transformation. The Basin does not suffer from cold. It is made by cold. The cold is not its enemy; it is its architecture.

So the power would not warm the Basin.

It might be tempted to give the grazers eyes.

This would seem an especially tender gift. Eyes would let them know one another. Eyes would let them see the dim silver-violet sky, the stars hard in the daytime, the far cliffs worn into their ancient profiles. Eyes would let them witness the place they inhabit rather than merely inhabiting it.

But wisdom would understand that eyes bring comparison, and comparison brings distance. The grazers do not need to see one another because they do not need to become more than they are. They do not need to see the sky because they are already under it entirely. Eyes would create a gap between the grazer and the stone, between the grazer and the film, between the grazer and its own slow life. They would introduce the possibility of fear: fear of distance, fear of difference, fear of death seen approaching instead of merely arriving.

So the power would not give eyes.

It might be tempted to give song.

The thread-dwellers already make a note so fine it is less heard than noticed. A benevolent power might wish to lift that note into music, to make the Basin resound gently with its own being, to let the water know itself as beautiful.

But wisdom would know that music, in such a place, would become a demand for repetition. The Basin would begin to listen for the song. It would prefer one note to another. It would develop longing. The silence would no longer be full; it would become the space where song is expected. The Basin does not need beauty announced. It is already beauty under restraint.

So the power would not give song.
