Their bodies would be understood before they were helped.

The being who lives there is not one creature in the way a human romance would prefer — not a lone walker, not a king of the plain, not a mind standing apart from the landscape and naming it. The being is plural, slow, and distributed: the herds. The armored grazers. The broad, low presences that move across the pale plant-stone in long processions, their plates catching the hard light, their foot-pads pressing the resonant ground without wounding it. Their bodies are vessels and valves, sealed chemistries and patient pressures. They are polished by dust, cooled by thin air, made economical by dryness. They lose almost nothing. They ask almost nothing, except to continue.

A benevolent and wise power, noticing them, would first have to learn not to pity them.

That is harder than it sounds.
