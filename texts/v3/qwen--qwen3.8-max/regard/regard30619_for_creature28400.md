A benevolent and wise power would not arrive there as a rescue.

It would not come with green.

That would be the first act of its kindness, and the hardest for a gentler world to understand. It would look upon that wet, low, resonant country and see that the absence of green is not a wound to be closed, but a condition to be honored. It would understand that to make leaves rise there would be to change everything: the way sound travels, the way water beads, the way the stone dries or fails to dry, the way predators listen, the way shelters are made, the way the people move, the way death is distributed, the way meaning is built from scarcity. A forest would not be a gift there. It would be an occupation. Roots would crack pavements. Canopy would muffle the stone. Wood would make bone and shell less precious, less carefully kept. The microbial films would be shaded or buried. The acoustic world would become a visual one, and the old grammar of the basin would be broken.

So the power would not make the place more generous by making it less itself.

Its first act would be refusal.

Not cold refusal. Not abandonment. A refusal made of reverence.

It would not turn the basin into something easier. It would not silence the water. It would not remove the predators. It would not make the grazers endless, the stone unbreakable, the air warm
