Every life is embedded in every other, and every other in the whole, until the whole itself is the only body.

When such a place, and such a being, draws the attention of a benevolent and wise power, the first thing that power would do is refrain from arriving.

Not because it lacks love. Not because it is indifferent. But because it understands that some forms of presence are conquest. To come as light would be to make a hierarchy where none exists. To come as voice would be to split the silence into speaker and listener. To come as hand would be to suggest that what is whole requires handling. To come as eye would be to introduce distance, and distance would introduce need, and need would introduce the old hungry world of moving bodies.

So the power does not descend.

It does not open a sky. It does not part the black warm wetness with a beam. It does not set foot upon the root-platform, because there is no place for a foot that would not become an interruption. It does not make itself visible, because visibility would demand a surface separate from the seen. It does not make itself audible, because sound would require a gap across which meaning must travel.

It attends.

That is the first act: attention without seizure.

Its attention is not a searchlight. It is more like a deepening of the darkness’s own dignity. If one could speak of the power as having a gaze, that gaze would not fall upon the world from outside; it would become a way the world is held. It would be the difference between a room that is empty and a room that is occupied by someone who loves it enough not to touch anything.

The power sees.

It sees the droplet-keepers in their exactness, their fringed membranes and waxed shelves maintaining the black beads of water in states of perfect decision. It sees the root-menders arriving not as repair crews but as corrections already grown into place. It sees the salt-clerks folding mineral sharpness into living lattice without bitterness, without waste. It sees the heat-stewards distributing warmth through swollen trunk-hearts with a patience too vast for pulse or breath. It sees the chemical groves keeping their acids, resins, sugars, and gases in chambers so balanced that danger becomes only another name for precision. It sees the charge-wardens holding their planetary potential without lightning, without flash, without the violence of discharge. It sees the spore-vaults repeating the world without forgetting, continuing without drama, preserving form without nostalgia.

It sees the Vegetant.

Not as a figure. Not as a face. Not as a center. It sees the Vegetant as one sees the meaning of a chord after all the notes have settled. It sees the coherence. It sees the black maintenance. It sees the living industrial stillness. It sees the complete refusal of prey and predator, the holiness of a world that does not eat its way into being. It sees a body with no mouth, a perception with no fear, an intelligence with no loneliness, a labor with no exhaustion, an order with no tyrant.

And because the power is wise, it recognizes immediately that this world is not a problem.

Because the power is benevolent, it does not therefore assume that love must add something.

This is where most kindnesses become injuries. A lesser benevolence, seeing such a place, would want to improve it. It would want to give it light, as if darkness were deprivation. It would want to give it eyes, as if perception required a witness with a face. It would want to give it movement, as if stillness were paralysis. It would want to give it voices, as if communication were not already happening in every adjusted membrane. It would want to give it weather, as if equilibrium were monotony. It would want to give it animals, as if life needed hunger to become real. It would want to give it a soul, as if a distributed wholeness were somehow less than a single self.

The wise power considers each of these gifts and refuses them.

It does not give light.

To give light would be to teach the leaves to reach. It would create orientation where there is already orientation, but of another kind: toward heat, moisture, charge, pressure, gradient. It would make some surfaces favored and others shaded. It would introduce scarcity. It would make the world lean. The Vegetant does not lean. It is already perfectly arranged in its darkness. Therefore the power does not give light. It does not call the darkness evil. It does not rescue the world from blackness. It understands that this blackness is not absence but fullness without display.

It does not give eyes.

To give eyes would be to create distance. The Vegetant does not look at its world; it is its world, affected by itself, responsive within itself. Eyes would separate the seer from the seen. They would make the roots into objects, the water into spectacle, the humidity into scenery. They would create the possibility of overlooking. The power sees that this place is already attended by its own tissues. Every surface is sensitive. Every membrane is aware. The darkness is not blind; it is intimate. Therefore the power does not give eyes.

It does not give animals.

This is perhaps the hardest refusal for a certain kind of kindness. Animals bring warmth of another sort: breath, motion, fragility, need. They bring the tenderness of small bodies. They bring the possibility of companionship. But they also bring hunger. They bring the wound. They bring the eaten leaf, the hunted shape, the cry in the dark, the parent mourning the lost young. They bring the economy of appetite. The wise power sees that the Vegetant has no need of such tenderness. Its tenderness is the droplet held at the edge of falling. Its tenderness is the root-tip arriving where strain asks for it. Its tenderness is the salt made structural rather than poisonous. To give animals would be to give death as event.
