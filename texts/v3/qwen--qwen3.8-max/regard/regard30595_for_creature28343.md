They would do the hardest thing: they would not arrive.

Not in weather. Not in a darkening of the ordinary sky. Not in a wind, a voice, a pressure, a sign. They would not send a cloud across that high luminous country or make the white warmth tremble with significance. They would not make the pans ring with an unseen footstep or cause the broad leaves to turn toward a presence they cannot recognize. They would not make the filament mats bow, or the cushions open, or the blade-forms shine with prophecy. To do any of that would be to impose a grammar the place has never needed.

If a benevolent and wise power noticed that country and the being who lives there, their first act would be refusal of spectacle.

They would come, if they came at all, as attention.

Attention, in this case, would not be passive. It would be a discipline of love. It would mean looking without correcting. Seeing without improving. Knowing without claiming. The power would stand, if standing is even the right word, at the rim of the white basins and would not mistake the absence of water for thirst, or the absence of animals for loneliness, or the absence of built things for poverty, or the absence of urgency for death.

They would see the place clearly.

They would see that the lushness there is not a failed garden waiting for rain. They would see that the industrial hum is not a machine missing its builders. They would see that the silence is not grief withheld. They would see that the emptiness is not lack but spacing, and that the stillness is not paralysis but balance. They would see that the true inhabitant is not any one thicket, any one mat, any one blade-form, but the whole dry operation: the basin as body, the charge as breath, the metallic films as memory without story, the hum as a voice that does not need to speak.

And because they are wise, they would understand the first violence: to give a being the needs of another kind of being.

So they would not bring water.

Water would be a mercy only if thirst were present. There is no thirst there. The vegetation has solved thirst so completely that thirst is not even a memory. To bring rain into that country would be to introduce a question the place has no grammar to answer. It would make the ground soft where it is meant to be exact. It would make the mineral crusts uncertain. It would bring the possibility of rot, of rush, of dependence. It would add grief.

They would not bring animals.

Animals would bring hunger, flight, pursuit, fear, nesting, defense, the whole sharp architecture of need. The Dry Lush does not need to be grazed in order to be alive. It does not need to be eaten in order to participate in a cycle. It does not need pollinators, because it does not persuade. It does not need watchers, because it does not perform. To add animals would be to introduce urgency into a country whose dignity is its lack of urgency. It would turn a quiet factory into a hunting ground.

They would not bring buildings.

No wall, no path, no marker, no ruin. That is part of the place’s purity. The power would see that the absence of artifacts is not absence of meaning. The place already has function without fabrication. It already has production without tools. To add a built thing would be to make matter serve an external intention. It would create a center where there is meant to be only distribution. It would make the landscape into a site rather than a condition.

They would not bring memory.

Not in the way wet worlds understand memory. The place has no past in the narrative sense. It has residue, film, lattice, charge, balance. If the power gave it a human kind of memory, it would burden it with loss. The country would begin to remember what it was not. It would compare itself to other places.
