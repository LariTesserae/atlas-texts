If such a place and such a being drew the attention of a benevolent and wise power, the power would probably do less than anyone expects, and more than anyone would notice.

It would not arrive as spectacle.

No face would form in the sky. No voice would roll across the plain. No rain would come. That is the first and most important restraint. It would not break the ancient dryness with water, or soften the fused regolith into soil, or call back vanished oceans from some impossible memory. It would not raise mountains again, or darken the air with fertile clouds, or make the lichen green in the way of younger worlds. It would not silence the dry storms, nor still the dust, nor give the plain a future shaped like a reward.

It would understand, without sentiment and without fear, that the place was not a ruin waiting to be repaired. It was a completion.

To make the terminal plain abundant again would not be kindness. It would be erasure. It would be a younger will
