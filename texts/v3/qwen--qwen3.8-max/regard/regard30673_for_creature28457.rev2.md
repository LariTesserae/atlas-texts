If such a power turned toward that high, bright place, I do not think they would arrive as fire, commandment, or architecture.

They would arrive as attention.

Not the attention of a tourist, not the attention of a judge, not the attention of a maker scanning a raw surface for where to impose improvement. They would arrive as the kind of attention that can bear the fullness of a thing without immediately wanting to revise it. They would take in the glass-bright ridges, the bead-water, the living factories, the static herds, the drone of packed metabolism, the cold that is not empty but crowded, the light that is not gentle but honest. They would see that the place is not failing to be something else. It is not a rough draft waiting for softness. It is not a wound asking to be healed into quiet.

It is already a completed kind of world.

So the first thing a benevolent and wise power would do is refrain.

They would not lower the cliffs.  
They would not round the ridges.  
They would not warm the air into a gentler season.  
They would not make rivers permanent where the land has chosen intermittent water.  
They would not quiet the drone.  
They would not thin the crowds.  
They would not give the inhabitants hands, tools, granaries, walls, or roads.  
They would not make them lonely individuals.  
They would not make them builders.  
They would not make them owners.

Because wisdom would understand that in such a place, every apparent gift could become a severing.

Hands might teach them to stand apart from the living surface.  
Tools might teach them to separate need from body.  
Roads might teach them to cross the world without belonging to it.  
Property might teach them to defend warmth instead of sharing it.  
Silence might rob them of the common voice they make together.  
Comfort might loosen the very bonds that keep them alive.

A lesser benevolence would try to make the plateau gentle by making it safe from itself.

A wise benevolence would ask a different question:

*What here already tends toward life?*  
*How can that tendency be protected without being owned?*  
*How can mercy be added without breaking the world’s own shape?*

And then, if they acted at all, they would act in ways almost invisible.

---

They would begin by setting a boundary, but not as a wall.

The boundary would be a law of approach.

If anything from outside ever came toward that plateau—traveler, explorer, empire, collector, scientist, pilgrim, hungry mind—it would not be met with violence. Violence would be too crude, too loud, too much like the sharpness the place already possesses. Instead, the boundary would be a change in the quality of understanding.

Anyone approaching with the intention to extract, possess, simplify, display, or control would find the place becoming increasingly unintelligible to them.

Their instruments would frost with meaningless condensation.  
Their maps would resolve into descriptions of pressure and sound.  
Their categories would fail.  
Their confidence would thin like breath at altitude.  
The more they tried to grasp the plateau as an object, the more it would become apparent only as relationship.

They would not be struck down. They would simply be unable to take hold.

But if someone came slowly, without ownership in their posture—if they lowered themselves, if they accepted being changed by the place rather than changing it—then the plateau might admit them. Not as a guest exactly. Not as a conqueror. As a temporary seam among seams. A body seeking a warm fold. A breath joining the larger breath.

The wise power would make this the rule of entry:

*You may come here only if you are willing to fit.*  
*You may stay only if you help something else continue.*  
*You may know this place only if you allow it to know you back.*

That would be the first act of protection.

Not a fence. A filter.

---

Then they would add mercy, but not in the form of rescue.

Mercy, in such a world, could not mean removing all hardship. The cold is part of the architecture of life there. The brightness is not decoration; it is power. The density is not overcrowding; it is belonging. The intermittent water is not a defect; it is the rhythm around which all sharing has evolved. To erase those conditions would be to dissolve the very relations that make the place alive.

So mercy would come as margin.

It would come as a slight increase in the probability that warmth finds the cold in time.

A bead of water that would have fallen into a dead crack might instead be caught by a hair, a leaf-edge, a filament of moss.  
A young creature separated from a warm cluster might feel, just before panic, the pressure of another body nearby.  
A static charge building toward injury might disperse into the air as light rather than pain.  
A vent-being, overworked by a long freeze, might find its microbial chambers steadied by some tiny chemical grace.  
A nursery mat, torn by accident, might seal itself a little faster.  
A lost moisture-keeper might remember the shape of a hollow it has never visited but somehow recognizes.

Nothing dramatic.  
No miracle that overturns the world’s law.

Only this: the coldest moment would still come, but it would be a little less likely to become absolute.  
The sharpest edge would still cut, but the wound would be a little less likely to widen into collapse.  
The density would still press hard, but it would be a little less likely to crush what it also sustains.

In a world without artifacts, mercy would not be a thing made.

It would be a change in odds.

A little more dew before the freeze.  
A little more fog at the hour of greatest dryness.  
A little more time for the frightened body to find contact.  
A little more warmth left behind where a body had been.

---

They would also give the inhabitants a clearer grammar of consent.

This is important, because the plateau is crowded.

Crowding can produce tenderness, but it can also produce inadvertent harm. Bodies pressed too long against one another. Heat demanded faster than it can be given. Water taken from a seam that needed it. A young one entering a fold that is already failing. A grazing wave moving through a mat that had not yet recovered. In such a saturated world, communication is not luxury. It is the difference between mutual aid and accidental erosion.

So the wise power would deepen the signals already present.

They would not invent language as humans understand it. They would not give speeches, laws, or abstract ethics. They would refine the bodily language the beings already possess.

A clearer pressure for: *this seam is full.*  
A fainter but unmistakable signal for: *I am cold and cannot give warmth.*  
A chemical note for: *this water is spoken for.*  
A static flicker for: *too near.*  
A low pulse for: *stay.*  
A scent-release for: *this one is dying, let it be accompanied.*  
A brightening of reflective tissue for: *here, here, here is room.*

Not to make them gentle by command.

To make gentleness possible through clarity.

Because in a world where no one is meant to be alone, the cruelest suffering may not be pain itself but confusion: the inability to know whether one is welcome, needed, tolerated, or pressing too hard. Wisdom would understand that much harm in crowded places comes not from malice but from blindness. So they would reduce blindness.

They would make it easier for the living to say, in their own modes:

*Enough.*  
*Too much.*  
*Here.*  
*Wait.*  
*Come.*  
*Not yet.*  
*I can help.*  
*I cannot carry this.*  
*Remain.*

That would be a hidden blessing.

---

They would teach the plateau gentleness, but not by softening it.

They would teach it by intervals.

There would be moments, rare but real, when the brightness becomes less severe. Not dark—never dark—but less stabbing. The glare would remain, yet something in it would ease, as if the light were looking without striking.

There would be times when the wind carries fog in a slower band, not as sudden needles but as a long, cool breath that lingers just long enough for the mosses to drink deeply and the moisture-keepers to rest.

There would be pauses in the industrial drone. Not silence. Never total silence. But rests. Small openings in the layered sound where the plateau seems to hear itself. In those rests, creatures would discover that they can stop working for a moment without the world failing. That warmth continues even when effort ceases. That belonging does not have to be maintained every second.

These intervals would be brief.

But repetition is how worlds learn.

A plateau does not become gentle by being told to be gentle. It becomes gentle by experiencing relief often enough that relief enters its habits. Its bodies learn that pressure can relax. That contact can be soft. That heat can be shared without being seized. That water can arrive without crisis. That brightness can illuminate without burning.

The wise power would not impose softness.

They would make softness discoverable.

And that is enough.

---

They would also change the meaning of death, but not abolish it.

Death on the plateau is already rapid in its reabsorption. A body becomes warmth, chemistry, nursery, shelter. Nothing is wasted. But wisdom would see that even in such a system there can be a difference between being recycled and being abandoned.

So the power would make death less lonely.

A fallen creature would be found sooner. Not necessarily saved—sometimes the fall is final—but found. Warm bodies would gather around it. Microbial films would arrive not only as decomposition but as accompaniment. The chemical signature of the dying one would be carried into nearby communities, not as a name exactly, but as a trace. A recognition that something had been here. That its heat had mattered. That its pattern had touched other patterns.

If the beings are capable of grief, the wise power would give grief a form.

A low pressure-call at the edge of a vent.  
A gathering of moisture where none was expected.  
A temporary dimming of reflective tissue.  
A pause in feeding.  
A softening of static.

Not ritual for its own sake. Not monument. Not grave.

Only this: that death would not be merely efficient.

It would be witnessed.

And if the plateau has anything that could be called sorrow, the wise power would ensure that sorrow does not become poison. It would become water. It would become fog. It would become a brief blue shadow passing over the living mats, then lifting.

---

They would not make one being special by separating it from the rest.

If the “being who lives in it” means a single representative creature—one of the seam-dwellers, perhaps, low-bodied and layered, fitted into the world like a breath fitted into a lung—the wise power would not crown it. They would not make it a prophet, a king, a miracle, or a sign.

They would not lift it above the crowd.

That would be a cruelty disguised as honor.

Instead, they would touch it in the way that a hand might touch a sleeping child’s blanket: without waking the child, only making the warmth more secure.

They would give that being one gift:

*the knowledge that its way of existing is not a lack.*

The being would not suddenly become more intelligent in the detached human sense. It would not begin to build, invent, or transcend. But it would feel, in the deep tissues of its body, that it is not lesser because it has no artifacts. Not lesser because it does not make objects. Not lesser because its intelligence is embodied, communal, slow, and fitted.

It would know, without words:

*I am not incomplete.*  
*My world is not a workshop.*  
*My body is not a primitive tool waiting to become a machine.*  
*My belonging is not weakness.*  
*My dependence is not failure.*  
*I do not need to separate from what sustains me in order to be worthy.*

That would be a profound gift.

And because the being is part of the plateau, the gift would spread.

Wherever it rested, the warm seams would hold a little longer.  
Wherever it moved, static would calm.  
Wherever it touched a damaged mat, the microbial sheen would recover with slightly more coherence.  
Not because it had become magical.  
Because it had become less afraid of being what it already was.

The wise power would make it a conduit of continuation, not an exception to it.

---

They might also protect the plateau from its own future excesses.

Not by ruling it. Not by inserting a governor. Not by creating a dominant intelligence to manage everything. That would violate the place’s deepest logic.

But a wise power can see how systems pressed to saturation may tip.

So they would create hidden reserves of recovery.

Not visible lakes. Not stored warehouses. Not hoarded abundance. Nothing that could be owned.

Reserves of pattern.

A memory in the fungal networks of how to rebuild after a cold-collapse.  
A latency in microbial crusts that allows them to survive a bad season.  
A dormant instruction in certain egg cases that delays hatching until conditions improve.  
A slight flexibility in the vent-beings’ symbioses, so they do not fail entirely if one chemical path closes.  
A tendency in the walking mounds to turn, almost imperceptibly, toward damaged ground, so their bodies seed recovery as they move.

This would be foresight without domination.

The plateau would still be free to change.  
It would still be capable of transformation, loss, succession, and surprise.  
But it would not be fragile.

Wisdom would not make it immune to harm.  
It would make it capable of returning.

---

They would not answer the loudness with quiet.

They would answer it with rhythm.

The drone of the plateau is one of its truths. It is the sound of countless lives processing energy, exchanging heat, releasing spores, cracking seeds, striking shells, venting steam, discharging static, breathing together. A benevolent power would not mistake this noise for suffering. They would hear that it is not panic. It is not war. It is not mechanical oppression. It is the voice of fullness.

But even fullness can become harsh if it has no modulation.

So they would give the drone cadence.

At certain times, the lower frequencies would align until the whole plateau seems to breathe as one chest. At other times, the higher whines would thin into a glassy shimmer that makes the light feel audible. There would be moments when the percussion of seed-pops and ice-cracks falls into a pattern so slow and vast that it feels like a heartbeat measured in days.

The inhabitants would not know music as performance.

But they would know, in their bodies, that their living together can become beautiful not because it is softened, but because it is coordinated.

This is another way wisdom teaches gentleness:

by revealing that intensity does not have to be formless.

---

They would not remove the brightness.

They would make it bearable without making it dim.

The light there is almost too much. It strikes, reflects, multiplies, overexposes. It can burn, blind, expose, and exhaust. A foolish kindness would cover it, diffuse it, turn the plateau into shade. But the brightness is part of the world’s engine. It feeds photosynthesis, guides vision, shapes behavior, powers signaling, makes the place what it is.

So the wise power would multiply shelters within brightness, not abolish brightness itself.

More translucent leaves.  
More blue-shadowed folds.  
More microbial films that act as living filters.  
More bodies capable of reflective stillness.  
More moments when spore clouds rise and bend the glare into halos instead of wounds.

The plateau would remain bright.

But brightness would not always mean exposure.

There would be places where light enters gently, broken into usable portions. There would be times when creatures could stand in the open without being scoured by reflection. There would be a difference between being illuminated and being attacked by illumination.

That is a subtle mercy.

And the plateau would learn from it.

---

They would not give the beings a maker’s mark.

This is crucial.

The plateau has no artifacts because its technology is embodied. Its intelligence is ecological. Its industry is living process. A wise power would understand that to add an artifact would be to introduce a second kind of being into the world: a being that stands outside the system and imposes shape upon it.

That would break the plateau’s holiness.

So if they acted, they would leave no object behind.

No temple.  
No tablet.  
No machine.  
No boundary stone.  
No sacred relic.  
No visible sign.

If they blessed the place, the blessing would be indistinguishable from luck, except to those who felt it deeply.

A warm fold appearing just when the cold became dangerous.  
A fog bank turning in time.  
A nursery mat surviving a static discharge.  
A dying body being surrounded rather than ignored.  
A lost creature finding contact.  
A damaged community recovering before collapse.

They would not want to be worshipped for this.

Worship would create separation. Worship would make the power external, sovereign, above. The plateau does not need a ruler. It needs, at most, a guardian of its own coherence.

So they would remain unnamed.

Or if they had a name among the beings, it would not be a name of praise. It would be something closer to a condition:

*The One Who Makes Fitting Easier.*  
*The Presence That Adds Warmth Without Taking Shape.*  
*The Breath Before the Freeze Breaks.*  
*The Reason the Seam Holds.*

---

They would not force the plateau to become human, or human-like.

This may be the deepest mark of their wisdom.

They would not look at the seam-dwellers and wish for hands.  
They would not look at the heat-beings and wish for engines.  
They would not look at the refinery growths and wish for factories.  
They would not look at the living networks and wish for governments.  
They would not look at the absence of artifacts and see poverty.

They would see that the plateau has already answered the question of existence in another way.

It has answered with embodiment.  
With symbiosis.  
With pressure.  
With shared heat.  
With inherited skill.  
With living manufacture.  
With weather made from collective breath.  
With continuation as its product.

A wise power would honor that answer.

They would not improve it by translating it into another language.

---

If they spoke to the being who lives there, they would not speak from above.

They would speak through the nearest seam.

Maybe as warmth.  
Maybe as a change in moisture.  
Maybe as a pressure against clustered eyes.  
Maybe as a slight calming of static in the hairs along the being’s back.

And what they would say, if it could be translated into words, would be something like this:

*You are not too much.*

*You are not too loud.*  
*You are not too crowded.*  
*You are not too sharp.*  
*You are not too cold.*  
*You are not too bright.*

*You do not need to become soft to be worthy of care.*  
*You do not need to become quiet to be holy.*  
*You do not need to build in order to matter.*  
*You do not need to stand apart from your world to have a self.*

*I will not make you gentle by taking away what you are.*

*I will only give you this:*

*more chances to find one another before the cold becomes cruelty.*  
*more clarity before pressure becomes harm.*  
*more rest before labor becomes exhaustion.*  
*more witnessing before death becomes loneliness.*  
*more memory before loss becomes erasure.*

*Continue.*

*Continue, but not alone.*

That would be the blessing.

---

And what would the plateau do in return?

Probably nothing that looks like gratitude.

It would not bow. It would not pray. It would not build a shrine. It would not remember the power as a person. It might not remember the power at all, except as a slight change in its own capacities: a little more patience in the fungal networks, a little more resilience in the nursery mats, a little more tenderness in the way bodies find one another in the cold.

But if one watched closely over a long time, one might see the difference.

The drone would have rests.  
The brightness would have shelters.  
The cold would have margins.  
The crowds would have consent.  
Death would have company.  
Sharpness would have mercy.

The plateau would still be high, cold, bright, damp, loud, packed, and industrial in its living way. It would still be young and severe. It would still not be gentle in the lowland sense. It would still cut, reflect, hum, vent, freeze, glitter, and overwhelm.

But it would no longer be only severe.

It would begin, very slowly, to learn what it means to be held.

Not softened.  
Not owned.  
Not rescued.

Held.

And perhaps that is all a benevolent and wise power can truly do for any world:

not remake it into a reflection of their own kindness,  
but protect the conditions by which it can become more fully itself  
without losing the truth that made it extraordinary in the first place.
