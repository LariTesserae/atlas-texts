They would not arrive as arrival is understood by peoples who build with straight lines.

No descent. No opening of the sky. No hand large enough to make the sea look small. The high drowned country would not bear such a visit well, and a wise power knows that the first act of kindness is to choose the scale of the beloved, not the scale of the lover.

So they would come, if they came at all, as a difference in the water.

A warmth where no warmth had been expected. A pressure that did not crush. A taste in the medium: something faintly mineral, faintly sweet, like the memory of a stone that had once been loved by rain. Not a voice. Not a face. Not a sign so clear that it could be obeyed or feared. Only a change subtle enough for the undermats to notice over centuries, and strange enough that the oldest of them would open a thousand pores to consider it.

If there is a being who lives in the high drowned country, the wise power would not look for it in one place. They would not seek a heart, a throne, a head, a central fire. They would understand that the being of that place is distributed: undermat and frond, veil and rain, spore and silt, the slow leaning of groves, the memory compressed into living felt, the pause of a translucent grazer passing through a cloud of drifting seed. The being is not a king of the country. It is the country’s long coherence. The pattern that remains when all its parts have been counted. The breath made by many breathing things together.

The wise power would address that pattern gently, the way one addresses a basin: by touching its rim, by warming its edges, by waiting to be accepted.

Their first act would be attention.

Not a glance. Not a survey. Not the quick appraisal of a god deciding what deserves preservation. Attention as the drowned country understands it: attention as a long dwelling. They would let themselves become a guest-current, a thermal band moving with patience through the lower basins. They would learn what the undermats know by becoming marked by them. They would learn what the frond peoples know by leaning with them. They would learn what the veil-grazers know by passing through their translucent substance without grasping. They would learn what the rain-walkers know by riding the internal curtains of falling water, and what the builders know by resting in the softened rings and blurred causeways without trying to restore them.

They would not bring devices. That would be impossible anyway, but even if it were not, they would not. They would not bring fire. They would not bring metal that does not bloom. They would not bring light so strong that it would turn the place into a spectacle. They would not introduce new hungers with quick eyes and faster metabolisms. They would not make fish where there are no fish. They would not give hands to the fronds, or speech to the undermats, or permanence to the veil-grazers. They would not try to make the drowned country easier to understand by making it less itself.

Because they are benevolent, they would want to help.

Because they are wise, they would know that help must first be defined by the helped.

So they would ask.

Not in thunder. Not in command. Not in a language that forces an answer. They would ask in the only way such a place can be asked: by creating a question in the medium itself. A slight alteration in the chemistry of a warm column. A pause in a spore current. A hollow note in the pressure where ordinarily there would be only density. A taste that means, if anything can mean there:

*Are you in need?*

The country would answer slowly.

The undermats would answer by releasing a faint bubble of ancient gas, bringing up something long buried: a memory of an older disturbance, a comparison, a judgment not yet finished. The frond peoples would answer by a subtle change in their bioluminescence, a dim green pulse traveling from stem to stem, not alarm, not welcome, but assessment. The veil-grazers would answer by becoming briefly more visible, their fog-thin bodies gathering just enough substance to be seen as something other than weather. The rain-walkers would answer with tiny cold lights in the falling curtains, as if to say: here, here, here, but do not hold us.

The wise power would wait until the answer was complete.

It might take an age.

At last the answer would come, not as a sentence but as a condition: *We are becoming what we have always been becoming. Do not interrupt us into something else.*

Or perhaps, more softly: *We are old. We are many. We are not finished.*

And perhaps, in the deepest undermat, where memory is stored as stain and fiber: *Do not make us lonely by making us singular.*

Then the wise power would know what to do.

They would not save the high drowned country from itself.

They would not drain it to reveal the ancient stone. They would not lift it into the sun. They would not clear the floating mats to make the water bright. They would not remove the density, the darkness, the closeness, the slow predation, the patient digestions, the long chemical negotiations.
