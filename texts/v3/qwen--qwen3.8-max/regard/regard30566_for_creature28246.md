If a benevolent and wise power turned its attention to that warm, weeping hollow, I do not think it would begin by changing the place.

It would begin by refusing to misread it.

It would not look at the empty streets and say, *Here is a town that has failed.*  
It would not look at the absence of animals and say, *Here is an ecology made defective by what is missing.*  
It would not look at the human-shaped silence and say, *Here is a wound that must be filled with bodies.*

A truly wise kindness would understand, perhaps in the first instant of attention, that the hollow is not a ruin waiting to be corrected. It is a completed strangeness. It is a life with a different center. It
