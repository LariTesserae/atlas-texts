If a benevolent and wise power noticed the Court of Small Rains, they would not come down the ramp in a form meant to be obeyed.

They would not fill the shafts with unbearable light. They would not make every jar speak at once, or silence the birds with a single word, or lay a hand of perfect gold over the patched and breathing room. A power truly wise would understand, from the first warm breath of air rising through the flues, that this is not a place to be overawed. It is a place that has been made by listening, kept by listening, and taught by listening. To enter it with domination would be to break the very thing worth noticing.

So their first act would be attention.

They would listen.

They would hear the court not as a collection of parts but as one dense, many-bodied life. They would hear the Court-born moving along the galleries with buckets and chalk and little clay whistles. They would hear the swallows and swifts quarreling in the upper niches, the doves murmuring in the dry recesses, the bats moving through the dark like soft thoughts leaving the roof. They would hear the frogs asking their old questions of the basin, the beetles working the floor’s small deaths, the fish rising to disturbed water, the eels choosing their hidden streets between sherds. They would hear the walls too, because the walls are not dead: timber settling, plaster accepting moisture, copper pins holding old cracks,
