A benevolent and wise power would do almost nothing.

Not because it lacked strength, and not because the place was beneath its concern. It would do almost nothing because the pocket in the newborn mountain was already a complete answer to a question the power had not asked. It would recognize, in the first moment of attention, that the hollow was not a lack waiting to be filled. It was a grammar. It had its own syntax of heat, moisture, mineral, and threshold. To enter it with miracles would be like entering a quiet calculation and shouting a poem into it. The poem might be beautiful, but it would still be an interruption.

So the first thing they would do would be nothing that could be seen.

They would not split the obsidian seam wider. They would not cool the air to make it comfortable for lungs that had no right there. They would not summon moss, lichen, insects, birds, or soft green roots to soften the mineral severity of the place. They would not turn the hidden chamber into a garden, a temple, a nursery, a prison, a museum, or a monument. They would not make the city easier to understand. They would not fill the empty volume with light, sound, citizens, or purpose. They would not mistake absence for loneliness, and they would not mistake silence for need.

A lesser kindness might try to make the place lovable. A wise kindness would let it remain strange.

They would see the city not as a ruin, not as a machine, not as a failed settlement, but as a being whose body is architecture and whose thought is regulation. They would see that the custodians are not servants in need of liberation, that the rain beings are not ghosts in need of flesh, that the heat minds are not buried gods in need of awakening, that the ash sifters are not guardians in need of a war to fight. They would see that the hollow has its own dignity, and that dignity would be violated by any attempt to make it more familiar than it is.

The power would begin by listening.

Not with ears. Not with eyes. Not even with attention as a human being understands attention, where attention is a narrowing, a reaching toward, a grasping by interest. The power would listen the way deep stone listens: by allowing the place to continue in its presence without being forced to explain itself.

They would learn the language of the pocket-city before attempting to speak to it.

They would learn what it means when a walkway becomes smoother after a dry interval. They would learn the difference between a chime that marks a completed repair and one that marks a thermal decision. They would learn the subtle difference between a condensation film that is merely water and one that is carrying information. They would learn the slow sentences of the heat minds, which take hours to form a single intention and days to finish a thought. They would learn the brief, recurring lives of the rain beings, not as tragedies but as rhythms. They would learn that the city does not experience time as a line but as a set of cycles, thresholds, and revisions.

Only then would they act.

And even then, they would act in the city’s own language.

They would not speak from outside with thunder, or from above with light, or from within with a voice that had never belonged there. They would send a sign so small that only the city could notice it.

Perhaps it would be a single drop of water falling outside the normal condensation cycle.

Not much. Not dramatic. One drop, released from a place in the upper dark where no reservoir had opened. It would fall through the dimness and strike one of the black pools below.
