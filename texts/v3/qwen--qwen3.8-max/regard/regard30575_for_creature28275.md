They would not tear the ceiling open. They would not bring a sky.

A benevolent power, if it is also wise, would understand immediately that the chamber is not a problem awaiting a larger room. It is a world that has been compressed, not diminished. To expose it would be to change it beyond recognition. So the first act would be restraint: no beam of outside light, no sudden silence, no hand lifting the lid to see better. The first miracle would be the refusal to perform a miracle that makes the place easier for an outsider to love.

They would come, if they came at all, as a change in listening.

The chamber would not become quieter. Its drip-percussion, its ventilation-roar, its insect pressure, its amphibian knocks
