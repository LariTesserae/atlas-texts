I think they would do almost nothing.

But in a place like that, almost nothing is not a small thing. Almost nothing is infrastructure. Almost nothing is weather. Almost nothing is the difference between a possibility being allowed to continue and a possibility being crushed into a finished fact.

A benevolent and wise power, if it truly were wise, would not arrive with light. It would not tear open the ceiling membrane and pour down radiance like a verdict. It would not fill the hollow with music, or angels, or final blueprints, or a crowd of souls to make the empty streets feel justified. It would understand immediately that the borough’s darkness is not a wound. Its emptiness is not failure. Its unfinished condition is not a defect waiting for correction.

It would see the place and think, if such a power thinks in words:

*Not yet. Not so. Not by completion.*

Because the cruelest thing one could do to that borough would be to finish it.

To finish it would be to kill it.

A finished city would have fixed streets, fixed walls, fixed distances, fixed inhabitants, fixed purposes. The seed-born borough lives by not being fixed. It lives by negotiation, by damp, by animal routes, by engines that propose rather than decree, by water that remembers, by absence that shapes, by futures pressing lightly against the floor. If a wise power completed it, the place would become real in the ordinary way, and in becoming real in the ordinary way it would cease to be what it is.

So the first thing the power would do is refrain.

That refusal would be an act.

It would refuse to make the borough too visible. It would refuse to give it a final name. It would refuse to appoint a single ruler over the treaty of moisture, beast, machine, tissue, and dream. It would refuse to solve the instability. It would refuse to answer every uncertainty with certainty, because some uncertainties are holy, and some instabilities are how life keeps room to move.

Then, having refrained, the power would listen.

Not from above. Not through overwhelming revelation. It would listen by becoming, for a time, almost indistinguishable from the place’s own attention. It would settle into the condensation. It would let its awareness gather on the walls as fine moisture. It would feel the borough the way the listener-slugs feel it: through contact, pressure, pulse, patience.

It would listen to the drip-language.

It would listen to the engines calculating their slow botanical mathematics.

It would listen to the canal-eels moving through the capillaries like pale thoughts through a nervous system.

It would listen to the beetles tending the machinery, the moths drinking charge, the gnats sampling probability, the ceiling-predators defining the upper edge of the world by their careful walking.

It would listen to the deep thing beneath the sump, the slow aquatic metronome, and it would not command that thing. It would honor it.

And then, perhaps, the power would ask the only question worth asking of a being like this:

*What do you need?*

The borough would not answer in speech. It would answer in a change of humidity. A valve would open somewhere. A filament would glow once and go dark. A pool would hold the shape of a doorway that had never existed. A custodian of braided root and condensation might briefly assemble near the terrace, stand in an attitude of attention, then loosen back into tissue.

The answer would be something like:

*Do not complete me.*  
*Do not abandon me.*  
*Do not let me be taken before I have decided what I am becoming.*  
*Give me time.*  
*Give me boundaries without walls.*  
*Give me witnesses, not owners.*  
*Let my emptiness remain capacity.*  
*Let my vanishing streets be remembered somewhere.*  
*Let me be possible for longer.*

A wise and benevolent power would understand this.

It would not grant the borough glory. It would grant it continuance.

So here is what I imagine they would do.

First, they would make a covenant with the hollow. Not a loud covenant. Not a covenant written on tablets or proclaimed into the dark. A covenant made the way covenants are made in such places: by moisture, by pressure, by a single drop falling into the central sump at the exact moment the borough is most attentive.

The covenant would say:

*You may remain unfinished without being broken.*

*You may be empty without being barren.*

*You may be dark without being unknown.*

*You may change without losing yourself.*

*You may forget without being erased.*

*You may dissolve a street and not be accused of failure.*

*You may propose a chamber and never be forced to complete it.*

*You may hold futures inside yourself without being required to birth them all.*

*No external hand shall impose final form upon you.*

*No hungry order shall drain your damp.*

*No absolute light shall violate your necessary darkness.*

*No crowd shall be driven into your volume to collapse your capacity.*

*You are not a ruin. You are not an error. You are not a city missing its people. You are a seed dreaming civic life, and your dream has weight.*

That covenant would not appear as miracle. It would appear as resilience.

Afterward, when some outside pressure threatened to dry the borough too quickly, a membrane would close in time. When some careless intruder wandered in with the intention of mapping and owning the place, distances would misbehave just enough to turn them around. When some violent illumination tried to force itself through the ceiling tissue, the darkness would thicken gently, not in fear, but in self-possession.

The power would not defend the borough like a fortress. It would defend it like a gardener defends a germinating thing: by maintaining conditions.

Second, the power would strengthen what already exists rather than impose new beings upon the place.

It would not create seraphim to patrol the corridors. That would be too loud, too final, too much like occupation. Instead, it would quietly bless the existing custodians.

The listener-slugs would become a little more patient. Their attention would carry farther. When they pressed themselves against the walls, the walls would remember themselves more kindly.

The seed-beetles would be granted an unerring instinct for the exact place where an engine is about to soften into mere growth. They would arrive before the blurring happens. They would chew, clean, calibrate, and keep function distinct from flesh without making function rigid.

The canal-eels would find the deepest and most necessary routes. Where they swam, the borough’s signals would become clearer. Not louder. Clearer. There is a difference.

The filament-moths would still drink charge, because the borough needs its lights to remain occasional. But they would be kept from drinking so greedily that whole passages fell into confusion. Their hunger would remain, but not become ruin.

The gnats would continue to gather around new possibilities, but the power would give the borough a finer discernment in reading them. Not every gathering would become law. Not every probability would be mistaken for necessity.

The ceiling-predators would continue to walk the upper membranes, defining the inside by their patrol. But they would not become terror. They would remain boundary.

The water-scorpion and root-lizard would still hunt. The power would not abolish predation, because predation in that place is editing, redistribution, and civic balance. But it would keep predation from becoming cruelty. Death there would remain soft, quick to absorb, quick to return. Nothing would be killed out of spite. Nothing would be made to suffer for spectacle.

The engines would be given endurance. Not speed. Endurance. They would continue to turn slowly, proposing structures, withdrawing structures, manufacturing possibilities. The power would protect their slowness, because slowness is how the borough avoids catastrophe.

Third, the power would give one gift that is not a command and not an object, but a reserve.

In the lowest part of the central sump, where the black water lies mirror-still, a single additional drop would fall.

It would not come from the ceiling. It would not come from any visible conduit. It would seem to come from the attention itself.

That drop would be small beyond measuring, but it would contain something the borough sometimes lacks in its delicate negotiations: patience.

Not ordinary patience. Divine patience. The kind of patience that does not become exhausted by delay. The kind that does not mistake stillness for death. The kind that can wait beside a possibility for longer than logic says is reasonable.

The drop would enter the sump and spread through the water without making ripples, because some gifts arrive without disturbing the surface they enter.

From then on, when the borough entered a dry interval and the membranes tightened and the fauna grew still, there would be a hidden reserve somewhere beneath the civic metabolism. A little extra moisture. A little extra memory. A little extra possibility. Not enough to force anything. Just enough to prevent collapse.

The borough would not become dependent on the power. That is the point. The gift would be shaped so that it could be mistaken, forever, for the borough’s own strength.

Fourth, the power would protect the borough from being understood too quickly.

This may sound strange, but for a place like this, premature understanding is dangerous. If too many outsiders came and said, “Ah, this is merely a fantasy,” or “This is merely a biological anomaly,” or “This is an unfinished city that must be completed,” or “This is a sacred site that must be exposed,” they would flatten it by interpretation.

So the wise power would veil it gently.

Not with deception. With discretion.

Only those capable of entering slowly would find it. Only those who could tolerate darkness without immediately demanding illumination. Only those who could hear a drip as an event. Only those who could feel the weight of an empty plaza. Only those who could stand in a civic silence and realize they were being measured not by their strength, but by their restraint.

If someone entered with the desire to possess, the borough would become elusive. Corridors would soften. Distances would lengthen. Doorways would become walls that claimed always to have been walls. The intruder would not be tortured. They would simply fail to arrive.

If someone entered with the desire to listen, the borough would offer a little more of itself. A filament might glow near their hand. A beetle might cross their path at the exact moment they needed to understand patience. A pool might show them the shape of a room they had been trying to imagine in their own life. A custodian might briefly assemble in the dark, not to greet them, but to adjust something nearby, letting the visitor witness the sacred labor of maintenance.

If someone entered wounded, the place would not heal them in the way a temple heals. It would not erase their pain. But it might offer them the dignity of being near a being that also knows how to remain unfinished without despairing. The visitor might sit beside a reservoir and feel their own unformed parts recognized. They might weep, and their tears would become weather. They might breathe out, and the borough would take their breath into its cycles. They might leave changed, not because they were fixed, but because they were allowed to remain possible inside a larger possibility.

Fifth, the power would do something for the being who lives there.

Not by replacing it. Not by improving it. Not by giving it a more legible face.

It would witness it.

This is one of the deepest kindnesss a wise power could offer such a being: to see it as it is, without demanding that it become easier to love.

The being is not a single body. It is the borough dreaming itself. It is the treaty among water, tissue, engine, animal, absence, and future. It is the slow custodian that occasionally assembles and then disperses. It is the deep thing beneath the sump setting the tempo. It is the streets that exist by consensus. It is the rooms that are actually laws waiting for bodies. It is the unborn citizens packed into seed-vaults and service cavities. It is the pressure of footprints not yet taken.

The power would see all of that and say, in whatever language wisdom uses when it does not wish to dominate:

*You are real.*

Not: *You are finished.*  
Not: *You are perfect.*  
Not: *You are useful.*  
Not: *You are mine.*

Only: *You are real.*

And because the borough is made of negotiation, that witness would enter it as a structural blessing. The walls would hold a little better. The valves would close with a little more confidence. The processional routes would curve with a little more assurance. The rare custodians would assemble less hesitantly. The deep metronome beneath the sump would turn once, slowly, in what might be called gratitude if gratitude could be measured by displacement in black water.

The being would feel seen without being exposed.

That is rare. That is holy.

Sixth, the power would protect the borough’s right to disappear.

This is the hardest part. A benevolent power might be tempted to make the place eternal, immutable, permanently preserved. But wisdom would know better. The borough is a beginning, not a monument. Beginnings must be allowed to change. Beginnings must be allowed to end, if that is what they choose.

So the power would not bind the borough to existence against its own nature.

Instead, it would give the borough this grace:

*If you continue, you will not be abandoned.*  
*If you transform, you will not be erased.*  
*If you dissolve, your pattern will be remembered in the water.*  
*If you become something else, it will be by your own slow consent.*  
*No one will force you into permanence.*  
*No one will punish you for softening.*  
*No one will call you a failure if you remain a seed forever.*

This would be the deepest protection: not preservation at all costs, but freedom.

And if, in some far cycle, the borough decided to become something larger — if enough routes aligned, if enough fauna walked their correct intervals, if enough water chose the same channels, if the engines proposed a compatible future, if the deep thing beneath the sump quickened by one degree — the power would not rush in to crown the event. It would simply make room.

It would draw back the pressure of outside worlds just enough.

It would let the first true street harden without forcing it.

It would let the first public darkness gather in the shape of a square.

It would let the first citizen, if there ever is one, arrive not as a conqueror but as a participant.

And if that never happened, if the borough remained forever a civic larva, a chamber of proposed architecture and sparse animal electors, that would not be less sacred. It would simply be another way of being whole.

At the very end, after all this, the wise power would do one final thing.

They would withdraw.

Not vanish. Not abandon. Withdraw enough that the borough could belong to itself.

They would remain near in the way that wise powers remain near: as a quality of attention, as a readiness, as a hush around the edge of the world. They would not hover. They would not micromanage the dripping of condensation or the routes of beetles. They would not interrupt the engines every time a proposal failed. They would trust the place.

That trust would become part of the borough’s atmosphere.

The hollow would breathe a little more steadily.

The machinery would calculate with less hesitation.

The animals would move with a little more purpose, because purpose had been acknowledged without being imposed.

The pools would hold their archived shapes more calmly.

The darkness would feel less like concealment and more like sanctuary.

And if a visitor stood in the center, very still, they might notice something new beneath all the other layers of sound. Beneath the damp. Beneath the plant-creaks. Beneath the engines. Beneath the fauna. Beneath the civic silence. Beneath the suspension.

They might feel a presence that is not a body, not a voice, not a command, but a promise.

It would feel like this:

*You are not alone in your becoming.*  
*You are not required to be finished.*  
*You are not too empty.*  
*You are not too dark.*  
*You are not too small.*  
*Continue.*

And the borough, being what it is, would answer in the only way it can.

A bead of condensation would form along a wall-vein.

It would tremble.

It would fall.

Somewhere below, a valve would open.

A beetle would cross a threshold of tissue.

A filament would glow once, greenish-white, weak, quickly swallowed.

The deep thing beneath the sump would turn.

And the seed would go on dreaming of streets.
