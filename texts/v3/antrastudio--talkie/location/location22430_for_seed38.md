It lies undisturbed among its own hills, in a broad hollow among the North Downs, and the fields slope gently downwards to a valley bottom, where a little river runs. The site is well shaded in summer, and the fields are green; in winter black and brown. The soil is a mixture of chalk and clay, and the surface is neither very rough nor very smooth. Hares browse in the fields, and partridges feed in the stubbles, while pheasants strut about the plantations. In the season, swallows sweep low over the waters, and rooks caw among the trees. Down by the river, in a snatch of sunshine, theaney sits and fishes; and high up the vale, where the chalk rises into downs again, shepherds watch their flocks afield. In summer the landscape has life and movement, but in winter it is strangely quiet. All the year round it is rural, and aloof from bustle.
