The place is an oval hollow in a domed hill-side, dropping gradually from high ground to low. Most of it is green pasture, but there are bits of ploughland, and along one side runs a strip of woodlands. A brook slips through, and by the brook is a little hamlet. All around, the heights rise steeply; to southeast, they climb to a citadel-crown on whose scalp are buildings that can be seen far. Roads go up the passes, and one of them drops into the hollow, where it forks, the branches spreading to east and west along the brookside. In summer, the place is quiet, but in winter the winds come roaring down the ravines, and the brook becomes a torrent. Diurnally, the sunrise reddens it, and the sunset purples it, and at night the stare gaze at it from a clear sky. By day, the birds fly over, and the lapwings cry. In season, you may see wild flowers and may hear the murmur of insects. The green pastures feed sheep and cattle, and the ploughlands grow winter corn. Once a year, the hamlet wakes to life, as the folk gather for harvest-home festival. All the year round, the place lies sleeping, and, waking, it only stirs again to wakefulness at burial.
