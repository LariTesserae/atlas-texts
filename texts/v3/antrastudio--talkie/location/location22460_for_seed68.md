An obscure flattish plain, intersected by crooked rivulets, and dotted with ambiguous hamlets, spreads between swelling uplands. On the skyline, foliage rises easily against a horizon of clouds; in the foreground, lanes straggle among ploughlands and pastures. The waterside is mean, with here a wharf, and there a mill. The fields are green in summer, grey in winter; the sky is dusted with smoke in both seasons. Life is stagnant, communication easy, thought unfertile. The silence is broken only by the clamour of children, the cackling of geese, and the crowing of cocks. The place has an air of desolation, and yet of contentment. On Sundays, it is thronged; at other times, solitary. The thatched roofs hang slovenly over the walls; the walls themselves are weatherstained, and the thatch moth-eaten. Here and there, a black chimney emerges; and here and there, a red brick chimneypot. The streets are muddy in winter, dusty in summer, and both in spring and autumn. The folk are slow of speech, and slower of thought; they live laborious lives, and die obscure deaths.
