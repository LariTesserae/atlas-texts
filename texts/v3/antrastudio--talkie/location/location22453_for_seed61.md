The landscape is lonely and remote, a sort of outlandish wilderness, lying under a high fell country, remote from habitation, amid peat moors and heathery fells, beside a silent tarn. The ground is brown and dry in summer, black in winter, and broken into hillocks and hollows. A black mountain stream runs through it, and here and there are pools. In spring it is yellow with gorse, in autumn purpur with heather, in winter uniformly dusky. The silence is broken only by the querulous cry of a curlew, or the uproar of a pack of geese flying overhead at morning and evening. In summer the moors are full of bees, and in warm weather there is a smell of peat-smoke from the scattered cottages. In autumn the moor-fowl shoot across the twilight like flying shadows, and in winter a white mist lies over all the valley by day, and the hills are silent in the night. There is a burying-ground on the hillside, with rude crosses over forgotten graves, and a little chapel where service is held in summer. The thatched roofs of the cottages can be discerned from afar, and in summer the sound of children playing about them comes over the waste places.
