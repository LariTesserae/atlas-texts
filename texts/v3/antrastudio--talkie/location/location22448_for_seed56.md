The place is weird and wild. It has an aspect of solitude and aloofness. The ground is rough and heathery, rising into knolls. There are blocks of stone, here and there, among the bent grass. In the solitude, the ear catches the sound of a waterfall. The waterfall comes down from the heights, and goes away through a glen. Far up, among the heights, is a shepherds’ hut. Mingled with the sound of the waterfall is the bleating of sheep. The sheep wander over the rough ground, and are lost among the knolls. In the grey distance are blue hills. The place has an antique look. It seems to have been little changed since first it was fashioned into shape. Since then, nations have arisen and persisted, and fallen; languages have been born and have died; races have appeared and have mixed and vanished -- but the solitude remains. -- George MacDonald, Phantastes (1858).
