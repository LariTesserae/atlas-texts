It lies amid a quiet region of clustered hills, between valleys full of peace, but arrayed in brave variety of hue from green to brown, amid whose hollows a river winds. Rough reckonings give the valley floor an elevation of five hundred feet above sea level, and the crests of the inclosing hills a thousand feet. There is prospect of open country to the north, and the south is shut-in by woods. The ground slopes gently downward from the ridges to the stream, and the streamside steepens off to meet the water. The immediate landscape shows mild diversity of feature — pasture, arable, and woodland, with farms and hamlets interposed, and here and there a little church. Among the hills the prevalent rocks are red and grey, but there are patches of limestone, and here and there outcroppings of chalk. The soil is mainly a stiff loam, but there are variations, gravelly and sandy, between loam and clay. The climate is temperate and healthy, neither too wet nor too dry, the annual rainfall ranging from twenty to thirty inches. In summer the days are warm, and the nights cool; in winter the mornings are sharp, and the afternoons mild. The vegetation includes clover, ryegrass, and other pasture herbs, wheat, barley, oats, and beans, roots and green vegetables, fruit trees, and hops. The fauna comprises birds of common species, four-footed beasts of service, and insects plentiful enough to be annoying. The inhabitants are rural and sober-minded, given to homely pursuits, and on the whole a prospering folk. From the higher grounds you look down on roads and tracks that stretch away into the plain, and hear the rumbling of wheels and hoofs, the clang of bells, and the voices of travellers. The region has its measure of history, and traditions linger among the people. The region has also its Ghost.
