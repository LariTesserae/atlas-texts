It lies in a narrow valley, enclosed by low hills. The roofs of the houses in it are visible from a distance, but the street itself can be discerned only when one is close upon it. The main street runs east and west; and at the eastern end stands the parish church, with a tiny graveyard round it. Towards the west the street dips, and loses itself in a confusion of yards and small fields. On the north side dwell the poor people, on the south side the more respectable inhabitants. In summer, time seems to stand still in the place; but in winter the place wakes up, and becomes active and busy. The inhabitants are chiefly employed in the manufacture of cutlery. The parish has many charities, supported by voluntary contributions. The people are very honest and very independent, and the poorer sort are proud of it. They are also very steady-going, and very religious, and proud of that, too. On Sundays, the church is full morning and evening; and in the afternoon all the children are taught to read and repeat catechism. In the summer evenings, the men walk abroad in the fields, and smoke by the hedges, but in the winter they stay at home, and read the newspapers. They go to bed early, and rise early, and live quietly all the year round.
