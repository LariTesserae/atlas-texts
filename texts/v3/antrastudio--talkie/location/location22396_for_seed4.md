It lies in a secluded valley, out of the way of the main roads, sunk between low hills. The terrain is composite, partly cultivated, partly wild, with a few cottages among trees. The materials are local stone and earth, the former weather-worn to greys and browns, the latter pale in tint. The atmosphere is mostly quiet, but there are westerly gales in winter. The sounds are those of fields and furrows, of birds and beasts, and the occasional knell of a passing train. The ecology is mixed, with hedges, pasture, arable land, and waste; and the rules of the world are observed in a sort of rural Sabbath. The place has an air of permanence, and seems to have lain there for ages. The life in it is simple and scattered, and the dead are quietly buried in a corner of the little churchyard.
