It lay on an open westerly plain, where superbly distant hills bounded the prospect. In front, but nearer, rose a larger eminence, — a rounded green mound, — which came almost to the margin of the river, and was crowned by a small wood. The stream itself was bright and lively, running swiftly between steep banks and bright shallows. In summer-time, the fields on either side were green and fertile; in winter, desolate and gray. The plain sloped upwards from the water's edge to a height of three or four hundred feet, and then stretched level for many miles. Far away, the plain passed into the dim distance, and was closed by the purple line of the remote hills. Along the base of the mound, ran a country road; and just where it reached the river-side, a little hamlet lay sheltered in trees. The dwellings were few and scattered, and the place seemed lonely; but in the summer evenings it was lively enough, for the hamlet had a green at its back, and the green had bowling alleys and a skittle ground. In winter, the green was brown and bare, and the alleys were silent. All the year round, the sound of the running water was heard; and in the summer, the place was pleasant and quiet enough, for the most part, save when the noise of the bowlers disturbed the evening air.
