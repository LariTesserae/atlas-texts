It is a small district in south-western England, about eight miles from the sea, with aCOLL tributary running through it, and lying between two branching-railways. All around, it is enclosed by low hills, and has itself a moderate elevation. The ground slopes gradually downwards from north to south, and is for the most part arable, with fields and orchards, except where broken by copses, and here and there patches of common. The fields are small, and the inclosures numerous. The soil is rich, and the crops good. The stillness of the place is only disturbed by rural sounds, and the traffic of the two railways. In summer, it has a pleasant green look; in winter, it is dull and brown. It has no features of striking interest, and no buildings, except churches and a few farmsteads. It has no special character, but is simply an average tranquil spot of English rural life.
