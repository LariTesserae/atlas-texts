The place is quiet and peculiar, lying in an open hollow among low hills. The hills rise gently from it on either hand, and shut it in; they are smooth and green, sprinkled with trees, and edged with bushes. In the bottom runs a little brook, clear as crystal, but very sluggish. The brookside is grassy, with here and there a patch of rushes; and all along it are willow and alder trees. The fields on both sides the water are grassy too, but whereas on the nearer side they are level, on the farther they slope upward to the feet of the hills. In summer the fields are green and pleasant to the eye, but in winter they are dreary enough, for the brook is then ice-locked, and the whole place is silent and deserted. The only sounds that break the stillness are the occasional cawing of rooks, and the casual tapping of a woodpecker. In summer, too, the loneliness is seldom complete, for birds sing in the trees, and insects buzz about the puddles. The sky is visible above; but, owing to the hollow character of the ground, the horizon line is very near, and the view is limited. From end to end the place cannot measure a mile. In shape it is rather oval than anything else; and it lies northeast and southwest. Such is the landscape which I have tried to describe.
