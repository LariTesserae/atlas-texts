The terrain is a wide land of singularity, strangely various, setting in pleasure and awe. There is water in the rivers, grass on the flats, woods on the hills, wolds, bogs, heaths, and moors among the ribbed eminences, and traces of men everywhere. The streams run seaward, the roads follow them, and the railway links town to town. The climate is moist and mild, with warm spells in winter and cool spells in summer. The elevation ranges from below sea-level to above a thousand feet. The soil is old, red, and fertile, and the rocks beneath are ancient. The scale is broad and the density of population sparse. The remains are mainly structural, and link the past with the present. The features are larger than any familiarity would predict, and the landscape uncommon. The noise of life is heard, but the silence of death is felt. The place is weird.
