This landscape is quietly uneven, spread abroad upon a valley bottom between high, wooded hills. In front, a river winds away towards plain country; behind, it bends back again towards the hills. The waters are brown and clear, reflecting the sky in summer, foam-flecked in spate. A footbridge crosses just below the forehills; a highroad goes by along the farther bank, and a railway embankment runs parallel a little higher up. The steady hum of trains is often heard in afternoon and evening; the coaches pass quickly, visible for a space, and gone. On either hand, the fields lie green in summer, brown in autumn, black in winter; they are dotted with trees, and enclosed by hedges. Here and there a farmstead shows white among them, and a church tower rises above the hills. In the flat season, the entire prospect lies quiet and plain, visible for miles; but in the spring it is lively with birds, and in the winter sheets of snow cover it, and the hills are hidden. At all times it is lonely, and at night fall very still.
