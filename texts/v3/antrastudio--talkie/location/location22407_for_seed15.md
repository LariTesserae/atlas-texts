It lies below the sea-level, in a soft warm climate, where the temperature seldom falls below 50° Fahrenheit, and is never higher than 90°; where the air is generally clear, but fog sometimes prevails; where the rainfall amounts to about 40 inches in the year, and the soil is fertile. The locality is on an ancient marine formation, and the shores are skirted by young thriving vegetation. The ground rises gently from the beach to an elevation of about 150 feet, and spreads inland for a mile or so, to where a line of low cliffs marks the limit of the plain. All the plain is cultivated, and much of it enclosed; the remainder being used for pasturage. It is dotted over with farmhouses,steadings,and hamlets; and traversed by little-used roads. Its waterside is bordered by a line of sandhills, beyond which lie the waters of the German Ocean. The plain itself is split into fields by fences; and these are again sub-divided into smaller compartments, by lesser fences of dry stone. The enclosures are partly arable,partly pasture, and partly occupied by orchards and gardens. The arable land grows good average crops of grain, roots, and herbage, for the support of local inhabitants and the feeding of live stock. The pasturage supports sheep and black cattle; and the orchards and gardens supply fruits for the household, and some for market. The plain is well supplied with running water, from springs and brooks; and has one considerable hamlet, with a parish church and a dissenting chapel. The hamlet lies about a mile from the shore, and has a station on a branch line of railway. The entire district has an air of rural tranquillity, and an aspect of prosperity; and the plain itself bears the name of Peacefield.
