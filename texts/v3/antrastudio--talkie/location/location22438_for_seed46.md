Landward from the sea the place is a bare, desolate, sparsely peopled plain, running back to a blank horizon. The soil is a lean stone dust, dotted with wrinkle-scars, and pitted with eroded hollows. Plant life is absent, save for a rare and scanty herbage, withered in summer drought to dusty drab. The waterside is bordered by a beach of shingle, and the waste stretches inland to meet a line of sandy hills. Here and there these rise into rocky knolls; here and there they dip into shallow dells. Between them run water-courses, dry for the most part, but swelling after rain into brown torrents. The region has been little touched by man, save where, in sheltered hollows, he has set down hamlets of bleak cottages. These cluster round grey parish churches, and the whole appears to stagnate in a cheerless monotony. Outward from the shore the scene changes. The plain ends, and the ground begins to rise. At first gently, then steeply, it shoots upwards into a benched and broken upland. The benches are dotted with sheep, and the hollows between furrowed with rivulets. Steep pastures merge into steeper woodlands, and these climb upwards to a climax of crags and scars, dropping again into further reaches of green flying hill. Finally, the broken land plunges down into a hollow, and there the airy tumult is hashed into silence. Nothing follows, save a broad, smooth, flowing river, and beyond, flat pasture and arable fields, reaching to the boundary of cultivation. Far as eyesight can follow, the plain rises out of sight, and the plain below melts into the plain above. The lower reaches are dim, grey, desolate, the higher show green and smiling in the sunshine. From end to end, there runs a central water-parting, and the drainage slopes diverge, to west and east, into separate sea-channels. This, where the landscape is most itself, is wild and bare enough: wild with the bleakness of naked hills, bare in the poverty of a toiling peasantry. Much of it is common ground, but plots of private ownership break the uniformity of waste. Here and there rise isolated homesteads, survival of an obsolete agriculture; here and there gather hamlets, where the people crowd together for companionship, and shelter in weather-beaten dwellings. Amid the clatter of looms, such hamlets sink into manufacturing towns; amid the clang of forges, they swell into busy cities. The centres of trade and toil lie thickly on the eastern side of the watershed, for thither the rivulets run, down easy gradients, to a commercial seaboard. Westward, the streams dash steeply into steep ravines, and break into falls, where slaughter and turmoil hinder traffic, and life itself becomes half savage. Then, where the brawling cutlery ends in placid water, man has built himself dwelling-places, along the banks he has set down pleasant villages, and, further inland, he has reared a growing capital. Between these termini of civic industry, the country-side partakes, in a sober and modified fashion, of the same life of labour, by which its seaboards prosper.
