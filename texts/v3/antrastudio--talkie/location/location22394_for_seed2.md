A waste place, vast, hollow, and empty, lies between sea and sky, in north temperate latitude, near a world’s rim. The sea is quiet, blue, and broad — like an ocean; and the sky has calm weather, above rainy clouds. The solitude has life in it: scattered birds fly over, and small beasts haunt the shores. The shore itself is new, freshly emerged from waters, and sharp-featured in youth. It has spells of erosion, and spells of peace; in the former, the wasting action of waves builds up shingle, in the latter, it builds nothing, but lets the shingle stay. The waste place has scale, vast, enough for weather of its own; continental, it has climate, and shares in planetary things: earth-shake makes it tremble, and night darkens it. It has density, almost nothing per volume; vast spaces between its presences. The waste place is built, completely, of constructions made by man. Engines and factories clamour in it, and scales production; it is electrified, and alive with industry. Normal light illuminates it by day, and normal sound fills the air by night. Being active, it has dynamic life; manifold processes stir in it, weather cycles move over it, and a biological pace quickens its ecosystem.
