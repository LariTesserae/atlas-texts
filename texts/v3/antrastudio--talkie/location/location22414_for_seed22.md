A place that has become tangled and wild, shut in by hills, and hidden from the main roads, lies between two small streams. Its territory, some six hundred acres, is partly heathy, partly arable, partly woodland. Its grey stone buildings are mostly in ruins. In summer, it is very quiet; in winter, very lonely. The only sounds that break the silence are the murmur of waters and the cry of birds. The waters meet, and go on together. The birds are chiefly696 blackbirds, and fieldfares in the autumn, and Thrushes in the spring. A church, long disused, stands on a knoll, among trees. The churchyard is full of graves, some modern, some ancient.
