It comprises a low-lying valley, wherein a sluggish stream crawls between meadow and woodland. Beyond, the fields rise steeply to uplands, that fall away more gradually to the horizon. In summer, the valley is a scene of tranquil greenness, dotted with placid cattle and dappled by shadows; in winter, it sleeps white under a uniform pall of snow. All the year round, it has an air of seclusion and repose. The silent highway that traverses it is bordered by pastures and slopes upward between hedges to a hamlet, which clusters round a thatched church among trees. At the church-end the valley broadens into a little plain, where homes stand apart, and children play. Higher still, the plain gives place to a scaur of chalk-pits, whence the ground steepens to the downs. All along, the chalk scars the surface with shining white scars; and, where the last houses disappear, the chalk scars pass into flints. The landscape has no salient features; it is featureless, save for an occasional windmill, and, on clear days, the sea may be discerned upon the far horizon. In summer, the air is sweet; in winter, it is keen. The scene has an income of peace, and an outgo of quiet labour. It suggests rest after work well done, and foretastes tranquillity.
