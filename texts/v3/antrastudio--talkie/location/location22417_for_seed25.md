The landscape lies bleak and dull upon the ebbing tide, midway between high-water mark and low; a little to the westward, of a sheet of desolation, featureless, feature-formed, and featureless again, wild, waste, and watery. Along its banks, the sand is heaped in hills, among whose hollows lie dull pools. All, as far as eye can reach, is bare and barren; brown waste, with black specks here and there; a desert in little, parceled out and portioned. Shoals of glistening fish leap up and down the shallows, and the sea-mew skirts the shores. In places, stand black stakes, to show where nets have been, and further out, lie buoy and buoy-rope, swinging with the tide. All else is silent, solitude and sameness.
