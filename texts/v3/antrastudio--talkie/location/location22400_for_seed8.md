Plain lies a wide landscape, sparsely vegetated, with ancient water-worn hills blessing its outline, and gray weather-stains deepening its colour. Softly the level stretched itself between near and far, and in its monotony is peace. Skies change over it, and shadows pass; clouds shadow it, and sunshine warms it; the sweet air bathes it, and life is in it. Here in the quiet, ancient life moves on, slowly; the seeds of things germinate and ripen, and the harvest comes in due season. Birds fly over it, and bees hum in the flowers; animals graze on it, and sport upon it. In winter it is white with snow, and in summer green with leaves; but always it is itself, unwearying, unvaried, everlasting. Man marks it for his own, and makes his dwelling places there; but the fields are still fields, and the hills are still hills, and the waters run in their old courses. And so the plain abides, while generations of men come and go upon it, and leave traces of themselves, which are lost again, and it remains a thing quiet and unchanged.
