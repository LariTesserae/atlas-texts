The place is quiet and empty. Its waters are lead-coloured and its shores are drab. Nothing floats upon them but dead leaves in autumn, and nothing walks beside them but a timid hare. The shores slope steebly down to meet them, and the fields rise greenly up behind. In summer it is all green fields, with scattered farms and solitary trees; in winter all black and brown ploughlands, with scatterings of white. There is hardly anything to break the uniformity, except the parish church and the parson's grey house; and the road runs monotonously straight, between the two. The parish lies in a hollow of the downs, and the hollow has an air of seclusion, as of something shut in, and kept safe from the world. The fields are small and separate, and the enclosures numerous; and all day long, and far into the night, the bleat of sheep rises from them continually. In summer it sounds pleasantly, but in winter mournfully. The place has an ancient cemtery, and a pool reputed to be haunted. And that is all.
