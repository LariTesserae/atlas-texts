The place is remote, yet easy to reach, and has a wild charm. In its freedom, it seems akin to the big world, but in its smallness, to the little one. Water sleeps in a tarn that broods beneath steep hills, and their shadows blot out the sky. Green herbage clings to the rocks, and a few trees dip to the lake. All else is bare, brown moor. No life appears, save such as sports in the water, or flits among the bushes. Silence dwells here, broken only by the lap of waves, the cry of wildfowl, and the whispering wind. The scene has its own style; it is wild, and lonely, and solemn.
