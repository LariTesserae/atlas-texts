The landscape lies among hills, in a hollow of the central Midlands, and slopes southward to a river. Close by, on the west, the ground rises steeply to a height of some three hundred feet, and on the east drops again towards the plain country. The river runs from north to south, and the length of the hollow followed by it is five miles. The inclosures are small fields, and scattered hamlets; and the chief road through the hollow keeps to the west of the stream, running nearly parallel with it, from north to south, and rising gradually all the way to the upland. There are trees in plenty, and they line the course of the river, and cluster about the hamlets, and shade the fields; but, on the whole, the landscape is open, and in summer bright with green. In winter it is drab and uniform. The ground is arable, and the scene of sober husbandry. From the heights, on either hand, you look down on the tts' quiet life, and hear the faint sounds of it, but are yourself out of reach of its activity. In the midst of the hollow stands a tiny county-town, where the roads meet, and the thoroughfare through the length of it is exchanged for one across its breadth. East and west, the district has railway communication, but north and south it is unprovided, and lies off the main lines of traffic.
