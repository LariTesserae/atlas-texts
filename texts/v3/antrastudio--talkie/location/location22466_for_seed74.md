An oblong space of ground, lying in relative peace between throbbing activity and silent solitude, amidst acres of arable country; lies in the bosom of an eastern plain, between two low hills, at the foot of one, and the head of the other, with a brook running through the midst of it. It has an area of some fifty acres, and is bounded by a road on the south, and a railway embankment on the north. The soil is mainly loam, here and there passing into gravel, and diversified with patches of clay. The face of the ground slopes gently from the hills down to the water-course, which lies along the base of the former, in a dotted line of silver. The brook has a shy, troutful look, and seems to love the solitude it lives in; for it runs lonely, though not quite silent, between banks that shut it from general view, and has only here and there a companion cottage to keep it cheerful company. The Cottages stand at intervals along the line of rivulet, each with its little plot of garden ground, and each plot cultivated after its own particular fashion. Here flowers are cherished, and there vegetables; here the place is neat and trim, and there neglected and weedy. The houses themselves vary from absolute cleanliness to comparative dirtiness; but all are small, and all appear inhabited. The hamlet (for such it is) straggles along the water-course, for the space of a quarter of a mile, and no more; and at either end, it is closed by a mill; the one for grinding corn, and the other for cutting paper. Between the two mills, the brook broadens into a pool; and, just beyond the pool, it falls into bigger company, and vanishes. About a dozen rural tenements complete the hamlet, which is thus far rural, while all around it is become urban; for the plain has been turned from green fields into cultivated land; and the plain itself is Part only of a larger tract, so cultivated, all the way up to the feet of the bordering hills. The boundary road is bordered by trees, and the embankment has a hedge. Between these limits, lies the oblong space of ground, which has been spoken of. In summer time, the fields are green and yellow; in autumn, brown; in winter, grey; and in spring, green again. Birds fly over, and small quadrupeds hunt a livelihood, along the place. In seasons of drought, it dusts; in seasons of moisture, it muddies. And that is all.
