The landscape was strange and lonely, lying solitary in the midst of an old world. It was so antique that its very strangeness had become familiar, and its loneliness had ceased to be desolate. Quiet fields stretched away on either hand, until they met the quiet sky. In the far distance rose a line of quiet hills. The fields were green in summer, brown in autumn, and white in winter; and the hills were green all the year round. In spring they were covered with new-sprung wheat; in summer, they bore fair harvests of ripe grain; in harvest-time, they were yellow with stubble; and in winter, they lay bleak and bare. The only living things visible were a few white farmsteads and a quiet church among the trees, with a gray churchyard round it. All else was dead silent waste. This was the landscape, which for centuries had been familiar to one generation of quiet folk after another, until it seemed part of themselves, and they could no more have imagined it taken away, than they could have imagined the removal of their native county.
