The landscape is one of quiet waters and green shores, where woods come down to meet them. It lies in a low-lying country, and the waters are broad and deep. Coming from seaward, you sail up a fair reach of river, between banks that slope gradually back to either hand, from green fields to darker green woods. The fields are not rich, but cheerful in the summer sunshine, with blossoming crops; and in the season of harvest, they are yellow with ripening grain. The woods are mainly of young beech and birch, with here and there an older oak or elm; and they climb the slopes in pleasant sheets of green, until they pass upwards into dimmer and dimmer traces, which vanish among the clouds. The silence of the place is broken only by the plash of passing oars, and by such birds and insects as haunt fresh waters and green shades. In the spring-time, you may see fish leaping; and in the autumn, wild fowl fly over it. The shores are pleasant to walk upon, firm under foot, and clean to the eye. In summer, the walker may gather fresh-water shells; and, in winter, he may watch the skaters. Finally, the landscape has an old-world air of peacefulness and safety; and in the summer evenings, it looks quiet and cheerful, as children playing by the waterside, and weary workers resting on the green banks, beside their cottages.
