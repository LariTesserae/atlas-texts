Donne village lies among the chalk-downs of central England, a dozen miles from a railway, and thrice as far from any large town. It stands in a dip of ground, between two rounded heights, and has an easterly aspect. Rows of houses face a green, and other rows run at right angles to them. The place has a character of its own, and a look of age and ease. The green is pleasant in summer, but imparts a mournful air in winter. The church stands at the upper end, and a row of old timbered cottages faces it, along the highroad. East from the green, the streets drop to a brook, and beyond the brook they climb again to an open common. Eastward, the common stretches to a skyline of low hills. Westward, the view is blocked by the higher chalk-downs, but, from their feet, you may look down on the roofs of Donnë. In the summer evenings, the place is alive with children.
