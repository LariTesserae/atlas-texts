It lies in a wild upland valley, where moor and mountain meet. Here the ground smooths into a long, brown hollow, between heights that fall sheer away to either hand. The hollow is obliterably green in summer, red in autumn, and white in winter. A stream slants down it, here showing, there hidden, and always^: murmurous. The bordering hills are heathery, and marked with sheep. In midst of the valley rises a little mound, whereon are collected some cottage roofs. The place is lonely, but not desolate, and in the summer months is haunted by many birds.
