The landscape lies on the lower slopes of a hilly country, where the harsher features of the hills have been worn down and rounded off into a general contour of easy grace. Here and there bold scars mark the original surface, but for the most part the ground swells and falls in smooth and flowing outlines. The hollows are filled with green life, and the broader summits are bright in the sunshine. The lines of the country run from north to south, and the fields slope gently downwards towards a winding river, which shows silver in the plain below. The whole scene is alive with movement and stir, for the roads are busy, and the railway lines are never still. Here come and go the trains, shaking the earth with their thunder, and here the highway traffic rolls ceaselessly by, in dust and smoke, in speed and turmoil. The villages lie thick along the route, and the hamlets cluster around the stations. The land has been long in cultivation, and is thickly covered with growing crops. The woods are small and scattered, but they help to break the monotony of the plain, and in the summer time are green and pleasant to look upon. In the hollows and by the watercourses are marshes and water-meadows, where the wild life of the district has its haunt. The birds flit to and fro across the sky, and the cattle feed in the pastures. In the midst of it all rises a great grey church tower, among the trees, and hard by stands the manor house, a red-brick pile, with stately stacks of chimney. Far away, on the horizon rises a blue line of hills.
