A solitary place in a remote valley runs up from the mainroad in an easy shallow dip to where a brook crosses it, and then bends round to the left in a sweep of fields. On the farther side, the fields end abruptly against steep woods, which climbs upwards to the hilltop, and so on into unseen continuance. The brook pursues a wandering course between banks that vary from five to ten feet in height; sometimes it spreadingly occupies the whole visible space, at others it narrows to a single line of running water. The valley here is not more than a quarter of a mile wide; the fields on either hand slope genteelly upward, and are enclosed by a plain line of hedge. The woods are partly natural, partly planted; in places they are dense and black, in places thin and brown. The region has an air of quietness and seclusion, yet not of desolation. Life is there, though life that keeps out of sight. In summer, the green of fields and trees is pleasant to the eye, and the sound of running water to the ear; in winter, the whole place has a duncoloured soberness, and becomes dumb. On Sundays and holidays, it lies still; through weekdays, it remains partially busy. The road is traversed by a few passable cars, and an occasional cyclist goes droningly by; also, at rare intervals, a foot passenger.
