It is a wild region, moorland and fell, among the border hills of northern England. The air is clear and keen, and the soil dry and unsoftened by rain. The hill tops are bleak in winter, but pleasant in summer, with their sheep and their prospect of sea and plain. In the valleys between, little farms lie scattered, each with its cottage and homestead buildings, and each having a strip of arable land along the lower slopes. A few small hamlets gather round the parish churches, and in the shelter of the woods which clothing the steepsides mark the course of the becks or rivulets. The becks unite into streams, and the streams flow into a river, which itself passes into the plain country, and so to the German Ocean. Along all the water-courses are meadows, where in summer the cattle feed; and the roads follow the tracks of the streams, from farm to market-town. In the lower reaches of the valleys, orchards appear, and the ground becomes more fertile and better cultivated. Finally, the plain is reached, and with it ceases all that is peculiar to the hills.
