The place is wild and lonely. Underfoot are stones, and heather, and soft peat. The region has been stormed by the sea, and is cut into deep gullies. The crests of the hills are hog-backed, and the hollows between them are like troughs. Along the shore runs a line of black rocks, weather-beaten and spray-washed, among which the waves come rolling in, and break with an eternal roar. Inland, the ground rises steeply to a height of some five hundred feet, and there stretches away, broad and brown, to moorland that stretching far back is closed by the blue line of the mountains. The only life in sight are a few seagulls, and here and there a white-stemmed birch shaking in the breeze. From time to time, a black cloud shifts over the sunshine, and the peatsmoke curls upward. All else is silence and solitude.
