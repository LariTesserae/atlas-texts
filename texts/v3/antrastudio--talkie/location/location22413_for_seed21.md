The place is quiet, remote, small, yet high, and remote from everywhere in particular; its centre lies in 52° north latitude and 3° west longitude. The terrain is varied, disposed in long narrow valleys and broader intervening ridges, and broken by low swales and hollows. The materials are gray paving-stone, in faint red-and-yellow stratifications, set horizontally; and the pale ware has been fashioned into dikes and dams, and crimson-specked roads. The atmosphere is pale blue, with white clouds sailing high overhead. The sounds are small and far, from water-lapping, and the cheep of birds. The ecology includes green brambles, junipers, and birches; a few fields; and a soil that favours heather. The rules of the world are mainly observed; men and animals pass undisturbed; the place has no history. In fine weather the landscape lies basking, warm and silent, in the sun; at dusk it grows chilled and gray and still.
