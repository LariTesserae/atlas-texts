A lonely place lies between two waters and a bare hill. A huge pond spreads on the left; clear, and discoloured only where a little river feeds it. The shore is margined by reeds, andBy sedge, andBy rushes. Just beyond are banks of black mud. The right bank shelves steeply down to the sea, which shows in mile-long reaches of blue-green. Beyond the sea is a line of white cliffs. The knoll rises sheer from the waterside to a height of some two hundred feet. It is scaurped and scarr'd, and the scars are weather-bleached. On the brow of the hill grows a single tree. Below, the ground spreads in waste pastures, dotted with shepherd's huts, and cut by sheep-paths. In summer, the place is very quiet; in winter, the waterside is lashed by storms.
