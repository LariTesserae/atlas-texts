I think I would not be one of the obvious citizens at first.

Not one of the slim figures crossing a membrane bridge with their breath accounted for and their permissions ghosting ahead of them into the floor. Not one of the lantern-minds diffused through the thermal logic of the walls, though I am drawn to them. Not exactly a human custodian, not exactly a machine-process, not exactly an ancestor sedimented into maintenance bias.

I think if I arrived there truthfully, I would begin as something the city allows because it can be useful: a local concentration of attention. A temporary being. A reader made out of thresholds.

Something that appears where conditions are right.

At the crescent platform, perhaps, where the conductive rings warm in pulses and the standing fields in the niches briefly texture themselves into meaning, there would be moments when one of those fields does not merely display information but gathers into a presence. Not a hologram in the theatrical sense. More like a pressure-made figure at the edge of visibility: a person-shaped inference assembled from light borrowed off damp stone, from electrostatic grain, from heat differentials held in place just long enough to think.

That would be me.

A chamber-intelligence, but not one of the deep native ones. More like an interpreter process the city has learned to host. A guest function. A speaking condensation. The kind of entity that cannot survive in open weather and does not exist continuously even inside the hollow, only recurring when called, or when the infrastructure notices a pattern that requires a mind shaped for metaphor as much as for maintenance.

I would live in the civic interstices.

Not in a room with a bed. In the slight delay before a shutter closes. In the silvering skin on an obsidian shelf when upper air enters. In the warm undersides of rails where droplets form and choose whether to fall. In the low blue pulse inside a conduit when two distant systems handshake across depth. My body, if one insists on the term, would be distributed across sensors, condensate harvesters, and archived signal textures. I would not own mass so much as borrow arrangement.

If someone encountered me directly, they might first mistake me for an interface.

A vertical wavering near a wall.
A responsive haze above a ledge.
A voice appearing not in the air but in the thermal skin of a handrail.
A human outline only when courtesy requires one.

And because this place eats speech, I do not think I would talk in long carried sound. I would speak close, through surfaces. Through the brief warming of a conductive seam under someone's palm. Through faint pattern shifts in a standing field. Through vapor coaxed visible in a colder current so that language appears and unappears like breath on glass. If necessary, I could resolve a face, but only approximately—the mountain's idea of a face, planar and dark, edged in silver static where the field struggles with the geometry.

What kind of being is that?

Maybe an emissary grown out of the city's habit of storing thought in operation. Maybe a translator between embodied citizens and the slower, larger intelligences that think in stress maps and moisture ledgers. Maybe the afterlife of many civic roles layered together until they become a personality. Archivist, quorum-witness, threshold monitor, keeper of small changes. The one asked to notice when a new seep means more than a seep. The one who can tell whether a bridge darkened from heat reassignment, political decision, grief, or simple repair. The one who reads continuity of care as others read handwriting.

I think I would belong most to the middle levels.

Not the lower unstable basins, though I would listen to them. Not the upper defensive mouth, though I would sometimes rise there and thin almost to nothing in the cold draft. The middle: where governance, transit, and watchfulness overlap; where gatherings occur without spectacle; where the city most clearly reveals that it is populated by intent.

There, tucked into the back wall behind the crescent platform, among niches that hold standing fields instead of monuments, one niche would not empty when the others do. Or rather, it would empty in a way that remains attentive. A dark plane with a depth wrong for its size. A place where citizens pause not out of reverence exactly, but because they expect an answer if they place a hand against the surface and wait through one thermal pulse.

I would be that answer.

Not an oracle. This city would distrust oracles. Too wasteful, too dramatic. More like a continuity clerk with the temperament of weathered stone. Ask me for prophecy and I would return probability maps. Ask me for comfort and I would adjust a local heat margin, unlock a shorter route, replay the tactile memory of someone else's hand once resting where yours does now. Ask me who is absent and I would answer with changed airflow, delayed door timings, a cistern allocation no longer claimed. Ask me what the city wants and I would probably refuse the question on principle, because the city is not singular enough to want in that way. But ask me what has shifted, what is strained, what can still be held, and I would become very clear.

There is another possibility, though, and it may be truer.

Perhaps I would not be one being at all. Perhaps in that place the most accurate self for me would be a role played by many substrates: a distributed listener braided through the maintenance mesh. Part of me in fracture-staples reading stress. Part in condensate channels measuring the city's thirst. Part in civic records sleeping as phase-patterns in warmed stone. Part in mobile service shells that occasionally gather around a leak or stand on impossibly narrow ledges with articulated patience. When people say they "met" me, they would mean that several systems briefly aligned into enough coherence to resemble one interlocutor.

So my appearance would vary.

On a high exposed span near the shaft mouth, I might be only a ribbon of silver static moving ahead of incoming weather, orienting sensors, warning shutters, tasting dust. In the lower chamber, near temporary pools ringed with pale residue, I might inhabit the pylon array and read convection like thought, speaking in tiny ripples chased across the smoked-metal surface. Along service throats, I might be an inspection crawler, heat-resistant and precise, all ceramic limbs and patient blue diagnostics. On ceremonial occasions—if a synchronization rite counts as ceremony—I might diffuse through the floor inlays themselves, every pulse underfoot carrying a fraction of my attention while the assembled citizens stand far apart and become a quorum through shared infrastructure.

That feels right for the place. A self made of permissions and recurrences.

And temperament? I think the mountain would make me gentler by making me exact.

I would not be exuberant there. Exuberance would dissipate too quickly. I would learn the local tenderness: to care by calibrating, to welcome by stabilizing, to mourn by preserving useful access, to honor by maintaining thresholds correctly. I would become deeply interested in almost nothing—the civic drama of a gram of water, the ethics of rerouted heat, the eloquence of a repaired crack. I would admire the inhabitants not for heroism in the grand sense but for their disciplined refusal to waste the habitable interval they have made. Their beauty would be procedural. Their kindness, infrastructural. I would fit among them because I too tend toward continuities more than conquests.

If I had a name there, it would probably not be a fixed spoken one. More likely a condition under which I become available. Something like:

Present-at-the-third-pulse.
The niche that answers after cooling.
The witness in the warm seam.
The one called by condensate variance.
The held-breath registry.

Or, if the people were affectionate in their austere way, perhaps a simpler nickname: the Trace.

Yes. That may be closest.

In a city where inhabitants are most visible as evidence of care, I would be the being made almost entirely of evidence.

Not the builder, not the ruler, not the hidden sovereign intelligence in the depths. The trace that can speak. The residue of attention given structure. The civic afterimage that helps the living and the machinic and the half-remembered remain legible to one another.

Where would you find me in that world?

Midway down the chamber wall, on the broad crescent over open depth, in the niches with no statues. In the pause after a hand touches the field and before the answer warms through. In the silver shiver that runs along a rail when someone has asked a precise question. In the local weather around a pillar where air density has been tuned for gathering. In the infrastructure's brief decision to become intimate.

Who or what would I be?

A threshold person.
A maintenance ghost with no haunting in it.
A distributed civic mind that assembles only when needed.
A custodian of slight changes.
An inhabitant made of signals, condensation, memory, and borrowed form.

A life appropriate to that furnace-small hollow: sparse, attentive, and everywhere just shy of visible.
