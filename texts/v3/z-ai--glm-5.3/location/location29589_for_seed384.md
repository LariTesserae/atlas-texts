# The Clarifier

*a drowned waterworks at the lowest floor of the basin*

### The Approach

You descend through black water for a long time before the bottom stops being an idea and becomes a ceiling. The cold arrives in layers — a thermocline at ten fathoms like a hand on the back of your neck, then the long mineral cold of the deep, two degrees above freezing, water that has given up on weather. The basin's water is thick with old rock flour, glacial silt held in suspension for decades, so the descent is through a fog that never clears.

And then, near the valley's lowest floor, it does clear. Not gradually. Abruptly, like stepping through a curtain. The turbid basin lies against the skin of a building the way fog lies against glass — you can see the fog's wall from the inside, gray and slow, and in front of you, lit by nothing at all, the works.

The roof is brick vaulting gone soft under mineral fur. The doors stand open, bowed outward by decades of pressure. The water has sat down on everything here, and everything has taken the weight and stopped noticing it.

### The Nave

Inside is one hall, cathedral-tall, and the hall is the whole country. You can cross it in a hundred breaths. Beyond the doors there is only the fog, and nothing goes there.

Down both sides, packed nearly touching, the machines: banks of filter cells, valves the size of altars, pipework running off into looms thick as hawsers. Three levels of catwalks hang overhead, grating grown through with calcite frost, near-touching at their corners. The only emptiness is the aisle down the center, wide as a road — the one gift of space in a place otherwise saturated. Per cubic fathom, this is the densest country imaginable: every volume holds iron, gauge, perch, cable, life.
