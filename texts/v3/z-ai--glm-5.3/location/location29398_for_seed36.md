# The Understory

You come down to it through cold galleries, and the cold ends all at once, like a door. Heat arrives first — greenhouse heat, greenhouse wet — and then the light, which is everywhere and comes from everything.

You are underground. You are also standing in one of the brightest places you will ever be. The ceiling is high enough to be a sky, stone gone with distance into a pale vault, and from that vault hangs a canopy of leaves lit from within, frosted lamps the size of your hand, overlapping by the million. There is no sun here and never has been. The light is leaf-colored all the way through — green-white with gold in it — and it does not flicker, not once, not the way fire or starlight obliges you with movement. It has never dimmed. There are no days behind this place, only the one continuous noon it is made of.

The floor, where you're standing, isn't a floor. It's root — woven root and bark and living stem, a mat that gives underfoot like good loam and is warm underfoot like a flank. The heat is the place's own. All this mass is metabolizing; deep in the mat something is quietly, perpetually composting; the valley runs as hot as any warm-blooded thing and holds you inside its fever. The air is thick with the fever, wet with it, tasting of snapped stems and hot stone and green — a greenhouse in July, a breath someone is breathing directly at you. Sweat doesn't dry here. It just joins the air.
