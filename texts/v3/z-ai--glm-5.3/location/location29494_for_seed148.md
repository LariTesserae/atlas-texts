You come in through the scar.

It's an oval in the shell, puckered and pale, the place where this thing was once attached to a branch — a branch the machinery has since stopped confirming. Beyond the scar there may be a tree; there is a *hypothesis* of a tree; there is a hypothesis of a world with trees in it. The machinery sent probes through once to check. They came back carrying weather instead of answers. Beyond the scar, in other words, is not this place's business.

Inside is this place's business, and inside is everything.

The whole of it would fit in a hamlet, if a hamlet were folded up and sealed — eleven streets, one square, one sun. The vault overhead is the underside of the coat: thick, cool, furred, sweating. The air is the temperature of the inside of a melon — not cold, only *uncommitted*. Your breath hangs in it like small testimony. Everything slopes, very gently, down toward the center, as though the floor were listening for a water table in a world that hasn't been issued yet. Drains are being laid everywhere, patiently, for a flood the machinery has also drafted. The city believes in preparing its emergencies first.

**The streets agreed, provisionally, to be streets.**

They were veins before they were streets, and they have kept the old job under the new one. Each one is a pale groove in a living floor — the pavement is moss, mown to regulation height by beetles, springy, faintly drinkable if you were desperate.
