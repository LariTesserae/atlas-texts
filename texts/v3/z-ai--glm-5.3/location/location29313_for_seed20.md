The rain has been falling for years, and you are beneath it — not sheltering under it, but under it the way a stone is under a river. The surface is eleven meters above your head. If you rose through it, you would find no sky worth the name, only the rain's gray loom, the sea shuddering under the hammering, the horizon abolished. You do not rise through it. There is nothing up there but more of what is already everywhere.

This is the highest ground for a hundred miles. It is a summit. It is a shelf of new stone the size of a cathedral's nave, and the fact that it lies drowned does not make it less of a summit — altitude, here, is measured downward from the rain, and no one alive, or no one at all, stands closer to the surface than you do now.

The light arrives as if through bottle glass. Green-amber, secondhand, tired before it reaches you. It quits at fifteen meters, sometimes at thirty, sometimes at two — the boundary of the visible world is not fixed by physics but by the water's mood, breathing in and out like a lantern carried by someone pacing. Beyond the boundary there is not darkness exactly; there is *nothing shown*, which is not the same thing. Where the churn is most violent, the silt strikes light from itself in tiny cold sparks — the sea flinting against its own edges — and for a heartbeat there are stars below you and beside you, and then they go out.

Everything here is young. That is the first thing your hands learn. The rock is dark and fresh, its fracture planes bright as new knives; the gravel still has its corners; the shelf's lip is a fresh-broken line no wave has yet been given time to forgive. The hill was born in the same weather that drowned the world — the rain began, the sea answered, and the hill came up out of the floor of everything to meet it — so nothing here is older than the flood, and nothing here is smooth. The world has not had time to round anything. Touch gently. Everything cuts.

The water is warm the way blood is warm, and warmer along the seams, where the young rock still sweats its birth-heat through the shelf. Only the top meter, just under the rain, runs cold — a chilled skin, a season with a border. Rising into it feels like autumn; sinking out of it feels like walking back into summer. The whole year exists here as layers you swim through.

And the sound. You cannot be told about the sound; you can only be *inside* it, which you are, which everything here is. It does not come to your ears — it comes through your jaw, your sternum, the water in your own tissues.
