# The Loom

It begins as humidity. That is the first fact of this place: the air is not air so much as a held breath, warm and saturated, thick enough that walking through it feels like parting something. Long before any light could define a horizon—and there is no light, not really, only a diffuse vegetal glow so faint it exists more as a rumor than a fact—the place announces itself through the skin. Everything here is damp in the specific way of the inside of a mouth.

The ground is low. So low that "sea level" is a concept the place has heard about and declined. It sits barely above some vast dark water table, and the water sits barely below the surface, so that the distinction between the two is mostly theoretical. In some places the surface of the earth is a skin: a woven mat of rhizome, pale as veins, that dips under your weight and springs back with a slow sigh of displaced moisture. You do not walk on this land. You walk on its tension.

And this is where the first strangeness asserts itself, because the rhizome mat is not merely ground. Nothing here is merely anything.

## The substrate is the industry

The place is young. Whatever cataclysm or slow emergence brought it above the water happened recently on geological terms—features are sharp, the landscape still has the surprised look of something that hasn't had time to erode into complacency. But the young surface has been fully claimed, and here is the reconciliation of the impossible parameters that define this world: there is no construction, no artifact, no made thing anywhere—and yet the place is industrial to its core.

The plants *are* the factories.

Every organism here has been shaped—not by anyone, there is no one, there never has been anyone—by gradients it maintains. The great barrel-trunked columnaries that rise where the glow gathers stand like smokestacks two hundred meters tall, and they perform exactly the function of smokestacks: they vent. Warm moist air rises through their fluted columns with a slow hydraulic roar, except that the roar arrives attenuated, softened by the density of the atmosphere, until it becomes a low continuous organ note that you feel in the sternum rather than hear. Each columnary breathes at a different fundamental frequency. Across the continental span of this place, they harmonize. The soundscape is a single sustained chord, so vast and so constant that it stops registering as sound at all and becomes something like the acoustic equivalent of the darkness: a ground state.

Along the mat-runners, the rhizomes do not merely grow; they *transport*. Swollen nodes at intervals along the great roots are pump-stations, peristaltic chambers that draw water from below and sugar-sap from the columnaries' feed-lines and push both onward at a steady, regulated rate. Place your palm on a node and you can feel it: a deep slow pulse, once every eight seconds, patient as a piston in a factory that has never once shut down. In the faint glow you can see the sap moving through translucent sections of the runners—amber columns of light flowing through the darkness like trams through a black city, endlessly, pointlessly, magnificently, toward destinations that are themselves pumps feeding other pumps.

There are reaction-chambers: basins sunk into the mat where the roots deliberately die and are deliberately digested by their own fungal partners, the released chemistry channeled through stone-like concretions the plants build around the decay to hold the heat. Fermentation vats, self-contained, self-regulating. The warmth you feel everywhere—warm earth, warm air, warm water welling up through the mat when you press it—does not come from the sun. There is no sun here to speak of. The heat is metabolic. This entire low dark continent is composting itself into warmth, a controlled burn at the temperature of blood.

## The economy of a place with no one to spend it

What is all this production *for*? This is the question the place slowly teaches you to stop asking, and then to ask again with better grammar.

There are no animals. Not one. The niche of "creature" is filled by plant-matter doing creature-things: motile tendrils that sweep slowly across the mat's surface, gathering condensation; spore-pods that pressurize and vent in the deep warm hours; filter-fans, broad leaves held over the wettest hollows, that collect the airborne moisture of the dense atmosphere and channel it down dedicated gutters to the root network. Every process that in another world would be performed by legs and lungs is performed here by hydraulics and growth. The whole ecosystem is a single organism's idea of an economy: extraction, processing, transport, venting, waste reclamation. It runs at a profit, but the profit is only more of itself.

And it does not change. This is the deepest strangeness, the one that takes longest to feel. The place is at equilibrium—not the loose equilibrium of ordinary mature ecosystems, which are actually roiling storms of turnover, but a true frozen equilibrium, a machine-idle. The pumps pump at the same rate they have pumped for what the geology suggests is an extraordinarily long time. The columnaries vent the same note. The sap runs the same amber. Even the erosion—the one process that could age the place—has been solved: the mat weaves itself back over every gully as fast as the thin warm rain wears one open. The land is fresh-faced and simultaneously ancient, a face that has been young for ten thousand years.

Time here does not pass. It *circulates*.

## The dark, and the glow within it

The light deserves its own reckoning. There is no sky to speak of—the condensing atmosphere closes overhead in a permanent low cloud-deck, and beneath it the world is a darkness so total that distance becomes a kind of faith. What light there is comes from the place itself. Certain fungal mats that line the transport-runner junctions emit a cool dim luminescence, enough to see by at the scale of a hand, never at the scale of a landscape. The sap-glow in the translucent runners adds rivers of faint gold. The net effect is that the place is lit the way the inside of a body is lit: no external source, only a phosphorescence of process. You are always inside something here. The darkness is not the darkness of night, which promises a dawn. It is the darkness of interiority.

## Silence, except for what it does

Quiet, but not empty. Beneath the great chord of the vents, at the smallest scale, the place ticks and murmurs: the click of a spore-pod releasing, the thick drip of condensation off a fan-leaf, the creak of the mat adjusting to its own water-load, the wet sliding sound of a tendril sweeping its arc. These are close sounds, small sounds, sounds with their volume turned nearly to zero, and against the scale of the place—a continental floor of pumping darkness—this intimacy is disorienting. Everything here is enormous and quiet at once, an entire planetary engine that has been idling, damp and warm, since before anything had a name for the dark.

Stand still on the trembling mat, palm flat over a pumping node, feeling the eight-second pulse of a heart that beats for no one, and the final quality of the place settles over you: it is not waiting. There is nothing to wait for. It is a factory whose product is the factory, running at equilibrium in the warm wet dark, and if it has a purpose, the purpose is contained entirely in the running.
