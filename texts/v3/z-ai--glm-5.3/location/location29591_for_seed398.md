# The New One

The mountain has no old name because it isn't old. It rose sometime within the last human lifetime — some say in a single season, some over a decade of nights that the valley villages spent listening to the ground clear its throat — and everything about it is still raw. The ridgelines are knife-arêtes of black glass, sharp enough that the wind makes a thin continuous note along them, a flute made of obsidian. There is no soil. No lichen. No rounded anything. The talus slopes are cones of fresh, angular shards that ring like struck porcelain when they shift, and they shift often, because the mountain is still deciding its shape. From the neighboring peaks it looks like a piece of broken ceramic standing up out of the range, too recent to have gathered even dust in its joints.

The air up here is thin enough that the sky reads dark even at noon — a deep indigo, stars faintly present, the sun a hard white coin that gives light without warmth. No birds cross it. Nothing grows on it. If you circled the summit on foot, as the first survey party did, you would find nothing but heat-shimmer rising off the glass and a slit.

The slit is the whole secret, and it is nearly invisible: a fissure in the crown, half a meter wide, breathing. By day the mountain exhales through it — a warm, mineral-smelling updraft, hot iron and clean glass and a trace of sulfur. By night, cold thin air pours down into it. The slit sings on the exhale, one low held tone, the note of a bottle the size of a mountain. The surveyors lowered a line into it and found the line came back changed. They went down themselves, most of the way, and gave the chamber inside its only name.

They called it the Vault, and the city inside it the Diagram.

## The Vault

You descend through glass-walled throat into a hollow crown — a chamber a few hundred meters across, domed like the inside of a bell, dim ember-lit, and hot. This is the paradox the whole place runs on: the highest point in the range is also among the hottest. The magma that lifted the New One has not finished cooling beneath it, and the vault's walls carry that heat openly. Seams of pale porphyry cross the black basalt like stove coils, glowing a dull arterial red, brightening and banking with the mountain's slow internal weather.

The air in the vault is the temperature of a sauna left on too long. Sweat comes immediately and never fully dries, because the walls are sweating too. Cold outside air, drawn down the slit on the night half of the cycle, slides across the hot rock and falls out as condensation — the whole chamber breathes out water. Beads stand on the glass overhead. Runs crawl down the walls and gather in shallow basins in the floor's young, unrounded hollows: little grottoes of warm water, each one steaming faintly, each one faintly opalescent, as if something almost-alive were suspended in them. No one has agreed whether the cisterns are sterile. There is no biology here in any sense anyone would sign off on — no plants, no animals, nothing that eats — but the water has a look about it, a sheen that moves wrong.

And in all that heat and damp: emptiness. The vault is mostly void. The chamber holds its one small city the way a bell holds its clapper — everything else is dark, warm, echoing absence.

## The Diagram

The city clings to the northern wall of the vault, terraced like a hillside town, and it is small — walkable end to end in twenty minutes, though you would take longer, because every street is worth stopping in. It is built of nothing quarried. Its material was deposited from vapor, layer by flawless layer, something like brushed graphite crossed with nacre, seamless at every joint, warm to the touch, and utterly silent underfoot: the streets eat sound. The whole city is anechoic by design, because the whole city is an instrument.

It is, precisely, a seismogram you can live inside. The streets follow the fault lines of the mountain's deep interior, mapped one to one. Where strain accumulates in the magma chamber below, the corresponding district glows — a dim photoluminescence, the strength of a watch hand, rising off the paving in slow-breathing waves — and the walls of that district accrete new rooms overnight, new stair-streets, new balconies over the drop into the vault's dark. Where stress below resolves, a district grays, crumbles, and is quietly recycled into the substrate. The city renovates itself constantly and slowly, on a pace you can perceive if you stay a month: a lane that didn't exist; a plaza one tier lower than yesterday; a lintel thickened by a millimeter. No hands are ever seen doing this. Nothing maintains the city. It is maintained anyway.

The only human artifact ever left inside — a single abseil anchor from the survey — has been absorbed. Deposited over, swallowed into nacre, preserved like an insect in amber on a street corner, the Diagram's one memento of its visitors.

The doorways are scaled for bodies no one has ever seen. Human-height, roughly, but wrong in proportion: the doorways are all cut exactly to the height where the chamber's mist pools.

## Water, Which Is a Clock

Nothing flows here on a river's schedule. Water exists only in the encounter between cold outside air and hot inside rock, so it comes in tides governed by the mountain's breath. Twice a cycle, the draft through the slit reverses, and for a few hours the vault walls weep openly — ticking beads, hiss where drips land on the glowing seams and flash to vapor, little glass channels filling and running. The cistern plazas sit at the bottoms of the terraces to catch it. This water is the city's only supply and, in a sense, its workforce: the same mineral-laden vapor that deposits the city's fabric precipitates with it, so every tide of condensation is also a tide of slow construction. The water does not feed life. It feeds architecture.

The settlement keeps time by it. Older residents — there are none, but the city behaves as if there were — would tell you the hour by the wetness of the walls.

## Light and Sound

The vault is lit the way a coal is lit: seams of ember-red in the rock, and above them, the Diagram's own low luminance tracing the streets, so the city hangs on its wall like a constellation with a street plan. It is never brighter than deep dusk. Shadows do not so much fall as accumulate.

And it is quiet in a way flat silence isn't — engineered quiet, listening quiet. The anechoic streets swallow footsteps and voices, and what remains is an inventory of rare, meaningful sounds: the tick of condensation, a bead landing every few seconds somewhere; the long flanged hiss of water flashing off a hot seam; the slit's held low note when the mountain exhales; once an hour or so, a deep subsonic groan as the young peak settles another degree onto its own shoulders; and, outside, after the coldest part of night, the bright chime of a glass blade somewhere on the ridgeline cracking under thermal stress, a note like a struck wineglass arriving through the fissure from the dark. Underneath everything, below hearing, the city hums. It is working.

## The Population

At the turning hour, when the draft reverses and the walls begin to sweat, the condensation doesn't only bead. It pools. It flows along the street canyons the way any fog flows along any street — and because the streets are shaped like streets, the mist takes the shapes of what streets hold. At the intersections it gathers into person-shaped stillnesses, shoulder-height, faintly catching the ember-light. They stand. They drift, unhurried, along the canyon-walls of the lanes, a pedestrian crowd at the pace of weather. When the tide passes they thin, unravel, and are gone.

No one has agreed what they are. They are not fauna; nothing here is alive in any agreed sense. They may be nothing but humidity obeying architecture — the city built to channel its own breath into these shapes, for reasons, or for beauty, or as part of the readout. The survey party's log says only: *the fog walks the streets at the turning hour, and the streets appear to have been expecting it.*

## The Rules

A few things about this place are dependable:

The mountain is young, and everything here is subordinate to that fact. Its interior shifts weekly; the city is the readable form of those shifts — grow where it strains, gray where it rests. The light is a report. The water is a clock. The silence is a method: the city is a stethoscope pressed to a heart the size of a range, and everything soft has been removed so the heart can be heard.

Nothing here eats, and nothing is eaten. The economy of the Diagram is attention, paid in condensation, accrued as architecture.

And the end of it is legible. Someday — decades, centuries — the magma beneath the New One finishes cooling, the strains resolve, the seams bank to black, the cisterns stop filling. The streets will gray district by district, the diagram will complete, and the mist, with no draft to obey, will have nowhere to walk. Until then the city glows dim on its wall, taking notes on a young god's self-portrait, hour by hour, in the only light a mountain gives off while it is still deciding what it wants to be.
