# The Nursery Caverns of the Unmade Range

## First Descent

You come down through a wound in the hillside — not a cave mouth exactly, but a split, a seam in the rock that hasn't decided yet whether it's a crack or a corridor. The walls on either side are warm to the touch. Not sun-warmed; *internally* warm, the way a hand is warm. The stone is young. You can feel it in the way the strata look wrong — not layered like sediment laid down over epochs, but bunched, folded, half-formed, like clay still under thumbs.

Somewhere below, it is very loud.

## The Gallery

The seam opens, without warning, into a gallery the size of a valley turned on its side — a cavern so wide that its far wall dissolves into warm golden haze. Light comes from everywhere and nowhere in particular: a low amber glow rising out of the stone itself, brightening and dimming in slow breaths, as if the entire chamber were the inside of a lung. Shafts of stronger light drop from fissures in the ceiling a hundred meters up, and in those shafts the air is visible — thick with motes, with drifting grit, with *movement*.

Because the cavern is full of animals.

Not a few animals. Not even a herd. The floor of the gallery — and it *is* a floor, of a sort, a rough chaotic plain of hummocks, basins, ridges still rising — is carpeted with them. They are the overwhelming fact of this place. You cannot look anywhere without seeing twenty, fifty, a hundred of them, and then your eye adjusts and you realize the ones you counted were only the nearest, and the plain seethes with them all the way out into the amber distance.

## The Fauna

No species you have a name for. Some are low and many-legged, like pale woodlice the size of dogs, trundling in dense processions along invisible roads. Some are tall, stilt-legged, neckless, with heads like smoothed river stones, and these stand in silent clusters of dozens, facing the walls. Some are neither — soft, frilled things that drift through the light shafts like jellyfish that have mistaken air for water, pulsing slowly upward to graze on the glowing motes.

They are loud. The gallery hums and clicks and thrums with them — a chitinous percussion, a dry rasping of thousands of bodies against stone, and under it all a deep organic bass note, felt more than heard, that rises and falls with the light. When the glow brightens, the noise swells. When it dims, everything hushes for a heartbeat, and then erupts.

They pay you no attention. You are, apparently, irrelevant to whatever this is.

## What This Place Is Doing

This is the crucial thing, and it takes time to understand: the cavern is *growing*. Not the life in it — the cavern itself.

Watch the floor long enough and you'll see a hummock swell, split, and become a new ridge. Watch the wall and a shelf of stone slides silently outward, wet and gleaming, and hardens over minutes. Dampness is everywhere — not flowing water but *condensation*, beading on every new surface, running in thin uncertain rivulets that find no channel because no channel exists yet. Pockets of warm water collect in the fresh basins, steaming faintly. The air smells of wet chalk and hot iron and something mineral and clean, like the inside of a kiln.

The geology is embryonic. This whole region — and the guides from the surface villages say it extends for miles, gallery after gallery, a honeycomb of birchambers under the hills — is being *built*, or perhaps *born*, and it is not finished, and it may never be finished, and the turbulence is the point. In a single hour you can watch a wall rise, a passage close, a pool form and drain and form again. Nothing here is older than a season. Some of it is younger than your boots.

## The Human Presence

People come down anyway. They always have.

On a shelf of older, settled stone near the entrance — a rare patch that has held its shape for a generation — stands the **Hollow Post**: a single low building of dry-laid stone and timber, roofed with hide and lashed poles, its walls hung with tools. Bone picks. Hammerstones. Baskets woven from the stiff pale bristles that the drifting frilled-things shed when they die and dry. A smoker for the fat, sweet grubs the processional woodlice-things carry in their mandibles — a food source, the harvesters say, that renews itself faster than anyone can take it.

There are four or five other waystations scattered across the known galleries, marked with cairns that must be rebuilt every month as the floor shifts beneath them. The harvesters who work here live in a state of cheerful permanent improvisation. Paths are re-cut weekly. Maps are painted on hide and corrected so often they become palimpsests, dark with overdrawn lines. There is a saying among them, shouted over the din of the herds: *"Ask the floor."* Meaning: no plan survives the cavern; you go where the stone permits, today.

They worship nothing here, exactly. But they are careful. When the amber light dims longer than usual — when the great breath seems to hesitate — every animal in the gallery goes still at once, and the harvesters stop too, and set down their baskets, and wait. Nobody has ever said why. Everybody does it.

## Rules of the World

- The stone is warm, young, and motile. Anything not tended moves within weeks.
- Water is everywhere but nowhere fixed — condensation, seep, newborn pools. You are never dry; you are never drowned.
- The fauna are the economy, the noise, the traffic, and the weather all at once. Their migrations reshape paths faster than the geology does.
- The light breathes. Everything else breathes with it.
- The deepest galleries, the ones still forming, are entered only by the reckless. Stone there is soft enough to sink into.

## Standing at the Hollow Post at Dusk-That-Isn't

There is no dusk. The light simply dims, slowly, on its long exhalation, and the herds settle into the low hollows, and the sound drops from a roar to a murmur to something almost like the sea. A harvester hands you a bowl of smoked grubs and gestures out at the darkening plain, the ridges still growing, the pools still filling, a thousand animals settling into a world that did not exist last year and may be unrecognizable next year.

"Big place," she says, unnecessarily. Then, after a while: "Young place."

And the floor, very faintly, beneath both of you, continues to swell.
