## The Germination Yards

There is a district beneath a city that the city has not admitted to yet. You reach it by descending, but not by stairs — by a ramp of root that has learned to grow in flights, each step firmer underfoot than the last, as if the ground is deciding to be ground as you trust it.

It is a small place — a few hundred meters across, bounded by things that are walls the way a seed coat is a wall. Cool, almost cold; your breath shows. And dark. Not the absence of light so much as the *withholding* of it. The architecture here is photophobic on your behalf: the bioluminescent veins in the ceiling-roots pulse faintly, twice a minute, like a slow enormous heart, and between pulses you stand in a blackness so complete it has texture — a nap, a grain, as though darkness were something grown and harvested here.

The buildings are plants. This needs qualification, because at first they look like buildings: terraced blocks, gantries, smokestack-funnels, a waterwheel turning in a seep of condensate that runs down one wall in a permanent silver ribbon. But touch a wall and it is warm and slightly yielding, fibrous under a bark-rind like cladding. The smokestacks exhale spores, not smoke. The waterwheel is a whorl of modified stipules, spinning because the wall sweats on a schedule. The whole district is industrial the way a flower is industrial: production, throughput, logistics, all of it cellular. Funicular pods run on rails of lignified runner-stem, hauling nectar and dissolved mineral to unseen apices. Nothing was built. Everything was *expressed* — grown into the shape of a factory because a factory is what this place needed to be, and plants, given enough time and enough ambition, are shameless about borrowing good designs.

The moisture is rationed, ambient. Rain does not fall here; instead the dark releases it — a fine cold drip from the vault-roots at intervals you begin to predict, wetting the leaf-litter streets in patterns that look suspiciously like street grids. Puddles gather in the hollows between buttress-roots, and each puddle is a market. That is where the fauna comes in.

Despite the near-emptiness — and it is empty, achingly so, distances stretched thin, a single illuminated figure crossing a plaza visible for long heartbroken seconds before it arrives — the animal life is abundant and small and loud in its silences. Glowing springtails. Hairless root-oxen the size of cats, dragging caddis-cases of bark. Moth-things with mirror wings that navigate by the pulse-light and, in the dark intervals, by the drips. Something in the deep floor-root hunts by sound and so everything here has evolved to be quiet, and the quiet itself has become the loudest feature of the place: a hush with acoustics, a silence you can hear the shape of.

And here is where reality begins, politely, to fray. The district is *young* — geology still soft, laws still wet. Distances disagree with themselves: walk away from the central spore-stack and the plaza behind you arrives in front of you, gently, without embarrassment. Some corridors loop unless you whistle in them, and the locals — there are a few, dim-silhouetted, patient, doing maintenance on growths they will not explain — whistle constantly, low and tuneless. The drip-rain falls upward in the northwest quarter and everyone agrees to find this unremarkable. Once, watching a gantry crane of braided vine lower a seed the size of an oven into a pit, you notice the seed is the pit, has always been the pit, and your memory files the correction without complaint.

The Yards are under construction — their own construction, and possibly yours. Everything here is pre-formation, embryonic, first-draft. The whole place hums with the labor of becoming: enzymes you can hear as a sub-audible tick, growth you can almost see at the edges, the dark ceiling putting out new root-buds like a sentence reaching for its verb.

You realize, eventually, what it is for. The city above is mature. This is its nursery — the district where the next city is being grown, warm and blind and quiet, one drip at a time. You are standing inside a future that hasn't finished deciding whether you'll be in it.
