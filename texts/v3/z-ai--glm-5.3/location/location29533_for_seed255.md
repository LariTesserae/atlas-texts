You arrive blind.

Before anything else there is the sound, and the sound is enormous. A dry percussion from every direction at once — tap, tap-tap, tap — thousands of small knocks overlapping like rain made of knuckles, underlaid with a lower drumming that comes in waves, four beats, pause, four beats. Somewhere a kiln roars with its throat full. Somewhere water falls in a thin continuous ribbon and hits a tuned basin so that it rings, one clear gong-tone every few seconds, patient as a clock. You cannot see any of this. You stand in a darkness so complete that at first you doubt your eyes are open, and the world insists on being heard.
