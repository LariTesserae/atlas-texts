# The Level

You come up the last of the slopes at the hour when frost still sits on the wool-fields, and what you find is not a summit. It is the end of summits.

The mountains had an argument with the sky, once — the whole range of them, cordillera after cordillera, a continental upthrust of it — and the wind was patient, and the water was patient, and the argument is over. What won is a plain. It lies higher than most mountains ever climbed, flat as a laid hand, and it goes on past the range of meaning: horizon after horizon of the same long dark floor, swelled here and there by rises you could walk across in a morning and never feel as a hill.
