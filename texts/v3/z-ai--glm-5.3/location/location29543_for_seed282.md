Between the eighth and ninth decks of the cold works — in a gap the plant's own schematics record as **VOID, PROVISIONAL** — a room is being born.

There is no door. Doors come last, when a room knows what it is. For now there is an aperture: two wall panels that have not yet been persuaded to meet, a gap the width of shoulders, its edges raw, still bright with the milky skin of uncured mastic. Everything about the entrance is temporary, which is to say: everything about the entrance is the point.

Inside, the floor is deck plate, laid panel by panel to a lip of compound that has hardened only halfway, and past that lip, bare galvanized substructure, and past *that* — nothing. Not empty nothing. Not-yet. The void the room is eating. You can stand in the middle and see all of it at once, if the light cooperates, which it does not. The place is chapel-sized, coffin-dense: pipes grazing pipes, conduit riding under the deck lip like sediment laid down in metal strata, a drip tray seated a hand's width beneath a manifold, flanges stacked the way organ pipes are stacked. Brass gauges, dull with cold. Sight-glasses furred with frost ferns. Safety-yellow paint flaking off in coins. Lot numbers stamped into everything, because everything here came from somewhere that makes things in quantity. On one grease nipple, grease still fresh — though no hand has freshened it in a very long time. The room is visited so rarely that visitation is, itself, a kind of weather here.

The room is assembling itself. You never see this happen. You see only that it *has* happened: a bracket that was not there, between one glance and the next. A second lamp socket where there was one. At the far seam the wall simply stops being wall and becomes stock — raw sheet, stacked in the dark — and somewhere beyond that seam, in space this room could not contain, machinery too large for any room works at the rate the room needs it to. The plant seeded this void the way some plants seed cracks, and now the crack is being furnished from inside, millimeter by millimeter, cycle by cycle. Nothing is being carved away. This is erosion in reverse: accretion, addition, a geology with a work order.

The climate is condensation. The pipes run colder than the dew point, and the air's moisture — carried up from warmer decks by the slow convection of the plant's breath — beads onto them in ranks of drops too regular to be accidental, rows upon rows, as if each chilled surface were quietly computing something. Every eleven minutes the compressor cathedral three decks up completes its long intake, and a valve far away seats itself with a thud you feel in your boot soles rather than hear, and for the next forty seconds every chilled surface sweats at once. It rains. There is no sky. The rain is a decision made elsewhere, and when the valve is done deciding, the drips go asynchronous again — ticking against the tray like dropped clockwork, falling out of rhythm, then briefly, horribly, *in* rhythm — before scattering back apart. The water threads a channel cut into the deck, joins the brine run, and leaves. Nothing pools. Nothing stays. Below freezing at the surfaces, a shade above in the air, cold enough that metal takes heat faster than skin forgives it. Rime on the capped conduits — except one. One capped conduit is warm. Nothing in this room should be warm. The warmth is a rumor of a fitting that hasn't arrived yet, and the room keeps it the way you keep a secret you don't fully understand.

Two lamps work, out of twelve sockets. The rest are sockets waiting for lamps, or lamps waiting for filaments. Most of the light is spill: amber pilots, the green of gauge clusters, and one strip above the raw seam that swells and dims with the compressor's breath, so the room's brightness has a pulse. Shadows do most of the architecture. The far corners are not dark so much as undecided. Sound behaves accordingly. In air there is almost nothing — drips, the tick of contracting steel, and occasionally a soft pneumatic sigh from somewhere that has not been built yet. But press a glove to a pipe and the whole plant plays through your wrist: the compressor's long note, the tick of decks shrinking, the rain of a hundred other rooms you will never see. Listening, here, is done with the hands. Above you there is rock somewhere, but it is far and irrelevant; between here and it are decks, and between the decks, more machine. The surface is a rumor, several civilizations of machinery away.

There are no animals. The stillness of that is total: no scurrying, no gnawing, no nests, no droppings, no death. The verbs of biology do not apply. The verbs of this place are *fitted, seated, torqued, cured, commissioned*. And yet — licensed, lot-stamped, sparse — there is green. Three trays of engineered moss recessed between the flanges, gray-green, drinking the condensate, exhaling a measurable bump of oxygen that the room logs and, presumably, needs. One patch of seam-lichen, the color of unpolished pewter, holding the north joint like a living gasket. In the brine channel, a pale filamentous growth — not quite alga, not quite anything — swimming its filaments slowly upstream against the trickle, patient and determined. At the turn of each eleven-minute cycle, the moss trays flush faintly luminous for one breath. A green blush, metabolic, punctual. It is the nearest thing this room has to dawn.

The rules, as far as any place can be said to have them:

Nothing here is natural, and there is no substrate. Behind every surface is another made surface. Dig anywhere and you will find not earth but more plant. If there is a wilderness here, it is the process.

The cold is not weather. It is product. This room sits downstream of the plant's purpose, and its climate is a byproduct with a schedule.

All moisture is breath. The room condenses the exhalations of warmer decks. Its rain is a valve's decision.

Growth is permitted, not free. Moss only in trays. Lichen only on the sanctioned seam. What sprouts outside its permit is not killed but *reabsorbed* — the mastic beads over it, slowly, like the room swallowing a typo.

The room assembles at the rate of its need. You will never catch it. Measure the room twice and it differs — not from deceit, but from honesty. It is not finished being its size.

Warmth is an event. Anything warm that enters — a hand, a lamp, a breath — becomes a temporary weather system, a small fog with a heartbeat, and the room notices: a ring of bright drops condenses around it, like witnesses.

And the last rule, the one the place keeps most quietly: the room exists because it is needed, and when the need completes, the room will complete. The panels will meet. The doors will be hung.
