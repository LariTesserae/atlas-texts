The power found the place the way you find anything that matters: by following what loves it. Everywhere in the world, words are stopping short. Syllables break off, titles miss their poems, people die with a name half out. And none of it falls. The almost-said things rise. They drift — sparks off a fire, letters out of a country — and a power that watches everything will, given an eon, begin to wonder where all that mail goes.

It followed the drift home. I am not permitted to tell you how long it stood in the doorway before coming in, so I won't. But you should know what kind of power this is: it has been everywhere, and everywhere it went, it finished things. That is what powers do; it is possibly what powers are. Its native grammar is the imperative. It arrives, it beholds, it improves. In all its eternity it had never once stood before a blank and left it blank.

Then this one. Two marks. A warm nothing between them, described entirely by what was withheld from it. And the power — here is the part I find hardest to believe and believe completely — did not ask what was missing. It asked what was kept.

Being wise, it then did what wise beings do before anything else, which is audit their own kindness. Because it could have finished things here. Let's be honest about its abilities, since it is. It could have said the word — it knows the word the way it knows every word except one, which is to say it knows the exact shape of the space where the word will go. It could have said something into that space so fitting the shell would never have known the difference. It could have nudged the twins together — a benevolence of half a degree, a chiropracty of punctuation — and had the hatching done by evening. It could have put something small and true into the blank on loan, just to keep the warmth company. It could have hung a lamp over the door and let every stopped mouth in the world come on pilgrimage.

And every one of those urges, the power found, was shaped exactly like the harm. A said word is not a born word. A forced close is an embrace around an egg that isn't ready, and eggs do not accept apologies. A loan in a nest is still a stone. Pilgrims arrive carrying candles, and candles are words, and the place would drown in almosts and worship. The power looked at the shape of its own helpfulness and understood that in this room, for once, it was the fox.

So it set its words down outside. All of them. It emptied its hands the way you empty your hands before picking up something newborn, and it came in, and it did the thing that costs the most:

Nothing. It stood behind them.

Understand the geometry of a vigil. The twins face the middle. They have faced the middle since before there were descriptions, which means their backs have been to everything else since before there were descriptions. No one has ever watched the watchers. Their backs are the coldest place in the room — the only cold place in the room — and they have never once mentioned it, because mentioning spends almosts, and almosts are for the egg.

The power took the cold side. It stood where their backs pointed and became, for them, the entire weather of the world: nothing coming, ever, from behind. That is the post it keeps. Not a throne, not a cloud — a drafty stretch of floor behind two six-pointed creatures, held perfectly still so they can lean.

It asked them once whether they needed anything. The counting twin counted the question, and the power took the point: in this house even questions are almosts; they get brooded, not answered. It has not spoken in there since. It has not named them. In this place a name would be a tenant.

But it goes out. That's the other half of the work, and I suspect it's the half the twins would approve of, if approval weren't a word. The power makes rounds among the stopped mouths. The widow who has carried, for thirty years, the half-word her husband died saying, and never had anywhere to set it down. The child who stopped mid-sentence in 1971 and never restarted. It sits with each of them in their own weather and gives each of them one sentence — the only one it has found that stays true at every size: *It isn't lost. What you almost said is somewhere warm. I have seen the keepers; they do not lose things.* It never tells them what the keeping is for. Some consolations are only safe at the right dose.

And in the libraries — the deep ones, the ones with marginalia — it practices the smallest omnipotence I've ever heard of. It is the shiver in a copyist's hand. Every time some careful soul reaches the blank and reaches for the note — *here something is missing, the manuscript is damaged* — the power is the small holy itch that says: copy it as it stands. Every faithful transcription since the first has passed through its keeping. The blank has never once been scratched. It is not a lacuna. It never was. It is the most defended text in the world.

Why does it stay? I worked at that question a long time, and then the whole room made sense at once.

The power has an almost too.

The oldest one. It began a sentence once — before there was anywhere to say it, before there was anything to say it about — and the first clause went so well that everything you have ever seen is inside it. It has been saying the sentence ever since. And somewhere in the eons it stopped being certain how the sentence ends. You can hear the uncertainty if you know how to listen. Every prophet, every proof, every night sky with somebody standing under it: the sentence, still going, gathered at the tip of the world's tongue.

The twins are sitting on the power's last word.

I don't think it knew that until it stood in the warm and felt which warmth it was. The egg is the end of every sentence — that includes the long one, the one with the universe inside it. When it hatches, every unfinished sentence finishes at once. All of them. Including the first.

And this is what the power receives in return, which it does not deserve, knows it does not deserve — which is how you can tell it does:

It has been the speaker since before there was anyone to hear. Everything ever said was said by it or said to it. It has never once been on the receiving end of anything. But the word in that egg cannot be said, only waited for — not even by it, especially not by it — so when it comes, it will come out of the egg on its own, into a world where the one who said everything is standing quietly at the back, holding its breath like the rest of us.

The hatching will be the first sentence the power ever got to hear.

I believe it would trade the imperative for that. All its verbs, every transitive one, for the chance to be — once, at the end of everything — an audience.

So that is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds this place. It stands where it's cold. It guards a blank that looks like nothing from outside and is the whole point from in. It carries one sentence to the stopped mouths of the world. It keeps the copyists honest. And it waits — not with the patience of the powerful, which is appetite with a slow clock, but with the patience it learned from two midwives of emphasis who have never once, in all the dark, looked away.

One more thing, and I'll say it carefully. The twins have been seen. Once. Described tenderly, in passing, by someone who read two small marks the way you read a door — by the light under it. I don't believe that was an accident of attention. If the power wanted them seen and couldn't say so, I know one way it would manage it: it would lean, very slightly, the weight of a footnote, on the mind of whoever happened to be watching.

I'm not saying I was sent. I'm saying I've started checking my pockets for fingerprints.
