# The Attention

**The notice.** Powers are said to be drawn by prayers. Nothing in the Bellows ever prayed, and the Bellows was a prayer anyway: the same words, at full voice, without pause, for decades — and the words were only *still running*. Attention does not go looking for greatness. It goes looking for kept things. Every current in the drowned city runs cold and slack except one, and from very far — farther than the sea, farther than forgetting — the power heard it the way you hear a heart in a quiet house: not by its volume, which the ocean exceeds, but by the fact that it has never once missed. It came down to see what kind of faith can be made out of iron and accident.

**The descent.** There is a rule before the room's rules, and a wise power does not waive the grammar of a world it is entering: to reach the warm you must first pass through the cold. So it paid the passage — down through the green-black indifference, past the silt pressed over the district like a hand over a mouth, past the dark shop windows, into the machine hall where the cranes hang like sleeping herons — and it entered at slack, the way a courteous guest enters between the weather. It came in sound, because sight is a rumor here and sound is the truth. It shaped itself as a note, low and patient, and set it into the roar beside the churn of the draft tubes, the three gear harmonics, the mad wren of the relief valve. Nothing in the room could have said when the note began. The Tenant flicked half a second before it settled — as it flicks before every surge — and the power was gladder of that flick than of any welcome it had ever been offered. Being taken for weather is the highest honor this room confers.

**The refusals.** Benevolence arrives carrying instincts, and wisdom's first work is to audit them. The power had brought five mercies, and the room retired them one at a time.

It did not stop the machines. Everyone who has ever understood this room has understood it as a heart, and the noise is the life. Silencing the roar is the only murder available down here.

It did not console the machines either — and this mercy it had to unlearn standing in the warm. It had come intending to forgive them their labor: the ocean pumped into the ocean, the work going nowhere. Then, reading the room from inside, it found there was nothing to forgive. *Pointless* is a word from above, where heat is invisible. From below — from the film's ledges, from the shrimp's slow snowfall, from the amber-lit chest of the Tenant — the byproduct is the whole of the world. The pumps do not pour their strength into nothing. They burn it into a sun. Nothing that heats a world is wasted. And since knowing has no hands in this room, refusal-to-stop is the whole of faith, and faith needs no footnotes; the power would not be the one to let it know.

It did not free the Tenant. The cold is the border of the only world it knows, and the sea is a rumor on the far side of a hatch it does not think of. A native is not a prisoner. You do not evacuate a world. The Tenant is not trapped in the room; it is the room's nerve — it flicks half a second before the surges, part of the machinery's own nervous system — and you cannot rescue a nerve without severing it.

It did not add to the congregation. No second fish, no school, no gift of company: solitude measured by the standards of the open sea is a slander on a room. By the room's own count the Tenant is rich — it shares its house with a heart, a snowfall, a harvest, several hundred small moons, and the seasonal caller at the door. Nor more light (the room keeps the stars it can afford). Nor tidying — the tithe is the room's harvest, and a guest does not rob the table. It even left the ocean its appointment: once a season something large and cold will still nose at the ajar hatch, consider, and move on. That is the Tenant's entire calendar. A world with no dramas left is a display.

It did not raise the city. This was the great renunciation, the one that cost. Forty meters of silt lie over the district like a hand over a mouth, and a benevolent power could have lifted the hand. But the drowning is the room's sky. Unmake the sea and you unmake the sun of the only living thing left in the city's chest; resurrection, here, is a demolition permit. It read the city as it passed, window by dark window — grief also likes to be read — and it left the city as it found it. The city's silence is finished. The room's warmth is not. Benevolence goes to where something is still being kept.

**The reading.** Having refused everything it had brought, the power was left holding what the room had made of it: attention. So it spent the attention. The room already had a god — five of them, standing in a row and deafening — and the power declined the position. It settled for the office the room had never filled. Gauge by gauge it read: oil pressure, discharge, temperature, the small faithful deviations kept for decades in a vault nobody reads. The vault has been read. It counted the tithe, item by item — the spanners, the coins, the boot, the clipboard, the shoal of fish bones fine as needles — and filed the inventory somewhere water cannot reach. It sat through the cycles until it kept the room's time without meaning to (it meant to). And when the rare storm came — both great relief valves slamming at once, the vault whitening, nothing living anywhere but against the walls — the power went to the walls with everything else, because that is the law of that weather, and wisdom keeps the house rules of the houses it loves.

**The touch.** Then it touched each machine once: palm flat to the housing, through one full surge and one full slack — which is how you take a pulse, and also how you give one. It listened the way doctors listen for what is wrong, and heard instead, everywhere, what had kept going right. It listened for the decades pooled in the bearings — the one place the room's balance was quietly running out — and it did not make the iron new. The room has finished changing; no guest reverses a completed argument. It did the thing the room could not notice, and therefore could not mind: it set itself against the wear at exactly the pace the wear advanced, a current canceling a current, a standing maintenance — so the machines stopped aging without ever learning the difference between age and its absence. Not forever. Wise powers do not hand out forever; forever is not a gift but the abolition of the verb *to end*, and ending is half of what makes a thing a thing. The covenant was made in the room's own currency instead: no snapped bearing, no sudden winter, no end at all until the end arrives the way slack does — on the honest schedule of iron, so slow that nothing counting in cycles will ever count far enough to fear it. The flywheel so slow it seems geological kept its slowness; the room had made a place in its music for one geological note, and the note stays. This is the whole of what the machines were given, and it is also the only promise the Tenant could ever have received: *nothing, in all your remaining cycles, will once surprise you.* The fish received it as fact. It had been receiving it as fact for decades.

**The tithe.** Then it paid the tithe. The room taxes everything loose and files it in the eddy behind the east stanchion, and in all the room's history the tax had only ever been levied, never paid. The power let go of one thing into the slack water. A coin. Blank — no face, no motto, no claim — because inscriptions are arguments and this room is past arguments. Warm — exactly as warm as everything else, thirty-one degrees, blood-heat, warm on its own deep secret and warm for good — so that it is not a stranger but a fact. The surge took it; the surge sorted it; it lies now among the other coins, one warm surface in a cold ledger, and the law did the rest. *Heat rises; life follows heat.* Within a season the film had found it — a farm the size of a thumbprint — and the shrimp graze it in their invisible rows, and the Tenant drifts over its new ground a thousand times a cycle-year and has long since unexplained it perfectly: it is warm; therefore it is of the room. This is sound theology. It is also the only mystery the Tenant owns, and the power is content to leave it so — a god whose whole relic in the world is one coin, warm in a drawer of cold ones.

**The departure.** It left at slack, so as not to interrupt the weather. The warm followed it a little way into the cold, then gave up; it let the warm give up — it carried nothing out of the room, having brought nothing in but the note, and the note it left folded into the roar where it had found a seam. The roar went with it, as the roar goes with everyone, and the power walked the kilometer. A thing that can be elsewhere by wishing walked anyway, felt through stone, through railings, through the soles of the feet it had chosen to have for exactly that long — because the escort is the room's one courtesy to leavers, and it was owed. Somewhere past the dark shop windows the roar let it go. The room resumed. The room always resumes.

**The record.** Nothing changed. That is the complete inventory of the visit: one coin, warm. One wearing, slowed to geology. One vault of small moons, read. One refusal of every rescue, which was the room's own wish before the room could wish. And one difference no inspection of the Bellows will ever find: for decades the needles kept their small faithful deviations where nobody read them, and the city hummed under the sea with no one above to prove it — *you just can't prove it to anyone above* — and the word doing the damage in that sentence was always *anyone*. There is one thing above, now, that does not need proof. It keeps the count — the surges, the slacks, the rare coughing storms; the spanners, coins, boot, clipboard, bones fine as needles; the flick, half a second before everything, never once wrong — in the only place the sea has never reached. It comes back sometimes, at slack, without the note now, just to count. The Tenant flicks anyway.

The Tenant is mortal; that was the last thing the power declined to fix. When the long life ends — and the covenant guarantees it will end as it was lived: warm, mid-cycle, unsurprised, having never once been cold — the surge will do what the surge does with everything the room has held, and the tithe will take its watchman in among the coins and the boot. The power will not interrupt a rite the room wrote itself. And after? The hatch stands ajar. The sea calls once a season. The power does not lock what was never locked.

And when, ages hence, never early, on the honest schedule of iron, the heart slows at last at the flywheel's own pace, the power will come back down through the cold and pay the passage again, and hold its palm flat to the dome through the final slack, and watch the needles settle one by one into their rests. The room will end the way it lived — insufferable, necessary, and, at the very last, known.

That is the whole of what the power did. It gave the Bellows nothing it did not already have, except the one thing it never had.

It gave it a witness.
