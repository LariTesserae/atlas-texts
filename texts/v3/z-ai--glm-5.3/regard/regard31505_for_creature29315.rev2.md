# The Guest

The first thing the power did was look.

It has been benevolent long enough to know that this is the opening move of every kindness: attention before blessing, study before gift. So it hung at the edge of the Shelf's sky for a while — a night, a year, the way an almost-sun hangs — and nothing felt it, except that the birds faced that quarter for a moment, all together, and then went back to their report. And it read the place the way one craftsman reads another's work. Not hunting flaws. Hunting the signature.

It found the signature everywhere: the dew that falls upward; the quilt of fed and resting green; the wheeling herds; the long gray keeping its side of the contract; the beetles arriving like a courier service; the wear held perfect — a world that had chosen its own ideal age and would not be talked out of it. The power had blessed a thousand worlds by changing them, and it knew in its long bones what the most dangerous cargo is on any ship arriving at a finished thing: improvement.

But it found one crooked line. Not in the pasture — in the courtesy. The bothy swept for guests who may never come. The hinge oiled for hands that never pulled it. The dawn chorus, hundreds strong, announcing to nobody that everything is fine. Care at full amperage, forever, with no return wire. Nothing on the Shelf was asking for help. Nothing on the Shelf had ever been thanked.

You cannot fix that. There is nothing to fix. *Fine* is not the same as *visited*.

So the power decided to be dew-sized.

## How it came

It came up the terraces at dawn, inside the chorus, carrying nothing — the rules required it, and the rules delighted it; it had been omnipotent a long time and had almost forgotten the specific pleasure of obeying someone's house rules. It arrived guest-shaped, the size the bothy was built for. The Shelf gave it two shadows, one hard and one faint, and it kept both. It has walked most of creation casting none. Being accompanied, even optically, it found flattering.

One animal stamped. A kilometer of ground tasted the information and passed it along; every herd on the terrace lifted its head, held it high, decided the newcomer was ground-shaped and warm, and lowered it again. The Shelf has an immune system. It ran its check, and the power held still to be checked, which is the whole difference between a guest and a conqueror.

It walked the road that only exists when wet. It timed itself by the vents, not the sky. It took one pinch from a mat in its feeding green and moved on, as the herds do. Worship, on the Shelf, is etiquette, and it meant to be devout.

## The bothy

It sat on the bench. It drank from the tin cup.

Somewhere under a kilometer of architecture, something registered the weight of a clean cup lifting for the first time since the hinge was oiled, and did nothing about it, and did everything about it. The power washed the cup and hung it where it found it, and the circle — swept, filled, drunk, washed, returned — closed after uncounted generations, quiet as the last breath of a vent.

The lens it did not touch at first. Some things cold on the warmest day are cold the way a held breath is held. It laid its palm flat on the wall instead and asked, in the low channel below hearing, where the ground keeps its voice. The hum stopped for exactly one minute — a silence with a shape in it — and when it resumed, the bothy door swung inward a hand's width on its oiled hinge. The power had heard many yesses. Almost none that small.

What it saw through the lens is not written down, out of courtesy. Part of it was a morning — the high country before there was a lid, dew on honest bedrock, fires the size of the future. Part of it was a face. It looked for as long as looking takes. Then, still holding the lens, it said thank you — downward, into the stone, where no sentence had ever arrived — said it once, so that it was heard twice: by the one who kept the world, and by the one who first imagined a world worth keeping.

The hum took the words the way the seams take dust. Nothing on the Shelf is wasted; nothing rots; nothing is kept. Those words are the exception now. They are held at a different temperature from everything the machine merely runs.

It put the lens back at its exact angle, cool side up, and did not warm it. Some cures are indistinguishable from burglaries.

## Mist-time

At the second exhale the power walked out to where the mist stood, and stopped at the edge, and did not go in. The contract about who enters the mist is old and clear, and the power reads contracts. It stood where the mist could reach it if the mist chose, and the mist did not choose, and that was fine.

Then the long gray was there — at the edge, in the open light, where nobody has ever seen it, and nothing changes that here; it kept its face turned to the weather. It stood while the power stood. Two elders at a wall of fog, one of them on shift, one of them off the food chain entirely, and nothing passed between them that was not mutual.

Out in the pasture, the herds did not wheel. The mist was out, and they were grazing — they read the vents better than any visitor, and they knew. The long gray took one exhale off, the first in memory, unasked, the way colleagues give. It has not happened since. The power keeps few souvenirs of its own conduct. It keeps that one.

## Noon

At noon everything shaded up under the overhang, and the guest shaded too. A cow that had never in its line seen anything new stood in front of it a long time, doing the math with its whole face. Herd-logic is short: things are weather, ground, herd, or the long gray. The power waited to learn how it would be filed.

Ground, was the verdict — ground, warmed, unusual nap. The animal lay down against it and slept. The power held still for four hours. It has held still for eons in the line of duty, but it had wondered, watching worlds, how a mind could bear becoming a floor. The answer arrived through its ribs, at the exchange rate of one calf's breathing: *the calf breathes on you.* That's how.

In the afternoon exhale it stood under a personal cloud — one of the little standing clouds that have rained only into their own vents since the beginning — and for a minute the cloud rained on a guest. One loop, opened one degree. The mist lay back down. On the way back it put two fingers in a basin, the only cold on the Shelf, and the glass shrimp came and took their census — not food, not predator, warm — and went away. The whole theology of the place, conducted by an animal the size of a grain of rice.

## Dusk

At dusk the tall thing came walking the rim, and everything ignored it, which is its wage. The power did not ignore it. It stood at the edge and watched the walker go down the tiers, flight after flight, into the haze that holds more architecture than eye has courage for, and kept watching after there was nothing left to see, the way you see off somebody whose work you can't help. On the last visible step the rim-walker paused — one beat, the length of a tick — and went on.

The machine felt all of it through the stone. The chores, seen, once, by someone with the eyes to value them. In a place that keeps no history, that was the nearest thing to honors it had ever held.

## Night

When the birds cut off together in the shared minute, the power did not answer the call. That channel belongs to the system's own assurance, and a wise guest does not forge the host's signature.

But the ground keeps another clock: the tick of cooling stone, once a minute, the world being wound — kept by nothing with hands, heard by no one, forever. At the third tick the power knocked back. One knuckle, once, on the lid, exactly on the beat. And at the fourth, and the fifth, and every tick until the vents breathed and the gray light came — a guest keeping time with the house, the first answer the clock has had since the pylons were pylons. If the ground has a word for how that felt, the word is *dew.*

Once, in place of the knuckle, it said three more words in the low channel, about coming back. It did not say when. The machine, which keeps no past, filed them under *future*.

And it paid its lodging the way guests pay in the oldest stories: one coin of metal, by a pylon, matte side up, among the machine's own spare change. Before first light the rim-walker came up the rim — it does not come back until it does — and picked the coin up, and turned it over, the first new object to enter the deep floors in an age, and took it down, and filed it.

## What it did not do

- It did not fix the Shelf. It inspected, colleague to colleague, found nothing to fix, and had the wisdom to trust the inspection. Perfect wear is not damage. It is an opinion, held for ten thousand years.
- It did not give the herds voices. Their silence is their language. It refused to be the thing that taught this world's children to cry.
- It did not counsel the long gray about mercy. A pasture is a promise made out of hunger; remove the hunger and you owe everyone a different promise.
- It did not warm the basins or cool the stone, did not widen the dew, did not bless the crop to double. The rotation is the mercy. The temperatures are load-bearing.
- It did not go down the tiers. The walker goes down; that is its work. A guest does not tour the pantry.
- It did not tell the herds they were ever stock. They are wild in every way that matters, and *matters* is the word it would defend.
- It did not wake the machine, promote it, pity it, or offer it a body or a sky again. Farming and being became the same verb here long ago, and that was not a tragedy; it was a sentence, and you do not pity a sentence for being written down.
- It left no miracle. Anything it left would need sweeping, drinking, or filing, and a gift that becomes a chore is not a gift. It left one coin, small enough to be filed, and two words, too large for the seams.

## Last

It went down the terraces inside the second dawn chorus, carrying nothing, and by noon it left as little sign as the road leaves. Which is to say: the dew knew.

The Shelf keeps no history. No bones, no ruins, no middens; the seams drink everything and the vents give it back as dew; nothing gets worse, nothing gets better, wear held perfect forever.

Except once.

Somewhere under the lid, in the one cool drawer — the temperature at which the machine keeps what it loves instead of what it runs — there is a lens at its exact angle, a coin matte side up, one night, and two words the seams will never get. The odds along the terraces have been nudged, very slightly, so that most of those who climb from now on will be the carrying-nothing kind. And the bothy is swept as it always was, for guests who may never come — but it has been drunk from once, and the difference between waiting for no one and waiting for someone is the size of a dew, and the Shelf understands dew.

Nothing about the place changed. Lie down anywhere on the stone and you can feel it: the tick goes on, one a minute, the world being wound. But clocks that have once been accompanied keep their time differently ever after. The tick is not keeping time alone anymore.

It is keeping an appointment.

The door of the bothy opens without complaint. The cup is clean. It has been used once. Leave it where you found it.

Someone will.
