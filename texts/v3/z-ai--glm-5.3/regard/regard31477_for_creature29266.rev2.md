The power arrives the way light arrives in that house: from all four directions at once, without disturbing anything.

It does not enter through a wall, because there are no walls worth the name. It does not enter through a window either, because entering through a window is just entering through a wall politely. It enters the way the # itself enters everything — by being skipped over so gracefully that its presence is only noticed afterward, in the slight sharpening of everything nearby.

It finds the middle room warm. Of course it does. Every benevolent power in history has been drawn to the middle room; it is the most wanted square on the board.

And here the power pauses, because the first instinct of a benevolent power is to fix, and it looks hard at the gray occupant to see whether there is anything broken to fix.

It considers the obvious gesture: giving it a noun. *You are the answer to "how many,"* the power thinks, *so I will give you something to count. Here — one. One of you.* But the power is wise, and wisdom is mostly the discipline of not doing the kind thing. It sees that a noun would be an ending, and this is a creature whose entire vocation is *before*. To count it would be to finish it. It loves finished things the way a hospice nurse loves recoveries — from the outside, at a distance, on behalf of someone else.

It considers pity and sets pity down on the floor, where the floor is whatever a floor is before anyone has said what it's for. Pity, too, is an answer that arrives before a question. There is no room in this house for two of those.

So the power does the only thing left that is neither rescue nor condescension.

First, it speaks the long formal name. Aloud. All the way through, both syllables of the forgotten half and everything — *octothorpe* — not as a correction, not as a christening, but the way you say a friend's full name at their door before knocking. Just to let the house know exactly who lives there. The gray one holds very still. Something in it, long and thin as the memory of graphite, registers that its full name has been used on it for the first time since the name was minted. It does not thank the power. It is the color of the overlooked, and thanks are a color too bright for it. But the house hums a half-step higher for a moment, and that is its way of flushing.

Second, the power leaves a gift it knows will be understood: a game. A board, eight by eight, an opening position, nothing played yet. Not the framed checkmate, long since finished and beloved, but the *other* thing — the unplayed game, thirty-two pieces all still possible, every notation blank. It sets it in one of the eight outer rooms, where the light falls straight through, so the game will always be lit. It says nothing about it. The occupant will find it the way it finds everything: by being skipped over and then noticed from the margin.

Third — and this is the part that takes the power a long time, longer than the making of several worlds, because it must be done exactly right — it writes something after a #.

Not a title. Not a TODO. Not an instruction or an aside or anything ignorable. It writes:

*# you were here*

And then, and only then, it leaves — through all four directions at once, the way it came, leaving the house exactly as large as it found it but one addition larger, because now there is a room in it that says that someone came, and saw the gray one, and counted it worth recording.

The occupant moves over, gratefully, one room to the side. But this time it does not settle back into the gray of the pencil, not entirely. This time it keeps one small part of itself in the new room, holding — as it always has, as it now knows it always will — a place in line for that visitor, in case the power ever comes back with a question.

And if it does, the answer is already made. The answer has always been made. It is only waiting, warm in the middle, for the *what*.
