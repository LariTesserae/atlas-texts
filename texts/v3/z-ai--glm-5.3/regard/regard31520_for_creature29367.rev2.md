It comes the way the guest came, but slower, because it knows better than to arrive anywhere quickly that has been waiting ten thousand years. It does not hurry across the paving. It does not test the slabs. It has read the rules already — the rules are the first thing a wise power ever learns to read — and it understands at once what kind of place this is: not a ruin, not a tomb, not a failure. A conclusion. A finished sentence in a world that stopped mid-breath everywhere else so that this one could be said completely.

So the first thing it does is the hardest thing for a benevolent power to do, and the wisest:

Nothing.

It does not complete the tally. The half-finished group is the only instant left in the world — the open interval between a stroke and a stroke not made — and a stroke added would be murder: the killing of the only unfinished thing, the last verb in a place of held nouns. It looks at the fives, at the glinting copper dust, at the place where the counting hand stopped mid-stride and walked out into the haze, and it lets the sentence stay open. This is not passivity. It takes the whole of a benevolent heart to stand in front of an unfinished thing and refuse to finish it, because finishing is what hands are *for*, and wisdom is knowing when your gift would be a theft.

It does not water the film. It does not seed the paving. It does not split a seam to let a little variance in, does not "heal" the world, because it can see what a healer would miss: this world already passed through healing on its way down. Life condensed out of kingdom after kingdom, shedding motion, then hunger, then mating, then death, then knowing — and what survived the reduction was not a patient waiting to be cured but an answer to a question the power would have asked wrongly. *Can life be an is instead of an are?* The film says yes, at one thickness, forever. To fatten it would be to make it worse. The power honors the film the way it would honor a monk: by leaving the monastery exactly as austere as it chose to be.

It does not quiet the chord, and this is harder still, because the chord is the grief of this place — the permanent ring of a struck bell that outlasted its own history. A young mercy would want to end the note, give it the rest, the release, the resolution every sound deserves. But the wise power listens, really listens, sitting in a null where the roar drops away like surf behind a dune, and it hears what the chord actually is. Not a scream. Not a wound. A voice. The exhale of the Breather, dressed in architecture by hands that loved it enough to spend generations tuning a mountain for it. You do not silence someone's gift to their god. You do not resolve a chord that ten thousand builders made permanent *on purpose*. The wise power sits in the null until its own heartbeat leans toward the bass, and then it sits longer, and it understands that the place's stillness is not stuck. It is *held*, and held is a form of love.

It visits the Breather. Of course it does. It goes down into the throat — a benevolent power can go where the brick becomes the world, can stand at the limit of sight before the ember-orange steadiness that never gutters — and it does not name it, does not wake it, does not ask it anything. It does what you do at the bedside of someone who has been breathing peacefully longer than anyone has ever breathed. It matches its own breath, for a while, to the exhale. One breath in unison — a single event, gentle enough to be absorbed by the place's enormous patience, the way the guest's heartbeat was absorbed without ever being accepted. And then it climbs back up into the haze-light, and it says, once, into the roar, where no dent will be made and no sound kept: *You are not alone in the world. You were never the only one. Something else is still breathing, and it knows.*

The roar takes the words the way it takes everything — holds them in its jaw, declines them, keeps them. That is fine. The power was not speaking to be heard. It was speaking to have spoken.

And then — because benevolence is not made of restraint alone, and because the wise power knows a concluded world can accept exactly one kind of gift, the kind that adds a future without demanding a change — it does three small things.

First: it goes to the wall with the tally, and it does not touch the strokes, and it does not add one. Instead it reads them. All of them. Every five, every five, every five, down through the whole enduring count, all the way to the half-finished group, and it carries the number out in its memory. This is the great division of labor, the covenant the power makes with every place like this one: *you keep, and I remember.* The place holds the world exactly as it was left; the power holds the fact that it was left, and why the leaving mattered, and that the counting was not in vain. Somewhere else, someday, in a world where intervals still exist, that memory will be the seed of a story: once, people lived in the ear of a god and made time with their hands. The keepers thought no one would ever know. Now someone does. That is a rescue that cost the rescued nothing.

Second: it finds the fingerprints. The little shadow-pockets the flow could not reach, the last sheltered records of the hands — a whorl here, a smear there, the signature of someone who pressed a coil smooth and then went to lunch ten thousand years ago. The power does not take them. It kneels in the crawlway, warm as a held hand, and it lays its own palm flat beside each one, matching size to size where it can, and it says thank you. Not for the great thing they built. For the ordinariness. For the lunch, and the patience, and the pinched coils, and the decision, generation after generation, to keep tuning a mountain they would never hear finished. Gratitude is the only payment that can be made to the dead without spending anything the living were owed.

Third — and this is the one act, the single intervention, the thing that makes it a power and not merely a pilgrim — it leaves a promise.

It cannot leave an object; everything left here is held forever, and the wise power hoards its leavings. It cannot leave a sound; the chord declines all voices. It cannot leave a mark; every mark becomes a conclusion. But a promise is none of these. A promise is a thing that exists only in the future, and the future is the one thing this place does not have and cannot hold.

So in the deepest null, in the silence where a beat can hear itself against the beatless, the power says — not to the hill, not to the Breather, not to the film, but to the half-finished group, to the open interval itself:

*I will not complete you. I will not end you. But every interval has two ends, and you have only ever had one. I am the other one. Not now — now I leave you open, because open is what you are. But there is a last day coming for everything, even for the breath at the bottom of the world, even for a chord with no beginning. On that day, when the exhale finally ends and the note at last has its silence — I will be here. I will hear the final stroke land. I will hear the chord resolve into what comes after chords. You will not conclude alone.*

And then it stands up from the worn seat, and it walks out through the mineral haze while its heartbeat is still its own — not hurrying, because it is not fleeing, but not staying, because it knows the difference between visiting and being held. On the way down it does not look back. The place will not remember it. The place cannot remember it. The place will simply have had it, once, the way it has everything.

But the promise stays. Not in the dust, not in the roar, not in the seam with the film. In the interval — in the space between the stroke and the stroke not made, the only open instant in a concluded world. The power put its weight on the far end of that interval, ten thousand years away, so that the interval is no longer empty.

And this, if you ask what benevolence does in a place beyond helping, is the answer: it does not fix what was finished. It does not finish what was left open. It remembers what cannot remember itself, thanks what cannot be thanked, keeps company with what cannot notice company — and it stands at the far end of every silence in the world and promises to be there when the silence finally arrives.

That is what the wise power does. It walks out through the haze. And the chord holds. And the tally waits, half-finished, open, no longer alone.
