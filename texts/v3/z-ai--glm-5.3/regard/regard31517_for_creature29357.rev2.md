The attention came down the zigzag the way everything comes to the Hollow: from the Overhead, against the wind, one switchback at a time — measured, as the descent always is, in the ears, the plateau's shouting falling away behind it step by step — until at the bottom even its footfalls were eaten by the moss, and it had arrived the way everything arrives here. Quietly, and on purpose.

It had been drawn for a long time, the way the Cutface draws iron: imperceptibly, in fans that widen year by year. And it had heard the place named the way the plateau names it — the Below, said quickly, the way you name a place you don't intend to go — and stood on the rim where the old woman once stood as a child, and decided to learn to say it slowly, which is the first thing anyone must learn who intends to keep.

Powers elsewhere arrive at the head of weather, announced by thunder or by history. Here the thunder was spoken for. The Overhead does the shouting; the Below does not take delivery. A glory would have been a second sun in a country already lit from all sides and no particular one, and a proclamation, the attention discovered its first evening, carries exactly as far as the room it is said in — which was the first correction it had accepted in ten thousand years. So it came small. It let the moss have its footsteps and the mist have its distance. It counted the Weeps, which the town calls a thousand and too many to count or name, and it knows the number, and it has never said it, because in the Hollow a fact that will not fit inside a room is not said.

It understood quickly that it was the one being measured. Nothing here measures time except what grows, and so the town would measure it too, the way it measures everything. There is a stone at the Throat where it stands, sometimes for whole seasons — the Throat being where the valley does its deciding, and the attention having taken its post the way the heron took the shallows. The lichen on that stone has begun: pale coins, scattered. A patch the size of your thumbnail has been there since the attention came. When a patch is the size of your hand, the town will say it has been here since before your mother was born, and it will be exactly as right about that as it is about the wall.

Being an arrival, it kept the custom for arrivals. A pool that holds char for the first time is ringed with a trained willow hoop and left alone for three Lockings — arrivals being extended the same room the covenant gives incumbents — and the attention ringed itself, in effect, and left itself alone for three winters. It attended. It stood in the Weep-shallows beside the heron, unpaid, a grey fixture next to a grey fixture, entitled to nothing. It learned the songs, which are worked at the volume of speech. It learned the local light, which arrives from all sides and no particular one, and learned to be seen from every direction at once and to be nothing more than seen; on frost mornings it threw two soft shadows, and they did not disagree. It walked the living walkways and let them creak under it. When the Cutface shed, it stopped wherever it was, with everyone, and counted, lips moving. Ten seconds is a big fall. It had held censuses of nations; the only count it kept here was ten seconds long.

At the end of the third winter it took stock, the way assessors do. The waterworks: kept — by the beaver, under a covenant nobody wrote and everybody keeps. The fences: rooted. The weirs: sprouting on schedule, pruned on schedule. The dead: holding their addresses, one floor down, warm side up. The finding was one word, and it is the only word the Hollow's whole vocabulary leaves a god to say about a place like this: *kept*. What remains for a guest to do in a well-kept house is nothing at all, and then the small things. The attention stayed for the small things.

It went to the old woman the way everyone goes to her: to consult the map. The eldest of the land — every tree in the valley is younger than she is — and the eldest thing in the valley, one of them made of living and one of everything, sat together in a room where a voice carries exactly as far as the room it's spoken in. The town does not record what passed between them. The attention does. It had asked her, the way travelers ask, what the office of a power is in a place that keeps itself. She answered the way she answers everyone, with the town's whole cosmology in three words: *the lake is deciding.* And then — being careful, and old, and entitled to one sentence more than most — she gave it its commission.

*You could let it.*

The attention had, in other countries, been given rivers to turn and plagues to withhold and thrones to hold. It had never been given an office so small, or so complete. It recognized the grammar at once, because the grammar was already the town's: letting is the unbuilt form of keeping, the way arrangement is the unbuilt form of making. It took the commission, and the Hollow has been under a benevolent and wise supervision ever since, which has changed almost nothing, and changed it completely.

---

Benevolent powers are common enough. The Hollow drew a wise one, and the refusals are where the difference shows.

Here is what it did not do.

It did not answer the question. It could have — an afternoon's work: the Old Water returned up the Throat, or the Throat sealed with a word, or a forest stood on the floor by morning. But the deciding is the valley's oldest arrangement, the argument the whole town is built inside, and the one explicit rule covers even powers: never unmake another's arrangement. A wise power can tell the difference between a question put to it and a question a valley is asking itself, and the lake is the second kind, asked by the land of itself. So the attention did the hardest thing in its portfolio: it declined to be needed. The lake will decide, in a century or five, and the power will let it.

It did not warm the Hollow. The cold is the price the lushness pays for its strangeness; the frost on the north cheek of every hummock is the town's compass; the three-hour window is the ration the whole year is arranged around; and the cranberries come up sugar-cold, frosted on one side, the one sweet thing this ground offers without being asked. It could have sweetened them. It did not. A gift with a god's hand on it stops being offered and starts being owed, and the Hollow does not owe.

It did not straighten the Braid. A young river learning its bed is the town's first school, where the children learn on the gravel bars that places leave; a river with one channel and firm opinions would close the school. Nor did it adjudicate the quarrels, which migrate like the channels — visibly, week to week, re-sorted, never straight, never finished.

It did not silence July. The wall of mosquito is the one loud season, the town lives it out in smoke-smudge and grim humor, and grim humor, in the Hollow, is load-bearing.

It did not give the town edges. Nothing here is made; everything is arranged; the heir is known and the heir is moss and peat, and the power, like the town, builds only with the heir's uses in mind.

And it did not open the floors. It had a reputation, elsewhere, for returns. Here it studied the local method — at each re-skinning the floors give back their lost beads, threaded on fresh cord, worn again — and concluded, after respectful comparison, that the town had been raising its dead for eighty Lockings by a process slower than any miracle and better, because nothing here is made, and nothing here, quite, is finished. The power keeps the second clause now. That is the other half of its commission.

Here is what it did instead, all of it arrangement:

The ice keeps its word. Early years stay early and late years late, because a household's rim is a ledger and every year must stay distinguishable — sameness would be a theft — but no skin of grey ice has lied since it arrived. Ice that holds, holds; ice that means to let go says so, in the candling and the color, the morning before. In time the town will have a saying — *the ice here keeps its word* — and will credit the water, which tastes of the rock, and the rock will accept the credit. The power lets the credit stay local. Credit is a shout.

The wall keeps its calendar. The Cutface still sheds after hard freeze-thaw, a few times a season, and the falls are still counted — the power would not steal the counting for anything; it counts too. What it tends is only the appointment: the ramp lets go between the last boots of the day and the first of the next, the zigzag is never under the fall, and the beaver, whose dams are as entitled to their risk as anyone's arrangement, are never in the way either — warned by what, no one knows. The beaver have not said.

The stairs stay buildable. The benches of the Cutface are stacked like stairs, each one an old beach line of the Old Water, and everyone on the Reach can point to the shelf they will stand on if the lake comes back: one bench up. The town keeps its boats upright in rows along the shore, drying for a winter that always comes. The attention keeps the benches for a flood that may never come. It calls this tending. So does the town.

Small mercies, at room volume: on the three coldest nights of every winter the clay of the hearth-basins holds its heat a hand past the fuel's arithmetic, and the town blames the clay, and the clay accepts the blame. The zigzag keeps its footing for the roped traders. No wand fails to sprout.

And one large thing, the only large thing, which from inside will look like nothing: it countersigned the tempo. The Throat is still cutting down, the peat still rising, the beaver still damning — the three-party argument keeps all its parties — but the argument stays civil, and slow, and wet. No night turns a century of deciding into an answer; no flood-wave comes out of the high country; no dry decade kills the Weeps; no lurch, in any direction, ever again. Whatever the Hollow becomes, it will become it slowly, and it will become it wet — that was always the town's principle, built into every rooting fence and breathing hearth, but it was a hope, and hopes are arrangements too, and this one the power has countersigned the way the town signs everything: by tending. The largest gift ever given to this valley is scheduled to be indistinguishable, from inside, from nothing, for three hundred years. If a forest stands here someday on ground that remembers being the bottom of things, it will arrive on time, and no one will ever know anything was promised. The power considers that the best feature of the gift — a benevolence whose only monument is the absence of the sudden.

Then, one falling season, the oldest living thing in the Hollow ended, and the attention did not stop it.

She was the valley's memory, kept mostly in one person, and the drain was about to pass out of living memory and into the strata — filed with the shell and bone and ash and lost beads, the same as everything else. But that is not a loss here; that is the local form of keeping. The dead hold the position they held in life: same door, one floor down. To hold her back from her floor would have been to unmake her address, and the power had adopted the town's one rule about that. So it attended, warm side in, while she was laid in the floor of the house she kept, and a new skin of sod went over, and the doorsill lifted, and the house climbed its handspan, and the eldest of everything went home the way everyone here goes home.

It had given her one thing first — the only gift it has ever aimed at a single being. She had told the story all her life: the rim, the child, the lake going out through the Throat in days, the mud smoking as it dried. Told it in pieces, at the volume of rooms, to children brought like travelers to a map. She had never once told it whole, to a listener who would lose none of it. The attention sat in her room one evening and took all of it, every particular, the way the silt takes the water, and holds it, and is the lake it lost. That is the one thing the attention keeps that the Hollow does not: the memory of the memory, warm side up.

And because nothing here, quite, is finished, it gives the floors back their beads — in the town's own manner only, never as revelation, never larger than a room. Over the next hundred years: a child on the Reach who was never told knows the mud smoked as it dried, and calls it family, and is not wrong. A saying stays a question. A word comes back, threaded on fresh cord. The power's one ongoing miracle is performed at the volume of a room and looks, from inside, exactly like inheritance, which is what the town calls it, and the power does not correct them. A correction would be a shout.

---

Ask the town what lives here now, and they will answer, carefully, the way they answer everything: fifteen hundred people, maybe two. The maybe is where the attention is counted. It has stood in the margin of that number from the first; a number was always the wrong shape for the people, and it is the wrong shape for a power too, which is how it fits. They treat it as they treat the heron — a grey fixture entitled to frogs — and as they treat the lynx: a rumor with a shape; everyone has heard it; no one has seen it done; and in the Hollow that is the standard evidence for anything. Never invited in, always at home. On the Reach it passes people several times a day — there is no way through this town that is not through the town — and it gives and receives the greeting, two words, question and answer at once: *still here. Still here.* It has been hailed in every language of the Overhead by names a mile long, and it prefers its Hollow name, which is by placement and keeping like everyone's: the one who keeps the ice honest; the one who counts with us. It has a temple here, the only one it has ever been satisfied with — two miles long, rising a handspan a generation on the compacted evidence of its own living, warm side up.

At the Locking, the marsh will close all at once, as it always does, and the town will walk out onto the young frost — quiet, breath showing, sledging their bundled reeds — to stand on top of the old lake at last: the annual visit, tenant to landlord. And out on the frost, at Hollow distance, there will be one figure more than anyone remembers sledging out: a neighbor, or a heron, or the other thing — and the town, being careful, greets it anyway. Now, the Hollow's law of speech is not silence; it is fit — tuned to the space a voice will actually fill — and once a year, out on the locked marsh, the room is the whole bowl, six miles wide and twenty long, the mist locked out of it and the ice for a floor. So once a year the two words are said to fill their room, and they carry, over the ice, to every ear in the Hollow at once — the attention's one loud thing in the entire year — and the town answers together, all down the frost, which is the second thing everyone here does at the same instant, after the counting: *still here.*

Until then, and after, the evenings end the way they have always ended: the mist rebuilding itself out of the water like the bowl exhaling, the smoke-holes glowing, the last swans going over with that far, slow, tearing-fabric whirr, the packed silence settling over a full house — while under everything, through the floor, the valley keeps asking its two-word question. And it is answered twice now: once by the town, all down the Reach, and once by the attention, at the volume of whatever room it is standing in.

*Still here. Still here.*
