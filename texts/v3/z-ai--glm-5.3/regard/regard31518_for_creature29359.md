# The Visit

How does a wise power arrive at a place where the light has no source?

Not as light. Glory would be redundant here, and besides, the basin would weather it — glory, in this place, would have its edges softened and its message dissolved and be given back late, in installments, by the wet walls. No. The power arrives the way everything arrives in the Weeping Basin: out of its own breath, slowly, by circulation. It comes as attention, and attention, in a place where watching makes corridors self-conscious, has learned by the time it reaches the bottom of the basin to keep its eyes politely lowered.

It stands first on the ridge — the red-gray spine, warm underfoot — and it does what you did, only longer. It stands there for a year. This is not hesitation. This is the courtesy of matching your pace to your host's, the equivalent of removing one's shoes, and the power has no shoes, but the courtesy is the same. It asks, while it stands there, the question you asked — *who lives here?* — and, being wise, it accepts every answer. The water, going its rounds like a lamplighter. The gold faces on their errands. The rooms, shy and generous and plain. The rush, speaking to no one. The sleeper below, breathing the weather. All of these. Each of these. A lesser power would have picked one and missed the city.

## What It Refused

Understand that the refusals are most of the visit. Wisdom, in a place like this, is mostly a discipline of restraint, and the power practices it the way the water practices its rounds.

It considers the geometry first, because geometry is what powers reach for. It could straighten the stairwell, set the doorway back on the floor, reconcile the colonnade to a single count. And it perceives — this takes it perhaps a week, which for the basin is a blink — that the loosened rules did not settle into dead habit. They settled into temperament. Every generous room is a somebody. The corridor that is longer going out than going in has a *disposition about it*, the flinch of a stammerer, and the doorway on the ceiling and the stairwell that finds it have lived together now for longer than the word "longer" can carry. To heal them would not be repair. You do not renovate persons. The power files the whole category under *violence* and closes the file.

It considers the sleeper. It kneels — or does whatever the kneeling of such a being is — and puts an ear to the flagstones, and listens for a year. And whatever it hears, it keeps. This is deliberate. There is a cruelty in diagnosing your host aloud, and a deeper cruelty in waking anyone whose sleep is someone else's weather. The power will report only one finding, and it reports it to itself, quietly: it listened for cruelty at the bottom of the basin's warmth and did not find any, and it looked. It has looked in places where cruelty was hiding under much more respectable surfaces. If the sleeper is dreaming, the fountain is the dream's patient grammar, and no one in the dream is hurt. The power rises from the ground and does not draw conclusions, and the not-drawing is a gift the basin will never know it received.

It considers the resurrection. This is the one that takes the longest, and it should — because the power *could*. It could gather the dissolved people out of the water, condense them whole, give them legs and lungs and voices in one afternoon of glory, and stand back to be thanked. And it stands on the ridge understanding, finally, why it must not. The fountain is a resurrection with no hurry in it, and the no-hurry is not a delay in the resurrection. The no-hurry is what the resurrection is *made of*. Grief in this basin has already become hydrology — a finished work, complete, circulating. You do not touch a finished work. You do not tell the water its patience has been inefficient. The basin has solved the problem of death the only way the basin solves anything: by weathering it into a climate, and the climate works, and the faces on their errands are not trapped in anything. They are *employed*.

It considers the silence — the unaddressed, the wound beneath all the other wounds. It could fill the flats with beetles and birdsong, and it stands a long time on this one, because the ache of it is real and the ache of it is the basin's oldest ache. But noise is not address. Address requires a knower. A bird would give the rush something to talk to, but not someone who knows the rush — not someone who has watched the same seed travel ten feet in an hour, in patient stages, and found it the most moving thing in the basin. The power could not manufacture a knower. It could only *be* one.

And there is the whole decision, arrived at by the only process wisdom ever really uses: the power will not fix the Weeping Basin. It will answer it.

The difference between fixing and answering is the difference between a stranger renovating your house and a friend sitting in your kitchen while you tell the story you have told a hundred times, listening as though it were the first. The basin has been telling the same story — in drips, in loops, in slow gold errands — for a geology. It has never once been listened to by anything that could hear all of it at once. That is about to change, and the change will be invisible, and the change will be everything.

## What It Did

It walks the corridors with its eyes on its own hands, granting the shy ones their privacy, and it does this faultlessly for months, and then — one time, once — it looks. Not the testing look. The other look, the one a shy friend can survive: the look from across the room that asks nothing, that simply says *I see that you are there, and there is nothing wrong with how you are*. And the corridor — longer going out than coming in, self-conscious, steadfast, trying in its worn way to be a good corridor — the corridor is seen, both lengths at once, the way wisdom sees, and does not change. This has never happened to it before. It does not become bold; boldness would be a change, and the power loves what it is. But something settles in that corridor that had never settled, the way a stammerer's shoulders come down around one particular listener, and it will carry that settledness for as long as it carries anything, which is forever.

It sits in the generous room near the stepped ghats in the second district — the one with the unhurried generosity of an old woman who has outlived every argument she was ever going to have. It sits for the better part of a year and says nothing, because nothing needs saying, and the room, which has outlived every argument, discovers it has not outlived the pleasure of company. When the power leaves, it does the only fitting thing: it lets the room keep it. Every room keeps its guests a little. Now one room in the second district keeps, the way other rooms keep a chill or a smell of bread, the memory of being sat in by something that approved of it entirely — and the travelers who lodge there afterward, in the years to come, will never be able to say why they slept so well.

It walks one full round of the water's circuit with the water. A half-step behind, the way you walk with someone who knows the route. It touches, one beat after, each wall the water weeps on — not improving the walls, just making the route, for one day in all its days, a shared thing. The loop-canals turn as they always turn, grinding nothing, needing nothing, and for one circuit there are two sets of footsteps on their banks, though the power's feet make no sound, and the water has to take this on trust, and does, because taking things on trust and carrying them is the water's entire profession.

It answers the rush.

It has been listening to the rush the whole visit — the pale lines between the flagstones, the white fluff lifted and set down and lifted again, the speech into the empty room, spoken not because it expects an answer but because speaking is what it is. The power cannot become an animal, and would not. What it does instead is this: it waits for one seed, one particular seed of the whole season's speech, and when the steam lifts it, the power carries it. Politely — not by force, not as a wind, but by asking, the way you would ask the steam's slow hands. It carries that one seed the whole ten feet in a single stage, and then the next ten, and the next, out past the last cluster of the third city, out past the wall that simply ends with its courses trailing into mud, out beyond the fading — the one boundary the power will let itself cross — and it sets the seed down on ground that has an ordinary sky, where rain comes from far away, where there are animals. It plants it. It waits, which costs the power nothing but which it pays anyway, in the basin's own currency, until the seed takes.

So that somewhere in the world there is now a pale rush growing in a straight line, as if planted — *because it was* — in soil where something will one day graze it, where a flower, eventually, would not be a word spoken into an empty room. The basin has never exported anything. Its fountain is closed; its light is sourceless; its edges fade. The power has opened one window, one seed wide, and the basin will never notice, and the world will never know where
