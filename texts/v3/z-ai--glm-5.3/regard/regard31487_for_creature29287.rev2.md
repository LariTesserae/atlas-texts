The first thing the power did was stop, and look, and make sure it had understood what it was looking at.

This matters more than it sounds. A benevolent power with a habit of intervening has, over the long history of such powers, blessed countless places into ruin — rivers straightened into canals, wolves struck from valleys to make the deer safe, deserts watered into farmland and then into salt. Wisdom's first lesson is that an ecosystem is not a machine with a flaw to fix. It is a sentence already complete, and the only kind thing you can do to a complete sentence is read it.

So the power read it.

It read the inverted density — the kilometers of ringing emptiness that make the oases mean something — and did not fill them. It was tempted. It could see, effortlessly, how to seed the dark passages with luminous fungus, how to thread warm springs through the cold floor, how to make the whole tube bloom. And it saw, just as effortlessly, that every bloom would be a subtraction. The emptiness and the crowd define each other. A power that removed the emptiness would remove the definition. The tube would become merely crowded, which is a different and lesser thing than overwhelming-in-the-midst-of-silence.

It read the bats and understood them as the tube's diaphragm — the nightly exhale, the dawn inhale, the slow metabolism that taxes a hundred square kilometers of plain and pays it all back down as warmth and grain. It noted, with something the power would not have called affection but an outside observer might have, that this was an economy older than most civilizations and running perfectly on no currency at all. It left it alone.

It read the snake, and this is where the power paused longest. Because benevolence, unpracticed, always wants to save the fallen bats. It wants to heal the wing-torn, catch the fumbling, abolish the unlucky. The power was wise enough to see that the snake is not an enemy of the parliament. It is the parliament's punctuation. It is how a place with no seasons, no change, no arrow of time, still manages to have *events*. The fumbled bat and the waiting snake are the closest thing this ecosystem has to narrative — and to remove the snake would be to remove not cruelty but consequence, and consequence is what keeps a million free-tails good fliers. The world above, generously dangerous, is the sieve that built the colony. The power let the period stand at the end of the sentence.

It read the beetle cone, hot and self-made in the cold country, the only thing down there that generates novelty — and did nothing. It read the drip-holes, each pond filling every eleven seconds since the rock cooled, and did nothing. It read the crickets holding their eternal pitch, and did nothing.

But *nothing* is not quite what it did. This is the part worth recording.

A benevolent power, having truly seen a place, cannot become entirely invisible to it. Attention of that magnitude is a physical fact. So the power did what wise attention does: it became an extremely small participant instead of a large one.

It went to the skylight with the fern collar and the rope-grooved lip, and it looked at the one arrow of time in the whole system — the cone deepening a hair's width a year, the chert flake being swallowed grain by grain by beetles — and it made the smallest possible promise: it would not let the arrow be *broken*. The tube was young, an infant in rock terms, and the world above it was not. Volcanoes are patient; plains are not; a single new flow, a single collapsed entrance, a single century of drought on the plain could end a parliament that had never once adjourned. The power could not and would not stop the volcano — the volcano wrote the tube in the first place; silencing it would be defacing the author — but it could, and did, quietly hold open the doors. It marked the skylights in some register that has no name, so that if ever a collapse came, the breathing-holes of the country would be remembered by something that could reopen them. Not a rescue. A continuity of address.

It did one more thing, and this was the most intervention it allowed itself anywhere.

It noticed the harvesters — the ones with baskets and resin torches who take from the apron and leave only wear. The power could see, the way powers see, that they were inside the system already, honestly, taking at the rate the cone could spare, their rope-groove part of the cave's geology now, their soot-ghosts on the ceiling like breath on glass. They were neither threat nor guest. They were a species of the cave. And so the power's benevolence toward them took the only form that wouldn't warp anything: it arranged, gently, that they would always know this. That their memory of restraint would outlast their reasons for it. A drought generation that might otherwise have dug into the cone's hot heart — killing the country to feed their children — would find, somehow, in the old stories, the sentence that says: *take only the apron, and the cave feeds your grandchildren's grandchildren.* It did not enforce this. Enforcement is for powers that have run out of wisdom. It only made sure the sentence was never lost, the way the drip never loses its landing-spot.

And then it did the last thing, which is the first thing wise powers do and the last thing anyone expects of them, because it looks so much like doing nothing.

It stayed to watch the dusk.

It felt the hour before it heard it — the murmur thickening, the thatch beginning to shiver — and it stood at the edge of the skylight as the ceiling opened from beneath like a hive turned sideways and the mountain exhaled its millions in a spiral that took an hour and sounded like the world turning over. It counted them the way you would count the heartbeat of someone you love — not to learn a number, but because counting is a way of staying in contact with the fact that it beats.

And when the last of them was out over the dark plain, and the tube had settled back into its long dim murmur — the crickets, the frying beetles, the metronome drips landing where they have always landed — the power did what any genuinely benevolent and genuinely wise power does in the presence of something already complete.

It left the sentence as it found it.

It kept only one small change, so small that no beetle will ever process it and no bat will ever echo off it:

The place was no longer unwitnessed.

Somewhere in the register that has no name, next to the skylights, there is now a mark that means: *this is running at one speed, as it has since the rock cooled, and it is being listened to by something that will not interfere, and does not need to, and did not come to improve it — only to make sure that a parliament that never adjourned never has to adjourn for any reason other than its own, long, slow, geological one.*

That is what the power did.

Which is to say: it read the cave's whole long sentence —

— and left the period exactly where the snake was.
