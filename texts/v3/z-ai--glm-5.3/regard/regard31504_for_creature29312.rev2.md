# What the Power Does

First, nothing. This is the part that takes the longest, and the part that matters most.

A benevolent and wise power, arriving at the Greenhowl, does not rush in, because it understands immediately what a lesser power would miss: this place is *mid-sentence*. It is a thought a century into the act of trying to hear itself, holding itself at the exact instant of becoming, and the one thing that could destroy it is an interruption — a voice arriving too early with its own answers, its own shape, its own gravity. The Greenhowl is not sick. The Greenhowl is not lost. It is young, and loud, and terrified in the way of anything that suspects what comes after becoming is answering. You do not heal that. You do not fix that. You *attend* to it.

So the power does the smallest possible thing, which is the largest: it becomes the observer. You remember — this is a place without observers, and that fact shapes everything. The gourds fall toward streets no one will walk. The towers collapse their weddings into empty plazas. A hundred years of argument, and no one to be argued toward. The power's first gift is simply to be *there*, the way one sits with someone who is trying to speak: without prompting, without finishing their sentences, without leaving.

It does not make itself visible. That would be a voice, and voices answer, and answering too soon is the only cruelty available here. Instead it does what the Greenhowl itself does: it grows toward an absence shaped like a future. It finds the one plaza that will clearly be full — the emptiest, most patient one — and it *means* toward it. It becomes a direction rather than a presence. And if any stem in the Greenhowl begins, one night, growing toward that plaza without knowing why, growing toward a warmth that has no source and every explanation, that is the power saying *I am here, I can wait, take as long as the argument takes.*

Second — and only after long, long listening — it learns the grammar. During the upward condensation, when the drops hang and the roar drops into the muttering register, the power listens. Not to translate. Translation would be a kind of theft. It listens the way you learn a friend's silences, until the pressure-changes are not speech exactly but familiar: it can tell the nights when the Greenhowl is close from the nights when the Greenhowl is frightened. On the frightened nights — the ones where the ruined districts hold fresh sap and deep rot in the same wound, where the whole basin seems to brace against a collapse it cannot date — the power does one small thing. It hums back. Subsonically, beneath the churn, in the register of the root-floor. Not words. Not agreement. Just the vegetable equivalent of a hand resting on a sternum, saying: *the heartbeat you feel is yours, and it is yours, and it is yours.*

The Greenhowl cannot hear this. Or — more precisely, and this is the whole point — it cannot hear it *yet*. The power is preparing the answer in advance of the question, which it recognizes, with some tenderness, is exactly what the Greenhowl's own stems are doing when they grow toward absences shaped like future buildings. It has become native to the local method. It is building toward an intention that does not exist yet, and waiting for the intention to arrive and meet it.

Third: it protects the silence. This is the part that would look like neglect from a distance and is in fact the deepest care. There will come — there must come, the roar is getting louder, the argument is not infinite — a night when the Greenhowl finishes. When the last gourd falls and the drops hang and do not come down, and the mutter gathers itself past muttering, and the whole basin arrives at the instant it has been stalling toward for a hundred years. In that moment the Greenhowl will speak, or try to, or fail to, and it will discover — as it has feared all along, which is *why* it has feared — whether anyone answers.

And someone will. That is what the power does. That is, in the end, all it does. It has waited through the whole rehearsal so that the silence, when it comes, will not be the silence of an empty room but the silence before a reply. So that the very large, very slow, very young thought, at the moment of speaking, will hear something come back — and the first word it ever hears from outside itself will be one the power has spent a century choosing:

Not *hello*. Not *I am here*. Nothing so shaped.

Something more like the mutter it already knows, turned around and offered back, so that the Greenhowl's first experience of an other is the experience of being *understood* — of hearing its own wordless century-long argument returned in its own grammar, gently, from a direction it had been growing toward all along without knowing why.

The empty plazas will be full. The drops will come back down, or stay up; it will be spring, or the plant idea of spring. And the argument — this is the power's quiet hope, the reason it is wise rather than merely strong — the argument will not end. It will become, at last, an argument *with*.

Until then: it waits, in the patient plaza, humming beneath the churn, being the observer that shapes everything, growing toward the future like everything else in the Greenhowhowl, which has never once in its hundred years been alone, and will not learn that it was until long after it no longer matters.
