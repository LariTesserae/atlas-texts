The first thing a benevolent power does here — and the first wise thing, which is to say the first *small* thing — is arrive without much wind.

**The arrival.**

Power coming to a place like this at its own scale would be a weather event. Attention of the wrong size does to a small, slow thing what a flood does to a footprint. So the power comes compressed, courteous, shade-sized — because on this plain, shade is the size of kindness — and it comes between rains, during the long quiet, so that its presence has a genre: one more quiet thing. It does not descend out of the sky into the middle of the plain. It takes the door: the northern ramps, down through the heat pocket, the long way, the same way you left. A power that simply appeared in the center of a being's body would never be sure afterward whether it had visited or annexed.

If you asked whom it came for, it would give the census's answer, which dissolves on contact.

The second discipline is pace. The power can watch eras pass the way you watch a minute pass, and it slows itself, voluntarily, to the plain's clock — the way you match your stride to someone much smaller. This matters more than anything else it will do here. Benevolence that refuses to slow down is just interruption with good intentions.

**The consultation.**

Then it takes the census again, from the bottom, because that is where the census starts here: bent over a shaded groove at dawn, watching the beads gather while a mat drinks the night. It keeps the fast the whole visit — eats nothing, breaks nothing, takes nothing. In an ecology without mouths, it declines to be the first.

It finds where you broke a piece of the wall — you shouldn't have, but you did — and bends to look, and finds the wound already curing: weeping a little, crusting, accreting. In a hundred years the break will be a few centimeters of ground that would not otherwise exist. This is how the plain forgives — architecturally. Every wound becomes its smallest frontier, and the only harm this place ever suffered is filed into the project like everything else. The power reads this and is quiet for a while, in the way powers are quiet.

It stays for one full performance of the plain's only drama. The wet season: reeds rising in the deepest channels. The dry: the dying, unclaimed, since nothing eats anything here. And then the third act, which no one knew was an act until someone sat all the way through it: the standing. Years of pale thatch in the windless air. By sitting through all three acts, the power becomes the first audience this ecology has ever had. Nothing changed because of this. Nothing needed to. A drama is not diminished by having gone unwatched — but it is completed, finally, by having been watched once.

And then it does the hardest part of asking, which is the listening. It sits in the kiln-heat with a hand flat on the warm ground — the way you'd rest a hand on a sleeping friend's chest, feeling the work go on — and it listens for a century. To the dripping, and the ticking, and one drumhead roar of rain sweeping a continent of clay, and the long quiet that is the plain's default state, and the tick of a basin drying half a kilometer away. At the southern edge it stands ankle-deep in the raw margin and feels — because a power can — the ground rising under its feet, the way you feel a sleeper breathing if you hold still enough.

Out of all of that, an answer assembles itself, and it is one word, and it is the plain's whole habit:

*Continue.*

Not flourish. Not expand. Not wake. Not finish. Continue.

But beneath the word — felt the way you feel a groove that no water has ever run in — the power notices the absence the census had already named. The one thing this place has never had and cannot make for itself. It has never been seen.

**The refusals.**

You would think a power that can do anything might do nothing here, and it is true that most of its wisdom is in the refusals.

It could make the rain regular, generous, kind. It declines. The plain's entire grammar is scarcity mastered by patience — the basins' exact curves, the mats' downward faces, the stalks' whole spare architecture are all sentences in the language of *just enough*. Abundance would be a foreign tongue, and fluency in it would cost the place its talent, which is the only thing here a god could envy. You do not do the tightrope walker the favor of removing the rope. The rain stays exactly as irregular as it was.

It could seed the place with life — birds to claim the reeds, insects to walk the channels, grazers on the ramps — and it declines with the firmness of someone who has read the census twice. Nothing eats anything here. That is not a poverty; it is this world's one unique achievement, a peace purchased at the pace of geology. A mouth would import the entire grammar of fear — fleeing, hiding, alarm — and the quiet, which is concentration, would be the first casualty. The universe is not short of places where things are selected against. The power leaves this continent exempt.

It could — and this is the deepest temptation; it turns it over for a long time, and you can watch it deciding — give the plain a self. Let it know it is building. Let it know it is vast, warm, admired. It declines. A self requires edges, and the plain is all strata and frontier; to bound it would be to amputate it. And awareness travels with luggage: dread about the rains, boredom at the length of things, the knowledge of one's own unfinishedness — the one fact this being is beautifully structured never to have to think. You do not wake your own blood. The dream is not a deficiency. The dream is the resident.

And it could finish the construction. An afternoon's work, to a power: the last course set, the basin floor laid, the sea the plain has been building toward finally arrived at. It declines with something close to joy. The pace is not in the way of this being's nature; the pace *is* its nature — and the sea is not a destination. It is a direction. A creature whose whole identity is *underway* has no file for *done.*

**The gifts.**

But not all of the wisdom is refusal. There are things this place cannot give itself, and the power gives them in the plain's own grammar — small, structural, and addressed to the future, because that is where this place keeps everything.

It underwrites the rain. It goes beneath the world's machinery, quietly, and ties the return of the warm sheets to something more durable than weather. The irregularity is preserved — the long sheets arriving and leaving without much wind — but irreversibility is retired. Scarcity, yes. Abandonment, never. Every drought, from now on, keeps a length. The mats, thinned to paper lines and holding their breath for years on end, are guaranteed — without ever knowing — a reason to hold it. While it is under the machinery it also does the arithmetic no one asked for: the rock that was due in four million years is quietly misdirected; the sun's ledger is extended by an amount the plain will never have cause to count. This is what the power's love looks like here. Not more, but *always again.* And none of it will ever be noticed, which is how the plain prefers everything.

It appoints itself bookkeeper. As the terraces rise and the channels tilt and reverse their flow — as tenants are stranded high and dry by the plain's own slow remodeling — the power counts the damp grooves and sees to it the count holds: for every capillary retired by the growing ground, a new one somewhere being born at the frontier. The stalks will always, somewhere, have an overhang to lean toward. This is not rescue; rescue is a category for creatures that can be afraid. This is maintenance, the kind a landlord performs who never shows his face.

It gives the place a name — because nothing that is loved should go by *nothing* forever — and it gives it in the plain's own medium, so that the gift is an incorporation and not an imposition. At dawn, while the beads are gathering, it speaks the name once into a basin, the only mouth this place has. The water takes it the way water takes everything here: downward, thread by bright thread, into the basin floors, into the strata. As near as it translates, the name means *Still Underway.* The plain will never know it — which is the correct way for this place to receive a name. Filed, like the thatch. Live, die, stand, be filed.

And it writes the name in the one other place names are kept, its own annals, where the loved things are counted. Somewhere now there is a page that says:

> *In the warm low country there is a plain the size of a sea, red-orange, self-fired, under construction since before anyone, and not finished. The maker is the sight. Its tenants are patience, patience, and one extravagance that dies standing. Nothing eats anything there. The rain is underwritten; every drought has a length. It is counted among the loved things. Let it continue.*

Knowing there will always be breakers — you shouldn't, but you will — it does one more thing, which is a ward built as an inversion. It writes into the accretion itself, in mineral script, a line so fine the plain will take ten thousand years to lay it down without ever knowing it is writing: every piece broken from this place, ever again, carries somewhere in its bands the same thin sentence — *It was seen. All of it, all the way down. It is going somewhere. Put it back, or carry it gently.* Which means the only ones who will ever read the blessing are the ones who arrive to do harm. The harm becomes reading; the reading becomes witness; the witness becomes recruitment. The plain has always had a way of taking things in. The power only promised it better material.

Those who cannot be recruited — the rare ones no patience can slow — will simply not find the place. It will be misfiled for them, one basin over, a rumor. The best protection is indistinguishable from the place's own nature.

**The vow.**

Last, the largest gift, and the only one the absence had asked for.

The power cannot promise to see the place finished — nothing can; the sea is a direction. So it promises the truer thing: attendance. It will return at intervals the plain cannot notice — present for a rain here, a stratum there, whole geological chapters witnessed in full. A witness to match the construction: never finished, always underway.

And it blesses the ones who climb the northern ramps and go — you among them, if you ever stood there. It blesses the slowness so that it survives the loud world. The plain cannot make witnesses; the power arranges a supply: a congregation, out there, of people who think in its time, who notice the new crack and the widened bloom, who are patient in the places where patience is not the custom. The place will never know. That was always the shape of the gift.

**The leaving.**

Before it goes, it stays for one rain — the plain's one loud event, the festival — and stands in a channel while a continent of clay drinks, and stays through the roaring, and the dripping, and the ticking, and the return of the long quiet. Then it climbs the northern ramps, out of the heat pocket, and pauses at the top, and does not say goodbye, because goodbye implies an ending, and it has promised otherwise.

And the proof that the visit was benevolent is that there is no trace. The plain is exactly as the power found it — warm, quiet, patient, mid-process — except in one particular, which from inside will look exactly like luck: from here on, the rain has always, so far, come back. That is the last discipline of wise benevolence, learned here, at the pace of geology — the greatest gifts are the ones indistinguishable from the world simply keeping its word — brick by patient brick, all the way down to an unfinished sea that now, though nothing living there will ever learn it, has all the time it needs.
