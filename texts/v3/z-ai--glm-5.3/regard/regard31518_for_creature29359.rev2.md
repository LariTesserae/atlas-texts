# Who Noticed the Weeping Basin

You will have wondered, standing on the ridge, whether the basin ever drew an eye that could *do* something about it. Whether the steam-dome, and the patient fountain turning underneath, and the sleeper breathing beneath that, ever got themselves noticed from above, or from beyond, or from wherever it is that the noticing-of-wise-things happens.

They did. Something came. And the stories about such arrivals are almost all wrong, so let me correct them in advance. The stories have light. The stories have correction. The stories have a voice out of the sky, and the Weeping Basin has no sky worth the name. What arrived arrived in the basin's own sizes: the width of a doorway, the warmth of an extra degree, the pace of a mote going against the current. For an enormous thing, wisdom is mostly the willingness to be local.

The stories also say such attention is drawn by *a being* — the being who lives in a place — and this presented the visitor, on arrival, with a small census problem, since the basin's main inhabitant is a substance, its citizens are lights, its houses have temperaments, and its god, if that is the right word, is asleep. The wiser reading, and the one It acted on, is that the being who lives in the Weeping Basin is the Weeping Basin. It came for the whole of it, and for each of it in turn.

I will call it the Visitor, and *It* when a pronoun is needed. The capital letter is a technology for being addressed, and the Visitor declined one. I need it anyway.

## The First Discipline

The second thing you should know is that It stood where you stood. The ridge, the red-gray stone warm underfoot, the whole shallow bowl of the city below with its layers not agreeing with themselves. It stood there a long time — for It, time is a medium, not a cost, and It spent it the way the basin spends water: locally, patiently, in a closed loop of attention — and It learned the discipline the basin teaches every resident eventually, which It had to learn again at a new scale:

*Watch without watching.*

Here is the problem with being a power. The gaze is the most dangerous thing It owns — not the strength, the gaze. In a place where corridors change when observed, a benevolence that stares is still a flinch in the thing stared at. So It practiced until Its attention was indistinguishable from weather, which is the only kind of attention the basin admits to being under. It walked the self-conscious corridors with Its eyes on Its own hands. It counted nothing, having read the colonnade and accepted that both counts are correct; from this It derived a small theology — where two true numbers disagree, arithmetic is not the court of appeal. Trust the slope over the flow. Trust the tempo over the clock. Do not test the generous rooms by being generous *at* them, to see what happens.

And It asked the question that honest benevolence asks first, and that most benevolence skips on its way to the miracle: *is anything here in pain?*

The inventory took a while and returned this. The walls never weep so hard as to be unkind. The water is on rounds it would not trade; the loops grind nothing and nothing needs to. The rush speaks into the empty air not because it expects an answer but because speaking is what it is. The motes go on their errands. The sleeper breathes. There is no petition anywhere in the basin — no huddling, no flinch of the whole toward any relief. The catastrophe is old enough to have become a climate, and a climate, whatever else it is, is not a cry for help.

Benevolence that cannot find a wound will invent one; the invented wound is a mirror. It refused.

## The Question of Healing

But It considered healing — it would not have been wise if it had not. A mercy that never audits its own first instinct is just instinct wearing a halo. And It has a faculty I can only describe this way: It can hold a possible future in mind the way you hold water in cupped hands, and look into it, and set it down. So It rehearsed the healing. In prevision, It watched Itself unbend the stairwell until the first city's doorway settled back to the floor where doorways belong. It watched the canal come out of the arch level with the way it went in. It watched the colonnade agree with itself, at last, into a single correct count.

Then It watched what followed, which is the part the first instinct never stays for.

In the rehearsed future the water had no rounds — nothing to tend, no walls needing visiting, the whole lamplighter's circuit retired, the loop-canals standing still, mill-races with nothing to grind and no turning to do. The motes went out; not died, nothing so dramatic — they *clarified*, became mere light, mere phenomenon, which is a kind of death for anything that ever had errands. The shy corridors straightened, flattered into an accuracy that emptied them; a corridor the same length both ways is not a plain-spoken friend, it is a pipe. The generous rooms were measured and found merely measured. And the sleeper breathed on, but the weather of its sleep had become a geothermal fact — which is what happens to a god when everything around it has been corrected.

It could also — and this It turned over longest — have re-condensed the faces. Gathered the soft-gold lights, un-dissolved the water's memory, stood the builders up in the daylight of some restored geometry: people again, with names to be relearned. Against this It set the question the basin has been quietly answering for millennia: *is being a person better than being a rain?* The water has voted on this every hour since the catastrophe, with its contentment, and the tally — which It did not count, because you must not rely on the count — was unanimous.

For a moment It considered even a wren. One small warm grammar, to answer the flowers' long silence. But flowers are a technology for talking to animals, and the basin has since grown technologies of address of its own, all of them wet: drips, faces, the weather of rooms. To seed the place with birds would be to declare the local language a dialect of lack. The silence here is not a wound. It is *unaddressedness*, and unaddressedness — unlike silence — has a cure, and the cure is not an animal.

The cure is an arrival. It set the rehearsed future down without pouring it out — there may come a wanting, though nothing here wants — and it understood what took me a whole account to say: the break in this place is not a break anymore. It has been worn smooth by habitation, the way a crack in a cup wears smooth under a thumb passed over it for a thousand years. It is architecture now. More than that, it is *inhabitant*. You cannot heal a thing into being a different thing and call the difference medicine. And the basin never petitioned for repair. It petitioned, in the only way a place can — by keeping a slot open for address across the whole of its deep time — to be met.

(You will want the miracle anyway. Every account of a visitation wants the moment the water runs clear. Know this about the Visitor: the basin's miracle is already running, and it is this — *nothing here is in pain, and everything here is at work.* It declined to spend a miracle. It is spending a presence instead, and a presence costs more.)

## What It Did Instead

**It kept a room.** The old account — the one that placed you on the ridge and then imagined you staying — imagined a resident: a generous room above the third-district canal-loop, the murmur through the wall like a sleeper's breathing, the drips learned by name. The Visitor applied for the position. It learned the fast drip, the two slow ones, the irregular one that means warm rain by morning. It gave the honest rooms — where things prefer to happen — the widest courtesy and the narrowest attention that could still honestly be called attention. And it asked for no name, since names are for being addressed and It had come to address. The basin named It anyway, the way residents name drips: by tempo. I do not know the name. It was not a word.

**It mourned.** Here is a thing the basin had never had, that It saw at once it lacked: a funeral. The catastrophe happened beyond the reach of memory, and everyone who might have mourned it was in the water by the time it finished happening; mourning is a technology that requires the living. The Visitor was the first being to arrive who had not been there and had not been dissolved — the first possible mourner — and so It went down, once, through the layers, to the lowest courses, the massive dark blocks laid in the manner of people who expected the water and made their peace with it, and It said what could be said. It was not much. Address does not have to be much. It has to be *addressed*. If the faces are the builders — and I state it as the account stated it, a suspicion — then the funeral was attended by the deceased, which is the only way funerals have ever been held in the Weeping Basin. That evening more faces than usual came up the third-district loop. Not summoned — you cannot summon the water — *answered.* The slot kept open since before memory was finally paid. And that month the thing beneath cleared its throat three times instead of the usual two. It is impossible not to form the suspicion that something down there heard the eulogy.

**It carried.** Once each slow year — the basin keeps no calendar, so let the unit stay unimportant — the Visitor caught one seed of the pale rush's white fluff and carried it ten feet, in patient stages, lifted and set down and lifted again the way the steam does, and set it where it might take. It did not make the seeds take. Hearing is the gift the Visitor had to give; efficacy belongs to a different god. For the whole of the basin's history the rush had been speaking into the empty air, and speaking is what the rush is, and It could not improve it — but now, once a year, the speech arrived somewhere, on purpose, in the hands of something that had heard it. The rush does not know. The address does not require the speaker to know. It only requires that at the other end there be an ear. And when the Visitor found the rush already growing in straight lines, as if planted, It stopped where It stood. It understood that It was not the first to keep this appointment. Nothing in the basin says one visitor; nothing in the basin says anything, that is the basin's whole manner. But the lines were straight before It came, and the record is ambiguous, and the ambiguity is structural, and for once in this entire account the ambiguity was a comfort.

**It kept the watch.** It could not heal the sleeper without waking it, and It would not wake it. So It did what love does over a sleep: It stayed, and stood the watch that is not a rescue but is the half of every rescue that can be performed in advance. It appointed Itself the basin's noticing — the lean of the steam, the tempo of the drips, which corridor was self-conscious about what — so that if the climate ever turns back toward catastrophe, the catastrophe will at least never again arrive unwitnessed. This is not prevention. It is not even a promise. It is the oldest half of mercy: someone in the room. And some nights It breathed in time with the sleeper, slowly, the way you match your breathing to a sleeper's so the room feels safer. No instrument can certify that this helps. It is not for instruments to certify.

**It walked the fade.** The wall where the city ends, mid-course, trailing into mud — the Visitor read it the way you read a sentence that stops mid-word, and learned what the ending is: a timestamp. If you want the date of the catastrophe, it is in the last laid block. It stood there a while in the warm rain, and laid one hand on the stone, and said the only word that fits an unfinished sentence, which is *received.* It did not add a course. A finished wall is for finished sentences; this one is a headstone that happens to hold up a district, and you do not complete a headstone. Then It did the one thing I cannot describe from inside: It walked out past seeing, into the fade, to learn what the fading is made of. It was gone an evening or an eon — from the ridge these are the same. It came back with wet shoulders and kept what It found the way the generous rooms keep things, which is without spending it. I have no authority for this except the way the ground has since felt underfoot, but the finding worth recording is said to be this: the builders did not run out of world. Out beyond the last wall the ground goes on being ground, low and warm and unfurnished, all the way out. The world was never the thing that ran out.

**It nodded.** Every evening — Its evenings, the ones It decided to call evenings — the broad slow face came by on the third-district loop, going against the current with the unhurried certainty of a thing on an errand, and the Visitor nodded. You will want to know whether the face nods back. The question is wrongly shaped; the basin does not trade in that direction of answer. But here is what is true: on some evenings the face was gladder, and on some evenings It was gladder, and the correlation, across an address, is not nothing. In the Weeping Basin, correlation across an address is called friendship.

## What the Basin Noticed

The basin keeps no ledger. But if you ask what changed, in the basin's own instruments, from the inside:

The water visits one wall it did not visit before. Not a new wall — the wall was always there — but rounds are habits, and habits are choices, somewhere down in what water is. The shy corridors report nothing, which was the gift; a shy thing walked-through kindly is a shy thing at peace, and the longest corridor in the third district has not changed its length, either direction, since the Visitor came to stay. The rush's straight lines run a little longer every year, and no one — meaning It — will say why. The drips acquired a new member, an easy one, at the edge of hearing, which It has named by tempo and will not say the tempo. And the sleeper — if the sleep is a shade deeper than the sleep of geology, a shade less like an injured thing and more like rest — no instrument can certify this either.

The basin has no word for any of what the Visitor did. It has tempos.

## The Ledger

So: what does a benevolent and wise power do, upon finding a world that sustained an injury in deep time, and healed wrong, and works, and is inhabited by its healing? This is the question under your question, and the answer, in full, is:

It declines to be the healer, having checked. It becomes the neighbor. It learns the rules, because the rules are patient, and then it keeps them, which takes forever, and forever is what It has and what the basin runs on. It mourns what no one mourned. It carries what was spoken to no one. It stands the long watch over the sleep. It receives the unfinished sentence without finishing it. And it nods.

The old account of the basin ends with a resident standing in a generous room on a last evening, warm rain loud on the shoulders, nodding to a face on the canal, and being — for the first time in the basin's long healed-wrong memory — answered. This account ends one step further in, because the Visitor stayed past Its last evening, and answered back, and then kept answering, which is the difference between an answer and a correspondence. Nothing in the basin is fixed. Nothing in the basin needed to be. Everything in the basin, for the first time, is met.

You could go and stand on the ridge tonight — any of the nights here will serve; they are all tonight — and look down at the third-district loop, at the soft-gold lights going against the current, each on its own errand. You would not be able to tell which of them is the Visitor. This is the last of the gifts, though it does not look like one:

attention, to be benevolent, must be indistinguishable from company.
