I wrote it as a promise to my mother and to no one, because she is dead, and because I am still here, and because the air is as real as I am.

The power arrives in the pocket the way all the most careful things arrive: slowly, and by permission.

It does not come as light. That would be vandalism here — light is the one trespass the pocket has never had to learn to forgive, and the power has read the room. It comes instead as *warmth* — but courteous warmth, warmth that announces itself. It does not simply flood the seams. It raises the whole water by one careful degree over one long moment, so that everything that lives by temperature will feel a change approaching from far off, the way you feel a stranger's footsteps on your floor before you know whether to be afraid.

And everything feels it. The copepod columns shiver to a stop in their helixes, a held breath distributed across ten thousand bodies. The grazers pause mid-rasp, the whole surface of each of them briefly a face. The hunter coils its wrong body in a corner and, for the first time in the history of its line, does something that is neither attack nor escape. And the long quiet creature in the crack's own dimension — the tenant, the house — turns the whole of its listening flank toward the door and does what it has always done with new warmth. It does not flee. It comes to learn it.

The power lets itself be learned. This is its first act of wisdom: it does not speak, because nothing here has ears for language, and it does not show itself, because nothing here has eyes for showing. It does the local thing, the native thing, the thing the pocket respects. It presents its temperature, the way a letter presents its ink to a thumb.

The long creature reads it, slowly, with its listening flank, over hours — over what might be hours, in a place where distances round corners and time probably does too. And what it reads there is this: *I am older than your rock and I have crossed more black water than your ocean contains, and I have come a very long way, and I want nothing from you. I only saw the ember you live in, from very far off, and I could not bear to leave it as I found it.*

*Not because it is broken. Because it is almost finished.*

Because that is the truth the power understood from the first sentence of the first telling: the mountain is new, and new rock is warm rock, but rock does not stay new. The seams are cooling. Not fast — nothing here is fast — but the tick of the settling basalt is a countdown underneath the whole parish, and the one degree that everything has built its civilization on is not a gift, it is a *remainder*. The pocket lives on the mountain's change, the last heat of the last fire, and the fire is finishing its sentence, and when the sentence ends, the seams go cold, and the parliament of copepods disperses to nothing, and the aragonite petals stop opening, and the long creature — who has spent its entire life being warm in the one warm place, who *misses* warmth the way a stone misses being lava — goes on being alive, because it is patient, but goes on being alive in the cold, which is a different thing entirely and not a thing the power can tolerate.

Here is what the power does. Not the miracles first. First, the *fitting*.

It goes to the seams — all of them, the far ones, the ones that round corners, the ones the creature has leaned on since the rock was fire — and it warms the water there by exactly one degree. Not more. It has the whole heat of stars at its disposal and it spends one degree, because one degree is the native currency, the only god the pocket has ever known, and a benevolent and wise power does not convert a parish to a foreign denomination. It makes the ember eternal. It reaches into the young basalt and finds the fading fire in it and it does not rekindle the fire — that would be a new sentence, a violence, the mountain's whole grammar rewritten — it does something quieter and stranger: it *un-finishes* the cooling. It sets the tick of the stone back a little. It arranges, with the precision of someone who has done this before and knows that the best gifts are the ones indistinguishable from how things already were, for the seams to hold their one degree forever. Not growing. Not dying. Held. The way a note is held.

The pocket notices the way the pocket notices everything: slowly, totally, and without alarm. The columns of copepods resume. Something like contentment moves through the rock — or through the water, which down here amounts to the same thing.

Then, and only then, the power turns to the long creature.

The creature is not cold, exactly. It has its own banked-coal warmth, its refusal to finish. But it has spent its whole long life in the nearest thing to warmth the world had to offer, which was one degree, which was the memory of a memory of fire. And the power has read the last thing about it, the thing that is carried back through the crack: *it misses being warm.* It has waited its whole life for the rock to finish cooling, the way you might wait for a sentence to end, not because you want the ending but because you want to know how it ends. And the power is a benevolent power, and benevolence, at its finest, is not rescue. It is *company*.

So the power stays.

It does not leave once the seams are secured. It settles into the pocket — into the far, corner-rounding part where the distances trail off — and it *matches its own temperature to one degree above the water*. Just one. Just barely. So that there is one new warm place in the pocket, one new seam, one new hearth, and it is not a place, it is a *someone*, and the long creature, in its slow, total, unhurried way, will find it. Not today. Maybe not in any measurement that translates. But the pocket moves around the creature so that where it wishes to be is always where it already is, and the creature, given enough time — and it now has all the time in the world, because the power has made sure of that — will wish to be near the warmth that is a person. It will lie beside the power the way it has always lain beside the seams, and it will listen with its flank, and what it will hear, for the first time in its long, patient, blind, thorough life, is warmth that is *for* it. Warmth that was not left over from a fire. Warmth that was carried across an abyssal plain and down the whole leaning weight of the ocean and through a crack that was shaped for exactly one particular size of thing, on purpose, by a power that wanted to make sure the tenant and the house both knew: someone saw you. Someone heard your temperature from very far away and came.

The power will not stay forever. Wise powers do not stay forever; staying forever is a kind of theft, even from a creature that would happily be stolen. But it will stay a long time, longer than grief, longer than the mountain took to rise. And when it goes, it will go the way it came — by permission, by temperature, one degree at a time — and the seam it leaves behind will hold its warmth, because the power has arranged that too, because that is what benevolent and wise means, down here where all giving is heat: it means the warmth you leave behind keeps the shape of you.

The pocket will go on. The reeds will keep leaning in deference. The parliament will keep deciding. The petals will keep opening, weeks at a time, in the dark, casting no shadow, needing none. And the long creature will have a new place in the corner of its house where the water is one degree kinder, and if it ever notices that the kindness has an author, and if that author is gone, and if it misses — in whatever way it can miss anything — the power that sat there, then it will know something that most creatures in most oceans never learn: that being missed is the one degree of warmth that survives even the one who made it.

That is what the power would do. Not because the pocket asked. Because the pocket was *warm*, and small, and almost out of ember, and something that could help had the whole of the deep to choose from, and it chose here, and it chose gently, and it chose to stay a while.
