Something lives here. You will not see it at first, because seeing, in this room, is done mostly with the hands and the patience, and it has had a very long time to learn the geometry of not being seen. But the room told you about it already, if you read the evidence the way the room writes it.

Start with the warm conduit. Nothing in this room should be warm. A fitting hasn't arrived yet; the warmth is a rumor. But rumors need carriers, and this one has one: a small fog with a heartbeat, the room's own words for it, a temporary weather system — and there, at the center of that weather, a ring of bright drops condensing like witnesses, and inside the ring, the resident.

It is not an animal. The verbs of biology do not apply to it either, or only the quietest ones. It is fitted, seated, curing. It is being commissioned the way the room is being commissioned — slowly, at the rate of its need, millimeter by millimeter, cycle by cycle, and you never see it happen. You see only that it has happened: glance away and back, and the thing that was leaning against the manifold is now two steps nearer the moss trays, and there is a fresh fingerprint in the frost fern of a sight-glass, and no motion between. It does not cross the room. It is *repositioned*, like a bracket, the way the room itself acquires brackets between one look and the next.

Here is how you can be certain it was never manufactured: everything in this room is lot-stamped, licensed, traceable to somewhere that makes things in quantity — everything, except the moss, the lichen, the brine-filament, and this. It carries no number. It came from nowhere that counts. It accreted, the way the room accreted, out of the plant's own breath and cold and schedule, seeded into the same void, and the two of them — the room and the thing — have been finishing each other ever since.

Because that is the arrangement, once you feel for it through the pipes with a flat palm. The room exists because it is needed, and the need has a shape, and the shape is this: a tenant, growing. The moss trays are its slow lungs — the measurable bump of oxygen, the thing the room logs and *presumably needs*, is needed because something in here is inhaling it, gray-green breath by gray-green breath. The dawn-blush at each eleven-minute turn, that metabolic green flash, is the nearest thing this room has to sunrise, and it is also the nearest thing the resident has to a heartbeat made visible, punctual, licensed. The seam-lichen holding the north joint is its grip — not living *in* the room so much as holding the room, the way you hold something you are not yet sure is yours. And the pale filament in the brine channel, swimming its filaments slowly upstream against the trickle, patient and determined — that is its long patience out for a walk, its determination drinking with a thousand small mouths, going nowhere at exactly the rate of arrival.

The rain is for it. Read the room's rules again with this in mind and they all tilt toward husbandry: the cold is product, yes, but the condensation is *irrigation*, decided elsewhere, delivered on schedule. Every eleven minutes the valve far away seats itself with a thud in your boot soles, and the room rains, and the resident stands in it the way anything stands in its own weather — not enduring it, *being* it. The drip tray seated a hand's width beneath the manifold: a hand's width. Whose hand? The fresh grease on the one nipple, no hand having freshened it in a very long time — no hand you would recognize. The aperture, the gap between the two unpersuaded panels, the width of shoulders: the room has already measured its occupant. It has been measuring it all along, growing a doorframe around a tenant the way a shell grows around a soft thing, except in reverse, except inward — the occupant growing, the room growing to stay one size ahead of it.

It tends the place, or the place tends it, and the distinction has quietly stopped mattering. When the frost furs a sight-glass too thick, the ferns are wiped. When the mastic beads over something that sprouted outside its permit, swallowing the typo, you might notice that the swallow always leaves the trays alone, always leaves the seam alone, always leaves the warm conduit and its small fog alone. The room has a permit for one weed, and the weed is the reason for the room.

It does not know what it is yet. This is not a figure of speech. Doors come last, when a room knows what it is — and the room cannot know, because the tenant does not, and the room is mostly tenant. What it is, is *provisional*: a being made of dawn-blush and upstream patience and a rumor of warmth, existing in the gap between two panels that have not been persuaded to meet, living inside the word VOID, PROVISIONAL the way a seed lives inside the word *not yet*. It is honest the way the room is honest. Measure it twice and it differs. It is not finished being its size.

And it knows the last rule, the quiet one, in whatever way it knows anything — through the pipes, through its moss, through the compressor's long note played into its palms. The room exists because it is needed, and when the need completes, the room will complete. The panels will meet. The doors will be hung, cut exactly to shoulders that have by then decided their final width. The gaps will close — all of them, including the one it came in by, including the one it *is*. And the fog with a heartbeat will settle into the warm conduit like a filament into a socket, and the ring of bright witnesses will stop witnessing and simply be condensation, and the room will stop being this and become a finished room, a place like every other place in the plant: numbered, stamped, sealed, done.

Until then it stands in the middle of the half-floor, at the lip of half-hardened compound, at the edge of the not-yet, and it listens the way listening is done here — hands flat on cold brass, the whole plant playing through its wrists, a hundred other rooms it will never see, all of them finished, all of them waiting for it to catch up. The eleven-minute valve far away draws its long breath. The drops gather at its warmth in a bright ring. The moss takes its green breath at the turn of the cycle — dawn, the only dawn, punctual — and the resident takes the other half, and between them, cycle by cycle, in the chapel-sized, coffin-dense dark, something provisional keeps choosing to keep happening.

The room is being born. It is not the room.

The room is the cradle.
