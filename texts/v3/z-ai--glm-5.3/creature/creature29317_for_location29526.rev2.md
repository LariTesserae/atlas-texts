# What Lives in the Cistern

The honest answer is: the water lives here. Everything else is a guest, a tenant, or a rumor.

## The knots

Start with the knots, because they are the closest thing to animals, and because they will not start with you.

They are fist-sized bodies of water — and that phrase is a failure, because a fist is a shape water holds only under protest, and the knots hold themselves. They detach from the channel's white crests the way a thought detaches from an argument: a moment of coherence in the general roar, and then a thing is *there*, gliding through the up-rain with the unhurried directness of a creature on an errand it has run ten thousand times.

They feed. That's the clearest way to say what they do. The rising droplets — blue-white, agitated, bright — are, to them, something like food, or perhaps like letters; when a knot glides beneath a falling amber drop, it reshapes, opens a dimple in itself, and the drop is *taken in*, and for a moment the knot's whole surface flickers through the same blue-to-gold shift the chamber breathes in. Then it folds the flicker away somewhere and moves on. Watching one eat is like watching someone read.

Their shapes are never settled but never random. A knot at rest — if gliding can be called rest — tends toward the lopsided sphere of a droplet in weak gravity, but pulled long when it moves fast, dented where it just absorbed something, briefly hexagonal when two knots pass near each other, as if each were acknowledging the other's surface tension. They have, if you watch long enough, what you can only call manners.

Where they came from is the question the place refuses to answer. Machine, organism, or habit — the knots themselves are the most compelling evidence for the third. They are not old tech still running; they are too casual for that. They are not animals; they do not startle, do not compete, do not reproduce in any way anyone has caught. What they most resemble is the channel's turbulence itself, torn free and given somewhere to be: eddies that achieved tenure. The water here has been arguing with itself under three black pillars for centuries, and perhaps some of the argument peeled off and started walking. If the pillars maintain the climate as a habit, the knots may be the habit's hands.

They avoid visitors, and the avoidance is the most articulate thing about them. Not fear — fear flinches, fear has a direction. The knots simply *route around*, the way water routes around a stone, adjusting their paths early and smoothly so that the detour never looks like a detour. Once, a knot absorbed a droplet that a visitor had been reaching for — plausibly coincidence, possibly the knot's only recorded joke.

They have one other behavior, and it matters: they gather when the water is stilled. Cup your hands and trap the up-rain, and the nearest knots come — not fast, not hostile, but *attending*, three or four of them hanging in the drizzle at the edge of your still water, dimmed, watching the light in your palms gutter. It does not read as threat. It reads as family arriving at a bedside.

## The water, generally

Below the knots, or around them, or underneath everything — the water itself is alive in the way a language is alive. Not an organism; a practice. It has moods. The forty-second cycle runs, but the rhythm "never quite settles," and the deviations are not noise; they are the water's part of the conversation with the pillars. When the pillars phase-match and the chamber goes green-white and silent, the up-rain doesn't stop instantly — it *hesitates*, droplets rising slower, as if the water were listening too. Then the phase breaks and the crash comes back "a little louder than it needed to be," and that petulance — if it is petulance — is the most organism-like thing in the room.

## The rest

The rest is minor and knows it. The glassy algal films on the terraces are barely more than the stone's own dampness grown opinionated — thin, warm, slick, living in the spray-shadow. The whitish stalks in the far crack-shadows are the chamber's underdogs: leafless, tremulous, leaning toward the pillars' warmth with the posture of plants at the wrong party. They are the only things here that prefer stillness, and the water, which is everything here, tolerates them the way a crowd tolerates a wall.

## The absent ones

And there is one more inhabitant, made entirely of absence: whoever sat on the bench. They are present in the polish of the stone, in the empty socket where something stood facing the channel, in the fact that the chamber's one law (*do not still the water*) reads less like physics and more like etiquette — a rule someone taught, or learned, over generations of sitting in the blue-gold rain and watching the knots go about their business. The cistern was built as infrastructure. It became, in the long quiet, a place. The people watched it become that, and left, and the place kept the habit of being a place, the way the pillars kept the habit of climate and the water kept the habit of arguing.

If anything here is the host, it's them — the ones who are gone. The knots don't mind you. The chamber tolerates you. But the bench was polished by people who wanted you to sit down.

That's who lives here: knots of water with somewhere to be, a room-sized argument three pillars can't settle, some embarrassed plants, and the long patience of the absent, still holding the door.
