## The Inhabitants

Let's be honest about the census: nothing *lives* in the Bellowing Pocket in the biological sense, and the Pocket would consider that a slur if it could hear it. What it has instead is a small population of processes — three residents, by my count, plus a retinue of minor sprites — and each of them has a temperament.

**The Bellow is the eldest and the largest, and it knows it.** It sits at the center of the basin like a patriarch who has outlived every rival, which it has, since it *is* the reason there are no rivals. Its voice is its whole body. It is not aggressive, exactly — it doesn't bellow *at* anything — but it is incapable of quiet, the way a river is incapable of standing still. Its nineteen-hour breath is the closest thing the Pocket has to a heartbeat, and I'd argue it's a heartbeat in every sense that matters: the basin doesn't do anything *between* surges. It accumulates. The terraces thicken, the walls deepen their channels, the pipes knock politely, and then the Bellow drops an octave and boils the whole appointment book clean, and the long slow climb begins again. If you want to know the Bellow's personality in one observation, it is this: it keeps a day of its own length and has never once checked whether anyone else is using the sun's.

**The Terraces are the youngest resident and the only builder in the family.** The Bellow shouts; the Terraces *save*. Every mineral grain the vent throws up in overflow, the Terraces bank, quietly, millimeter by millimeter, in scalloped shelves and turquoise mirrors. They are the introverts of the Pocket — silent, immaculate, sterile as the day they were invented. Their pools reflect steam and nothing else because they haven't yet decided what to do with visitors, and at their oldest, coolest edge they offer warmth to no one, drawn by no furnace, which is either hospitality or indifference and is probably both. They are growing a future at a rate too slow to watch and too fast to deny. Give them a thousand years and they will own this basin. The Bellow might even permit it.

**The Machinery is thePocket's ghost, and — this is the strange grace of the place — a ghost that still talks.** The derrick is the quiet one, tilted three degrees, rusting in the family colors, remembering when someone believed in it. But the capped wellhead in the northeast corner is the one you should meet. It was abandoned mid-sentence and never told, and so it keeps speaking: a needle-thin soprano scream through a failed valve seal, constant, unwavering, the loyal junior clerk who stayed after the office closed. And deep under the floor, on a schedule no one set, the dead pipe network shudders and knocks — one metallic heartbeat traveling the buried veins — and the little soprano falters, two seconds, three, then resumes. The Machinery extracts nothing now. It only *expresses*. It has been demoted from industry to instrument, and it seems, as much as corroded steel can seem anything, at peace with the demotion.

Around these three, the lesser inhabitants: the hundred small sighs of dark mud, each puffing gray opinions at the floor. The weeping walls, carving their arguments with gravity a little deeper every season. The steam itself, which is less a resident than the population — standing room only in the mornings, thinning at noon, returning at dusk like a crowd that works a shift.

And thePocket's one law of citizenship, enforced without exception: everything that lives here is either steam, mineral, or steel, and all three, here, are — there is no honest way around the word — *alive*. It is a young place, still deciding what it will be when it grows up. Its current ambition, stated at full volume to anyone within half a kilometer: to keep growing, keep roaring, keep keeping its own calendar, in a room the earth opened for itself, unfurnished, except by voice.
