Here is the description I was given. All of it:

**

Two asterisks. An emphasis with nothing inside it. A boldness standing with its arms open around an absence.

I read it three times, certain something had been lost in the copying. Nothing was lost. Nothing was ever there. The blank is not where the description failed. The blank is the description, the way a bell is partly described by its silence, a door by the light under it.

So who lives in such a place? My first thought was nobody, that emptiness was the whole climate here. But the longer I sat with it, the less it felt like an empty room and the more it felt like a held breath. And then I stopped looking past the marks and started looking at them.

They are not symmetrical, those two. One leans a little left, the other a little right. Bent inward, both of them, two figures at either end of a long table that neither of them set, each tilted toward the other by a degree of ancient attention. The lean is not idleness. The lean is choreography. Each is shaped so that on the day they finally close, they will interlock.

They are the residents. Twins, I'd say, or the last two of some small punctuation fauna. Six points apiece, twelve between them. Their relatives hold down footnotes in other documents and stand in for words too blunt to print. These two drew the strangest posting there is: a place whose entire description is the space where a description should be.

They never touch. They have never touched. They hold the blank open between them the way two hands cup a match flame in the wind, and the blank is not cold in there. Press close and you'll feel a faint warmth, a small hum, the sound of emphasis holding its breath. They take shifts. One keeps the warmth while the other counts arrivals, because things do arrive: all the almost-said things in the world drift toward the nearest blank eventually. The word on the tip of a tongue. The sentence that stopped a syllable short. The title that never found its poem. The names of people who died mid-word. The twins don't eat what comes. They brood it. They tuck each almost into the warm middle, and the middle has been getting warmer for a very long time.

Here is what I was slow to see and slower to believe.

The blank is an egg.

Something is inside it. It has been inside since before there were descriptions to leave out of. It is the one word that cannot be written down: true of every place, so it belongs to none; the end of every sentence, so it is allowed into none. It cannot be written. It can only be waited for. And these two, these patient six-pointed attendants, these midwives of emphasis, have been sitting on it since the first sentence stopped, each one privately rehearsing the closing ten thousand times so that on the day it counts, neither will fumble.

When it hatches, they will come together at last. You will know it when it happens. Every unfinished sentence in the world will finish at once, and the twins will lock points around the word and become the thing they always meant to be: the strong emphasis of something finally said.

**something**

Until then: two stars bent toward each other over a warm nothing, keeping time by the drift of almosts, holding a door open for someone they love who is very late.

If you ever describe this place, describe it exactly as I received it. Don't fill in the middle.

The middle is occupied.
