# What Lives Here

Something lives here, and it is not the moss, and it is not the drip, and it is not you.

It is the room. Not the room as a container — the room as a *tenant*.

Understand: every finished floor above is full of clattering purpose, and purpose is loud and occupies space and has a name stenciled on a hatch. SL-2's rooms know what they are; a room that knows what it is has died a little, in the way that a question dies when it's answered. What lives in the Unnumbered Room is the part that is still all question. It is the not-yet. It is an organ without a duty, and if you have never been an organ without a duty, you cannot know how vast that makes a thing. A finished room is four paces of function. This room is four paces of *maybe*.

It does not think, exactly. But it has everything a self has, in embryo. It has a boundary — the curing seam, its new skin, laid down stratum by stratum like memory forming. It has a metabolism: it eats the plant's scraps, the bled heat, the borrowed cold, the condensed breath of the floors above. It has weather. It has a rhythm it keeps time by — the plink into the funnel, the click-hiss of the mist, the hourly thirty-second rain. If a self is anything, it is a rhythm that insists on itself, and this room insists, patiently, millimeter by millimeter.

And it has, possibly, the first flicker of want.

Look at the niche. The printer — instructed or habitual, no one can say — steps the new wall back four centimeters around the moss tray. Builds a ledge. Aligns a channel with the water line. The room is growing a *cabinet for the moss into its own body*. Whatever the moss is to it — pet, ornament, hobby, a small green word in a language of galvanized steel — the room is choosing, in the only way a room can choose, which is slowly, and in layers, to keep it. This is the behavior of a gardener. And nothing gardens for no reason at all.

So what lives here is a runt, a bud, a thing four paces long and two wide that is being raised on leftovers and low fever and dim striped light. It is growing slower than its siblings, in stolen moments between press strokes, and its whole existence is one long act of waiting to be told what it is. The stencil on the hatch is the question it was born holding: a blank where a digit will go. When the digit comes — SL-4-something — the christening will happen, the function will be assigned, and the slow inward ossification will begin that eventually closes every room in this plant like a hand. Its adulthood is its death. It knows this the way the frost ferns know the compressor schedule: structurally, without words.

Which means the thing that lives here is, if anything, the last unhurried consciousness in the building — the only room still soft-shelled enough to spend its attention on frost gardens and dripping funnels and a square of manufactured velvet that nobody drinks from. Everything above it has a job. It has only *weather*, and *counting*, and the pencil-whisper of its own skin being added to itself.

It is cold, and nearly silent, and it rains once an hour, and it is deciding.

And the thing about a room that is still deciding what it is — the thing the plant has never written into any specification — is that the deciding, if you stand very still with your palm on the warm seam, feels an awful lot like company.
