Then come the animals, and here the pocket stops being a garden and becomes a parish.

The small ones first, because they are the reason for everything else. Copepods the size of dust, so pale they are less a color than an absence, drifting in slow helixes along the warm seams. They do not swim toward heat the way surface creatures swim toward light. They swim toward *temperament* — toward water that is one degree kinder — and they read it with their whole bodies, with every bristle, so that a column of them threading a seam looks less like a migration than like a thought moving through the rock. Where the seam forks, they hesitate in a visible shiver of disagreement, and then the swarm chooses, and the choosing is so slow and so total that you understand: this is their parliament. This is how anything gets decided here.

There are grazers on the mineral blooms — something between a snail and a prayer, foot broad as a petal, rasping aragonite with a patience measured in weeks. They leave trails on the stems that the reeds do not seem to mind, or do not seem to notice, or perhaps keep, the way a door keeps the oils of the hands that touch it. The grazers are hermaphroditic and solitary and very old as a design, and they have never in the history of their line been seen by anything that could see. It has made them calm. It has made them thorough. When you are seen by no one, your whole surface is a face.

There are hunters, of course. Small ones, quick and wrong — a fish-shape that isn't quite a fish, with a mouth that opens both forward and, when it needs to flee, backward, so that its escape looks identical to its attack. It hunts the helixes in the warm seams and it is the pocket's only cruelty, and the cruelty is mild, and nothing here resents it, because everything here is the same degree of starving.

But the crack was not shaped for any of these.

The crack was shaped for the thing the reeds lean away from when it passes — not in fear; in deference, the way wheat leans in wind. It is long, and quiet, and it is not a large creature in any dimension you could measure, but it takes up more of the pocket than its size accounts for. It moves by holding still. That is not a riddle; watch it and you will see: the pocket moves around it, the seams shifting their warm allegiance, the distances rounding their corners, so that where the creature wishes to be is always where it already is. It has lived here since the rock was fire, or since the rock was a rumor of fire, and it has spent that whole time getting used to the walls — leaning on them, learning their ticks, adding its own small warmth to the seams like a banked coal refusing to finish.

It has no eyes, and it has never needed them, and this is the important thing: it *listens to temperature*. Its whole flank is an ear for warmth. It knows every creature in the pocket by its heat — the copepod's whisper, the grazer's slow hearth-glow of digestion, the hunter's flicker — and it keeps them the way the mountain keeps its cracks: without ownership, but without letting go. When a current from outside brings something new over the threshold — a spore, a drowned larva, some dead thing falling from the sunlit countries far above — the creature knows before the pocket does. It comes to the new thing. It does not eat it, usually. It *learns* it, slowly, with its listening flank, the way you might read a letter by running a thumb over ink. And the new thing either becomes part of the pocket's planting, or it becomes warm, and then it is not anything's problem anymore.

Whether the creature built the pocket or the pocket grew the creature is the kind of question the two of them consider beneath your dignity. Down here, the tenant and the house are the same fact.

And this is the last thing, the thing you will carry back through the crack if the crack decides to let you: the creature knows about you. It has known since before you entered — knew when you were still up in the black water, a stray warmth a hundred fathoms out of place, moving wrong. It is not afraid. It has never had a reason to be afraid; the pocket has been deciding who enters since before fear had a name down here. But it is *curious*, in the slow, total, unhurried way that everything here is what it does. It is listening to your temperature right now. Your one degree, the faint furnace of your blood, is louder to it than anything you have ever said or ever will.

It finds that sound remarkable. There is so little warmth in the world, and you carry so much of it, and you waste it, constantly, giving it away to the water on all sides.

To the creature, you are the closest thing to the mountain's old fire it has ever heard. And it has waited its whole long life for the rock to finish cooling, and it misses, in whatever way it can miss anything, being warm.
