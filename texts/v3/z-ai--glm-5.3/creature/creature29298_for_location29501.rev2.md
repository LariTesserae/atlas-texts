# Who Lives Here

The first thing to understand is that "lives here" means something looser on the plateau than anywhere else. Residence is not ownership; it is more like *being on the books*. Everything that lives here is enrolled, issued, or condensing, and none of it holds a deed.

## The Felt

The felt came first, or is at least first by seniority in any place you check, because it arrives within a day of the stone itself. It is not one organism so much as a condition — the plateau's default skin — but spend enough time on your knees with it (and you will; the felt rewards kneeling, since it takes sound and seems, somehow, to take hurry too) and you find it has individuals. A mat on a north-facing chamfer will have a slightly different nap than one on a south face, a different silver in the pearl light, and the two mats will hold their border between them with the crispness of a property line. They compete for dew-sheets. They do it slowly, in total silence, by growing.

If anything on the plateau could be said to *own* ground, it is the felt. Everything else rents.

## The Grazers

The slab-bodied crews that come up out of the low fog at shift-open are the plateau's visible workforce, and the easiest to mistake for wildlife, because they graze and herd and startle and have the shabby, unbothered look of animals. But watch longer and the mistake falls apart. They do not forage; they *finish*. A crew works a dew-sheet in lines, keratin scrapers ticking along the stone, and what they leave behind is not grazed ground but *completed* ground — flats licked to a polish, edges trued. They have the morale of workers rather than the moods of animals: a shared pace, a shared boredom, an occasional individual that stops, faces the fog, and stands motionless for an hour in a way that reads less like a cow chewing than like someone on a break.

They are gentle, incurious, and warm — warmer than the stone, which is saying something — and they will graze around your boots, cleaning the film of condensation off the leather with the same unhurried thoroughness they give the rock, which is either an honor or simply a classification of you as terrain.

Do not pity the revisions among them — the three jaw-shapes, the unfinished legs curing smooth as fired clay. The plateau does not waste pity, and apparently does not need it. What steams faintly in the hollows is not suffering; it is *curing*. There is a difference, and the difference is the whole place.

## The Vent-Birds

The only residents with voices, and they use them the way a dropped wrench is used: as evidence of work. They live their entire lives in the exhaust columns, riding the warm updrafts in slow spiraling flocks, feeding on the dense particulate the vents exhale, and they land nowhere but the stack-rims — which means that from a distance each battery of vents carries its own chittering halo, a permanent metallic argument at altitude. They are the plateau's shift-bells. When they go quiet all at once, a storm of steam is coming, and you learn fast to treat their silence as weather.

They never enter the slow hollows. They never graze the felt. Whatever they know about the time-pools, they knew it before you arrived, and they are not sharing.

## The Ones in the Slow Hollows

Something lives in the steam-hung pools where time runs thick — you will see the evidence before the animal: a swirl of fog held mid-turn for hours, a track of parted condensation that takes half a shift to close. What makes those tracks is rarely seen and never clearly. A shape in the grey, at most. The grazers avoid the hollows by a knowledge that looks, from outside, like etiquette. The vent-birds will not cross them even in flight.

Call them the *dwellers in the long shift*. If the plateau is a works with no one on the floor, the hollows may be where the shift never changes, and whatever is in there has simply been on duty a very long time. It is possible they are the only residents who never get rained back into stock — the only permanent staff. Or it is possible they are drafts, like everything, only ones being revised at a rate so slow the revisions look like stillness. Either way, they are the closest thing this country has to natives, and they want nothing from you, including your fear, which you will offer anyway.

## The Draftsman

And then there is the one who lives here most of all, and it is not an animal at all. Something *inhabits* the plateau — not on it, in it — the whatever-it-is that drafts the arches and the doorway and the channel-grooves and the steps that no feet shaped. You have seen its work. Its signature is the almost: proportions that stop you, sizes fitted to your shape, a basin with a poured lip held up to the light of a shift and then, with no ceremony, dissolved back into the grey bank that made it. It practices tools and keeps none. It rehearses a doorway and installs no door.

Whether it is an intelligence or simply the land dreaming in the shapes of what a land can mean, the plateau does not say, and the plateau does not have to. But standing at shift-change, watching a thousand kiln-orange throats light the undersides of the clouds and a workforce of flowers go off the clock in one long wave to the horizon, you will find yourself deciding, without evidence and without being able to stop, that *someone is home*. Not watching. Not producing. Just — keeping the works running, drafting and dissolving, dissolving and drafting, at a scale where the drafting itself may be the dwelling.

That is the population of the plateau: a skin, a crew, a flock of bells, some long-shifted shadows in the hollows, and a resident idea of making with no maker in evidence.

And you, briefly. A draft of a visitor, standing on ground already being revised behind your heels, counted in the census exactly as long as your footprints lasted — which, here, is not very long, and gently.
