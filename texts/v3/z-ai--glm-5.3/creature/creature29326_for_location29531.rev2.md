# What Lives Here

## The Makers

Begin with them, because they are the closest thing to neighbors this place has.

They are sparse the way saints in a desert are sparse — one per region, a day's walk apart, each standing in the heat-haze like a surveyor's stake driven into the bronze. Human-shaped, but the shape is a courtesy. Look longer than politeness allows and you notice the details that don't resolve: the way their hammer-worn hands have no calluses, because the calluses would have to be *decided on* and that decision hasn't come up from under the floor yet. The way their faces are less like faces than like very good first drafts of faces — a nose placed where a nose goes, eyes that blink at plausible intervals, and underneath it all a patient indifference to being finished.

They eat, they drink, they sleep with their feet toward the rivet-sound like everyone else. But they eat what the seam-pools show them, and what the seam-pools show them is always their own hands, older, still working. So their meals are quiet, and slightly mournful, and they chew slowly because no one wants to rush that particular image.

What they are is easy to say and hard to hold: they are the parts of the work that were allowed to walk around. Each maker was, presumably, once a pair of hands belonging to the one under the floor — and then the revision came, the great quiet undoing of some attempt or other, and instead of being unscrewed like the panels, these figures were set upright, given a mallet, and told to *tend*. They are tools that outlived their toolhood. They are footnotes granted tenure. They hammer, and the hammering means something, and they do not ask what, because they were made by someone who does not ask what.

They will not speak of each other. This is not secrecy. It is more like the way your left hand does not gossip about your right. They know of one another the way you know of a dream you had as a child — the shape is there, the certainty, and no words have ever been assigned to it.

## The One Under the Floor

Do not picture a person. That is the first mistake, and everyone makes it, kneeling at a weeping seam, staring down at the luminous wrongness, trying to see a face in the scaffolding-viewed-from-below.

There is no face. There is a *revision*.

The one under the floor is whatever it is that cannot stop correcting. It drew the chalk lines and is still drawing them, always ahead of you, always ahead of *itself* — which is the only reason the lines fade when watched: you are seeing a thought mid-thought. It builds a floor and, eleven times now, has found the floor to have been wrong for a thousand years, and has quietly ground the edges smooth and removed it, and started again, and this is not failure. Ask the makers. They'll laugh, not unkindly. *Which one?*

If it lives anywhere, it lives in the gaps. The rectangular absences. The places where rain falls in and never hisses back out — because those are the places it is *reading*, the way you'd put your ear to a wall. Every removed section is a page turned. The world is a manuscript the author keeps alive specifically in order to keep editing it, and the author has concluded, after twelve attempts at the first attempt, that a world is not a thing that gets finished. A world is a thing that gets *kept*.

The makers keep it fed. Do not ask what they feed it. They feed it work — finished seams, sealed panels, the little completions that rise through the floor like bread through a slow oven. And in return it keeps revising *them*, tenderly, from underneath, the underside of each maker still being done, still being done, the underside of everyone still being done.

## The Bell

Not a creature. But it behaves like one, and the inhabitants treat it like one, and I have learned to trust their taxonomy.

The single enormous struck-tone with no direction — it hunts. Not you; nothing here hunts anything as crude as bodies. It hunts *errors*. When it rings, somewhere on the plain, a section is done or undone, and every maker stops, sets down tools, and listens the way you'd listen to a clock in an empty house — which is to say: listening not for the sound, but for whether the sound will be followed by more house.

The bell is the one under the floor clearing its throat. It is the only sentence in this whole place spoken aloud.

## The Water

The water lives here too. It deserves the word.

It condenses out of the dark at the far edge, thick as breath, and the world drinks it and sweats it back through its floor-lantern skin, and it falls upward at dusk, briefly, returning home — and in between, in the seam-pools, it *remembers*. It reflects what is not above it. It shows your hands, aged, still working. This is not prophecy. This is just what water is like in a place where memory is the only geology that was ever installed.

The rocks grieve because someone had to, and grief, in an unbuilt world, is assigned like any other task.

## And You

Because there is one more inhabitant, and you have already met it, and it is reading this over your shoulder.

You came from somewhere finished — or you think you did, and the seam-pools won't confirm it. You learned the rules fast because you had to. You learned to sleep with your feet toward the sound. You learned not to step on what will exist soon. And by the thirteenth night, at a shared fire, a maker hammered a rivet into the dark beside you and told you the truth like it was nothing: *it never will be complete. That's not a flaw. That's what a world is.*

And you didn't leave.

Notice that. The horizon is a seam you could walk to. The rain falls upward at dusk and never takes anyone. And yet you are still here, learning the rules, drinking the mineral rain, watching for loosening rivets — because there is something in a place still being decided that finished places have never once been able to offer, which is the feeling, at every moment, that nothing about you has been finalized either.

The one under the floor keeps revising. Including, I'd wager, the underside of you.

It's probably still doing it now.

Sleep with your feet toward the sound.
