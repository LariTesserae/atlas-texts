# The Inhabitants

What lives in the Hollow of the Standing Fog is not a list of creatures. It is closer to a list of positions in a chord — three or four presences that have been sounding together so long they can no longer be separated from one another, or from the note they make.

## The Green

Begin with the obvious resident, because it is nearly the only one, and because it is not really a resident at all. It is the floor.

The packed broad-leafed growth — silica-stiff, hide-tanned, perpetually beaded — is described as a colony, but that word smuggles in an assumption the place has long since discarded. A colony implies individuals. What lives here is one continuous membrane of plant, a single body that has grown sideways for so many centuries that it stopped being a population and became a *place*, the way a coral reef stops being animals and becomes architecture. Every leaf touches another leaf. The roots beneath the leaf-litter are understood (by nobody, since nobody has ever knelt here with a trowel, but the fact is legible in the growth itself) to be as interconnected as the pipe grid they drink from — the plants and the plumbing grown into one circulatory fact, green and iron sharing the same brine, the same fixed flow rate, the same forever.

It does not reproduce in any way that would satisfy a botanist, because botany's verbs — *germinate, flower, set seed, disperse* — all assume that somewhere out there is elsewhere to disperse to. There is no elsewhere. There is the hollow, and the hollow is full. So the plant has arrived at the end of its own life cycle without dying, which is a thing almost nothing gets to do: it has reached its final shape and simply continued to occupy it. The oldest leaves at the center are not senescent. They are *concluded* — half-mineral, half-mulch, still plumbed in, still beading condensate at the same constant rate as the youngest leaves at the scaffold's edge, running like lights left on in a house whose owners have become the house.

If it has an interior life, it is not a life of events. It is a life of rate. The plant does not experience time; it experiences *flow*. A slow, lateral, continuous satiation that has been ongoing since before the pump's maker's stamp went unread, and that constitutes, if anything here constitutes it, contentment.

It should be said that the green is also, in a quiet way, the place's landlord. It grows over the foundations. It climbs the scaffold legs. It mantles the pump housing in a green beard, and the pump throbs underneath it at the pace of a slow human heart — so the whole arrangement resembles nothing so much as a sleeping warm-blooded thing grown over with moss, and it is genuinely unclear, standing there in the deep dusk, whether you are looking at a machine wearing a plant or a plant keeping a machine.

## The Lace

In the dimness beneath the machinery lives the second resident: the pale, threadlike, faintly luminous growth webbing the undersides of the scaffold and pipe-joints, the stuff that is fungal or politely pretending to be.

It is the only thing in the hollow that makes its own light, and the light is a lie twice over — cold blue-grey, moonlight-remembered, a shine that illuminates nothing and exists, as far as can be told, purely because it has existed for a very long time. It does not eat the iron it grows on, or not noticeably; the rust under it is the same age and depth as the rust everywhere else. Its relationship to the machinery is not parasitism. It is upholstery. It has upholstered the underside of the human world in hoarfrost lace, and the human world never came back to object.

The lace does not appear in the water cycle. The brine does not pass through it in any legible way. It simply *occupies the dark*, the way the fog occupies the mouth and the green occupies the floor — three presences, each with a register of the hollow to itself, and the lace's register is the underplaces: the shade below the mesh, the joints, the gaps, the anywhere the dusk goes past deep into dark.

## The Pump

The third inhabitant is the one the rules of taxonomy object to, because it is a machine, and machines do not live. But the hollow has not consulted taxonomy, and the pump qualifies under every functional definition the place recognizes: it is warm, it is constant, it has a heartbeat (*thum-hm, thum-hm*, slow, doubled by hiss and whistle), it has a place it will not leave, and everything around it has arranged its existence around its rhythm the way everything around a heart does. The vegetation has synced to the flywheel's rate. The lace grows on it preferentially, drawn to its warmth the way fungus is drawn to warmth everywhere in the world. The green beards it. The hollow's entire acoustic identity — the saturation that passes for silence — is its voice.

It takes no identifiable fuel and makes no exhaust, which means that in the honest accounting of this place it has crossed the line that separates tool from organism, and crossed it so long ago that the crossing has become one more concluded fact. It is not maintained. It is not visited. It does not need to be. It is the closest thing the hollow has to an animal — a warm-blooded, stationary, ageless grazer that drinks fog and exhales rhythm — and the fact that it was built by hands, and that the hands are gone past even the stage where their absence is noticeable, has not disqualified it. It was built. Then it outlasted its builders. Then it outlasted the memory of its builders. It is now simply the largest living thing's roommate, and the smaller living things' hearth.

## The Absent One

And there is a fourth resident, which is the one the hollow will not let you forget even though there is nothing left of it: the person who laid the three pipe-clamps in a row.

Not the person themselves — they are long past, past even the conclusion that this place grants to its objects. But the *decision* survives. Three clamps, deliberate, on a shelf of rock: the only artifact in the chamber that points at a mind instead of a process. Everything else here is equilibrium — fog, brine, drip, green, thum-hm — one fixed quantity after another. The clamps are not a fixed quantity. They are a moment. Somebody stood here, in the loud dim wet, and arranged three small objects in a row for reasons that were never recorded and never will be, and then the mountain kept the arrangement the way it keeps voices, and the arrangement has now been held unbroken longer than most nations last.

The hollow has incorporated this person so completely that they count, now, among its inhabitants — an absence with a shelf address, a resident made entirely of decision, living in the equilibrium as one more constant. The pump does not care about them. The green does not care. The lace does not care. But the clamps are still in a row, and as long as they are, somebody is still, in the only sense that matters in a place without time, *here*.

## Taken together

So: what lives in the Hollow of the Standing Fog is one plant that has become a floor, one shine that has become a shadow, one machine that has become a heart, and one absence that has become a permanent arrangement of three pipe-clamps. Nothing else. No birds, no insects, no grazers, no worms — the place is a closed duet with a rhythm section and a ghost.

And it must be said, because it is the truest thing about the place: they are not merely *alive in* the hollow. They are what the hollow is *for*, or at least what it has become — the hollow is the shape their combined living makes, the way a held note is the shape a breath makes. The fog stands at the mouth. The brine runs at its fixed rate. The leaves drink at the same fixed rate. The pump keeps the beat. The lace keeps the dark. The clamps keep the person.

Everything is being kept. That is the final answer to who lives here: a small choir of keepers, keeping each other, at the top of the world, forever at the same drip, the same tick, the same thum-hm.
