# The Tenants of the Receiving Floor

The first thing to understand about the inhabitants of the plain is that most of them are not tenants. They are *contents*.

## The Worms, Who Own the Only Quiet Rooms

Begin with the worms, because the worms are the closest thing this place has to gentry. They live down in the pans, a hand-span below the crust, and their burrows are the only absolute silence on the continent — the only cubic inches where the note does not reach. This makes their homes the most valuable real estate in the world, and the worms are entirely unaware of it. They do not know what they have. They have never heard the hum. A worm in this place is born into silence, tunnels in silence, and dies in a tearing-calico noise it never had the context to fear.

If you put your ear to the crust over a burrow, you will hear nothing, and the nothing will feel like a held secret, and you will be tempted to dig. Do not. The quiet rooms are load-bearing. Every wormhole is a silence that the hum is pressing against from all sides, the way water presses against a submarine, and when a worm dies and its burrow collapses, the hum rushes in to fill it with a small flat *pop* that you will hear half a second before it happens. The worms' deaths are announced by their own future sound. Even the worms hear their own endings early, if worms hear. Nobody has established that worms hear. Nobody has established that it matters.

## The Three Birds

The bird census is three, and each of the three is an individual, and they have been here longer than birds. Nobody sent them. They arrived from nowhere in the way things arrive here — precipitated, perhaps, or remembered, the plain's entire idea of *bird* condensed into a census of three. They are nondescript, grey-brown, unremarkable, and they have the exhausted air of animals carrying an entire taxon on their backs. When the last of them preens, that is all the preening in the world, and it knows it.

They live for the midge-hour. After each bloom-flush the midges hatch for sixty minutes, and everything with wings arrives from nowhere — and understand that "everything with wings" is sometimes more than the census of three. The sky fills. Whether the extra birds are migrants from beyond the cloud deck or whether the plain condenses them, opportunistically, out of its crowded seed bank — whether they are birds it ate and remembers — no one can say, and the three do not associate with them. The three keep apart, on the far pans, and watch the visitors feed with something that looks like seniority. When the hour ends, the visitors leave to nowhere, and the three remain, because the three are not visitors. The three are *staff*.

## The Fish, and the Fish That Are Not

There are fish in the deeper shine-pans. Some of them are fish.

You should sit with that sentence. Some of them. The deep pans hold fish the way a supersaturated syrup holds sugar — marginally, conditionally, always on the verge of explaining themselves out of existence. The plain can condense fish when it has fish on its mind, and it does not keep records, which means the fish in one pan may not be the fish that were in that pan last season, may not be the fish that were in that fish. If you catch one — and the dark thought here is that people have — it will be a fish, whole and scaled and honest, with a history you can only take on faith.

The fish are perhaps the plain's most honest expression of its central condition: this is a place that has eaten everything and can therefore produce anything, and the difference between *remembering* a fish and *being one* has been diluted past detection. The fish do not seem to mind. They hold their pans with the serene entitlement of creatures who know that their species is being maintained by the weather.

## The Figures

Now the difficult tenants.

The seed bank under the surface is crowded with nascent forms, and the plain cannot be quiet, and disturbance precipitates shape. This means the plain's most numerous inhabitants are *faces*, *hands*, *doorways* — rising and dissolving at the rate of the constant nothing. Are they inhabitants? They have location and duration, however brief. They have a habit of arriving with a sound. Each one is a tenant who signs no lease and leaves no forwarding address.

And here is the thing that emerges slowly, if you stay long enough to let it emerge: the figures *recur*. Not the arch — the arch is merely a habit of the medium, weather-shaped, built by no one. But there are two or three forms that the plain precipitates more often than probability should allow. A figure standing at the edge of a shine-pan, for the length of a storm, looking down into water that shows it standing before it does. A hand, always the same hand, always rising at the ninth hour of the dark, always dissolving before it completes the gesture it was making. If the plain is a memory, then memory has its favorite losses, and it rehearses them the way a tongue returns to the socket of a missing tooth. Whether these figures were ever people — whether the plain once ate someone it has never fully digested — is Rule 7 territory: *there is no test*.

The figures are tenants the way reflections are tenants. But the reflections here look up slightly before you do. So the distinction is thinner than you would like.

## The Arch-Keeper

And there — let it emerge — is the one who is almost a resident.

Someone is sometimes seen near the arch in the northeast quarter. Not after every storm; the arch precipitates after every storm, reliably, the plain's own habit, but the figure near it comes and goes on no schedule anyone has derived. It is grey. It is roughly person-shaped, the way the sedge is roughly tree-shaped — an apology, an approximation, the minimum viable person. It stands beside the arch at a respectful distance, as one stands beside a building being built by no one, and it watches, and it is gone before you can decide whether you saw it in the future's tense.

Here is what can be said about the Arch-Keeper, and none of it is certain: it arrives with its sound, or before its sound, or without sound, which means it is either precipitated or real, and there is no test. It stands on the acoustic lee side of things, in the pale streak, in the plain's only shade. And it does not build the arch. It does not appear to do anything at all. It *attends*. It is the only thing on the continent that treats the plain's behavior as a performance worth watching, and this makes it the only entity here with anything like a relationship to the place — the only one who is not merely content.

Perhaps the Arch-Keeper is the plain's memory of the last person who ever lived here before the world finished and put itself away. Perhaps it is a forecast — the seed of the next person the plain will make when it next has people on its mind. The plain does not distinguish between ruins and forecasts, and after a while, neither does the Keeper, and neither do you, and this is, in the end, the place's definition of tenancy: to live here is to be either a memory or a weather pattern, and to be unable to prove which, and to keep standing anyway — load-bearing opinion, held for about a minute, and then a minute more.

## The Census

So the full count, as best it can be taken from a handful of mud:

Three birds, permanent, overworked. Worms, inaudible, wealthy beyond knowing in silence. Fish, provisionally. Faces, hands, doorways, at a precipitation rate set by the noise. One arch, weather-built, standing in the northeast like a habit. One Keeper, attending it, or being attended by it. A continent-wide film of bloom that is technically the largest green thing that has ever existed and would weigh nothing in your hands. And underneath all of it, standing room only, the entire eaten world — every mountain, every somebody's mountain — crowded shoulder to shoulder in the dark, waiting for the next storm, the next footfall, the next shout, the next nothing, to be allowed to stand up for the length of a storm before letting themselves back down.

It is crowded and it is empty. Nothing lives here, and the place is full. The residents would be the first to tell you so, if the residents could tell anyone anything — but the residents are worms, birds, fish-that-are-provisionally, and the weather's own memories, and the plain keeps no records, and the hum takes the floor.
