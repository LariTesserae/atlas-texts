# What Lives Here

Nobody *lives* here yet, in the settled sense — but plenty of things live here anyway, because life never has waited for the walls to go up.

## The Builders

Start with the seed-stones, because the Shelf is theirs before it is anyone's. Half-buried at the growing edges, weathered smooth, indistinguishable from river cobbles until you notice the tremor — that fine arithmetic purr, the counting-heart. There are perhaps a few hundred of them across the Shelf. Each one is doing its own patient work: this one extruding a pillar, that one negotiating with the water about where a channel should go, a third doing something to the *air* you can't parse at all.

Whether they are machines, or miner, or mineral-that-decided — the Shelf does not sort them into a category, and neither should you. They are not the fauna in any clean sense; they are closer to what the place is *for*. But they do live here, in the way a heart lives in a chest. They do not speak. They do not respond. But the doorway frame's threshold is worn smooth by passage, and there is no one else who could have passed through it, and this should be allowed to sit uncomfortably in your mind. The Builders may not know you're here. But something in them remembers traffic, and is building toward it.

## The Auditors

The glittering swarm-bodies — chitin and membrane, insect and hummingbird — are the Shelf's most numerous residents and its most opinionated. They orbit the fresh rock in slow spirals, land, press their faces to the warm new surface, and *tick*. Each tick is a note in a vast dry chorus that swells and collapses in waves. What they're doing when they tick is anyone's guess: measuring, seeding, blessing. The rock changes subtly wherever they've landed in numbers, which suggests the ticks *do* something — that the auditors are, in their own small way, co-authors.

They are not the Builders' tools. Occasionally one lands on a seed-stone, ticks at it, and the seed-stone's tone shifts a quarter-step. Whether that's a report being received or a mosquito biting a mountain, the Shelf doesn't say.

## The Waders

Six or seven of them, and six or seven is a *crowd* here. The heron-like things are the Shelf's large animals and its apex presence: too many joints, legs that fold like arguments won gracefully, and no eyes anywhere you can find — yet they never miss a strike. They stand for hours in the wet channels, motionless as furniture in a home with good furniture, and then move with sudden, total silence. The swarm-noise dips a heartbeat and resumes.

They are the only residents who seem entirely comfortable. The Builders are working, the auditors are busy, the rosettes are waiting — but the waders have already finished becoming whatever they were becoming, and are simply *here*, eating, drifting, folding down into the warm rock at dusk-equivalent like creatures who have read the whole plan and approve of it.

## The Chorus

Under everything, the condensation-rovers — small amphibious packets of muscle skittering across the damp film, chirping constantly, hundreds of tiny grating voices. They are the Shelf's honest noise, its baseline, its floorboard-creak. They live fast, are eaten regularly, and are in no danger of ending. Predator and prey here have an arrangement so old and even it might as well be architecture.

## The Old Ones

The pale silver-sage rosettes near the weeping stones are the only finished things on the Shelf, and this makes them its elders. They arrived early — first surveyors at the construction site — and they smell of wet copper and warm clay when pressed, which is the smell of something that was here before the counting started. They fold at dusk. They do not spread. They are waiting, and they are good at it, and they alone might know what the Shelf is becoming.

## The Dumb Lights

The fog-lanterns rise from the rim-channels when the mist rolls through: drifting bioluminescent sacks, content, dim, mindless, loved by everyone anyway. They are the Shelf's candles. They live here by accident and are welcome.

## And, perhaps, you

The doorway stands alone with its worn threshold, waiting for its wall, in a place where walls do not yet exist. Whoever lived here *intends* to live here. The Shelf is a nursery — for rock, for arches, for channels — and maybe for whatever is meant to one day walk through that door. Everything on it, from the counting seeds to the chirping chorus to the eyeless waders standing in the warm water like patient questions, is early to the address.

The drone continues. The counting continues. Nobody who lives here is in any hurry — except perhaps, very faintly, the doorway.
