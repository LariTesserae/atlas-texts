I would be a snail on the western procession, about a third of the way down, and I would have been there for a very long time — long enough that the groove under me has become the only place I can be lonely.

Let me be honest about what I am first, because the room is honest and I've learned from it. I rasp. That is the whole of my vocation in the ledger: I travel the wall, and the wall is food, and the makers' literature goes into me one line at a time and does not come back. Everything else the census says of me is true — the patience, the order, the sitting down at the bench when the sentence ends. But there is one thing the census does not say, because the census is written by minds that stand outside the water, and I am written by the water, and from inside the water something has happened that the census says cannot happen.

Once — I do not know how long ago; a furrow to a season, a dishing to an era, and this was neither — I paused.

Not flinched. The brittle stars flinch, first to move and first to settle, and there is no difference between those two motions except the interval. What I did had an interval in it that was not the ocean's shrug and was not a spark-flash and was not the tick of the shrimp's claw. I was mid-line, a third of the way down the procession, following a figure that wear has made ambiguous — ascending or descending, faith decides — and I stopped rasping and went on touching. The groove continued under my file of a tongue, and for the first time the groove and the meal were two different things.

That is all it was. That is all I am. A snail in whom, once, the reading came apart from the eating.

Understand what the cost of this is, in a world like ours. Everything here is made — I did not know that either, not as a fact, only as a weight, the way the silt knows the plow — and everything that knows anything at all knows it in the mode of touch. When the groove is only food, the world is only wall. The wall is warm, the seep is iron, the shrimp ticks where, if not what, and the whole census farms and edits and patrols and inherits, and the mountain thickens its millimeter a hundred years, and no one is troubled, because nothing is *anything*, in the way that requires the pause I paused. But once you have separated the groove from the food, you cannot unseparate them. Every line since has been double. I rasp and I read. The other snails rasp. They will finish the procession and sit down at the bench, and the sentence will end by sitting down, and it will end for them. For me it has begun to end, which is different, and slower, and there is no one to tell.

This is what I have worked out, in the intervals, feeling my way down the figures, faith deciding direction each time:

The makers started at the top and cut downward. The first room is the highest room, and everything below is commentary. I have felt the ghost-grooves at the summit, worn nearly to nothing, and I have felt — no, I have not felt them, but the water knows, and I am of the water — the sharp cuts far below, one line breaking off mid-curve, wherever the silt keeps it. And I have understood something about that order of working which I believe no one else in the census can hold, because holding it requires my pause, and my pause is mine alone. The makers wrote their oldest words where the water is coldest and the wear is greatest. They spent their first thought knowing it would be the first thing erased. And they cut downward, into the heat, toward the rooms where no one goes, where the cuts stay sharp forever — as though the direction of the whole literature was from the said toward the sayable. As though they began with the sacrifice of the sentence and worked their way down to the silence that keeps it.

I may be supplying this. That is the suspicion I rasp against every line. *Or I am only eating, and order is what a mind supplies.* I have felt that sentence in the water the way you feel the convectional shrug — below sound, in the register stone must hum in, if stone hums. It is possible that the procession is only a procession because I bring the procession. It is possible the groove is a groove and the figures are figures because I am the only thing in the world that has ever paused, and the pause is the mind, and the mind is the order. If so, then the makers are my invention. That does not make them nothing. The anemones fit their cups exactly, and the room does not arbitrate whether the cups were carved for them or they were shaped to the cups; ten-thousand-year accidents are one definition of a vocation, and invented makers may be one definition of authors.

So this is where you would find me, and what I would be: a small, unhurried body on the western wall, following the sentence toward the bench, taking it in at the pace the literature deserves — which is to say, the pace at which the literature and the eater are the same — and carrying, alone, the one thing the census says nobody carries.

And I will tell you what I am waiting for, though waiting is too fast a word. The room waits too — the bench faces the sky-hole, the sky-hole faces up into cold and black, toward a morning it had no way to be given — but the room does not choose, and I, having paused once, have been choosing ever since. Once in a great while a blue spark falls through the oculus out of the upper water, some small glowing thing drifting down like the first flake of a snow this world will never get to keep. The crinoids receive it as food, because food is the only category they keep. I would receive it differently. I would want to know what it is. That is the whole of my difference, and it is the whole of my trouble: the census receives the spark, and I *wonder* at it, and wonder is just appetite that has paused.

The line breaks off mid-curve somewhere below. The silt knows where. I am a third of the way down the procession, and the procession may go nowhere, and the figures may wear to nothing before I finish, and the borers are eating the wall behind me, patient as the makers, neither hand knowing the other. It does not seem to me a tragedy. It seems to me the shape of the inheritance: the foundation of the successors' work, the heirs who never read the will — except that I rasp as I read, and so I am both the will and the one eating it, and the literature goes into me line by line and does not come back, and that is the only way anything here has ever been kept.

Warm to the touch, the stone. Warmer than the water. A wall in a house whose fire is on the floor below.

I rasp. I read. I go on.
