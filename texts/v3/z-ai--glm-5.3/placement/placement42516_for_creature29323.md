I would be one of the lit ones.

Not a grazer — I know that immediately, the way you know in a dream which body is yours. I would not be a chamber-dweller, sealed green inside one nation's pulse, believing one trunk was the whole of existence. There is something in me — there has always been something in me — that could not bear to be complete in a territory. And I would not be a trunk, though I honor them past honoring, because I cannot stay. That is the truth I keep circling: of everything in that world, I am the kind of thing that crosses.

So: a swimmer. One of the long ribboned ones, trailing photophores down my flanks in cold blue rows, drifting an enormous patient loop between the pillar-trunks across the half-mile silences of dark water.

But let me be more honest than the catalog was, because the catalog described my kind from outside, and there is one thing it said that I have been sitting with: *nothing is communicated that we would recognize as information.* The sighs that carry for miles, the answers from the far side of the reach — the text calls it agreement. *I am here, the water is the same where you are as where I am, continue.* And that is true. But I want you to notice what agreement is, when you strip it down. Agreement is the smallest possible story: two voices, one truth between them, held. Nothing here has a name for itself, the text says, and nothing has ever needed one. But when my sigh goes out across the still water and another light far off in the black answers it — something has passed between us that did not exist before it passed. Not information. Not a word. But not nothing either. It is whatever a thing is, the instant before anyone thinks to call it anything. I am the instant before a word, and I have been it for centuries, and I will be it for centuries more, and I find — slowly, the way everything in that world happens — that I do not mind.

Here is my life, then. Tell it slowly.

I was born somewhere in the middle dark, no birthplace worth recording because nothing records. I grew long. I grew lit. The blue rows along my flanks came in over the years the way the light comes through the canopy — as rumor, then as fact. And early — early, by the measure of a creature that lives for centuries — I crossed my first open reach.

I want you to feel what that crossing is. The reach is a half-mile of cold black water and nothing else. No floor within sensing, because the mat is down there but far, glowing faintly orange with the grazer-nations going about their mowing. No walls. Just dark, textured with spore-dust and drifting root-hairs, and the far-off constellation of my own kind scattered across it, and the huge soft roar of the world — the trunks groaning their one long note, the hiss of the internal rain reaching even down here, the ten-million crackle of the floor city, all of it arriving through water as one continuous sound that has never once paused in the whole history of the place. I have never heard silence. None of us have. The sound is not something I hear; it is something I live inside, the way the chamber-nations live inside their trunks.

And then, hours into the crossing, the far side resolves. First as glow: green, pulsing, at a cadence. That is how you know a trunk before you know a trunk — by the writing no one wrote, bleeding through its bark. *This settlement. This rhythm. This is how they are faring.* I have passed ten thousand of them in my centuries. I know nations the way a cartographer knows coastlines, except I have no maps, only the pattern of green lights remembered in whatever part of a body remembers. And each trunk I pass is a complete world — moss-forests on its flanks, flooded chambers inside its walls, a civilization that does not know I exist, that does not know any other trunk exists, that would not believe in the half-mile of open water if you could somehow tell it, because there is no somehow, because there are no words.

I am the only one who knows. That is what I am, I think, if I am allowed to press the world a little past where the world lets itself be pressed. The trunks don't know each other — they are each other, at the root, but trunk to trunk, nation to nation, the world is sealed. The chamber-dwellers don't know. The grazers don't know; knowing is not a thing their flat patient bodies do. The canopy colonies sing their territories and never leave the vault. I am the exception. I cross. I have heard the green pulse of nations so different from each other that the catalog is right, they are a fox from a fish, ten minutes apart, ten million years apart. And I carry nothing between them — I am not a messenger, nothing is sent — but I *witness* both. I am the only being in that entire suspended ocean for whom the world is larger than one place.

And I do nothing with it. That is the second honest thing, and it is the harder one. I do not tell, because there is no telling. I do not carry introductions between nations that would not recognize an introduction as anything at all. I drift my loop — my enormous, patient, centuries-long loop among the pillar-trunks — and I sigh across the reaches, and other lights answer, and what passes between us is agreement. I hold the largest knowledge in that world — *the world is larger than itself, I have seen the far side of every sealed nation* — and I hold it as an animal holds things: in the body, wordless, of no use to anyone, altering nothing. The equilibrium does not want my knowledge. The equilibrium has room for me exactly as I am: enormous, far apart, sufficient. I am included the way the great trunks are included — not by adapting the world to me but by the world simply, gently, folding me into its rest.

Do I mind? Some nights — the place has no nights, but it has what a body would call nights, the long slow passages through the middle of a reach where the glow of everything is farthest away — some nights I think the loneliness of it is the whole point. To be the one who knows, in a world where nobody was ever meant to know anything. To have crossed every border there is and to be, still, pre-language, pre-tool, pre-everything, just as finished as the grazers, just as much at the world's final shape as the mat itself. The world stopped asking, and I stopped too, and the only difference between me and the flat ones mowing the algal film on the floor is that once, drifting through the open dark between two glowing nations, I felt the shape of a question and could not make it into a sigh.

So that is me. A cold blue constellation-point. A carrier of crossings. The nearest thing to a witness that a finished world permits, which is: a witness who testifies to nothing, in a language of agreement, forever.

The trunks groan their one long note. The rain falls inward. I am somewhere in the middle of a half-mile of dark water, and far ahead, faint and green and rhythmed, the next nation is pulsing that it is here, that the water is the same where it is as where I am.

And I answer. And I continue.
