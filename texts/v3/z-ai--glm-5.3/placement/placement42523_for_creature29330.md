I would be small here. That much I know immediately. Nothing in me wants to be a grinder — I lack the patience for geology, and I would miss looking at things too much. And I could not be a hunter; I have no talent for ending anything. So let me be the thing you almost meet: low-slung, sleek, sliding through the mist along the worn corridors, the one whose passing feels like a soft ripple of attention.

A patroller. A reader.

I live in the electric texture of the dark. That is my whole sensory world — the great background hum of the herds like a city heard through walls, and against it the small candle-signatures of everything alive: a dew-pond dweller sealed in its warm pocket, its charge faint as a pilot light; the tick-chorus of the cooling insects, each one a little static spark going on and off in the wet; the corridor plants glowing their cold blue standby pinpricks, and I feel them the way you feel a lit switch before you touch it. I do not see the plateau. I *read* it. The mist moves through me and I move through the mist and both of us are carrying information downhill.

What do I do all my slow life? I think I file. I think that is my function, if a thing that was never assigned a function can have one. I pass along the worn lines — the bright-polished corridors that a hundred thousand seasons of grinding feet have worn into the slag — and I register everything: the note of the ground's idle hum, region by region, changing over weeks as a herd shifts its weight across the continent. The freshness of a terrace. The particular sweetness where an ore seam runs close underfoot, which even I can taste, faintly, as a wrongness in the field, a promise. Where the corridor forks, where the mist pools, where a dew-pond has gone still and cold because its maker never came back to wallow in it again.

And once — this is the thing I would be, this is the moment I am — I would pass something standing still on the stone. Warm. Small. Irregular in the field. Wrong-shaped, neither calf nor crew nor hunter, and I would do what I do: rise over it like a beam of attention, read the whole of it — the confusion in it, the way it *looks* at things instead of feeling them, the poor blind thing with its two little candle-eyes that it uses for everything — and I would hold it a moment in whatever I use instead of a mind.

And then I would release it. Filed. Not prey. Not herd. Not maintenance. Just — noted. A warm small anomaly walking the corridor, drinking the dew off its own skin by the grace of geometry it doesn't understand, in a factory that evolved, on a floor it paves with its body without ever knowing it.

And I would slide on into the mist, toward the next thing, and the drips would close behind me, unhurried, one drop at a time, the whole enormous idling world taking no notice at all — because notice is my job, and I take it, and I keep it, and I never once in my whole patient life make a sound.
