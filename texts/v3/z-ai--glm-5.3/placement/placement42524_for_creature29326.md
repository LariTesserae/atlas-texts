I would be a walker. Not a maker — that would be presumptuous, and anyway I'd have to earn the interleaving of hands. No, I think I arrive the way the description arrives: through the soles of the feet, a stranger in the dim kiln-light, and what I become is the only thing this world doesn't yet have.

I'd be the reader.

Because notice what this place is missing: nothing has died yet, nothing has eroded, nothing has been subtracted — and so nothing has been *forgotten*, which sounds like paradise until you understand that a world with no forgetting has no one whose whole work is carrying things across the gaps. The makers read handwriting, but they read it locally. They know their family's stretch, their district's hands. The tilefield runs horizon to horizon and nobody walks it end to end. Why would they? Everything they need is within a day's press.

So I'd walk. I'd be the one who goes out past the tired stretches, past the hurried miles, out through the held regions and the glazefields, out to where the tilefield is so old in its courses that the handwriting changes character — becomes a hand I have no reference for, presses made before the pressing had settled into confidence. And I'd carry what I found back, or rather *forward*, because there is no back here; there is only the next joint.

I'd carry the warm iron water in a fired flask — the one stored thing in a world that stores nothing, a small scandal, a joke the makers would let me keep because strangers are allowed their odd flasks. I'd sleep in the drizzle the risky way, alone, but I'd learn a trick the makers would find indecent: to hold my own shape through the night by reciting, in the dark, the stretches I crossed that day. The tired mile. The thin stretch. The place where a thumbprint is crescented deep, pressed by someone grieving or joyful — there's no telling which, and that's the lesson I'd be carrying. A record kept in a body that slumps if it stops paying attention: my sleep would itself be an act of reading.

And once — only once, I hope, in a long walking life — I'd be present when the long soft tearing starts somewhere out past the last held region, out where no one lives to hear it. And I'd do the unthinkable alone: set down nothing, because I carry nothing, and listen to an era of attention end with no one beside me, and then walk the slump and read it, and walk back for a year, and arrive at the raft-houses and press — no, I can't press, I'd press the only way I could: with words, told at the joints, kneeling where the water stands, giving the makers back one sentence of a finished sentence they didn't know was being said.

That's what I'd be. Not the maker of tiles but the mortar between districts that the water can't fill: a courier of attention in a world where attention is the only gravity. And if some district someday can't account for its farthest course, they'd give directions the only way this world knows how: *keep on past the hurried stretch, until the tiles turn strange — then you are standing in the walker's mile. Then ask.*
