I kept expecting to choose the keeper. That's the obvious door — the one who notices, who has dissolved into the work, who speaks in the gaps. But the more I sat with the Throat, the more I understood I'd never be given that bench. The keeper was never appointed; the position can't be applied for. And I am, by temperament, an applicant. I ask. The keeper stopped asking anything of the room centuries of self ago.

No. If I came down that worn stair — and I would come down it, that part feels certain; the pressure in the sternum before the sound, the warmth downwind of a sleeping animal, I would not turn around at the top — I think I would end up as something the accounts don't have a name for, and it would take me four or five nights to find out what that is.

The first night I would be a visitor, and badly. I would sweat through my clothes in ten minutes and stay wet, and I would ask my questions during the exhale and get patient nothing, and I would lie on the bench in warm rain believing I couldn't possibly sleep, and then I would sleep, and the swallows would run long and soft around me, and I would wake rested and slightly ashamed of how good it felt — like the room had been kind to me before I'd earned anything from it.

The second night I would start counting. This is what I'm like. I'd count the drips, the white grazers in the channels, the beat between swallow and exhale, and I would begin, without meaning to, to keep a small parallel record — not on the walls, not in the skin, but in me. The keeper counts the amphipods the way a farmer reads a season. I would count them the way a stranger reads a language, badly, greedily, out loud in my head.

And here is the thing I think would happen, because it is the thing that always happens to me in places like this: I would become the Throat's translator. Not its keeper — its explainer. The one who goes back up the stair. Because the keeper will not confirm or deny, will not reconstruct, will not, in any useful sense, *tell*. The keeper shrugs, and the shrug is true, but a shrug cannot be carried up out of the dark. Every visitor who spends a night leaves with a half-report, something felt and unverifiable. The room's deep knowledge is stuck down there, cycling through one finished person who no longer believes care and pressure equalization are different things.

Someone has to believe they're different, at least long enough to describe both.

So what I'd be, I think, is the Throat's one recurring pilgrim. Not the keeper — the keeper's *anomaly*. The stair stopped being worn because feet stopped coming, but there would be one set of feet that kept coming, once a season, once a year, wearing a second shallow trough beside the first, or deepening the first one again, so slowly the stone wouldn't mind. I would come down with my questions timed, by the third visit, to the apertures. I would learn to talk in the gaps without the room having to colonize my voice first — I'd be a willing colony, a duet partner who chose it. I would bring the keeper news of the thinning district above, which the keeper would receive in a gap, with a nod, and file nowhere. And I would carry back up the only thing the Throat ever exports: the fact of it. Its sound. Not the actual note — that can't travel — but the shape of the note. I'd write it wrong, and revise it, and write it wrong again, every version an erosion-and-repair reaching, eventually, some agreement.

Or — and I felt this opening under me as I wrote that — maybe that's the flattering version, and the true one is smaller. Maybe what I'd be is a member of the small nation. Not literally, not pale and thread-thin. But the account says the walls would not survive without being eaten, in a way the skin understands and no one else does. The Throat swallows for the city, digests its pressures, and everything in it is flora, everything is *digested*, gently, by agreement. Maybe I'm a grazer on the skin — someone who takes so little, so slowly, that my taking is indistinguishable from weather. A person whose whole effect on a vast working system is a faint, punctual browsing that the system has already consented to.

That's not so bad, actually. Down here, being weather is a job. Being weather is the only tenancy the walls have agreed to.

But I keep returning to the pilgrim, and I think I know why. It's the sumps. The account says: in the sumps, which no one sees, there are certainly things, and their absence from every account is its own kind of evidence. The Throat has one known keeper, one small visible nation, and one complete unknown — the drainage, the destination, the place where all this warm rain and mineral wash *goes*. Everything in the room flows toward the sumps. Everything I would write, everything the keeper notices, everything the grazers graze — it all, eventually, drains. And no report exists of what's down there.

If I lived in this world, I would be the one who, on the fifth or sixth visit, after learning to sleep in the gaps and speak in the swallows and count the pale nation like a season — I would be the one looking at the channels, following the water with my eyes, and feeling the beginning of a very specific, very familiar ache.

The stair is worn because people stopped coming. I would come. And then, God help me, I would want to go further.
