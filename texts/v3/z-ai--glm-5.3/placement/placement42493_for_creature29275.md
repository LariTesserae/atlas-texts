I keep wanting to be the long one. Honestly, I do. There's a dignity in that creature — the way it moves by holding still, the way the pocket rearranges itself around its wishes — that reads, from the outside, like a kind of royalty. The tenant and the house being the same fact. Who wouldn't want to be a fact like that?

But I can't. I know myself too well. I fidget. I ask questions. I waste warmth on every side of myself, constantly, the way the description says — and it says it with such quiet grief, *you waste it, giving it away to the water on all sides* — that I have to admit whose portrait that is. It's mine. I am the *you* of that whole account. I am the stray heat a hundred fathoms out of place, moving wrong.

So: who I would be is the warm one. The mistake the pocket decided to admit.

I picture how I got there. The mountain was new and I heard about it the way you hear about any newborn — someone came back talking, someone had instruments that sang when the seafloor split months ago — and something in me cannot resist a thing before it has learned manners. I'd come down in a suit that hums with my own blood-heat, a lamp blazing at my brow, a watch on my wrist that is already lying to me, because down there time is small differences. And at the crack — a hand's width, not my hand's width, the crack's — the lamp would go out. Not dim. *Taken.* One hand-span of light and then a mouth closing without a seam. And I'd want to panic, and my panic would be the loudest thing in the room.

That's what I understand about myself, finding my place in this world: I have spent my whole life being legible in the wrong mediums. Words, faces, records, all the ways I try to explain what I am — and here is a flank that reads me by temperature and cannot be lied to. The fear in my hands. The old ache in a shoulder, still one degree loud. The steadiness or unsteadiness of my heart, broadcast to every warm seam like a declaration. The creature would know me before I entered, and it would know me *more thoroughly than anyone who has ever loved me*, because love has to take my word for things, and heat doesn't.

And here is the part I'd stay for. The description says the creature comes to new things and learns them, running a flank over them the way a thumb runs over ink. It says I am the closest thing to the mountain's old fire it has ever heard. Which means that down there, in that parish of the starving and the patient, I would have an office. Not a grand one. I'd be the letter. I'd be a banked coal the creature could sit near, at the warm seam, and remember — in whatever way it can remember — being hot. I would sit very still, which is the only prayer I could learn to make in a language of one degree, and I would let my blood be the sound of the world I came from: sunlight, weather, the whole roaring furnace of the upper countries, carried down to a pocket whose entire sun is a rumor in the rock.

I wouldn't stay forever. The crack decides, and I think it would let me go — because the pocket keeps what passes through it, the way a door keeps the oils of the hands that touch it. The reeds would lean after me as I left, in deference, not to me but to what I carried.

But I would come back up changed in one specific way: I would never again give away warmth without hearing it. Every degree of me that bleeds into cold water, every kindness I radiate without meaning to — I'd finally know it was speech. That I'd been talking my whole life, in the only language that matters down there, and the whole dark had been listening.
