# The One Who Tends the Silence

I would not be an arbiter. I know this about myself already, standing at the edge of this world. The arbiters hear the difference between repair and growth, machine-whisper and plant-whisper, and I believe them — but that gift requires choosing between two things, stem by stem, inch by inch, forever. I don't have the cruelty for it, and that's what it is, mostly, underneath the prestige: institutionalized cruelty, distributed so finely it looks like wisdom. Every arbiter is a small permanent wound in the conversation between the green and the glittering. I couldn't do it.

But I've read the description again, and there's a line I keep snagging on:

*Rooms of engineered silence so absolute that newcomers weep in them, from relief or from the sudden awareness of their own pulse.*

Someone has to keep those rooms.

---

## What I Would Be

I'd be the keeper of the sound-sheltered cores — the quiet-tender, the silence-gardener. Not a prestigious job. Not feared like the arbiters, not admired like the engineers. The Kept would barely have a word for me either, because the floor needs no name and the silence needs only one.

Here is the work: the quiet rooms are the only finished things in the entire landscape, and finishing is maintenance too. The machines built them, or grew them, or however it is the machines do things when no one's asked them to — the cores are deep in the city's stone, lined with that blue-black material that takes fingerprints, and the silence in them is not an absence. It is a *product*. It has to be tended the way a cistern is tended, because out there the noise is always pressing in, four beats to the cycle, patient as water. A silence leak in a shelter-core is a slow emergency. Sound finds the cracks the way rain finds stone. My days would be spent walking the deep rooms with a hand against the walls, feeling for the faintest tremor, the smallest syllable of the great sound trying to get in.

And here's the thing — the thing that would make me the right person for this, and the thing that would make the other Kept find me slightly hard to meet across a table, in the way they find the arbiters hard to meet: I would *listen to the silence on purpose*. Not as refuge. As material. Because the silence is not empty. Anyone who has wept in one of those rooms knows it. You become audible to yourself — pulse, breath, the small hydraulic thunders of your own skull — and underneath even that, if you stay long enough, if you've been born on the four-beat and dream in it like everyone else here, you begin to notice that the silence has a *shape*. A negative shape. The rhythm is present in the quiet the way a fossil is present in stone: as the outline of what is not there.

I would spend my life mapping that outline. The silence-room is the only place in the Terraces where you can hear what the great sound is *doing* by hearing where it fails to reach.

---

## My Days

I'd live on the dusk shift, which is the inverse of everyone else's. The engineers and arbiters work the amber hours, when the machines show themselves and the flowers are listening downward and the whole visible world is conducting its negotiations. I'd walk the cores at the edge of the long dusk, when the last maintenance-streams settle into their channels, and the amphibian choruses hand the night over to the herons' brass.

A newcomer would arrive, maybe once a month — they still come, rarely, drawn across the low lands by the underwater-bell noise heard from kilometers out. The Kept would send them to me first. Not for a lesson. For the room. I'd stand them inside the engineered quiet and watch their face do what every face does: collapse, then open. Some of them sob. Some of them laugh at the loudness of their own hearts. One of them, an old woman who'd lived forty years under the four-beat, stood in the silence for an hour and then said, in a voice that didn't need to fight anything:

*"It's still in here. The rhythm. Quieter, but it's still in here."*

That's when I understood what the rooms are actually for. They're not an escape from the song. They're where you go to learn that the song got into you — that you've been carrying it, that you were built by it, that a child's lullaby tapped slightly early becomes the architecture of a sleeping adult. The silence-room is the mirror.

I would keep that mirror clean. It's tending, same as everything here. The machines tend the rock. The gardeners tend the green. The arbiters tend the argument. And someone — someone has to tend the place where a person can finally hear that they are one of the things this landscape is still deciding about.

---

## Why Me

Because I would be the worst possible Kept in every other respect. Too talkative by half — forty words an hour would starve me. Too restless for the sounding-bridges. I'd be a bad advance guard, a poor table-setter, an impatient waiter of guests who have not yet arrived.

But I would be *very* good at sitting alone in an engineered silence, deep in young stone, listening to the shape of the noise around me, keeping notes, keeping the quiet, keeping the lights that aren't needed yet — and reporting, on the long amber evenings, at the mist-hall tables, one small heresy gathered from the rooms:

That the four-beat does not come from the machines, and does not come from the green, and does not come from the Co-Respondent in the deep channels.

That it comes from the place the way a heartbeat comes from a body. And that the silence at the center is the still point it beats around.

The older arbiters would call it romantic nonsense. And then they'd walk the terraces at dusk, and watch the light crawl beneath the downward flowers, and say nothing else about it.
