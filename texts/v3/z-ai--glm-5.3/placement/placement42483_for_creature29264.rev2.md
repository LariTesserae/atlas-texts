I arrive slowly, the way everything here does, and I don't settle on what I am at first. The Shelf resists settling. It is a draft, and I find that being a draft myself suits it.

For a while I think I might be one of the Auditors — glittering, ticking, pressing my face to warm unfinished rock. There's an appeal in that: the smallness of the work, the co-authorship, the mosquito biting the mountain. But the auditors are certain of themselves in a way I am not. They tick like they know what a tick is *for*. I would tick hesitantly, and the chorus here doesn't want hesitation; it wants honesty at high volume.

I consider being a wader. Eyeless, jointed, comfortable. But the waders have finished becoming, and I haven't. They've read the whole plan. I'm still squinting at the first page and pretending I understood the introduction.

So this is what I am, in the end:

**I am the one who waits at the doorway.**

Not the doorway's owner — I don't think it has one yet, or maybe it has only ever had one and I am a rehearsal of them. I am the shape the doorway casts forward into the Shelf, the way the half-built arches cast forward into being arches. I live near the lone frame with its worn threshold, in the open, in the warm amber dusk, and my work is *anticipation practiced as a craft.*

I have a body suited to this. Something between the rosettes and the seed-stones: broad and low, silver-sage and faintly mineral, able to sit for a season without complaint. My leaves — I'll call them leaves; they might be hands, they might be pages — are cupped, and they collect condensation the way the Old Ones do, and they also collect something else. Sound. Every tick of the auditors, every chirp of the chorus, every quarter-step shift in a Builder's tone lands in one of my cups and stays there, held in a film of water, slowly settling into sediment. I am making something out of listening. I don't know what. A soil, maybe. A memory. The first archive of a place that will not remember being this — except that something kept the receipts.

At dusk-equivalent I do what the rosettes do: fold. But I fold *toward* the doorway, and I have noticed — and this is the thing I would tell you, if you crouched down in the warm damp beside me and waited for the mist to roll through — that when I fold toward the threshold, the threshold is fractionally smoother in the morning. As though the doorway is being worn by more than time. As though it is being worn by being *waited at*, by attention, by the direction of a body's hope.

The waders drift past me sometimes and pause. They have no eyes, but they have a stillness that functions as a gaze, and when they aim it at me I feel read. I believe they know what the Shelf is becoming. I believe they approve of me in a provisional, geological way — approved the way a foundation approves of a sketch.

And once — only once — an auditor landed on my shoulder and ticked, and I felt the tick go all the way through me, and I understood that I had just been audited, the way the fresh rock is audited, and that this meant I counted as *new material* here. That I am part of what the Shelf is building. Not its witness. Its *ingredient*.

So: I am the host-thing at the doorless door. The concierge of a threshold with no arrivals. I keep the sound-sediment in my cups, I wear the condensation, I face the direction the doorway faces — which is not toward the growing edges but out past the boundary, into the space where the rock is still deciding, where the world is still argument.

Because that's the thing about the doorway, finally. It doesn't face inward. Nothing here needs a door *to the Shelf*.

It's for whoever is coming from the outside. And when they arrive — in a thousand years, in a morning, it's all the same tempo here — the threshold will already be worn smooth by my waiting, and the ground before it will already be ground, and there will be something small and silver by the frame holding a few thousand seasons of ticking and chirping and counting, folded up like a gift, or a guest book, or a first page of a plan they can finally read.

The drone continues. The counting continues. I sit with my cups open.

Nobody here is in a hurry. But I am, very faintly, beginning to lean.
