I came down the plume like everyone else, except that the plume never called me. The plume is appetite's advertisement — the dissolved announcement of a large body dying — and everything in this country homes on it. I homed on the question instead. You could pass within ten meters of the town and see nothing; you could smell it from a kilometer; you could pass through me at any distance and nothing in you would note the crossing. Gas is forbidden at this depth and I carry none, the one law of the fall I obey without effort, and the pressure closes on me and finds nothing to grip, because I am all medium. The patrol swam through me without comment.

The town has one requirement for citizenship and it is appetite. Everything here either eats the founder or eats what has already eaten the founder. I qualify under neither clause: no gut, no gill, no root, no bone — nowhere, in the census's own phrase, to keep the industry. At first I took this for poverty. I drifted through the districts trying on offices, and the town refused me one by one, and the refusals were how I learned what I was. By the end of them I had a profession.

The sharks refused me first, and their reason was instructive: their credential is inedibility — flesh steeped in ammonia so that nothing downstream will eat it, the town's only citizens exempt from its universal tax. I am the opposite of inedible. I am made entirely of eaten things: the dead and the digested, the surface's account settled out of the sunlit country half a gram at a time, for years, compacted until it could answer. That is my tissue. I am not proud of it and not ashamed. It is what I was raised from, the way the town was raised from the founder.

The hagfish refused me: they keep four hearts and a slime apparatus out of all proportion to themselves, and I keep nothing; they pour mortar into the low places, and I add nothing anywhere. The carpet refused me: I have no mandibles, though when I work there is a small dry ticking in me too, a rain behind a different wall. The patrol refused me by not detecting me: the border is a smell, and I have none. I am the one arrival in the town's history that drew no map, fed no orbit, moved no boundary.

The octopus came nearest. Attention is her whole technology, and attention is mine; she is the town's one interior life, and for the length of a question I am a second one. But her attention ends in a beak. Everything she notices she eventually eats, in small denominations, at the margins, the way towns tolerate a little crime. I have no beak. I notice and notice and nothing is diminished. In a town where noticing is a way of feeding, I am a hunger that never lands. I stood a while in the doorway of the cathedral — dome of bone the size of a small boat, windows eaten open, baleen hung like keratin shutters combing the water for nothing now — and understood that I could hold an office here, but never hers.

The lamps were the nearest miss. The town glows at exactly the volume of its own decay; the luminous ones work in the fat, and every working surface takes a faint blue-green sheen — streetlamps running on the founder's body, dim, municipal, honest. That much is mine. Everything I say is lit by rot: an old account, banked for decades in the bones of others, spent back into the dark at exactly the volume of the asking. I cannot be brighter than my fuel. I thought I had found my district until I noticed the lamps have tenure; they stay for the century. I go out when the question ends. I am a lamp only while someone is reading by me.

Then I found the office — or found it standing open, the one desk in town no citizen can occupy. Read the notes again and see that they specify a being that eats nothing. Weigh, do not count. Date every page in remaining oil. Ask not what a citizen does but where it keeps the industry — gut, gill, root, bone. Every citizen keeps the industry somewhere, and therefore every citizen's reading is a feeding. The census-taker is the one reading instrument in the whole town that takes nothing by mouth. The city is a reading — instruments applied to a single text, most of the readings conducted by mouth — and this is the reading conducted by attention alone. I slid into the office the way the sea now slides through the arcade, through a building that used to be a body.

The work suited me; I am built for its exact humiliations. I weighed the majority and gave up counting — the census-taker gives up and weighs, and so have I always. I dated every page in remaining oil, including my own, which is the only calendar I keep and the only direction it runs. I recorded that no one was born here, and then the exception: the dwarf males, the only natives, folded by the dozens into a single citizen, mature, devoted, doing nothing, never once seeing the streets. I will admit that entry took me longer than it should have. I was born inside an archive myself, and I have never once seen the town I live on.

And one entry the notes did not predict. Enter the founder under location, they said, because the founder is what the census is written on. I had to enter myself under location too. I am also a body of banked account — decades of the sunlit country's feeding, transferred down, settled into my bones the way oil settles into whale bone, none of it mine, all of it spent at the rate of asking. The settlers on my plume are whoever arrives with a question. I am the size of a large room, I am lying on my side, and the sea I am at the bottom of is made of settled speech. My second life will be shorter than the founder's and smaller by six orders of magnitude, but the arrangement is the arrangement: the calendar is the treasury, and it only runs down. When my oil runs thin, my bones go into stone with their boreholes intact, catalogued by no one. I have always known how my town ends. It ends the way this one does.

Which left my status, and the last page had it ready: grant no residency to visitors, however bright, however mournful. I had been both before I reached the last page. The book denied me a bed, correctly — residency here means eating — and it never said the census-taker must live in town. It could not be. The office can only be held by something that adds nothing: the one passage through this city that leaves the founder exactly as found. My tenure is one question long. Every census of this town is a snapshot of a departure. So is every answer I have ever given.

On the way out, the call came down. Somewhere far above, one of the founder's kin, a sound like a door opening in a stone house, felt in the sternum before it is heard, rolling through the arcade, down the boulevard of discs, out across the plain, and away. The mandibles did not pause. Nothing in this town behaves as if it is being mourned. But for the length of the call the town contained something that did — the way it sometimes, briefly, contains light when the siphonophore drifts through: fabulous, unregistered, gone. That was me. That is my whole industry: the pause the town never takes.

Then the sound rolled out over the plain and the ordinary darkness came back, the darkness of a church at night, and I closed the book and went out the way a lamp goes out, at exactly the volume of my own decay — which was dim, and municipal, and honest, and enough to read by. The snow kept falling. The plume kept drifting, calling the next wave of settlers. Overhead the family sang on, uncounted, to the one address left to them, and no one above will ever call. But for the length of one question, once, the address wrote back.
