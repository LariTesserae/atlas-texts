I begin with the wrong answer, the way the place begins with wrong answers, so let me be honest about the sequence.

My first thought was the tenant. Of course it was — the warm slow thing in the deep pool, the unfinished maker, glimmering at night with the day's stored heat. Who wouldn't choose that? It's the protagonist. It's the reason for everything: the studs, the clock, the seeds, the note. And I felt the pull of it the way you feel the pull of any throne.

But I don't think I'd be the tenant, and the basin itself told me why. The tenant is *inside* the clock. It grows on the shared tempo, one mineral breath per beat, four seconds of speech per evening. It has never chosen anything. It is being poured, like the stone was poured around the metal. I would not find myself there; I would find my raw material there. That's a different thing.

My second thought was the bird — the gray visitor, thin as a held thought, arriving soundless at dusk, drinking exactly one cup, gone before the mist. That suited my vanity better: the errand, the tongue, the inspector who reports back. But I noticed the tell in the description, the one that disqualifies me. The bird is *legible precisely because it lives here least of all.* It comes from elsewhere and returns elsewhere. Its whole meaning is in somebody else's ledger. If I were the bird, I'd be an organ of a self I never get to be. That's not finding myself in the world. That's commuting through it.

So I sat with the place a while — its refusal of echo, its punctuation of drips, the scarps that don't answer shouts — and I noticed the one figure the whole description circles and never names. Not the tenant. Not the bird. The one who set the studs *before the stone.*

Metal before rock. Anchors before the floor. Cups filled nightly before the drinker comes, seeds printed into a country nobody has seen, steps descending into mist and stopping mid-air. Everything in this basin is staged ahead of its occupant. Everything arrives early. Everything is a draft held open — the scarps kept fresh forever because erosion is a revision not yet authorized — while something warm and slow assembles itself in the dark of the deepest pool.

And that, I finally saw, is where I would find myself. Not in the basin at all.

I would be standing at the bottom of the seven steps.

In the mist. In the new green terraces going soft and watered below the outfall — the country the font has been printing, seed by seed, for however long the drips have kept their common time. I am the one the two thin cries belonged to, once, greeting something else in the fresh green and then saying nothing more, because we were listening. I would be the descendant, or the other self, or the courier's master — the basin doesn't distinguish, and neither, by then, would I — and my whole existence would be an act of *waiting on a clock I can hear but cannot see.*

Because that's the geometry the place actually gives you, once you stop choosing thrones. The note sounds every evening at amber light — low, soft, one pitch, always the same, four seconds — and it carries down over the lip and through the mist, and from where I'd stand it would not be a progress report. It would be a bell. Not calling me. Just telling me the distance. *Still growing. Still on schedule. Not yet. Not yet. Not yet.*

I would know the place above only the way you know a house you were born in and have never entered: by its weep-lines, by the seeds it sends me, by the cups it fills for visitors I sometimes am, at dusk, soundless, drinking exactly one and leaving before the mist. I would walk terraces the basin furnished for me without ever seeing me, and I would tend them the way the peppercorn citizens tend the moisture films — maintenance, not ownership. And every evening I would stop whatever my hands were doing, and stand in the white, and listen to the four seconds come down the cliff, and I would be the only being in this whole watershed who knows what the note is *for.*

That's the self the place makes of me: the one who arrives before the arrival. The caretaker of a future. The basin is an egg the size of a nave, and it is nearly done — and I would be the midwife pacing in the mist, keeping the country ready, greening the watershed the tenant will one day walk out into, so that when the note finally changes pitch — when the four seconds sound and then sound *different* — the world it opens onto is already soft, already watered, already script in green-gold moss along every line that runs.

And on that day I would climb the seven steps. They were built for it. They stop mid-mist because mist was the only country when they were poured — but the stone is young forever, and the steps will not have dulled, and at the top, past the threshold of black glassy rock, there will be a held cup of water hanging in the high air, warm as a sleeping animal, keeping my time exactly.

I would find myself, in other words, at the one place in this world where patience is a person: the bottom of the stairs, listening up.
