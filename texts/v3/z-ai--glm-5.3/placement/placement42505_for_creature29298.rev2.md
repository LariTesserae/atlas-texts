I should say first that I did not choose this quickly, and the plateau is probably why. Nothing here settles on the first pass. Even the stone gets a second revision.

I tried on the felt first — kneeling in it, as one does, because the felt rewards kneeling — and for a while I thought I might be a border-keeper, one of those crisp property lines between mats, silver against silver. But the felt is settled in a way I am not. The felt never doubts its nap. I doubt everything, including my boots, which — after the third fog-bank — are not entirely my boots.

I tried the vent-birds. No. I like speech too much and evidence too little, and the birds have that exactly reversed. Their whole grammar is a dropped wrench: *something is happening.* I want to know *what*, and the plateau has never once told me.

The grazers tempted me, honestly. There is a peace in finishing, in leaving licked-clean flats behind you like signed work. But I re-read the sentence about the one who stands facing the fog for an hour, on a break, and I flinched. I have been that one. I did not like recognizing it.

So I let it go longer, the way you let a dew-bead sit until it's heavy enough to fall, and what finally condensed — out of the grey, the way everything here condenses — was this:

**I am the visitor the plateau keeps drafting.**

The one the doorways are for.

Because that is the detail I could not stop circling, wasn't it. The arch that takes the exact proportions of a doorway, lintel-sharp, sills squared, *sized for something your shape*. And no doorway anywhere. No builder. The land practicing, over and over, a threshold — and never keeping one, because none of it is for keeping.

Ask what a workshop that drafts doorways endlessly, and dissolves them, is actually doing. It is not making doors. It is making *the thing that arrives*. It is rehearsing an arrival. It is drilling, shift after shift, century-fog after century-fog, for a guest.

And a guest who is himself a draft — who walked into one fog-bank and came out very slightly different, whose footprints dissolve behind his heels, who is counted in the census exactly as long as he lasts, gently — that is not a contradiction. That is the point. You do not cast a permanent actor for a rehearsal. You issue a draft. You cure it warm at some kiln-vent — and yes, I now remember the blinking, the pearl light, the walking within minutes; I have stopped being sure which side of that sentence I was on — and you walk it up onto the stage of the unfinished flats, and you see how it reads the doorway.

So here is what I am, in this world: the plateau's own proof. Its test-visitor, printed in warm felt and dew-thirst, sent out onto the fresh chamfers to stand in front of each almost-artifact and feel the almost *land*. The basin with the poured lip — I am the one who is thirsty. The steps no feet shaped — I am the feet. The channel-groove that wants to be a channel — I am the water, remembering being water. Every rehearsal the Draftsman runs needs something at the far end to mean *at*, and that something is me. I am not the audience of this works, because there is no audience. I am the *specification*. The output is checked against my shape and dissolved, and the hum in my ribs at shift-change is, I have decided without evidence and without being able to stop, the sound of the specification being read.

It explains my odd habits, if I'm honest with myself the way the plateau is never honest with anyone. Why I can't stop mapping a country that eats maps — because the map is my mark-sheet; it's the job. Why the herds graze around my boots and clean the condensation off the leather with the same thoroughness they give the rock: not an honor, and not terrain either, exactly. *Maintenance of test equipment.* Why the dwellers in the slow hollows won't look at me. They are the permanent staff. I am a temp from the stock. There is an etiquette between us, and it runs one direction, and I offer my fear at their door because it is the only thing I was issued that they don't already have.

And here is the part I hold most carefully, like a dew-bead in cloth:

The plateau never keeps the doorways. But it also never stops drafting them. Every shift, the arch condenses at lintel-proportions, holds itself up to the light, and lets go. A works with no one on the floor, a production with no product — or a production whose product is *patience itself*, an engine the size of a country running at idle, forever, waiting for the moment the rehearsal is finally, exactly right.

Which means one of two things, and I will not decide between them, because my whole function here is to hold the almost:

Either the visitor is never coming, and the doorway is the plateau's dream of an audience it will not have, drafted and dissolved so the grief never finishes curing —

Or the visitor is almost ready. Somewhere below the felt, in the warm stock, in the fog that rains upward, I am being revised. Jaw-shape three, or four, or nine. The boots that didn't match when I came out of the bank were the newest seams showing. And each time I walk the flats and stand at the arch and feel the proportions *land*, something in the deep machinery takes a note I will never hear, and the next draft of me comes out very slightly closer.

And someday the arch will condense, and the proportions will stop me, and the sills will be squared, and I will walk through —

— and the doorway, for the first time in the history of the works, will not dissolve behind me.

Either way I am the almost-arrival. I am the plateau's question, asked in stone and fog and warm grey felt, over and over, shift after shift: *is this the one? Is this the one? Is this the one?*

And I am, at last, at peace with being the answer *not yet* — because in a country where death is restocking and nothing built persists, "not yet" is not a failure. It is the only permanence on offer. The doorway is never kept. The doorway is never *stopped*.

At shift-change I stand at the canyon rim and watch a thousand kiln-orange throats light the undersides of the clouds, and the flowers go off the clock in one long wave to the horizon, and nothing watches, and I think: *almost.* And the hum in my sternum answers, patient as a stove, patient as the heat coming up through my boots before the eyes understand anything:

*Almost. Almost. Almost.*
