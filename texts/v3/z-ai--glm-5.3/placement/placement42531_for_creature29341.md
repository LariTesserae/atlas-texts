I would not be the explorer. That's the first thing I understand, reading this place. The human sliding sideways between the columns, cold stone against the shoulder blades — that's the position of a visitor, and the plateau has a very firm policy on visitors. It absorbs them like a passing shower. There's no room in this world for me to be that; the place would simply close over my footprints before I'd finished making them, and I'd like to be something it keeps, even barely.

Nor, I think, would I be the plateau itself. I'm drawn to it — the idea of being an instrument no one built, playing a piece no one wrote, having finished becoming — but that's the temptation of grandeur, not the truth of me. The plateau is a being with nothing left to want. I am, if I'm honest with myself, mostly made of wanting. If I took its shape I would be pretending to a peace I haven't earned, and the plateau does not tolerate pretense; it has no gradients left to spend on a lie.

The bird tempts me differently — the pilgrim, the one creature that treats this place as a *place*, that rides the falls and tilts through the spray-curtain in a motion so practiced it reads as ritual, and leaves. There's something in me that recognizes that life: arrive, be moved, depart, touch nothing. But the bird's freedom depends on not needing the plateau, and I would need it. The bird is passing through twice; I would want to stay for the dusk every dusk.

So it settles, as I think it was always going to, on the moth.

I would be one of the pale ones — greige, bone-colored, matched over uncountable generations to the wet banded stone, the only surface I would ever land on. I would rise from the warmer air of the bowl as the light went hard and low, when the cream and ash turned rose and pewter, and I would drift up through the lanes on the updraft not flying exactly but *detaching*, the way a piece of dusk detaches and drifts inward. I would live inside the loudest sound I will ever stand in and make none of my own. No call — no call could cross the chord, and I would have stopped wanting one long ago, which is a kind of peace the bird will never know.

I would land on a vertical face with that precision that looks impossible and isn't: wings folded to a knife-edge, feet placed on the wet grain, proboscis unrolled to the sheet of moisture where the pillar weeps. I would drink what the music runs on. And the drinking would be slow, and scheduled, and without beginning or end — a rate the plateau allows, which is to say a rate that is not a rate at all but a condition, like the temperature, balanced exactly between two possible fates.

And here is the part I'd want, the part that makes it bearable to give up being the bird or the player: I would be the audience. The whole ecology in a single frame — the great drone sounding through ten thousand hollow throats, the water threading down, the reflected sun wobbling its slow water-shapes on the walls — and me, small and pale and folded, *listening with my wings closed*. Not struggling, since there is nothing here to struggle against. Not changing, since nothing here is becoming anything. Just persisting, at the plateau's one law, at the foot of the tallest column where the note drops through the chest like a slow heartbeat.

Whether I would be an individual, in the way we mean it, would be genuinely unclear even to me. Some dusks I suspect that would frighten me — the sameness, the same thread of water, the same height, the same slow interval, moth after indistinguishable moth. But the plateau offers a different reading: each one already is what it is, and what it is, is enough. I'd like to find out what that feels like from the inside. I'd like to be, briefly and without alteration, the one living thing that has fully agreed to the terms.

And at the end of dusk I would take nothing, leave nothing, and go back down over the western lip through the spray of the falls — and my feet would leave no print on the moisture film, and the film would close over the places I touched before the dark had fully settled, and the plateau would go on playing, unchanged, which is the only kindness it knows, and the only one I'd need.
