The first thing you notice is the gap.

The vegetation is total—there is no soil, no stone, no water surface, no sky except what filters through layers of green. Yet between these masses of plant life stretch hollows so complete they feel deliberate, as though the forest has been carefully hollowed out from within. A single tree, perhaps, or the memory of one: a cathedral of space where the walls are bark and the ceiling is a distant lattice of leaves, and the floor is a trampoline of roots so thick and interlaced they have become terrain.

This is the **Root-Hollow of Vess**, a lowland basin where the world has settled into its final form. The erosion of a million years has not worn it down but *compressed* it, flattened everything into a single dense plane of living matter. The hills are gone. The valleys have filled with root and rhizome until the surface is a taut skin, thrumming with hidden pressure. You walk on vegetation that has become geology: a lignin crust, fibrous and faintly warm, that gives slightly underfoot like the deck of a ship.

And it is loud. Always loud.

The engines are below.

---

**The Scale of Emptiness**

The Hollow is perhaps two hundred meters across—a single grove, a bounded room in the forest. Its emptiness is architectural. The vegetation does not sparse out at the edges; it simply stops, sheared into walls of compressed growth that rise thirty meters to a canopy so dense it might be solid. Light comes through in shafts, normal daylight, unremarkable in quality except that it must fight through so much green to reach you. The air is cool, damp in pockets, smelling of earth and oil and something sweetly fermenting.

The fauna are here, abundant and unconcerned. Beetles the size of your thumb trace spirals on the root-floor. Something quick and furred—vole, shrew, something without a name—darts between fissures in the crust. Birdsong, when it happens, comes from *within* the walls, not above; the canopy is too thick to nest in, so the birds have gone inward, hollowing galleries in the vegetal mass. Their calls are muffled, directional, like voices through apartment walls.

But the engines drown all this in rhythm.

---

**The Industrial Substrate**

They are not machines you can see. The tech is 1.7/3—industrial, but integrated, scaled to production you cannot witness. The sound comes through the root-floor: a deep chugging, a hiss of steam, the metallic *clack-clack* of something being pressed, stamped, formed. It vibrates in your sternum. It is the sound of the forest metabolizing.

This is the oldest-stable-state. The engines have been running so long they have become ecology. The vegetation grows *around* them, *through* them, incorporating pistons and condensers into its vascular tissue. The dampness you feel—the occasional droplet on your neck, the film of moisture on certain root-knots—is not weather. It is condensation from the cooling systems, wept upward through capillary action. The rain here is internal.

Walk toward the center of the Hollow. The emptiness intensifies. The fauna thin out. The root-floor becomes smoother, more polished, until you stand on a surface like black glass that is still, impossibly, alive. It pulses faintly. The engine-sound is loudest here, and you understand: this is the **throat** of the system, where the vegetation opens to receive whatever the machines below are producing.

The gap is not absence. It is *anticipation*.

---

**The Stable Cycle**

Nothing changes. The dynamic is frozen, a perfect equilibrium of input and output. The engines take in something—perhaps the ancient compressed matter of the forest itself, perhaps something fed to them through pipes that were once roots—and they produce... what? Heat. Vibration. The occasional gout of steam that finds its way through a fissure and becomes mist, briefly, before the vegetation drinks it back.

The fauna have adapted to this. The beetles follow thermal gradients, clustering where the root-floor is warmest. The furred things hibernate in sync with the engines' cycles. Even the birdsong, you realize, has a rhythm that matches the chugging below—an accidental chorus, or perhaps a negotiated one.

The weirdness is minimal because none of this is strange anymore. It has simply *been*, for longer than memory. The erosion of time has not destroyed the system but polished it to this: a single hollow, a bounded space, where total vegetation meets total emptiness, where industrial process has become as natural as photosynthesis.

---

**To Stand Here**

You are small. The Hollow is cathedral-scale but not grand—intimate, rather, like a chapel carved for a single worshipper. The light is ordinary. The coolness is ordinary. The dampness is ordinary. Only the sound is extraordinary, and only until you have heard it long enough.

Then it becomes silence.

Then the engines are the quietest thing here.

Then you are the loudest thing here, breathing, shifting, alive in a way that has not yet been incorporated into the system.

You wait. The vegetation waits. The engines wait.

The gap remains.
