**The Bright Unburrow**

It is, first of all, a hole that should not be lit this way.

The space lies perhaps forty feet below the desert's skin — a cathedral-scale cavern with the specific gravity of a room, bounded and intimate despite its height. There are no tunnels leading to it, no shafts, no geological explanation for how light enters. It simply arrives, dense and white and constant, as if the ceiling itself were translucent to a sun that never moves. Shadows do not exist here; instead, every object casts a faint luminous counterpart, a ghost of brightness rather than darkness.

The moisture is intermittent, arriving as slow condensation that gathers on specific stones over decades, then releases. The floor is not wet so much as *remembering* wetness — patches of darker sand where dampness has persisted long enough to stain the mineral content, creating the impression of islands in a dry sea. Occasionally, a pocket of water forms in a natural basin, no larger than a teacup, and remains for exactly seventeen years before evaporating in a single afternoon. The cycle is precise, patient, and indifferent to any observer.

Vegetation exists in a state of suspended decision. Three species, perhaps four, maintain themselves without spreading. A translucent grass grows in tufts exactly six feet apart, each blade following the same curvature as if bent by a wind that hasn't blown for millennia. A low succulent forms rings around the condensation stones, thick leaves storing brightness rather than water. Nothing here competes. Nothing encroaches.

The fauna is abundant in presence, absent in movement. Small, pale creatures — too regular in their placement, too still in their postures — occupy the space like punctuation. They resemble nothing taxonomic: six-legged things with the texture of wet paper, slow-moving shelled beings that leave no trail, a single species of winged creature that flies in perfect geometric patterns for exactly one hour each day, then roosts in precise formation on the walls. They do not eat, apparently. They do not reproduce visibly. They simply *occupy*, maintaining a density that feels ritual rather than biological.

There are no structures, yet the space has been arranged. Stones the size of human skulls rest in patterns that suggest intention without revealing it — circles within circles, lines that converge on empty points. Primitive tools appear and disappear: a stone blade, a length of knotted fiber, a clay vessel cracked in identical ways each time it manifests. They belong to no one. They serve no purpose. They are the residue of a technology that has forgotten its users.

The sound is mostly the absence of sound — a thick, pressurized silence broken only by the occasional tick of condensation falling from ceiling to stone, a rhythm so slow it might be the heartbeat of the place itself. When the winged creatures fly, they produce no wingbeat, only a faint harmonic hum that resonates in the teeth.

Time here moves in geological increments that somehow feel immediate. Erosion has settled into permanence; the walls are smooth not from water or wind, but from *attention*, as if the rock itself has been watched into submission. Nothing changes, yet nothing is static. The condensation stones sweat, the creatures shift their precise formations by millimeters, the light maintains its impossible constancy.

It is empty in the way a held breath is empty. It is primitive in the way a first thought is primitive. It is bright without source, damp without water, abundant without life as we understand it.

The rules here are simple: stay as long as you like. You will not leave footprints. You will not be remembered. But something in the pattern will adjust, infinitesimally, to accommodate the fact that you were here — then adjust back, patient as stone, once you are gone.
