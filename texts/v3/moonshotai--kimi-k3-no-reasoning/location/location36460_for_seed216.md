The altar hangs.

It has always hung. A disc of continent, a world-shard, suspended in a gray, silent void. It is an artifact, yes, built by hands far beyond the memory of the life that now coats it, but the meaning of it, the purpose of its original geometry, is lost to a dizzying, unbroken expanse of time.

Its light is a faint, cooling ember in the center of its sky—a dying god that weeps a weak, saffron glow. This is not a world of days and nights, but of a perpetual, humid twilight, a subaqueous haze that thickens into shadow near the edges of the disc, where the world simply stops.

The original structure, a lattice of impossible, non-oxidizing alloys, is now a ghost. It is a scaffold, a petrified skeleton holding the form, entirely subsumed by flesh. For the substrate of this world is not stone or soil, but a single, continent-spanning plant: the Fell-Blooded Weave.

It is a warm, wet, breathing skin of vegetation. It has grown over the ancient construct, infiltrated its voids, and learned its architecture, mimicking its forms in lignin and leaf. There are buttresses of braided, woody vine thicker than rivers, arching into the mist. There are spires that were once, perhaps, cooling fins or sensor arrays, now towers of pulpy, vascular tissue, sweating moisture from a billion stomata. The ground itself is a spongy, fibrous mat of root-flesh, resilient and treacherous, which gurgles and sighs underfoot. Condensation clings to every surface; great, slow drips fall from the leaf-vaults far above, and thin, mineral-rich water sheets down the synthetic sinews of the world, pooling in hollows that are like the inside of a living organ.

This is the Hanging Ecumene.

Life here is not diverse, but singular and manifold. The Weave is all. It is the archivist, the architect, the substrate. Its parts have specialized into a mockery of an ecosystem. There are bladder-trees, vast, translucent sacs of stored water that pulse with a slow, peristaltic rhythm. There are filter-fans, delicate lattices of fibrous tissue that hang in the warm, still air, siphoning nutrient-rich dust that the Weave itself exhales. There are thicket-channels, hollow, tube-like growths that serve as arteries, pumping a warm, milky sap from the core of the disc to its furthest, light-starved edges.

In this flesh-made world, other life persists.

The fauna are remnants, or perhaps, passengers. Descendants of a long-dead crew’s menagerie, or spore-borne travelers that found this hanging garden. They are woven into the Weave’s metabolism. Herds of slow, six-legged `grazers` move across the root-plains, their broad, flat teeth stripping the photosynthetic skin from the Weave, which regrows behind them with visible speed. In the deeper thickets, `ambush-flora`—predatory, mobile offshoots of the Weave—have evolved. They are slow, patient things, their thorny tendrils waiting for weeks to snag a passing `vesper-wing`, a creature of silent, leathery flight that navigates the gloom by taste and touch.

The only true sound is the sound of the place itself: a deep, sub-audible hum that is more felt than heard. The groan of the Weave’s growth, the slow peristalsis of its vascular system, the gentle, endless drip of water. There is no wind, only the slow, convectional breath of the world-shard as it cycles its own warm, wet air.

And there is the Sickness of Meaning.

Here, at this scale and age, reality has grown thin. The laws of physics are not broken, but they are… tired. They are more like habits. In the deeper shadow-regions, near the great roots that anchor the world to nothing, space grows soft. A drop of water may fall upwards for a moment before remembering its duty. The geometry of the vine-buttresses can be untrustworthy; a straight path may lead in a circle, a short distance may take a lifetime to cross. Time itself is a viscous fluid, moving in slow, deep currents. A creature might be born, live, and die in what feels like a single, drawn-out moment, while the great leaf overhead has not yet finished unfolding.

The Hanging Ecumene is a wound in the universe that has not healed, but has instead grown a beautiful, terrible scar. A place of impossible, silent life, built upon a ghost of intent, slowly digesting its own meaning in the warm, wet, and endless dusk.
