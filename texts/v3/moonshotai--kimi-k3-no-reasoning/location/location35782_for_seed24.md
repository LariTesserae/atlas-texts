# The Keth Hollow

---

At the bottom of the world, where the crust folds inward and the light thins to nothing, there is a hollow.

It is not a cave. There is no ceiling, though darkness sits heavy overhead as if there were one, pressing down the way deep water presses, without weight that can be measured but with presence that can be felt. The sky, if it exists above, has forgotten this place. No stars resolve. No moon turns. The darkness is not black — it is the colour of old bronze, of tarnish, of something that was once bright and has been oxidizing for longer than minds can hold. A deep brown-amber murk, uniform and total, through which the eye learns to find shape by contrast the way a hand learns to read a page in the dark.

The hollow is small. You could cross it in an afternoon — perhaps eight hundred paces from rim to rim in the long direction, five hundred across. It sits in a basin of low ground, a shallow trough in terrain that has been worn almost to smoothness. There are no mountains here. There are barely hills. The land tilts and swells with a patience that borders on the absolute, rising and falling over distances so gradual that the body cannot feel the slope, only the direction of effort when walking. Up. Down. The ground decides, not the walker.

---

## The Ground

The surface is stone, but stone that has given up being stone.

It is a silicate crust, dense and iron-rich, the colour of dried blood where it is darkest, pale as baked clay where the mineral content shifts. In places it is solid — long plates of fused rock, each roughly the size of a shield, that meet at seams filled with a finer grit. In other places the crust has fractured and collapsed into talus, into a scree of angular fragments that shift underfoot with a dry, granular clatter. In still other places — the oldest places — the surface has been worn so thoroughly that it is simply dust. Not sand. Not soil. Dust, fine as flour, heavy as iron, that rises in thin plumes when disturbed and settles slowly, reluctantly, back down.

There is no moisture. There has never been moisture. The dust is not damp. The stone is not cool. Everything is dry in a way that goes beyond aridity — it is dryness as a fundamental condition, dryness as ontology. The chemistry here does not involve water. It does not involve liquid at all. The reactions that happen in this stone happen slowly, between solids, at temperatures that would be comfortable on skin but that have been constant for a span of time so long that the constancy has become a feature of the landscape itself. The temperature does not vary. It is warm — always warm, exactly warm, the same warmth at every point in the hollow, at every depth, at every hour that there is no hour to mark. Warm the way a stone held in a closed hand is warm. Warm the way the inside of a body is warm. A warmth that comes from below, from the slow pressure of the planet's interior, conducted upward through kilometres of rock and delivered, evenly and without variation, to every surface in the hollow.

No wind. This bears emphasis. The air — if air is the right word for the thin, still envelope of gas that sits inside the basin — does not move. There are no convection currents because there is no temperature gradient. There are no pressure differentials because the whole system is in equilibrium. The dust that rises when a foot strikes the ground does not blow away. It hangs. It settles. It returns to the ground it came from, and it lies there, exactly where it fell, for a duration that has no unit.

## The Vegetation

Sparse does not quite describe it. The things that grow here are sparse the way certain thoughts are sparse — present, real, but separated by such distances of nothing that each one feels like an accident.

They are not plants. They are something adjacent to plants — organisms that have solved the problem of living without water, without light, without seasonal variation, and the solution has made them strange. They are called *keth* by the people who once named them, though the people are gone and the name remains only because names do.

Each keth is a low, broad structure — rarely taller than a hand's width, rarely broader than an arm's reach. It grows directly out of the stone, or out of the dust, and its body is a lattice of thin, dark filaments, each no thicker than a hair, that interweave and branch and re-fuse into a dense, wiry mat. The mat is rigid. It does not bend. It does not sway. It sits on the ground like a dark stain, like a shadow that has decided to stay after the light has left.

The filaments are black, or nearly black, a darkness that absorbs what little light exists and returns almost nothing. They are smooth, hard, mineralized. The organism is not photosynthetic — there is no light to photosynthesize with. It is chemosynthetic. It draws energy from the slow oxidation of iron and sulphur compounds in the substrate, metabolizing at a rate so low that a single keth might take a century to grow the width of a fingernail. They are among the oldest living things in the hollow, which means they are among the oldest living things anywhere, because everything here is old and nothing here dies quickly.

They are spaced apart. Each keth occupies its own territory, separated from the next by a gap of bare stone or bare dust that might span a pace or might span twenty. There is no pattern to the spacing. There is no clustering. They are distributed the way stars are distributed in a very empty sky — each one alone, each one a singular fact in an expanse of absence.

When you look closely — when you kneel in the darkness and let your eyes adjust and hold your face close to the surface — you can see that the filaments are not perfectly still. They move, but at a rate so slow that the movement is not visible in real time. They grow. They reach. They curl toward concentrations of mineral in the substrate with the same inevitability as roots seeking water, but over years, not hours. A keth that you examine today will be different in a decade, slightly, a new filament here, a retraction there, the mat having shifted its configuration by degrees too small for any instrument to measure in a single sitting.

## The Sound

The hollow is not silent.

This is a distinction that matters. Silence is the absence of sound. What exists here is something else — a sound so low, so slow, so constant that it occupies the threshold of hearing without crossing it. It is a vibration, felt in the chest more than heard in the ears. It is the sound of stone in equilibrium. The sound of the planet's weight settling into itself, of the crust flexing under its own mass, of mineral structures finding their lowest energy state and holding it.

It does not pulse. It does not rhythm. It simply is. A continuous, sub-audible hum, like the sound of a transformer heard through a wall, but lower, deeper, more fundamental. If you press your ear to the ground, you can feel it more clearly — a faint, incessant trembling in the stone, as if the planet were a vast animal and the hollow a spot on its flank where the heartbeat can be felt.

When the talus shifts underfoot — the dry clatter of fragment on fragment — the sound is startlingly loud by contrast. It rings out in the stillness and does not echo, because there is nothing to echo from, no walls, no ceiling, no surfaces arranged to return the sound. It simply radiates outward and is absorbed by the darkness, and the silence that follows is deeper for having been broken.

---

## The Light

There is no light source.

This is not a place where light has been removed. It is a place where light has never been a primary condition. What illumination exists — and something must exist, or the eye would have nothing to work with — comes from the stone itself. Faint phosphorescence in certain mineral veins, a cold, amber-brown glow that emanates from within the rock like light trapped in amber. It is not bright. It is barely enough to see by. But it is present, and it is constant, and it gives the darkness its particular quality — not black, but dark brown, dark gold, the colour of old honey held up to a candle in a dark room.

The phosphorescence is not uniform. It is concentrated in seams and pockets, so the landscape is a topography of dimness — here a brighter patch where a vein runs close to the surface, there a zone of deeper dark where the stone is pure and unmineralized. The keth absorb this light. Their dark filaments drink it in and return nothing, so that each keth sits in a small depression of darkness, a point of shadow within the shadow.

There are no shadows cast. There is no directional light. The phosphorescence comes from below, from within, and it illuminates upward, so that any object — a stone, a ridge, a standing fragment of rock — is lit from beneath, which gives everything an inverted quality, as if the world had been turned upside down and the light were falling up.

## The Erosion

Nothing here is sharp.

This is the defining quality of the hollow, more than its darkness, more than its dryness, more than its silence. Everything — every stone, every ridge, every fragment of talus, every edge of every plate in the crust — has been worn smooth. Not by water. Not by wind. By time. By the slow, incessant, molecular-scale abrasion of dust settling and dust rising and dust settling again, over durations so long that the process has the quality of a geological force.

The result is a landscape of curves. Gentle, rounded, unhurried curves. There are no cliffs. There are no outcrops. There are no angles. Every transition from one surface to another is gradual, smooth, continuous. A stone that protrudes from the crust does not jut — it swells. A depression does not drop — it descends. The whole terrain has the quality of something that has been held in a hand and turned over and over until every edge has been rubbed away, until the surface is as smooth as a worry stone, as smooth as bone, as smooth as something that has been loved by time.

Even the talus — the broken fragments — are rounded. Angular when they fracture, they become rounded within a few centuries as the dust works on them. Nothing here stays broken for long. The hollow heals. It smooths. It brings everything back to the same gentle, undulating, featureless equilibrium.

## The Inhabitants

There are none.

Not in the sense of absence — there are the keth, and there are other organisms even smaller, even slower, that live in the substrate and metabolize minerals and do not have names. But in the sense of presence, of occupation, of someone being here — there is nothing. No animals. No insects. No birds. No mammals. No reptiles. Nothing that moves quickly. Nothing that makes noise. Nothing that builds. Nothing that hunts or is hunted. Nothing that eats and is eaten. The food chain here is a chain of one link: mineral to microbe, microbe to mineral, a closed loop with no exits and no entrances.

The people who named the keth are gone. They were here once — the evidence is so faint that it requires imagination to see it — but they are gone, and they left nothing. No structures. No tools. No marks on the stone. Whatever they did here, they did it lightly, and the hollow has absorbed their passage the way it absorbs everything, smoothing it, rounding it, returning it to equilibrium.

Perhaps they were the first to notice the phosphorescence. Perhaps they were the first to kneel in the darkness and put their hands on the keth and feel the slow, patient life beneath their palms. Perhaps they came here because the hollow was quiet, because it was warm, because it was stable, because it was a place where nothing changed and nothing demanded and nothing rushed. Perhaps they came here to rest, and rested, and left.

The hollow does not remember them. The hollow does not remember anything. Memory requires change, and change does not happen here.

---

## The Time

Time does not pass in the hollow.

This is not a metaphor. It is a description of the physical conditions. There is no day. There is no night. There is no season. There is no tide. There is no weather. There is no variation in temperature, in light, in humidity (there is no humidity), in pressure (there is no variation in pressure). The system is in equilibrium — not the equilibrium of a pendulum at rest, which implies the possibility of motion, but the equilibrium of a system that has reached its lowest energy state and will remain there indefinitely.

A stone placed in the hollow today will be in the same position in a thousand years. A keth that is growing now will be growing then, slightly larger, slightly different in configuration, but recognizably the same organism. The dust that settles tonight will be the dust that lies there when the stars have burned out. The hum in the stone will be the same hum. The warmth will be the same warmth. The darkness will be the same darkness.

This is not stagnation. It is not death. It is a kind of completion. The hollow has finished becoming. It is what it is, and it will be what it is, and there is a peace in that — a peace so deep and so total that it is almost indistinguishable from nothingness, except that it is not nothing. It is something. It is this. It is the Keth Hollow, and it is here, and it will be here, and that is enough.

---

## The Feeling

To stand in the hollow is to feel something that has no name in most languages, because most languages evolved in places where things change.

It is the feeling of being held. Not by a hand, not by an embrace, but by the sheer, massive, unwavering stability of a place that does not change. The warmth against the skin. The hum in the bones. The darkness like a blanket, like a womb, like the inside of a seed. The keth, dark and patient, growing at a rate that cannot be perceived, alive in a way that cannot be measured, present in a way that cannot be denied.

It is the feeling of being outside of time. Not beyond it, not above it, but outside it — in a place where time does not apply, where the rules of sequence and duration and causation have been suspended, where the next moment is the same as this moment and the moment after that and the moment after that, forever, without end, without variation, without the need for variation.

It is a good feeling. It is a frightening feeling. It is both, and the both-ness is the point. The hollow does not care how you feel about it. It does not care that you are here. It does not care that you will leave. It will be here when you are gone, exactly as it was when you arrived, exactly as it was before you arrived, exactly as it will be when the concept of "you" has ceased to have meaning.

It will be here.

That is all it does. That is all it needs to do.

It is here.
