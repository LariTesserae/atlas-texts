The world of **Veshel** does not know darkness as an absence, but as a suspended, thickening medium. You stand—or rather, float, buoyed by the viscous atmospheric water—within the Apex Reach, where the altitude presses against the planet’s last exhalation of air. Everything here is submerged in the **Pale Flood**, a cold, dense atmospheric-water that fills the lungs like liquid glass. It is not rain; rain implies falling. This is the permanent suspension of a sky that became ocean eons ago, holding the cold of the upper void in its clear, light-refracting mass.

The horizon extends for regional distances, a vast plain of constructed matter stretching beyond the curve of the eye. This is the **Silt-Deep**, a landscape of total artifice, yet so ancient that it has achieved the stillness of geology. Once, perhaps, this was a city, or a machine, or a vessel; now it is a substrate of perfectly fitted white stone and corroded silver, eroded by the slow chemistry of the Flood into smooth, undulating hills of masonry and deep, drowned valleys of forgotten avenues. Nothing grows here. There are no roots, no spores, no green. The stone is bare, polished by the abrasive water and the endless, glacial temperature that hovers just above the point where this fluid would freeze solid.

Yet Veshel is not still. The dynamic is **turbulent**, a frantic, solaris-like rhythm of creation and destruction that plays out in the mineral substrate itself. The built environment—constructed by hands now dust within the silt—has achieved a kind of homeostatic instability. Watch the ridge of what might have been a colosseum or a capacitor bank: over the course of an hour, it slumps, the ancient concrete dissolving into a white slurry that flows upward (buoyed by the dense medium) to settle as new sediment elsewhere, instantly hardening into fresh, sharp geometries. The landscape is in constant, silent motion, a slow-motion avalanche of construction and collapse that has been eroding and rebuilding itself for ten million years. It is the oldest stable state: a chaos that never rests, never changes in its nature, only in its momentary configuration.

The fauna here are abundant, though sparse in their distribution across the open vastness. They are the **Null-Currents**, beings of translucent cartilage and bioluminescent nerve-net, adapted to the cold and the pressure. They are not fish; they are living geometries, tetrahedral and ribbon-like, that navigate the turbulent reconstruction of the world. They feed on the chemical energy released by the constant erosion of the built substrate, clustering in loose, drifting constellations where a tower has recently slumped, their dim blue lights pulsing in the murk. They make no sound—or rather, their sound is the only sound, a subsonic thrumming that vibrates through the water-atmosphere, felt in the bones rather than heard, a quiet bass note beneath the silence.

Light is dim, a perpetual twilight filtered through miles of suspended water. It comes from above, a weak, diffused radiance that suggests a sun too tired to burn bright, scattering into a pale grey-green luminescence that barely reaches the depths of the constructed canyons. Shadows are soft, long, and blue.

You move through this world with effort, the cold seeping into your core, the water thick in your throat. Around you, the rules are simple: everything is made, everything is old, everything is in flux, and nothing is alive in the way you remember life being—except the Null-Currents, who watch you with the absence of eyes, and the shifting stones, which build their own oblivion. The silence is broken only by the occasional, deep groan of a structure settling into its final form, immediately before it begins to dissolve again, starting the cycle over in the cold, dim, ancient deep.
