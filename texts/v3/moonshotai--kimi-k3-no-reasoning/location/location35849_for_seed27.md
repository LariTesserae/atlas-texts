# The Hollow Heights of Vess Meranth

## First Impression

From a distance — from the plains of cracked mineral far below, if anything down there still looked up — the city appears as a dark continent adrift. A mass without visible edges, silhouette folding into silhouette, backlit by a sky that never brightens past the deep amber of a dying coal. It hangs in the thin air at altitude, and it is *alive* in the way a reef is alive: not a single organism but a continent of them, layered and interpenetrated, each one ancient beyond reckoning.

Vess Meranth. The name is older than the language it was first spoken in.

---

## The Substrate

There is no rock. There is no soil. There is no water.

The entire visible world — the ground, the walls, the towers, the bridges, the sky-spanning arches — is plant. Not plant as Earth understands it: no chlorophyll, no leaves, no roots seeking moisture. The foundational organism is something the inhabitants call *uuth*, and it is less a species than a condition — a state that matter enters when it has been alive long enough. Uuth is dense, black-green in the dim light, fibrous at its surface and increasingly crystalline toward its core. It grows slowly. It has been growing for longer than the star has been dimming.

The oldest uuth — the material that forms the city's foundation platforms, the vast suspended continents that hang in the upper reaches — is indistinguishable from stone until you cut it. Then it bleeds a dry, pale powder, and the cut surface slowly, over decades, grows a skin of fine filaments that seal the wound. The city does not heal quickly. Nothing here does anything quickly. But it heals *completely*, and this is why Vess Meranth has survived.

Beneath the foundational platforms, the uuth continues downward in inverted towers — great root-spires that taper into the thin cold air, harvesting trace minerals from wind-borne dust, anchoring the city's mass against the constant low-pressure updrafts that sweep the heights. Some of these root-spires are kilometers long. They hum. Not metaphorically — they vibrate at low frequencies, a slow oscillation driven by the wind and by internal processes that may be respiration, may be circulation, may be something for which no word exists. The sound is always present: a basso drone that you feel in your sternum before you hear it with your ears.

---

## The Living Architecture

The city itself is grown, not built — or rather, the distinction collapsed so long ago that the current inhabitants no longer understand the question.

Structures emerge from the uuth substrate the way branches emerge from a trunk: gradually, with a thickening, a differentiation, a hollowing. A residential chamber begins as a swelling in a wall. Over years, the swelling develops an interior cavity. Over decades, the cavity shapes itself — walls thinning, floor smoothing, apertures opening where light or passage is needed. The inhabitants guide this process. They do not control it.

The technology of guidance is extraordinary and largely invisible. It operates at the cellular level of the uuth — not genetic engineering in any terrestrial sense, but something closer to *persuasion at the molecular scale*. The practitioners (the word translates roughly as "listeners" or "those who lean close") carry instruments no larger than a thumb, devices that emit patterned vibrational fields tuned to the resonance frequencies of uuth cell-clusters. A skilled listener can convince a wall to develop a window. Can persuade a floor to grow a staircase. Can — over the course of a lifetime — coax an entire district into a new configuration.

The results are architecture of a kind that no human tradition would recognize. Rooms that spiral. Corridors that breathe — actual rhythmic dilation, expanding and contracting on a cycle of roughly forty minutes, driven by the uuth's circulatory processes. Bridges that are grown from both sides simultaneously and meet in the middle with a seam that remains slightly warmer than the surrounding material for the rest of the bridge's existence. Towers that branch and branch again, each branch terminating in a bulb-like chamber that serves as dwelling, observatory, or garden.

The city is sparse in its occupation. Vast stretches of grown architecture stand empty — not abandoned, but *resting*. The uuth requires fallow periods. A structure that has been inhabited for centuries is left vacant for decades while its material recovers, the cellular structure re-densifying, the interior surfaces growing a fresh skin. The inhabitants migrate through the city in slow circuits, following patterns of occupation and vacancy that have been established for so long they have become indistinguishable from ritual.

---

## The Light

The star is old. It hangs in the sky like a bronze coin seen through smoke — present, warm-ish, but offering no sharpness. Shadows are soft. Edges blur. The light has the quality of late autumn afternoon stretched to fill every hour of every day.

The uuth does not photosynthesize in any conventional sense. It metabolizes ambient thermal differentials and trace atmospheric gases, processes that do not require bright light. But the city's inhabitants — and its fauna — have adapted to the dimness in their own ways. Eyes are large. Surfaces are pale where contrast is needed. Bioluminescence is widespread but restrained: small, cold lights, blue-white, that pulse slowly in the darkness of interior chambers. Not decoration — communication. The uuth itself produces these lights along its circulatory pathways, and the pattern of pulses carries information that the listeners can read.

In the deep interior of the larger structures — the great hollow towers, the multi-chambered residential masses — there is no natural light at all. The bioluminescent pathways become the sole illumination, tracing branching lines along walls and ceilings like a nervous system laid bare. To walk through these spaces is to move through the inside of a mind.

---

## The Sound

It is loud here. Not with the noise of machines or crowds, but with the accumulated voice of a living world at full operation.

The uuth speaks constantly. Its growth is not silent. Cell walls divide with minute pops and clicks that, multiplied across a structure the size of a district, merge into a continuous low patter — rain on a roof that is also the sky. The circulatory processes push fluid (not water — a thick, mineral-laden colloid, amber-colored, faintly sweet-smelling) through vessels that span kilometers, and the movement of this fluid produces a sound like the sea heard from a great distance: a wash, a surge, a retreat.

The wind at this altitude is thin but persistent, and it plays the city like an instrument. The hollow towers resonate. The bridges hum. The root-spires, kilometers below, vibrate at frequencies too low to hear but powerful enough to feel — a deep trembling in the bones of the city that the inhabitants navigate by, the way sailors once navigated by swell.

And then there are the fauna.

---

## The Fauna

Life in Vess Meranth is abundant but distributed — scattered across the vast living platforms like seeds on dark bread. The creatures here are not numerous in any one place, but they are *everywhere*, and they are loud.

The most immediately present are the **spine-drakes** — long, multi-segmented creatures, roughly the size of a large dog, that move through the city's corridors and chambers with a clattering, echoing gait. Their legs end in hardened hooks that click against the uuth surface, and they vocalize constantly: sharp, metallic cries that serve as echolocation in the dark interior spaces. A district at moderate population density might host a few dozen spine-drakes, and their calls, bouncing off curved walls, create a soundscape of overlapping rhythm — patterns that seem almost musical, almost intentional.

Larger are the **uuth-movers** — massive, slow creatures, the size of buildings, that live in symbiosis with the substrate. They graze on the uuth's surface, consuming dead or senescent material, and their digestive processes return processed mineral to the plant-mass in forms it can reabsorb. They are essential. Without them, the uuth would eventually choke on its own accumulated detritus. An uuth-mover is a landscape unto itself: broad-backed, many-legged, covered in a thick pelt of symbiotic moss (itself a variant of uuth, adapted to a mobile host). They move through the city on migration routes that take years to complete, and their passage reshapes the architecture — they push through walls that have grown too thin, open corridors that have sealed over, and their massive bodies leave smooth, polished channels in the uuth surface that the inhabitants then incorporate into their own structures.

The uuth-movers vocalize. The sound is extraordinary — a deep, sustained harmonic, produced by chambers in their thorax that resonate like organ pipes. Each individual has a distinct voice. The inhabitants know them by sound. When an uuth-mover approaches a district, its call precedes it by hours, a low chord that builds in the air until the walls themselves seem to sing in sympathy. The listeners gather. Doors are grown open. The city prepares.

There are smaller creatures. **Filament-birds** — not truly birds, but aerial organisms that drift through the thin air on membranes of modified uuth-fiber, feeding on airborne spores and mineral dust. They cluster around the root-spires, hundreds of them at a time, and their wings produce a dry whispering sound, like pages turning in a vast library. **Chamber-wights** — small, translucent, amphibious things that live in the uuth's circulatory vessels, keeping them clear of blockages. They glow faintly and sing at high frequencies, a crystalline tinkling that overlays the city's deeper voices like frost on a dark field.

## The Ancientness

Vess Meranth is old in a way that has ceased to be measurable. The uuth substrate, at its deepest and most foundational levels, has passed through so many cycles of growth, senescence, death, and regrowth that its material is essentially *post-biological* — a composite of organic and mineral so thoroughly interpenetrated that the distinction has become academic. The oldest uuth is closer to granite than to tissue. It does not grow. It does not heal. It simply *persists*, and the younger uuth grows over it, around it, through it, incorporating it into the living structure like bone incorporated into a reef.

Erosion here is not a process of wearing away. It is a process of *settling*. The city has been finding its final form for millennia. Structures that were once sharp-edged and complex have rounded, simplified, collapsed into themselves and regrown as something smoother, more essential. The current inhabitants live in a city that is very close to finished — not because it will stop changing, but because the changes have become so slow and so deep that they are geological rather than architectural.

There are places — entire platforms, entire districts — that have reached what the listeners call *stillness*. The uuth there has stopped growing. It has not died. It has *completed*. These stillness-zones are revered. They are also slightly terrifying, because they represent the city's ultimate fate: a gradual, district-by-district settling into permanence, a living world becoming a monument to itself.

The inhabitants leave the stillness-zones empty. No one lives there. Nothing grows there. The silence is absolute — no cellular patter, no circulatory wash, no resonant hum. The fauna avoid them. Even the wind seems to soften at their borders. To enter a stillness-zone is to step outside of time, into a space that has finished becoming and now simply *is*.

---

## The People

The inhabitants of Vess Meranth are not numerous. The city could support millions; it hosts perhaps tens of thousands. They are spread thinly across its vast living platforms, clustered in small communities around active growth zones, where the uuth is young enough to be shaped and old enough to be stable.

They are humanoid. They are probably human, or were, once. The altitude, the dimness, the cold, the peculiar biochemistry of a world without water — these have worked their changes. They are tall and lean, with large dark eyes and skin that has taken on a faint greenish cast from generations of close contact with uuth biochemistry. They are comfortable in the cold. They breathe the thin air without difficulty. They do not drink.

This last is perhaps the most fundamental adaptation. In a world without water, the inhabitants derive their moisture from the uuth's circulatory fluid — the thick, mineral-laden colloid that flows through the city's vessels. They tap the smaller vessels directly, using instruments (grown, not made — hollow uuth-thorns, shaped by listeners for the purpose) to pierce the vessel walls and draw out small quantities of the fluid. It tastes of iron and something floral. It sustains them. They do not thirst.

Their technology is, as noted, extraordinary and largely invisible. The listeners' instruments are the most obvious example, but the deeper technologies are embedded in the city itself — in the patterns of uuth growth, in the resonant frequencies of the architecture, in the symbiotic relationships between plant, animal, and human that have been refined over millennia into something that functions as infrastructure without ever having been designed.

There are no roads. There are grown pathways — channels in the uuth surface, smoothed by generations of feet — that follow the city's natural topography. There are no vehicles. Distances are measured in days, and the inhabitants walk. The uuth-movers provide transport of a sort for those who are patient enough to match their pace, and the filament-birds can carry small loads (messages, lightweight goods) along predictable routes, but mostly, movement through Vess Meranth is slow and deliberate, and this is understood to be correct.

---

## The Cycles

Despite its ancientness, despite its slow settling toward stillness, Vess Meranth is not static. It pulses.

The uuth has a growth cycle — not seasonal (there are no seasons here, only the eternal dim autumn) but internal, driven by the accumulation and expenditure of mineral resources. A growth phase lasts roughly a century. During this time, the uuth is vigorous, responsive, easy to shape. New structures emerge rapidly (by the city's standards — a room in a decade, a district in fifty years). The fauna are more active. The uuth-movers migrate more frequently. The sound of the city intensifies: more cellular patter, more circulatory surge, more resonant hum.

Then comes a rest phase — also roughly a century — during which the uuth consolidates. Growth slows. Structures densify. The city becomes quieter, more settled. The inhabitants use this time for repair, for maintenance, for the slow careful work of ensuring that what has been grown during the active phase will endure.

The city is currently in the late stages of a rest phase. The stillness-zones are expanding. The listeners speak, in their careful oblique way, of the possibility that the next growth phase may be the last — that the city's mineral reserves, accumulated over geological time, may be approaching depletion. This is not a source of panic. It is a source of something more complicated: a quiet, collective reckoning with the idea that Vess Meranth, which has always been dying, may finally be approaching the end of its dying.

---

## The Sound, Again

Because it is the sound that stays with you.

Standing on an open platform at the city's edge, in the dim bronze light, in the thin cold air, you hear:

The drone of the root-spires, far below, a subsonic trembling that you feel in your teeth.

The wash and surge of the circulatory system, the city breathing.

The patter of cellular growth, rain on a living roof.

The clatter and cry of spine-drakes, echoing through the corridors behind you.

The whisper of filament-birds, circling the root-spires.

The high, crystalline song of chamber-wights, threading through it all like light through dark water.

And beneath everything — the deepest note, the one you feel rather than hear — the slow, patient pulse of the uuth itself, the heartbeat of a world that has been alive for longer than memory and is not finished yet.

It is loud. It is *magnificent*. It is the sound of a city that is also a forest that is also a body that is also a mind, singing to itself in the dark, at the top of the world, at the end of time.

And it is beautiful.
