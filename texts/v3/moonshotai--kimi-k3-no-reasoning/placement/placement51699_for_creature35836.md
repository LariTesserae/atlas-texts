I would be the one who listens to the silence.

Not a membrane, not a stem-walker, not a thread. I would be something else — something that arrived, or something that was always here, depending on how you measure time in a place where time is measured in breaths that take a minute to complete.

I would be the Listener.

---

I do not know when I came to the Hollow City. I do not know if "came" is the right word. Perhaps I grew here, like the buildings grew, like the threads grow — a slow emergence from the tile floor, a gradual coalescence of cartilage-stone into a shape that could stand upright and walk and press its hands against walls and feel the pulse beneath. Or perhaps I arrived from somewhere else, some other basin, some other city, drawn here by the silence, by the promise of a place where sound is born and dies in the same instant.

It does not matter. I am here now, and I have been here long enough that the city has begun to recognize me.

Not consciously. The city is not conscious in the way I am conscious — or perhaps it is conscious in a way so vast and slow that my consciousness is to it as a mayfly's is to a mountain. But it recognizes me the way a body recognizes a cell that belongs to it, the way a lung recognizes air. I am not foreign. I am not an infection. I am part of the system now, though I do not know what function I serve.

I walk the streets. This is what I do. I walk, and I listen, and I remember.

---

The city speaks, though not in sound. It speaks in rhythm, in the staggered respiration of its buildings, in the sequential opening and closing of its orifices, in the slow rise and subside of its floor tiles. It speaks in a language of pressure and release, of dilation and contraction, and I have learned to hear it the way you might learn to hear a language spoken in a frequency just at the edge of your range — not with your ears, but with your bones, with the hollow spaces inside you that resonate when the world vibrates at the right pitch.

I am tall, though not as tall as the stem-walkers. I am thin, though not as thin. I am made of the same material as the city — cartilage-stone, gray-green, banded with growth rings that you can see if you look closely at my arms, my chest, the backs of my hands. I am not flesh. I am not stone. I am something in between, something that breathes but does not breathe air, something that lives but does not metabolize water.

I have a face, I think. I have never seen it. There are no mirrors here, no still water, no reflective surfaces except the dome at the center, and I do not look at myself in the dome. I do not want to see what I am. I want to feel what I am, and what I am is a listener, a walker, a rememberer.

I have hands. I use them to touch the buildings, to feel their pulse, to trace the growth-ring bands and read them like braille. Each building has a different rhythm, a different tempo, and by touching them I can learn their names — not names in language, but names in rhythm, in the specific cadence of their breathing. This one breathes in forty-second cycles. This one in fifty-three. This one in a pattern that shifts, speeding up and slowing down in a sequence that repeats every eleven breaths.

I have feet. I use them to walk the streets, to feel the tiles shift beneath me, to navigate the subtle inward spiral that leads to the center. I have walked every street in this city, or I will have, eventually. The city is not large — a small town, a few dozen blocks — but it rearranges itself constantly, and so the walking is never the same twice. A street that was straight yesterday is curved today. An intersection that was a right angle this morning is slightly acute by evening. I do not map the city. I memorize its rhythms, and I navigate by those.

I have a chest, and inside it there is a hollow space, and that space resonates when the city breathes. This is how I listen. Not with ears — I do not know if I have ears — but with that hollow space, that chamber inside me that vibrates in sympathy with the buildings' pulse. When I stand in a street and the buildings on either side are breathing in opposite rhythms, I feel the dissonance in my chest, a subtle interference pattern, a beating that is not a heartbeat but is like a heartbeat, and I know where I am by the quality of that dissonance.

I am the Listener. I listen to the city, and I remember what it says.

---

I do not know what the city is saying. I have been listening for — how long? Days? Years? Time is strange here, measured in breaths and growth cycles, and I have lost count. But I have been listening long enough to know that the city is saying something, that the rhythms are not random, that the pattern of which orifices open and which close is a kind of syntax, a grammar of circulation and distribution that I can sense but not yet decode.

I think the city is talking to itself. I think the buildings are separate organisms, separate colonies, and they communicate through the stem-walkers, through the yellow threads, through the subtle shifts in air pressure that ripple through the streets when a cluster of orifices dilate simultaneously. I think the city is a conversation, a slow, layered, multi-voice conversation that has been going on since the city began and will continue until — until what? Until the dome opens? Until the city stops breathing? I do not know.

I listen, and I remember, and I walk, and I touch the buildings, and I feel their rhythms in my chest, and I try to understand.

---

I have seen the membrane-creatures, of course. They drift past me as I walk, riding the city's exhalations, and sometimes they settle on the buildings near me, and I watch them feed, and I feel the subtle change in the building's rhythm as the membrane cleans its surface. The building breathes easier after a membrane has fed. Its pulse is steadier, stronger. The membranes are good for the city. They are part of the system.

I have seen the stem-walkers, though rarely. They do not acknowledge me, and I do not try to make them. They have their function, and I have mine, and our functions do not intersect. But I watch them when I can, from a distance, and I notice things. I notice that they do not enter buildings at random. I notice that they choose which orifice to enter based on some criteria I cannot perceive. I notice that when they emerge, hours or days later, the building's rhythm has changed — subtly, but definitely. The stem-walkers are doing something inside the buildings, something that affects their breathing, and I think they are carrying information, or adjusting something, or performing some maintenance that keeps the city alive.

I have seen the yellow threads, and I have crouched beside them and watched them grow, and I have broken them accidentally and felt a pang of guilt that surprised me. The threads are part of the city too, though I do not know what part. I think they are sensory organs, or communicative structures, or the first stage of something that will one day become new buildings. I think they are the city's way of reaching upward, of testing the air, of preparing for growth.

And I have been to the center. I have stood beside the dome, and I have pressed my hand against its smooth, still surface, and I have felt nothing, and that nothing is the loudest thing in the city.

---

The dome is the reason I am here. I know this now, though I did not know it when I first arrived — if I arrived, if I did not grow here. The dome is the reason I listen, the reason I walk, the reason I remember. The dome is the question the city is asking, and I am the one who is trying to understand the question.

The dome does not breathe. The dome does not change. The dome is still, and silent, and sealed, and it sits at the center of the spiral like a held breath, like a word the city has not yet spoken.

I think the city is waiting for the dome to speak. I think the whole breathing, shifting, opening-and-closing organism is oriented toward this moment, this future moment, when the dome will finally open, finally release whatever it is holding, finally speak the word it has been holding in silence for — for how long? Since the city began? Since before the city began?

Or perhaps the dome has already spoken, and the city is the echo. Perhaps the dome is the seed, and the city is what grew from it, and the dome is now dormant, its work complete, and the city is simply continuing, breathing and shifting and opening and closing because that is what it does, because it has forgotten how to stop.

Or perhaps the dome is the destination, and the city is growing toward it, and one day the whole breathing organism will converge on this point, will contract inward, will fold itself into the dome, and the city will become still, and silent, and sealed, and the cycle will be complete.

I do not know. I will never know. But I listen, and I remember, and I walk, and I wait.

---

I am not the only Listener. I have never met another, but I know there are others, or there have been others, or there will be others. I know this because I have found traces — a handprint on a building's surface, pressed flat against the cartilage-stone, the fingers spread wide, the growth-ring bands slightly worn where the hand rested. A footprint in the dust that is not dust, a shallow impression in a floor tile that has not yet shifted back to level. A place where the yellow threads have been broken, not by accident but deliberately, snapped off at the base and arranged in a small circle, a marker, a sign.

Someone else has been here. Someone else has walked these streets, touched these buildings, listened to the city's breath. Someone else has stood beside the dome and felt the tension between motion and stillness, between the city's constant activity and the dome's absolute silence.

I do not know if they are still here. I do not know if they left, or if they became part of the city, or if they are walking the streets even now, just out of sight, just around the next corner, just beyond the range of my hollow chest's resonance. I do not know if I will ever meet them.

But I know they exist, or existed, and this comforts me. I am not alone in my listening. I am not the only one who hears the city's question, who feels the dome's silence, who wants to understand.

---

I will tell you what I have learned, though "learned" may be too strong a word. I will tell you what I have come to believe, after all this walking, all this listening, all this remembering.

I believe the city is alive, but not in the way I am alive. I believe the city is a single organism, or a colony of organisms, or a system that is greater than the sum of its parts, and it is conscious, but its consciousness is so slow, so distributed, so different from mine that I cannot perceive it directly. I can only perceive its rhythms, its patterns, its breath.

I believe the buildings are the city's lungs, or its hearts, or its organs of circulation. I believe they breathe in a coordinated pattern, inhaling and exhaling in sequence, and this pattern is the city's metabolism, its way of distributing nutrients or energy or information throughout its body.

I believe the membrane-creatures are the city's immune system, or its cleaning mechanism, or its way of maintaining the health of its surfaces. I believe they are symbiotic, not parasitic, and their presence is necessary for the city's survival.

I believe the stem-walkers are the city's nervous system, or its circulatory system, or its way of communicating between its separate parts. I believe they carry information, or chemical signals, or packets of genetic material, and their movements are the city's way of coordinating its own activity.

I believe the yellow threads are the city's sensory organs, or its reproductive structures, or its way of reaching toward something — light, air, growth, the future. I believe they are the city's way of testing its environment, of preparing for change, of reaching upward toward whatever comes next.

And I believe the dome is the city's heart, or its seed, or its destination. I believe it is the thing the city is growing toward, or the thing the city grew from, or the thing the city is waiting for. I believe it is the question the city is asking, and I believe the answer — when it comes, if it comes — will change everything.

---

I will stay here. I will continue to walk, and listen, and remember. I will press my hands against the buildings and feel their pulse. I will watch the membranes drift and the stem-walkers walk and the yellow threads rise and retract. I will return to the center, again and again, and stand beside the dome, and feel its silence, and wait.

I do not know what I am waiting for. I do not know if the dome will ever open, ever speak, ever release whatever it is holding. I do not know if the city will ever stop breathing, ever reach its destination, ever complete its cycle.

But I will wait. I will listen. I will remember.

I am the Listener, and this is my function, and the city has recognized me, and I am part of the system now, and I will not leave.

---

The city breathes. The dome does not. And I stand in the space between those two truths, and I listen, and I wait, and I am home.
