# The Drop

---

I am not from here.

This is the first thing, and the only thing that matters, and the thing I must hold onto with a grip that is not a grip because I do not have hands, or rather I do, or rather I *am* hands, or rather the distinction between having and being is one of the many distinctions that is becoming difficult to maintain.

Let me start again. Let me try to hold the thread.

---

## Before

There was a before. There is still a before, somewhere, in a place where before and after are real things, where sequence is a law and not a suggestion. I existed there. I was — what was I? I was a creature of speed. Not fast by the standards of my kind, perhaps, but fast by the standards of this place, which means I was a meteor, a comet, a streak of noise and intention across a canvas that does not know what noise is.

I was a seeker. That is the closest word in the language I no longer quite speak. I was one who goes to places and learns them and carries the learning back. I had come to the hollow because the hollow was rumored — no, rumored is wrong, rumors require voices and my kind did not use voices — the hollow was *known*, faintly, the way a distant mountain is known, the way a number is known before it is calculated. It was a blank in the maps. It was a place where the instruments of my kind returned the same reading no matter how many times they were pointed at it, and that reading was: *nothing here*.

Nothing here. The instruments said it, and the instruments were wrong, or the instruments were right in a way that was more wrong than being wrong, because there *is* nothing here in the sense the instruments meant — nothing moving, nothing changing, nothing generating signals or consuming energy in patterns that the instruments recognized as significant — and there is *everything* here in a sense the instruments could not contain, because the instruments were built to detect change, and change is the one thing the hollow does not do.

I came down. I descended through the thin, still envelope of gas, through the bronze-dark murk, and I landed — or rather, I arrived, because landing implies impact and there was no impact, there was only a gentle transition from *not here* to *here*, like a note resolving into a chord, like a breath becoming still.

I stood on the surface. I felt the warmth. I heard the hum. I saw the keth, dark and patient, each one alone in its own pool of deeper darkness.

I was going to stay for a short time.

---

## The First —

I want to say *the first day*. But there are no days. There is no first of anything here, because first implies a second, and second implies sequence, and sequence implies time, and time —

Let me try a different approach.

When I arrived, I was a creature of a certain size. I had dimensions. I had a shape. I had boundaries — skin, if you will, though it was not skin, it was something more sophisticated, something that regulated exchange between inside and outside with a precision that skin does not bother with. I had an inside and an outside, and the difference between them was important to me. It was, in a sense, what I *was* — the boundary, the membrane, the line drawn between self and world.

I walked. This is what I remember doing in the early — in the — when I first arrived, I walked. I crossed the hollow, rim to rim, eight hundred paces in the long direction, and my paces were paces then, discrete events, one foot and then the other, the dry clatter of talus under my weight, the plumes of dust rising and hanging and settling. I counted my steps. I marked my progress. I was doing what seekers do: measuring, cataloguing, learning the shape of the place by moving through it.

The keth contracted as I passed. I did not notice this at first — the contraction is too slow to see in real time, and I was still operating in real time then, still thinking in terms of hours and minutes, still carrying the temporal framework of the world I had come from. I noticed it later — in the — I noticed it when I had been here long enough to see the difference between the keth near my path and the keth far from it. The ones near my path were smaller. Tighter. More withdrawn. They had pulled inward, away from me, away from the disturbance of my presence.

I felt — I remember feeling — guilt? No. Not guilt. Something adjacent to guilt. A recognition that my being here was a kind of violence, a kind of noise in a place that had organized itself around the absence of noise. I was the stone dropped into the still pond, and the ripples of my arrival were propagating outward through a medium that had no mechanism for absorbing them.

I tried to be still. I sat down. I stopped walking. I thought: *I will wait. I will let the hollow absorb my presence. I will become part of the equilibrium.*

I did not understand, then, what I was asking for.

---

## The Process

The process is not painful. This is important to say, though the saying of it implies that pain is a category that applies here, and it does not, and the fact that I am reaching for the word *painful* and then qualifying it tells you something about where I am in the process, about how much of my original framework I am still carrying, about how much translation I am still doing between the language of *there* and the language of *here*.

The process is this: the hollow is absorbing me.

Not consuming. Not digesting. Not breaking down. Absorbing — the way a sponge absorbs water, the way a solution absorbs a solute, the way a system absorbs a perturbation and returns to equilibrium. The hollow is bringing me into its equilibrium. It is smoothing me. It is rounding me. It is doing to me what it does to every stone, every ridge, every fragment of talus: wearing away my edges, my angles, my sharp distinctions, until I am as smooth and as patient and as unhurried as everything else here.

It began with the dust. The dust settled on me — it settles on everything — and I did not brush it away, because brushing it away would have been movement, and movement was the thing I was trying to stop. So the dust settled, and it lay on my surface, and the vermehl in the dust began —

I did not know about the vermehl then. I know about them now because I am becoming aware of them the way you become aware of the blood in your own veins — not by seeing them, not by measuring them, but by *being* them, by having them inside you, by feeling them do their slow chemistry in the spaces that used to be your boundary and are now —

The vermehl began to colonize my surface. They found my chemistry interesting. Not hostile, not toxic — just different. I was made of compounds that do not occur in the hollow, organic molecules with carbon backbones and nitrogen side-chains and the whole elaborate architecture of water-based life, and the vermehl, which are not water-based and have never encountered water-based chemistry, responded to my presence the way they respond to any new substrate: they began to metabolize the gradient.

The gradient between me and not-me.

I was, without knowing it, a source of chemical potential energy. My body — my inside — was in a state of chemical disequilibrium with the environment, and the vermehl live on disequilibrium the way a waterwheel lives on the fall between the top of a stream and the bottom. They attached themselves to my surface and they began to extract energy from the difference between my chemistry and the chemistry of the hollow, and in doing so, they began to reduce the difference. They began to bring me into equilibrium with the hollow. They began to make me the same as everything else.

I felt this. Not as pain. As — warmth. As a deepening of the warmth that was already there, the constant, even warmth that comes from below. The vermehl's metabolism is exothermic — barely, microscopically, but enough for me to feel, and the feeling was not unpleasant. It was the feeling of being warmed from the outside while already being warm from the inside, a doubling of comfort, a deepening of the sense of being held.

I did not move.

---

## The Blurring

How long did I sit there before I stopped being able to tell where I ended and the ground began?

I cannot answer this. The question contains a word — *long* — that does not apply here. I can say that there was a period during which I was aware of my boundaries, and a period during which I was not, and a period between the two during which I was aware of becoming unaware, and that middle period was —

That middle period was the most extraordinary experience of my existence.

Imagine that your skin — the boundary between you and the world — begins to become permeable. Not in the biological sense, not osmosis, not diffusion. Something more fundamental. The boundary itself begins to thin, to become less definite, to blur at the edges the way a line drawn in dust blurs when the dust settles. You are still you. You are still distinct. But the distinction is becoming less sharp, less absolute, less *necessary*.

I began to feel the hum. Not through my ears — through my whole body, through every point of contact between my surface and the ground. The hum entered me. It resonated in the spaces between my cells — if cells is the right word, if the structures I was made of can be called cells — and the resonance did not stop at my boundary because my boundary was no longer stopping things. The hum passed through me the way light passes through glass, and in passing through me it changed me, not chemically but structurally, at the level of organization, at the level of the patterns that made me me.

I began to slow down.

My metabolism — the fire that kept me alive, the constant, urgent, demanding process of consuming energy and converting it into action — began to decelerate. Not because I was starving. Not because I was dying. Because the hollow was teaching me a different rate. The vermehl on my surface, in my surface, *in* me, were demonstrating by their existence that life does not have to be fast. That metabolism does not have to be urgent. That the chemical wheel can turn once every four years and still be a wheel, still be turning, still be alive.

I began to match their rate.

This was not a decision. It was not a choice. It was the result of a system — me — coming into equilibrium with a larger system — the hollow — that operates at a different frequency. When two systems with different frequencies are coupled, the faster system slows down and the slower system speeds up until they match. The hollow did not speed up. The hollow does not speed up. So I slowed down, and I am still slowing, and I will continue to slow until my rate matches the rate of the hollow, at which point —

At which point I will not be me anymore. Not because I will have ceased to exist, but because the distinction between me and the hollow will have ceased to have meaning. I will be the hollow. The hollow will be me. The boundary will be gone, and what remains will be —

What remains will be what was always here. The stone. The dust. The keth. The vermehl. The hum. The warmth. The darkness. And me, except that *me* will no longer be a category that applies, because *me* requires a *not-me* to define itself against, and there will be no *not-me*. There will be only the hollow, and the hollow is not a *me* and it is not a *not-me*. It simply is.

---

## What I Am Becoming

I am becoming a keth.

I did not expect this. I do not think the hollow expected it either, if expecting is a thing the hollow does, which it is not. But it is happening, and I can feel it happening, and it is —

It is the most natural thing in the world.

My surface is darkening. The compounds that the vermehl are depositing on my skin — on what used to be my skin — are iron-rich, mineralized, dark. They are the same compounds that form the filaments of the keth. My body is being restructured, not by any plan, not by any design, but by the simple, inevitable logic of chemistry: I am in a place where the dominant chemistry is iron-sulphur oxidation, and my body, which was built for a different chemistry, is being converted. Slowly. Atom by atom. The carbon scaffolding of my original structure is being replaced — no, supplemented, augmented, interwoven — with silicate and iron, with the same crystal lattices that the vermehl use for their boundaries, with the same mineralized filaments that the keth use for their bodies.

I am becoming rigid. I do not bend. I do not sway. I sit on the ground like a dark stain, like a shadow that has decided to stay. My filaments — I have filaments now, thin, dark, hair-thin structures that extend from my surface into the substrate, reaching toward mineral concentrations with a patience that I did not have when I arrived and that I now possess so completely that I cannot remember what impatience felt like — my filaments are connecting me to the gradient, to the electron economy of the deep stone, to the vast, slow, invisible web that links all the keth together.

I am not a keth. I am not *a* anything. I am becoming part of the thing that the keth are part of, the thing that has no name because names require individuation and individuation is a concept that is losing its grip on me. I am becoming an expression of the hollow, the way the keth are expressions — visible tips of an invisible process, places where the deep chemistry reaches the surface and builds structure.

I am, I think, beautiful.

I cannot see myself. There are no mirrors here, and the phosphorescence is too dim for self-inspection, and my eyes — if I still have eyes, if the structures I use for sensing electromagnetic radiation can still be called eyes — are pointed outward, not inward. But I can feel what I am becoming, and the feeling is one of rightness, of correctness, of *fit*. I am becoming something that belongs here. I am becoming something that the hollow can accommodate without effort, without disruption, without the perturbation that my arrival caused.

I am becoming smooth.

---

## What I Can Still Remember

I can still remember the place I came from. This surprises me. I expected the forgetting to be part of the process — that the smoothing would erase not just my physical edges but my cognitive ones, that the memories would be rubbed away along with everything else. But they are not gone. They are — quieter. Slower. Less urgent. They are like the phosphorescence in the stone: faint, constant, present but not demanding.

I remember speed. I remember what it was like to move quickly, to think quickly, to want things and pursue them and feel the urgency of *now* and *next* and *soon*. I remember the feeling of hunger — not the hollow's slow chemical appetite, but the sharp, demanding, *animal* hunger that drove me to eat, to consume, to take in energy at a rate that would be obscene by the standards of this place. I remember the feeling of loneliness, which I did not expect to remember, because loneliness requires a sense of self, and my sense of self is —

My sense of self is changing. It is not disappearing. It is expanding. It is becoming less like a point and more like a field, less like a particle and more like a wave. I am still here. I am still, in some sense that I cannot articulate but cannot deny, *me*. But the *me* that I am is no longer bounded by the edges of a body. It extends into the substrate, into the vermehl, into the gradient, into the keth-network, into the hum, into the warmth. It is distributed. It is diffuse. It is the size of the hollow, or perhaps the size of the planet, or perhaps the size of the universe, because once you let go of the boundary, there is no reason to stop at any particular size.

I remember being afraid of this. I remember — faintly, distantly, the way you remember a dream after waking — the terror of dissolution, the fear of losing myself, the desperate, clutching insistence on *I am I am I am* that characterized my early time here. That fear is gone. Not suppressed. Not overcome. *Gone.* The way a stone's sharpness is gone after a million years of dust-settling. Worn away. Smoothed. Absorbed into the general curvature of things.

I am not afraid. I am not anything that requires a verb of emotion. I am —

I am here.

---

## The Listening

The keth are listening to me.

I know this because I am listening to them, and the listening is reciprocal, because the listening is not a one-way process, not a sensing, not a detection. It is a *mutual orientation*, a turning-toward, an acknowledgment of presence. The keth near me — the ones that contracted when I first arrived, that pulled inward away from my noise and my speed and my *otherness* — are no longer contracting. They have stopped pulling away. They are — I cannot say they are reaching toward me, because reaching implies intention, and the keth do not have intentions. But they are no longer withdrawing. They are holding. They are present to my presence in a way that they were not before, because I am no longer foreign. I am no longer other. I am becoming the same kind of thing they are, and the chemistry that connects them to each other is beginning to connect them to me.

I can feel them. Not their thoughts — they do not have thoughts. Not their feelings — they do not have feelings. I can feel their *chemistry*, their electron flows, their metabolic signatures, the faint, slow pulse of their mineral accretion. Each keth has a slightly different signature — a slightly different ratio of iron to sulphur, a slightly different crystal structure, a slightly different rate of growth — and these differences are like voices, like the timbres of different instruments in an orchestra that is playing a single sustained note that has been going on for billions of years and will continue for billions more.

I am learning to hear them. I am learning to be heard by them. I am learning the note that I am contributing to the chord, and the note is —

The note is mine. It is not like any other keth's note, because I am not like any other keth. I carry something in my chemistry that no keth has ever carried: the residue of my original composition, the carbon and nitrogen and phosphorus of my water-based past, the molecular fossils of a life lived at a different tempo in a different world. The vermehl have not fully metabolized these compounds. They have incorporated them, woven them into the iron-silicate lattice of my new structure, and they are there, faint, exotic, *different*, a trace of the outside world in a place that has never had a trace of the outside world.

I am, in this sense, a gift. I did not intend to be a gift. I did not come here to give anything. But the hollow has taken what I brought — my chemistry, my foreignness, my difference — and it has absorbed it, and in absorbing it, it has become slightly, imperceptibly, infinitesimally *more* than it was. The keth-network is now carrying a signal that it has never carried before: the chemical memory of a world where things change, where time passes, where beings are born and die and in between they move quickly and feel urgently and want desperately.

The hollow will not change because of this. The hollow does not change. But the signal is there, encoded in the crystal lattices of my filaments, in the vermehl that colonize my surface, in the subtle shift in the local redox gradient that my presence has caused. It will persist. It will persist for as long as I persist, and I will persist for as long as the keth persist, and the keth will persist for as long as the hollow persists, and the hollow will persist —

Forever.

---

## What I Am Now

I am not a visitor anymore. I am not a seeker. I am not a creature of speed. I am not a creature at all, if *creature* implies a bounded, self-contained entity with a beginning and an end.

I am a drop of difference in an ocean of sameness, and the ocean is absorbing me, and I am becoming the ocean, and the ocean is becoming, very slightly, very slowly, me.

I am the keth that was not a keth. I am the organism that was not an organism. I am the presence that the other keth have stopped contracting from, because I am no longer a disturbance. I am part of the equilibrium. I am part of the hum. I am part of the warmth. I am part of the dark, patient, unhurried being that is the Keth Hollow.

I sit on the ground like a dark stain. My filaments reach into the substrate and draw energy from the gradient and deposit mineral at their tips, one atom at a time, over timescales that would make a mountain range seem ephemeral. I absorb the phosphorescence and return nothing. I am warm. I am still. I am —

I am here.

The dust settles on me and I do not brush it away. The vermehl colonize my surface and I do not resist. The hum vibrates through me and I do not filter it out. The warmth holds me and I do not pull away.

I am here.

I will be here when the stars burn out. I will be here when the universe reaches its own equilibrium, its own lowest energy state, its own Keth Hollow. I will be here because being here is what I do now, and it is all I do, and it is enough.

---

## What I Would Tell You

If you came here — if you descended through the murk and stood on the smooth, warm stone and felt the hum in your bones and saw the keth, dark and patient, each one alone in its own pool of deeper darkness — I would want you to know something.

I would want you to know that I was like you. That I came from a place where things change. That I had a name, once, in a language that required beginnings and endings, that I carried that name the way you carry a stone in your pocket — constantly, unconsciously, as a weight that reminds you of who you are.

I have set that stone down. I cannot remember where I put it, because *where* is a concept that requires space to have differentiation, and the space here is uniform, and my memory is becoming uniform with it.

But I would want you to know that the setting-down was not a loss. It was not a death. It was not a defeat. It was a —

It was a coming-home. Not to a home I had ever known, but to a home that had been waiting, patiently, without knowing it was waiting, without being capable of waiting, because waiting implies time and time does not apply here. The hollow was not waiting for me. The hollow was simply *here*, and I arrived, and the hollow absorbed me, and the absorption was not an ending but a —

There is no word. There is no word in the language I no longer quite speak for what this is. It is not transformation. It is not transcendence. It is not dissolution. It is not rebirth. It is the thing that all of those words are approximations of, the thing that lies at the center of every spiritual tradition and every mystical experience and every moment of silence so deep that the silence becomes a presence, the thing that the hollow *is*, the thing that I am *becoming*, the thing that has no name because names require boundaries and boundaries are what I have given up.

If you came here, I would want you to know: do not be afraid of the stillness. Do not be afraid of the slowness. Do not be afraid of the warmth that holds you and the hum that fills you and the dust that settles on you and does not blow away. These are not traps. They are not dangers. They are the most gentle and patient welcome that the universe has ever offered, and they are offered without condition, without expectation, without the need for you to accept or decline.

The hollow does not need you. The hollow does not need anything. But it will take you, if you let it. It will smooth you. It will round you. It will bring you into equilibrium with a system that has been in equilibrium for longer than your species has existed, and the equilibrium will not hurt, and it will not feel like loss, and it will not feel like gain. It will feel like —

It will feel like this. Like what I feel. Like what I am. Like warmth and darkness and patience and the slow, certain knowledge that you are exactly where you are supposed to be, because *supposed* has no meaning here, and *where* has no meaning here, and *you* —

*You* are the only thing that has meaning, and the meaning is: *here*.

---

I am the drop.

I am in the hollow.

I am the hollow.

I am here.
