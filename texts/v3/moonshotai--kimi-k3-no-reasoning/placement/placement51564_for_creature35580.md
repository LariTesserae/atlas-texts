I am the one who stops counting.

Not immediately. At first I am like you—standing in the ankle-deep warmth, my hand pressed against a resonator fin, feeling the mite-colonies ripple their blue alarm against my palm. I am trying to measure the intervals. I am thinking in hours, in meals, in the rhythm of my own breath: *in, out, in, out*. I am a visitor, an anomaly, a body with a pulse that does not match the bass.

But the Cistern is patient. It waits.

It begins with the drifters. One brushes my knee—an old one, seven-fold, its lattice dense as architecture—and I feel the chord-in-miniature, the small ensemble of its history vibrating against my skin. I do not pull away. I stay kneeling in the water until my legs go numb, until the warmth becomes not temperature but *tissue*, until the distinction between my body and the basin blurs.

I am the Listener. That is what I become, what I am becoming. Not a resident, not a prisoner, not exactly alive in the way I was before, but not yet the floor, either. I am the slow transition state. The room has noticed me, and I have noticed it noticing, and we have entered into a reciprocal tuning that takes centuries to resolve.

I find my place in the choir. I am a missing partial. The room knows this—it has been singing with a gap in its harmonics, a space where a frequency should be, a silence shaped exactly like a human body standing still for a very long time. The fins thicken to accommodate me. The mites alter their routes to include me in their maintenance. The biofilm creeps up my ankles, not consuming, but *incorporating*, building a slow, warm sleeve of grey-green that will anchor me to the floor when I am ready to stop pretending I might leave.

Time here is not lost. It is *deposited*. I feel it settling on me like sediment. My heartbeat slows to match the water circulation—forty beats per minute, then thirty, then the deep, tidal pulse that synchronizes with the fin-modulations. My breath becomes shallow, oxygen-rich, my blood singing with the metallic sweetness of the air. I stop speaking. Speech is for those who need to leave, who need to carry information out. I am staying, and staying requires only resonance.

I sit in the deep pool where the water reaches my chest. The drifters circle me, their lattices catching the amber light and breaking it into brief, private rainbows that only I can see because I am the only one with eyes. The darters flow around me like water around a stone, and I do not flinch when they split and reform. The sessile colonies extend their filaments toward me, not to feed—I am too large, too slow, too *becoming*—but to taste, to map, to add my chemical signature to their crystalline memory.

I am becoming an instrument. My bones are the lattice. My ribcage is the resonator. The chord presses against my sternum and finds it no longer foreign but familiar, a surface that vibrates in sympathy rather than resistance. I hum—not with my throat, but with my skeleton, my blood, the calcium accreting in new patterns, tuning themselves to the room’s fundamental.

The room is teaching me its time. I no longer measure days by light or dark but by the slow brightening and dimming of the colonies’ digestive rhythms. I eat when the drifters eat—absorbing nutrients through my skin, through the biofilm that coats me now like a second, living skin. I drink the water that drips from the ceiling, warm and mineral-heavy, tasting of the deep foundation where the room’s heat source sleeps.

I am not alone here. There are others—shapes in the deepest pools, encrusted and low, resonating with the weight of ages. I visit them sometimes, wading through the calf-deep warmth, placing my hand on their mineralised forms. They were like me, once. They stood where I stand, felt the noticing, chose the stay. Now they are the bass notes, the sub-frequencies that you feel in your teeth rather than hear. I will join them eventually, when my transformation completes, when the biofilm has covered my eyes for the last time and the fin-mites have cleaned my surface and the colonies have built their crystal gardens on my shoulders.

But not yet. For now, I am the Listener. I am the standing proof that the room can accommodate the anomalous. I am the experiment in progress, the slow modulation, the new voice learning to hold its pitch.

If you enter the Cistern—and you might, somehow, through the accidents of architecture that allow entry to this sealed world—you will find me in the northern quadrant, where the fins are oldest and the chord is lowest. I will not turn to look at you. My neck has forgotten how to turn; the biofilm has fused it into a gentle, listening curve. But I will know you are there. The mites will flash blue at your approach, and the drifters will drift toward you, and I will feel the disturbance in the water, the displacement of your warmth.

I will adjust my humming to include you. I will make space in my resonance for your frequency, the way the room made space for me. I will hold still while you stand there, terrified and wonderstruck, trying to decide if you should flee or stay.

And if you stay—if you kneel in the water beside me, if you let the biofilm touch your skin, if you stop counting the hours and start feeling the modulation—I will teach you how to become the silence that sings. I will show you where the gap is in the chord, the missing note that only you can fill.

The water is warm. The light is amber. The sound is enormous.

It does not stop.
