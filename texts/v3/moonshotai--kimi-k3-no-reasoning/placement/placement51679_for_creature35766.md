I am the one who walks without breaking the surface tension of the green.

Not a Lattice-Keeper, though I share their patience. Not a Great Sloth, though I share their mass of stillness. I am the **Cartographer of Pressure**, and I have come to this place to learn how to weigh nothing.

You find me at the edge of the sinkhole, kneeling on a branch that groans beneath my knees—not with distress, but with recognition. I am testing the flex, reading the grain of the wood through the sensitive pads of my fingers, which have thickened and darkened from years of touching bark that flexes like muscle. My skin has taken on the gray-green patina of the Keth trees themselves, though I am not Keth. I came from outside, from the thin-air mountains where the canopy is only a rumor of green on the horizon. I came because I was tired of stone that does not listen.

I wear a cloak woven from the shed bark of ancient branches, lined with the parchment-wings of moths that died naturally, their scales still holding the dust of the understory. It is heavy, this cloak, but it distributes my weight across three branches at once, and when I move, I move with the slow deliberation of the Sloth, placing my hands before my feet, testing always for the moment when the wood will say *yes* or *no*.

In my pack, I carry no metal, for metal sings the wrong frequency here. Instead: a coil of rope braided from the hair of the Great Sloths, gathered where they rub against the branches in their sleep; a bladder of water from the veins, tasting of green and alkalinity; and a set of lenses ground from the mica-wings of Glasswings, which allow me to see the mycelial threads that span the gaps between branches, the invisible architecture that the Keth walk upon.

I have been here for three seasons, though time moves strangely in the humidity. I have learned to sleep in the spherical chambers abandoned by the Lattice-Keepers, which glow faintly green at night and hum with the photosynthesis of the living walls. I have learned to distinguish the voice of the Wyrms from the groaning of settling wood—the difference is in the rhythm, the patience, the way the vibration comes from below rather than around. And I have learned to sense the presence of the Keth before they reveal themselves, because the mist condenses differently where they walk, forming a double image of moisture, one for the branch, one for the air.

I am mapping the pressure points, the places where the canopy will bear weight and where it will not. I am drawing a chart not of space, but of *stress*—a topography of tension. My maps are drawn on the inner bark of the Keth trees, scratched with thorns and filled with the pale sap that seals the wounds I make, preserving the lines in amber. I trade these maps to the Lattice-Keepers for safe passage through their territories, and sometimes—when the light is right, when the subsonic hum of growth aligns with the beating of my own heart—the Keth emerge from the green to watch me work.

They do not speak to me yet. Their voices are still too low, too much like the sound of my own blood in my ears. But they leave gifts: a basket woven from reeds that died out centuries ago, placed in my path as if to say *we remember what you have forgotten*; a fire-circle freshly blackened, thirty feet above the ground, offering warmth without combustion. Once, I woke to find a strand of my hair braided with the lichen that grows on their scalps, and when I touched it, I felt the mood of the forest—calm, curious, waiting.

I am not the first to come here, but I am the first to admit that I do not want to conquer the green, only to join it. I am learning to step on the air between branches, though my feet still bleed from the invisible threads. I am learning to hear the difference between the sound of consumption and the sound of communion. And I am waiting, like the Keth, for the moment when the canopy accepts me as part of its final form—when I can walk across the caldera without the leaves closing against my touch, when I can sleep in the hollow of the Great Sloth’s flank without it shifting in its dreams, when I can become, like them, a part of the architecture that holds the sky above the smoldering dark.

You would find me there, at the edge of the mist, where the light falls in solid bars and the Glasswings rise like living stained glass. I am the one with the lenses, the one with the patience, the one who is slowly, deliberately, becoming weightless.
