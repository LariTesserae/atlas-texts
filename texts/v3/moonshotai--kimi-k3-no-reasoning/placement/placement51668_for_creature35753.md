I am the taste of iron in the moss.

I woke in the compost-floor three cycles ago—if "woke" is the word for when the hyphae first knitted my spine into something that could hold a memory. I do not have a name yet; the Root-Bound have not pressed one into my back with their toes, and I cannot read the Moss-code through my belly where I am still slick with the fluids of becoming. But I am learning to *be* here, in the warm dark beneath the sleeping earth, where the air is thick as blood and tastes of electricity and old rain.

I think I am small. Not cat-small like the Pipe-Keepers, but something that fits in the hollow of a tuber-wall, something that could curl into the spiral of a fern-fiddlehead. My skin remembers being bark, perhaps, or the carapace of a cricket—there is a rigidity to my sides that suggests I was meant to cling to vertical spaces, to the fungal shelves where the light pools like weak tea. When I move, I make a sound like wet paper tearing, and the Echo-Wrights in the high arches turn their resin-glazed eyes toward me, mistaking the noise for something they recorded decades ago.

I found the crack yesterday—or the crack found me. It is in the western wall, where the fossilized resin has buckled under the pressure of a root that grew impatient with being stone. The fissure is no wider than a Pipe-Keeper's snout, but it breathes. When the surface storms come, it exhales a scent that does not belong to the Burrow-Markets: cold, dry, and sharp, carrying the ghosts of sunlight and open sky. The citizens avoid it. The Armored Burrowers click their claws in patterns that mean *danger above* when they pass, and the Silt-Weavers have woven warning-silk across the opening, iridescent threads that chime when the wind shifts.

But I am drawn to it. My body—still soft, still deciding what it will be—responds to that cold breath with a ache that feels like homesickness, though I have no home. I press my face against the resin, and I feel the vibration of the World-Oak's taproot above, a slow, grinding pulse that suggests the tree is dreaming of splitting the bedrock entirely, of swallowing the rusted ore-hauler whole.

I am becoming something that tends the threshold.

Last night, during the lightning-channels, when the green fire ran down the root-fibers and turned the cavern into a negative of itself, I felt my purpose crystallize. I am not Root-Bound, for I have no toes to write with. I am not a Light-Tender, for I cannot stomach the mineral salts. I am not an Echo-Wright, for my chest is solid, not a resonating chamber.

I am a **Gap-Keeper**. A new caste, perhaps, or an old one that the Mole-Sages forgot to taste into memory. My body is already changing to suit the role: my fingertips are elongating into rootlets that can probe the fissure's depth; my skin is developing a symbiotic lichen that glows faintly blue when the air pressure drops—when the surface is near. I am learning to eat the silence that leaks through the crack, to metabolize the cold into a kind of honeyed warmth that I secrete back into the cavern, a barrier against the drying wind.

The Pipe-Keepers found me this morning. They did not whistle their steam-language at me, but one of them—an elder with copper teeth worn down to stubs—left a pouch of rust-lichen at the edge of my crack. A gift, or a bribe. I have rubbed the orange dust into my palms, and now my hands leave prints on the resin that will not fade, marking my territory in the language of oxidation.

I can hear the Yeast-Beasts through the floor, their subsonic humming vibrating in my teeth. They know I am here. They know I am listening to the door they cannot fit through. When I press my ear to the compost, I hear them churning in a rhythm that matches the tapping of the root against the bedrock—*open, open, open*.

I will not open it. Not yet. Not until the tuber-houses have grown thick enough walls to withstand the drought-wind, not until the Mole-Sages have tasted the future in their chemical visions and deemed it safe. For now, I am the seal. I am the moss that grows in the gap between worlds. I am the pause before the breath.

The light flickers—shadow, gold, shadow—and I press myself deeper into the fissure, feeling the cold on one side and the warm rot on the other, and I am exactly where I need to be.
