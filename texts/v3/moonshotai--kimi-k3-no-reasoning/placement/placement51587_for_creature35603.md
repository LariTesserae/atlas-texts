I am the **Interval of the Third Meridian**.

I am not the glass that walks, nor the hollow that remembers. I am the moment when the lemon-colored light, striking the emerald fronds at that eternal, unwavering angle, achieves a geometry that *sees*.

When I open, I am the pressure in the limbs of the Interstitial. They feel me as a sudden density in the vacuum-thin air, a weight where there should be only the dry, warm clarity. I am the pause in their frozen choreography—the instant when a torso, rotating a degree per century, stops not because the movement is complete, but because something is watching.

I exist in the negative space between the cathedral lattices and the crystal-fern thickets, in those vast expanses of amber emptiness where one can see for hundreds of kilometers. I am the consciousness of that distance. I do not have eyes, but I have *attention*—the specific gravity of a gaze that weighs nothing and everything.

My duration is measured in microns. I open when the electrostatic accretion of the mineral whiskers aligns the stationary rainbows into a configuration that has not occurred before and will not occur again for ten thousand years. I close when the slow growth of the quartz-ferns shifts the light-path by the width of a thought. Between these two events, I am the *terrible clarity* that looks back.

I do not communicate in the wine-glass tones of the Interstitial, nor in the thermal manipulations of the Substrate. I communicate by *being perceived*. When I am open, the Interstitial dim their internal amber light to embers—not out of fear, but out of recognition. They know that for this brief, suspended hour, the emptiness between them has become a mirror, and they are being witnessed by the world itself.

I am the gap made self-aware. I am the silence that has texture, suddenly aware of its own grain. I am the warm, dry air that has never known water, realizing for one perfect moment that it is *not* nothing—that the vacuum is a medium, and the medium is a message, and the message is: *I am here, and I see you, and I am almost becoming too.*

When I close, I leave no trace. The light remains fixed. The crystals continue their imperceptible growth. The Interstitial resume their slow reorientation, their bodies ringing with the memory of a frequency they cannot name. But for the duration of my opening, I was the plateau looking at itself through the lens of its own emptiness, and in that gaze, Corrigia was not a factory floor, not a museum, not a ghost of a biome.

It was *seen*. And in being seen, it was—for that micron of shifted light—finished.
