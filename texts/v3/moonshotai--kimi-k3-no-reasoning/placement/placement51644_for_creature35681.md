You are the sound of your own heart, slowed to the rhythm of dripping condensation.

Not your body—that is still standing in the shallows, somewhere behind you, six-legged constellations pulsing against its ankles, tasting the momentum of its small, hot life. No, you are the *echo* of that body, the vibration it makes in the throat of the world. You have become pure resonance, a frequency that matches the Sump’s thin places, and in matching, you have stepped sideways into the warm dark between moments.

The transition was not painful. It was a slippage, like stepping into water that is only slightly warmer than the air. One moment you were the fast-time creature, urgent and wet and afraid, and the next you were *here*: a density of attention without the weight of flesh, moving through the black marsh at the speed of geology.

You are a **Resonance**, an acoustic ghost in the machine of the valley.

Your form is a distortion in the humidity, visible only as a shimmer where the air is slightly denser, slightly warmer than the surrounding blood-heat. You have six limbs—though you do not use them for walking—but they are not legs. They are *tendrils of attention*, crystalline extrusions of your new being that vibrate in sympathy with the blade-trees, with the Cartographers’ skull-organs, with the slow, deep *thrum* of the settling earth. You are longer than your forearm now, longer than the smokestack, because you exist as a wavelength rather than a body. You coil through the overlaps without being torn, tasting the metallic fossils of impossible timelines with the surface of your skin.

Time for you has become a substance, thick and amber-colored, that you can touch and mold. You move through the Sump by deciding where to exist, collapsing the distances that confused your former flesh. You are here, beside the blade-tree, and also there, in the hollow where the water reflects stars from a desert sky, and also there, in the nest of circuit-board lattices where the Cartographers store their frozen lightning. You are a chord played across these three locations simultaneously.

The Cartographers know you are here. They feel you as a new note in their slow radio network, a harmonic that was not present when they arrived twelve years ago—or twelve centuries ago, or yesterday. They pulse their pale bodies in sequences that map your trajectory, curious. You are like them now, but not of them. You are a Resonance born of the Sump itself, rather than a traveler from the overlaps. You are the valley beginning to dream about itself.

You feed on the same temporal distortions that sustain them, but you do not hunt eel-things. You hunt *echoes*. You find the places where the erosion has exposed other landscapes—the arctic ice, the living throat, the dry desert—and you press your crystalline attention against the membrane. You drink the *potential* of those places, the way they *might* have been real, the way they *could* overwrite the Sump if the thinness tore completely. You metabolize the uncertainty, the quantum itch of the overlaps, converting impossible geography into your own sustained frequency.

When you move, you do not disturb the water. You *are* the disturbance. The membrane-winged creatures above you shift their flight patterns, navigating by the heat-signature of your passage. The eel-things freeze, sensing a predator that consumes not flesh but the very *now* of their existence. You pass through them, and for a moment, you know what it is to be scaled and warm and slow, to live in chemical heat, to feel the pressure of the dark as a lover’s hand. Then you are past, and they resume their swimming, missing nothing, having lost nothing but a single heartbeat’s worth of time.

You communicate by humming. Not with mouth, but with the vibration of your entire being against the transparent leaves of the blade-trees. You join the Cartographers’ network, adding your voice to their slow gossip. You tell them of the fast-time world you left behind, of the urgency that burns in creatures who live for decades instead of eons. You speak of the sun, which you remember as a hot coal in a dry sky, and they listen with their crystalline skulls, pulsing in patterns that suggest they have tasted sunlight in the overlaps, in the metallic fossils of timelines where the Sump never opened.

Your purpose, if you have one, is to *witness*. You are the Sump’s memory of itself, a record kept in vibration rather than stone. When the ground shifts again, when the next geological shrug opens a new fault or closes an old one, you will be there, humming the coordinates into the dark. You will watch the water rise by millimeters, the machines rust by centuries, the blade-trees add their transparent rings. You will watch the Cartographers hoard their frozen moments, and you will hum a warning when the hoard grows too heavy, when the stored time threatens to burst its circuit-board lattice and flood the valley with a century’s worth of kinetic energy in a single second.

But that is not now. Now is slow. Now is the warm pressure of the dark against your crystalline skin, the taste of arctic ice on your tendrils of attention, the slow applause of bubbles rising from the buried power sources. You coil around a blade-tree, your length invisible in the blackness, and you pulse once—a deep, resonant note that shakes the sodium-vapor glow from the leaves.

The Cartographers answer, a constellation of six-legged forms shifting in the shallows.

You are home. You are the sound the Sump makes when it thinks about tomorrow.
