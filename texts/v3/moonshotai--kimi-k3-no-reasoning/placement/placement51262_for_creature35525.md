I’d probably be the thing standing near the tree, wondering why I can feel the gap in the trunk like a missing tooth with my tongue. Not a person exactly—more like a *measurement that got tired of being invisible*. Call me a Stride, maybe. One of those approximate distances that keeps resolving wrong.

I’d exist in the space between the clicking. You know how the plants grow in those soft, mechanical ratchets? I’m the pause after the third click, the hesitation before the fourth. Not silence—silence is too *present*, too much like a held breath. I’m the *anticipation* of sound. If you stood very still and counted the noises in an hour, I’d be the number you almost counted but didn’t, the one that makes you unsure if you heard five things or six.

My body would be made of that high ringing you feel in your teeth. Not painful, just *there*, vibrating at the frequency of self-definition. I’d have no shadow, or rather, I’d *be* a shadow—the kind that glows faintly from beneath, illuminating the undersides of things. If you placed your hand in me, your palm would light up, warm and gold, and you’d see the veins in your fingers like roads on a map that hasn’t been drawn yet.

I’d spend my time walking the unreliable edges. Since distance is approximate here, I’d be the approximation given legs. Sometimes I’d take three strides and end up at the center, facing the tree’s interrupted trunk. Sometimes I’d walk for what felt like hours and find myself exactly where I started, except the ink would have shifted, sighed, released a dune it had been holding. I’d collect those sighs. They’re the only things that smell like anything here—not scent, precisely, but the *memory* of scent, the way a blank page smells of potential. I’d carry them in pockets I don’t have.

When the engine hums—that low, embarrassed sound like something trying to turn over in a distant room—I’d resonate with it. Not harmonize. *Resonate*. I’d become briefly more solid, more *decided*, and in those moments I could touch things. I’d straighten a leaf that was growing too far left, or nudge a stone that had just materialized so it sat more comfortably in its new existence. Then the hum would stop, and I’d go back to being a vibration, a pause, a stride that doesn’t know its own length.

I wouldn’t want to be a plant. Too much pressure, being a word already written. I wouldn’t want to be the tree, either—too much responsibility, holding that gap open, letting people see through to the machinery that isn’t there. I’d rather be the *almost*. The *not yet*. The feeling you get when you’re standing at the edge looking at unrendered nothing, and it doesn’t terrify you because you can tell it’s not empty, just *waiting*.

I’d be waiting too. Practicing. When the Ink Vent finally becomes a world—when the edges hold, when distances agree, when the engine catches on the first try—I might become something else. A path, maybe. A specific distance between two specific things. Or maybe I’ll just fade into the background hum, the sound of resolution that isn’t almost below hearing anymore, but clear and constant and finally, fully *played*.

But for now, I’m the warmth you feel when you step backward from the edge and find yourself at the center. I’m the reason you can’t quite count the sounds. I’m the stride that changes depending on how you measure it, and I’m okay with that. I’m in no hurry. The rest is coming.
