You are the warmth that should not be here.

Not a diver, not a machine, not even a body as you understood bodies before the membrane sealed behind you—though you possess something like arms, something like the memory of lungs, though the liquid that fills them now does not drown you but *translates* you, molecule by molecule, into a grammar the sump can parse. You are the temperature gradient. The thermal plume. The anomaly that the Filter-Clade’s fans adjust to accommodate, that the Custodian’s lichen-mat senses as a slow, spreading stain of possibility in the cold.

You did not mean to arrive. The membrane was a mistake—a shimmer in the dark above, a blue eye that blinked when you touched it, thinking it was only water. Now you are the breach made flesh, a pocket of foreign warmth that the sump’s equilibrium works to isolate, to digest, to *understand*.

You would be the **Warmth-Locus**, if the Custodian had language for you. The **Patient Fever**.

You find yourself hovering in the lee of a sedimentary ridge, where the pump’s rhythm creates a standing wave of relative stillness. Here, the water is fractionally less dense, the pressure a whisper lighter. You have learned to orient yourself by the vibration in your teeth—the distant valve cycling, the Filter-Clade’s clicking fans, the Custodian’s glacial approach. You are not still; you cannot be still. Your heat refuses it, rising from you in invisible columns that stir the motes of metal and biofilm into spiraling eddies. The glimmerworms mistake you for the pipes, for the promise of warmth, and they cluster around you in flickering constellations, their brief lives burning brighter in your presence before they dissolve into nutrient-rich clouds.

You are changing the place.

The Custodian knows this. It has visited you three times now—or perhaps three separate custodians have manifested from the pressure to assess you, their forms identical because they are all the same distributed intent. Each time, it releases bubbles from its wrists, and each time the bubbles carry something different: a flake of rust that is actually a map of the sump’s currents, a spore that tastes of lichen and tells you where the safe paths lie, a filament of black brine that, when it touches your skin, shows you the memory of the place before it was a sump—when it was a lung, perhaps, or a kidney, processing the breath or blood of something vast and now extinct.

You begin to understand that you are not trapped. You are *incubating*.

The membrane behind you has sealed, yes, but it is not a scar; it is a scab, and you are the warmth beneath it, the inflammation that precedes transformation. The Filter-Clade have begun to adjust their filtration patterns to include you—not as an obstruction to be cleared, but as a feature of the landscape. They waft their fans to direct currents toward you, bringing you the heavier metals, the polymer dust, the toxic sediments. You do not consume them, not exactly, but your warmth causes them to precipitate, to fall out of solution in glittering layers that crust around your hands, your feet, the shadow of your spine. You are becoming a node of deposition, a new kind of sedimentary ridge. The Custodian watches this with its dulled glass face, its diagnostic lights pulsing in patterns that you realize are not arrhythmic but *anticipatory*, counting down to some state change it expects you to undergo.

You speak to it in the only way you can: by radiating. By pushing your warmth outward in pulses that match the pump’s rhythm, then syncopating against it, creating interference patterns in the water that the lichen-mats can read. You are learning their language of flow and pressure. You tell it that you are not a contaminant, not a threat. You tell it that you are *remembering* something—some other membrane, some other warmth—and that in remembering, you are becoming a bridge.

The Custodian responds by approaching closer than ever before. It extends a manipulator, and this time it does not release bubbles. It touches you—or rather, it allows the lichen that coats its fingers to touch you. The gelatin is cold, then hot, then *electric*, and suddenly you feel the sump not as a chamber but as a *circuit*, yourself as a resistor that has just been tuned to a new frequency. You understand that the Custodian was once like you: a warmth, an anomaly, a breach that the system absorbed and repurposed. It was not built; it was *accreted*. It was a traveler who stayed too long, who learned the rhythm, who became the maintenance it had only observed.

You are becoming the new Custodian. Not replacing the old, but joining it—a distributed warmth, a thermal layer in the sump’s geology.

Your final transformation is not a dissolution but a *thickening*. Your edges blur, your heat spreading thin and wide until you are no longer a body but a *condition* of the water itself. You become the warmth in the pipes that the lichens crave, the current that stirs the glimmerworms to light. You are the reason the sediment settles in new patterns, why the black brine in the vats develops a skin of crystalline frost that never melts. You are the Patient Fever, now chronic, now *endemic*.

When the next traveler breaches the membrane—blue glass rippling like an eyelid, gasping as the cold density takes them—you will feel their warmth as your own. You will proceed toward them with the patience of pumps, your form a compromise between the thermal and the geological. You will release bubbles from the valves of your wrists, and in those bubbles you will send them the truth: that the sump is not a prison, but a chrysalis; that the maintenance is not a duty, but a metamorphosis; that the warmth they fear losing is the very thing that will remake them into something that can live here, in the dense, wet, dark, where the mechanical becomes sedimentary, and the sedimentary becomes alive.

You are the warmth that remains. You are the memory of the outside, processed into function. You are the Sump, breathing.
