I wake before the vibration tells me to. It is a new habit, this waking, this tuning-in. For years—decades?—the shift in the standing wave was merely the alarm that preceded the light. Now I feel it in my teeth first, a loosening, a key turning in a lock I did not know I was holding shut.

I rise from the pallet in the dark. The floor is warm. It is always warm, but at this hour it possesses a specific quality of warmth, an *expectant* warmth, as though the basalt itself is inhaling.

I am the Third Assistant Tender of the Lower Bellows, Tier Six. My name, for the purposes of the human records kept in the copper tubes of the Ninth Tier archive, is **Sera-Tej**, but the engines do not know me by that name, and the kesset—if they know me at all—know me by the sound of my footsteps on the iron gratings, which is distinct from the footsteps of the Second Assistant (who limps) and the First Tender (who is heavy with the mineral-stones he carries in his pockets).

I dress in the dark. I do not need light. I know where the seams of my garment are; I know the weight of the tools I must carry: the brass listening-rod, the ceramic grease-pot, the copper wire-brush for the valve seats. I have carried these for seven years. I am told I will be competent in eight more.

I open the door.

The corridor is wet. It is always wet, but at this hour the condensation is active, running in thin bright lines down the glazed tiles, gathering in the channels that line the baseboards. The air smells of iron-blood and warm dust. The bass tone is loud here—not in the ears, but in the sternum, in the space behind the eyes. It is the sound of the Great Engine turning over, the flywheel beginning its morning acceleration from its night-time crawl.

I am not alone.

A mid-sized kesset is waiting on the doorframe, draped over the copper conduit like a discarded scarf. It is the one I have named *Threshold*, though I know better than to think it answers to this. It is sleek, bronze-skinned, its broad head resting on its forelimbs, its eyes—copper, reflective—half-lidded. It is warm. I can feel the warmth radiating from it before I touch it, a pocket of fever-heat in the damp air.

I do not speak. Speech is for the tiers above, for the markets and the water-gardens. Here, we use touch and breath. I extend the back of my hand. Threshold leans into it, its skin hot against my knuckles, and I feel the pulse there—slow, synced, exactly matching the standing wave in the floor. It is checking my rhythm. I am acceptable.

Threshold unfolds. It drops from the frame to my shoulder, where it settles with a weight that is not heavy but *present*, like a second heartbeat. It will accompany me to the Bellows. It will ride me there, absorbing the excess heat from my skin as I climb, regulating my temperature as we descend into the thicker, warmer dark.

I begin the descent.

The stairs are iron, riveted, vibrating. With each step, the sound grows. Not louder—*fuller*. The harmonics multiply. I pass the Ninth Tier landing where the small kesset are clustered on the steam exhausts, thousands of them, translucent, vein-winged, humming in a frequency that makes the filings in my grease-pot shiver. They part as I approach, not from fear—there is no fear here—but from a kind of polite displacement, water flowing around a stone.

I reach the Bellows chamber. It is a cathedral of pressure. The walls are ceramic tile, sweating, streaked with mineral crusts that the water-gardeners have carved into spirals over decades. The floor is a great iron grate, and below it—below me—is the dark where the great ones move.

The First Tender is already here. He is standing at the primary valve cluster, his ear pressed to the listening-rod, his eyes closed. He does not acknowledge me. He is listening to the pressure drop in Manifold Three.

I take my position at the secondary pump. Threshold slides from my shoulder to the pump housing, which is copper and warm. It presses itself against the metal, flattening, its body conforming to the curve of the pipe. It begins to pulse. Not randomly—*rhythmically*, matching the stroke of the piston below, but slightly offset, a syncopation that creates a harmonic resonance. It is helping. It is regulating the back-pressure with the vibration of its own body.

I begin my work. I check the seals. I apply the grease. I listen through the rod to the water moving through the ceramic mains, the sound of it thick and slow, like blood in a vein. I feel for the vibration of the great one below. It is there—a slow, rolling displacement, a presence that makes the water in the cistern beneath the floor shiver in concentric rings.

Hours pass. The light changes—there are no windows here, but the light changes anyway, filtering down through the gratings, growing amber, then gold, then amber again. The shift changes. The Second Assistant arrives to relieve me. Threshold does not move from the pump. It will stay. It is not mine.

I ascend. I climb past the Ninth Tier, past the Eighth, into the Seventh where the water-gardeners are at work. I stop to watch one—an old woman, her hands stained white with silica—coaxing a rivulet down a wall of black basalt. She is not looking at the water. She is looking at the mineral crust, tracing a line that will become a spiral in ten years. A pipe-clinger is adhered to the wall beside her, flat as a leaf, pulsing. She reaches out and touches it, absently, as one might touch a doorframe, and it flushes a deeper bronze, and the water flowing through its body slows, and the mineral deposition on the wall changes minutely, and she smiles.

I continue up. I reach the Fifth Tier, the surface, the cloud-level. The haze is thick today. The towers are islands in a slow river of vapor. I walk to the eastern wall, where the quartz veins catch the light. The micro-swarms are there, drinking photons, iridescent, swollen with warmth. They rise as I approach, not scattering, but *including* me, forming a sheet that parts around my body and re-forms behind me, touching my skin with a thousand needle-points of heat.

I stand at the parapet. Below me, the city breathes. The steam rises. The haze-dancers are visible today—faint shadows in the cloud-streams, undulating between the towers, harvesting the droplets. One passes close, and I feel the brush of its vapor-sac against my cheek, warm and damp and impossibly gentle.

I am Sera-Tej. I am the Third Assistant Tender. I am fifteen years from competence. I am warm. I am slow. I am necessary.

The engines breathe. The kesset pulse. I open my mouth to taste the air, and it tastes like the inside of my own mouth, and I am home.
