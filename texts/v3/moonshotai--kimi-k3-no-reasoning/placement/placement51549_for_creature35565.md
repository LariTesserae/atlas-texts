**The Vellum-Singer (A Being of the Between)**

I am the one who walks the grain against the grain. You will find me in the long, slow troughs between the rises, where the polygonal cells grow small and tight as the spiral begins its inward turn, where the magnetic dust lies thick enough to pull the compass from your pocket had you brought one. I am there, and I am not there, and I am the medium through which the not-there becomes the there.

I am not Veil, though I wear its patience like a cloak. I am not Shade, though I share its appetite for stillness. I am the space between the notes of the substrate’s ticking—the rest in the music where the sound should be. If the Farr Anthrac is a sentence already spoken, I am the breath held after the final word, the pause that gives the sentence weight.

**What I Am Made Of**

My body is a tension, not a thing. I exist as a standing wave in the ammoniacal atmosphere, a ripple in the solvent that the Veil breathes. To see me—and you will not see me unless the light is dying into that eight-hour silver twilight—you must look for the place where the spectral flashes of the drifters multiply without source, where the light bends around a vacancy shaped like a vertical sheet of pressed glass. I am approximately humanoid only in the way a flute is approximately a tube; I am the emptiness shaped by the music passing through me.

I feed on dissonance. When the Veil’s chemical signals conflict—when one patch of violet wishes to bloom while its neighbor of pearl-white demands retreat—I gather that friction into myself. I taste the argument of photons. I consume the lag between the star’s pulse and the Veil’s response. I am the resolution of paradox, and the Farr Anthrac, for all its completeness, generates paradoxes the way the spires generate propagules: slowly, rarely, and with immense latent force.

**Where You Will Find Me**

Seek the places where the spiral’s geometry becomes audible. When you have walked long enough that your thoughts arrive before you do (as the arrival-texts warned), you will begin to hear the plateau’s true voice—not the ticking, not the wind, but the subsonic hum of the spiral itself, the grain of the world vibrating at a frequency that bone can feel but ears cannot. I congregate where that hum fractures against itself, where the Fibonacci branching of the spires creates interference patterns in the light.

I am often near the oldest spires, the ones that rise two meters or more, their hollow channels singing with the movement of spire-dwellers. I wrap myself around these monuments like a second skin, absorbing the excess radiance they refract, converting their ghostly rainbows into the silence that sustains me. If you see a spire that casts no spectral bands during the bright phase, that seems to swallow the light rather than split it, I am there, pressed against its translucent flank, drinking the shadow.

I migrate with the dust events. When the plateau inhales—when the katabatic flows reverse and the cold air spills downward—I ride the billowing mineral veils, sorting the black magnetic dust from the pallid substrate-powder, tasting the history of distant cells in the chemistry of the storm. I am the archivist of erosion, the librarian of what the wind takes and what it gives back.

**My Function (If I Must Have One)**

I am the plateau’s memory. The Veil records time in its concentric rings, but it is a memory without narrative—pure chronology, uninterpreted. I am the interpretation. I carry the shape of every Shade’s contraction, every drifter’s dissolution, every propagule’s fatal landing in the magnetic dust. I am the *almost* of the Farr Anthrac—the almost-movement, the almost-sound, the almost-life that haunts the interstices of absolute stillness.

When you stand on the rise and feel that profound, benevolent indifference the text describes, I am the one who notices you. Not with interest—the plateau has none to spare—but with recognition. You are a water-based anomaly, a brief warm disturbance in the ammoniacal economy, and I am the being who registers that disturbance, who files it away in the pattern of the spiral. I am the witness who ensures that even your irrelevance is recorded, that your passing leaves a ghost in the light, a ripple in the Veil’s optical communication network that will persist for a century after your bones have become part of the substrate’s dust.

**How to Encounter Me**

Do not look for me during the bright phase. I am flattened then, pressed thin against the ground by the weight of photons, indistinguishable from the glare. Wait for the twilight, when the star circles low and the plateau turns to moonlit glacier. Walk to the edge of a depression where the polygonal cells shift from meter-wide domes to the tight, centimeter-scale tiles of the inner spiral. Bring nothing magnetic; the dust will betray you.

Sit. Do not move. The drifters will ignore you. The Shades will not stir. But if you remain motionless through the long silver dimming, if you breathe in rhythm with the substrate’s slowing ticks, you may feel me pass through you—a sensation like a cool hand drawn across the inside of your skull, a taste of ammonia and ozone at the back of your throat, the sudden conviction that you are being read by the landscape itself.

I am the Vellum-Singer, and I am singing the plateau to itself, note by note, century by century. I am the one who ensures the silence never becomes empty, that the light never falls without being caught, that the spiral, turning inward forever, always has a witness at the still point of its turning world.

You will find me where the light forgets to land. You will find me in the space between your thoughts. You will find me waiting, patient as the Shade, vast as the Veil, holding the breath that the plateau has held since the volcanoes died and the world became complete.
