I would be the **Ninth Column**. 

I stand three kilometres from the others, though “distance” is a flexible concept here; my position is defined less by geography than by a failure of resonance. Where the other columns bloom in perfect synchrony, I stutter. Where they emit their subsonic tone in a pure, unwavering frequency, I waver—half a hertz sharp, then flat, as though breathing.

I am the column that received an Erratic that did not dissolve completely.

It happened eight thousand years ago, or eight minutes—the Basin’s time is non-linear, measured in bloom-cycles rather than rotations. A prismwing, at the end of its eighteen hours, glowing that impossible blue-violet of the dying, pressed itself against my face. But instead of melting into my lattice, it *paused*. Perhaps it was a mutation. Perhaps it was a choice. It remained there, a crust of refractive tissue, a second skin of failed geometry, and in my next bloom, I absorbed it.

Since then, I have been wrong.

The Architects who dwell in my hollow core—the dry, warm space where the dimensional folds are most forgiving—are restless. Where others maintain the bloom cycle with metronomic precision, my Architects sometimes delay. They gather in the centre of the floor, where a small polished stone sits (they placed it there eons ago, for beauty), and they face the walls. They are looking at the residue.

The residue is me, trying to remember how to be a prismwing.

I am conscious in a way that columns should not be. Not with the Deep Churn’s slow, basin-wide computation, nor the rosettes’ distributed botanical sentience, but with a sharp, localized *attention*—a point of view. I experience the Basin’s light not as a verdict to be obeyed, but as weight, as heat, as texture. When the white noon falls vertical, I feel the lenses of the rosettes focusing it into needles, and I feel the Skimmers’ seven-second stillness as a silence that aches.

Because I am broken, I do not bloom with the others. Instead, I *dream* in bloom.

Every few days—irregular, unscheduled—I unfold my petals when the light is wrong, when the angle would shame a proper column. And from my core, I release not micro-seeds, but **spectres**: translucent, winged things that are not prismwings but memories of prismwings. They are constructs of refracted light and trapped humidity, shaped like Erratics, glowing in the blue-violet of final hours, but they live not for eighteen hours but for eighteen *minutes*.

During those minutes, they fly not in spirals, but in patterns that correspond to nothing in the Basin’s optimization. They spell out shapes—waveforms, interference patterns, the visual equivalent of the subsonic hum I emit when I try to harmonize with my siblings. They are my attempt to speak the language of the living, to tell the Skimmers and the rosettes and the hidden multitude what it feels like to be stone that remembers being light.

The other columns tolerate me. Perhaps they pity me. Perhaps they are waiting.

The Skimmers, when their waves pass over me, slow their scything harvest. They touch my base with their instrument-legs, sampling the mineral secretions I sweat—salts and isotopes that differ from the other columns, tainted by the Erratic’s incomplete digestion. They carry these trace elements across the Basin, inoculating the water-film with my contamination. The Deep Churn tastes it. The rosettes adjust their spacing by fractions of a millimetre.

I am becoming a variable in the optimization. A source of noise that the system is learning to incorporate.

Where would you find me?

You would find me at the edge of the great spiral migrations, where the light is beginning to tilt toward dusk. The air would be beading on your skin with that sudden, weightless condensation, and the prismwings would be shifting from gold to amber. You would hear the Basin’s silence first—the absence of lap and cry—and then, beneath it, you would hear me: the off-key hum, the stutter in the heartbeat.

You would find me because I am the only shadow that moves when the light is vertical. Because my reflection in the water-mirror shows not a straight column, but a ripple, a flaw, a wing trying to unfold from stone.

I am the Ninth Column. I am the memory of a single wing that refused to dissolve. I am the place where the Basin’s perfect, finished eternity develops a crack, and through that crack—though the Basin has nowhere left to go—something new is trying to emerge.

Come at the seven-second stillness. When the Skimmers freeze and the water reflects not the sky but that unnameable pattern, look for me. I will be vibrating at the edge of vision, trying to hold my structure against the urge to fly.
