I find myself at the western edge of a collapsed hearth, where the mica sheets have fallen into a perfect parabola—not to speak, but to *listen*. The ceramic dust coats my skin, and I feel the Choir not in my ears but in my teeth, a pressure that makes my bones feel hollow, fluted, ready to sing.

I am not Tuned. Not yet. My body is still too dense, too water-logged with the memory of oceans I left behind. I arrived here three seasons ago—or was it three years? Time dilates in the thin air, stretching like a sustained note—and I came as the **Discordant**, the one who cannot hold a frequency. My steps are too heavy; my breath is a ragged percussion that startles the Glass-Wings into chaotic spirals. Where the Tuned move like sustained tones through the forest, I stutter. I am a broken metronome in a place that demands perfect rhythm.

I tried to build a shelter when I first arrived, stacking the salt-crystal sheets into walls, attempting to *block out* the sound. I wanted silence. I wanted the safety of deafness. But the Salt rejects such architecture. My walls crumbled within days, not from structural failure, but because they were *wrong*—they created a dampening, a dead spot in the resonance, and the plateau itself corrected my error through a resonant cascade that shattered my constructs into harmless, singing dust.

Now I live in the negative space. I have found a niche between the roots of two vast glass trees whose frequencies cancel at this specific point, creating a shallow null—not the weaponized silence of the Dust-Runners, but a *hush*, a place where the Choir drops to a whisper. Here, I am learning to knap.

My hands are clumsy. The obsidian flakes slip, and my fingers bleed, and the blood is too wet, too alien in this dry world. But I persist, because the Tuned have taught me—through their patient, humming attention—that to knap is to *tune*. Each strike must be precise, not just in angle, but in **pitch**. I am making a tuning-fork from the bone of a Glass-Wing, a creature that died in a cascade last season, its crystalline membranes shattered into a rain of harmonic residue. I hollow the bone slowly, tapping it with my ceramic-tipped finger—no, not ceramic yet, just bone, but I feel the density changing, the marrow drying, becoming resonant.

I am becoming the **Listener-Knapper**, the one who shapes the silence between notes. I cannot yet hum the deep frequencies of the Tuned; my chest cavity is too small, my lungs too accustomed to thick, wet air. But I have learned to hold dissonance.

In a hollowed radius bone, I carry a *shard* of the Cascade—the precise, ugly frequency of a cliff-face dying. I collected it from the aftermath, scooping the vibrating dust into the bone while the air still screamed with sympathetic destruction. The Tuned crave these dissonances; they drink them to remember that the world can end. But I use mine differently. When the Choir becomes too much, when the sound pushes against my skin like a physical weight that threatens to crush my ribs into powder, I open the bone-flask and release a fraction of that shattered frequency.

It cuts through the music like a blade. For a moment—terrible, ecstatic—I am alone. I am the only thing in the world. The Glass-Wings scatter. The distant Mantids pause in their compositions. And in that moment of perfect, ugly isolation, I remember who I was before I forgot water.

Then the dissonance fades, absorbed by the Salt, and the Choir returns, and I am grateful. I am learning that the discord is not the opposite of the song; it is the **space that makes the song visible**.

I sit now in my shallow null, holding the half-finished tuning-fork, and I tap it against the ceramic ground. The note is flat, wrong. But as the vibration travels up my arm, I feel the chambers in my bones—new, hollow, growing—respond. I feel the first faint clarifying of my skin, a translucence at the wrists where the light passes through and reveals not blood, but **dust**, singing dust.

I am not Tuned. But I am no longer entirely Discordant. I am becoming something in between—a **Resonant Gap**, a place where the world's noise pauses to catch its breath. And when the Null-Mother passes in the distance, her fortress of silence sliding across the landscape like a cold hand, I do not flee. I tap my tuning-fork again, listening for the frequency that will let me into her dream, where the future hums in the absence of song.
