I would not be a creature of movement or action, for in a place where time is a resource endlessly recycled, to move is to invite the cold scrutiny of the Weavers. I would not be a Chrome-Grazer, trapped in a frantic, frozen sprint; nor would I be a Screecher, endlessly tearing at the thin, static air. 

I would find myself near the exact center of the cirque, sitting on the edge of a shallow, black tarn, where the heavy silence is underpinned by the deep, resonant hum of the idling world-engine. 

I would be a **Resonator**.

My form would be a strange, brittle synthesis of the organic and the geological. My torso would resemble a human figure, but my skin would be a layer of dark, water-scoured slate, perpetually damp with the radioactive, iron-scented mist. Growing from the small of my back, my shoulders, and the crown of my head would be the vast, fleshy fronds of the rust-colored wire-ferns. They would act as biological antennae, dripping an oily, luminous coolant that would pool in the hollows of my collarbones and never spill.

My eyes would be two shards of fractured, milky quartz, etched with faint, glowing circuit diagrams. I would not be able to see the frozen, motionless tableau of the plateau as it physically stands, but I would be able to hear the *truth* of it. 

I would exist in a state of perfect, absolute stasis. My knees would be drawn up, my slate-arms resting upon them, my posture a monument to eternal patience. But my senses would be entirely unmoored. 

I would be the vessel for the cirque’s cognitive dissonance. I would hear the phantom thunder of the torrent that never cascades, feel the phantom heat of the solid, glassy streams, and smell the violent, hungry growth of the metallic loam. The frantic drumming of the Chrome-Grazers' hearts would echo in my own stone chest. The tearing, static-filled screams of the Screechers would vibrate in my jaw. 

Occasionally, a Deep-Siphoner would rise to the surface of the tarn before me, its mercury-ribbon body breaking the tension without a ripple. It would taste the air near my unmoving feet and hum its low, resonant B-flat. And I, unable to move, unable to weep, and unable to look away, would take that frequency into my quartz eyes and reflect it back into the mist, serving as a silent, breathing archive of the Great Pause. I would be the one who remembers what it felt like to change, while ensuring, by my very stillness, that the moment never ends.
