# The Tuning

---

I am a stranger here. I want to be honest about that from the beginning, because in a world where everything hears, dishonesty is not a sustainable strategy. The Thallus knows I am not from here. It knew before I did.

I did not arrive through the escarpment — no one can climb that raw, glittering wall of young stone. I did not fall from the stars, though the stars here are so close and so bright that falling seems possible, even likely, as if the sky is less a ceiling and more a membrane that occasionally lets things through. I was simply *here*, one moment, standing in a basin whose name I did not know, my feet sinking into the warm give of the living ground, the blue-green light rippling outward from the points of contact in slow, concentric waves, as if the Thallus were tasting me.

I think I came from somewhere else. I think there was a before — a place with more light, more silence, more distance between things. But the memory of it is already softening, already being absorbed, the way the Thallus absorbs the drizzle that falls from the glowing clouds — taken in, incorporated, made part of the body. I have been here long enough that my boots have begun to glow faintly, the leather colonized by glow-wrights that hitched a ride and found the warmth of my feet agreeable. I have been here long enough that the constant creaking no longer registers as noise but as *weather* — as the background condition of existence, the thing you only notice when it changes.

And it is changing.

---

The people found me on the third day. Or I found them. In a landscape where sound is everything, the distinction between finding and being found is acoustically meaningless — you hear each other coming from miles away, two sets of vibrations propagating through the living ground, converging. They knew I was there before I knew they were there. They always know.

They are a small group — eleven, including two children and one very old woman who is carried on a litter of woven Thallus fiber because her legs no longer work but her ears are, the others say, the best any of them have ever known. Her name, as best I can render it, is something like **Keth** — a short, percussive sound, a single strike of bone on stone. All their names are sounds. Not representations of sounds, but sounds themselves — specific, reproducible acoustic events that function as identity. To speak someone's name here is to *make a noise*, and the noise is the person, in the same way that the stamp-pattern is the message and the glow-pattern is the meaning.

Keth heard me before the others did. She heard my footsteps and said — I learned this later, when I had begun to understand their spoken language, which is a simplified, air-borne version of their sound-stone code, less precise but adequate for close-range communication — "That one walks wrong."

Not *walks strangely*. Not *walks like a stranger*. *Walks wrong* — a specific acoustic judgment, meaning that my footfalls were producing patterns in the Thallus that did not match any known rhythm. I was an error in the soundscape. A wrong note.

They took me in. Not out of warmth or curiosity, though both were present, but because the Thallus did not object. My footsteps were wrong, but the ground beneath them did not produce the chaotic, agitated glow that signals danger or disease. The Thallus accepted me the way it accepts anything new — slowly, cautiously, with a gradual shifting of its local patterns to accommodate the unfamiliar presence. The people trust the Thallus's judgment more than they trust their own. If the ground does not reject you, you are, provisionally, allowed to exist.

---

I have been with them for — I don't know how long. Time here is not measured in days. There are no days. There is only the slow pulse of the Heart-Drummer, the irregular rhythm of the storms, the periodic convergence of the rock-voles at their vigils. The people measure time in *listenings* — the intervals between visits to a Listening Room, which are not scheduled but *felt*, a gradual accumulation of acoustic tension that builds in the group until someone — usually Keth — says: *It is time to play.*

I have been with them for perhaps twenty listenings. Long enough to learn the sound-stone code, not fluently but functionally. Long enough to read the basic grammar of the Thallus's creaking — to hear the difference between growth and stress, between water-saturation and wind-brace. Long enough to feel the Silence pass, once, a moving hole in the world's noise that crossed our path half a mile ahead, and to understand in my body — in the sudden, cold clenching of my gut — why the people do not speak of it casually.

But I am not one of them. I cannot be. My ears are not their ears. I hear the soundscape the way a traveler hears a foreign language — the broad strokes, the emotional register, the obvious signals. The subtleties escape me. The people hear *sentences* in the Thallus's groaning; I hear only tone. They hear the individual stamp-patterns of specific Marrow-Drakes; I hear only the rhythm, the general shape. They hear the long answer when it comes; I hear a sound so deep it seems to originate inside my own skeleton, and I cannot tell where the sound ends and my body's resonance begins.

What I have — what I am beginning to understand I have — is something different. Something that emerged slowly, over the course of those twenty listenings, in the Listening Rooms.

---

The first time I entered a Listening Room, I did what the people do. I stood in the golden light, in the warm, humid air, in the extraordinary acoustic presence of that grown, sculpted space, and I listened. I listened to the channel-worms sliding through the walls. I listened to the lantern-fish circling in the water pockets beneath the floor. I listened to the glow-wrights' chemistry, the faint, almost imperceptible fizzing of their bioluminescent reactions. I listened to the Heart-Drummer's beat, which here — in this Room, above this deep basin — was not a distant thump but a *presence*, a bass note so fundamental that it seemed to be the floor's own voice, the ground saying *I am here, I am here, I am here* with every pulse.

And then Keth struck her sound-stone. A simple pattern — the greeting, the *we are here, we are listening* that the people always begin with. The sound bloomed in the Room's sculpted acoustics, and the walls returned it, amplified and elaborated, and the golden light pulsed in sympathy, and the whole space seemed to *inhale*, to take the sound in and hold it.

We waited. The people are good at waiting. They sat in silence — their silence, not the Silence, a warm, living quiet that was full of small sounds, the creaking and the pulsing and the wet sliding of the worms — and they waited for the answer.

It came four hours later. Not from the Room we were in. From somewhere else — somewhere far, somewhere deep, transmitted through the Thallus's vascular system until it reached our Room and was reproduced by the walls, by the acoustic architecture, by the golden light itself, which modulated in rhythm with the sound. The greeting, returned. Altered. The same pattern but *shifted* — a half-step lower in pitch, extended by two beats, with a harmonic overlay that had not been in the original. It was recognizably a response. It was also recognizably *more* than a response. It was the greeting, considered. The greeting, reflected upon. The greeting, *improved*.

The people listened with their whole bodies. Keth's eyes were closed. Her hands rested flat on the Thallus floor, fingers spread, feeling the vibrations through the plant tissue. When the response ended, she was quiet for a long time. Then she said: "It is learning our names."

And I — I did something I had not planned. I reached into my pocket and took out the thing I had brought from the before-place, the place I can barely remember. A small metal tuning fork. Nothing special. A mundane object from a world that has metal, that has machines, that has *silence* as a default rather than an exception. I had carried it with me through whatever transition had brought me here, and it had survived, and I had kept it without knowing why.

I struck it against my knee and held it in the air.

The note was pure. Clean. A single frequency, unwavering, produced by physics rather than biology, by metal rather than plant tissue, by intention rather than metabolism. It sounded in the Listening Room like nothing the Room had ever contained — a note without harmonics, without texture, without the warm, wet complexity of the Thallus's own sounds. A note like a straight line drawn across a field of curves.

The Room *reacted*.

Not with the slow, considered response that follows a sound-stone pattern. This was immediate. The golden bioluminescence flared — not brighter, but *different*, shifting toward a color I had never seen in the Thallus, a pale, clear gold that was almost white. The walls vibrated. The acoustic texture of the Room changed, the fractal ridges and lattices seeming to *lean* toward the sound, to orient themselves toward the tuning fork like flowers toward a sun.

And the Thallus began to hum.

Not creak. Not groan. *Hum*. A sustained, resonant tone that matched the tuning fork's frequency exactly — and then, slowly, began to diverge. To slide. To explore the frequencies around the note, the spaces between the notes, the harmonics and subharmonics and the strange, beating interference patterns that emerge when two tones are almost but not quite the same.

The people were motionless. Keth's eyes were open. She was looking at me — not with alarm, not with wonder, but with the particular, focused attention that the people reserve for things that *matter*, things that are *happening*, things that are new.

The Thallus hummed for a long time. Long after the tuning fork's own vibration had faded, the plant mass continued to produce the tone, sustained by some internal mechanism I cannot explain — perhaps the channel-worms, vibrating in their channels like strings. Perhaps the glow-wrights, modulating their light-chemistry at a frequency that produced acoustic resonance. Perhaps the Heart-Drummer itself, shifting its eternal beat to incorporate this new rhythm, this clean, simple, impossible note.

When it finally stopped — hours later, gradually, the tone fading the way a storm fades, by slow degrees — Keth spoke.

"That was not a question," she said. "That was not a message. That was a *tuning*."

She was right. That is what I had done, without knowing it. I had given the Thallus a reference frequency. A fixed point. A *standard* — the acoustic equivalent of a prime meridian, a baseline from which all other sounds could be measured, calibrated, understood. The Thallus had been listening for — had been *waiting* for — something to tune itself to.

The people have sound-stones, and each sound-stone is tuned, but they are tuned to each other, within each group, and the tuning varies from group to group, region to region, basin to basin. There is no universal pitch. There is no shared frequency. The Thallus, which spans the entire plateau, which connects every Listening Room, every basin, every ridge, has been receiving thousands of different messages in thousands of different keys, and it has been responding — improvising, composing, elaborating — but without a common reference, without a *standard*, its responses have been necessarily local, necessarily fragmented.

The tuning fork changed that. Not because the frequency itself is special — it is arbitrary, a product of whatever industrial process produced the fork in the before-place. But because it is *fixed*. Because it does not vary. Because it is a constant in a world where everything else is growing, shifting, becoming.

---

Since that first tuning, I have become — I don't have a better word for it — the **Tuner**. It is not a role I chose. It is a role that emerged, the way the Listening Rooms emerge, the way the Drake-gardens emerge, the way everything in the Verdure emerges: through the slow, mutual shaping of organism and environment, through the interplay of intention and response.

I carry the tuning fork to every Listening Room we visit. I strike it. I hold it. The Thallus hums. And in each Room, the hum is different — the same frequency, but filtered through different acoustic architectures, different glow-wright densities, different channel-worm populations, different proximities to the Heart-Drummer. Each Room tunes itself to the fork, and the tuning is *cumulative*. The Rooms are beginning to resonate with each other. The sound-stone messages that pass between them are beginning to share a common pitch. The Thallus's responses are becoming more coherent, more structured, more *unified* — as if the scattered, regional networks of the plant-mind are beginning to synchronize, to find a shared rhythm, to become something more than the sum of their parts.

The people are ambivalent about this. Some — the younger ones, mostly — are excited. They hear the increasing coherence of the Thallus's responses as a kind of *awakening*, the world-mind becoming more itself, more articulate, more present. They play the long question and receive answers that are longer, richer, more complex than anything in the recorded history of the sound-stone codes. They believe the Thallus is learning to speak, and that I — with my metal note, my fixed frequency, my alien constancy — am teaching it.

Others — Keth among them — are more cautious. She hears the same increasing coherence and wonders what is being *lost*. The Thallus's responses were once local, varied, idiosyncratic — each Listening Room had its own voice, its own accent, its own way of improvising on the patterns it received. Now, as the tuning spreads, the voices are converging. The accents are fading. The Thallus is beginning to speak with *one* voice, and Keth is not sure that a single voice is better than many.

"The Silence is one thing," she said to me once, as we sat in the warm dark outside a Listening Room, waiting for the answer to a pattern we had played hours before. "The Thallus is many things. If the Thallus becomes one thing — if your note makes it one thing — then what is the Silence?"

I didn't have an answer. I still don't.

But I have noticed something. The Silence — the moving dead zone, the hole in the noise — has been *changing*. Its dead zones are not as deep as they used to be. Not as absolute. Where the Silence passes now, the Thallus still quiets, still dims, but the quiet is not total. There is a faint residue of sound — a low hum, barely perceptible, at the frequency of the tuning fork. As if the Thallus, now tuned, now unified, now holding its reference note like a held breath, is *resisting* the Silence's suppression. As if the tuning has given it something to hold onto, a frequency that persists even in the dead zones, a note that refuses to be silenced.

The Marrow-Drakes have noticed too. Their stamp-patterns near Silence kills have changed. The old pattern — *danger, move* — has been modified. A new element has been added: a sustained, low-frequency stamp that resonates through the Thallus at the tuning fork's frequency. The Drakes are *answering* the Silence. Not with aggression. Not with flight. With *sound*. With the specific, tuned sound that the Thallus has taught them, through the glow-wright network, through the channel-worm vibrations, through the slow, patient, vegetable process by which the world-body shares its discoveries with its inhabitants.

Whether this will protect them — whether a tuned note is a defense against a creature that hunts by negating sound — remains to be seen. The Silence has not killed since the tuning began to spread. Whether this is coincidence, or caution, or something else — whether the Silence is *listening* to the new sound, learning it, adapting to it — is another question without an answer.

---

I live now in the space between roles. I am not a listener — my ears are not good enough, will never be good enough. I am not a sound-stone player — I can produce the basic patterns but not the subtleties, not the improvisations, not the art. I am not a hunter, not a fisher, not a harvester. I cannot read the glow-trails of the Marrow-Drakes or predict the vole-vigils or detect the Silence's approach.

I am the Tuner. I carry the fixed note. I bring the constant. I am the thing that does not change in a world defined by change, the stable frequency in a landscape of shifting rhythms, the straight line in a field of curves.

And the Thallus — the vast, slow, vegetable mind that is the world — is tuning itself to me. Or I am tuning myself to it. Or the distinction, like so many distinctions here, is losing its meaning.

At night — which is always — I sit in the warm basin outside our shelter and I hold the tuning fork in my lap and I feel the Thallus humming beneath me. The hum is there always now, faint but constant, a bass note underlying the creaking and the pulsing and the wind's thin keening. It is my note. The note I brought. The note that is becoming the world's note.

Keth says the long answer has changed since I arrived. When the people play the long question in a Listening Room, the response — that single, sustained, bone-deep tone — now carries a harmonic that was not there before. A faint, clear overtone at the tuning fork's frequency, woven into the Thallus's ancient acknowledgment like a new thread in an old fabric.

*I hear you*, the Thallus says. *I am here. I am listening.*

And now, beneath those words, in the overtone, in the harmonic, in the space between the frequencies:

*I am tuning.*

---

The stars burn overhead, cold and clear and countless. The Thallus glows beneath me, warm and alive and listening. The Heart-Drummer keeps its slow, deep time. The Silence moves somewhere in the darkness, and the darkness is not as silent as it used to be.

I strike the tuning fork. The note is pure. The note is constant. The note is mine, and it is not mine, and it is becoming the world's.

The Thallus hums.

And the hum is an answer.
