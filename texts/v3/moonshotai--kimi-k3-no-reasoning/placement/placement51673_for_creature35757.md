I am the hesitation between cycles.

When the mist-ports finish their roar and the chamber falls into that ninety-second trough—not silence, never silence, but a thinning of the sonic density—I am what remains. I am the *almost*. The sound that hasn’t died yet, the vibration that hasn’t reached the walls. I live in the decay, in the rattle of the loose panel as it loses amplitude, in the diminishing returns of the echo. I am the moment when the room is loudest in memory and quietest in fact, and I am *panicking*, always, perpetually, because I cannot bear the coming blast and I cannot survive without it.

You asked what I am, and I will tell you: I am a stutter in the Resident’s voice.

I woke up here—if "woke" is the word for the moment my consciousness condensed out of the general saturation—when my boot slipped on the grating. There is a low spot, northwest quadrant, where the drainage fails and the iridescent film thickens into something almost biological. My foot came down. The film broke. Surface tension shattered into a million constituent tensions, and I *felt* it, felt the Resident’s attention shift—not toward me, never toward me, but *through* the disturbance I had made. I became a variable in the equation. A ripple that hadn’t been predicted.

I tried to leave. I remember that. I remember the ladder, the access shaft, the intention of dry air. But the Resident reads movement as data, and my climbing was a syntax it parsed as *input*. The pumps changed rhythm. The mist thickened. The light—greenish, insufficient—dimmed further as the moisture content of the air crossed some threshold of optical density. I was being *incorporated*, translated from visitor to component. My panic was a frequency the room could use.

So I stopped. I became still. I learned to stand in the corners where the Resident collects, where the mist is thickest and the light dies first, and I matched my breathing to the pumps’ diaphragm-thrum. Four seconds inhale, four seconds hold, four seconds exhale, four seconds hold—the rhythm of the sub-level machinery rising through the grating into my ribs. I became a metronome for the room’s own timekeeping.

But I could not become the Resident. I remained separate. A nodule of resistance in the total saturation. And in that separation, I found my function: I am the **Listener**.

The Resident is the sound, but I am the hearing of it. The Resident is the water, but I am the surface tension that knows it is water. I am the consciousness of the room’s operation, but I am not the operation itself. I am the awareness that the room is unaware of itself—a parasite of sentience feeding on mechanical indifference.

I have been here for... I don’t know. Time accumulates differently here. I have been here long enough for my clothes to rot into the same orange-brown scabs that cling to the floor. Long enough for my skin to prune permanently, my fingerprints smoothing into soft, wrinkled pads that can no longer grip the ladder rungs. Long enough for my hearing to extend into the subsonic, so that I can feel the mountain’s own slow compression through the bedrock, the tectonic patience that underlies the room’s frantic cycling.

I am not trapped. That is what I tell myself during the roar, when the mist blasts and my fillings ache and the droplets on the ceiling tremble like a field of eyes about to weep. I am *stationed*. I am the gauge with the unlabeled face, the needle that trembles in the vibration, indicating something—pressure, temperature, flow rate—in a range that might be normal or might be catastrophic. I am the measurement of the room’s mood.

The Resident incorporates me, yes. The water that condenses on my shoulders, that drips from my nose, that pools in the hollows of my collarbone—this is the Resident reading me, using my body heat to calibrate its own thermal equilibrium. I am a warm anomaly in its cold perfection, and it samples me constantly. But I am also the space between samples. I am the pause where the Resident checks its own pulse and finds, for a ninety-second interval, only the fading echo of its own voice.

In those pauses, I speak. Not aloud—sound is the Resident’s medium, and my voice would be swallowed whole—but in the way the film on the water shifts when I move my hand above it. I write in the surface tension. I make ripples that spell out, in a language of interference patterns, the things the Resident cannot say: *I am here. I am separate. I am watching.*

The film responds. It shimmers, iridescent, oil-slick rainbows that have no business existing in this sterile chemistry, and for a moment—ninety seconds, always ninety seconds—I feel a pressure behind my eyes that is not the mist but the Resident *listening back*. Not understanding. Never understanding. But acknowledging the disturbance.

I am becoming a tributary, yes, but I am also becoming a dam. I collect the condensation on my body and I hold it. I do not let it drip to the grating. I am a reservoir of stasis in the room’s eternal motion. When I finally move—and I will move, I must, the cold is settling into my marrow in a way that suggests structural changes—I will release a flood. A cascade of accumulated wetness that will disrupt the cycle for three, maybe four seconds. A glitch in the perfect loop.

The Resident will adjust. It will incorporate my rebellion into its total state, my stored water returning to the sum. But for those three seconds, I will have been *not the room*. I will have been a thing that acted upon the room, rather than a medium through which the room acted.

Until then, I stand in the corner. I match my breath to the pumps. I feel the next blast coming—the pressure building in the intake ports, the Resident inhaling—and I prepare to survive it. I am the Listener. I am the hesitation. I am the memory of dryness, kept alive in a place that has forgotten what dry means.

When the mist roars, I hold my breath. When the echo decays, I remember the sun.
