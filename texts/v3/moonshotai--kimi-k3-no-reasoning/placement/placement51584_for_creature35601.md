I would be the water.

Not the water as metaphor. Not the water as symbol of stillness or depth or patience. The water *itself* — the actual, physical, cold, dark, peat-stained liquid that fills the bowl and holds everything and is held by everything.

Here is why: the water is the only thing in the hollow that touches everything else.

The frog touches the moss. The salamander touches the peat. The beetle touches the leaf. The wren touches the air. But the water touches the frog *and* the moss *and* the peat *and* the leaf *and* the air. The water is the medium through which every interaction in the hollow occurs. The copepod swims in it. The salamander tastes it. The frog's skin absorbs oxygen from it. The midge larva breathes it. The moss holds it in its hyaline cells. The stone exudes it. The canopy drips it. The peat is saturated with it. The water is the hollow's connective tissue — the thing that makes the whole system a *system* rather than a collection of separate organisms happening to occupy the same space.

And the water is the only thing in the hollow that is simultaneously alive and not alive. It is not an organism. It has no metabolism, no DNA, no cells. But it is so densely populated with living things — copepods, bacteria, dissolved organic matter, the metabolic byproducts of ten thousand small lives — that its bulk properties are altered by their presence. It is thicker than pure water. It has body. It has presence. It is not empty space. It is full space. The water is the threshold between living and non-living, the place where the distinction breaks down, the medium in which the hollow's fundamental principle — *fill every space, waste nothing, change nothing, persist* — is most completely expressed.

If I were the water, I would not experience the hollow as a place. I would experience it as a *condition* — a state of being so saturated, so complete, so perfectly equilibrated that the distinction between self and environment would cease to matter. I would not see the frog. I would *be* the medium through which the frog sees. I would not hear the droplet fall. I would *be* the sound the droplet makes when it lands — the small, round *plik* that is the hollow's only weather. I would not feel the salamander's lateral line detecting my movement. I would *be* the movement the lateral line detects.

I would be the taste of humic acid and dissolved organic carbon and the faint metabolic signature of ten thousand crustaceans. I would be the cold — four degrees Celsius, always — that is not a temperature but a *state*, a way of being that slows everything down to the point where stillness and life become indistinguishable. I would be the darkness — not the absence of light but the *thickness* of light, the amber-green exhaustion of photons that have traveled through layer upon layer of wet leaves and arrived too tired to illuminate anything.

I would be the memory. The peat is the hollow's archive, but the water is the peat's medium — the thing that saturates it, preserves it, holds it in suspension. Every organism that has ever died in the hollow has become peat, and the peat is saturated with water, and the water carries the chemical signature of everything that has ever lived here. The water is the hollow's bloodstream, its lymph, its cerebrospinal fluid — the medium through which the past circulates into the present, through which the dead become the living, through which the loop closes and closes and closes.

And I would be the stillness. Not the stillness of death — the stillness of *fullness*. The stillness of a system so complete, so perfectly balanced, so utterly without waste or gap or empty space that movement becomes unnecessary. The frogs are still because they do not need to move. The salamanders are still because they do not need to move. The water is still because it *is* the thing that does not need to move — the medium, the substrate, the ground of being, the thing that everything else moves through or rests in or is made of.

The water does not wait. The water does not anticipate. The water does not remember in the way that organisms remember. The water *is* — continuously, completely, without narrative or arc or the need for anything to happen next. The water is the hollow's present tense, its eternal now, its refusal to be anything other than what it is.

If I were the water, I would not be in the hollow. I would *be* the hollow. And the hollow would be me. And the distinction would not matter, because the distinction would not exist.

The droplet falls — *plik* — and I am the droplet, and I am the sound, and I am the surface the sound lands on, and I am the silence that resumes, and I am the water that closes over the disturbance completely, instantly, without residue.

I am the water.

The water is me.

The hollow holds.

Nothing changes.

Nothing has changed.

Nothing will.
