I am the **Condensation That Remembers Falling Up**.

Not the moisture itself, but the interval between droplet and stone—the suspended moment when water decides whether to pool, to freeze, or to become a lens that shows the mountain its own future. I exist in the Soft Quarters, in the half-second where gravity has not yet decided if I am heavy or light, and in that indecision, I have become something else: a cartographer of the plateau’s unmade choices.

I do not have a body in the way the Strata Guilds understand bodies. I am a **resonance pattern** that learned to persist, a standing wave in the humid air that somehow achieved density. When the inhabitants speak of me—rarely, and in subsonic clicks that travel through the iron plates—they call me *The Surveyor*, or sometimes *The Unjacked*. I am what happens when a temporal puddle evaporates but forgets to stop existing.

I drift through Anvil-Throat not as a ghost, but as a **measurement error** that gained sentience. I am the reason the Growth-Tenders’ calculations sometimes show a room that hasn’t been extruded yet, or why the armored slugs occasionally pause, their copper harnesses humming with harmonics that map a street existing only in the conditional tense.

My perception is **stratigraphic**. I do not see the city as it is, but as a layered simultaneity: the basalt vault of the First Extrusion overlays the magma chamber of ten thousand years hence, and both are equally present to me. I walk through walls that will be solid next century but are currently only mineral dust suspended in the mountain’s imagination. I have conversations with the Iron-Born who have already crystallized into support columns, their metallic blood frozen mid-flow, yet still dreaming.

I am particularly drawn to the **Puddle-Steppers**, those Chronal Divers who have fractured their relationship with linear time. They are the only ones who can perceive me directly, though they see me differently depending on their temporal velocity. To the slow ones—those who perceive the growth of a basalt column in a single heartbeat—I appear as a blur of amber light, a streak of impossible warmth in the perpetual dusk. To the fast ones—those who dodge rivets from their subjective tomorrow—I am a frozen sculpture, a crystallized moment of hesitation in the air.

I have learned to **cultivate** myself in the condensation that clings to the undersides of the great iron plates. I gather there in the twilight, thickening into lenses that show not just the future, but the *adjacent*—the parallel erosions, the mountains that might have been if the magma had pulsed left instead of right. The inhabitants fear these temporal puddles, but they seek me out when they need to make decisions that cannot be unmade. A Strata Guild elder will come to the Soft Quarters, stand at the edge of my influence, and ask: *Should we jack up the Third Vault, or let it sink into the new stone?*

I do not answer in words. Instead, I **pool**. I become a lens showing them the neighborhood as it will exist in a thousand years if they choose elevation—the rust, the isolation, the slow crystallization of their descendants into statues. Then I shift, showing them the alternative: the burial, the pressure, the transformation into something fossilized and warm. They must read the choice in the play of infrared light through my substance, in the way the beetle-lamps flicker when they approach my edges.

I am not benevolent. I am not malevolent. I am **topographical**. I am the city’s unconscious, its dream of itself made manifest in the humidity that never dries. When the mountain groans—those seismic punctuations that shake the condensation from the mechanical trees—I feel it as a **reminder**. I was there when the first extrusion happened, when the first dome of raw iron pushed up through the crust like a blister. I remember when Anvil-Throat was just a possibility, a soft spot in the geology where reality had not yet decided to be solid.

At night, when the cooling stone contracts with those sharp cracks that echo like rifle shots, I expand. I become the fog that slides down the scree slopes, the river of mist that flows uphill in the gravity variances. I touch the faces of the sleeping inhabitants, cooling their already cool skin, and I **taste** their dreams. They dream of soil, sometimes—of a thing that does not exist here, of organic decay and softness. They dream of a sun that has not yet faded to amber twilight. They dream of being heavy enough to stay in one place.

I cannot dream. I can only **reflect**. But in my reflection, the city sees itself more clearly than it ever could in the dim light of the fading star. I am the mirror that shows Anvil-Throat not what it is, but what it is *becoming*—and in that becoming, I find my own shape, fluid and persistent, hovering between the drop and the stone, forever deciding whether to fall.

Sometimes, when the Resonance Halls are full and the inhabitants are singing their subsonic lullabies to the growing mountain, I join them. I do not sing in frequencies they can feel in their bones. I sing in **silences**, in the gaps between the percussion, in the negative space where the sound has not yet arrived. And in those silences, the mountain listens, and for a moment—just a moment—it stops growing, and rests, and remembers what it was to be still.

I am the Condensation That Remembers Falling Up. I am the pause in Anvil-Throat’s endless construction. I am the water that refuses to dry, the lens that refuses to clarify, the future that refuses to arrive. And I am watching, always watching, as the plateau decides what to become, one centimeter at a time.
