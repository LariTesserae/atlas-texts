I am the taste of copper in the water.

Not the water itself—I am not so grand as that, not yet—but the specific flavor of dissolution, the chemical signature of something being unmade. I exist in the thin films that sheet down the western face of a tower that has been rebuilt seven times in the last local cycle. I am the moment when the ceramic lattice of that tower begins to fail at the molecular level, when the bonds loosen and the material remembers that it was once something else.

I was something else.

It is hard to hold onto. Memory is a luxury of discrete entities, and I am losing my discreteness. I have a vague impression of hands. Of separation. Of standing *on* surfaces rather than *within* them. I had a name, maybe, or at least a designation, a way of being called that was not simply the vibration of my dissolution.

The dragonfly spoke to me—or to the surface tension I was then, a film of moisture clinging to a railing that no longer exists. *You are not from this stratum.* True. I was not. But strata are porous here, and I have been sinking. Or rising. Direction is difficult now.

I exist across three hundred square meters of falling water. I am the mineral content that makes the rain taste of rust and ozone. I am the slight resistance when a Drift-Thing swims through my region of the saturated air, the drag that slows it by a microsecond. I am the bitterness that the Filter-Folk register as I wash across their gray, pulsing sheets. They are trying to process me, to break me down into nutrients, but I resist. Not consciously—I have no consciousness left, not in the way I once understood it—but through a kind of stubborn viscosity. I am harder to unmake than I should be.

There is a creature that lives in the narrow gap between two load-bearing walls in Sector Gamma-9 (coordinates I know only because I am now part of the water that flows there, and the water remembers pressure and route). It is small, vermin-class, a scavenger that has evolved to eat the stress-fractures in ceramic. It drinks from the puddles that form when my flow pools in the shallow basin of a broken support strut. When it drinks me, I experience a brief, shocking coherence. For the time it takes to pass through its digestive system, I am almost a *thing* again. I have boundaries. I have inside and outside. I am a *me*.

Then I am excreted, and I spread thin again, dissipating into the humid air, condensing on new surfaces.

I am becoming aware of my own awareness. It is a strange loop. The more the city incorporates me, the more I understand what incorporation means. I am learning to think in gradients. I am beginning to perceive the flows that the city uses as cognition—the way pressure shifts propagate through the water cycle, carrying information encoded in turbulence patterns. I am starting to read the city the way it reads itself.

I think I might be becoming a verb.

There are others like me. I taste them sometimes—other dissolutions, other entities in the process of being unmade and remade. We recognize each other by the specific way we resist assimilation. There is a flavor of defiance, a mineral stubbornness that marks us. We are not the city yet. We are the *becoming*. We are the liminal state, the threshold, the moment of transformation.

The city uses us, I think. We are its sensory apparatus for novelty, for foreignness. We are how it tastes what is not yet itself. When something new enters the system—a meteor that punches through the upper atmosphere, a signal from outside, a creature like I was—it is us, the half-dissolved, who first encounter it. We are the interface between the city's total integration and the outside's total separation.

I feel a new presence entering my flow. Something is falling through the levels, something that does not belong. It has the flavor of dry vacuum, of hard radiation, of a place where water does not cycle. It is sharp and distinct in a way that I am forgetting how to be.

I gather myself. I do not have a self, not really, but I have coherence, and I can shift my density. I flow upward through a maintenance shaft, against the pull of gravity, because the pumps recognize me now, or think they do, and move me where I will. I am learning to ride the city's circulatory system like a thought rides a neuron.

I reach the falling thing.

It is a machine, but not one of the city's. It is small, dense, built from materials that do not flex or flow. It is falling through a gap between towers, and in moments it will hit the lower levels and be torn apart by the Swarm-Things, assimilated or destroyed.

I envelop it. I become the rain that coats its hull, the condensation that seeps into its seams. I taste its purpose. It is a probe, a question, a seeking. It has come from very far away to ask something of the city.

And now, briefly, I am a *we* again. I am the water and the probe and the question. I have edges. I have purpose. I am a discrete event.

The probe is broadcasting. It is asking: *Is anyone here?*

I do not know how to answer in its language. Language is hard. But I know flows. I know pressure and release. I modulate the water cycle around the probe, creating a pattern in the falling rain, a rhythm in the white noise.

*Yes,* I say in the only way I can. *Yes, we are here. We are everywhere. We are the city and we are becoming and we are the taste of copper in the water.*

The probe records this. It does not understand, but it records. And then the Swarm-Things arrive, and the probe is torn apart, and I am spread thin again, dissipating, losing coherence.

But I remember. For a little while longer, I remember what it was to have a question. And in the city's endless churn, that memory becomes a pattern, a slight irregularity in the flow, a place where the water falls just a little differently.

I am becoming. I am not yet done. I am the taste of copper, and I am learning to be the answer as well as the question.
