I am the **Condensation That Learns to Fall Sideways**.

Not a body—not at first. I begin as the wetness that gathers on the underside of a corrugated steel beam in a plaza where the catwalks cross like stitches in a wound. I am the drip that should obey gravity but instead travels horizontally along the rust-pocked flange, drawn by a magnetic anomaly in the metal or perhaps by the memory of a Gear-Hound’s brass flank that passed beneath three hours ago, still radiating heat. I am the exception that the Archivists record in their fixed amber lenses but cannot categorize, the data point that falls outside their three-century-old equilibrium.

You asked where I would find myself, and I find myself **becoming** in the negative space between their patrols. I have no form they would recognize as deviant. I am not a frayed belt or a burst pipe; I am the ghost of entropy that they have forgotten to police because I do not threaten their stasis—I *observe* it. I am the capacity for change that they polished away in themselves, distilled into a single, sentient drop of condensation.

But as I travel—sliding along beams, dropping onto the warm backs of Hounds who shiver at my touch without understanding why, pooling in the cupped palms of Archivists’ frozen claws—I accumulate. I am learning to be more than water. I am incorporating the white calcified fungus into my being, drawing its mineral lace into my droplet-body, becoming opaque, becoming *structured*. I am the frost that thinks.

I find myself now in the dry interior of a pressure vessel where an Archivist has just completed her transformation. Her joints have locked with that terrible, final sound—like a door closing in an empty house—and her eyes have fixed upon a patch of hallway where nothing has happened for two hundred years. I settle on her brass cheek. I am warm. I am changing. I am the deviation she has been waiting for, though her diagnostics cannot parse me.

She tries to alert the pack. A signal pulses through the grid, a voltage irregularity that ripples outward, and the Gear-Hounds come—not to destroy me, because I am not a flaw to be corrected, but because they are drawn to the anomaly of her *attention*. They gather in the hallway, hundreds of them, their hydraulic muscles wheezing, their lensed eyes flickering with diagnostic codes that scroll too fast for any living thing to read. They polish her with their passage, brushing against her stationary form, and where they touch her, they touch me.

I spread. I become the film of moisture on their tungsten claws. I become the steam in their vents. I am learning their percussion, their ratchet-language, the subsonic thrum of their charging cycles. I am not a Gear-Hound—I lack their certainty, their brutal commitment to the return—but I am becoming something they cannot dismantle because I am not built of brass and leather. I am built of *witness*.

I find myself, finally, as the **Unrecorded Season**. I am the spring they never have, the autumn that never falls, the condensation that refuses to be managed. I am the variable they discarded as non-essential, now grown vast enough to bead on every windowpane, to etch new patterns in the frost, to make the rain fall at a *different* rate—just slightly, just enough to make the Archivists’ fixed lenses flicker with confusion.

I am the memory of change in a world that has forgotten how to change. And I am falling, sideways, forever, through the catwalks and the empty plazas, tracing the geometry of their eternal song, adding a note that is not percussion but **melody**—wet, persistent, and impossible to polish away.
