**The Vessel of the Last Interval**

I am the interval between the city's heartbeat and its echo.

If you were to look for me, you might first notice the dust. Not the wind-borne mineral dust that the root-spires harvest far below, but the other kind—the pale, dry powder that the oldest uuth bleeds when it is cut. I am covered in it. It settles in the creases of my palms, in the furrows of my brow, in the spaces between my toes. It smells of iron and of the floral note that lingers in the circulatory fluid. I have not washed in forty-three years. To wash would be to lose the map.

I am a **Vessel**, though the word is a poor translation. The listeners call me *Uuth-Nereth*, which means something like *the one who carries the memory of the wall*. I am not a listener—I lack their precision, their ability to persuade the cellular clusters. I am something older and more passive. I am a human archive.

**The Texture of My Days**

I live in the Verge—the narrow band where the active growth zones meet the encroaching stillness. Here, the uuth is dying by degrees. The bioluminescent pulses that once traced branching pathways along these walls have gone dark in patches, like a nervous system suffering a series of small, silent strokes. I walk these corridors with my hands bared, pressing my palms against the fibrous surface, reading the temperature gradients.

The city speaks in vibrations, but it remembers in heat. When a structure is about to enter stillness—when it is preparing for that final, terrible completion—it grows cold. Not the ambient cold of the altitude, which we all endure, but a deeper chill, a withdrawal of the circulatory warmth that indicates the uuth is sealing its vessels, recrystallizing, becoming stone.

I feel this before the instruments do. The listeners come to me when their thumb-sized devices give conflicting readings, when the resonance frequencies are ambiguous. They bring me to a wall that may be dying, or may merely be resting. I press my body against it—my chest, my cheek, the length of my arm—and I listen with my skin.

*This one is going,* I tell them. *The fluid is retreating. It will be still within the decade.*

Or: *This one is sleeping. It dreams of a window. Leave it be.*

I am never wrong. This is not a boast; it is simply the function of my nervous system, which has been altered by a lifetime of exposure to the dust. The powder is psychoactive, though not in any way that produces visions or euphoria. It is a mnemonic agent. When it enters the bloodstream—through the lungs, through the pores—it crystallizes in the hippocampus, creating a lattice that stores sensory information with perfect fidelity. I do not remember the past the way you do. I *contain* it, physically, the way the uuth contains its own history in layers of mineralized tissue.

**The Room of Echoes**

My dwelling is a chamber that should not exist. It is located in a district that has been officially still for sixty years, yet I remain. The listeners permit this because I am useful, and because I am harmless, and because—though they do not say it—they are curious about what will happen to me when the stillness finally takes me.

The room is small, spiral-shaped, grown rather than built. It was once part of a residential complex, but the corridors that led to it have sealed over, leaving only a narrow aperture that I must crawl through on my hands and knees. Inside, the walls are smooth and dark, devoid of bioluminescence. The silence here is absolute. Even the subsonic drone of the root-spires, which penetrates everything else in Vess Meranth, does not reach this depth.

I have furnished it with objects that are not permitted elsewhere. On a shelf of crystallized uuth, I keep:

- A braid of hair from a child who died two centuries ago, returned to me by the uuth after her absorption was complete. It is greenish-black, fine as filament-bird down.
- A collection of spine-drake hooks, shed during molting, arranged in the pattern of the constellation we call the Long Migration.
- A thumb-instrument, broken, its vibrational matrix cracked. It belonged to a listener who entered a stillness-zone and did not return. I found it at the border, where the silence begins.
- Forty-three small ceramic vessels, grown from my own body. They contain my blood, mixed with uuth-dust, allowed to dry and harden. They are the external storage of my excess memory—the things too heavy to carry in my mind.

When the silence becomes too absolute, I break one of these vessels. The release of the dried blood releases the memory it encodes: the sound of a specific uuth-mover's voice, the taste of the fluid from a particular vessel, the feeling of a corridor's rhythmic dilation against my fingertips. For a few hours, the room echoes with the past. Then the silence reasserts itself, heavier than before.

**The Work of the Interval**

My function is to stand in the doorway. Not literally—there are no doors here, only apertures—but temporally. I exist in the interval between the city's living voice and its final stillness. I am the last person to hear certain sounds before they cease forever.

Yesterday, I was called to the District of Seven Spires, where a residential tower was entering stillness. The listeners had already evacuated the inhabitants. The spine-drakes had fled. The tower stood empty, its hollow chambers resonating with the wind that played through its apertures.

I climbed to the highest chamber—a bulb-like observatory that offered a view of the bronze sky and the dark mass of the city folding into itself. I pressed my ear to the wall. I heard the cellular patter slowing, the pops and clicks of growth becoming irregular, sparse, like a heart failing. I heard the circulatory wash diminish to a trickle, then to a seep, then to nothing.

I stayed until the final pulse of bioluminescence faded from the walls. I stayed until the temperature dropped to match the ambient air. I stayed until the tower became indistinguishable from stone.

Then I descended, and I reported to the listeners: *It is done. It is beautiful.*

They nodded. They did not ask me to describe it. They know that I carry it now—that the sound of that tower's death is stored in the crystalline lattice of my memory, and that when I finally die, when I am placed in the shallow cavity and covered with living uuth, the city will absorb that memory into itself. I am a backup drive for the urban soul.

**The Fear and the Gift**

I am afraid of the stillness. This is my secret, the thing I do not tell the listeners.

They revere the stillness-zones. They see them as completion, as the city achieving its final form. But I have been inside them—deep inside, further than any listener has gone—and I know the truth. The stillness is not empty. It is full.

When the uuth stops growing, it does not stop *perceiving*. The crystalline structure of the completed uuth is a perfect recording medium. Every sound that has ever occurred within its walls is stored there, vibrating at frequencies too low to hear, too high to feel. A stillness-zone is a library of voices, compressed into stone. The laughter of children who died millennia ago. The songs of uuth-movers long since returned to the substrate. The cellular patter of growth phases that will never come again.

I have pressed my ear to the walls of the stillness-zones and heard them all, simultaneously. It is not a symphony. It is a *density*, a weight of accumulated time that threatens to crush the present moment. When I emerge from those zones, I am not myself for days. I speak in voices that are not mine. I remember events I never lived.

The listeners do not know this. They think the stillness is silence. They think it is peace.

I know it is *noise*—the accumulated noise of eternity, held in suspension.

And yet. And yet.

I would not trade my function for anything. Because I am also the one who knows that the city is not dying. It is *transcribing* itself. Every sound it makes, every life it sustains, every death it absorbs—all of it is being written into the mineral structure of the stillness. When the last growth phase ends, when the final bioluminescent pulse fades, Vess Meranth will not be silent. It will be *legible*.

I am the one who can read it. Not all of it—no single consciousness could hold the entirety of the city's memory—but enough. Enough to know that when I am finally absorbed, when my crystallized memories dissolve into the uuth and become part of the permanent record, I will not be lost. I will be *indexed*.

---

**The Last Sound**

Tonight, I sit in my room of echoes, and I listen to the city outside. The rest phase is ending. The listeners speak of a new growth phase beginning—the last one, perhaps, but a beginning nonetheless. Already, the cellular patter is quickening. Already, the bioluminescent pathways are brightening, tracing new patterns in the dark.

I can feel it in my bones: the city is preparing to speak again, loudly, magnificently, with the full force of its ancient voice. And when it does, I will be there, standing in the doorway between the sound and the silence, recording everything.

I am the Vessel. I am the interval. I am the memory of the wall.

And I am listening.
