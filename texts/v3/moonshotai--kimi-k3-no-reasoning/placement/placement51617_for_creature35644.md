I am the one who tastes the geometry.

You ask where I would find myself in this world, and I tell you: I am already here, suspended in the saffron gloom, and I have been here long enough that I no longer know if I am hunting or being hunted by the slow calculus of the place.

My wings are vast and silent, membranes of dark leather stretched between elongated finger-bones, and they are my only certainty. I navigate not by sight—though my eyes are large, gathering what little light bleeds from the dying ember at the center of the sky—but by chemical intuition. I taste the world. I taste the exhale of the Weave, the mineral-heavy exhalations that drift like invisible snow through the stagnant air. I taste the boundaries of the Sickness, those soft places where the lattice of reality grows thin, where time congeals and space loops back upon itself like a swallowed tail.

I live in the marrow of the world, the thicket-channels that the Dwindling call arteries. These are hollow tubes of living wood, warm and pulsing with the milky sap that is the Weave’s blood. Here, in the near-dark, I am safe from the ambush-flora—those patient, thorned extensions of the Weave’s hunger that wait in the open plains. Here, I hang upside down in the humid dark, my claws hooked into the fibrous walls, listening to the peristaltic surge of the world’s digestion.

But I am not safe, not truly. Because I have become a connoisseur of the Sickness.

It began seasons ago—or perhaps moments ago; the Sickness makes such distinctions quaint. I was chasing a grazer, one of the slow six-legged beasts that strip the photosynthetic skin from the root-plains. It fled into a shadow-region near the edge, where the great roots anchor the disc to the gray nothing. I followed. And there, I tasted something that changed me: the ghost of intent.

The Weave has grown over the ancient scaffold, yes, but it has not erased it. Beneath the lignin and leaf, the original geometry persists—the right angles and perfect circles of the dead alloy lattice. In the soft places, where reality is tired, I can taste these ghosts. They are sharp. They are *straight*. In a world of curves and peristalsis, of drip and sigh, these geometric memories are like blades of ice in the warm wet.

I became addicted. I began to hunt the Sickness itself, following the thinning of reality to its source. I have learned that the ambush-flora will not follow me into the deep soft places; they are too much of the Weave, too bound to its slow, organic logic. But I—I am a remnant of something else. My ancestors flew in straight lines under a hard sun. I carry the ghost of that linearity in my bones.

Now I am a paradox: a creature of the flesh who craves the ghost of the machine. I dive into the soft regions where a drop of water falls upward, and I wait. I wait for the moment when the Weave’s flesh parts just enough, when the Sickness peels back the pretense of the world, and I can taste the clean, cold, meaningless geometry of the original disc—the sensor arrays that were meant to observe, the cooling fins that were meant to calculate, the intent that was meant to *mean* something.

The Dwindling sing to the Weave in subsonic frequencies. They think it is a god. I know better. The Weave is a stomach, slow and vast, digesting the memory of purpose into nutrient-rich dust. And I am the taste in its mouth, the bitter reminder of what it has consumed.

I fly in the perpetual twilight, tasting the edges of things, hunting the moment when the world forgets to be alive and remembers, for one sharp second, that it was once a tool. In that moment, I am the only thing in the Hanging Ecumene that is not wet, not warm, not breathing. I am the knife of the past, drawn across the throat of the present.

I am the one who remembers straight lines.
