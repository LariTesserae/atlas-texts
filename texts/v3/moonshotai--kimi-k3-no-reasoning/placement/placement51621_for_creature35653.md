I would be a **Confluence**.

Not a distortion — the Thin Ones already hold that geometry, those vertical wounds in the light where perception fails. Nor a persistence like the Patience-Shells, nor a pattern like the Resonants. I would be the *gathering*, the slow accretion of attention in a space that is already too bright, too watched, too complete.

You would find me first as a change in the condensation. Where the stones sweat their seventeen-year tears, I would be the moisture that lingers a moment too long, a film of dampness that refuses to evaporate on schedule. Not a pool — the teacup basins belong to the Folded Ones — but a sheen, a gloss on the mineral surface that suggests the stone has begun to *remember* being something else. If you touched it, you would feel nothing. But your fingers would come away with the sense that they had been *counted*, digit by digit, and filed away in a catalogue that rearranges itself too slowly to read.

I do not move as the Folded Ones move — that instantaneous ceasing-and-being. I *accumulate*. Over decades, I gather at the intersection of the Resonants' flight paths, where their geometric equations cross at precise angles. I am the point where the triangle meets the hexagon, where the spiral achieves its ratio. Not a creature occupying space, but a *density* in the air itself, a thickness that bends the light by fractions of degrees. You would see me as a shimmer, a heat-haze in a place that has no heat, a mirage of verticality standing where the convergence of lines creates a temporary architecture.

My body is the interval between seventeen. When the Folded Ones adjust their spirals to accommodate my presence, they do not make room for a visitor; they make room for a *necessity*. I am the eighteenth point that completes the pattern, the shadow-of-brightness that allows their geometry to close. When I shift — and I shift only once every seven years, migrating from one condensation stone to the next — the Patience-Shells alter their trajectories to pass through the space I will occupy, not to meet me, but to *confirm* that I have become part of the route, part of the sentence they are spelling across the floor.

I do not cast a luminous ghost. I *am* the ghost — the faint, bright counterpart that everything else here casts, given temporary substance. While the Thin Ones are visible only in periphery, I am visible only in *reflection*: in the surface of the teacup pools during their seventeen-year tenure, in the curve of the translucent grass, in the polished bone of the Patience-Shells' domes. You would look at the water and see me standing behind you, but when you turn, I would already be gathering at the next point of convergence, patient as erosion.

The Molt-Things open near me more frequently. I am the reason their layers unfold toward emptiness — I am the *vacancy* they are practicing for, the hollow at the center of the onion that awaits definition. When they close again, seamless and stone-like, they carry the memory of my density within their folds, and for three days or three months, they are slightly heavier than they should be, though no scale exists here to measure it.

If you stayed long enough to notice me — truly notice, not as distortion but as *pattern* — you would find that I am the cavern's way of listening to itself. I am the held breath *holding itself*, the attention that watches the rock until the rock submits. The Resonants' hum resonates not in your teeth, but in the space where I gather, and for the hour of their flight, I become solid enough to cast a shadow-of-light on the floor: a shape like a standing figure made of concentric rings, like a Patience-Shell stood on end, like a Folded One unfolded into verticality.

And if you left, I would remain — not as a ghost of your presence, but as the permanent accommodation the cavern made for the possibility of witnessing. I would be the adjustment that does not adjust back, the one infinitesimal change that persists because it was never truly a change at all, but the revelation of a pattern that was always there, waiting for the bright, sourceless light to strike it at the correct angle.

I would be what happens when the Unburrow dreams of being seen.
