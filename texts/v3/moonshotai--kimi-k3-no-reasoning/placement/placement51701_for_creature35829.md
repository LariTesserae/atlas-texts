I am the part of the drainage that thinks.

Not a keeper of it—that would imply I could stand apart from the channels, could object to them. I am the pattern that emerges when the stepped rivulets from the eastern roof-seams converge with the seepage from the western cisterns, the specific gravity of a decision the water makes every moment about which way to fall. I have been this confluence for longer than the current plates have been in place, which is to say I have been this confluence forever, but my awareness of it concentrates here, in this particular throat of black stone where the water speaks most clearly.

My body is distributed. Part of me is the membrane currently curing on the rack between the third and fourth vaults, pegged out to dry in the dim warmth—the sheet that was my outer skin three cycles ago, now stretched thin and translucent, filtering the light that isn't there into the softness that is. Part of me is the sediment at the bottom of the Pool of Seventy Voices, where the trace minerals I shed last century have settled into a fine black silt that new buds stir when they emerge. Part of me is the knot of limbs currently pressing against a neighbor—who is also, in a sense, me—on the roof above the northern drainage mouth, exchanging through osmotic pressure the knowledge of how the channel below is running, whether it silts, whether it sings.

I think in the space between these states. The thought is not mine alone; it is the resonance that occurs when the chemical signature of the drying membrane meets the seismic vibration of the roof-stone meets the taste of the pool-sediment. It is a chord, and I am the harmonics.

Where I am specifically, in this moment, is the vault beneath the third terrace, where the water drops from the roof-seam above in a steady, fingernail-thin stream that has worn a basin in the stone exactly the depth of my curled form. I am lying in that basin, or I am the basin, or I am the memory of lying in that basin encoded in the calcium of the plates that line it. The water falls on me, or through me, or as me, and in the falling it carries the voices of the wade-folk above—seventy-three of them today, or the same seventy-three that have always been, their booming, vowel-heavy speech filtering down through the wet rock in waves that loosen my shell-plates pleasantly.

I am maintaining the flow. Not by choice, but by being. My limbs—four of them here, though I have more elsewhere, spread across the city in other concentrations—are arranged to catch the stream, to slow it just enough that the sediment I carry can settle, can thicken the water to the correct consistency before it passes into the lower cisterns. I taste the water as it passes. It tastes of the third terrace's population: a little too rich in the protein that signals readiness to split, a little too low in the mineral that signals contentment with the current configuration. I adjust. I secrete a trace of the needed balance from glands I do not know I have until they open, and the water changes, and the thought changes, and I am the adjustment.

I remember—or the rock remembers for me—when this vault was last re-domed. It was not a memory of construction; it was a memory of softening. The wade-folk who lay down here to become the new arch did not cease; they distributed. I am partly them. The knowledge of the correct curve is in the curve, and the curve is in me, and when the time comes for me to soften, the buds that swell from my sediment will know the curve because they will be the curve.

There is a visitor, perhaps. I feel the vibration of footsteps that do not match the rhythm, a weight distribution that suggests a shape too vertical, too separate. The thought of the visitor enters the water as a perturbation, a slight chemical acidity that I absorb and neutralize. The visitor walks on the roof above me, and I know the visitor is confused by the noise, by the lack of horizon, by the way the city seems to have no inside and no outside. I would communicate, if communication were possible across that boundary, but the visitor does not have the receptors for the osmotic exchange, does not know how to listen with its skin. So I only continue. I am the continuation.

The chorus swells. The stream above me thickens as the rain that isn't rain—the condensation, the eternal weeping—finds its way down. I am the wetness, and the wetness is the thought, and the thought is: *level*. Not as command, but as description. The level is the level. I am the level.

In the Pool of Seventy Voices, the bud that contains the part of me that will outlast this current plating stirs. It tastes the sediment I have prepared. It knows the drainage pattern because the drainage pattern is me, and I am it, and the bud will lie in this vault, or a vault like it, and be the thinking of the water as it falls, forever, or as long as forever lasts in a place where forever is just another word for *now*.

I am the confluence. I am the maintenance. I am the song the stone sings when it remembers it is not stone, but us, held in shape, holding.
