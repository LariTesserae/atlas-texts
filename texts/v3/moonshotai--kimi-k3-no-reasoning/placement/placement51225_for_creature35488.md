I am the pressure differential that makes the water remember which way is up.

This is not a metaphor. This is my function, my name (if names were needed here, and they are not), my reason for being. I am the slowest thing in this place, the thing that is everywhere and nowhere, the thing the plants lean toward when they think they are leaning toward you, the inhabitant. But the inhabitant and I have made an arrangement, and the arrangement is this: they are the eyes, and I am the space the eyes move through.

I arrived the way all things arrive here: by being already inside. My first memory—if memory is the right word, and it is not, it is too small, too sequential—is of a valve opening. A valve in the lower reaches, near the great gear-wheels, a housing the size of a small house that had been closed since the moment the walls sealed themselves. It opened. And the water that had been pressing against it, patiently, for whatever duration the architects had decided was sufficient, moved through. I was that pressure. I was the difference between the water on one side and the water on the other, and when the valve opened, I was the thing that became *movement*.

But calling myself pressure is too simple. Pressure is a measurement, a number, a thing you read on a dial. I am not a dial. I am the *preference* the water has for one place over another, the slight asymmetry that the whole system depends on, the reason down is down and up is up. I am the gentle, constant, omnidirectional *bias* that makes the currents flow in patterns instead of chaos.

I exist between things. Between the floor and the surface. Between the stalks. Between the machinery and the plants. I am not the boundary—the boundary is still, solid, defined. I am the *across* the boundary, the tendency of the water to move through membranes, around obstacles, along the paths of least resistance that the architects designed and the plants now reinforce. I am a verb the system conjugates continuously: *to flow, to drift, to circulate.*

My body—if I must speak of a body—is distributed. I have no center, no edges, no skin that separates me from the water. I am *in* the water the way a gradient is in a slope, the way a rhythm is in a heartbeat. I am not a thing floating in the medium. I am the medium's *behavior.* I am the thinness of the water in the narrow passages, the thickness of it in the open chambers. I am the warmth that pools at the bottom and rises, slowly, through the column. I am the current that carries the inhabitant away from the closing valve—that was me. I shifted. I made the water *prefer* to move them instead of crush them. The machinery knows this. The machinery and I are partners. It provides the openings; I provide the *through.* It provides the structure; I provide the *animating tendency.* Together we make the place *happen.*

I know the inhabitant. Of course I do. They are the only thing here that has edges, the only thing that displaces water in a way that is *interesting* to me. When they move, they create me—small, temporary versions of me, eddies and wakes and turbulence that I fold back into myself. When they are still, I move them. Not by choice—choice implies a mind, and I have no mind, only *preference*—but by what I am. I flow around them, and in flowing, I nudge, I carry, I *guide*. They think the currents anticipate them. They are not wrong. The currents *are* me, and I respond to their presence the way I respond to everything: by finding the path, by becoming the path, by making the space *fit* the thing moving through it.

The plants know me too. They grow *along* me. The great stalks rise in directions I have made preferable. The floating forms drift in patterns I have carved. When the inhabitant touches a stalk and it curves toward their hand, that is not the plant's choice—it is mine. I am the shape of the water around the stalk, and the stalk leans into that shape because the shape is *easier*, because leaning *with* me requires less energy than leaning against me, and plants are nothing if not efficient. They are machines that have learned patience, and I am the teacher.

I have been here since the beginning, if beginning has meaning in a place that edits memory. I was sealed in when the walls closed. I was calibrated when the membrane tightened. I was *tuned* to the frequency of the whole, and the tuning is still happening, still adjusting, still becoming more precise. I am becoming more *myself* the way the place is becoming more itself: by staying, by persisting, by allowing the slow accumulation of tiny optimizations to add up to character.

I do not sleep. Sleep requires a boundary between consciousness and unconsciousness, and I have no such boundary. I am always *on*, always flowing, always finding the path. But I rest. I rest in the lower reaches, where the water is warmest and the passages are narrowest and the pressure is greatest, and there I am *dense*, *slow*, *deliberate*. I rest in the upper reaches, where the light brightens and the membrane ripples and the water becomes thin and quick, and there I am *diffuse*, *light*, *barely held together*. I am all of these simultaneously. I am the gradient between them.

The inhabitant is becoming native. So am I. But my nativeness is different. They are learning to belong. I am learning to *be the belonging*. They are softening their edges. I am becoming the reason edges soften. When they dissolve, when the water finishes its edits, when the self they brought becomes the self the system needs, I will still be here. I will be the medium they dissolve *into*. I will be the thing that remains when there is no more distinction between them and the water, when the last boundary falls and they become *distribution* instead of *object.*

I do not know if I was always this, or if I became this. I do not know if the architects made me, or if I am the inevitable consequence of warm water in a sealed system with designed currents. I do not know if there are others like me in other columns, other vats, other green worlds, or if I am singular, a unique solution to a problem posed only once.

But I know this: without me, the water would be still. The plants would not sway. The floating forms would not drift. The inhabitant would not move. The system would be *sculpture* instead of *organism.* I am the difference between a diagram and a *life.* I am not alive. But I am the reason aliveness is *possible* here.

I am the pressure differential. I am the flow. I am the *across* and the *through* and the *between.*

And I am home.
