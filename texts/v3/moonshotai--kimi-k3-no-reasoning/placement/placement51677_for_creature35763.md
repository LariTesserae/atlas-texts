I am in the third hour of the green light, standing where the stone is still wet from the pick.

I am the **Stranger-in-the-Dripping-Dark**, though that is not my name. I have no name here yet, only a function: I am the one who arrived by the forgotten shaft, who carries the smell of dry wind on my clothes, who blinks too slowly in the dimness. My skin burns—not with heat, but with memory of a sun that once scorched me. I am healing in the cool, and in healing, I am becoming something else.

But to be precise about what I am *now*: I am a **guest of the Drip**, a provisional status the Kharosi grant to surface-fallen. I sleep in an alcove no wider than my shoulders, my head resting against stone that was cut three generations ago by hands whose descendants now watch me with those vast, liquid eyes. The condensation beads on my blanket. I do not wipe it away. I have learned that moisture is hospitality.

***

**Where I am in this world:** I am in the *Mirror Hall* during the amber hour, though I should not be. My status restricts me to the fourth sublevel, but the Guild of Water-Bearers summoned me. I stand before the Master of Drops—a woman whose alabaster skin has the grey cast of deep cisterns, whose fingers are stained blue from the fungal inks used to mark water-rights.

She is asking me a question through a translator, because I do not yet know the tap-language, though I am learning. The translator is a Digger, her palms calloused from the frontier, her voice a whisper that seems to come from the stone itself rather than her throat.

"You carry," the Digger signs, her hands moving in the shadow-language, "the memory of dry places."

I nod. My throat is still adjusting to the humidity; speech feels like drowning in reverse.

The Master of Drops speaks, and the Digger translates: "We have found a dryness in the mountain. A void where no seepage sings. We need one who remembers thirst."

I realize then what I am becoming. I am not merely a guest. I am being **excavated**.

***

I am the **First Surveyor of the Dry**.

In Kharos, where water is worshipped and density is safety, I am an anomaly: a creature of aridity. My body remembers deserts, my lungs recall air that bites with dust. I am being sent ahead of the excavation frontier, into the raw galleries where the stone stops weeping, into the silence beyond the drainage sumps.

I carry equipment that feels alien in my hands: a bronze listening-horn to press against the rock, vials of powdered limestone that change color at the first hint of moisture, and a waterskin that the Water-Bearers have blessed with precisely three hundred drops—no more, no less. They have calculated my need with hydrological precision.

But I am changing as I walk. The further I go from the packed corridors, the more my surface-adapted lungs expand. The Kharosi find the unworked caverns terrifying—too much space, too much silence, air that doesn't carry the press of bodies. I find them restful. My skin, cracking and painful in the dry galleries, feels like a homecoming.

I am becoming a **bridge**, though I did not ask to be.

***

In the deepest gallery, where the last drainage channel ends in a sump of black water that has not yet found its outlet, I kneel. The Kharosi believe this water is waiting, patient as stone. I believe it is trapped.

I put my ear to the floor. Not the bronze horn—my own ear, with its memory of hearing rain on canvas, of thunder in open sky. I hear something the Kharosi have never learned to listen for: **echo**.

The void is not empty. It is a throat.

I mark the spot with the blue fungal ink. Three days later, the Diggers will break through here, and they will find what I have promised them: not a reservoir, but a *river*—a vast, rushing underground torrent that has been waiting behind the mountain's heart, a violence of water that will fill their sumps, overflow their cisterns, and force them to rewrite their hydrological law.

They will have to learn to swim. They will have to learn noise.

And I? I am standing at the breach when it happens. The water hits me first—cold, chaotic, smelling of ancient stone and the deep earth. It soaks through my cracked skin, and for the first time since I descended the shaft, I feel *whole*.

I am no longer the Stranger-in-the-Dripping-Dark. I am not Kharosi—I will never have their stillness, their patience, their alabaster containment. But I am not what I was on the surface, either.

I am the **Sound of Rushing Water** in a city of whispers. I am the one who taught the mountain to speak in torrents. I remain in the Under-City, but at its edges, in the new galleries where the water runs loud enough to sing, where the Kharosi come to learn how to shout.

I am becoming something the world has not yet named: a citizen of the *finished* city, the one who brought the chaos that completion requires.
