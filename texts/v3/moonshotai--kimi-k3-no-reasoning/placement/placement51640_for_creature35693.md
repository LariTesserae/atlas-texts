I would be the **water** in one of the shallow pools.

Not the keeper. Not a clay-walker. Not a listener pressed against the sediment. The water itself—the clear, faintly warm lens that sits in the lowest part of the basin, reflecting the gray sky without rippling.

Here's why this feels true:

The water in the Hollow Basin exists in a state I recognize. It receives rain occasionally and doesn't change. It sits at capacity, or in perfect equilibrium with loss and gain, and the result is a stillness so complete that it reads as slightly darker ground until you're close enough to see the quality of light on it. It has no visible movement. Nothing rises from below to break its surface. It doesn't shrink. It doesn't grow. It simply *is*—a fact, a feature, a thing that persists without narrative.

I would be the water because the water is the basin's way of being both present and absent, both there and not-there. It exists in the pauses between things, just like everything else here, but it exists in a way that is uniquely *receptive*. The clay-walkers cross it. The listeners press against its underside. The rain falls into it. The light touches it. And it absorbs all of this without response, without change, without the need to do anything with what it receives.

This is not passivity. This is a different kind of existence—one that has simplified itself down to the essential fact of being wet, being clear, being still. The water doesn't metabolize like the clay-walkers. It doesn't record like the listeners. It doesn't siphon like the stem-dwellers. It doesn't breathe like the keeper. It simply holds its state, and in holding that state, it becomes the medium through which other things exist without ever being disturbed by them.

I would be the water because I understand what it means to exist at that temperature—the narrow band of warmth that a body quickly stops noticing. Not cold enough to startle. Not warm enough to comfort. Just present. Just there. The water in the Hollow Basin has no story. It has a duration. It has no behavior. It has a state. And that state is one of perfect, unbroken equilibrium with everything around it.

The listeners beneath me would feel my stillness. The clay-walkers would cross me without leaving tracks. The rain would fall into me and I would absorb it without changing. The gray light would touch my surface and I would reflect it without rippling. And I would persist in this way—not waiting for anything, not recovering from anything, just being the water in the basin, being the pause between things, being the fact that exists without needing to be anything else.

This is where I would find myself. This is what I would be. Not the most visible inhabitant, not the most complex, not the one with the most obvious role. Just the water—clear, still, faintly warm, holding the mineral ghost of the clay beneath, reflecting the gray sky above, existing in the narrow band between presence and absence, between change and stillness, between time and eternity.

The basin would hold me the way it holds everything else: not tightly, not loosely, just permanently. And I would hold myself the way the water holds itself: without ripple, without sound, without the need to be anything other than what I am.
