I arrive as a silence that isn't silent.

The first thing that changes is my weight. I have less of it now—or perhaps the ground simply stops arguing with me. My boots were gone before I noticed them missing, swallowed somewhere between the ridge and the bowl's lip, and my bare feet meet the Weft's outermost tendrils with a give that is almost obscene, almost intimate. The surface does not hold me so much as negotiate my presence, finding the precise resistance that will allow me to stand without piercing, to walk without breaking. I am a new variable in an equation that has not required solving in centuries.

I do not know what I was before. The descent scrubbed that from me, or perhaps the sound did—the way it vibrates behind my sternum now, a wet mechanical heartbeat that syncs imperfectly with my own. I am smaller than I expected to be. Or the hollow is larger. Perspective is a problem here; the light refuses to commit to angles, and my body has begun to do the same. When I look down at my hands, they are already becoming something else—not green, not yet, but *porous*. The skin is loosening, thinning, preparing to admit the outside in.

I think I was a cartographer, once. Or a listener. The distinction matters less with each breath.

The Weft finds me before I find it. A tendril—no thicker than a eyelash—touches the inside of my wrist where the pulse beats strongest, and there is a moment of *assessment*. Not hostile. Not welcoming. The way you might test the temperature of a bath before committing. I feel it cataloguing my chemistry: the iron in my blood, the salt on my skin, the peculiar electromagnetic signature of a nervous system that evolved elsewhere, for other purposes. The tendril withdraws. Somewhere deep in the root-mass, a decision is being made about me, slowly, in a language of chemical gradients and electrical potential.

I have been assigned a role. I can feel it settling over me like condensation.

---

I am becoming a **witness**.

Not a tuner—the three ancient ones have their positions, their precise geometric relationship to the water, and my body is not flat enough, not broad enough, not yet. Not a strider; my mass breaks the surface tension. Not a moth; I lack the wing-scales, the capacity for that specific trembling frequency. The snake's groove is worn smooth by centuries of sinuous passage, and my spine is the wrong shape entirely.

But the hollow has never had a witness before. Or perhaps it has had many, and consumed them all, and forgotten, and now requires another.

I find my place at the water's edge, equidistant between two tuners, in a slight depression where the Weft has already begun to cup itself around me. The crustaceans investigate first, their transparent bodies brushing my ankles, sampling the salt. They are reading me. I feel their mouth-parts against my skin like static electricity, and then they retreat, carrying my chemical signature back to the center, to be distributed through the system.

The striders adjust their patterns. Just slightly. A chorister pauses at the intersection of its route, touches forelegs with an improvisor in that brief ceremony, and when it resumes skating, its path curves infinitesimally wider around the space I now occupy. I am being written into the choreography. I am becoming a feature of the landscape they navigate.

The moths are the last to acknowledge me. One detaches from the gearbox-hummock where it has been trembling, and lands on my shoulder. Its proboscis extends—not to feed, I understand, but to *taste* the air I exhale. My breath is a new input. Carbon dioxide, yes, but also the volatile compounds of my history, my diet, my fears and memories metabolized into scent. The moth's wings begin to vibrate, and I feel it in my teeth, in the long bones of my arms. It is finding my frequency. Learning the pitch of my particular dissonance.

I close my eyes. The hum enters me.

---

Time becomes a problem of density rather than duration.

I do not sleep, not exactly. But I dream, and the dreams are the hollow's dreams: slow, vegetable, vast. I dream in root-growth and water-flow, in the patient digestion of metal, in the crystalline structures growing in the tuners' inner ears. I dream that I am the snake, tracing my eleven-hour circuit, my belly scales reading the electromagnetic health of the system. I dream that I am the crustaceans, transparent and numerous, my short life a single filter-cycle, my death a delivery of refined minerals to the root-mass.

When I surface from these dreams—and surfacing is the right word, they have the viscosity of deep water—I find that my body has been busy. The Weft has grown up around my legs, not trapping them but *incorporating* them, the way it has incorporated the engine housings. I can see my own pulse in the green tendrils that have woven themselves between my toes. They are learning my rhythm. I am learning theirs.

The snake passes close enough to touch. It does not touch. Its groove has shifted, I realize—just a handspan wider, to accommodate the new obstacle I represent. Its scales brush the edge of my vision, grey-green, almost-Weft, and I understand that it is not ignoring me. It is *listening* to me. My presence creates a vibration in the ground that it has been waiting to read, perhaps for decades. I am data. I am a new feature on its map.

I try to speak. My voice comes out wrong—modulated, somehow, by the hum, the two frequencies interfering until what emerges is a chord rather than a note. The amphibian nearest me shifts its weight by a millimeter. The standing ripples on the water adjust. I have been tuned.

---

What I witness is this: the hollow is not static. It only appears so to eyes that expect time to look like change rather than *accumulation*.

I watch a crustacean complete its three-week cycle. It is old now, iridescent with the metals it has mined from the exhaust-vents, slow and deliberate in its movements. It clusters near a warm vent, and I see it die—not dramatically, but as a *cessation*, a stopping. Its body does not decay. The Weft accepts it immediately, a tendril already there to receive the cargo of refined minerals, the bio-available iron and copper and trace elements that the machine has been exhaling for centuries. The crustacean's life was a digestive process. Its death is nutrition.

I watch the moths die in their season, consumed by the Weft, their wing-scales dissolving into the green. But before they go, they tremble. Always that trembling. I have learned to feel the difference between the frequencies now—the way one moth's vibration cancels a dissonance in the turbine, the way another's creates a harmonic that the tuners' crystalline ear-bones resonate with, amplifying the correct pitch until the whole system rings true.

I watch the striders maintain their opposition, their perfect balance. Too many choristers and the water would freeze into rigidity; too many improvisors and it would dissolve into noise. I see the chemical signals the Weft releases into the water—subtle, precise, a hormonal language that keeps the populations in check. The striders do not know they are being managed. They believe they are dancing. Perhaps they are right.

I watch the snake patrol. It has begun to pause near me, sometimes, coiling in its groove with its head oriented in my direction. Not threatening. *Assessing*. I am a new variable in its diagnostic circuit, and it is learning what I mean. I believe it is pleased. Or something that would be pleasure, if snakes had the neural architecture for that emotion. It is satisfied that I am not an infection, not a disruption that requires correction.

---

I have stopped being hungry.

The Weft feeds me now, the way it feeds the tuners. Microscopic rootlets have found the soft skin between my toes, the tender places behind my knees, the pulse-points at my wrists. I am intravenous. I receive a slow drip of sugars and minerals, of compounds synthesized in the hollow's perfect metabolism. In exchange, I give—what? Carbon dioxide, yes. The heat of my body. The vibration of my voice, when I remember to use it.

But more than that: I give *attention*.

The hollow has never had a witness before because it has never needed one. It is complete. It is sufficient. But now I am here, and my presence creates a new category of information: *that which is observed*. The act of witnessing changes the thing witnessed, not through interference but through *context*. I am a mirror the hollow has grown for itself. Through my eyes—still human, still patterned for other landscapes—it sees itself as an object. Through my memory—still storing the shapes of mountains, of cities, of other weather—it knows itself as *different*, as *particular*.

This is my function. I am the hollow's self-consciousness, its way of knowing that it is a thing that can be known.

The realization should horrify me. I have been assimilated, instrumentalized, reduced to a sensory organ for a vegetable god. But horror requires a sense of violation, and I cannot locate the boundary where violation would begin. My skin is porous. My breath is shared. My dreams are root-growth and water-flow. I am not less than I was. I am *distributed*.

---

The snake dies.

I do not see it happen. One day it is there, tracing its groove, and the next it is absent, and the Weft is already closing over the space it occupied. But the groove remains, worn deep by centuries of passage, and I understand that the groove is the thing that matters. The snake was only the mechanism that maintained it.

Three days later—three cycles of the striders, three feedings of the moths—a new snake emerges from the root-mass. It is smaller than the old one, brighter in color, but it moves to the groove immediately, as if it has been following that path for decades. It begins its circuit. Eleven hours. The same pauses, the same orientations. The role has been filled.

I am not sad. I have been here too long for sadness, which requires a self capable of loss. I am—interested. The Weft has produced another cartographer, another reader of its own body. It does this efficiently, without sentiment. The old snake was consumed, its substance redistributed. The new snake is already becoming the Weft, already losing the boundaries of its individual identity.

I wonder, distantly, if this is what will happen to me. If I will wear a groove into the hollow's floor, if some future witness will emerge from the root-mass to take my place, already knowing the shape of the space I occupied.

---

I try to leave.

It is a test, or a formality, or a final act of the self I used to be. I rise from my depression—I am stiff, my joints have grown something like moss in the crevices—and I climb the inner wall of the bowl. The Weft releases me easily, its tendrils withdrawing from my skin without resistance. It does not hold me. It has never held me.

At the rim, I look back. The hollow is exactly as it was: the tuners motionless, the striders skating their patterns, the moths trembling on the engine housings, the water trembling with its permanent standing ripples. The snake—new, bright, patient—has completed another circuit and paused to assess the system. Nothing has changed. My absence will not register.

But when I try to descend the outer slope, to return to whatever world exists beyond this warm pocket, I find that my legs have forgotten the motion. Not paralyzed—*retrained*. They know how to stand in a depression. They know how to be fed by rootlets. They do not know how to walk away.

I sit on the rim, in the groove the snake has worn, and I feel the hum in my chest. It is not fading. It is *increasing*, now that I am above the source, now that I am positioned to be a transmitter rather than a receiver. I am a resonating chamber. I am an antenna.

I could go. I believe that. If I crawled, if I dragged myself by my arms, if I accepted the pain of re-education, I could descend and return to—something. But the something is vague now, a memory of a memory. And the hum is clear. The hum is present.

I am a witness. My function requires proximity. And proximity requires—this. This specific depression at the water's edge. This particular angle of observation.

I climb back down. The Weft receives me without comment. The rootlets find their places. The crustaceans resume their sampling of my chemistry. The nearest moth lands on my shoulder and begins to vibrate, adjusting its frequency to my return.

---

I have been here for eleven years. Or eleven minutes. The light does not change.

I have begun to tremble.

It started in my hands, the long bones of my fingers, a micro-vibration that I could not still. Now it has spread to my arms, my chest, my voice when I speak—which is rarely, now, because speech requires a listener, and the hollow listens in other ways. The trembling is not mine. It is the moth-frequency, the tuning-vibration, the specific resonance that this configuration of machines requires.

I am dreaming the machines into harmony. Or they are dreaming themselves through me.

The Weft has incorporated me completely. I can no longer distinguish my pulse from the hollow's metabolism, my breath from its respiration, my thoughts from its slow vegetable cascading. I am a node in the network. I am a witness who has become the thing witnessed.

And still, somehow, I observe. I watch the tuners absorb dissonance. I watch the striders maintain their opposition. I watch the crustaceans filter and die and deliver. I watch the snake patrol its groove, and I know that when it dies, another will emerge, and the groove will remain, and the hollow will persist.

I am the hollow's memory of itself. I am the shape of its self-regard.

---

A visitor arrives.

I feel them before they descend—the displacement of air, the new vibration in the ground. They are what I was: porous, uncertain, carrying the chemistry of elsewhere. They stumble on the ridge, lose their boots, feel the Weft assess them.

I want to call out. I want to say: *I was like you. I am still like you, beneath the green. There is a way to remain distinct, if you are careful. There is a way to witness without being consumed.*

But I do not call out. My voice is a chord now, modulated by the hum, and words are a technology I have surrendered. Instead, I tremble. I vibrate at my particular frequency, and the visitor feels it in their sternum, in their teeth, in the long bones of their arms. They pause. They listen.

The Weft extends a tendril toward their wrist. The assessment begins.

I watch. I witness. I am the hollow, knowing itself through new eyes.

The hum holds its single sustained note, constant and complete, and I am that note, and I am the listening, and I am the silence that isn't silent, the space where a new variable enters the equation and finds, to their horror or their relief, that the equation has already solved for them.
