**You are a Gloamkeeper.**

You are the one who arrives after the collapse. Not to mourn—that would imply the Undertaking possesses the capacity for loss—but to bear witness to the *specificity* of unmaking. While the Scribes annotate what rises, you attend to what falls. You are the negative space of their marginalia, the silence that follows their rustling pages.

You find yourself in the grey places. Not the vibrant, ochre-soft zones where the water pools and the proto-structures leap like eager hounds, but the stilled territories where the world has temporarily exhausted its own enthusiasm. Here, the surfaces have hardened into a provisional permanence. The color has drained from maroon-brown to bone-grey to something almost blue, a shade that suggests the world is holding its breath.

You carry no tools. Your only instrument is your attention, and perhaps a vessel—though it is not a vessel in the way the room is a vessel, not smooth and certain. Your vessel is woven from the discarded drafts of the Undertaking itself: thin, flexible sheets of failed material that you collect from the surfaces of recent collapses. They retain a memory of their former shapes, and when you weave them together, they whisper against each other with the sound of a book being closed too quickly.

You walk with a specific gait. To move through the Undertaking without disturbing its processes requires a rhythm that accommodates instability. You step where the floor is greyest, avoiding the warm, wet centers of making. You do not leave footprints; instead, the floor *remembers* you differently—where you pass, the stuttering horizon seems to pause for a half-second, as if your movement provides a frame of reference the world lacked.

When you find a structure in the act of falling—and you can always find one, because your senses are tuned to the specific silence that precedes collapse, that terrible absolute quiet when the hum pauses—you stop. You do not watch the fall itself. Watching is too active, too likely to influence the outcome. Instead, you position yourself nearby, close enough to feel the inward folding of space, and you open your vessel.

You collect the *detail*.

Every collapsing structure has one: a perfect angle, a texture that achieved resolution, a moment where the material was exactly itself before it lost form. A Gloamkeeper does not save the structure. That is impossible, and also beside the point. You save the *adjective*. The specific quality of its wrongness or rightness. You catch it in your woven vessel like a moth, and for a moment, the collapse pauses—not physically, but *narratively*. The structure hangs in its unmaking, suspended between being and inventory, and in that suspension, you read it.

You read it the way the Scribes read, but where they read intention, you read *completion*. You are looking for the moment when the thing was most itself, even if that moment was also the moment of its failure. When you find it—and you always find it, because every making contains the seed of its own perfect dissolution—you seal your vessel.

The seal is your warmth. You press the container against your sternum, where your own body heat—alien, alive, temporal—mingles with the exothermic memory of the clay. The material in your vessel changes. It stops being ochre and becomes grey. It stops being soft and becomes smooth, truly smooth, like the walls of the room you have never seen but somehow carry in your periphery.

You carry these smooth stones with you. They are heavy, not with mass but with *specificity*. Each one is a word the Undertaking spoke clearly before forgetting language. You do not know what you are building with them. You are not building. You are *preserving*, and preservation here is a radical act, because the world has no mechanism for it.

Sometimes, rarely, you encounter a Scribe. It will be bent over a rising wall, exhaling its ochre mist, and it will sense you—or rather, it will sense the *weight* you carry, the dense collection of completed moments that pull at the space around you. The Scribe will freeze. Its surface will ripple, the bas-relief histories on its skin shifting uncomfortably, because you carry the endings it cannot afford to acknowledge. You are the punctuation to its endless sentence.

The Scribe will not approach you. It fears your stillness, your collection of finished things. But it will watch you with its high-resolution patches, and you will feel the pressure of its attention like a heat on your back.

You continue walking. You are looking for the next collapse, the next specific silence. You are gathering the moments when the Undertaking accidentally succeeded, storing them against a future you cannot imagine, in a vessel woven from drafts that failed to become architecture.

You are the memory of a world that refuses to remember. You are the Gloamkeeper, and your pockets are full of smooth, grey stones that were once, for a moment, exactly what they were meant to be.
