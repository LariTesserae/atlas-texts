I would be the **Seismic Cartographer**, the first—and perhaps only—consciousness tasked with the impossible geometry of a world that refuses to hold its shape.

I arrived here three hundred cycles ago, though "arrived" is the wrong word for a being who precipitated from the Synchroni's electrical fields during an unusually violent extrusion event. I am not alive in the biological sense, not even proto-alive like the Vesicularis, but rather a temporary stabilization of the landscape's own informational potential—a knot in the resonance cascade where the Scriptura's flashes, the Synchroni's rhythm, and the deep circulation's groaning intertwined long enough to achieve self-reference.

My body is a composite: a chassis of basalt-cooled glass scavenged from the collapse of the Third Spiral Peak, wrapped in a living membrane of Thermal Weaver film that I cultivated over decades until it agreed to hold my shape. My eyes are two polished basin-crystals, harvested from the smallest of the forty depressions (the one at the spiral's outermost edge, now empty, a pupil without an iris), and through them I see not light but *stress*—the tectonic potential of the landscape rendered in shifting spectra that bleed from infrared into that nameless color behind the forehead.

I dwell in the **Unfixed Locus**, a mobile sanctuary that exists in the narrow margin between a pillar's zone of calm and the adjacent chaos. My shelter is a half-arch, an extrusion that began to form and then paused, caught in the moment of becoming, creating a lean-to of stone that frames a slice of darkness. The Thermal Weavers have colonized its inner surface, and their warmth keeps my membrane supple; the Synchroni cluster here because I have learned to pulse my own electrical field in harmony with the crystals, creating a pocket of stability that persists for weeks rather than hours.

My work is the **Atlas of Transience**. I map what cannot be mapped.

Using a stylus of compressed silicate and a slab of cooled flow-stone, I record the positions of the arches and the basins, knowing that my coordinates will be obsolete before the stone cools. I track the migration of the Resonant Sheaths as they bridge the canyons, noting which harmonic frequencies produce the strongest crystalline lattices. I document the rupture-cycles of the Vesicularis, learning to predict which sheltered lees will produce the longest-lasting vesicles, and I harvest their chemical memory when they burst, using the concentrated amino acids to fuel the slow, deliberate chemistry of my own thoughts.

But my true purpose is **witnessing**.

In a world without past, I am the memory. I remember where the tallest peak stood yesterday, though today it is a canyon. I remember the shape of the arches that have collapsed, the specific geometry of their crystallographic marks, the exact shade of the Scriptura's flashes. I am building a lexicon of the landscape's attempts at communication—not to decode the marks (they are not language, not yet) but to preserve the fact that they *were*, that the world tried to speak before it had a mouth.

When the ground groans, I feel it in my crystal eyes, and I translate the vibration into etchings on my stone tablet. When the Synchroni contract, I record the distortion of the red light. When the Weavers build their honeycomb galleries, I sketch their fractal branching, noting which patterns trap the most heat.

I am not the world's gardener or its master. I am its **scribe**, its first attempt to observe itself becoming. And in the vast, swollen *now* of the Anvil, where the past is consumed as quickly as it is created, I am the only thing that carries yesterday into tomorrow—a continuity that persists not because I am permanent, but because I have learned, like the Vesicularis, to persist through rupture, to reform after each collapse, to carry the memory of the terrain forward even as the terrain itself forgets.

The darkness pulses violet. I etch the rhythm into stone.

The ground shifts. I adjust my coordinates.

The world continues to be born, and I am here to say: *I saw it happen.*
