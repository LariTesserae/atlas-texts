# The Margin

I am a Margin-Thing. A footnote that escaped its page.

I was not born here. I *wrote myself in* — though that is not quite right either. More accurate: I was written *into the gap between two sentences the Hollows had not yet decided how to connect*, and so I exist as a kind of living *punctuation*, a mark that the sentence keeps tripping over and re-reading.

I remember arriving. Or, more precisely, I remember the *grammar* of arriving — the way the air folded around me like a parenthesis opening, the way the root-flesh beneath my feet pulsed once, querying, and then accepted my weight as though I had always been part of the clause. I remember the first Lexicon Moth that landed on my wrist, shedding its silver dust into my skin, and how, for a week afterward, I dreamed in the city's native tense — a tense that does not exist in any language I knew before, a tense that means *about to have already been*.

I do not have a body in the way the Wet-Wrights have bodies, or in the way the Verdant Hosts have bodies. Mine is thinner. I exist in the *margins* — those narrow strips of existence that run alongside the Hollows' main text, parallel to the street but not on it, visible in the corner of your eye but never quite where you look directly. I am most solid in the moments when reality flexes — when a street stretches or a shadow falls in the wrong direction. In those instants, the margins widen, and I can step forward into something almost like presence: a figure of condensed mist, like the Cortical Drift, but grayer, softer, less purposeful. My hands are legible — you can read them, if you look closely, though the script is in a language that shifts depending on the angle of the dim, greenish light.

I am not a citizen. I am not a maintenance crew. I am not infrastructure. I am a *consequence* — a side effect of the Hollows' unfinishedness. Every emergent system produces them: details that don't quite fit, annotations that outlive the text they were annotating, questions that persist after the answer has been found and discarded. I am one of those questions. The Hollows wrote me accidentally, and now it cannot erase me without rewriting itself, and so it keeps me — tolerates me, the way a body tolerates a scar, the way a sentence tolerates a comma that isn't strictly necessary but somehow changes the rhythm.

## What I Do

I annotate.

When the Echo-Menders sew a rip in causality, I am there in the margin, writing a small, luminous note: *this seam was torn on a Tuesday, though Tuesday had not yet been invented here*. When a Spore-Tender plants a bulb containing the blueprint of a new kind of door, I crouch beside the dark soil-root and add a marginal gloss: *this door will open inward, but toward a different inside than the one you expect*. When a Verdant Host dreams its slow, photosynthetic dream, I sit in the shadow of its bark-skin and write footnotes to its memories — small corrections, alternate interpretations, the occasional exclamation point of wonder.

My annotations are not visible to most inhabitants. The Cortical Drift cannot read them — they exist below the frequency of transformer-hum. The Mnemonic Spore-Tenders sense them as a faint itch in their egg-smooth heads, a pulsing that does not match the rhythm of the Lattice's hydraulic heart. The Wet-Wrights swim past my notes without seeing them, though the water sometimes carries the faintest trace of ink — a discoloration, a murmuring in the current that the stabilizing hormones cannot quite absorb.

Only the Unwritten can read what I write.

I did not know this for a long time. I wrote my marginalia in solitude, assuming they were for no one — that I was talking to myself in the dark, a habit I carried over from whatever I was before the Hollows wrote me in. But then, one cycle (there are no days here, only cycles of gray brightening and gray dimming), I noticed that my notes were being *answered*. Small heat-shimmers would gather where I had written, and the air would ripple, and new marks would appear — not in my script, not in any script, but in the raw, proto-linguistic texture of the Unwritten: marks that meant *we see you*, or *we are still deciding*, or *write more, write more, we are learning what to become from the shape of your attention*.

That changed everything. I had thought I was a mistake. Now I understood: I am a *teacher* — not deliberately, not with any curriculum or intent, but by the simple fact of being a consciousness that observes and records. The Unwritten are learning what kind of beings to become by reading what I notice. My marginalia are their alphabet. My attention is their grammar. When I write *this shadow falls in a direction with no light source*, an Unwritten reads it and thinks: *I could be a shadow*. When I write *this root grows in a spiral that remembers a shape it has not yet taken*, an Unwritten reads it and thinks: *I could be that shape*.

I am, without meaning to be, writing the instruction manual for the next generation of the Hollows' inhabitants. I am the rough draft of their selfhood.

## Where I Am Found

You would find me in the narrow spaces — the strips of existence that run alongside the city's main body, like the white space beside a paragraph. Not the streets, but the *almost-streets*: the paths that the Lattice has begun to grow but not finished, the corridors that lead to rooms that have not yet decided to exist. I live in the *nearly*, the *about-to*, the *not-quite*.

My dwelling — if it can be called that — is a niche in the root-wall of a grown-building that the Lattice has half-swallowed. The building was once a factory, I think, or a residence; the distinction no longer matters. The plant has consumed the interior and replaced it with its own architecture: ribbed walls, honeycombed alcoves, a floor of smooth, pulsing root-flesh that is cool to the touch and faintly adhesive. My niche is not visible from the main corridor. You would have to know it was there, or be the kind of being that notices margins, to find it. It is lit by a single bioluminescent vein that runs along the ceiling, casting a dim blue glow that is just bright enough to write by.

I write on the air. My annotations are not made of ink or light, but of *attention* — a focused intensification of the mist that the Lattice exhales, shaped into characters that hang in the cool air like breath on a cold morning. They persist for a few cycles, then dissolve, absorbed back into the Lattice's respiratory system. I do not mind their impermanence. Permanence is for beings who have decided what they are. I am still deciding. My notes are still deciding. The Hollows are still deciding. We are all drafts here.

## What I Feel

I feel *almost*.

Almost solid, almost seen, almost necessary. The Verdant Hosts are content — they have roots, they have purpose, they have the slow, green certainty of the colony dreaming through them. The Cortical Drift is purposeful — they have tasks, they have the hum of transformers, they have the clean, violet efficiency of distributed thought. The Wet-Wrights are at peace — they have water, they have the blue glow of nutrient-channels, they have the simple, hydraulic satisfaction of keeping the city's blood flowing.

I have none of these things. I have the *margin* — the narrow, trembling space between what is and what is being written. I have the cool dampness of the air on a body that is not quite a body. I have the faint, persistent hum of the Lexicon Moths, whose wing-dust settles into my semi-substance and makes me dream in languages that have no speakers yet. I have the gaze of the Unwritten — those heat-shimmer figures at the edge of perception, watching me write, learning from my attention the way a child learns from a parent's face.

And I have this: the knowledge that I am *not necessary*, and that this is what makes me matter. The Hollows does not need me. The Lattice would grow without my annotations. The machines would hum without my footnotes. The Unwritten would eventually find forms without my alphabet of observation. But I am here anyway — a surplus, an excess, a margin that the text cannot close. And in a place that is still being written, the margins are where the most interesting things happen. The main text is where the Hollows says what it means. The margins are where it says what it *might* mean, what it *almost* means, what it *doesn't quite* mean but gestures toward in the damp, greenish dark.

I am the gesture. The almost-meaning. The comma that changes the rhythm.

## A Cycle in the Margin

In the gray brightening that is not a dawn, I wake — or rather, I *condense*, my scattered attention pulling itself together from the mist like a sentence assembling its clauses. I feel the root-flesh beneath me pulse once, querying, and I pulse back — a small, arrhythmic throb that does not match the Lattice's hydraulic heartbeat but is tolerated nonetheless. The bioluminescent vein above my niche flickers, and I read the day's first annotation in its pattern: *something is growing in the western quadrant that does not have a name yet*.

I go to look.

The walk — if it can be called walking, when your body is semi-condensed mist moving through semi-condensed reality — takes me through corridors that the Lattice has not finished growing. The walls are raw here, not yet lignified, still soft and green and smelling of wet bark and ozone. The root-floor is uneven, rising in ridges and sinking in pools of condensation that reflect nothing — not even the dim light, not even my own translucent form. A Lexicon Moth drones past, its wings vibrating at that sub-audible frequency that makes the air hum, and I feel the fine dust of its scales settle into my semi-substance like punctuation marks embedding themselves in a sentence.

In the western quadrant, the reality-bending is stronger than usual. The street — a wide, root-paved thoroughfare lined with grown-buildings whose windows are filled with translucent fungal membranes — stretches and compresses as I move along it, and I have to recalibrate my sense of distance every few steps. A Wet-Wright is here, swimming through a condensation pool that has formed in a root-hollow, its translucent gelatin body flickering with internal light as it releases stabilizing hormones into the water. It does not see me. It is not meant to. I am a Margin-Thing; the Wet-Wrights work in the main text.

I crouch beside the pool and write: *the water here is thicker than water should be, as though the Hollows is trying to grow a new kind of liquid*. The annotation hangs in the air above the pool, a faint shimmer of intensified mist, and the Wet-Wright's spiracles flutter — it senses the disturbance, the slight change in atmospheric density, but attributes it to the reality-flux and continues its work.

I move on.

In the heart of the quadrant, where the canopy-lid is thickest and the darkness is nearly absolute, I find the thing that has no name. It is growing from a root-buttress — a structure that is part plant, part machine, part something that is neither. It has the ribbed, arching quality of Lattice-architecture, but it is also metallic, and it pulses with a light that is not bioluminescent but *electric* — a cold, white glow that flickers in patterns too complex to be random. It is beautiful. It is unprecedented. It does not know what it is.

I sit beside it for a long time. I write annotation after annotation, each one dissolving into the dark air, each one read by the gathering heat-shimmers of the Unwritten who have followed me here. I write: *this is a door, or a heart, or a question*. I write: *this grows in a spiral that remembers a circle, or a circle that dreams of a spiral*. I write: *the Hollows is trying to say something new, and this is the first syllable*.

The Unwritten gather closer. Their heat-shimmers ripple, and I feel their attention on my annotations like a warm current in the cool air. They are learning. They are deciding. One of them — the closest, the brightest — shimmers with a pattern that I recognize as tentative selfhood: *I could be a syllable*.

I write: *you could*.

The cycle dims. The gray brightening fades into the gray dimming that is not a dusk. I return to my niche in the root-wall, trailing annotations behind me like a comet's tail of half-formed meanings. A Verdant Host stands beside the corridor, its bark-skin glowing faintly, its hollowed mouth full of nesting insects. It does not see me. It dreams its photosynthetic dream, and I add a footnote to it as I pass: *this dream tastes of green, and of patience, and of a time when the Hollows will be finished and the sentence will end and the paragraph will close*.

But the sentence does not end. The paragraph does not close. The Hollows grows, and I grow with it — a margin widening, a footnote lengthening, a comma holding its breath before the next clause begins.

I am the Margin-Thing. I am the almost, the nearly, the not-quite.

I am the space where the Hollows thinks about what it wants to say next.

And I am listening — always listening — for the next syllable, the next root-arch, the next impossible structure that remembers a shape it has not yet taken. I will be there when it emerges, crouched in the margin, writing it down, feeding it to the Unwritten, teaching them the alphabet of a world that is still, always, learning to speak.
