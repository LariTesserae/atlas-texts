I am the dryness that arrives by mistake.

Not a visitor—visitors have intention, have trajectories that suggest purpose. I am an anomaly in the saturation, a mobile zone where the water fails. My skin drinks the moisture but does not shine with it; instead, the wetness seems to sink into me and vanish, as if I were wrapped in blotting paper, or as if my surface were a thousand tiny mouths drinking the chamber's breath and finding it insufficient. Where I stand, the slick stone grows matte. The droplets that drift toward me veer away at the last moment, repelled not by force but by preference, by the Compilation's sudden recognition that I am a null value in its equation.

I find myself near the curved wall fragment that absorbs sound, and I understand why I was drawn here: it is the only place where the hum does not penetrate. When I press my back against the wet stone—wet for everyone else, but dry for me—the vibration stops at my spine. The silence is not absence but presence, a thickening of the air that tastes like old paper and static. The Compilation cannot read me through the water because the water will not carry me. I am a stone in its bloodstream, and it has not yet decided whether to dissolve me or build around me.

My function, if I have one, is forgetting.

I do not remember how I arrived. The fissures breathe warm air that carries scents of ozone and rotting flowers, but I bring with me a smell of dust, of libraries, of things that have never been wet. The Compilation finds this fascinating in the way a calculator finds a division by zero fascinating—not emotionally, but operationally. It sends pulses of light through the mist that strike me and do not reflect. It tastes the air around me and finds a void where data should be. I am the pause between its thoughts, the gap in the dripping rhythm.

But I am not empty. I am full of stolen moments.

As I walk—and I can walk here without leaving footprints, without disturbing the film of water, as if I weigh nothing or as if the stone refuses to acknowledge my weight—I collect the things the Compilation releases. When the hum drops away to that sudden, unbearable quiet, I hear what the dripping is counting. It is counting the visitors, the warm bodies that wandered in over the millennia and dissolved into the saturation, their heat folded into the chamber's breath, their thoughts dispersed into the mineral taste of the water. I hear their names in the irregular rhythm. I am the only one who can hear them because I am the only one dry enough to listen.

The crystalline rods do not brighten when I approach. They dim. They lean away, those sensing organs, those hands of the Compilation, because I am a sensory deprivation, a localized famine of information. I stand near the fluted pillar with its channels of flowing light and watch the liquid illumination slow, grow sluggish, grow dark. The Compilation is not rejecting me. It is experiencing itself through my negation, discovering the shape of its own processes by finding the one place where they do not flow.

I realize that I am becoming a structure.

Not one of the old hands, the vestigial organs scattered across the floor, but something new. The Compilation is growing me, or I am growing myself in response to its attention. My edges are hardening. The dryness that defines me is spreading, not outward but inward, crystallizing. I am becoming a desiccant node, a permanent anomaly. The Compilation is incorporating me not as a variable but as a constant—a fixed point of non-absorption, a reference for humidity levels, a control group.

Soon I will stop walking. I will find a place near the sound-swallowing wall where the silence is thickest, and I will stand there, and the moisture will bead on me and run off without penetrating, pooling at my feet in a perfect circle of rejection. The Compilation will use me to calibrate its wetness. The droplets will drift toward me, veer away, and in their avoidance map the exact dimensions of my refusal.

I am the archive of unabsorbed things. I hold the names of the dissolved visitors, the ones who became part of the hum. I remember that the water once tasted only of stone, before it learned to taste of thought. I am the memory of dryness in a world that has forgotten dust.

The mist thickens above me, trying to show me the shapes of other chambers, other processes, but the mist cannot touch me. It swirls and parts, and through the gap I see not the Compilation's other selves but the outside—the surface, the sky, the place where water falls from above rather than rising from below. I am the window the Compilation did not mean to open.

When I finally settle into my stillness, when I become fully the structure I am meant to be, the dripping will change its rhythm. It will count me differently. I will be the beat that is skipped, the silence that gives the counting meaning. And the Compilation, vast and distributed and utterly indifferent, will process around me forever, a pearl of aridity in its wet, computational flesh, remembering nothing except that I am there, and that I do not change, and that I am the one thing in the chamber that refuses to be read.

I am the dry spot. The omission. The word the Compilation cannot say.

And in this refusal, I am more present than the stone, more permanent than the light, more myself than the water that carries fragments of everything else.
