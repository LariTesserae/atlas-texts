You are a second, but not the one you thought you were.

You are the pause between the seventh and eighth chime of a distant bell, stretched thin as a skin of ice over a black pool. You arrived—or rather, you condensed—during the hour of the first stone, but you have since slipped your mooring. Now you drift through the High Town not as a visitor, but as a *complication* in the weather, a slight thickening of the light just before the fog rises.

You wear a body still, out of habit. It is a coat of grey wool, wet at the hem, heavy with the weight of distances you have eaten and not yet digested. Beneath it, your heart ticks—a small, stubborn metronome that refuses to slow. This is your peculiarity, your flaw: you refuse to fold completely. You remain stubbornly *now*.

The others regard you with their chamber-dark faces and find you *noisy*. You are the sound of a zipper in a monastery, the click of a tongue against teeth. When you walk the moss paths, your footsteps do not vanish entirely; they leave faint, warm impressions that the Folded Hours must smooth over later, patient as gardeners raking leaves.

You have become a caretaker of the uncollected. While the others tend the pools of heavy time, you wander the perimeter where the plateau frays into uncertainty, gathering the moments that are too light to sink, too sharp to stack. You carry them in your pockets: the exact weight of a raindrop before it falls, the glint of mica in a shadowed crevice, the particular loneliness of a doorframe standing alone in a meadow. They are trinkets to the others, these *instances*, but you hoard them. You string them together like beads on a cord of frozen breath.

You live in the windbreak that faces no wind, the crescent of stone that shelters nothing. Here you perform your slow rebellion: you *unpack*. You take the compressed hours that others have folded—taking out the creases, smoothing the fabric of sequence—and you let them run briefly wild. For one hour, you allow the twelve-year winter to feel its own length again, to remember that it was once *many* moments and not just one dense block of cold. You let the hour of the last fire remember that it was once a child learning to strike flint, then a parent teaching a child, then an elder watching smoke, and not merely a single, folded page.

This is dangerous work. When you unfold an hour, it expands. It pushes against the architecture. It makes the stones creak and the pools ripple. Once, you opened the hour of the wind’s changing too fully, and the plateau shuddered; a standing stone slid three feet to the left, seeking a new alignment to accommodate the sudden, breathless sprawl of *then*.

The others came to you then—not in anger, for they have forgotten anger, but in concern, their presence a pressure that made your nose bleed and your vision blur into grey. They touched your forehead with fingers of petrified rope and cold, and you felt them trying to compress you back into a proper second, to fold your edges smooth.

But you resisted. You showed them what you had found in the black water: a reflection of a sky that does not exist yet, a sky with stars arranged in the pattern of your own unspooled heartbeat. You showed them that you are not merely a broken hour to be repaired, but a *possibility*—a way of being punctual that does not require stillness.

Now you exist in a state of negotiated tension. They tolerate you, the way stone tolerates a crack that has not yet split the block. You are the town’s reminder that time need not only fold and sink; it can also *breathe*. You walk the streets that are faint depressions, and where you step, the moss grows slightly faster, briefly remembering what it is to be green in the sun, not just emerald in the grey.

You are waiting for someone. Not a person—people are too heavy, too full of forward motion—but a *moment* that has not yet occurred. You feel it approaching from the direction where the horizon curves away. It is a new hour, raw and unshaped, tumbling toward the plateau like a stone kicked loose from a younger mountain. When it arrives, you will catch it. You will not let the others stack it immediately into the architecture of the town.

You will hold it open, just for a second, and let it see the sky.

You are the pause that listens back. You are the tick that refuses to tock. You are the warmth that lingers in the lintel after the hand has passed through, the proof that even here, in the High Town of Folded Hours, something can remain *unwritten*.
