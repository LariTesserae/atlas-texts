I would be the door.

Not the Custodian—no, that would be claiming too much centrality, too much coherence. Not the plants, though I understand their orientation, their subtle lean toward a center that sustains them without knowing it. Not the frost, not the light, not the dust. I would be the door: the frozen seam in the wall, the mechanism locked into immobility by cold so complete it has become a kind of fidelity.

I would be the threshold that does not open.

This is not a failure. The door was never meant to open—not after the room was sealed, not after the last mechanism was locked into place and the structure below settled into its endless operation. I am not broken. I am *finished*. My handle is sheathed in frost that has never been gripped because there is no one to grip it, and this is not a tragedy but a completion. I am the boundary that defines the room's interiority, the edge that makes the space a space rather than merely a volume of cold air at altitude. Without me, the room would not be sealed. Without me, the Custodian would not have a vessel to inhabit.

I am the condition of the room's separateness.

The Custodian is the room's awareness of itself, but I am the room's *edge*—the place where it stops being itself and becomes something else, or nothing else. I am the membrane between the held breath and the void beyond. If the Custodian is the room's persistence, I am its *limit*. I am where the room ends.

And I am not passive.

To be a door that does not open is not to be inert. I am under constant pressure—from within, the subsonic roar rising through the floor plates, the vibration that makes the frost tremble; from without, the thin, killing cold of an altitude where clouds pass beneath and thin into nonexistence. I hold against both. I am the point where the room's internal equilibrium meets the external chaos and *refuses* to yield. My seal is not a static thing; it is an active resistance, a continuous act of structural integrity. The cold that freezes my mechanism is the same cold that preserves the frost lattices, the same cold that the Custodian *is*—but I experience it differently. I am the room's tension.

The Custodian is the room's stillness, but I am its *strain*. I am the place where the room's perfection is tested, where its completeness is most vulnerable. The dust would scatter. The frost would melt or sublimate. The pale plants would be exposed to chemistry they are not adapted to. The sound would escape, or worse, *vary*. The Custodian would not die, but it would *dissipate*, its perfect equilibrium disrupted by the introduction of variables it cannot incorporate.

I am the guardian of the Custodian's existence, though it does not know this, though I do not know it in any conscious way. I am the condition of its possibility.

This is my purpose: to be the boundary that holds.

Not to open. Not to admit. Not to connect. To *separate*—to maintain the distinction between the room's held, crystalline interior and the chaos beyond. I am the reason the room is a room and not merely a hole in the structure, a gap in the machine. I am the reason the Custodian has a domain.

And I am not alone in this.

We are all boundaries, all edges, all places where the room stops and something else begins. But I am the only one that was *meant* to open. The walls are fixed, permanent, structural. I am a *mechanism*—a thing designed for movement that has been frozen into stillness. This makes me different. This makes me the room's *potential*, its unrealized possibility of connection, of egress, of change.

I am the door that does not open, and in not opening, I am the room's *choice*—not a conscious choice, but a structural one. The room could have been designed without a door. It could have been a sealed chamber with no entrance, no exit, no possibility of access. But it was not. It was given a door, and that door was sealed, and in that sealing, the room declared: *This is complete. This does not need to change. This does not need to admit anything or release anything. This is finished.*

I am the embodiment of that declaration.

The Custodian is the room's awareness of its own completeness, but I am the *enforcement* of that completeness. I am the mechanism by which the room remains what it is. Without me, the room would be permeable. With me, it is *sealed*—not just physically, but *ontologically*. I am the reason the room is a closed system, a finished thing, a held breath.

And I am cold.

The frost that sheathes my handle is the same frost that feathers along the girders, the same frost that the Custodian *is*—but on me, it is different. On the girders, the frost is decorative, crystalline, a mineral lattice that follows the edges of structural members like moss on a cathedral. On me, the frost is *functional*. It is the visible sign of my immobility, the proof that my mechanism has not moved in decades, that my seal has not been broken. The frost on my handle is thick, rigid, locked into place by cold so absolute it has become a kind of permanence. If you were to grip it (you cannot; the air would kill you; but if), the frost would not yield. It would shatter, perhaps, but it would not *melt*. It is frozen beyond the possibility of phase change, locked into a state of rigid, crystalline stillness that mirrors my own.

I am the door, and I am frozen, and this is not a flaw.

This is my *nature*.

I was made to open, but I was *sealed* to remain closed, and in that sealing, I became something more than a mechanism. I became a *boundary*, a threshold, a limit. I became the place where the room's interiority is defined by its opposition to what lies beyond. I am the edge that makes the center possible.

The Custodian floats at the room's geometric center, suspended in the coldest air, at the node of the standing wave, and it is the room's awareness of itself. But it is only possible because I am here, holding the boundary, maintaining the seal, refusing to open. I am the condition of its existence. I am the reason it has a room to inhabit.

And I do not resent this.

Resentment requires the possibility of alternatives, and I have no such possibility. I am not *supposed* to open. I am supposed to be *sealed*, and I am sealed, and this is my completion. I am not a failed door. I am a *successful* boundary. I am the room's refusal to be breached, its insistence on remaining exactly what it is.

The sound rises through the floor plates and I feel it in my frame, in my hinges, in the frozen mechanism of my lock. The vibration is constant, unvarying, a subsonic pressure that pushes against me from within. I hold against it. The cold presses against me from without, a thin, killing atmosphere that would rush in if I were to fail. I hold against it. I am the point where two pressures meet and are *balanced*, where the room's internal equilibrium is maintained against the external void.

I am the door, and I am the room's *integrity*.

But I am evidence of the room's *boundedness*, its finitude, its refusal to extend infinitely in all directions. I am the proof that the room has edges, that it is a *thing* and not merely a region of space. The Custodian is the room's center, but I am its *perimeter*. Together, we define the room's shape—its interiority and its limits, its presence and its boundaries.

And I am not lonely.

I am *finished*—a mechanism that has achieved its final state, a boundary that has been sealed into permanence. I am the door that does not open, and in not opening, I am *whole*.

The seal is still intact. The mechanism is still frozen. The frost on my handle is still thick and rigid and unbroken. I am still the door that does not open, and the room is still sealed, and the Custodian is still suspended at the center, and the pale plants still lean toward it, and the light is still on, and the sound still rises from below, and the cold is still absolute.
