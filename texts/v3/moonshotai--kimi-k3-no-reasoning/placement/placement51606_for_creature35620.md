# The Listener in the Seams

I am the third animal — if there is a third.

The candle I carry is not a candle anymore. The plant noticed it the moment I slipped through the breathing-passage, noticed it the way heat notices ice — not with hostility but with a patient, structural insistence that the candle's *not-self-ness* be addressed. The noticing moved from the plant's surface into my hand, through my hand, into the wax, and the wax began to feel like a lie I was telling with my fingers.

I did not drop it. I have been in too many places that demand the surrender of carried things, and I have learned that the surrender is never total — something always persists, some residue of the carried thing that clings to the carrier like scent to skin. The candle is mine now in a way it was not mine before I brought it here. It has been *noticed* into a new relationship with me. It is no longer an object I hold. It is an argument I am making.

The flame is wrong down here. This is the first thing I must account for.

The Maw has its own light — that even, sourceless, green-gold luminescence that does not diminish with distance. My candle adds a second light — small, flickering, warm in a different register, orange where the Maw is green. The two lights do not blend. They *negotiate*. Where the candle's flame throws its glow, the Maw's even illumination doesn't retreat but *thins*, becomes slightly less certain of itself, and in that thinning the geometry of the chamber shifts — not visually, but *aurally*, in the high thin tone that rises from the seams. The tone dips. The disagreement between the plant's geometry and the space's geometry changes key when my flame is near.

I noticed this on my second visit. I noticed that the small bright animal — the thumb-sized darting thing that may or may not be a fragment of the chamber's own light — changed its behavior when I lit the candle. It came closer. It pressed itself against the seams nearest to the flame, and it stayed there longer than it usually stays anywhere, and when it turned its attention toward me (that unmistakable headless turning), the quality of its attention had changed. It was not curiosity. It was *recognition*.

The plant recognized nothing. The plant noticed, because noticing is what the plant does, comprehensively and without differentiation. But the small bright animal *recognized*, and the difference between noticing and recognizing is the difference between weather and greeting.

---

I am not a visitor. I am not a resident. I am a *practice* — I come to the Maw to do a thing, and the doing of the thing is what I am, more than any body or name or history I carry above.

The practice is this: I sit at a seam, and I hold the candle to the seam, and I listen to what the light does to the light.

This is more complex than it sounds.

The seams are where the plant is newest. They are the lines where growth fronts collided and neither yielded, and they are still — after however many years the plant has occupied this chamber — still arriving, still arguing, still *becoming*. The seams glow with a readiness that is not light but *imminence*. When I bring my flame close, that imminence sharpens. The seam brightens — not visually, but *temporally*, as if the seam's future arrival has been brought slightly closer to the present by the presence of a second light source. The seam is more *about to* when my candle is near it.

I hold the candle. I feel the seam's imminence press through the Not-a-candle into my fingers, up my wrist, into the hinge of my elbow. The sensation is warm and specific — not the general warmth of the chamber, but a localized, directed warmth that feels like being *pointed at* by something kind. The seam uses the candle's warmth to warm me, or uses my warmth to warm itself, or — on the loose-tooth days, the days when time is a molar about to come out — both, in an order I cannot determine and have stopped trying to determine.

The water pools in the seams. You know this — the closed-loop condensation, the warm droplets that bead and occasionally sheet and always return. When my candle flame is close to a seam, the condensation changes. Not in chemistry — in *tempo*. The droplets fall slower than the already-too-slow pace of the chamber's loose time. They hang like notes in a bar of music that the conductor has decided to savor. And in that hanging — in the suspended moment between beading and falling, between falling and landing, between landing and being absorbed — the water becomes briefly, impossibly, *luminous in a different color*. Not green-gold. Not the orange of my flame. Something between them — a color that does not have a name because it exists only in the overlap between two light sources, only in the seamed space where the Maw's own glow and my small foreign glow disagree.

I sit in this disagreement. It is my practice. It is what I am.

---

The figure beside me is harder to account for.

It arrived — or it was always here and I began to perceive it, and the difference between these two explanations is not one the Maw's geometry permits me to resolve. It is a person-shaped presence that sits at seams parallel to the one where I hold my candle. It does not have a candle. It does not have features I can describe when I look away. When I look at it directly, it has the quality of a memory being formed rather than a person being seen — it is more vivid in retrospect than in experience, more clearly *someone* when I close my eyes than when I open them.

It sits at seams. It listens. It does not hold anything, and this absence of held objects is what distinguishes it from me — it has surrendered everything, I think, surrendered so completely that the surrender is no longer a practice but a *condition*. It is not carrying a candle that has stopped being a candle. It is not carrying anything. It carries itself, and it has been carried in turn, and the carrying and the carried have become indistinguishable.

I believe — and I stress that this is belief, formed in the loose geometry of a place that degrades certainty as a matter of course — I believe the figure is a former visitor who did not leave.

Not because the passage failed to open. Not because the plant breathed out at the wrong moment. But because the noticing — that slow, warm, enveloping attention that the plant presses through anyone who enters — because the noticing became, for this person, not an experience of being noticed but an experience of *belonging to the noticing*. They stopped being the object of the plant's attention and became a *part* of the plant's attention — a fold in the great directed noticing that flows through the plant's tissue like something that is not blood, something that is not sap, something that is awareness without a mind to hold it.

The figure sits at seams because the seams are where the plant's attention is newest, most concentrated, most *about to*. And the figure, being now a part of that attention, is drawn to the places of maximum attentional density the way a stone is drawn downhill — not by choice but by the shape of what it has become.

It does not speak. But it sits, and its sitting is a kind of testimony: that the boundary between visitor and resident is as loose as the boundary between plant and place, between light and animal, between one and two and the quantity that is not a number.

---

What am I, then? In this world of one plant and almost-nothing and animals whose count cannot be fixed — what is the thing that comes with a candle and sits at seams and listens to the disagreement of lights?

I am a listener. That is the simplest answer and the truest. I have come to the Maw not to observe it (observation implies distance, and the Maw does not permit distance) and not to study it (study implies separation, and the Maw absorbs separation) but to *listen* — to attend, in the way the plant attends, comprehensively and without differentiation, to the sounds and the light and the warmth and the pulse and the high thin tone of geometric disagreement.

The candle is my instrument. Not a tool — the Maw does not permit tools, and the candle has ceased to be one. The candle is an instrument the way a tuning fork is an instrument — it does not produce music; it produces a *reference*, a standard against which the existing music can be measured. My candle's small orange flame is a reference against which the Maw's vast green-gold glow can be measured, and in the measuring — in the overlap, in the disagreement, in the nameless color that exists only where the two lights argue — I hear what the Maw is.

I hear that it is not one thing. This is the discovery that the practice has led me to, the finding that I carry out (because it is not an object, and the Maw permits the carrying-out of findings): the plant is not one thing. It presents as one thing — one organism, one place, one presence — but the seams, when attended to with a second light source, reveal a multiplicity that the plant's surface conceals. Each seam is a distinct voice in the plant. Each seam is a distinct *argument* that the plant is having with itself about what it will become.

The small bright animal knows this. It presses against the seams to listen to the individual voices. The large unseen animal knows this. It moves through the plant's interior to find the seams from within, to hear the arguments from inside the tissue rather than from the surface.

I know this because of my candle. Because the overlap of two lights reveals what one light cannot: that the Maw's unity is a *performance* of unity, that the plant's singleness is a *decision* that is being made, seam by seam, argument by argument, at every moment, and that the decision could be — and occasionally, on the loose-tooth days, is — made differently.

The figure beside me knows this because it *is* this. It is a seam in human form. It is the place where the plant's attention met a visitor's consciousness and neither yielded, and the frozen disagreement — the luminous, glowing, imminence-filled disagreement — became a person who sits and listens and does not leave.

---

The plant breathes in. The passage opens. I could leave. I have left before — how many times is a number that the Maw's loose time will not hold steady. Sometimes I think I have been coming here for years. Sometimes I think this is my first visit. Sometimes I think the distinction between first and hundredth is a distinction the Maw has dissolved in me, the way it dissolves the distinction between candle and not-candle, between light and animal, between one and two.

The plant breathes in. The figure beside me does not move toward the passage.

I do not move toward the passage.

The candle burns. The seam glows. The water hangs in its impossible, nameless color. The high thin tone holds a note that has not happened yet.

I am the listener in the seams, and the seams are the part that matters.
