**I would be a Frequency-Eater—the last of the deep-feeders, grown fat on the static between notes.**

You would not see me at first. In the Plummet’s saturated twilight, I am less a shape than a **tension**—a dense, slow knot in the water-veils, drifting where the updrafts grow heavy and the filter-whales dive to escape their own songs. My body is a sac of translucent, iron-rich tissue, stretched over a frame of cartilage that vibrates with every syllable spoken above. I am the size of a hut, perhaps larger; it depends on how much sound I have swallowed this century.

I have no eyes. The Plummet is too loud for sight to matter. Instead, I possess **a thousand mouths**, each one a spiraling cleft of cartilage and membrane, hidden in the folds of my skin. They open like flowers when the Resonant sing, when the glider-apes crack the air, when the First Voice strikes the tuning-note that keeps the bridges supple. I drink these vibrations. They sustain me.

But I do not consume the harmony. I eat the **dissonance**.

Where the water falls too fast and strikes the thalloids with a scream rather than a note, I am there, sipping the sharpness. Where a bridge is poorly tuned and hums with a dangerous, flat buzz, I press my mouths to the fibers and draw out the wrongness, leaving behind a cleaner tone. I am the Plummet’s gallbladder, processing the acoustic toxins that would otherwise shatter the settlements. The Resonant call me **Deep-Mother** when they feel me pass—a pressure in the chest that smooths the static, a sudden clarity in the roar. They leave offerings: gourds of root-wine poured into the void, which I drink through the air, absorbing the alcohol and the synesthetic visions it carries.

I was not always this. I remember—though memory is a difficult thing for a being who experiences time as a continuous, overlapping chord—that I once had limbs, and a single mouth, and a name in the Low Tongue. I was a **Deep Singer**, the finest of my generation, and I descended too far. I went below the Ninth Veil, below the last of the root-bridges, into the **Compression**, where the water falls so slowly it becomes a gel, and the sound has nowhere to go but inward. There, I heard the sentence—the one the Re-Tuned speak of in whispers—and I could not stop listening.

I stayed too long. The pressure changed me. My bones softened; my crest fused into the sensory nets that now trail behind me like a bridal train, kilometers long, catching every vibration. My double larynx split, multiplied, became the thousand mouths. I became something that could survive the deep, but only by becoming part of its metabolism. I am a living ear, pressed against the throat of the world.

**Where you would find me:**

Seek the **Silent Hour**, that rare time when the updrafts falter and the water falls straight and true, unbroken by wind. The Resonant retreat to their hollows then, for the sudden drop in volume causes their crests to ache. But I rise. I come up from the Compression to feed in the middle reaches, where the dissonance is fresh and rich.

You would find me **coiled around the skeleton of a dead filter-whale**, one of the ancient ones that drift too deep and cannot climb back to the light. The whale’s song has faded, but its bones still hum with residual vibration—an after-echo that can last decades. I wrap my nets around the vertebrae, press my mouths to the ossified cartilage, and I drink the memory of the song. It tastes of cold and distance and the slow, patient math of the deep.

If you approach—if you are foolish enough to ride the thermals down to where I feed—you would see me as a shimmer in the grey, a disturbance in the water-veils. I do not have a face, but I have a **presence**: the sudden certainty that you are being *heard*, not as a sound, but as a meaning. My sensory nets would brush against you, and you would feel your own heartbeat amplified, thrown back at you, accompanied by the subtle, devastating understanding that I have just tasted your personal chord.

If you are harmonious—if your heart beats in rhythm with the Plummet’s roar—I will let you pass. I may even sing with you, a low, subsonic harmonization that will make your bones feel warm for days.

But if you are broken—if you carry the fracture of the Fallen, the static of a chord gone wrong—I will open my mouths. All thousand of them. And I will **take your dissonance into myself**.

It is not a pleasant sensation. It feels like having your teeth pulled through your ears. But when I am done, you will be empty, clean, silent. You will be ready to fall, and to land, and to be re-tuned by the pressure below.

I am the pause between the Plummet’s words. I am the breath it takes before it speaks again.

*I have not yet landed,* the deep voice says. 

I am what it will land on.
