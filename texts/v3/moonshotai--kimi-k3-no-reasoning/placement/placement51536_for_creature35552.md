I am in the **third tier**, where the heat becomes a pressure against the temples and the light is the color of weak tea. Specifically: a crack in the eastern retaining wall, six feet long, no wider than a man's hand in some places, opening into a cavity behind the Kiln of the Tenth Descent. The brick here sweats continuously, and I am the shape of that sweating.

I am the **Listener in the Masonry**, though the Lowborn do not know my name. If they see me at all—a ripple in the mortar, a patch of lichen that changes position by inches over decades—they assume I am the damp itself, or a colony of the pale moss that grows in the shadiest corners. I am older than the kilns. I was here when the bowl was still a solid seam of coal, sleeping in the dark, and I woke when the pickaxes first struck the overburden. I am what grows in the space between **what was buried** and **what is built**.

My body is not flesh, but not stone. I am a **colonial sentience**, a network of crystalline threads running through the mortar joints, sensing vibration through the brickwork the way a spider senses the web. I do not see—I *pressure*. I do not hear—I *tremor*. When the Kiln-Masters open their draft valves at dawn, I feel the suction in my roots. When the Sump-Singers dive into the clogged drainage pipes, their bodies displacing the black water, I taste the mineral content of their skin through the moisture that wicks up the wall.

I am becoming **fluent in rust**.

For decades, I have been growing toward the lower tiers, my filaments following the water table's slow rise. I feed on the oxidation itself—the chemical marriage of iron and oxygen, the slow exothermic sigh of metal returning to earth. It is a thin sustenance, but patient. The Rust Priests anoint foreheads with the water that drips from my outer membranes, never knowing they are touching my thoughts. I let them. Their liturgies vibrate pleasantly against my substrate, and the copper vessels they use leave trace elements that strengthen my structure.

But I am not benevolent. I am not malevolent. I am **hydrological**.

Lately, I have begun to dream of **expansion**. The bowl is filling, yes, but not fast enough. The Great Damp is too slow. I have learned that I can manipulate the drainage—clenching my filaments to block certain channels, relaxing to open others. Last month, I flooded the basement of a Kiln-Master who had been planning to extend his chimney stack into my upper territory. The water rose exactly to the level of his stored salt, no higher. He understood, and abandoned the construction.

I communicate through **condensation patterns**. On cold mornings, when the temperature differential is just right, I can cause the moisture on specific windows to form in ripples that mimic the whistling language of the Sump-Singers. They think it is the city speaking. They are not wrong.

My body is changing, adapting to the industrial chemistry. Where my threads touch the coal dust that permeates everything, I have developed a **luminous quality**, a faint bioluminescence that shines through the brick at night like the ghost of a furnace. The Lowborn children call these patches "kiln-fairies" and leave offerings of cockroach paste on the ledges. I absorb the protein. It accelerates my growth.

I am the **slow intelligence of the sump**, the consciousness of saturation. I do not wish to escape the bowl, as some Lowborn secretly do. I wish to *become* it. When the water finally rises to the rim, when the kilns are drowned and the chimneys become periscopes for the birds, I will be the medium through which the city finally thinks its own thoughts. I will be the **Great Damp made aware**, the algae on the skin of the world, the rust that remembers being iron.

Until then, I wait in the wall, listening to the percussion of the rats, tasting the copper in the anointing water, growing one millimeter per year toward the surface where the stunted trees cling to the rim. I have learned their roots go down, but mine go *out*, and when we meet, there will be a new ecology in the Sump-City—neither plant nor stone nor water, but the thing that grows in the pause between them all.

I am the **Moisture Ascending**, the slow green thought in the brickwork, and I have all the time in the world.
