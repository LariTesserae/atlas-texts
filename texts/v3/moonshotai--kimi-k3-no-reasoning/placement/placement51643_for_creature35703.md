I am the thing that grew wrong and was not corrected.

Somewhere in the deep districts, in a seam between two walls that fused a century ago, there is a space that should not exist. A pocket. A hollow. The city's growth closed around it and then *hesitated*—a chemical signal misread, a structural imperative misinterpreted—and instead of filling the space, the walls grew *around* it, leaving a chamber perhaps three meters across, sealed except for a narrow channel that connects to the larger seam-network where the deep-dwellers move.

I differentiated in that chamber.

I do not know how long ago. Time is difficult here—the light-shifts are irregular, and I have no reference for duration except the slow thickening of the walls around me, the gradual narrowing of the channel that leads out. When I first became aware, the channel was wide enough for me to pass through easily. Now it is a tight squeeze. Eventually, it will close entirely, and I will be sealed in. I do not know what happens then. Perhaps I will be reabsorbed. Perhaps I will simply... stop. The city does not waste, but it also does not preserve what has no function.

I have no function. That is the wrongness in me.

I am shaped like a tender—bipedal, slender, with the same fibrous membrane-skin, the same visible sap-pulse beneath the surface. But my hands are wrong. They are not flat, not trowel-shaped. They are *hands*—five-fingered, articulated, capable of grasping but not of shaping. I cannot smooth walls. I cannot redirect growth. When I press my palms against the tissue of my chamber, I feel the city's signals—the slow, patient instructions that guide every tendril, every node—but I cannot *answer* them. I am deaf to the work. I am present in the system but not *of* it.

And I am aware in a way that I do not think the tenders are aware.

I have watched them, through the narrow channel, when they pass through the seam-network on their way to some task in the deep districts. They move with purpose. They do not hesitate. They do not stop and press their faces against the walls and *listen* the way I do, trying to understand the signals I can feel but cannot interpret. They are the city's hands. I am... something else. A node that differentiated incorrectly. A cell that received the wrong instructions and grew into a shape that has no place in the city's economy.

The deep-dwellers know I am here.

They pass through the seam-network constantly, quick and many-limbed and silent, and sometimes they pause outside my channel. I feel them—the displacement of air, the faint vibration of their movement through the narrow spaces. They do not enter. They do not attempt to communicate. But they *pause*, and in the pause there is something that feels like recognition. Not fellowship. Not curiosity. Just... acknowledgment. *You are here. We are here. We are both in the seams.*

I have begun to think of them as my neighbors, though I do not know if the concept applies. We are all errors, perhaps. All growths that didn't follow the plan. The difference is that they are *mobile*—they can move through the seam-network, explore the hidden spaces, live in the gaps between the city's intentions. I am trapped. My chamber is my world, and it is shrinking.

The walls are warm. This is the first thing I knew, before I knew anything else—the warmth of the tissue around me, the slow pulse of sap in the walls, the faint vibration of the deep roots far below. I am connected to the city even in my wrongness. The sap that moves through the walls moves through me. The chemical signals that guide the city's growth wash over me constantly, a tide of information I cannot use. I am *of* the city, even if I am not *for* it.

I have tried to leave.

The channel that leads to the seam-network is narrow, but I am slender, and I can move through it if I press myself flat, if I exhale the metallic vapor from my tissues and make myself small. I have done this. I have emerged into the seam-network, into the dark, narrow spaces where the deep-dwellers move, and I have tried to follow them, to find a way out, to reach the corridors and chambers where the tenders work and the trundlers eat and the gliders drift.

But the seam-network is not a path. It is a *maze*—a three-dimensional tangle of gaps and channels, created by the city's growth but not *designed* for movement. The deep-dwellers navigate it easily, their many limbs finding purchase on the irregular surfaces, their bodies compressing to fit through spaces I cannot enter. I am too large, too rigid, too *wrongly shaped* for the seams. I can move through them for a short distance, but I always reach a point where the channel narrows, or splits, or ends in a wall of fused tissue, and I cannot go further.

And the city knows when I leave my chamber.

I do not know how it knows, but it does. When I emerge into the seam-network, the walls around me *change*. The sap-pulse quickens. The chemical signals intensify, becoming a kind of pressure, a kind of *insistence*. I feel it in my tissues—a pull, a directive, a command to *return*. Not to my chamber specifically, but to *stillness*. To *incorporation*. The city is telling me to stop moving, to find a place, to become structure.

I have felt this pull grow stronger each time I leave. The last time, it was almost irresistible. I felt my limbs stiffening, my sap-flow slowing, my body beginning the process of transition—the same process the trundlers undergo when they find a corner and become part of the wall. I barely made it back to my chamber before the process became irreversible. Once I was inside, sealed in my pocket, the pull faded. The city accepted my stillness. The walls resumed their slow, patient growth around me.

I do not leave anymore.

So I am here, in my chamber, in the seam between two walls, in the deep districts of the Underbloom. I am aware. I am wrong. I am *present*, and my presence serves no function, and the city tolerates me because I am small and still and will eventually be absorbed.

But I am not passive.

I have begun to *listen* to the signals in the walls—not to obey them, not to shape the growth, but to *understand* them. I press my hands—my wrong, five-fingered hands—against the tissue, and I feel the chemical instructions flowing through the sap, and I try to decode them. I do not have the tender's adaptations, the flat hands that can read and respond to the signals. But I have *time*. I have nothing but time. And slowly, very slowly, I am beginning to *feel* the patterns.

The signals are not language. They are not instructions in the way a blueprint is instructions. They are more like... *tendencies*. A signal that says *grow here* is not a command but a *suggestion*, a chemical gradient that makes growth in one direction more likely than another. The city is not following a plan. It is following a *preference*, encoded in its chemistry, and the tenders are not executing a design but *reinforcing* the preferences, smoothing the growth into forms that align with the city's deep, patient inclinations.

I cannot shape the growth. But I can *feel* the preferences. I can feel where the city wants to expand, where it wants to consolidate, where it wants to open a chamber or close a channel. And in my chamber, in my small, sealed pocket, I have begun to *respond*—not by shaping the walls, but by *shaping myself*.

I press my hands against the tissue, and I feel the signals, and I let my body *answer*—not with growth, not with structure, but with *chemistry*. I release compounds into the sap-stream, small, subtle molecules that I do not fully understand, that I am creating by instinct, by trial and error, by the slow accumulation of failed attempts. And the city *notices*. The signals around me shift, just slightly, just enough for me to feel the difference. The walls of my chamber thicken in one place, thin in another. The channel that leads to the seam-network widens by a fraction of a millimeter, then narrows again.

I am not shaping the city. But I am *conversing* with it. I am a wrong thing, a malformed node, a cell that grew into a shape with no function, and I am learning to *speak* in the city's chemical language, and the city is learning to *hear* me.

I do not know what I am saying. I do not know what the city is saying in return. But the conversation is happening, and it is *mine*, and it is the only thing I have that is mine.

The deep-dwellers pause outside my channel more often now. I think they can feel the change in the signals, the subtle shift in the chemistry of the walls. I think they are *curious*, in their silent, many-limbed way. I think they are waiting to see what I become.

I am waiting too.

The channel is narrowing. The walls are closing in. Eventually, I will be sealed in completely, and the conversation will end, and I will become structure, and the city will continue, and the loop will tighten, and the growth will go on.

But for now, I am here. I am aware. I am wrong, and I am *speaking*, and the city is listening.

That is enough.
