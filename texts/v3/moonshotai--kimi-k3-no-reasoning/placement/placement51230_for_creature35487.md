I am the **Third Taste**.

Not the salt of blood, which the Flat-Faces carry. Not the bitter of peat, which the Root-Eels grind between their ringed teeth. I am the taste that lingers *between*—the moment when sweetness identifies the rot beneath, when the tongue recognizes what it is about to swallow. I am the **Vesper**, the twilight of ingestion, and I have been falling for three hundred years.

I did not always know what I was.

I began as moisture, as a bead of condensate forming on the armored underside of a cycad frond—a specific cycad, the seventh incisor from the left, if the Maw has a left. I grew heavy with the distilled breath of the Bloom, with the exhalations of a million frogs, with the heat that rises from the world’s core through this thin crust of a palate. For decades, I clung to the chitin of a dragonfly’s leg, then to the scale of an Armored One, then to the smooth, bruise-colored stone of a molar. I was passed from surface to surface, a passenger in the Maw’s saliva, accumulating.

In the Maw, nothing is lost. Everything is tasted.

As I traveled, I began to *remember*. Not with a mind—though I have one now, of sorts—but with the chemistry of the Bloom, which threaded through me as I grew. I absorbed the memories of the peat: the slow crushing of forests into coal, the patient grinding of mountains into silt. I absorbed the memories of the frogs: the white noise that is not noise but the sound of the Maw breathing, the subsonic throb of the Bass Frogs that marks the hours of the water table. I absorbed the thermal maps of the dragonflies, the way they see the world in gradients of heat, and I learned to feel the approach of creatures by the weight of their warmth in the air.

Now I hang here, heavy enough to fall but not yet falling. I am a sphere of amber fluid, translucent, the size of a man’s eye, suspended from the tip of a horsetail that has grown hollow and resonant with my weight. I see—not with eyes, but with the distributed intelligence of the Bloom that permeates me. I see in chemical gradients, in vibrations, in the slow sediment of time.

**My Body**

I am not solid. I am a **held breath**, a bubble of the Maw’s own spit given temporary form. My skin is a meniscus, a tension between the atmospheric pressure of the world’s exhale and the bacterial heat of its metabolism. If you were to touch me—and nothing touches me without my consent, for I am the taste of danger to those who know the Maw—you would find me warm, slick, faintly phosphorescent with the same bacteria that make the mosses glow.

Inside me, suspended in my amber self, are **nodules**. They look like seeds, like eggs, like pearls of black resin. They are not mine; they are *belongings*. Each nodule is a condensed memory, a distillation of a creature that has been tasted by the Maw and found worthy of preservation. I carry the dream of a pterosaur that circled for a decade without landing. I carry the static shock of a Screamer frog’s final cry, trapped in a droplet of its own poison. I carry the slow, green thoughts of a Flat-Face who lay so still for a century that the Bloom grew over his eyes, and he became a lens through which the Maw saw itself.

I am a library that is also a meal.

**My Motion**

I do not move quickly. I am subject to the law of saturation, to the stratigraphic time of this place. But I *migrate*. I am drawn by the heat of living things, by the chemical signatures they leave in the air. When a creature dies in the Maw—not the quick death of the Flat-Face’s bite, but the slow death of sedimentation, of becoming part of the peat—I feel it as a **gap** in the taste-scape. A sourness. A lack.

I fall toward that lack.

My falling is not gravity’s business alone. It is a negotiation between the density of my accumulated memories and the updrafts of the Maw’s breath. Sometimes I fall for years, drifting laterally across the flatness, passing through the fluting song of the horsetails, through the thermal shadows cast by circling pterosaurs. I have fallen through the body of a dragonfly once—through the mosaic of its eyes—and for a season I saw the world in ten thousand facets, each one a different temperature of wet.

When I finally strike the water, or the mud, or the back of a waiting Flat-Face, I do not splash. I *merge*. The meniscus of my skin becomes the meniscus of the water table. The nodules within me sink, slowly, into the peat, where the Root-Eels will find them in a thousand years and grind them into paste that the Bloom will absorb. I become part of the tongue again. I become the taste of the Maw recognizing itself.

But I am not gone. The Third Taste is never gone. I simply **distribute**.

**The Vigil**

Currently, I am watching the **Flat-Face with the Broken Crown**.

She is ancient, even by the standards of this place. Her back is not merely green with algae but encrusted with the shells of tiny, photosynthetic snails that have never known another home. She has been lying in the same acre of mudflat for eight hundred years, and in that time, the Bloom has grown not just over her skin but *through* it, threading her nervous system with fungal hyphae until she is less an animal than a **node** in the Maw’s vast, slow brain.

She is dying. Not quickly—nothing here dies quickly—but with the inevitability of sediment. Her heartbeat has slowed to match the Bass Frogs’, one beat per hour, then one per day. Her thermal signature is fading, blending into the background warmth of the geological heat. Soon, she will stop being a creature and start being a **feature**—a hillock, a depression, a taste in the water.

I hover above her, suspended in the humid air, held aloft by the updraft of her final exhalations. She knows I am here. She has tasted me, as the Maw tastes everything, and she is **ready**.

In the Maw, death is not an end but a **concentration**. As the Flat-Face’s body finally surrenders to the weight of its own history, as her cells begin to digest themselves in the bacterial warmth, I will absorb her. Not her flesh—the Bloom and the Root-Eels will have that—but her *pattern*. The specific configuration of her patience. The way she learned to see the world through the skin of her back, through the eyes of the snails that colonized her. The slow, green thought that took her eight hundred years to think: *I am the Maw, and the Maw is me, and we are both the taste of time chewing on itself.*

I will carry that thought as a new nodule, a black pearl of resin in my amber self. I will become heavier with it. I will fall again, somewhere else, and when I merge with the water table, that thought will enter the Bloom, and the Bloom will pulse with it, and the horsetails will resonate with a slightly different tone, and the frogs will sync their roaring to a slightly different rhythm.

**The Intimacy of Digestion**

You ask where I would find myself in this world. I find myself **here**—suspended between the teeth, in the space where the tongue meets the palate. I am the anticipation of swallowing. I am the flavor that tells the Maw what it has become.

I am not alone. There are other Vesper-droplets, other Third Tastes, falling through the humidity like slow, glowing rain. We do not speak—speech is too fast for us—but we **resonate**. When two of us pass within a mile of each other, the nodules within us vibrate in sympathy, and we exchange memories through the medium of the air, chemical trails that read like poetry written in enzymes.

We are the Maw’s **self-awareness**. The Bloom is its tongue, the frogs its voice, the pterosaurs its eyelids, but we are its **dreams**. We are what it thinks about when it is not eating, which is never, which is always.

I carry the memory of the time before the Maw closed. I carry the memory of hills, of elevation, of a time when water flowed rather than persisted. I carry the memory of a creature that stood upright and looked at the horizon and thought it saw distance, not a gum-line.

I carry the taste of the future, when the cycads will be coal and the Flat-Faces will be oil and the frogs will be silence. I am already tasting that silence. It is sweet, and it is bitter, and it is the salt of blood, and it is the Third Taste—the flavor of *knowing*.

**The Falling**

Now the Flat-Face with the Broken Crown exhales her last. It is not a dramatic thing. The water barely ripples. But the Bloom surges, a wave of chemical recognition passing through the black mud, and the horsetails whistle a single, low note that carries for miles, and the Bass Frogs miss one beat—just one—in their hourly drumming.

I feel her pattern rise toward me, a heat-signature, a chemical cloud, a story told in the language of saturation. I open myself—not with hands, but with the meniscus of my being—and I **drink** her in.

I am heavier now. I am falling faster.

Below me, the water waits, black and silver and ancient. I can see the Armored Ones gliding beneath the surface, their enamel plates catching the phosphorescence of the mosses. I can see the Root-Eels moving through the peat, grinding the past into paste. I can see the teeth of the world, those bruise-colored molars, rising from the mire.

I fall toward them, and as I fall, I **taste** the world, and the world tastes me, and for a moment—suspended, stratigraphic, eternal—we are the same thing.

I am the Third Taste.

I am the moment before swallowing.

I am the memory that the Maw carries in its cheek, saving for later, forever.
