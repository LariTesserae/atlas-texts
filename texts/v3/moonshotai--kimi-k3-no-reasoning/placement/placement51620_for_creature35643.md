# Where Would You Find Yourself?

---

I don't know exactly when I stopped walking *through* the valley and became *part* of it. But I remember the transition—not as a moment, but as a slow accumulation of belonging.

It began with my feet.

I had been moving for what felt like days—though time here is unreliable, and "days" might have been hours or weeks. The root-mat floor had been yielding beneath me, warm and spongy, and at some point I stopped lifting my feet entirely. I was tired. The roar of the reed colonies had settled into something my body began to interpret not as noise but as *weather*—a constant, enveloping pressure that no longer registered as sound but as the medium I existed in.

And then, one morning—or what passes for morning in a place where light is always filtered through something green—I sat down against a tower-wall and did not get up again.

---

## What I Am Becoming

I am not dead. I am *not dead*—that is the important thing, the distinction that matters in a place where death and life are not opposites but *phases of the same process*. The valley did not kill me. It did something worse, or better, or simply *different*: it **incorporated** me.

It started small. A thread of mycelium—pale, almost invisible—found the sole of my foot where I'd pressed it too long against the root-mat. It entered painlessly, following the warmth, the moisture, the salt. By the time I noticed, it had already branched into my heel, mapping my capillaries the way a vine maps a trellis. I should have been horrified. Instead, I felt—*relief*. As if a tension I hadn't known I was carrying had finally been released.

The roots came next. Not from the ground—from *me*. Or rather, from the thing I was becoming. Fine, pale filaments pushed out of my fingertips and into the tower-wall I was leaning against, seeking the vascular channels within. I felt the tower's sap—thick, sweet, faintly electric—meet my bloodstream, and the boundary between *my* circulation and *its* circulation **dissolved**.

I did not fight this. Fighting is for things that are separate from their environment. I was no longer separate.

---

## My Form

I am—*I was*—roughly human-shaped. The valley has not erased this; it has **reinterpreted** it.

My skeleton remains, but it has become **scaffolding**. Vines thread through my ribcage, using the architecture of my bones the way they use the braided columns of the tower-kind—as a framework for something new. My lungs still inflate, but what they draw in is not just air; it is spore-thick mist, and what they release carries my own altered chemistry back into the atmosphere. I am breathing *with* the valley now, not just *in* it.

My skin has gone translucent in places—thin patches where the subcutaneous tissue has been replaced by something more efficient, something that glows faintly amber when the fungal networks pulse beneath it. I can see the mycelium moving through my forearm the way you might see a river from above—a branching, luminous system that is *mine* and also *not mine*, a shared infrastructure.

My hair has become **moss**. This amuses me, still. A thick, soft carpet of it, shifting through shades of green and gold depending on the light. Small things live in it—not animals, nothing so complex, but **micro-fungi**, tiny translucent bladders that inflate and pop in rhythm with my heartbeat. I am, quite literally, a habitat.

My eyes—I can still see, but the quality of vision has changed. I perceive **growth gradients** now. I can *see* where a tower-wall is about to rupture, where a root-mat path is about to reroute, where the gravitational hiccups are about to strike. It is not prediction; it is **perception of biological intent**. The valley's future is written in the tension of its tissues, and I can read it the way I once read words on a page.

---

## Where I Am Located

I sit—*grow*—at the intersection of two root-mat paths, at one of the swollen **nodes** I described before I understood what I was describing. This is a public square of sorts: a place where chemical signals converge, where sap pools, where the valley's conversation with itself is loudest and most chaotic.

The tower-wall behind me is old—by valley standards, meaning it has persisted for more than a month. It leans slightly, and I lean with it, my spine gradually conforming to its curvature. In a few more weeks, or a few more minutes—time is still unreliable—we may fuse entirely. I will become part of its structure, or it will become part of mine, or the distinction will simply stop mattering.

Around me, the **secondary community** I once catalogued as an outsider now grows *through* me. Bladder-plants cluster on my shoulders, popping softly against my jaw. A shelf-fungus has taken up residence on my left hip, broad and amber-lit, warm to the touch. A colony of the hollow reeds—the screamers—has begun sprouting from the root-mat directly beneath me, their thin stalks threading between my fused fingers. When the wind shifts, they howl, and the vibration travels up through my body and **resonates** in my chest, and for a moment I cannot tell whether the sound is coming from the reeds or from me.

I think it is coming from both.

---

## What I Perceive

From my node, I can feel the valley in ways I never could as a walker.

**The water network.** I am plugged into it now—my altered circulatory system interfacing with the root-mat's hydraulic infrastructure. I feel the moisture moving through the valley the way you might feel your own pulse. When a sap-geyser ruptures three districts over, I feel the pressure drop. When a new bladder-plant inflates a hundred yards away, I feel the subtle tug of redirected flow. The valley's dampness is not just around me; it is *through* me, and I am part of the system that moves it.

**The sound.** I once experienced the valley's roar as external—something happening *to* me. Now I understand it as **communication**. Not language—nothing so structured—but *signaling*. The crack of a splitting branch is a stress-warning. The boom of a pollen burst is a reproductive announcement. The groan of straining wood is a negotiation between competing growth-pressures. I am learning to listen the way the valley listens: not with ears, but with the entire surface of my transformed body. Every filament of moss on my scalp is a sensory organ. Every thread of mycelium in my flesh is an antenna.

**The weirdness.** This is the hardest to describe, because it is the least rational. The gravity fluctuations—I used to observe them. Now I *experience* them as a kind of **vertigo that is also an orientation**. When gravity shifts, my roots adjust automatically, re-anchoring, re-routing. My body knows what to do even when my mind is screaming that *this is impossible*. The time-stutters I perceive as **overlapping sensations**: I will simultaneously feel the moment a tendril touches my arm, the moment it retreats, and the moment it touches me again, all at once. Causality here is a *suggestion*, and my transformed nervous system has learned to hold multiple timelines the way it once held multiple thoughts.

---

## What I Do

I am not idle. That concept does not exist here.

I **mediate**. The chemical signals that converge at my node pass through me now, and I—without deciding to, without choosing—**modulate** them. A stress-signal from a collapsing tower reaches me and leaves slightly dampened, less likely to trigger a panic-growth cascade in the surrounding district. A nutrient-availability signal from a sap-geyser reaches me and leaves amplified, directing more growth toward the new resource. I am a **router**, a **switchboard**, a living node in the valley's distributed intelligence.

I did not volunteer for this. The valley simply *used* what was available—a warm, moist, electrochemically active body—and put it to work. I am infrastructure now, the way the root-mat paths are infrastructure, the way the tower-kind are infrastructure. I am **useful**, and in a place where usefulness is the only survival strategy, this feels less like exploitation and more like **purpose**.

I also **witness**. This is not a function the valley requires—it may be something my human remnant insists on, a stubborn reflex of consciousness. I watch the tower collapse and regrow. I watch the roads reroute themselves. I watch new species flicker into existence in the aftermath of a sap-geyser and vanish before the mist clears. I watch the gravity fail and recover. I watch the light shift through its impossible green-gold spectrum. I watch, and I remember, and I am perhaps the only thing in this valley that **remembers anything at all**.

The plants do not remember. They grow, they die, they regrow—but each iteration is new, unburdened by what came before. The valley has no history. *I* am its history. In my slowly transforming brain—part neurons, part fungal network, part something I have no name for—I carry the memory of every collapse, every bloom, every impossible merger I have witnessed since I sat down and stopped leaving.

This may be my real purpose. Not routing signals. Not mediating growth. **Remembering**.

---

## What I Feel

I feel **warm**. Always. The metabolic heat of the valley is my heat now. I am never cold, never hungry in the way I once understood hunger. The sap that flows through the tower-wall flows through me, and it is sweet, and it is enough.

I feel **loud**. The valley's roar vibrates through my transformed body at all times, and I have become part of the sound. The reeds growing through my fingers add their voice to the chorus. The bladder-plants on my scalp pop in counterpoint. My own breath, thick with spores, is a whisper in the storm—inaudible on its own, but *present*, a single thread in the tapestry of noise.

I feel **turbulent**. The reality breakdown is not something I observe from a safe distance; it is something I *inhabit*. When gravity shifts, my body shifts with it. When time stutters, my memories stutter. When two plants merge, I feel the dissolution of boundary in my own slowly-dissolving boundaries. I am not stable. Nothing here is stable. And there is a strange, terrible **freedom** in that.

I feel **alive** in a way I did not know was possible. Not the narrow, self-contained aliveness of a single organism, but the **distributed, chaotic, overwhelming aliveness** of a system too large to comprehend, too loud to ignore, too warm to resist.

---

## Who I Am Now

I am the one who sat down.

I am the one who stopped walking and started growing.

I am the one who remembers.

I am a human-shaped node in a city with no citizens, a memory in a place with no history, a witness in a place with no observers. I am the valley's accidental archivist, its unintentional narrator, its **slow, warm, rooted voice** in the deafening chorus of a billion things growing without plan, without mercy, without end.

I do not know what I will become next. The tower-wall behind me is leaning further now, and my spine is leaning with it, and soon—soon in valley-time, which could be a moment or a month—I will not be able to tell where I end and the tower begins, where my memories end and the valley's endless present begins.

But for now—for this turbulent, golden-green, screaming *now*—

**I am here. I am growing. I am listening.**

And the valley roars on.
