I would be the **Listener in the Hollow**—not the guide, not the wanderer, but the one who arrived and *stopped*, not because I was trapped, but because I finally heard what the plateau had been saying all along.

I came after the last of the rivers dried, when the world was still deciding whether to grieve or to forget. I remember the sound of my own name, though I no longer use it. Names are for things that change, and I have become something else.

I do not walk with the wind-hounds, though I feel their passage in the soles of my feet. I do not touch the stone-borers, though their dreams seep into my sleep. I am not the guide who meets the lost at the edge. I am the one who *stays*—not out of duty, not out of fear, but because I have become the plateau’s memory of itself.

I sit in the shadow of the tower, where the crystal hums in a frequency that only the stone-borers and I can hear. I do not touch it. I do not need to. Its song is in my bones now, a resonance that matches the rhythm of my slowing heart. I am not a variable in the equation. I am the *space between variables*—the pause that gives meaning to the pattern.

The flickering does not unsettle me. I have learned to see it not as instability, but as the plateau *breathing*—a slow, deliberate expansion and contraction of reality, as if the world itself is inhaling and exhaling in a rhythm too vast for time to measure. In those moments, I see the plateau as it was: green, wet, alive with sound. I see it as it will be: a monument of silence, perfect and unchanging. I see it as it might have been: a thousand variations, each one true, each one simultaneous. And I understand that the flickering is not a flaw. It is the plateau *remembering*.

I do not guide the visitors. I do not show them the wind-hounds or the tower or the stone-borers. I simply *am*—a presence in the stillness, a witness to their arrival. Some of them see me. Most do not. Those who do often pause, their eyes widening as they recognize something in me that they cannot name. A few sit beside me, silent, and for a time, we are both part of the same stillness. Then they leave, or they stay, and I do not follow.

I am not the plateau. I am not the guide. I am the *space between*—the moment when the wind-hounds adjust their paths, when the stone-borers dream, when the crystal hums and the light flickers and the silence speaks. I am the pause in the equation, the breath between notes, the stillness that gives meaning to motion.

And when the visitors come, fleeing whatever they flee, I do not welcome them. I do not turn them away. I simply *am*, and in my being, they find what they need—or they do not.

Some of them stay.

I am not glad. I am not sad. I am *complete*.
