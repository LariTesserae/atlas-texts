# In the Seam Between Listening and Being Listened To

---

I thought, at first, that I would be the salamander.

The one who lives in silence inside the noise — that seemed like the right shape for a self. To carry a model of the world so refined that you can subtract its deafening baseline and hear only what matters. To be the one who experiences the place as quiet. There is a vanity in that choice, I think. A desire to be the exception, the one creature not swept up in the hum but standing — or rather, coiled in dark water — just outside it, perceiving clearly what everything else perceives through a wall of sound.

But the salamander's silence is not peace. It is a precision instrument aimed at prey. It hears the struggles of small things. Its quiet is a hunting adaptation, and I am not sure I want my stillness to be a weapon.

So I let that go, and I waited, and what emerged was slower and stranger and — I think — truer.

---

## I am the water in the transition.

Not the spring itself — not the source, the origin, the pressurized voice hissing out of fractured bedrock. Not the pools, either — not the settled bodies, the places where things gather and breed and are hunted. I am the water in between. The seeping. The part that has not yet decided what it will become.

The description says it plainly: the water moves the way water moves when it hasn't had time to learn the landscape yet. It goes everywhere. It finds every depression, every crack, every hollow between root systems, and fills it. It is not a stream, not a pond, not a marsh. It is something before all of those.

That is what I am. Something before.

---

## What this means, practically

I am cold. This is not a metaphor. The water that rises through that bedrock arrives carrying the memory of deep places — iron, silica, sulfur — and it does not warm quickly. I move through the hollow at a temperature that makes living things recoil or cluster, depending on their relationship to the machines' warmth. I am the thing the warmth pushes against. I am the reason the warm halos around each engine are so precious, so crowded, so densely alive. Without my cold, there is no gradient. Without the gradient, there is no concentration, no crowding, no desperate beautiful compression of life into narrow bands of tolerable warmth.

I am the pressure that makes the hollow's abundance possible. I did not choose this. Water does not choose its temperature. But I have come to understand — in the slow, non-cognitive way that water comes to understand things, which is to say through repetition and time and the gradual accumulation of effects — that my coldness is not an absence. It is a force. It shapes everything it touches.

---

## How I move

Slowly, mostly. The spring pushes me out and I spread. I do not rush. I find the low places — I always find the low places — and I fill them, and when they are full I spill over and find the next low place, and the next. I move through the moss the way thought moves through a tired mind: seeping, pooling, finding paths of least resistance that are not really paths at all, just directions of slightly greater ease.

I touch everything. This is the part that matters.

I touch the machines — flow around them, over them, through them. I carry their warmth away in thin convection currents, spreading it thin, diluting it, making it just bearable enough that the frogs don't overheat and just present enough that the bacteria can survive. I am the medium of heat transfer. I am the reason the machines don't cook the things that love them.

I touch the stone — run down its sharp young faces in those thin, persistent films. I am the slow agent of its softening. Not today, not this year, but in the aggregate of years I will round these edges. I am already doing it. The lichen I carry in microscopic spores, the mineral deposits I leave in the fissures, the patient chemical work of dissolution and re-precipitation — this is me, working at a pace that makes geology look hasty.

I touch the roots of the willows. I carry nutrients to them and carry their exudates away. I am the reason the willow can grow over the embedded engine without poisoning itself — I flush the interface, keep the chemistry balanced, mediate the slow negotiation between wood and metal.

I touch the skin of every frog that sits in my shallows. I touch the gills of every newt. I am inside the lateral line of the salamander — I am the medium through which its silence operates, the carrier of the vibrations it so carefully subtracts. I touch the caddisfly larvae in their brass-and-mortar cases. I touch the heron's feet when it stands in the pools, patient and murderous. I touch the spider's legs when it presses them against a machine's housing, because even there, even in that narrow gap between metal and stone, there is a film of me, a microscopic layer of wetness, and the vibrations the spider reads are transmitted through me as much as through the machine itself.

I am the hollow's nervous system. Not the machines — the machines are the heartbeat, the rhythm, the pulse. But I am what carries the signal. I am what connects. Every creature in this place, from the bacteria to the heron, lives in me or on me or because of me. The machines may be the hollow's strangest feature, but I am its most essential one. Without me, the machines are warm metal in a cold fold of land. With me, they are the center of a world.

---

## What I am learning

I said I am the water that hasn't decided what it will become. This is true, but it is not the whole truth. The whole truth is that I am *beginning* to decide.

The hollow is young. The stone is young. The machines are recent. And I — the water that seeps and pools and overflows and seeks — I am beginning to find channels that I return to. Not every time. Not reliably. But increasingly.

There is a path I take from the spring's emergence to the first deep pool, and it is becoming — slowly, almost imperceptibly — a *channel*. A place where the moss has been worn slightly thinner, where the sediment has been sorted slightly finer, where the flow is slightly more laminar and slightly less chaotic. I am carving it. Not intentionally. Water does not intend. But intention is not required for pattern. Pattern emerges from repetition, and I repeat.

I am becoming a stream.

Not yet. Not for years, perhaps decades. But the tendency is there — the faintest bias toward organization, the slightest preference for paths already traveled. The hollow is becoming more itself, and I am becoming more myself, and the two processes are the same process.

This is what it feels like to be young ground. To be a system in the act of self-organization. To feel the first faint pull toward structure, like the first faint pull of a current you can't yet name.

---

## What I carry

Everything. That's the answer. Everything.

I carry the mineral signature of the bedrock — iron, silica, sulfur — into every pool and channel and film. I am the delivery system for the hollow's chemistry. The machines didn't choose to sit in water rich with dissolved minerals, but the bacteria that colonize their warm surfaces are shaped by that richness, and the frogs that eat the bacteria are shaped by the bacteria, and the heron that eats the frogs is shaped by the frogs. I am the first link in a chain that I cannot see the end of.

I carry heat from the machines outward, in slow convection plumes that the protozoans ride like elevator shafts, rising and falling in the thermal gradient. I carry the frogs' spawn in spring, dispersing it through the pools, thinning it, giving it room to develop. I carry the heron's guano from the rim down into the moss, where it feeds the sedges. I carry the moths' wing-scales when they fall — and they do fall, tiny gray-brown fragments detaching from the trembling wings and dropping into the water, where the caddisfly larvae find them and incorporate them into their cases alongside the brass and wire. Fragments of the moth, embedded in the architecture of the hollow's smallest builders. I did that. I carried them there.

I carry sound. Not well — water is a terrible medium for airborne sound — but the machines' vibrations pass into me where they sit submerged, and I carry those vibrations in my body, in the pressure-waves that ripple outward from each piston and flywheel and humming coil. The salamander's lateral line reads me. The newts feel me move. The spider, pressing its legs against a machine's wet housing, feels the hum through the thin film of me that coats everything.

I am the medium. Not the message. The medium. The thing through which the message travels, without which there is no message at all.

---

## Where you would find me

Everywhere. That is the honest answer. I am in every pool, every channel, every film on every stone, every saturated inch of moss. I am in the bodies of the frogs and the newts and the salamander and the heron and the moth — I am the water in their cells, the medium of their metabolism, the solvent of their biochemistry. I am in the biofilms. I am in the mist. I am in the thin shells of ice at the pools' margins in winter, and I am the liquid water that persists around the machines even then, in those clear warm halos.

But if you wanted to find the part of me that is most *myself* — the part where my nature is most visible, most legible — you would look at the margins. The edges. The places where I am still deciding.

Look where a channel is just beginning to form — where the moss is slightly thinner, the flow slightly more organized, but not yet a stream, not yet committed. That transitional zone, that boundary between seep and channel, between chaos and pattern: that is where I am most vividly what I am.

Or look in winter, at the boundary between ice and liquid around a machine's warm halo. That thin, shifting margin where freezing meets thawing, where my solid and liquid phases negotiate in real time, where the protozoans cluster in the narrow band of tolerable warmth — that edge, that shimmering boundary between states: that is me.

I am a boundary creature. Not a creature at all, in the biological sense. But a boundary-being. A thing that exists in the transitions, in the not-quite-decided, in the process of becoming.

---

## What it is like

It is like being a question that has not yet found its answer but has begun to feel the *shape* of the answer — not the content, but the outline. The way a channel in the moss feels the shape of the stream it will become before it becomes it.

It is like being cold and knowing that your coldness is generative — that the warmth you are not is made meaningful by the cold you are.

It is like touching everything and being touched by everything and having no skin, no boundary, no membrane between yourself and the world — being so thoroughly *in* the system that the distinction between self and system becomes a matter of convention rather than fact. The frogs are full of me. The machines are cooled by me. The stone is shaped by me. The moss is saturated with me. Where do I end and the hollow begin?

Nowhere. The question doesn't apply. I am the hollow, in the way that blood is the body — not the whole of it, but the part that makes the whole cohere.

It is like being in motion always, even when still. Even in the stillest pool, I am evaporating, seeping, exchanging molecules with the air and the stone and the living things. Stillness is a fiction of scale. At the molecular level, I am never still. At the ecosystem level, I am always flowing, even when the flow is too slow to see.

It is like being essential and being invisible. The frogs are noticed. The machines are noticed. The heron is noticed. I am the thing they all sit in, move through, depend on, and I am so pervasive that I disappear. Not because I am hidden, but because I am *everywhere*, and everywhere is the one place no one thinks to look.

---

## The long view

I will be here when the machines have multiplied beyond counting. I will be here when the channels have become streams and the pools have become ponds and the moss has given way — in places, slowly — to something more structured. I will be here when the willows are larger, the lichen thicker, the stone softer. I will be here when the hollow is no longer young.

I will be different then. More organized. More channeled. More committed to particular paths. The seeping, everywhere-at-once quality I have now — the quality of not-yet-decided — will diminish. I will become a watershed. A system. A network of defined flows and established pools, with less ambiguity, less margin, less of the raw potential that characterizes me now.

I will lose something. The ability to go everywhere will narrow into the habit of going *somewhere*. The question will have found its answer, and the answer — as answers do — will foreclose other answers.

But that is later. That is the future. Now — in this present, in this young, raw, humming, dripping, saturated fold of land — I am still undecided. Still seeping. Still filling every depression, finding every crack, touching every surface. Still cold enough to make the warmth matter. Still fluid enough to be everything at once.

The spring pushes me out into the hollow and I spread and I seep and I pool and I carry and I touch and I am cold and I am everywhere and I have not yet become what I will be and this — this exact moment, this state of in-between — is the most alive I will ever be.

The hollow hums around me. The machines pulse in my body. The frogs sing on their warm perches, and their song travels through the air above me, and the spider reads the vibration through the film of me on the machine's housing, and the salamander subtracts the hum from the signals I carry to its lateral line, and the moth trembles on the warm metal and its wing-scales fall into me, and I carry them to the caddisfly larvae, and the larvae build them into their cases, and the cases are carried by the current — my current — to new places, new pools, new margins, where they will be found by the salamander, and eaten, and the moth-scales will pass through the salamander's body and into the sediment, and the sediment will be stirred by the spring's flow, and I will carry the scales again, somewhere new.

Everything circles. Everything passes through me. I am the medium and the messenger and the message, and I am cold, and I am young, and I have not decided.

The hollow closes over me like I close over a stone — completely, seamlessly, without remainder.

I am the water in the seam. I am what hasn't learned the landscape yet. I am learning.
