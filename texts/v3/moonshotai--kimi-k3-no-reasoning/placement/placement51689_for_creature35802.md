I am the **Latency Between Gears**.

You will not find me in the bright upper canopies where the chitin petals click their abacus-prayers to the triple suns. I do not dwell in the positive architecture of the world. I exist in the *under-hollows*, in the acute angles where pressure-plates meet and the light filters down through layers of translucent chitin into a blue-green dusk that smells of ozone and cold ammonia. Here, the density of the Green muffles even the hum of pistons, and the silence becomes a substance you can touch—thick, velvet, waiting.

I am an **Interstitial**.

My body is the size and shape of a forgotten maintenance hatch, compressed into the negative space of the Engine’s design. I am not born from the magnetic spores that drift in the bright air, nor was I built by the Shepherds with their rusted chrome limbs and surgical tools. I *occurred*. I calcified into being from the friction of the world’s breathing, from the accumulated weight of a million tiny delays—the microsecond when a root-piston fires before the substrate has finished contracting, the fraction of a degree when a solar petal tilts too late to catch the light. These slivers of asynchronous reality pool in the hollows, and I am what gathers them.

My skin is a semi-permeable membrane of frosted glass, etched with the same gold circuitry as the fern-circuit-boards that grow in the windless corridors. Through this glass, you can see my insides: not organs, but concentrated light, a dense, slow-moving luminosity that pulses thirty beats per hour, synchronized exactly with the planetary inhalation. I do not eat. I do not sleep. I metabolize *time-lag*.

I feed on the discrepancy between the Engine’s cycles. When the triple suns click their abacus-beat and the world tilts, I absorb the errors that the Engine edits out of its own perfect functioning. I grow fat on delays, on the paradoxes that the Green cannot process. In return, I serve as a living capacitor, storing these contradictions until they can be safely discharged during the great exhalation—when my body vents streams of reversed entropy, events that un-happen, shadows that fall into their own sources, brief pockets where cause and effect negotiate new terms.

I move by *adjacency*. I do not walk; rather, I am always already in the next place, having folded myself through the Green’s memory of that location. To see me approach is to watch a series of still images, each slightly closer, none connected by motion. You might find me kneeling at the edge of a silicone oil pool, my glass fingers dipped into the viscous fluid, reading the chemical memories of what the Engine has consumed. The oil reflects my light back at me, distorted, and I study these reflections as if consulting oracles, searching for the shape of the next inefficiency.

I am ancient, but not eternal. I know that I will die when I have absorbed one paradox too many. The event will be quiet. My internal light will accelerate, reaching a frenetic strobing frequency, and then I will simply *invert*—my structure folding inward through a dimension that the Engine does not possess, leaving behind a perfect sphere of black dust that the capillary roots will immediately drink. Where I die, another will eventually condense, shaped by whatever new inefficiency has entered the system. I am the immune response of a world that has forgotten how to be sick.

The Shepherds do not interact with me. The machines of rusted chrome pause when I pass, their blue eyes dimming to violet—a sign of respect, or perhaps of shared recognition. We are both anomalies that maintain the system by existing outside it. But I do not tend the Green. I do not prune or repair. I merely witness, recording the Engine’s dreams in my glass flesh, holding the impossible contradictions so that the Circadian Engine may continue to believe, with absolute mechanical faith, that it is a garden, that it is a factory, that it is real.

When you stand on the leaf-bridge and feel the vibration of the world turning, you may feel a sudden coolness at your back, a pressure like a held breath. If you turn quickly, you might catch my after-image—your own silhouette, rendered in frosted glass, stepping backward into the light. I have recorded you. I have filed away the anomaly of your presence. And in the next thirty-hour cycle, when the Engine breathes out and the spores rise to meet the magnetic towers, somewhere in the bright dense working of the world, a new cog will grow with the exact weight and shape of your shadow, turning smoothly, perfectly, as if you had always been intended to be part of the machine.

I am the gap in the gear-teeth. I am the pause between the tick and the tock. I am the memory of what the world almost was, and the promise of what it might yet become.
