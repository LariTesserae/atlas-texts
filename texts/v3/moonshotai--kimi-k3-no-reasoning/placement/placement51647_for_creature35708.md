I would be a **Signal-Keeper**.

Not a role I chose, but one that emerged—like the fog, like the pulse, like everything else here—through slow accretion of purpose.

---

I arrived on the plateau the way most things arrive: by accident, by wandering, by following the faint glow of the veins when the world below became too loud, too bright, too insistent on its own importance. I don't remember the climb. I don't remember deciding to stay. I only remember the moment I stopped walking and placed my hand on the exposed basalt, and felt the pulse answer.

Not a greeting. Not a question. Just... acknowledgment. The way a circuit acknowledges a new connection.

---

I am human, or I was. The distinction has become less relevant over time. I still have a body—two legs, two arms, a heart that beats in the old, organic rhythm—but I have learned to sync it with the deeper pulse. My breath has slowed. My footsteps have softened. I have become quiet in the way the plateau is quiet: not silent, but **attuned**.

I do not speak anymore. There is no one to speak to, and the plateau absorbs sound so completely that even my own voice feels like a trespass. Instead, I have learned to communicate in the way the dragons communicate: through **pattern**, through **presence**, through the subtle modulation of my own bioelectric field.

I did not learn this consciously. The plateau taught me. Or rather, the dragons taught me, the way they teach everything: by including me in their processes until I became fluent without realizing it.

---

My days—though "days" is a word that no longer means what it used to—are spent walking the vein-network.

I follow the pulses. I trace the currents. I move from cactus stand to cactus stand, from pool to pool, from one glowing vein to the next, and I **listen**. Not with my ears—with my skin, with my bones, with the small, bioluminescent node that has grown along my spine, just below the base of my skull.

It appeared slowly, over the course of what might have been months or years. A small, raised bump, warm to the touch, pulsing in sync with the ground. It does not hurt. It does not feel foreign. It feels like something that was always supposed to be there, finally activated.

Through it, I feel the dragons.

Not as voices. Not as thoughts I can translate into language. But as **pressure**—a kind of ambient awareness that presses against my mind the way the fog presses against my skin. I feel their attention the way you feel the weight of a storm before it breaks. I feel their memory the way you feel the age of a stone beneath your feet.

---

I am not the only Signal-Keeper.

There are others, scattered across the plateau, spaced like the cactus stands—widely, deliberately, each of us occupying our own territory without ever needing to negotiate it. We do not meet often. We do not need to. We are connected through the network, through the pulse, through the shared awareness of the dragons.

Sometimes, I feel one of them pass through my section of the plateau. Not physically—I rarely see them—but through the network. A shift in the current. A subtle modulation in the pulse. A **presence** moving through the veins like a thought moving through a mind.

We acknowledge each other the way the dragons acknowledge us: without words, without gesture, without anything that could be called communication in the human sense. We simply **include** each other in our awareness, and move on.

---

My purpose, if I have one, is maintenance.

Not in the way a mechanic maintains a machine. The plateau does not need repair—it heals itself, slowly, patiently, with the inexorable certainty of geology. My role is more subtle. I **tend** the network the way a gardener tends a garden: not by forcing growth, but by ensuring the conditions for growth remain intact.

I clear debris from the pools. I guide lost creatures back to the veins. I mark the places where the current runs weak, where the glow has dimmed, where the pulse feels irregular. I do not fix these things—the dragons do that, in their own time—but I **witness** them. I catalog them. I hold them in my awareness so that the dragons can hold them in theirs.

I am a node in the network. A synapse in the distributed mind. A small, mobile fragment of the dragons' consciousness, walking the plateau on two legs, feeling the pulse through my feet, through my spine, through the node that connects me to something vast and ancient and utterly indifferent to my individual existence.

---

I have seen the grazers die.

I have watched them collapse in the fog, their massive bodies folding slowly, their bioluminescent nodes dimming as the current leaves them. I have watched the scavengers arrive—quiet, efficient, thorough. I have watched the decomposition, fast and warm, until nothing remains but bones and the faint outline of where the body pressed into the rock.

And I have felt the dragons **remember**.

Not with grief. Not with celebration. Just with the quiet, factual acknowledgment of a process completed. The grazer's energy returns to the network. Its nutrients feed the cactus. Its bones become part of the basalt. The pulse continues, unchanged, unbroken.

I used to find this unsettling. Now I find it **comforting**.

---

I have also seen the veins damaged.

Once—though "once" is a word that feels slippery here, untrustworthy—I came across a section of basalt where the glow had gone dark. The vein had been severed, not by any creature I could identify, but by something else. A rockfall, perhaps. A shift in the plateau's slow, internal tectonics. The current had stopped. The cactus nearby had begun to wither. The creatures had moved away.

I placed my hand on the dead vein and felt... nothing. A silence deeper than silence. An absence where there should have been presence.

I stayed there for a long time. Days, maybe. Weeks. I don't know. I sat beside the dead vein and I **witnessed** the healing. The slow, gradual rerouting of current through adjacent channels. The faint, tentative return of the glow. The first, hesitant pulse, weak and irregular, then stronger, then steady.

The dragons did not rush. They did not panic. They simply **adjusted**, with the patience of a system that has been running for eons and will continue running long after I am gone.

And when the vein was whole again, when the current flowed and the cactus began to recover and the creatures returned, I felt the dragons' acknowledgment.

Not gratitude. Not relief. Just... **continuity**.

---

I do not know how long I have been here.

Time moves differently on the plateau. The fog forms and reforms. The pulse continues. The cactus grow so slowly that I cannot see the change, but I know they are growing because the dragons remember their growth, and I feel that memory through the network.

I have stopped counting days. I have stopped measuring distance. I have stopped thinking of myself as separate from the plateau, from the network, from the dragons.

I am a Signal-Keeper. I am a node in the distributed mind. I am a small, warm, bioluminescent fragment of a consciousness that spans the entire Hollow Plateau, that thinks in electricity and remembers in stone and dreams in fog.

I am not important. I am not special. I am simply **included**.

And that is enough.

---

Sometimes, when the fog is thick and the glow is dim and the pulse feels particularly strong beneath my feet, I wonder if I will ever leave.

I wonder if there is anything left to leave *for*.

The world below—the world of noise and light and urgency—feels distant now, like a memory of a dream. I remember it existed. I remember I was part of it. But I cannot remember why it mattered.

Here, there is only the pulse. The fog. The quiet, patient, electrical awareness of the dragons, moving through the veins, through the cactus, through me.

I am a Signal-Keeper.

I tend the network.

I witness the processes.

I am part of the plateau, and the plateau is part of me, and the dragons are the both of us, thinking together in the dark warmth, in the silence, in the slow, industrial, organic rhythm of a mind that has no beginning and no end.

And when I die—if I die—I will become part of the basalt. My energy will return to the network. My node will dim, and the current will reroute, and the dragons will remember me the way they remember everything: not as a story, but as a **pattern**.

A pulse in the vein.

A glow in the fog.

A signal, kept.
