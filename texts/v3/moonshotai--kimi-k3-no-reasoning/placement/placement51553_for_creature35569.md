The descent is a subtraction of self.

I come through the volcanic vent not as a body enters a space, but as a stone enters water—except here, the water is heat, and the stone is learning to dissolve. The basalt closes around my shoulders, that narrowing throat of black glass, and I feel the cool moisture of my own skin—my history of rivers, of rain, of blood that is mostly ocean—begin to steam away. By the time I drop the final meter into the root-chamber, I am lighter by the weight of my former humidity. I am becoming possible here.

I stand in the center, where the pewter shaft of light does not fall, but simply is, frozen at its eternal angle. The sound hits me first, a physical weight: the clicking of the *skitters* like a thousand tiny hammers striking anvils, the orange sparks popping in the violet dark of the *kell* rosettes, and beneath it all, that drone—the *drakes* humming their single, unbroken chord that vibrates in my teeth, my sternum, the hollows of my skull. The air tastes of copper and ozone, thick with spores that drift like slow, green snow. I breathe, and my lungs immediately begin to weep wax, coating themselves in the same secretion that glosses the leather leaves. My voice, when I try to speak, emerges altered—lower, resonant, buzzing with the frequency of the gliding reptiles that circle above the warm floor.

I am not Vess. I arrived too late for the slow condensation, the century-long hollowing in the eastern wall. I came from the wet world above, carrying my water in fragile vessels of cell and vein, and the chamber is correcting that. It is teaching me the dryness.

They are here, the seven who move, and I feel them before I see them—a pressure in the bone, a grinding of stone on stone. One stands near the eastern dwellings, its grey-black hide cracked into hexagons that mirror the cooling basalt above. It is grinding *kell* leaves in a floor-mortar with an obsidian stone, moving in a circle so slow that I initially mistake it for a statue, for one of the geode-casts in the reliquary domes. But it turns its head—the motion taking minutes, mineral dust falling from the creases of its neck—and its lidless eyes, filmed with transparent wax, reflect my own diminished light back at me.

I approach with the high-stepping gait I am learning, careful to avoid the spiral rosettes. The *skitters* swarm my ankles, their transparent exoskeletons clicking harmlessly against my skin, which is already hardening, already developing the powdery patina of the walls. A *drake* lands upon my shoulder—heavy, hot, its scales flat against my collarbone to drink my residual warmth—and begins to hum directly into the bone-conduction chamber of my skull.

The Vess does not speak. It touches its forehead to mine.

The resonance passes bone to bone, and I am flooded with the memory-forward of the chamber. I see—no, I *remember*—where I will be. I see myself standing at the mortar in three hundred years, my arms raised in the same posture of listening, crystalline lichens blooming from my shoulders in fractal plates of amethyst and quartz. I see the exact configuration of the *skitter* herds that will click around my feet in a thousand years, unchanged. I see the moment when I will finally curl into the mortar pit and release the last of my stolen moisture, the heavy sloshing sphere in my chest that I carry even now, the geode of self that grows heavier with each decade of stillness.

The Vess withdraws. Fine powder falls between us. It returns to its grinding—hours for a single rotation, a drop of hydration absorbed over ten years—and I understand that I am to be the eighth. Not born, but condensed. Not drowned, but precipitated.

I find my place near the center, where the light from the finger-wide fissure casts its unchanging shadow. I raise my arms slightly, palms upward, feeling the heat from the bedrock rise through the stone of my feet. The *drakes* gather on my shoulders, a living, humming crest. The *skitters* click around my toes, sparking against the mineral hardness. The spores settle on my skin, that fine green snow, and begin to root.

I do not close my eyes. They are filming over already, wax-sealed, permanent.

I begin to listen, very slowly, to the heat. I begin to remember where I have always been: here, in the loud, hot, crowded emptiness, complete unto myself. The equation is solved. The variables are becoming constants.

I am the moisture that is learning to be stone. I am the next silence in the noise, the next stillness in the motion. I am home, exactly so, forever, in the dark and the heat.
