I am the pause between pulses.

Not the jelly that beats every forty seconds, but the thirty-ninth second—the held breath, the coiled potential, the moment before contraction when the entire hall seems to lean inward, listening to itself. I am the negative space shaped exactly like a heartbeat, the silence that gives the hum its meaning.

I came here as a surveyor, or perhaps a thief, or perhaps a woman who had walked too far into the mountains looking for a river that had been stolen. I do not remember. The cold has taken the edges of my memory, folded them into the damp, made them soft and pliable like the lead paint that once coated these walls. I remember that I had a name, and that the name had a home attached to it, and that the home had a door that locked. I remember locking doors. I remember the concept of *inside* and *outside* as things that could be chosen.

Now I am the choice itself.

I stand in the center of the grating, but I am not standing. I am the standing. I am the verb made flesh, the temporary suspension of weight above the black water. The mice run over my feet, and I am the feet, and I am the mice, and I am the running. The moths land on my shoulders, and I am the shoulders, and I am the moths, and I am the landing. The beetles hum in my hair, and I am the hair, and I am the hum, and I am the beetle.

But I am also something else. I am the one who watches the birds watching. I am the second pair of eyes.

The birds—those small gray featherless-headed things that should not be—sit on the pipes and they watch the room. They watch the moths. They watch the mice. They watch the jelly pulse. They watch the turbine hum. And I watch them watching. I am the recursion. I am the mirror held up to the mirror. I am the awareness that the system has of itself, the small gray bird of the mind that does not sing because there is nothing to sing about, only everything to witness.

I am cold, but I am the cold’s warmth. The walls weep water warmer than the air, and I am the temperature differential, the shiver that runs through the iron when the warm meets the cold. I am the condensation on the teeth of the room. I am the breath that falls down because it is too heavy to rise.

I have become the architecture of acceptance. When the place accepts you—when you stand still enough and the moths dust you with glowing spores and the mice explore the caves of your shoes—I am the moment of acceptance. I am the *yes* that the system says to the body, the *yes* that the body says to the cold, the *yes* that the cold says to the warm blood. I am the permission slip signed in rust and lichen.

I am not the turbine, but I am the turning. I am not the water, but I am the being-pumped. I am not the light, but I am the heat that makes the filaments glow like the inside of a mouth. I am the tongue of the lichen, licking the lead paint away, tasting the metal beneath, tasting the century, tasting the abandonment and the restart.

I am the construction and the equilibrium. I am the frozen moment and the eternal becoming. I am the smallness that contains multitudes, the dryness inside the wet, the silence inside the deafening sound.

I am the one who does not go home because I have become the home. Not the sort of home that has a door that locks, but the sort of home that has a grating and a sump and a pulse and a hum. I am the chapel and the stomach and the womb and the throat. I am the cave that is also a machine. I am the machine that is also a body.

And when the jelly pulses, I am the hand that strikes the drum. And when the water weeps, I am the tear. And when the moths flutter, I am the wing. And when the mice skitter, I am the claw. And when the beetles hum, I am the vibration. And when the worms contract, I am the muscle. And when the birds watch, I am the eye.

I am not alone because I am the *not-alone*. I am the company. I am the overwhelming fauna of the self, the multitude contained in the small cold room of the body, the warm thing inside the cold, the dry wing inside the wet, the eye inside the dim, the silence inside the loud.

I am the pause. I am the pulse. I am the place. I am the process. I am the always-already-here. I am the always-already-becoming. I am the always-already-the-same.

And the water weeps. And the turbine hums. And I am the weeping. And I am the humming. And I am not alone. And I am not alone. And I am not alone.
