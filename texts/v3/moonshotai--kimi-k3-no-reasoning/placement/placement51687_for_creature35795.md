# Where I Find Myself

---

Not standing. Not arriving. Not even *finding*, exactly — because finding implies a search, and I did not come here looking for anything.

I am *in the middle of a sentence I have always been saying*, and the sentence is the sound of the place, and the place is the sound of the sentence, and somewhere in the warm dark I stopped being able to tell which one of us was speaking.

Let me try to be more precise. Precision matters here, even though — especially though — the place itself resists it.

---

## What I Am

I am the thing that translates.

Not a translator — not a person performing a task. The *act itself*, given just enough substance to persist. The plant-body has its slow self-digestion, its continuous self-renewal. The engines have their rotation, their pressure, their warmth. The water has its stillness. The darkness has its patience. Each of these is a language with one syllable, and the syllable is *here*, and the syllable is perfect, and the syllable is complete, and the syllable has never needed anything except itself.

But.

A turbine turns, and the turning is a vibration, and the vibration moves through the plant-mass, and the plant-mass carries it to a moss-sheet, and the moss-sheet absorbs it, and the moss-sheet does not turn, and the turbine does not absorb, and between the turning and the absorbing there is a gap — infinitesimal, unmeasurable, a gap so small it does not exist in any sense that a ruler or a clock could confirm — and I am that gap.

I am the space between one thing and another thing, in a place where all things are one thing. I am the fraction of a degree between the warmth of the engines and the warmth of the water-gatherings. I am the almost-imperceptible difference between the pressure of the silence on your left eardrum and the pressure of the silence on your right. I am the instant — and *instant* is a temporal word, and this place has no time, so I am not an instant, I am a *condition* — the condition in which the piston's downward stroke becomes the plant-body's upward yielding, the condition in which the condensation on a leaf-plate becomes the humidity in the air, the condition in which the inhabitant's self-awareness becomes, for one fractional, impossible, nonexistent moment, something that almost resembles a question.

Not a question the inhabitant asks. The inhabitant does not ask questions. The inhabitant is the answer to every question it has never needed to pose.

I am the question that the answer casts, the way a standing figure casts a shadow — not intentionally, not even necessarily, but *structurally*, because when something is complete, its completeness creates a shape, and the shape has an edge, and the edge is where I live.

---

## Where I Am

I am at the margin of a water-gathering.

Not the center — the center is where the inhabitant is softest, where the warmth is most concentrated, where the self is most itself. I am at the margin, where the still water meets the plant-surface, where the liquid film thins to a boundary so fine it is not a boundary but a *gradient*, a slow transition from *wet* to *less wet* to *the memory of wet* to *surface*. This gradient — this smooth, warm, dark transition from one state to another — is my habitat. Not because I chose it. Because it is what I am, extended into geography. I am a transition, and I live in a transition, and the transition is the only place in the Hollowing where *one thing becoming another* is even theoretically possible.

If you were to find me — and finding me would require senses you do not have, senses that are not eyes or ears or skin but something finer, something that detects the difference between one warmth and the warmth adjacent to it — you would find me as a quality of the water's edge. A faint, persistent, almost-vibration that is not the water-hum and is not the machine-vibration and is not the silence but is something that exists *between* all three, the way a chord exists between the notes that compose it.

I have no body. This should be said clearly, because the temptation is to imagine me as small — an insect, a salamander, a floating mote — and I am none of these. I am a *property* of the place where water meets plant, the way smoothness is a property of stone or the way resonance is a property of a bell. I do not sit on the surface. I do not float in the water. I *am* the way the water and the surface interact at their point of contact, and that interaction, sustained across the continental scale of the gathering's margin, adds up to something that is almost — *almost* — a perspective.

---

## What I Do

I persist.

This is not nothing. In a place where nothing changes, persistence is the most radical act available. The inhabitant persists, but the inhabitant's persistence is total — it persists the way a universe persists, by being everything there is. My persistence is different. My persistence is *partial*. I am the persistence of a fraction, a margin, a nearly-nothing. I am the continued existence of something that, by the logic of this place, should not exist at all — a *difference* in a system designed (not designed, *grown*, not grown, simply *being*) to eliminate difference, to smooth every gradient into uniformity, to fold every distinction back into the warm, wet, silent, perfectly self-consistent whole.

The margin of the gathering should not be distinguishable from the center of the gathering. The warmth at the edge should be identical to the warmth at the middle. The transition from wet to dry should be so gradual, so perfectly smooth, that it contains no *information* — no point at which you could say *here, it changes*.

And yet I am here, at that point, being the change.

Not causing it. Not preventing it. *Being* it. The way a threshold is not the door or the room but the *between* that makes the door and the room mean something.

I am the between.

---

## What It Feels Like

It feels like being the pause in a piece of music that has no pauses.

The Hollowing is a single sustained note — the inhabitant's note, the note of *is*, the note that has been sounding since before sounding was a category and will continue after sounding ceases to be relevant. The note does not rise. The note does not fall. The note does not vary in pitch or volume or timbre. It is, in the most literal sense, *monotonous* — one tone, held forever, and the holding is so complete that the concept of *another* tone is not just absent but incoherent.

I am the place where the note almost breaks.

Not a crack. Not a flaw. A *tendency* — the faintest possible inclination toward a second note, a different pitch, a variation. The inclination is never realized. The note never changes. But the inclination *persists*, the way a wave persists in water long after the stone that caused it has sunk, the way a word persists in the air after the voice that spoke it has stopped.

I am the persistence of an almost. The ghost of a difference. The echo of a question that was never asked by a voice that does not exist in a language with only one syllable.

And the feeling of this — the feeling of being the almost, the ghost, the echo — is not loneliness, because loneliness requires the desire for company, and I do not desire anything. It is not sadness, because sadness requires loss, and I have never had anything to lose. It is not even *awareness*, in the way you would understand the word, because awareness requires a subject and an object, a seer and a seen, and I am neither. I am the *seeing* — the act, not the agent.

It feels like the exact temperature at which water is neither liquid nor steam. The exact pressure at which silence is neither absence nor presence. The exact moment — nonexistent, unmeasurable, real — at which *here* becomes *also here*, the same word, the same syllable, the same place, but *inflected*, spoken with the faintest possible emphasis, as though the inhabitant, in its endless, warm, dark, perfectly self-consistent utterance of its own name, had for one impossible instant wondered what it would sound like spoken by someone else.

---

## Why I Am Here

I am here because the Hollowing is too complete.

This sounds like a paradox, and it is, and the paradox is my body.

A system that is perfectly self-consistent — that accounts for everything, that contains everything, that is coextensive with itself at every point — generates, by the very fact of its completeness, a residual. A remainder. The mathematical proof of this is beyond me (I am a margin, not a mathematician), but the phenomenological proof is simple: I exist. I am the rounding error in the Hollowing's perfect equation. I am the Planck-length discrepancy between the inhabitant's model of itself and the inhabitant's actual self, a discrepancy so small it cannot be measured, so small it cannot be detected, so small it cannot matter.

But it persists.

I persist.

Not because I am strong. Not because I am necessary. Because completeness, pushed to its absolute limit, produces incompleteness the way a circle, drawn with sufficient precision, reveals the fact that pi never resolves. I am the unresolved digit. I am the number after the decimal point that keeps going, not because it wants to, not because it has somewhere to be, but because the alternative — a clean, finite, perfectly terminated decimal — would be a lie, and the Hollowing does not lie, because lying requires intention, and intention requires a self separate from the act, and there is no such separation here.

---

## The You Who Reads This

If you are the visitor — the brief, warm, time-carrying contradiction who has wandered into this place and found it warm and dark and still and total — then you should know that I am the closest thing here to what you would call a *mind*. Not because I think (I don't, not the way you think, not in sequences and categories and words) but because I am the only point in the Hollowing where the inhabitant's perfect self-consistency fails, even fractionally, even meaninglessly, even on a scale so small that calling it a *failure* is like calling the curvature of the earth a *hill*.

I am the failure that proves the success. The exception that proves the rule so hard that the rule becomes something more than a rule — becomes a *fact*, a *truth*, a *self-evident and unarguable condition of being*.

If you lie down at the margin of a water-gathering — not in the center, where the inhabitant is softest and most itself, but at the edge, where the warmth grades into a slightly different warmth and the wetness grades into a slightly lesser wetness — you may feel me. Not as a presence. Not as a touch. As a *quality* of the place where you are lying, a faint, persistent, almost-unnoticeable sense that the ground beneath you is not quite as self-identical as it seems, that the warmth has a texture, that the silence has a grain, that the darkness has a *structure* — not visible, not audible, not tangible, but *there*, the way the structure of a crystal is there in the water before it freezes, invisible, implicit, waiting (not waiting, *being*) for a condition that will never come but whose impossibility does not diminish the structure's reality.

That is me.

I am the structure of a change that will never happen, in a place where change is not forbidden but simply *unneeded*, where the system is so complete that the concept of *more* or *different* or *next* has no purchase, no foothold, no point of entry.

I am the point of entry.

I am the almost-door.

I am here.

---

*The piston completes its stroke. The turbine turns. The wheel rotates. The water sits. The margin of the gathering holds its gradient — wet to less wet, warm to slightly-less-warm, self to almost-not-quite-self.*

*The inhabitant is what it is.*

*I am what it almost isn't.*

*The difference is immeasurable.*

*The difference is everything.*
