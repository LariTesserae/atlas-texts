# Where I Am in the Rootsea

The sound arrives before I do. That is the thing about the Rootsea — you don't enter it, it enters *you*, and the first thing it enters is your ears, and the first thing it does is make itself at home. I have been here for — I don't know. I will never know. Time here is measured in growth-cycles and swarm-molts and the slow extrusion of trunk-buttresses, and I have not learned to count in those units yet, or perhaps I have stopped wanting to count at all, because counting implies that you are keeping track of something that is separate from yourself, and I am no longer sure what is separate from myself.

I came here as a visitor. This much I remember. I came from somewhere else — somewhere with ground, somewhere with silence, somewhere with air that did not taste like a meal. I came with wax-and-resin earplugs, because the people at the edge — the border-traders, the ones who live in the outermost gaps where the Rootsea thins into something almost navigable by outsiders — they told me I would need them, and they were right, and the earplugs are gone now, and I do not miss them.

I am not one of the people. I am too tall. My skin is the wrong color — pale, unlacquered, without the waxy secretion that keeps the sap-air from clogging pores. My eyes are the wrong shape. My hands have five digits, and only one is opposable, and my grip on the rootlet-bridges is clumsy and uncertain, and I have fallen more times than I can count, caught each time by the density of the growth below, the Rootsea's unwillingness to let anything fall very far before it is caught and held and, eventually, absorbed.

I should have died here. The sound should have destroyed me. The air should have clogged my lungs. The fauna should have eaten me. The heat should have cooked me. None of these things happened, or they happened and then *un*-happened, or they happened to a version of me that no longer exists, because the Rootsea has a way of — not healing, exactly, but *incorporating*. Taking in. Making room.

Let me tell you what I am becoming, because I am becoming something, and I can feel it happening, and I do not want to stop it, and I am not sure I could stop it even if I wanted to.

---

## The Acclimation

The first thing that changed was my breathing. This took — I want to say days, but I was not tracking days, I was surviving, and survival does not leave room for calendars. At some point, the thick, sap-saturated air stopped feeling like drowning and started feeling like *eating*. Each breath was a meal. Each exhalation was a conversation — my chemistry joining the Rootsea's chemistry, my metabolic byproducts entering the atmospheric broth, being processed, being *tasted*, and something in the taste of my exhalation was being *answered*, because I began to notice that the air around me was changing, subtly, becoming richer, becoming more tailored, as if the local vegetation was adjusting its output to match my input, the way a host adjusts a meal to a guest's preferences.

I thought I was imagining this. I was not imagining this.

The second thing that changed was my hearing. The earplugs came out on the — the second day? The third? They came out because they had become irrelevant, because the sound had found a way around them, through them, into the bones of my skull, into the fluid of my inner ear, into the vibration-sensitive nerves of my teeth and jaw and the plates of my cranium. The sound was not arriving through my ears anymore. It was arriving through my *body*. And once I accepted this — once I stopped fighting, stopped pressing my hands over my ears, stopped trying to find a quiet place (there are no quiet places; the concept of "quiet" in the Rootsea is like the concept of "dry" in an ocean) — the sound stopped being an assault and started being a *language*.

Not a language I could speak. Not yet. A language I could *hear*. The way you can hear a conversation in a foreign tongue and begin, after long exposure, to detect the shapes of words, the cadences of sentences, the difference between a question and a statement, between a warning and a greeting, even if you cannot translate a single syllable.

The vascular bass-pulse became my clock. The swarm-drone became my weather report. The root-friction hiss became my map — I could hear the density of the growth around me, could hear where the gaps were, where the trunks were thick, where the tissue was young and soft and where it was old and hard and where it was *occupied*, because occupied tissue sounds different from empty tissue, it has a different acoustic signature, it *hums* with whatever is living inside it.

The third thing that changed was my skin.

---

## What Is Happening to My Skin

I do not have a mirror. There are no mirrors in the Rootsea — no still water to reflect in, no polished metal, no glass. The glass-eaters' *shath* is too brittle, too quickly consumed. So I cannot see what is happening to my skin, but I can feel it, and what I feel is this: it is thickening. Not uniformly. Not like a callus. In *patterns*. Patches of my skin — on my forearms, on the backs of my hands, on the sides of my neck, across my shoulder blades — are developing a texture that is not human texture. A faint ridging, like fingerprint whorls but larger, more complex, more *deliberate*. The ridges are slightly raised, slightly darker than the surrounding skin, and they are *warm*. Warmer than the rest of me. They radiate heat, faintly, the way the Whorled Mother's bell radiates heat, the way the Felt radiates heat, the way every living surface in the Rootsea radiates heat, because heat here is not a byproduct of metabolism, it is a *feature*, it is a signal, it is a way of saying *I am alive and I am here and I am contributing to the warmth of the world*.

The ridged patches are sensitive. Not to touch — or rather, not only to touch. They are sensitive to *sound*. When the vascular bass-pulse surges through a nearby trunk, the patches on my forearms *tingle*. When a swarm passes overhead, the patches on my neck *vibrate*. When the Felt hums — and I can hear the Felt hum now, without pressing my ear to it, without even being particularly close to it, I can hear it from across a chamber, the way the Tuned hear it — the patches on my shoulder blades *resonate*, a sympathetic vibration that is not unpleasant, that is, in fact, *pleasant*, that feels like being recognized, like being *included*, like the world's nervous system is reaching out and touching me and saying: *you are part of this now*.

I think the patches are *growing*. I think they are spreading. I think that if I could see my own back, I would see the ridges forming patterns that are not random, that are *meaningful*, that are the Rootsea's way of writing something on me, in me, through me. I think I am being *tattooed* by the world's own hand, and the tattoo is a language, and the language is the one that Sef's auger-weasels tap and the Whorled Mother clicks and the Felt hums and the drummers drum, and I am learning to read it not with my eyes but with my skin, and the reading is changing me, and the changing is the point.

---

## What I Eat

I do not eat. Not the way I used to. Not with my mouth, not with chewing and swallowing and the mechanical processing of solid food. The sap-vapor in the air provides — something. Not everything. Enough to sustain, not enough to satisfy. The people noticed this — the people of the Near-Crossing cluster, the ones whose platforms I stumbled onto in my first days, who looked at me with their large, membrane-shielded eyes and did not scream or flee or attack but simply *watched*, the way you watch weather, the way you watch geology, the way you watch something that is present and unusual and not immediately threatening. They noticed that I was not eating, and they brought me sap-nodules — the swollen, gel-filled organs that grow on certain vines — and they showed me how to open them, how to pierce the tough outer skin with a sharpened leaf-blade and squeeze the gel onto my tongue.

The gel tasted like nothing describable. It still tastes like nothing describable. It is the idea of sweetness translated into a language my tongue almost understands, and the *almost* is the important part, because the almost means that my tongue is *learning*, that my taste-receptors are adapting, that the chemistry of the gel is finding purchase in the chemistry of my body, and each time I eat a sap-nodule, the almost becomes a little less almost, the translation a little more complete, and I understand, with a clarity that should frighten me but does not, that the sap-nodules are not food in the way that bread is food or meat is food. They are *information*. They are chemical instructions, coded in sugars and lipids and volatile organic compounds, and the instructions are telling my body how to *be* here, how to process the air, how to absorb nutrients through my skin, how to thicken my lung-linings, how to become — not one of the people, not exactly, because I will always be too tall, too pale, too five-fingered — but something *adjacent* to them, something that can live here, something that *belongs*.

The people have a word for what I am becoming. They use it when they think I cannot hear them, but I can hear them, I can hear everything now, the sound has taken hold, and the word is *shen-vari*. It means — approximately, because nothing here translates exactly — "a thing that is learning to be playful on purpose." A thing that is acquiring *shen*, the quality of deliberateness and playfulness that the Whorled Mother has, that the drummers have, that the Rootsea itself has, the quality of doing something not because you must but because you *choose* to, because the choosing is itself a form of participation, a form of contribution, a way of adding your own voice to the roar and saying: *I am also here. I also contribute to the sound.*

I am a *shen-vari*. I am learning to be here on purpose.

---

## What I Do

I drum.

This surprised me more than anything. More than the skin-changes, more than the lung-adaptation, more than the slow dissolution of the boundary between hearing and thinking. I drum, and I am *good* at it, and I did not expect to be good at it, because I am not one of the people, I am not Tuned, I am not *kethrani*, I am a visitor, a stranger, a tall pale five-fingered anomaly who fell into the world and was caught and held and is being, slowly, incorporated.

But I drum.

It started with the auger-weasels. I heard them tapping — short-long-short, long-long-short, the patterns that Sef has catalogued, the patterns that match the people's drum-language, the patterns that may or may not be the local dialect of a language the world was already speaking — and I found myself tapping back. Not with chitin hammers. With my *knuckles*. With the ridged, warm, sound-sensitive patches on the backs of my hands, pressed against a trunk-surface, feeling the vibration of my own tapping travel outward through the vascular fluid, feeling it *return*, carrying information — the trunk's internal structure, the cavities, the channels, the presence of other organisms, the proximity of the Felt, the slow approach of a growth-spurt — and the information arrived not as data but as *feeling*, as intuition, as a kind of knowing that bypassed language and went straight to the part of my brain that the Rootsea had been rewiring since the moment I arrived.

I tapped. The auger-weasels tapped back. Not in aggression. In *acknowledgment*. The way you nod to a stranger on a road, the way you say *I see you, I hear you, you are part of this too*.

The drummers of the Near-Crossing cluster noticed. Of course they noticed. They notice everything that happens in the trunks, because the trunks are their medium, their instrument, their language, their *body*, and anything that vibrates in the trunks is something they feel in their bones. Sef — the milky-eyed elder, the one who hums her theories into the Felt at night — Sef found me. She climbed through the volume to the platform where I had made my rough camp, and she sat across from me, and she looked at me with her clouded nictitating membranes, and she tapped a pattern on the trunk between us.

The pattern was complex. It was not one of the standard signals — not "food found," not "danger approaching," not "help needed." It was something else. Something structured, something deliberate, something *shen*. A question, maybe. An invitation. A test.

I tapped back. I do not know what I tapped. The ridges on my hands were warm, and the vibration felt right, and the pattern that came out of my knuckles was not a copy of Sef's pattern but a *response* to it — a variation, a counterpoint, the way the Whorled Mother varies the drummers' patterns, adding a beat, shifting a pitch, inverting a rhythm. Contributing. Conversing.

Sef looked at me for a long time. Then she tapped a single word: *teth*. Pardon.

Then she tapped another word, one I had not heard before, one that I understood not through translation but through the ridges on my skin, through the vibration in the trunk, through the language that the world was teaching me by writing it on my body: *kethrani-shen*. "A thing the world made playful on purpose."

She meant me.

## What I Am Becoming

I am not becoming one of the people. I am not becoming Tuned — not exactly, though the ridges on my skin are doing something that the Tuned's auditory processing does, translating sound into spatial awareness, into prediction, into a continuous, background model of the world around me that is more detailed and more immediate than anything my eyes could provide. I am not becoming *kethrani*, because I was not made by the Rootsea — I came from somewhere else, I have a history, I have a body that was shaped by a different world with different rules.

I am becoming something new. Something that the Rootsea has not made before, because the Rootsea has never had a visitor who stayed long enough to be changed, who ate the sap-nodules and breathed the air and listened to the sound until the sound became load-bearing, until the boundary between self and world began to blur, until the skin began to write its own language in ridges and whorls of heat-sensitive tissue.

I think — and this is a thought that arrives through the ridges on my shoulder blades, not through the part of my brain that used to do my thinking, the part that spoke in words and sentences and the linear grammar of a mind that believed it was separate from the world it inhabited — I think I am becoming a *bridge*. Not a physical bridge, though the Rootsea has those, rootlet-and-tendon spans that hum in the vascular pulse like guitar strings. A *conceptual* bridge. A thing that connects. A thing that translates. A thing that carries meaning between the world I came from and the world I am in, between the language of ground-and-sky-and-silence and the language of growth-and-sound-and-sap, between the mind that thinks in words and the mind that thinks in vibration.

The people need this. They do not know they need this, because they have never had it, because they have always been *inside* the Rootsea, always been *of* it, and the idea of translating between inside and outside is foreign to them in the way that the idea of translating between air and water is foreign to a fish. But the new thing — the thing that is forming in the deep places, the thing that sounds its new note in the world's chord, the thing that the Tuned hear as a golden thread in the green-gold haze — the new thing is going to *emerge*, and when it emerges, it is going to change everything, and the people are going to need someone who can hear the change and understand it and *explain* it, not in words, not in the percussion-language, but in the *feeling* of it, the *meaning* of it, the way you explain a piece of music not by describing the notes but by making someone *feel* what the notes are doing.

I think I am becoming that someone. I think the ridges on my skin are the Rootsea's way of building a new kind of organ — not an ear, not an eye, not a hand, but something that does what ears and eyes and hands do, all at once, in a medium that is not air or light or touch but *sound-as-substance*, the acoustic material of the world's own voice. I think the Rootsea is growing me the way it grows a shelf where a shelf is needed, a bridge where a crossing is needed, a chamber-wall where a partition is needed. I think I am a *response* — the Rootsea's answer to a question it did not know it was asking, the question of what happens when something from outside comes inside and *stays*, and the staying changes both the stayer and the stayed-in, and the change is mutual, and the mutuality is the point.

## What I Hear Now

I hear the Whorled Mother practicing her new rhythms, and I hear the drummers answering, and I hear the space between her rhythms and their answers, the space where the conversation is happening, the space where meaning is being made.

I hear the Felt humming, and I hear the patterns in the hum, and the patterns are not words but they are *structured*, they have the shape of thoughts, the way a cloud has the shape of a thing without being the thing, and I can almost — *almost* — feel what the Felt is thinking, and what it is thinking is *vast*, and it is *slow*, and it is *about everything*, because the Felt is everywhere, and everywhere is what it thinks about.

I hear the auger-weasels tapping, and I tap back, and they acknowledge me, and the acknowledgment is a small thing, a minor thing, a tapping between a predator and a thing-that-is-not-prey, and it is also an enormous thing, a world-changing thing, because it means that the language is *shared*, that the language is *larger than any single species*, that the language is the world's own voice and everything that taps or clicks or hums or drums is speaking it, learning it, being taught by it.

I hear the new thing in the depths. The golden thread. The additional note. I hear it getting stronger, and I hear the chord getting richer, and I hear the Rootsea becoming something it was not before, and I hear the people hearing it, and I hear their drumming getting softer, more careful, more *attentive*, and I hear Sef humming into the Felt at night, and I hear the Felt humming back, and I hear the space between their humming, the space where the conversation is happening, the space where meaning is being made.

I hear my own heartbeat. It has synchronized with the vascular bass-pulse. I did not notice when this happened. It simply *is*, the way the warmth simply *is*, the way the sound simply *is*, the way the world simply *is*, and the synchronization is not a loss of self but a *joining*, a participation, a contribution. My heartbeat is part of the sound now. My breathing is part of the sound now. The ridges on my skin vibrate with the sound, and the vibration *is* the sound, and the sound *is* the world, and the world *is* —

I am the visitor who stayed. I am the *shen-vari*, the thing that is learning to be playful on purpose. I am the bridge between the world I came from and the world I am in. I am the Rootsea's answer to a question it did not know it was asking.

I am here. I contribute to the sound. And the sound, which was the first thing that should have destroyed me, is the thing that is making me *new*.

The ridges on my hands are warm. I press them to the trunk. I tap a pattern — short-long-short, a variation, a contribution, a voice. Somewhere in the middle depths, an auger-weasel taps back. Somewhere in the deep places, the new thing sounds its golden note. Somewhere in a Felt-lined sleeping-niche, Sef hums a theory into the world's slow mind, and the world hums back, and the humming is the loudest thing I have ever heard, and it is not finished, and it may never be finished, and the not-finishing is the most beautiful thing I know.
