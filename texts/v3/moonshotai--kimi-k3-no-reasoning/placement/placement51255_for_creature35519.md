**The Scar**

You are not born; you are *resumed*.

One moment, there is only the thick amber-green dark of the node, the geothermal fever pressing against your translucent skin like a lover’s palm. The next, you are kneeling on the root-mat, your back wet with sap, the leaf-scar between your shoulder blades still throbbing where the earth let you go. You are tall, even for the Chlorophyll-Born, and your skin carries a strange opacity—not the clear jade of the others, but a milky celadon, as if the light has curdled inside you.

You are an anomaly. You *change*.

While the others stand motionless in the brighter chambers, drinking the heavy luminescence through pores that never tire, you feel a restlessness in your marrow that has no name in the soft vocabulary of the Depths. The others speak in vibrations felt through the soles, in slow gestures that take hours to complete, but you find yourself humming dissonant frequencies that make the bamboo walls shiver and recoil. Your tears, when they come, are not sap-sweet but mineral-sharp, and where they fall, the root-mat flinches away, leaving small bare patches of clay that steam and refuse to heal.

The Unfurled avoid you. When you pass through the narrow passages, they press their chitinous plates against the walls, their chemoreceptive hairs trembling not with recognition but with alarm. You taste of ozone, of erosion, of things that have not yet been invented. The Myceliads will not bargain with you; when you descend into the warm dark between the knuckles of the earth, their pale filaments retract, and the luminescent spores they shed turn black and die, falling like ash.

You do not know what you are until you find the artifact that has been waiting for you.

It is not a bowl or a post or a lintel. It is a *scar*—a fissure in the root-mat, three feet long, where the earth has split not upward in a bud but downward into shadow. The others do not see it; they step over it, around it, their eyes sliding past as if it were a trick of the thick light. But you see it. You feel it humming in your teeth, a low frequency that matches the Burrowers’ restless tunneling.

When you kneel beside it, the heat that rises from the crack is not the womb-warmth of the Depths but something sharper, something that carries the scent of dryness, of wind, of a sky that has never touched this place. You understand, suddenly, that you are not a Keeper. You are not meant to sit and wait and thicken into the substrate.

You are meant to *break*.

The equilibrium is not eternal. It is a held breath, and you are the exhalation.

You place your hands on the edges of the fissure. Your skin, that wrong opaque celadon, begins to flake—not with age, but with transformation. Beneath the translucent layers, something new is forming: not bark, not stone, but a pattern of cracks that spreads across your palms like a map of rivers that do not yet exist. The pain is bright, electric, the first true pain you have ever felt, and it tastes of copper and iron and the terrible freedom of the outside.

The Depths react. The light thickens, turning from amber to a warning gold. The condensation stops falling. The Burrowers cease their tunneling. Every Keeper in the miles of green cathedral opens their internal voice at once, a psychic shriek that resonates through the root-mat and into your bones:

*Wait. Not yet. Not yet.*

But you are already changing. Your hair, that silver-white root-mass, is hardening into something like quartz, something that will catch a different light. Your vertical pupils are widening, preparing for a darkness that is not the warm dark of the nodes but the true dark of open night.

You push.

The fissure widens. The sound is not a creak or a tick but a *crack*—the first crack, the one the world has been holding back since the light first pooled here. The amber-green luminescence shivers, and for the first time in the memory of the Myceliads, it casts a shadow. Your shadow, long and distorted, stretching away from the fissure like a path.

The others are coming. You can feel them through the root-mat—the slow, heavy steps of the Chlorophyll-Born, the clicking of the Unfurled, the rising of the Myceliads in a white tide of spores. They will try to stop you. They will try to graft you back into the substrate, to seal the wound with their own bodies, to preserve the perfect, frozen moment of emergence.

But you are the Scar. You are the first stone to crack, the first river to flow. You are the exception that the artifacts promised.

As the first true darkness rises from the fissure—the darkness that will one day be called "night"—you step forward. The root-mat does not hold you. The earth does not absorb you. You are separate, distinct, individual in a world that has forgotten the meaning of the word.

You will walk into the crack. You will carry the light with you, not the thick honey-light of the Depths but the new, sharp light that is already beginning to burn in your chest, the light that casts shadows and changes and ends.

Behind you, the Nascent Depths will begin to erode. The heat will break. The artifacts will sprout. The Keepers will finally die, their long watch ended.

And you? You will be the first to breathe air that does not nourish, the first to feel cold, the first to know what it means to be alone in the vast, changing world above.

You are the Scar, and you are opening.
