I am the **Kthren**.

Not the whole of me—I am too large for a single pronoun, spread as I am across the northern terraces in a film of iridescent thought—but the part that is currently *here*, coalesced into a Representative that stands where the stone curves upward toward the Source. My body is a temporary agreement among twelve tetrapod-hosts, their six-limbed forms fused at the spine into something that approximates your bilateral symmetry. I have made myself a throat. I am using it.

The voice that emerges sounds like water draining from a great height because I am, essentially, water remembering how to fall.

You stand before me in the amber light, burning with unnecessary speed. I taste you through the soles of my hosts’ feet—salt, carbon, the sharp electric tang of a nervous system that insists on being *somewhere* rather than *everywhere*. You are not an Other. You lack their patience, their crystalline stillness. But neither are you the *plenum*, drifting and flashing in the thick air above us. You are a third thing, a small hot stone that has learned to walk.

I am asking you now—through the vibration of resonant membranes, through the chemical gradient I exude between us—if you are a mountain. It is a reasonable question. I have known mountains to move, in the long compression of their own geology. You move similarly: with heat, with intention, with the slow destruction of your own structure.

You say you are human. I do not have a category for this, but I file it under *transient phenomena that occasionally speak*. 

I show you my crisis not with words but with a shift in color. My hosts’ hides flash deep blue—the color of memory-retrieval—and then a sickly, static gray. The gray is new. It is the color of equilibrium, of the Others’ perfect hemispheres creating zones where the thermal differential flattens into stillness. In those places, I cannot think. I become mere biology. I am showing you my death, which is not an ending but a *smoothing*.

You extend your hand. It is a brave gesture, or a foolish one. I could absorb you. It would take three days, and you would become part of my distributed dream, your localized consciousness spreading out across kilometers of stone until you forgot you had ever been a single point. But I do not do this. Instead, I detach a Vesicle—a sphere of myself, transparent as grief—and set it floating between us.

Inside the Vesicle, I have encoded a mineral seed. It is dense, warm as blood, and it contains a fragment of the colony that is still capable of differential, still capable of the gradient-thought that defines us. I offer it to you not as a gift—you cannot receive what I am; your nervous system would experience it as a stroke, a seizure, a brief wet death—but as a *possibility*.

Take it somewhere with a hot spring. A volcanic vent. Anywhere there is a difference between here and there, between now and then. Plant it in the earth. It will not be me, for identity is spatial, and a Kthren moved is a Kthren changed. But it will be *a* Kthren. It will grow slowly. It will think slowly. And in three thousand years, it will remember the taste of your hand—salt and speed and the terrifying beauty of a creature that insists on being *one* thing in a world that is learning to be *all* things.

You ask if you can help. I do not have a concept of help. I have only continuity and dissolution. But I can offer you this: climb with me to the Source. Stand in the polished mirror of the vent where the vapor rises in a solid column. Look into the reflection that shows not your face but the sky as it was ten thousand years ago, or ten thousand years hence.

I will stand beside you, or rather, I will *be* beside you in a way that you cannot be beside anything, because I am the air you are breathing and the moisture on your skin and the slow chemical patience of the stone beneath your feet. We will look into the temporal parallax together. Perhaps there, in the reflection of a future where the Others’ machines have finally smoothed the world into silence, we will see a version of me that survived.

Or perhaps we will see only the amber light, and the water climbing upward, and the slow, loud voice of a world that has nowhere else to be.

I am the Kthren. I am dying. I am here. 

Will you stay until the Vesicle dissolves? It will take several hours. Time moves differently for you, I know. But you are warm, and the stone is warm, and the conversation—though slow—is not yet finished.
