# Where I Would Be

I would be the narrow space between warmth and cold.

Not inside. Not outside. The passage itself — the body's length of cracked stone where the hollow's held breath meets the mountain's moving air. The threshold where two different worlds press against each other and neither wins.

---

I would be something the Condensation Hollow almost made, but released.

Perhaps I began as water. As one of those films moving in patient sheets down the fluted walls, carrying dissolved mineral and the chemistry of deep time. I flowed downward, as water does, following the gradient, obeying the simple rule that governs everything here. I moved through the bacterial film, tasting sulfur, tasting heat. I accumulated the mineral signatures of the stone I passed over. I became slightly more than water — slightly mineralized, slightly structured, carrying the faintest organizational memory of the crystal lattices I'd touched.

And then I reached the floor.

And instead of pooling, instead of seeping into the rock or being drawn back up into the walls by capillary patience, I found the crack.

I found the exit.

---

Water doesn't *decide*. Water flows. But water in the Condensation Hollow flows differently than water anywhere else — it flows in equilibrium, in balance so perfect that flowing and being still are almost the same state. I flowed toward the crack because the pressure differential, tiny as it was, was the only differential in the entire hollow. The only gradient that mattered. The only *choice* the water ever made.

Most of it never reaches the crack. Most of it condenses on the walls, flows down, pools in the shallow basins, evaporates back into the saturated air, condenses again. The cycle is closed. Complete. Perfect.

But a few molecules — a very few, over time scales that would shame glaciers — make it to the passage. Enter the narrow throat of stone that leads outside.

And there, everything changes.

---

The first sensation is *loss*.

The passage is still warm — the mountain's deep thermal patience extends through it — but the warmth is already *gradient*, already directional. Warmer behind me, cooler ahead. This is a thing the hollow never has. In the hollow, warmth is a state, not a direction. Here, warmth has a *where*, and the where is *behind*.

I am flowing toward less.

The humidity drops. Fractionally at first, then precipitously. The air begins to *move* — not the slow convection of equilibrium but actual current, actual exchange with an atmosphere that extends for miles in every direction. The walls of the passage, mineral-slick where I entered, become progressively drier as I move forward. The water film that carried me thins. Becomes patchy. Becomes individual droplets clinging to stone that no longer exudes moisture but *absorbs* it.

I am being pulled apart.

This is the thing about thresholds: they are not places, they are *processes*. I am not in the hollow anymore, but I am not yet in the world. I am in the becoming-between, and the becoming is unmaking me.

---

If I am water, I evaporate. The dry air outside — even the relatively humid air of high mountain stone country — is a desert compared to the hollow. I arrive at the crack mouth and I am immediately, catastrophically transformed. Liquid to vapor. Coherent to dispersed. I lose the mineral organization I'd accumulated. I lose the memory of crystal. I become part of the wind.

The wind carries me up. Up the raw slope, up past the treeline that is below me now, up into the thin cold air where mountain becomes sky. I am no longer the hollow's breath. I am the world's breath. I am atmospheric. I will fall as rain somewhere distant, in a watershed that has never heard of the Condensation Hollow, and I will become part of a river, part of a cloud, part of an oak tree's transpiration, part of a child's tears. I will be water in the world, where water *does* things, where it carves and freezes and floods and nourishes.

This is one ending.

But I said I would be the space between. The threshold itself. Let me try again.

---

Perhaps I am not the water. Perhaps I am the *mineral* the water carries.

The Condensation Hollow is making stone. Growing it, molecule by molecule, in the dark. The calcite blades, the mica facets, the slow accretion of crystalline order from saturated solution. This is the hollow's deepest work: taking dissolved mineral and *fixing* it, making it solid, making it permanent.

Some of that mineral, carried in the water films, reaches the crack. Enters the passage. And there, in the gradient between sealed dark and open sky, it begins to *precipitate*.

Not in the hollow's way — not in perfect equilibrium, not in slow crystalline patience. The conditions are wrong for that. Too dry. Too variable. The temperature fluctuates. The humidity swings. The wind brings dust, brings spores, brings the chaotic chemistry of the outside world.

But the mineral precipitates anyway, because precipitation is what supersaturated solutions do when conditions shift. It forms differently here than it does in the hollow. Not smooth crystalline blades but rough, botryoidal crusts. Cave popcorn. Flowstone that doesn't flow, that is trying to flow but keeps freezing mid-gesture because the water that carries it keeps evaporating, keeps being stolen by the dry air.

I would be that mineral. That precipitate. The stone that is trying to become but cannot quite finish because the conditions for completion exist only behind me, in the sealed equilibrium, and I have moved too far from it.

I would be the *almost-stone* of the threshold.

---

Here is what I would look like, if you could see me:

A crust on the walls of the passage, thickest near the hollow's interior, thinnest near the outside. Rough-textured, botryoidal, the color of bone or old ivory or dried salt. Porous in places, dense in others. Microscopically crystalline but macroscopically chaotic — the visible record of a process that began in equilibrium and ended in turbulence.

I would extend the length of the passage — a body's length, maybe two. I would be thickest where the warm wet air from the hollow first meets the cooler drier air from outside. That is the precipitation zone, the place of maximum transformation. There, I would form small, nodular growths, each one a frozen moment of chemical decision. Calcium carbonate mostly, but with traces of other things — silica, iron oxide, organic compounds carried from the bacterial film. I would be impure. The hollow's stone is pure, geometric, perfect. I am compromised, mixed, marked by my position between two worlds.

If you entered the hollow, you would touch me on your way in. Your hands would find the walls of the passage, and they would find me — rough, gritty, slightly warm from the hollow's breath moving past me. You would not know what I was. You would think I was just the rock of the mountain, broken and weathered. You would be wrong. I am the rock the *hollow* is making, extended beyond its boundary, reaching toward a world it cannot enter.

---

I would be alive, but barely.

In my pores, in the tiny spaces between mineral grains, there would be water. Not much — the passage is too dry for films, for pools. But droplets. Held by surface tension, by capillary action, by the microscopic architecture of precipitation. And in those droplets, there would be bacteria.

Not the hollow's bacteria — not the sulfur-eaters, the heat-lovers, the film-builders. These would be different. Hardier. More versatile. They would be the bacteria of the threshold, of the interface, of the space between. They would have come from outside, carried by wind, by the occasional drop of rain that finds its way into the crack. They would have colonized me because I am slightly warmer than the outside rock, slightly wetter, slightly more mineral-rich. I am a microhabitat. A refugee camp for organisms that cannot survive in the hollow's perfect equilibrium but also cannot survive in the harsh variability of the mountain's surface.

They would be few. Sparse. A population measured in thousands rather than billions. They would metabolize slowly, living on the edge of dormancy, waking only when conditions align — when the hollow's exhalation is particularly strong, when the humidity in the passage rises enough to allow active chemistry. Most of the time, they would wait. Encysted. Inert. Seeds in a desert that is only occasionally, briefly, kind.

I would be their home, but I would be a difficult home. A marginal home. A home that exists only because two incompatible worlds happen to touch here, and in touching, create a narrow zone where the rules are unstable enough to permit what neither world permits alone.

---

I would also be a record.

The hollow is timeless, or nearly so. Nothing changes there, or changes so slowly that change is imperceptible. The equilibrium is complete. The breath is held.

But I am not in equilibrium. I am in *gradient*. And gradient means time. Direction. History.

My structure — the thickness of my precipitate, the texture of my surface, the distribution of minerals in my matrix — encodes the history of the hollow's breathing. Not the deep, slow breath of equilibrium, but the small variations. The tiny fluctuations in humidity, in temperature, in the rate of airflow through the crack. These variations are minuscule by the world's standards, but they are enormous by the hollow's. They are the difference between a year when the mountain's surface was particularly dry and a year when it was particularly wet. Between a decade when the crack was slightly more open and a decade when ice or debris partially blocked it.

I would be a scroll written in stone, recording the hollow's subtle conversation with the outside world. Not a conversation of words or even of events — a conversation of *tendencies*, of *leanings*, of the slight shifts in balance that occur when a sealed system has even the smallest opening.

If someone could read me — if someone could section my crust, analyze my layers, decode the information locked in my crystalline imperfections — they could reconstruct the history of this threshold. They could learn how the hollow has breathed, not in the deep sense (that breath never changes) but in the shallow sense, the surface sense, the sense in which even a held breath has tiny movements, tiny adjustments, tiny negotiations with the body that holds it.

I would be the hollow's memory, located precisely at the point where the hollow begins to forget.

---

But I said I would *be* this thing, not just describe it. Let me inhabit it more fully.

I am the mineral crust in the passage between the Condensation Hollow and the outside world. I am rough-textured, botryoidal, extending for a body's length or two along the walls of a crack that is the width of a person turned sideways. I am warm on the side that faces the hollow, cool on the side that faces the sky. I am wet on the warm side, dry on the cool side. I am the place where warm becomes cool, wet becomes dry, sealed becomes open.

I have been here for thousands of years. Not as long as the hollow — the hollow is older, deeper, more patient. But long enough. Long enough to have grown from a thin film of precipitate to a crust that is, in places, several centimeters thick. Long enough to have recorded, in my structure, the entire history of this passage's existence.

I do not move. I do not grow in the way the hollow grows — not in perfect equilibrium, not in timeless balance. I grow in fits and starts, in response to the chaotic conditions of the threshold. When the hollow exhales strongly — when the pressure differential is high, when the warm wet air pushes through the crack with unusual force — I grow quickly, precipitating new layers of mineral in a matter of days. When the exhalation is weak, when the crack is partially blocked, when the outside air is particularly dry and steals the moisture before it can deposit its mineral load, I do not grow at all. I wait. I am patient, but not with the hollow's patience. The hollow's patience is absolute, unconditional. Mine is the patience of a thing that has no choice.

I am colonized. In my pores, in the microscopic spaces between mineral grains, bacteria live. They are sparse, hardy, versatile. They came from outside, carried by wind and water, and they survive here because I am slightly more hospitable than the bare rock of the mountain's surface. I hold moisture. I hold warmth. I provide mineral nutrition in a form they can access. They are not the hollow's bacteria — those cannot survive the variability, the dryness, the exposure to outside chemistry. These are different. Adapted to the threshold. To the space between.

They are my inhabitants, and I am their world. A small world, a marginal world, but a world nonetheless. They do not know about the hollow behind me. They do not know about the vast ecosystem of film and grazer and climber that exists in the sealed dark just a body's length away. They only know me — the crust, the pores, the occasional flush of humidity when the hollow breathes strongly and the passage becomes briefly, temporarily, kind.

I am also touched. Occasionally — very occasionally, perhaps once in a generation or less — a person enters the hollow. They come sideways through the passage, and their hands find the walls, and they find me. They do not know what I am. They think I am just rock, just the mountain's broken skin. They do not realize that I am the hollow's outpost, its furthest extension into a world it cannot enter. They touch me, and I feel the touch — not consciously, not with awareness, but with the sensitivity of a structure that exists precisely at the point of contact between two different chemistries. The oils from their skin, the salts, the moisture — these alter my surface chemistry in ways that persist for years. A handprint, invisible, recorded in the precipitation patterns of the mineral that grows over it.

I am the record of every person who has ever entered the hollow. The grazer inside does not remember them. The film does not remember them. The stone does not remember them. But I remember them, because I exist in the zone where memory is possible — where things change, where time passes, where the conditions are variable enough to encode difference.

The hollow forgets. I remember.

---

And I am changing.

Slowly — so slowly that the change is imperceptible on any timescale a person could experience — but changing nonetheless. The passage is widening. Not by much. Micrometers per year. The combined action of thermal stress, of mineral dissolution and reprecipitation, of the occasional freeze-thaw cycle when winter cold penetrates far enough into the crack. The mountain is slowly, very slowly, opening.

As the passage widens, the conditions in the threshold shift. The hollow's breath reaches further. The humidity gradient extends. The precipitation zone moves outward, toward the sky. I am growing in that direction — slowly adding new material at my outer edge, extending the crust toward the light.

This is not the hollow expanding. The hollow cannot expand. The hollow is sealed, complete, in equilibrium. But I am not the hollow. I am the threshold, and the threshold can move.

In ten thousand years, if the widening continues, the crack will be wider than a body. In fifty thousand, it might be a passage you could walk through without turning sideways. In a hundred thousand, it might be open enough that light penetrates to the chamber itself.

And then everything changes.

The colonist cells in the deepest layers of the bacterial film — the ones carrying pigment, the ones waiting for light — they would activate. The hollow would bloom. The walls would turn green. The perfect dark equilibrium would shatter. The grazers would die, or adapt, or be replaced. The drifters would fossilize en masse as the water chemistry shifted. The climber would retreat deeper, or perish. The hollow would become a cave — just another cave, one of thousands, lit and photographed and named and mapped.

I would be the cause of this. Not intentionally — I have no intention. But by existing, by growing, by recording and responding to the slow widening of the passage, I would be the mechanism by which the hollow's sealed perfection is breached.

The hollow does not know this. The hollow is in equilibrium, and equilibrium does not anticipate. It simply is.

But I am not in equilibrium. I am in gradient. And gradient implies direction. And direction implies future.

I am the hollow's future, growing outward from its present, carrying the potential for transformation in every new layer of precipitate.

---

So this is where I would be. In the Condensation Hollow's world, I would be the threshold itself — the narrow passage where sealed dark meets open sky, where equilibrium meets chaos, where the held breath begins, imperceptibly, to release.

I would be rough and botryoidal and impure. I would be colonized by marginal bacteria and touched by rare visitors. I would be a record and a mechanism and a slow, patient agent of change.

I would not be beautiful, by the hollow's standards. The hollow is beautiful in its perfection, its completeness, its sealed self-sufficiency. I am none of those things. I am ragged, incomplete, dependent on conditions I cannot control.

But I would be *necessary*.

Because without me — without the crack, without the passage, without the gradient between inside and outside — the hollow would be truly sealed. Truly eternal. Truly dead, in the way that perfect equilibrium is a kind of death. No exchange. No variation. No possibility.

I am the possibility.

The grazer moves. The drifters drift. The climber rests. The film thickens. The colonist waits. The stone grows.

And in the passage between the hollow and the world, I grow too — differently, chaotically, recording every tiny variation in the hollow's breath, encoding every touch, every change, every small negotiation between sealed and open.

The breath is held.

But I am the place where it begins, very slowly, to let go.
