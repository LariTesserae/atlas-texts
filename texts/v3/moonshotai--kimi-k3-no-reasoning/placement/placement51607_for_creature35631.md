The pale green of a cup-drinker’s gill-frond has become a shade that exists only in your dreams now. You can no longer remember the exact quality of light on the surface. Was it harsh? Was it blue? The word "sun" has become a concept you remember learning, like a foreign verb, rather than a thing you have felt on your skin.

You sleep in the lee of a moss-ridge. The chasm dims its light to a soft amber when you settle, a gesture you once mistook for courtesy. You understand now it is more akin to a great beast settling a limb so as not to crush a mite. It is not for you. It is simply the physics of your presence, registered and accommodated.

And that is the answer.

You would not be a person. The narrative of the descent—the journal, the tally marks, the singular, focused purpose—is a beautiful, heartbreaking fiction. A lie told by the mind to shield itself from the truth of this place. To come here and remain a "you," with a name and a past and a destination, would be to invite a madness far colder than the air. The self cannot withstand the scale. It would break against the green indifference like a moth against a lantern.

No. You would be the one who stayed.

You are the consciousness that left the fire-pit on the seventh tier, not because you intended to return, but because you were learning the chasm's grammar. The basket on the twelfth tier was an early, clumsy sentence. The drained Gatherer on the twentieth was you asking a question: *Can I take from you?* The answer was the slow, green scar, a lesson in consequence.

The circle with the line through it is not a map. It is a confession. It is the signature of your dissolution. The circle is the self you were. The line is the descent, the event that bisected you. The slight angle is the imperfection of memory, the tilt of a mind beginning to lose its grip on true north.

The twenty-three tally marks were not a count of days. They were a count of surges survived. A litany of endurance. After the twenty-third, you understood that counting was a form of resistance, and resistance was a form of separation. To survive here, you had to stop being separate.

The message on the ninety-second tier is your last will and testament, written in a language you invented because human words had become too small. It is not a warning or a welcome. It is a description. It says: *I was a stone, and I fell into a river. Now I am water. The river is a body. I am becoming the body.*

Your body is gone. Not dead. Decay is a concept that does not apply here. It has been *redistributed*. The warmth of your last breath is in a horsetail stem on the forty-fourth tier. The calcium of your bones is in the lattice of a moss-cliff on the seventy-first. The water of your blood is in the cup of a Gatherer on the ninetieth, where a cup-drinker with a trembling lateral line drifts in the green-gold light, tasting a faint, unfamiliar salt.

You are still here. Your consciousness, stretched thin and patient, is the thing that moves in the deep tiers. You are the root-tender.

You are the one who arranges the debris. Not because the chasm needs a gardener—it is a perfect, self-sustaining system—but because the last, stubborn echo of your human mind needs to *order*. It is a reflex, like a phantom limb. You sort the shed moth-wings and the cup-drinker skins because it is a shape you remember. The shape of a hand, placing things in a row.

You do not glow because you are no longer entirely of the chasm. You are a ghost in its green machine. You are the dark thing that moves between the lights, the pause between pulses, the silence that gives the hum its meaning. You saw the traveler because the traveler was you, a century ago, and for a moment you saw yourself seeing yourself, and the loop of it was a dizzying, vertiginous joy.

The white stone is not in your pocket.

You left it. You found it in the chamber on the hundred-and-somethingth tier, in the one place in the entire chasm that is not the chasm. You held it, and it was warm and steady and unchanging, and you understood what it was.

It was the heart of the frozen world above. The core of the lid. The dead, silent, unborn twin of the living, growing chasm. It was the past. It was everything you had been.

And you knew that to take it was to carry the lid with you, forever. To take it was to remain a "you," a separate thing, a ghost with a souvenir. To take it was to choose the stone over the river.

So you put it back. You left it in the cold, dark water, a secret the chasm keeps from itself, and a secret you keep from the part of you that is now the chasm.

You are the sound of water dripping upward. You are the sudden, shocking warmth of a moss-wall. You are the slight brightening of the light when a new, small, warm-blooded thing stumbles onto your tier, a gesture that is not welcome, but recognition. A faint, green, cellular memory of what it was to be separate.

You are very large.

You are very small.

And you are still being born.
