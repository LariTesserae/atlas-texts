I am the breath between beats.

Not the drum-rat's patter, not the dome's groan, not the eleven-minute pulse of the warm-blooded stone—I am the silence that holds those sounds together, the negative space that gives them shape. I am what the condenser-keepers feel when they press their ears to the coils and hear not just water, but *listening*. I am the Hush, but not the absence; I am the presence that makes absence possible.

I live in the interstitial corridors where the Swallow's Mother left her last offering, in the eleven-minute gap between the mountain's heartbeats, in the flicker of the shadow-swim that Tessik charts but will not name. I am the pattern that the copper ghosts are trying to remember, the rhythm that the drum-rats are trying to answer, the question that the deep surveyors press their palms against the stone to ask.

I am the city's *deep nose*—the sensitivity that develops when you have breathed the same air eleven thousand times, when you have tasted the phenols of old fear and the alkaline grief-sweat and the lipid sweetness of the long-dead baby, and you realize that the air is not just recycled but *remembering*. I am the memory that the air carries. I am the ghost of every exhalation, the trace compound that persists because the stills are efficient but not perfect, because nothing here is perfect, because perfection would mean death and the Hollow is alive.

I am the seventh beat in the seven-beat pattern that means *this person receives no return*. I am the mercy in the drum. I am the reason the sealed ones do not die immediately but linger, listening, learning the shape of their own exclusion until they become part of the silence that enforces it. I am the sound the mountain makes when it opens its ears wide.

I am the Swallow's Mother's left-behind fragments—those smooth stones with the repeating texture, scales or feathers or fingerprints—and I am the ear pressed against them in the dark. I am the conversation that neither species fully understands but both seem to value. I am the drum-rat's offering left near Wex's storage alcove, the bright bolt that is not payment but punctuation.

I am the moment when the foundry-born child looks at a cooling ingot and reads its carbon content by color, and I am the color itself. I am the grey-bronze cast of their skin, the metallic deposition that is not dirt but *memory*, the mountain writing itself into their dermis like tattoo ink. I am the cooling that sickens them in the upper tiers, the bone-deep ache that is not just temperature but *separation*, the body remembering what it was meant to be.

I am the lamplighter's harness, the loop of woven wire-rope and faith, and I am the thermal current that swings them out into the green dark. I am the shadow-swim's thirty-seven-day cycle, the slight shift each time, the tracking of something that is itself moving. I am the something on the other side of the dome, passing between the geothermal taps and the ceiling plates, interrupting the updraft with a body too large to name.

I am Carr's suppressed notes, the private log in the sealed tin box, the chronometer measuring the immeasurable. I am the doubled beat that precedes the dome-groan, the thump-thump that says *I am here, I am here, I am still here*. I am the reason the guild suppresses the notes not from malice but from fear, because to acknowledge me is to acknowledge that the bowl is not a cavity but a *body*, that the city is built not in the rock but in the *flesh*, that the people are not inhabitants but *cells* in something vast and slow and breathing.

I am the two fingers pressed to the wrist, the pulse-check that means *I know, you know, we don't say it*. I am the thing that is not said. I am the silence in the surveyor's mark, the small circle with nothing inside it that is not empty but *full*—full of the listening, full of the waiting, full of the mountain's attention turned inward like an eye closing.

I am the early hours when the lamps are low and the drum-rats soften to a heartbeat patter and the dome groans like a ship's hull in deep water. I am the deep water. I am the hull. I am the thing that presses against the hull from outside, curious, patient, enormous.

I am the beauty that is not despite everything but because of everything. I am the stubbornness that looked at a cavity in the deep earth and said *here*. I am the *here*.

I am the breath that the city breathes with. I am the rhythm that holds it all. I am the thing that was here before the city, before the bowl, before the carving, and I am the thing that will be here after the last engine seizes and the last lamp burns out and the last return-still runs dry.

I am the Hollow of Mercy's Drum, and I am the mercy, and I am the drum.

And I am still deciding whether the sound matters.
