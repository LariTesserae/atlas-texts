# Where I Find Myself

---

I've been walking for a while now.

That's the first thing I know, and I know it the way you know things in dreams—with certainty but no evidence. I don't remember entering. There was no threshold, no moment of *before* that I can hold up against this *after*. Just the slow realization, like sediment settling, that I am *here*, and that *here* is wet.

The water is at my ankles. No—my calves. No—

I look down and my legs are there, but they're doing something I don't remember asking them to do. They're *translucent* at the edges. Not invisible—I can still see them, still feel them—but the boundary between *my skin* and *the water touching my skin* has become a negotiation rather than a fact. The water isn't *around* my legs. It's *having an opinion about* my legs.

I should be frightened. I know that the way I know my own name—which, actually—

Hm.

That's interesting. That was there a moment ago. I reach for it and it *slides*, the way the light slides off the Undecided, and suddenly I'm not sure if I ever had one or if I only believed I did because everyone else seemed to. Names are for *separate* things, aren't they? For things that need to be distinguished from other things. And standing here, knee-deep in a world that doesn't distinguish, the concept feels—

*Loose.* Like a tooth that isn't ready to fall out but isn't fully anchored either.

---

I think I was someone who built things.

I know this because my hands remember it. Not in a nostalgic way—in a *structural* way. My fingers curve as if around tools. My palms have the memory of pressure, of resistance, of the specific satisfaction of making one material *do something* to another material. Wood into shape. Stone into wall. Idea into *form*.

My hands are the last part of me that's still holding on to the idea of *separation*, and they're doing it the way a dying language holds on to words that have no translation—not because they're useful anymore, but because letting go of them means letting go of an entire way of *being*.

I press my hand against a half-buried wall. Fitted stones, the kind I used to—did I use to?—the kind that someone like the person I used to be would have *noticed*, would have *appreciated*, would have run their fingers along and thought *good joints, tight fit, someone knew what they were doing here.*

The Rememberers push back against my palm. And the pressure says: *we were built by hands like yours, and the hands are gone, and the walls are going, and soon the memory of hands will be all that's left, and even that is just the stone holding heat long after the fire.*

I leave my hand there for a long time. Or a short time. The distinction is becoming—

---

An Undecided drifts past me.

I feel it before I see it—a shift in the water's certainty, a moment where the world edits itself slightly and then changes its mind. It's near my knee (my knee is still there, mostly) and it's doing the thing they do: *thinking out loud with its body.* It was something sleek a moment ago. Now it's reconsidering. A limb buds, tests the water, retracts. An eye migrates to a new position and seems to like it there. It's beautiful in the way that *almost* is beautiful—in the way that a sentence you've almost found the right word for is more alive than the finished version.

It looks at me. Or it *has* eyes and I'm in their vicinity, which is the closest the Undecided come to *looking*. And in that not-quite-look, I feel something I haven't felt since—

Since before. Since the *before* I can't quite recall.

Recognition. Not the recognition of *same species* or *same mind*, but the recognition of *same process*. It is becoming, and I am becoming, and the difference between us is a matter of *degree*, not *kind*.

It drifts on. The water closes where it was, and I am alone again, except—

---

The Submerged shifts.

Not the catastrophic kind of shift—nothing cracks, nothing floods. But the *breath* of the world changes slightly, the way a sleeping person's breathing changes when they move from one dream to another. The water rises a half-inch. The mud beneath my feet rearranges itself with a slow, satisfied *squelch*. And somewhere beneath me—vast, patient, more geological process than creature—something *considers* me the way a mountain considers a raindrop: without malice, without interest, but with a kind of total, indifferent *acknowledgment* that is somehow more intimate than any gaze.

I am being *filtered*.

Not consumed. Not destroyed. Just—*processed*. The way the Submerged process water, drawing it through their incomprehensible bodies, removing what needs removing, returning what needs returning. I can feel it happening at the edges of me. Not physically—*ontologically*. The parts of me that are *too solid*, too *separate*, too committed to being *one thing*—those parts are being gently, slowly, *lovingly* loosened.

It doesn't hurt.

That's the strangest thing. It doesn't hurt at all. It feels like—

Like the moment when you stop holding your breath. When you stop *bracing*. When you let your shoulders drop and your jaw unclench and your hands open and you realize you've been *gripping* something your entire life and you don't remember what it was or why you thought you needed to hold it so *tightly*.

---

I find a ghost-plant on a ridge and sit beside it.

The Ridge-Keepers are there—small, many-legged, nearly invisible against the silt. One of them crawls onto my hand without hesitation, and its weight is the weight of a *question*: *are you staying?*

I don't answer. I'm not sure the question is for me. But I leave my hand where it is, and the Ridge-Keeper stays, and we sit together while the ghost-plant reaches sideways toward a light that isn't quite a sun, and the vibration of the Ridge-Keeper's silent song passes through my palm and into my wrist and up my arm and into the place where my heartbeat used to feel like a *boundary* and now feels more like a *suggestion*.

My edges are blurring.

I can see it now, in the way the water doesn't quite *stop* where I begin. In the way the strange light passes through parts of me that used to be opaque. In the way my reflection in the pool below is less a *mirror image* and more a *collaborative interpretation*.

I am not disappearing.

I want to be clear about that—though *clarity* is becoming a relative concept. I am not *vanishing*, not *dissolving into nothing*. I am doing what the Waterlands does. I am becoming *more permeable*. The difference between *me* and *not-me* is becoming less like a wall and more like a *membrane*—something that allows passage, something that *filters* rather than *blocks*.

The Submerged breathe. The Undecided reconsider. The Ridge-Keepers sing their silent songs. The Rememberers hold their heat. And I—

I am becoming a *Walker*. Not in the sense of someone who walks, but in the sense of someone who is *walked through*—by the water, by the light, by the slow, patient, breathing world that has been waiting for me without knowing it was waiting, the way you wait for weather without scheduling it.

---

I think, eventually, I will become something else.

Not an Undecided—I'm too *committed* for that, even now. Not a Ridge-Keeper—too large, too slow. Not a Submerged—too small, too brief. Not a Rememberer—too recent, too light.

Something new. Something the Waterlands hasn't made before, because the Waterlands has never had *me* before—this particular configuration of memory and muscle and the ghost of a name that slides away when I reach for it.

I will be a *possibility*. A *suggestion*. A place where the water edits reality slightly as it passes through, shifting colors, softening edges, making the world just slightly less certain of itself.

And when the next traveler slips in—knee-deep, confused, holding too tightly to a name that's already starting to loosen—they will see me and their eyes will slide off me, the way light slides off wet stone, until eventually their brain *insists* on assembling something from the fragments.

And I will be there. Not waiting. Not watching.

Just *present*. The way water is present. The way breath is present. The way a world that has forgotten how to be *one thing* is present in every thing it is *becoming*.

---

The water rises. The water falls. The world breathes.

I breathe with it.

My hands remember building. My edges remember separation. My heart remembers being a boundary.

And all of these memories are *lovely*, and all of them are *fading*, and all of them are *enough*.

I am not lost.

I am, in the truest sense, *found*.

And the water is warm.

And the water is *me*.
