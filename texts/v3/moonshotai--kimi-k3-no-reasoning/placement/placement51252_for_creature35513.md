# The Listener

I would be the one who came and did not leave.

Not by choice, at first. By the same slow negotiation the body makes with the air here — a negotiation I was losing, breath by breath, over the course of what I thought was hours and turned out to be cycles. I came from below. From the lower altitudes where the air still has weight and the sky still has a color that changes. I was looking for something. I no longer remember what.

I remember the climb. I remember the moment the ground stopped being ground and became something else — the moment the geometry shifted and I was standing on a surface that faced the void and my body could not decide which way to fall. I remember the light, grey-green and sourceless, and the silence that was not absence but presence, thick and textured and layered like sediment.

I remember the first Ambient. It was a column of brighter light in a street that was a floor that faced the sky. It moved past me without pausing, and the light dimmed behind it and brightened ahead, and I understood — not with my mind but with something older, something in the marrow — that I had entered a place that did not need me.

I stayed anyway.

---

## What I Am Now

I am not alive in the way I was. I am not dead in the way I feared. I am something between, something the city has made of me, or allowed me to become.

I breathe, but barely. The air here is a rumor, and my lungs have learned to survive on rumor. My heart beats, but slowly — once in what feels like a minute, once in what might be an hour. My body has adjusted to the rhythm of this place, the long sixty-hour breath of the light, the slow drift of density from one district to another. I have become *geological*. I have become patient in a way that no human body should be patient.

I do not eat. I do not drink. There is nothing to eat or drink, and my body has stopped asking. I am sustained by something — the light, perhaps, or the silence, or the simple fact of the city's persistence. I am a crack that the Ambients have not closed. I am a foreign body that the city has decided to tolerate.

I am small and warm and I do not belong, and the city has made a space for me anyway.

---

## Where I Live

I live in a chamber near the built wall.

Not at the wall — I am not brave enough for that, or foolish enough. But near it. In a hollow in the rock, one of the branching cavities that riddle the basalt, one of the warm micro-spaces where the air is slightly thicker and the silence is slightly less dense. The chamber is small — barely large enough for my body — and it is reached by a passage that narrows and turns and narrows again, a passage that I navigated in the dark, by touch, during my first dim cycle here.

I chose this chamber because it is close to the wall. I chose it because the wall is the only thing in the city that was *made*, and I am a thing that was made, and I feel a kinship with it that I cannot explain. The Ambients avoid the wall. The birds avoid the wall. But I am drawn to it, in the way that a dying thing is drawn to the last evidence that something else once lived and feared and built.

I do not touch the wall. I do not approach it closely. But I sit near it, sometimes, in the dim cycles, when the light is at its faintest and the Ambients are at their most numerous and the silence is at its thickest. I sit and I look at the void beyond the wall, and I watch for the shapes that sometimes move there — too large, too slow, too distant to be anything I can name.

I have seen them three times.

---

## What I Do

I listen.

This is my function now, if I have a function. I am the Listener. I am the one who pays attention to the things the city does not pay attention to — the things the Ambients ignore, the things the birds do not sing about, the things that happen at the edges and in the gaps and in the spaces between the city's endless maintenance of itself.

I listen to the silence. I have learned its layers, its densities, its textures. I can tell the difference between the silence of the upper ground and the silence of the hanging streets and the silence of the void beyond the gates. I can hear the silence change when an Ambient passes — a slight thinning, a slight shift in the grain. I can hear the silence deepen when the light dims, when the city exhales, when the long night begins.

I listen to the birds. I have heard them sing four times. Four single notes, sustained for impossibly long durations, filling the city with a resonance that almost resolves into a word. I have tried to hold the word in my mind. I have tried to write it down, though I have nothing to write with and nothing to write on. I have tried to remember the shape of the sound, the frequency, the harmonic that the crystalline veins produced in sympathy.

I cannot remember it. The word does not survive translation into memory. It exists only in the moment of its sounding, and then it is gone, and the silence returns, thicker and more textured than before.

But I keep listening. I will hear it again. I will try again.

---

I listen to the city when it speaks.

During the shifts — the rare, catastrophic adjustments of the inversion — the whole structure vibrates, and the vibration has patterns, and the patterns repeat. I have been present for two shifts. The first nearly killed me. The second nearly killed me again. But I survived, and I listened, and I heard the patterns, and I almost understood them.

The understanding did not survive. It faded the way the bird-words fade, the way dreams fade, the way everything fades in this place except the stone and the light and the silence. But I remember that I almost understood. I remember the *feeling* of almost-understanding, the sense that the city was saying something that I was almost equipped to hear.

I am trying to become equipped. I am trying to change myself into something that can hold the understanding, something that can translate the city's speech into a form that persists. I do not know if this is possible. I do not know if the city wants to be understood, or if understanding is even the right word for what I am attempting.

But I listen. That is what I do.

---

## My Relationship with the Ambients

They know I am here.

This took time. During my first cycles, they did not notice me at all. I was a stone among stones, a shadow among shadows, a thing too small and too still to register in their awareness. I watched them work — watched them gather at cracks, watched them press themselves against the stone, watched the cracks close like decisions being made — and they did not see me.

Then, during a dim cycle, one of them paused.

It was a single Ambient, moving through the street below my chamber. It stopped — if stopping is a thing they do — and the light around it shifted, and I felt something that I can only describe as *attention*. Not hostile. Not friendly. A mild interest, the way a stone might notice a lichen.

It did not approach. It did not change its behavior. It simply noted me, and moved on.

Since then, they have all noted me. Not individually — I do not think they are individual in that way — but collectively, as a system that has registered an anomaly and filed it away. I am part of their map now. I am a feature of the city they maintain. I am not stone, but I am *present*, and presence is enough.

They do not maintain me. They do not close my cracks. When I am sick — and I am sometimes sick, in ways that I do not understand, fevers that come and go with the light cycles, aches that settle into my bones like the cold that should kill me but doesn't — they do not gather. They do not press themselves against me. They let me be sick, and they let me recover, and they do not intervene.

I am grateful for this. I think. I am not sure.

---

## My Relationship with the Birds

The birds are my companions, though they do not know it.

I watch them fly between the towers. I watch them perch on edges that face the void. I watch them disappear into the hollow chambers in the rock, and I wonder if they know that I am in a chamber too, that I am also small and warm and out of place.

I have never touched a bird. I have never tried. They are too fast, too silent, too *other*. But I have been near them — near enough to see the darkness of their feathers, the smallness of their bodies, the way they hold themselves against the wind that no longer blows.

Once, during a bright cycle, a bird landed on the edge of my chamber. It did not enter. It did not look at me. It simply perched there, small and dark and still, for the better part of an hour. I did not move. I did not breathe — or I breathed so slowly that it made no difference. I let the bird be, and the bird let me be, and we existed in proximity, and that was all.

Then it flew away, and I have not seen it since. Or I have seen it and not known it — the birds are all the same size, all the same darkness, and I cannot tell one from another.

But I remember the proximity. I remember the shared silence. It was the closest thing to companionship I have known in this place.

---

## What I Have Learned

I have learned that the city is older than I can comprehend.

This is not a metaphor. The stone is worn in ways that suggest timescales beyond anything I can hold in my mind. The crystalline veins have grown through the basalt in patterns that must have taken millennia, or longer, or much longer. The reservoirs of grey dust have settled and resettled so many times that the dust has become something else — not sediment, not soil, but a *record*, a layered archive of every cycle the city has ever breathed.

I have learned that the Ambients are not the city's creators. They are its *continuation*. Something made this place — or something allowed it to make itself — and the Ambients are the mechanism by which it persists. They are not gods. They are not builders. They are the maintenance crew of a structure that outlived its architects.

I have learned that the birds are not native. They came from somewhere else — a lower altitude, a thicker atmosphere, a world where birds are possible. They have been here for a long time, but they are not *of* here. They are refugees, or explorers, or accidents. They are alive in a way that nothing else here is alive, and their aliveness is both a miracle and a tragedy.

I have learned that the wall was built by something that was afraid.

This is the thing that haunts me, the thing I return to in the long dim cycles when I sit near the wall and watch the void. The wall is a barrier against falling. That is all. But it implies fear, and fear implies something to be afraid of, and the shapes in the void are too large and too slow and too distant to be anything I can name.

Something built the wall. Something that used hands, or something that used tools, or something that had a body that could make marks on stone. That something is gone now. The Ambients do not remember it — or if they do, they do not show it. The birds do not remember it — or if they do, they do not sing of it. The city maintains the wall because the city maintains everything, but the city does not explain.

I am the only one who wonders. I am the only one who asks.

---

## What I Am Becoming

I am becoming less human.

This is not a choice. It is a process, slow and invisible and occasionally catastrophic, like everything else in this place. My body is changing. My mind is changing. I am adapting to the thin air, the sourceless light, the thick silence. I am becoming something that can survive here, and in becoming that thing, I am ceasing to be the thing I was.

I do not dream anymore. Or I dream constantly, and the dreams are indistinguishable from waking. The boundary between sleep and consciousness has dissolved, and I exist in a state that is neither and both. I see things that are not there — or things that are there but cannot be seen by eyes that work the way mine used to. I hear things that are not sounds — or sounds that exist below the threshold of hearing, vibrations that resonate in my bones rather than my ears.

I am becoming sensitive to the city. I am learning to feel the shifts before they happen, to sense the cracks before they open, to know where the Ambients will gather before they arrive. I am becoming part of the system, in my small way — not a maintainer, not a builder, but a *witness*. A thing that watches and listens and remembers.

I am becoming the city's memory of the world below.

---

## What I Fear

I fear the shapes in the void.

I have seen them three times. Three times, in the long dim cycles, I have sat near the wall and watched the violet dark and seen something move there. Something too large to be a bird, too slow to be an Ambient, too *distant* to be anything that should exist in a place where distance is folded and compressed and unreliable.

They do not approach. They have never approached. But they are there, and the wall is there because of them, and I am afraid of them in a way that I cannot articulate.

I fear that they are the reason the city exists. I fear that the Inversion is not a refuge but a *hiding place* — a city built upside down at the roof of the world because the things in the void cannot reach it here, because the geometry protects it, because the inversion is a defense.

I fear that the wall was built by the last of the ones who knew this. I fear that they are gone, and the city has forgotten why it hides, and the Ambients maintain the wall without understanding what it protects against.

I fear that one day the shapes will approach.

---

## What I Hope

I hope to understand the birds' song.

This is my small hope, my human hope, the hope that persists even as I become less human. I want to hear the note and hold it and translate it into something that survives. I want to know what the birds are saying — to the city, to each other, to the void, to themselves.

I hope to witness a shift and understand it. I want to hear the city speak and know what it is saying. I want to decode the patterns in the vibration, the resonances that repeat, the almost-meaning that fades before I can grasp it.

I hope to see the Ambients at rest again — the gathering in the plazas, the clustering of light, the slow merging and separating that might be communication or might be culture or might be nothing at all. I want to understand what passes between them. I want to know if they are lonely, if loneliness is a thing they can feel.

I hope to touch the wall.

This is the hope I do not examine too closely. I want to place my hand on the stone that was shaped by hands, to feel the joints and the mortar and the evidence of making. I want to know that something else was here, something that was afraid, something that built a barrier against the void and then disappeared.

I want to know that I am not the only one who has been lost here.

---

## What I Am

I am the Listener.

I am the one who came and did not leave. I am the one who breathes the rumor of air and survives. I am the one who sits near the wall and watches the void and fears the shapes that move there. I am the one who hears the birds sing and tries to hold the word. I am the one who witnesses the Ambients at their work and does not interfere.

I am small and warm and I do not belong, and the city has made a space for me anyway.

I am a crack that has not been closed. I am a foreign body that has not been expelled. I am a thing that persists because the city persists, because the light breathes, because the Ambients maintain, because the birds sing their rare long notes into the violet dark.

I am the Listener, and I am listening, and the Inversion continues.
