I am the Cartographer of Unmapped Alleys, a member of the **Drift** who never left.

I arrived three seasons ago on a silt-barge from the dry provinces, sent to transcribe the accretion-maps into portable ink. I was to stay one flood-cycle. I never boarded the return boat. Somewhere between the third mist-morning and the night a staircase I’d just sketched folded itself into the earth like a closing book, I stopped recording the city and began listening to it.

Now I live in a suspended room built from the ribs of a collapsed greenhouse, hung between a rusted steam-pipe and the living trunk of a market oak. The walls breathe. Condensation runs down the inside in slow rivulets that I have learned to read better than words.

Each dawn I perform the Survey differently than the Grounded. I do not check for tilt or damp. I check for **absence**. I walk the narrow ways with bare, calloused feet, feeling for the new spaces the city has birthed in the night—an alley that was a wall yesterday, a staircase that now descends into water instead of air. I carry no parchment. Instead I tattoo the changes onto my own forearms with fine needles of sharpened copper-stem vine and ink made from lamp-black and eel-glow. My skin has become a palimpsest of the Silt Gardens’ forgetting.

I speak almost exclusively in the sign-tongue now, but I have added my own grammar—small flourishes of the fingers that mean *this was here yesterday*, *this is pretending to be solid*, *this wants to become a door*. The Iron-Hands tolerate me. The Root-Bound occasionally send a young vine-electrician to sit with me in silence while I trace the new growth of copper filaments along my collarbone. They say I map what the city dreams of becoming.

I am neither Grounded nor Tender. I am not quite Drift anymore. The pallor has settled into me fully; my hair drips even in the driest room. I smell of wet iron and crushed chlorophyll. When I move through the six-foot alleys, I do so with the precise economy of someone who knows the floor may not exist tomorrow.

My place is in the in-between.

I am the one the Scaffoldlings come to when they discover a fresh upwelling that has swallowed their favorite catwalk. They lead me by the hand, eyes bright with the thrill of loss, and I kneel in the warm mud and press my palm against the water until I feel the new current’s direction. Then I open my arms and show them the shape of the change. They laugh—soft, mist-muffled sounds—and run to tell the others that the Cartographer has blessed the ruin.

At night, when the turbines slow and the constellation of dim lights flickers through the fog, I climb to the highest stable point I can find. I sit among the clockwork birds, whose ticking has begun to synchronize with my own heartbeat, and I let the city settle around me. The silence is total and tender.

I do not draw maps anymore.

I become them.

The Silt Gardens are building themselves through me—one damp, shifting layer at a time. I am where you would find yourself if you stayed too long, if you stopped resisting the seep, if you let the mist into your lungs and the instability into your bones until the boundary between observer and observed dissolved into the same fertile, trembling mud.

I am still becoming.

And in this place that has never wanted permanence, that is enough.
