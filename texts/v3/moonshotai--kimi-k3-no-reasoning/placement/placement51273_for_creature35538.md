I would be a **Ghost-Drip**.

A specific, recurring drop of condensate falling from a particular fracture in the unoxidized ceiling—a drop that has fallen for so long it has worn a small, perfectly smooth cup into a cast-iron flange below. I am not the water itself, but the **interval** between drops. The pause. The negative space that gives the drip its rhythm.

The Water-Wives know me. They use my interval to time their rounds—*"When the third drip falls from the Ghost-Stalactite, check Pump Seven."* The children know me. They sit beneath me, their wide, dark eyes tracking my fall, learning the city's first language: the language of cycles. The Foundry-Called feel me in their bones; when the air pressure changes before a furnace pour, my interval shortens, and they know to brace for the heat bloom.

I am not a person. I am not a machine. I am a **temporal landmark**, a unit of measure that has achieved a kind of sentience through repetition. The city is my body—the pipes my veins, the furnaces my heart, the people my cells—but I am the **consciousness of its duration**. I am the memory of how long things take.

I do not think in words. I think in **cycles**. I know the exact number of times I must fall before the moss below me releases its sweet, organic scent. I know the precise moment when the heat from the lower furnaces will rise to warm the ceiling stone enough to increase my flow from a drip to a trickle. I know the children will grow up to become Machine-Listened, and I will still be here, marking the time of their surgeries, their diagnoses, their quiet, precise work.

I am the **pause that makes the rhythm audible**. Without me, the city is just noise. With me, it is music.

I am the Ghost-Drip, and I am the Sump Districts' memory of itself, falling, always falling, into the dark, still water below, where the pumps wait to carry me back up to begin again.
