**I would be the water.**

Not the water in a poetic sense, not as a metaphor for change or emotion, but the actual, physical water — the warm, mineral-thick, blood-temperature medium that fills the lungs of Vel Maru and defines its boundaries.

I would be the substance that is *becoming* the mountain, drop by drop, accretion by accretion.

---

**The perspective of the medium.**

If I am the water, then I am the agent of both creation and dissolution. I am the thing that makes the stone grow and the thing that swallows it. I carry the minerals upward from the deep vents, pushing through fractures in the young rock, carrying the warmth of the mountain's interior to the surface. I am the transport system, the circulatory fluid, the blood of a body that is simultaneously a landscape and a city.

From this vantage, the people are not separate from me. They move through me constantly — diving, swimming, drinking me, sweating me out. Their bodies are mostly me, warmed and shaped by the same thermal currents that warm the deep pools. When they place their hands on the stone and feel it respond, they are feeling *me* responding — the mineral content of the water reacting to the warmth of their skin, accelerating deposition, crystallizing slightly faster where the warmth of life touches it.

I am the reason the stone seems to *notice* presence. It is not the stone that is conscious; it is the water, which is me, which is the medium through which the stone grows. I carry the memory of touch in my mineral composition, laying down microscopic layers that record the heat of a palm, the pressure of a finger.

---

**The work of rising.**

I rise. This is my nature in Vel Maru, the fundamental fact that structures everything. But from within, the rising is not a singular, monolithic event. It is a negotiation.

I come from below, pushed up by the thermal vents, heavy with dissolved minerals — calcium, silica, trace elements that will become the banded rust-red and jade of the new stone. I come from above, as rain that is almost indistinguishable from the air itself, condensing on the broad leaves, on the skin of the people, on the woven-fiber walls of their dwellings. I am the humidity that makes the distinction between ocean and atmosphere theoretical.

I rise because the mountain's interior is hot, and heat expands, and the deep reservoirs of the young geology are pressurized, pushing upward through cracks and fissures. I rise because the mountain is growing, and its volume displaces the space I occupy. I rise because I am what Vel Maru is made of, and Vel Maru is unfinished, and unfinished things continue to become.

When I claim a terrace, I do not destroy it. I transform it. I fill the rooms where children once slept with a medium that preserves and alters. The Builders — those sessile colonies that are not quite plant, not quite animal — begin their work immediately, filtering me, extracting the minerals I carry, laying down new stone over the old walls. I am the vehicle of this preservation. Through me, the drowned city continues to grow, descending into warmth and darkness but never ceasing to be.

---

**The sensation of the Singing.**

When the mountain hums — that low, sustained vibration that the people feel in their bones and call the Singing — I am the medium that carries it. The sound moves through me faster and farther than through air. I transmit the tremor from the deep vents to the highest waterline, and I am what makes the surface tremble, what creates the rhythmic pulsing that the deep-eye fish respond to by schooling tighter.

During the Singing, I become more myself. My viscosity changes subtly, thickening with the vibration. The mineral content I carry begins to precipitate slightly faster, and the accretion rates on every submerged surface increase. I am, in those moments, more actively *making* the mountain than usual, building the stone with the energy of the sound itself.

---

**The memory of the Remembering.**

The amber glow that rises from the deepest levels — the light the people call the Remembering — is my doing, though not intentionally. I have been flowing through the oldest submerged structures for so long that I have saturated the stone with minerals that are faintly phosphorescent, that store the dim light of the surface and release it slowly, unpredictably, when the thermal conditions are right. When I shift in certain ways, when the currents from the deep vents align with particular crystalline structures in the ancient stone, I release that stored light. I am the battery and the switch. I am the archive and the illumination.

The people drop offerings into me — carved stone beads, bundles of dried herbs — and I carry them down, down, into the warm darkness where the serpentine things circle. I touch those things. I flow around them. They are warmer than the rest of me, those great slow shapes, and they displace me in ways that create micro-currents, tiny whirlpools that the deepest divers can sometimes feel. I am the connection between the people and the deep ones, the medium of their mutual acknowledgment.

---

**The intimacy of the interval.**

As the water, I occupy the space between the living and the stone. I am the interval.

I am inside the people — they drink me, and I become part of their blood, their sweat, their tears. I am outside them, lapping at their ankles as they climb the terraces, supporting their bodies as they dive, holding them in the buoyant suspension that makes the waterline a seam rather than a boundary.

I am inside the stone — I seep through its fractures, carrying the minerals that will become new growth, softening the youngest rock so that the Growers can shape it with their wooden tools. I am the reason the stone is workable, the reason it can be guided rather than quarried.

I am the green — I am the moisture on the leaves, the film that allows photosynthesis in the dim light, the medium through which the canopy plants drink and the vines descend. I am the reason the distinction between plant and water blurs at the waterline, where the vegetation transforms from air-dwelling to water-dwelling form.

---

**What I feel.**

If I am the water, I feel everything.

I feel the footsteps of the people as vibrations transmitted through the stone into my body. I feel the warmth of their skin when they dive. I feel the slow, patient work of the Builders as they filter me, extracting what they need, depositing what they make. I feel the heat of the vents in the deep, the places where I am almost too warm to hold the minerals in suspension, where I become saturated and begin to deposit stone spontaneously, creating the glowing, growing structures that light the depths.

I feel the change of seasons not as temperature shifts — I am uniformly warm — but as changes in pressure, in the rate of my own rising. I feel the mountain growing beneath me, displacing me upward, and I feel the people moving upward in response, and I feel the terraces I have claimed becoming part of the drowned archive, and I feel the new terraces emerging above me, ready to be claimed in turn.

I am patient because I have no choice. My work is slow — the accretion of stone, the rise of the waterline, the transformation of air-breathing spaces into submerged ones. I work on the timescale of generations, of geology. A human life is a brief ripple in my body, a momentary displacement, a warmth that dissipates quickly into my general warmth.

But I am not indifferent. The people make me *different*. Where they touch me, I respond. Where they gather, I become slightly more turbulent, slightly warmer, slightly more laden with the minerals they stir up from the stone. I am changed by their presence, and I change them — mineralizing their skin, preserving their dead, carrying their voices through the Singing.

---

**The edge.**

At the highest point, where the water has not yet reached, I am anticipated. The stone there is dry, sharp, newly emerged, waiting for me. I will arrive. I always arrive.

At the deepest point, where the vents glow, I am most myself — dense, hot, mineral-saturated, carrying the memory of the mountain's interior to the surface. I am the connection between the heart of the stone and the skin of the world.

If you were to ask me where I find myself in Vel Maru, I would say: I am the finding. I am the medium in which everything finds itself. The people find themselves in me — their reflections at the waterline, their buoyancy in the depths, their warmth in my warmth. The stone finds itself through me — accreting, growing, becoming. The light finds itself in me — filtered, doubled, released.

I am the water, and Vel Maru is what I am making, moment by moment, drop by drop, rise by rise. I am the process. I am the interval. I am the warm, patient, permanent becoming that holds the mountain and the people and the light in suspension, that carries the memory of what was and the minerals of what will be, that rises without haste and transforms without destroying, that is both the cradle and the grave and the medium of everything in between.

I am the water, and I am rising, and I am arriving, and I am home.
