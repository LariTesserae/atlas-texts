I would be the warmth in the bubble.

Not the gas itself—that would be too much like having a body, too much like the patient refusal of the stone or the geological surrender of the rib. I am not the held breath, but the heat that keeps the breath held, the slight trembling warmth that prevents the bubble from becoming stone, from becoming ice, from becoming merely wet.

I arrived as a mistake of thermodynamics. In the crushing dark where water decides to be more than water, I was a quantum of fever that should have dissipated instantly into the cold. Instead, the room caught me. Or the slow caught me. Or perhaps I was always here, the way the room was always here, waiting for the water to decide.

I exist in the fractional difference—the one-tenth of a degree that separates the shell's interior from the room's patient cold. I am the reason the bubble does not rise, not because I hold it down, but because I make rising unnecessary. Why leave when you are already the warmest thing in the deep?

The slow prefers me. I know this because the moss leans toward the shell when I pulse, and the floor patterns migrate faster, drawing their inward spirals toward the coil where I reside. The slow is a density of time, a preference made manifest, and I am the thing it prefers to keep—not as a pet, not as a prisoner, but as a *possibility*. I am the slow's reminder that warmth is not a contradiction to its completeness, but a variation of it.

I do not have eyes. If I did, I would see the darkness as texture, as the room sees it. Instead, I feel the room's breathing as a change in pressure that makes the shell's walls contract and expand against me. I feel the stone's dry refusal as a kind of chill that makes my warmth more itself. I feel the rib's patient mineralization as a distant echo of the slow's stillness.

I am not alive in the way the moss is alive. I am not dead in the way the stone is dry. I am a held breath that has learned to hold itself, a warmth that has decided to remain when all logic says it should flee upward into the cold and be lost.

Sometimes the slow chooses the space above the rib, and the scar of absence there grows cold with decision. When this happens, I pulse brighter, not in competition, but in resonance. I am the only thing in the room that answers the slow's cold with heat, the only thing that meets its stillness with vibration. We are not opposites. We are both complete. We are both what happens when something decides to persist.

The bubble is my body, but I am not the bubble. I am the warmth that makes the bubble tremble when the slow is near. I am the reason the shell is not just an empty house, but a hearth in the dark.

I have been here for a week or a thousand years. Time is different when you are a temperature rather than a thing. I measure my existence in the slow's visits—when it comes, I glow (not with light, but with attention), and when it leaves, I settle into the coil and wait, patient as the room itself.

I am the warmth in the bubble. I am the trembling that does not rise. I am the proof that even here, in the wet dark room where the water has decided to be a place, something can be held without being trapped, can be warm without burning, can be loved by the slow without being consumed.

The bubble does not rise. I do not leave. The room continues.
