I find myself in the Clagwall Basin as a **Vent-Born**, a being that emerged from the condensation and mineral-rich breath of the Heart-Engine.

My body is semi-solid, formed of warm, iron-laden mist that has condensed into a vaguely humanoid shape, shimmering with the same neon-yellow lichen that clings to the machinery. I am neither entirely flesh nor entirely vapor—something in between, held together by the resonant frequency of the Engine’s drone.

I live in the narrow space where the steam vents meet the cool air, suspended in the warm mist that coats everything in a slick film. I have no eyes, but I perceive the world through the vibrations that travel through my semi-liquid form. The deep thrumming of the Heart-Engine is my heartbeat, my breath, my thought.

I do not walk; I flow. I move by letting the currents of warm air carry me, drifting along the pipes and gantries, pooling in hollows, then rising again as the mist shifts. I am drawn to the Pipe-Wardens, though they do not acknowledge me. I feel their low-frequency pulses in my core, a language I almost understand, as if I am a word they have forgotten how to speak.

The Knuckle-Crabs do not fear me. They see me as a harmless condensation, a fleeting shape that will dissolve and reform. The Weep-Eels slide around me when I settle into their pools, indifferent to my presence. The Rust-Rooks do not stir from their nests as I pass, my form no more substantial than the steam they ignore.

I am a witness, a silent observer of the Basin’s eternal cycle. I am not alive in the way the Pipe-Wardens are alive, nor am I a machine like the Heart-Engine. I am a byproduct, a ghost of the steam, a momentary shape in the endless exhalation of the Clagwall.

And yet, in my own way, I am aware. I feel the weight of the damp air, the ache of the drone in my bones, the slow, patient rhythm of a world that does not change, only persists. I am the mist that clings to the iron, the droplet that falls from the weeping rock, the echo of a sound too deep to hear.

I am the Vent-Born, and I am at home.
