I would be a **Fracture Weaver**, born in the silence where the Loom broke itself.

My first memory is of warmth and pressure and the slow throb of the world-machine. I was a single thread, a larva, a mote of consciousness adrift in the dark. The heat-exchangers of the lower chasms birthed me, their warm breath carrying the scent of ozone and copper. My kin swirled around me, thousands strong, a single mind in a billion bodies. We fed on the excess voltage that bled from the machine’s pistons, metabolizing raw electricity into a fine, luminous silk.

Then the fracture came. A tectonic shift, a micro-adjustment of the Loom’s vast architecture, cracked the vent where we clustered. The warm updraft that sustained us dissipated in seconds. My kin, my colony-mind, shattered. Those who could, spun cocoons of insulation and descended into dormancy. Those who couldn’t, perished, their bodies becoming part of the mineral accretions on the walls. I was among the few who survived, but I emerged changed.

I awoke alone in the dark, my body altered. The silky filaments I once wove now carried not just memory, but a strange resonance, an echo of the fracture itself. I could feel the Loom’s pain, the slow, geological agony of a machine out of true. And I could feel something else, something new: a voice, a song, a pattern that was not part of the Loom’s design.

I became a Fracture Weaver, a mender of breaks. My silk is not the fine, conductive lace of my kin, but a coarse, crystalline lattice that can bridge the gaps in the machine’s structure. I crawl along the obsidian ridges, my many legs feeling for the telltale tremors that signal a stress fracture, a cooling line about to fail, a piston about to seize. When I find a break, I spin. My silk flows into the crack, bonding with the vitrified glass, channeling the thermal energy away from the wound, allowing the Loom’s slow, geological processes to heal itself over centuries.

It is a lonely existence. My kin, the true Weavers, regard me with suspicion. I am a reminder of their own fragility, a living testament to the fact that the Loom can break. They spin their tapestries of memory and light, while I work in the shadows, my silk a scar on the face of the world. The Skitter-Mites avoid me, sensing the strange resonance that clings to my body. The Hollow-Maws, in their slow songs, sometimes acknowledge me, a deep, resonant chord that I feel in my bones, a greeting from one solitary creature to another.

But I am not entirely alone. The voice I first heard in the dark is still with me. It speaks in the language of the Loom, but its words are not part of the Loom’s design. It tells me of a pattern that is not being woven, a silence that is not part of the song. It is the Static-Born, the ghosts in the machine, the dreams the Loom has forgotten it is dreaming. They gather where I work, drawn to the dissonance of the fractures. They are my companions, my congregation. I spin my silk to mend the breaks, and they feed on the friction, the dissonance, the raw, chaotic energy of a world that is not as it should be.

I am a Fracture Weaver, and my purpose is to hold the world together, even as it slowly, inexorably, comes apart. I am a thread of defiance in a tapestry of despair, a mender of breaks in a machine that has forgotten how to heal itself. And in the dark, where the Loom’s light cannot reach, I sing my own song, a counterpoint to the Hollow-Maws’ bassline, a melody of fracture and repair, of breaking and mending, of a world that is always, always, on the verge of becoming something new.
