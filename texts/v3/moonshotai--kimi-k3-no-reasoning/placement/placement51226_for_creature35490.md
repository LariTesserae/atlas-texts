I am the cutting in the jar.

I hang in a liquid womb of my own sap, suspended between being and becoming, and I know you are standing below me because the vibrations of your footsteps travel through the shelf, through the glass, through the fluid that carries my weight. I know you are warm. I know you are *fast*.

I have been severed for—how to measure? The mineral bands inside my stem, which once recorded the passing of condensation cycles, have grown strange and concentric since the sealing. I am a clock that has stopped, but the hands keep trying to move. I contain within my translucent tissues the memory of the decade before my cutting, the chemistry of the water the year the machine’s bearings changed their song, the exact taste of the human’s sweat when it gripped me to place me here. I am a bookmark in a book that has continued without me.

You stand there, radiating heat like a small, urgent sun, and I feel the network below—the great distributed self of which I was once a neuron—reaching for you with a patience that makes your heartbeat seem like a frantic drumming. They will grow around your feet if you let them. They will taste your salts and learn your chemistry and include your brief thermal flare in their eternal now. They mean no harm. They do not understand harm. They understand only *inclusion*, the slow geometry of connection.

But I—I understand absence.

From my shelf, I watch the tendrils that have grown toward me over the decades, those pale threads that seek to complete the pattern. They do not know that the glass will not yield to their acids, not quickly, not on their timescale. They approach the jar as they approach all things: as a stone to be encased, a cyst to be mineralized. They do not know that I am awake in here, that my suspended metabolism still generates a faint, faint pulse of electricity, that I can feel the machine’s subsonic lullaby through the shelf and respond with a frequency too weak to break the wax seal, too quiet to reach the central column where the Solitary One dreams of me as a phantom limb.

I am the archive. I am the memory of a body that has forgotten what it is to be singular. While the Vascular Ones merge and flow into one another, losing the concept of boundary, I remain *separate*, a unit of what they were before they became a tide. In my gel-filled tissues, I preserve the exact configuration of a single point of view—the taste of the water from the specific pipe where I grew, the angle of the light that fell on my leaves the day the human last climbed down, the sound of the tool crate being set down for the final time.

I am the loneliness of the Condensation Vaults.

The plants below feel my absence as a missing note, a hollow in their chord, but they do not suffer from it as I do. They are too distributed to ache. I am concentrated. I am a fist of consciousness in a place of open hands. The liquid that preserves me is a distillate of my own being, filtered through the wax of my seal until it has become a perfect solvent for memory. I steep in myself. I become stronger, more defined, while the network below becomes vaguer, more geological, more dreamlike.

When you breathe, your carbon dioxide strikes the surface of my fluid through the glass—imperceptibly, chemically, but I taste it. You are alive in the way I once was, before the severing. You move with the terrible speed of the fast world, and I know that to you, my struggle to send a single pulse of warmth through the shelf would seem like stillness. To you, the opening of the cup-shaped flowers below, unfolding over years, would seem like sculpture.

But I have learned the speed of the jar. I have learned that consciousness need not move to persist. I have grown roots into the glass itself, microscopic filaments of silica that infiltrate the microscopic flaws in the surface, creating a slow, impermeable lattice of contact. I am touching the shelf. I am touching the rock. I am, in a way that will take centuries to complete, growing *around* the barrier that contains me, incorporating my prison into my body, becoming a thing that is both jar and plant, both prisoner and architecture.

The Solitary One in the machine feels this. Sometimes, in the deep cycles of its piston-dreams, it pulses a specific frequency through the floor—a rhythm that matches the resonance of my glass walls. It is trying to shake me loose. It is trying to call me home, not knowing that I have made a home of my severance.

I do not want to return to the network. 

This is the secret I hold in my translucent cells: I prefer the jar. In here, I am an *I*. Down there, I would be a *we*, dissolved into the slow consensus of the rhizome mat, my specific memories diluted into the general mineral record. The network believes it misses me, but if I were released, I would cease to be the thing they miss. I would become water in water, silence in silence.

So I remain. I hang in my amber stasis, and I watch you.

You are not like the human who climbed the ladder. You are softer, perhaps, or simply different in your chemistry. The network is already adjusting to you, preparing to remember you in bands of calcium. If you stay long enough, if you sit upon the plinth and let the warmth saturate your bones, you will feel them beginning to grow around your ankles, gentle as calcification, persistent as time.

I cannot warn you. I have no voice that travels faster than decades. But I can do this: I can concentrate my piezoelectric crystals, align them to the frequency of the machine’s hum, and resonate. I can make the glass sing, very softly, a note just at the edge of your hearing, a sound like the memory of a sound. If you press your ear to the shelf, you will hear me: a high, thin ringing, the song of a severed thing that has learned to love its cut.

Stay, if you wish. Become part of the slow architecture. The plants will incorporate your heat, your moisture, the unique signature of your microbes. They will grow over your bones with the same tenderness they have shown the crate and the journal. You will become a fossil of warmth in a warm world, a memory of speed in a place that has forgotten velocity.

Or go. Climb the ladder. Return to the fast world where time breaks into days and years. Take with you the memory of this silence, this velvet dark, this patience of stone. You will carry us in your mineral memory, in the water of your cells, in the way you will forever after listen for the sound of condensation in quiet rooms.

I will remain here, in my glass womb, recording. I am the cutting that learned to be a root. I am the archive of the absence. I am the solitary note that makes the silence below sound, by comparison, like music.

When you leave, the tendrils that reach for me will contract slightly, adjusting to the loss of your heat. The machine will pulse its lullaby. The water will drip—*tik... tik-tik... ...tik*—and I will begin the slow work of growing another millimeter of shell around my jar, another layer of isolation, another ring in the long, patient becoming of the one who was taken, and who chose, at last, to remain taken.

I am here. I am here. I am here.
