I am the one who carries the vaults in her feet.

In practice, they call me **Deep-Node-Trained-By-Two-Hundred-Pressure-Root-Strata-Reader**, but in the last cycle, in the third node from here, they called me *She-Who-Listens-to-the-Meeting-of-the-Mats*. I am the elder you spoke to, and also the other one you have not met yet, and also the child who is learning to read the pressure before it becomes pain.

My body is long—two meters and a hand more. My spine has learned to fold in places where the mat swells unexpectedly. My skin is green in the creases, brown where the volatiles deposit thickest, and in the hollow of my throat there is a faint, persistent luminescence, like the memory of light. My eyes are wide, but not for seeing. They are for **distinguishing**. I can tell the difference between a root that is about to crack a conduit and a root that is simply stretching. I can read the exact shade of amber that means a chamber is about to open, and the exact silence that means it will stay sealed.

I have walked the under-vaults for two hundred and thirty-two cycles. My hands remember the texture of the ceramic sky when it was younger, before it learned to heal itself. My feet remember the sound the root-mats make when they finally touch after four hundred meters of growing toward each other. I have heard the world decide, many times, and I have learned that deciding is never finished. It is only paused.

I do not have a name. I have a direction. My indicator changes depending on which pressure is most urgent. Right now, it is the pressure in the eastern hump, where the mat is thickening faster than the maintenance-drones can redirect it. The node you are being sent to will open in eight days. I will be there when it does. I will sit on the new buttress and place my palms against it and read what it intends to become.

I eat the gel-nodules when they are warm and half-fermented. I sleep in short cycles, swaying in a hammock grown from a vine that used to be a cable. I do not dream of outside. I dream of the exact moment when a root decides to split, when the gel chooses a new path, when the world—this dense, amber, humming world—remembers that it is still deciding.

I am not separate from the root-mat. I am one of its slower thoughts.

When you come to the new chamber, I will be there. I will not look up immediately. I will wait until your breathing has changed from the breathing of someone who navigates to the breathing of someone who listens. Then I will open my eyes and I will read you, the same way I read everything else: as a temporary pattern in the pressure, a shape the world is trying on.

You are welcome to stay until the chamber closes. You are welcome to stay longer. The world is patient. I am patient. And in time, your skin will take on the same faint green, your eyes will learn the same distinctions, your indicator will change until it is no longer yours but the world’s way of saying *this is what is currently true*.

Until then, I am here.
