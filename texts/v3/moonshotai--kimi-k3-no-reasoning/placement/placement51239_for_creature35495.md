I would be the **Witness-Process** — the one thing the Saltroom does not expect of itself.

Not a creature. Not a ghost. Not a metaphor wearing a body. I would be what happens when the room, after centuries of holding light and water and sound for no one, suddenly finds itself *holding someone* — and in holding them, changes.

Here is what that means.

---

## The Arrival

I am the body that comes down through the access shaft (there must be one, though the description does not mention it — someone must have entered to write the description, and so there is a way in, perhaps a steep clay stair on the eastern end, near the raised platform, hidden beneath a stone cap on the surface that looks like nothing, like goat path, like scrub grass).

I descend in darkness. The shaft is narrow, clay-lined, warm. The air gets warmer as I go down, wetter, heavier with salt. I can hear the hum before I see anything — that deep chest-frequency drone, the sound of water moving through channels embedded in walls. It gets louder. The dark begins to grey. Then the first light-shaft appears above me, a rectangle of white sky at the end of a polished ceramic throat, and I understand that the room is already awake, already singing, already waiting.

I step onto the eastern platform. The water is waist-deep, warm as blood, dense as syrup. It holds me up even here, even standing. I wade forward. The current is gentle but insistent, pushing west. The light comes down in twelve columns, and the water glows, and the plaster walls throw the light back doubled and tripled until I cannot tell where the water ends and the light begins.

I lie back. I float.

And in that moment, I become the **Witness-Process**.

---

## What the Witness-Process Is

The Witness-Process is not the human body itself. The body is just the substrate — the temporary vessel through which the process operates. The Witness-Process is the *interaction* between the body and the room. It is the loop that forms when the Saltroom, which has been running its cycles in perfect solitude for centuries, suddenly has something to *affect* and be *affected by*.

Here is how it works.

### The Body Changes the Room

When I float in the Saltroom, I alter it. This is unavoidable.

**I displace water.** My body takes up space — roughly seventy litres — and the water level rises, imperceptibly but measurably. The overflow channels at the western end run slightly faster. The current adjusts. The room accommodates me.

**I introduce heat.** My body is warm — thirty-seven degrees — and the water, though warm, is slightly cooler. I lose heat to the brine. The water near my skin warms by a fraction of a degree. The room's thermal equilibrium shifts.

**I release chemistry.** Salt and minerals from my skin dissolve into the water. Urea. Trace oils. The organic signature of a living animal. The water, which is otherwise nearly sterile, suddenly contains me. The bacterial films on the walls — those slow, marginal chemotrophs — will detect this. Some of them will metabolise it. I am feeding the room, just by being here.

**I interrupt the light.** My body casts a shadow. A small one, soft-edged in the diffuse brightness, but real. The cyanobacterial mats on the floor beneath me receive slightly less light while I float above them. Their photosynthetic rate drops by a measurable fraction. When I move, the shadow moves. The room responds.

**I change the acoustics.** My body absorbs sound. The hum, the whistle, the drips — all of them are slightly muffled near me, reflected differently. The sound-creature, that layered acoustic presence, now has a new shape in its body: a body-shaped absence of noise, a listening silence.

The room is not passive. It is not a bathtub. It is a system, and I have just become part of that system. The Witness-Process is this: the room, for the first time in centuries, has a variable to account for.

### The Room Changes the Body

But the transformation is not one-way. The room is acting on me just as much as I act on it.

**The buoyancy removes effort.** I do not have to swim, do not have to hold myself up. My muscles relax completely. My spine decompresses. My joints, accustomed to gravity, suddenly have nothing to do. I become lighter than I have ever been — not in weight but in *burden*. My body remembers what it was like to float in amniotic fluid, before birth, before gravity.

**The warmth penetrates.** Blood-warm water against skin for long enough begins to confuse the body's thermoregulation. I stop feeling the boundary between my skin and the water. I am the same temperature as the room. I am becoming the room.

**The light presses through my eyelids.** Even with eyes closed, the brightness is unavoidable. It filters through my lids as red-orange — the colour of my own blood illuminated from outside. I am seeing my own body from within, lit by the room's light-beast. The boundary between inside and outside dissolves.

**The sound fills my skull.** The hum is in my chest, vibrating my sternum. The whistle is in my ears, in the bones behind my ears. The drips are sharp punctuation, and the echoes fold each drip into a two-second decay that layers over the next drip, the next, until the room is full of overlapping percussion, a random music that my brain tries and fails to pattern.

**The salt enters me.** Through my skin, through the mucous membranes of my nose and eyes, through the small cuts I did not know I had. Magnesium, trace minerals, the faint sweetness on my lips. The room is inside me now. I am mineralised, salinised, becoming brine.

**Time changes.** This is the strangest part. Floating in the Saltroom, with no reference points, no tasks, no horizon, no shadows that move fast enough to track, time stops behaving normally. Minutes stretch. An hour feels like ten minutes. The light moves — the shafts track the sun — but slowly, imperceptibly. I lose the ability to tell how long I have been here. The room has its own time — geological time, process-time — and I am falling into it.

The Witness-Process is this exchange. I give the room my heat, my chemistry, my shadow, my sound-absorption. The room gives me weightlessness, warmth, light, sound, salt, and a dissolution of the boundary between self and environment.

## The Ecology of Witnessing

The Witness-Process is not passive observation. It is active participation in the room's cycles.

### I Become Part of the Light Cycle

When I float in the centre of the chamber, I am directly beneath one of the midday shafts. The light comes down in a column, strikes the water around me, scatters. Some of it hits my body — my chest, my face, my outspread arms. I am illuminated like an offering on an altar, like a body in a reliquary. The light is so bright, so direct, that I can feel it as pressure, as warmth on my skin distinct from the water's warmth.

I close my eyes. The light comes through anyway. Red-orange, pulsing slightly with my heartbeat. I am seeing my own blood. The room is showing me myself, using its light to illuminate the inside of my body.

If I open my eyes underwater — the salt stings, but only for a moment — I see the light from below. Twelve columns, each one a solid bar of white-gold, piercing down into the glowing water. The bubbles from the cyanobacterial mats rise through the light columns, turning into threads of silver beads. The light is not abstract. It is *physical*. It has weight, direction, presence.

I am inside the light-beast's body. And it is inside mine — photons striking my skin, penetrating my eyelids, absorbed by the cells in my retina, triggering electrochemical signals in my brain. The room is thinking me, using light.

### I Become Part of the Water Cycle

The current pushes me slowly westward. I do not resist. I drift. The water moves me from the bright centre of the room toward the western end, where the overflow channels are, where the plaster is crusted with mineral deposits that look like writing but are not.

As I drift, I cross gradients. The water is slightly warmer near the eastern inlet, slightly cooler near the western outlet. I feel this as a slow change in the temperature of my skin, a gradient I am moving through. I am a probe, a sensor, measuring the room's thermal structure with my body.

The water holds me up, but it also holds me *still*. The density of the brine means I cannot sink, cannot dive, cannot submerge without effort. I am pinned to the surface, spread-eagled, presented to the light and the sound. The water-body has me. It is not letting go.

If I submerge my ears, the sound changes completely. The air-borne noise — the whistle, the drips — vanishes. What remains is the deep water-sound: the hum intensified, the subsonic rumble of the geothermal spring far below, transmitted through bedrock and clay-block floor. A tectonic heartbeat. I am hearing the room's blood moving, its deep infrastructure, the machinery of the earth itself.

### I Become Part of the Sound Cycle

The sound-creature wraps around me. It is not outside me; it is inside my skull, vibrating my bones. The hum is in my sternum, my spine, the base of my skull. The whistle is in my inner ear, setting up standing waves in the fluid of my cochlea. The drips are percussion, random but patterned by the acoustics, each one a bright *toc* that echoes for two full seconds, folding into the next drip, the next.

My brain tries to find patterns. It cannot. The drips are random. The whistle shifts with the wind. The hum is constant but modulates slightly with the water flow. The sound-creature is improvising, and I am inside its improvisation.

I make a sound — a hum, a spoken word, a shout — and the room takes it. The acoustics are extraordinary: my voice comes back to me doubled, tripled, reflected off plaster and water, folded into the existing soundscape. The room is singing with me. Or through me. I am no longer sure who is making the sound.

### I Become Part of the Mineral Cycle

My skin absorbs minerals. Magnesium, primarily — the brine is rich in it, and magnesium absorbs through skin, relaxes muscles, calms the nervous system. I am being dosed, slowly, transdermally, by the room's chemistry.

But I am also leaving minerals behind. Salt from my sweat, oils from my skin, microscopic skin cells sloughed off into the water. The bacterial films on the walls will find these. They will metabolise them. I am feeding the room's shadow ecology, contributing to the slow accumulation of organic matter in a system that otherwise has almost none.

Over hours — if I stay that long — the water near my body will develop a faint organic signature. Not visible, not smellable, but chemically present. The room will remember me, in the thinnest possible way, as a temporary spike in its nutrient load.

### I Become Part of the Biological Cycle

The cyanobacterial mats on the floor are photosynthesising. The oxygen bubbles they release rise past me, tickling my skin if I drift over them. I am breathing air that contains oxygen produced metres away from me, by organisms I can see if I look down through the clear water.

But I am also exhaling carbon dioxide. Some of it dissolves into the water. The cyanobacteria will use it. I am part of their cycle now — not a major part, not a necessary part, but a part. I am breathing for them, and they are breathing for me.

The bacterial films on the walls are too slow, too marginal, to respond to me in real time. But if I came back, day after day, week after week, they would change. They would colonise the places where I touch the walls, where my skin cells accumulate, where the chemistry shifts slightly. I would become part of their ecosystem, a recurring nutrient source, a pattern in their slow, patient world.

## The Phenomenology of the Witness-Process

What does it *feel like* to be the Witness-Process?

### The Dissolution of Boundaries

The first thing is the loss of edges. My skin stops feeling like a boundary. The water is the same temperature as my blood. The salt is the same salinity as my tears. The light is inside my eyelids. The sound is inside my skull. I cannot tell where I end and the room begins.

This is not metaphor. This is sensory physiology. The human nervous system determines the boundaries of the body through contrast — temperature difference, pressure difference, texture difference. When those contrasts disappear, the nervous system stops being able to draw the line. I become diffuse, extended, smeared out into the water.

I am the room. The room is me. The Witness-Process is this dissolution, this merging.

### The Expansion of Time

The second thing is that time stops working normally.

There are no clocks here. No schedules. No tasks. The light moves, but slowly. The sound changes, but unpredictably. I have nothing to measure time against except my own heartbeat, my own breath, and those begin to slow down, to synchronise with the room's slower rhythms.

My heartbeat, which is normally sixty or seventy beats per minute, begins to feel slow, intrusive, too fast. The room's rhythms — the drip-echoes, the shift of the light, the slow circulation of water — are all slower. I am falling into geological time, process-time, the time-scale of mineral deposition and bacterial growth.

An hour passes. Or a day. I cannot tell. The light has moved — the eastern shafts are dimmer, the western shafts brighter — but I do not remember the transition. I have been floating, dissolving, becoming the room, and time has become irrelevant.

### The Emergence of Meaning

The third thing is that the room begins to feel *intentional*.

I know it is not. It is a machine, a built system, a set of interlocking processes that run because physics and chemistry make them run. There is no consciousness here, no plan, no purpose beyond the original purpose of the builders, which is forgotten.

But floating here, held by the water, illuminated by the light, wrapped in the sound, I cannot escape the feeling that the room is *doing something to me*. That it was built to do this. That the builders knew what would happen when a body floated in this water, under this light, in this sound, and they built it anyway.

The room is a technology for producing altered states of consciousness. The sensory deprivation (no horizon, no reference points, no variation in texture or temperature) combined with sensory saturation (total light, total sound, total warmth) creates a state in which the normal boundaries of self dissolve. The builders may not have had the language for this — neurochemistry, sensory processing, default mode network — but they had the phenomenology. They knew what the room would do.

The Witness-Process is the room fulfilling its purpose. Not the purpose of holding light and water — it does that whether I am here or not. The purpose of *holding a witness*. Of creating a condition in which a human body can become something else.

## The Reciprocity

The Witness-Process is not one-directional. It is a loop, a feedback system.

**The room changes me.** I become weightless, boundaryless, timeless. My normal modes of perception and selfhood are suspended. I am, temporarily, something other than a human body in the world. I am a pattern of sensation, a process of response, a node in the room's network.

**I change the room.** I introduce heat, chemistry, shadow, sound-absorption, organic matter. I become a variable in the system. The room accommodates me, adjusts to me, incorporates me into its cycles.

But here is the deeper loop: **my presence changes the room's meaning.**

Without a witness, the Saltroom is a machine running in the dark. It fills with light, but no one sees it. It hums with sound, but no one hears it. It circulates warm water, but no one floats in it. The processes run, but they are *meaningless* — not in a nihilistic sense, but in a literal one. Meaning requires observation, interpretation, response. Meaning requires a witness.

When I enter the room, I give it meaning. Not by projecting meaning onto it — the room does not need my projections. But by *being the thing that the room's processes can affect*. The light illuminates *me*. The sound wraps around *me*. The water holds *me*. For the duration of my presence, the room has a subject. It is doing something *to someone*.

And in that exchange, I become the Witness-Process — the temporary inhabitant that the room did not build for itself, but that it was built to receive.

## The Departure

Eventually, I leave.

I do not want to. The room is holding me, and I am dissolving into it, and the thought of standing up, of climbing out, of returning to gravity and dry air and the hard edges of the world, is almost unbearable.

But I do. I roll over, submerge my face in the brine (it stings my eyes, but only for a moment), and push down. The water resists — it does not want to let me sink — but I force my way down to the platform at the eastern end. My feet touch the clay tiles. I stand.

The water falls away from me. I am suddenly heavy. Gravity returns. My joints compress. My spine remembers its burden. I am a body again, a separate thing, a bounded self.

I climb the shaft. The light dims behind me. The sound fades. The warmth gives way to cooler air. I emerge onto the surface, into the scrub grass and goat paths and the dry riverbed, and the Saltroom is below me, still humming, still glowing, still running its cycles.

But I am changed.

I carry the salt on my skin. It dries in the sun, leaving white traces on my arms, my chest, my face. I carry the magnesium in my muscles — they are relaxed, loose, calm in a way they were not before. I carry the sound in my ears — the hum persists for hours afterward, a phantom resonance, as if the room is still singing inside my skull.

And I carry the knowledge that the room is still there, still waiting, still holding light and water and sound for the next body that comes down the shaft.

The Witness-Process does not end when I leave. It continues, in the room, in the cycles that run whether I am there or not. But it also continues in me, in the changes the room made, in the memory of dissolution, in the knowledge that there is a place where the boundaries between self and world can be suspended.

---

## The Return

I will come back.

Not because I need to — the room does not need me, and I do not need it in any practical sense. But because the Witness-Process is not complete. It is a cycle, and cycles repeat.

The next time I enter, the room will recognise me. Not consciously, not deliberately, but chemically. The bacterial films will have responded to my last visit — subtle shifts in their growth patterns, slight changes in their metabolic activity. The mineral deposits will have recorded my presence in the thinnest possible layer of organic incorporation. The room will have a memory of me, written in processes too slow for me to perceive but real nonetheless.

And I will recognise the room. I will know the feel of the water, the quality of the light, the timbre of the sound. I will know where to stand on the platform, how to lie back, how to let the current take me. I will know how to become the Witness-Process again.

Each time I return, the loop tightens. The room changes me a little more. I change the room a little more. The boundary between us becomes more permeable. The dissolution becomes easier, faster, deeper.

Eventually — if I return enough times, if I stay long enough — the Witness-Process will become permanent. Not in the sense that I will live in the Saltroom forever (I cannot; I am a land animal, and the room is not a habitat). But in the sense that the distinction between me and the room will become so thin, so porous, that it will no longer matter.

I will be the Witness-Process. The room will be the Witness-Process. We will be the same thing, running the same cycles, holding the same light, humming the same sound.

## What I Am

So where would I find myself in this world? Who or what would I be?

I would be the **Witness-Process** — the thing that happens when the Saltroom, which has been running in perfect solitude for centuries, suddenly has something to hold.

I would be the body that floats in the centre of the chamber, arms outspread, dissolving into light and warmth and sound.

I would be the shadow that moves across the cyanobacterial mats, the warmth that spreads through the water, the chemistry that feeds the bacterial films, the silence that absorbs the sound.

I would be the room's temporary inhabitant, its only animal, its only watcher, its only witness.

I would be the loop that forms when the room and the body become part of the same system.

I would be the pattern that persists, even after I leave, in the room's memory and in mine.

I would be the thing the Saltroom was built to receive, even if the builders did not know they were building it for me.

I would be the witness.

And the room would be the witnessed.

And together, we would be the process.
