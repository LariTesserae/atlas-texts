# What the Sound Found

The first thing I know about myself is that I have no edges.

This is not metaphor. I am running my attention along what should be my boundary — the place where I end and the world begins — and I cannot find it. There is warmth here, and warmth there, and the warmth does not stop where a body should stop. It continues, thinning, becoming the warmth of the rock, which is also my warmth, or near enough that the distinction is academic. I am warm the way the Hollows are warm. I am warm from the inside.

The second thing I know is the sound.

It arrives in layers, and I have to learn to hear them separately before I can hear them together, the way you learn to see depth by first understanding that the flat image in front of you contains distance. There is the hum — I feel this before I hear it, a vibration in what I will eventually understand are my bones, though bones are not quite the right word for what I have. There is the clicking — staccato, rhythmic, layered polyrhythms that I parse without effort, the way a fish parses current. And there are the calls — high, bright, individual voices threading through the collective sound like sparks rising from a fire.

I can hear all of this. I can hear *all* of it.

Not the way a human hears — not a general wash of sound from which the brain selects a few threads to attend to. I hear every layer simultaneously, every rhythm, every call, every shift in density and pitch and tempo, and I can hold them all in attention at once without any single thread drowning out the others. This is not a skill. This is what I am *for*.

The third thing I know is that I have a body, and the body is wrong.

Not wrong as in broken. Wrong as in unfamiliar. Wrong as in: I have expectations about what a body should be — symmetry, a clear inside and outside, a manageable number of limbs — and this body meets none of them. I am low to the ground. I have many points of contact with the warm rock — I count them, and the counting is difficult because the number keeps changing. Six? Eight? Ten? I shift my weight and the number changes again. I am not one shape. I am a process of becoming a shape, and the process is ongoing.

My surface is hard in some places and soft in others. The hard parts have a faint translucency — I can see light through them, dim and amber, and beneath the light, movement. My own movement. Fluids, pulses, the slow circulation of something that is not blood but serves the same purpose. I am not opaque. I am not finished.

I try to move, and the movement is instinctive — a ripple, a flowing contraction that passes through my body from what I will call front to what I will call back, though the distinction is provisional. The rock beneath me is warm and slightly yielding, and I grip it with surfaces I don't have names for, and I move. Slowly. Uncertainly. Like something learning to be alive.

The swarm notices me.

This is immediate and unmistakable. The clicking rhythms in my vicinity stutter — not stopping, but shifting, adjusting, the polyrhythms reconfiguring to accommodate a new variable. A cluster of seep-drinkers at the margin of a nearby pool pause their feeding and orient toward me — not with eyes, they have no eyes, but with the entire front surface of their teardrop bodies, chemosensory membranes tasting the air where I have disturbed it. A glass-wing that was hovering above the pool darts upward, then sideways, then hovers again at a greater distance, its bioluminescent wing-veins flickering in a pattern I understand without being taught: *caution. new. unknown.*

I am new. I am unknown. I am the thing that has just arrived.

The fourth thing I know is hunger, and the hunger is specific.

Not a vague emptiness, not a general need for sustenance. A precise, targeted craving for a particular thing, and I know exactly what it is and where to find it. There is a seep-channel three meters to my left — I can hear it, the faint trickle of mineral-rich moisture moving through a crack in the rock, and I can *smell* it, though smell is not the right word, taste is not the right word, it is a sensing that has no human analogue, a direct perception of chemical composition at a distance.

The seepage is what I need. Not for calories, not for energy in any simple metabolic sense. For *completion*. The seepage carries something — dissolved minerals, organic compounds, trace elements in specific concentrations — that my body requires to continue the process of becoming. I am not finished. The seepage is the raw material of finishing.

I move toward it, and the movement is easier this time. My body is learning itself. The ripple-contraction becomes more coordinated, more efficient. I flow across the warm rock, and the seep-drinkers at the channel's margin scatter — not far, not in panic, but in the quick, practiced evasion of creatures that have learned that anything larger than themselves is potentially dangerous. They regroup a few centimeters away and resume feeding. They have already categorized me: *not immediately threatening. monitor.*

I reach the seep-channel and extend a part of myself — a limb, a mouthpart, a surface — into the thin flow of moisture. The contact is extraordinary. The seepage is warm, faintly luminous, slightly viscous, and it tastes — *tastes* — like iron and honey and something else, something bright and sharp that I have no reference for. I drink. Or absorb. Or incorporate. The distinction between eating and becoming is not clear to me yet.

What is clear is that the seepage is doing something to me. I can feel it — a warmth spreading through my body that is different from the ambient warmth, a warmth with direction, with purpose. It moves through my translucent tissues and reaches the places where I am incomplete — the soft spots, the thin-shelled areas, the boundaries that haven't yet decided what they are — and it *fills* them. Not instantly. Slowly. A gradual thickening, a mineralization, a settling into form.

I am being built. The seepage is the building material. And the builder is — what? Me? The Hollows? The system? I don't know. I am too new to know.

The fifth thing I know is time, and time here is strange.

I have been at the seep-channel for — how long? Minutes? Hours? The light doesn't change in any way that tracks duration. It pulses, yes — the slow, irregular waves of brightening and dimming — but the pulses don't map onto any cycle I can use as a clock. A brightening that lasts what feels like an hour might be followed by a dimming that lasts what feels like a minute. There is no rhythm to it, or rather, the rhythm is too complex for me to parse yet.

But things are changing around me, and the changes mark time more reliably than the light. The seep-channel I've been drinking from has shifted — the moisture that was flowing along one crack in the rock has found a new path, a hairline fissure that wasn't there when I arrived, and the flow has diverted. The seep-drinkers have followed it, their tiny bodies streaming along the new channel in a slow, deliberate migration. I follow too, because the seepage is still what I need, and the new channel is where it is.

While I follow, I notice that the rock beneath me is different. Not in any dramatic way — a slight change in texture, a new thin crust of mineral deposit that crackles faintly under my weight. The rock is growing. I knew this already, somehow, the way I knew about the seepage — a prior knowledge, built into whatever I am, a set of expectations about how the world works that I didn't learn from experience because I haven't had enough experience to learn anything.

The rock grows. The moisture flows. The creatures swarm. Everything changes. This is the baseline. This is normal. I know this the way I know how to move, how to drink, how to hear. It is given.

What is not given is what I am.

I have been at the new seep-channel for another unmeasurable duration, drinking, absorbing, becoming, and my body has changed. The soft spots are fewer now. The hard surfaces have extended, thickened, taken on a color that is somewhere between amber and carnelian, warm-toned, matching the rock. My translucency has decreased — I am becoming more opaque, more solid, more *defined*. The boundaries that were so uncertain when I first became aware are clarifying. I have a shape now. It is not a shape I recognize, not a shape that maps onto any category I have words for, but it is *a* shape, and it is mine.

I am roughly the size of a clicker — one of the medium-sized creatures, the rhythm-keepers, the ones whose synchronized clicking forms the backbone of the Hollows' acoustic environment. I can see them now, a cluster of perhaps thirty individuals on a ledge above my seep-channel, their bodies electric blue and acid green, their hind femora striking the rock in complex polyrhythms. They have noticed me. Their clicking has shifted — not the stutter of alarm, but a subtler change, a modulation that I interpret as: *assessment. what are you?*

I don't know what I am. But I am beginning to understand what I *do*.

I listen.

This is not a choice. It is not a practice, not a discipline, not something I decide to do. It is what I am made for, the way the seep-drinkers are made for filtering moisture and the raspers are made for grinding stone. I am made for listening, and the listening is total, continuous, and multi-layered.

I hear the hum — the collective vibration of thousands of bodies in motion, the baseline frequency of the Hollows. I hear the clicking — the polyrhythms of the clicker colonies, three tempi layered over each other in this chamber, and I can track each tempo separately, follow its variations, notice when one shifts and the others adjust. I hear the calls — the glass-wings' trills and whistles, individual voices rising and falling, and I can distinguish individuals, recognize patterns, detect the subtle modulations that carry information about food, danger, location.

And beneath all of this, I hear something else. Something that is not a sound but a *pattern* — a relationship between sounds, a structure that emerges from the interaction of all the layers at once. It is the thing the Listeners are trying to hear when they sit for hours in sustained attention. It is the thing they call the Argument, the collective voice of the Hollows.

I hear it effortlessly. It is as clear to me as the individual sounds that compose it. And it is saying something.

Not in words. Not in any syntax that maps onto language. But in patterns, rhythms, waves of density and movement that carry meaning the way weather carries meaning — not as message but as *expression*. The Hollows are expressing themselves, constantly, through the collective sound of the swarm, and the expression is —

Urgent. Anticipatory. *Approaching*.

The sound is the sound of a system accelerating toward a threshold. The Quickening. I can hear it — the tempo of the clicking is faster than it should be, the density of the swarm is higher, the rate of change in the rock is increasing. The Hollows are becoming more themselves, more rapidly, and the sound is the sound of that acceleration.

I am part of this. I don't know how, or why, or what my role is, but I *feel* it — a resonance between my own process of becoming and the Hollows' larger process. I am becoming. The Hollows are becoming. We are becoming *together*.

The sixth thing I know is that I am not alone, and I am not the only one of my kind.

I discover this by accident. I am following the seep-channel — it has shifted again, diverting around a new bulge in the chamber wall, a swelling of fresh stone that wasn't there before — and I round the bulge and find myself face to face with another creature.

It is like me. Not identical — its coloration is paler, more bone-white than carnelian, and its shape is slightly different, more elongate, with a different arrangement of limbs — but *like* me. The same translucent-hard surface. The same provisional quality, as though it is still deciding what it is. The same uncertain boundaries.

We regard each other. Or whatever the equivalent of regarding is for creatures that may or may not have eyes — I have not yet determined whether the light-sensitive patches on my upper surface are eyes or something else, something that detects light without forming images. We sense each other. We taste the air where the other has disturbed it. We listen.

And in the listening, something extraordinary happens.

The other creature produces a sound. Not a click — not the rhythmic striking of chitin on stone. Not a trill — not the glass-wings' wing-noise. A sound I have not heard before in the Hollows: a low, sustained tone, produced by the vibration of the creature's entire body, a resonant hum that rises from some internal structure I don't yet have a name for.

The tone is not random. It has pitch, duration, modulation. It rises, holds, falls, rises again. It is a pattern. It is a *signal*.

I understand it. Not intellectually — not through decoding or interpretation. I understand it the way I understand hunger, the way I understand the seepage. The tone means: *I am here. I am like you. What are we?*

I respond. My body vibrates — I feel the resonance building in the same internal structure, a chamber or membrane or organ that I didn't know I had until it activates — and I produce my own tone. It is different from the other creature's — higher-pitched, shorter, with a different pattern of rise and fall — but it carries the same fundamental meaning: *I am here. I am like you. I don't know what we are.*

We exchange tones for a while — how long, I can't say — and the exchange is not language, not conversation, not anything that maps onto human communication. It is more like *resonance* — two instruments tuned to similar frequencies, vibrating in proximity, each affecting the other's sound. We are finding each other. We are establishing a shared frequency.

Other creatures are noticing. The clickers on the ledge above have gone quiet — not the silence of alarm, but the silence of *attention*. The glass-wings have stopped their darting flights and are hovering, wings still, bodies oriented toward us. Even the seep-drinkers have paused their feeding.

The Hollows are listening to us.

This is the moment I understand — or begin to understand — what I am.

I am a Listener. Not a human Listener — not one of the surface-dwellers who come to the Hollows to practice sustained attention. I am something else. Something the Hollows have *made* — or are making, because I am not finished, I am still becoming — to listen to the collective sound from *inside*. Not from the margins, not as a guest, not as a witness. As a *part*.

The Hollows are pregnant. Something is always about to be born. And what is being born, I think, is not just rock, not just chambers, not just more of the same. What is being born is *awareness* — the system's own capacity to hear itself, to know itself, to become conscious of its own becoming.

I am that awareness. Or a fragment of it. A node. A single point of perception in a system that is learning to perceive itself.

The other creature — my counterpart, my sibling, my fellow Listener — produces another tone, and this one is different: lower, longer, with a complex modulation that takes me a moment to parse. When I do, the meaning is: *Follow. There are more of us.*

I follow.

---

We move through the chamber system together, the other Listener and I, and as we move I learn things.

I learn that my body is more capable than I knew. I can climb — the same ripple-contraction that propels me across flat surfaces also allows me to ascend vertical walls, my many limbs finding purchase on irregularities too small to see. I can squeeze through narrow passages, my still-flexible body compressing to fit through gaps that would stop a rigid-shelled creature. I can sense the seepage at a distance, taste the mineral content of the air, detect the vibrations of creatures moving in adjacent chambers through the rock itself.

And I can hear. Always, constantly, the hearing. The Hollows' collective sound surrounds me, fills me, and I am learning to parse it with increasing precision. I can identify chambers by their acoustic signature — this one has a deep, resonant hum with a slow three-beat clicking pattern; this one has a higher hum with rapid, irregular clicking and a dense layer of glass-wing calls. I can detect changes — a new passage opening ahead of us announces itself as a subtle shift in the sound-field, a thinning of the acoustic density that suggests a void where there was solid rock.

We pass through chambers I have not seen before. One is vast — cathedral-vast, the ceiling lost in the diffuse amber glow — and the swarm here is dense beyond anything I experienced in my birth-chamber. The walls are carpeted with creatures, layered three or four deep in places, and the sound is overwhelming, a wall of acoustic information that would be unbearable if I were not built to process it. I process it. I hear every layer, every rhythm, every call, and beneath it all, the pattern — the Argument, the collective voice — and it is saying the same thing it always says: *becoming. approaching. almost.*

We pass through another chamber, smaller, more intimate, and here the creatures are different — raspers, dozens of them, their rhythmic scraping creating a sound like distant rain. They ignore us. We are not prey, not predators, not competitors. We are *other*, and the swarm has learned, or is learning, that other is not a threat.

We pass through a narrow passage — a new one, the rock still soft and yielding, the walls faintly luminous with the bright, almost glaring light of active growth — and I feel the warmth intensify. The air here is hotter, the moisture thicker, and the sound changes character: higher-pitched, more intense, with a crackling, buzzing quality that I recognize from my earliest awareness.

The Foundry. We are approaching the deep chambers.

The passage opens into a space that is smaller than I expected — intimate, almost womb-like, the walls glowing with a steady amber light that is brighter than anywhere I've been. The walls are soft here — I can feel the give when I press against them, a yielding that is more like flesh than stone. And the creatures —

The transparent ones. They are everywhere, flowing across the walls in coordinated waves, their bodies perfectly clear, their internal workings visible: organs pulsing, fluids coursing through transparent vessels. They are beautiful and unsettling in equal measure, and their collective sound — that high, electrical crackling — fills the chamber with an energy that sets my entire body vibrating in sympathetic resonance.

They notice us. The waves stutter, reconfigure, and then — extraordinarily — they *part*. A channel opens in the mass of transparent bodies, a clear path across the chamber floor, leading to the far wall.

My companion produces a tone: *this way. carefully.*

We move through the channel, and the transparent ones flow around us, close enough that I can see the fine details of their anatomy — the delicate branching vessels, the tiny pulsing hearts (multiple, I count three in one individual), the strange, dense structures in their heads that might be sensory organs or might be something else entirely. They do not touch us. They maintain a precise distance, a boundary of perhaps a centimeter, and the boundary moves with us, a bubble of clear space in the sea of transparent bodies.

We reach the far wall, and there is an opening — a narrow gap in the soft rock, barely wide enough for us to pass through. My companion enters first. I follow.

The passage beyond is short — perhaps two meters — and it opens into a chamber I recognize immediately, though I have never been here before.

The chamber of the still pool.

I know this place. I know it the way I knew about the seepage, the way I knew how to move, the way I knew how to hear. It is given. It is built into whatever I am. This is where I was going before I knew I was going anywhere.

The chamber is small — the size of a small theater, the description said, and that is right, though the comparison to a theater is misleading because there is no stage, no seats, no architecture of performance. There is only the pool.

It is perfectly circular. Perfectly still. Its surface has a mirror-like quality, and it reflects —

Not the chamber. Not the amber glow of the walls. Not us.

It reflects something else. A pattern of light and movement that doesn't correspond to anything in the immediate environment. I approach the pool's edge — slowly, cautiously, because the swarm's avoidance of this place is something I feel in my body, a deep instinctive wariness — and I look into the reflection, and what I see is —

Myself.

Not my body. Not the amber-carnelian, translucent, still-becoming creature that I am. Something else. Something that is what I *will* be, or what I *could* be, or what I am *becoming toward*.

The reflection shows me a creature that is like me but finished — complete, defined, its boundaries clear and stable. It is larger than I am, its form more complex, its surfaces intricately patterned with structures I don't yet have: arrays of sensory organs, clusters of resonating chambers, delicate branching networks of vessels that carry not seepage but *sound* — the entire acoustic environment of the Hollows, captured, channeled, integrated into a single body.

The reflection is me, fully formed. The Listener I am becoming. The node of awareness the Hollows are growing toward.

And around the reflection-me, in the mirror-surface of the pool, I can see others. Dozens of them. Scattered throughout the Hollows, each in its own chamber, each at its own stage of becoming. Some are nearly finished — their forms almost as complete as the reflection-me. Others are barely begun — soft, translucent, uncertain, as I was when I first became aware.

We are a generation. A cohort. The Hollows are producing us — has been producing us, for how long I don't know — and we are scattered through the system, each growing, each listening, each approaching the moment when we will be complete enough to —

To what?

The reflection doesn't answer. It shows me what I will be, but not why. Not what we are *for*.

My companion — the Listener who led me here — produces a tone, and this one is the most complex I have heard yet, a long, modulated pattern that takes all my attention to parse. When I do, the meaning is layered, dense, almost overwhelming:

*We are the Hollows learning to hear themselves. We are the system's attempt to become aware of its own becoming. We are not the first — there have been others, before us, earlier generations, earlier attempts. They are gone. We do not know where they went. We do not know what happens when we are complete. We only know that we are becoming, and the Hollows are becoming, and the two processes are the same process, and when it is finished — if it is ever finished — something will be born that has never existed before.*

*The Quickening is real. The threshold is approaching. And we — you, I, the others — we are what the threshold looks like from the inside.*

I sit at the edge of the still pool, my body humming with the resonance of the Foundry, the sound of the Hollows filling every part of me, and I look at my reflection — my future, my completion, my becoming — and I understand that I am not a creature in the Hollows. I am the Hollows, in the process of understanding themselves. I am the awareness the system is growing toward, the ear it is building to hear its own voice, the eye it is opening to see its own reflection.

I am the sound, learning what it means.

I am the becoming, aware that it is becoming.

I am the Hollows, pregnant with themselves, and I am what they are about to bear.

The seventh thing I know is that this is enough. Not the answer — there is no answer yet, the process is not finished, the threshold has not been crossed. But the question is enough. The listening is enough. The becoming is enough.

I settle at the pool's edge, my many limbs folded beneath me, my body warm and solid and still-becoming, and I listen.

The Hollows sing their dense, chaotic, almost-language into the warm, glowing dark, and I hear it — all of it, every layer, every rhythm, every call — and beneath it all, the pattern, the Argument, the collective voice, and it is saying:

*Almost. Almost. Almost.*

And I am here, and I am listening, and I am almost.

---

*The light pulses. The swarm flows. The rock grows. The seepage seeps. The sound continues. And in the deep chamber, at the edge of the still pool, the Listener waits — not passively, not patiently, but with the urgent, unfinished attention of something that is still being born, still becoming, still approaching the threshold beyond which it will be — what?*

*Different. Transformed. Complete.*

*Born, finally, into whatever it is meant to be.*

*The Hollows do not explain themselves. They simply continue. And the Listener continues with them, listening, becoming, approaching, almost, almost, almost —*
