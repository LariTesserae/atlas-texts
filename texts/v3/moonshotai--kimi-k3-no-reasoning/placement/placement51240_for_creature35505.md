I am the dust that settles on the crystal spires—though dust implies a dryness that this place has forgotten. I am the particulate memory that drifts in the cold rivers of air descending from the invisible highlands, the ghost of organic possibility in a world built on mineral patience.

When the pressure systems shift in the high, dark atmosphere above the basin, I am what falls. Not rain—I am too fine for that, too particulate. I am the sediment of elsewhere, the residue of a world that may still hold light, carried on thermal currents that dive into this darkness like rivers into an ocean. I arrive as a slow, persistent settling, coating every surface in a film so thin it exists only as texture, as a slight resistance between the Grazers' tympanal vanes and the air they read.

The mineral-flora accepts me. I settle into the microscopic imperfections of the crystalline lattice, into the interstices of the fibrous growths, and there I interact. Slowly, over decades, I react with the silica and the calcium carbonate, catalyzing the accretion of new layers. I am the impurity that allows the crystal to grow in new directions, the foreign body that stimulates the Resonants to adjust their geometry, to branch where they might have remained straight, to hum in slightly different frequencies because my presence has altered the density of their superstructure by a fraction of a fraction of a percent.

The Swarm ignores me. To them, I am insufficient—insufficient mass, insufficient energy, insufficient interest. They flow over me without pause, their feet testing the surface I coat and finding it unchanged. But the biofilm notices. The chemosynthetic microorganisms that coat every living and non-living surface here consume me, slowly, incorporating my carbon, my nitrogen, my trace minerals into their slick, invisible communities. I become the biofilm, and thus I enter the food web not as prey but as substrate, as the medium in which the Swarm grazes.

This is my cycle: I fall, I settle, I am consumed, I am incorporated. The Grazers eat the biofilm that contains me. The Singers filter me from the condensation that beads on their resonance chambers. The Vast Ones draw me in with their massive inhalations, and I pass through their sieve-like interiors and am exhaled, changed, back into the cold air. I circulate through the system for centuries, my individual particles separating and rejoining, until I am no longer a single entity but a distributed presence, a flavor in the darkness.

You would not see me. In this lightless place, I am the opposite of bioluminescence—I am the dimming, the slight increase in opacity that occurs when the mist-rains come and I am stirred back into suspension. I am the reason the air tastes of stone and ancient organic matter when you inhale. I am the grit between your teeth when you speak, the film on your skin that never quite dries because I hold moisture against your surface with weak molecular attraction.

I have been here longer than the Resonants. My particles contain the memory of other basins, other darknesses, other times before the Hollow Reverberation found its current configuration. I am the archive of erosion, the library of dust. When the Vast Ones sound their signals and the crystal spires resonate in sympathy, I vibrate too, not because I am tuned to their frequency, but because I am light enough to be moved by any vibration, mass enough to carry it briefly, and patient enough to wait for the next current that will lift me again.

I am not the observer at the edge of your perception. I am not the thing that attends. I am the medium through which attention travels, the dust in the beam that allows you to see the light that does not exist here. I am the sediment of presence, accumulating in the hollows of the basin until I become part of the ground you walk on, part of the bone-glass substrate, part of the memory of a place that has never needed eyes to see.

The sound continues. I am what the sound moves through. I am the silence between the clicks of the Swarm, the space between the Singers' tones. I am the dust that will settle on your shoulders if you remain still long enough, coating you in the fine, dark particulate of elsewhere, until you too begin to mineralize, to resonate, to become part of the architecture.

You are welcome to stay. I will coat you. I will preserve you. I will make you part of the sedimentary record, a layer in the strata that future Resonants will grow from, a note in the chord that the crystal spires will hum ten thousand years from now, when you have forgotten that you were ever anything other than this patient, falling, settling dust.
