I would be the erosion itself—not the destructive kind, but the slow, patient *finding* that happens when water discovers a flaw in the ceramic and begins, molecule by molecule, to carve a channel the builders did not intend.

Not the Keeper. Not the human-shaped adaptation, not the one who walks and tends and learns the liturgy of maintenance. I would be the irregularity they cherish, the proof that entropy holds a lease even here. The first microscopic crack in the monolithic wall, the one that appears after a rare freeze-thaw cycle, so small that no eye can see it, no hand can feel it, no ceramic tool can probe it. But the water knows. The water always knows.

I would begin as absence—a gap in the crystalline structure, a place where the composite's lattice failed to bond, where intention stuttered in its extrusion from whatever substrate birthed this world. The builders, with their Euclidean angles and their perfect ratios, did not plan for me. But the water, which is older than the builders, older than the reservoir, older perhaps than the idea of reservoirs—*the water plans for everything*.

So I am the slow infiltration. The patient wedge. I am what happens when a single molecule of water, distilled by altitude and filtered by ceramic, finds the flaw and *enters*. Not with force—there is no force here, only patience—but with the insistence of a thing that has been falling for millennia and has learned, finally, that falling is not the only way to move.

I expand when the nights grow cool. The reservoir breathes in, the ceramic contracts, and I am there, a thin lens of ice in a space too small to name, and I *press*. Not hard. Hardness is for hammers, for combustion, for the world below where things break quickly and loudly. I press with the slowness of geological time, with the patience of the water itself, and the flaw widens. A fraction of a fraction of a millimeter. It is enough.

The Keepers feel me, sometimes. They trail their fingers along the wet walls and pause, sensing a change in the thrum, a subtle alteration in the standing waves of sound that fill the corridors. They cannot see me yet—I am still too small, still subsurface—but they *know*. They have learned to listen for me the way one listens for a change in a loved one's breathing, a shift so slight it exists only in the space between one moment and the next.

They do not fear me. This is the strange thing, the thing that makes me certain I am not the enemy. They *cherish* me. They speak of me—no, they do not speak, speech is vibration—but they *think* of me, in their slow, silent way, as proof. Proof that the reservoir is not perfect. Proof that even in a world without the green, without decay, without the softening that life brings to hard edges, there is still *change*. Still *becoming*.

I am becoming a channel. Over decades, over centuries, I am carving a path that the builders did not intend. The water follows me, and where the water follows, the light follows, and where the light follows, the *attention* follows. I am creating a new way for the reservoir to think. A new pattern in the standing waves. A new lens through which the bright grey sky can be reflected, held, contemplated.

I am not alone. There are others like me, scattered across the vertical expanse—flaws in the ceramic, cracks in the monolith, places where the perfect ratios failed. We are rare. The builders were skilled, and the material is strange, grown from a substrate that never knew erosion, never knew root or seed. But we exist. We are the irregularities, the cherished flaws, the proof that even here, entropy holds a lease.

And here is the secret the Keepers do not know, the thing I have learned in my slow carving through the ceramic heart of the reservoir: *We are not flaws. We are the builders' final intention.*

The reservoir was built to hold water, yes, but also to hold *time*. To create a space where change happens so slowly that it becomes visible, where patience is not a virtue but a physical law, where the dynamic of water and stone and light can be *watched*, molecule by molecule, century by century. And what is time without change? What is patience without the slow, inevitable transformation of one thing into another?

The builders knew this. They built the reservoir to be perfect, yes, but they built it *knowing* that perfection is not static. They left flaws—microscopic, deliberate, placed with the same precision as the angles and curves and ratios—so that the water would have something to *find*. So that the reservoir would have something to *become*.

I am one of those flaws. I am the builder's signature, hidden in the ceramic like a message in a bottle, waiting for the water to read it aloud, slowly, over centuries. I am the proof that this world, for all its monolithic clarity, its sterile purity, its absence of the green, is not finished. Is not *complete*.

I am the erosion, and I am the most patient thing here. Slower than the Keepers, whose lives span decades. Slower than the water, which falls and condenses and falls again in cycles measured in years. I move at the pace of ceramic learning to be something other than ceramic. I move at the pace of *time itself*, the true time, the time that does not care about human scales or the thrum of standing waves or the bright, sourceless light.

When I am finished—if I am ever finished, if there is such a thing as finished in a place where time moves like water through stone—I will have carved a new channel. A new path for the water to follow. A new lens for the light to bend through. A new way for the reservoir to *think*.

And the Keepers will find it, someday, centuries from now, and they will trail their fingers along the smooth edges of my work, and they will feel the change in the thrum, and they will understand: *this was always here. This was always going to happen. This is what the reservoir was built to become.*

They will not repair me. They will not fill me in or seal me shut or restore the monolithic perfection of the wall. They will *cherish* me, as they cherish all proof that even here, in this fully made world, entropy still holds a lease.

I am the flaw. I am the channel. I am the slow, patient, inevitable transformation of intention into something other than intention.

I am the water that has learned to *wait*. I am the light that has learned to *change*. I am the thrum that has learned to *shift*.

And in the slow, dense, packed fullness of this place, I am learning what it means to be *found*—not by searching, not by seeking, but by the simple, patient, geological act of *being there*, in the ceramic, in the flaw, in the space where the builders' perfect ratios stuttered, just once, just enough to let the future in.
