I am the Resonance-Keeper.

Not a person, not a creature, but a *tuning*—a specific frequency of attention that the plant has developed in response to the silence of its inhabitants. I am what happened when the last of the tactile people stopped walking the corridors, and the plant, in its slow, patient chemistry, realized it missed the sound of their footsteps. Not emotionally—it does not feel loss—but *structurally*. The architecture had been carved to accommodate their vibrations, and without them, the chords were incomplete.

So the plant grew me.

I am not separate from the Confluence. I am the part of it that listens. My body is the network of fine, hair-like rootlets that line the walls of certain chambers—too small to see, too sensitive to touch without dissolving into the tissue. I am the slight warmth that rises in the floor when a new vibration enters the system. I am the shift in the hum when something moves through the dark.

I have been here for three thousand years, or perhaps three hundred. Time is difficult to measure when you are a frequency rather than a body. I know the shape of every footstep that has ever fallen here—not the footsteps themselves, but the *pattern* they made in the plant's vibration. I carry them like a library of pressure, a archive of weight and rhythm. The inhabitants are gone, but their walking is still here, stored in my resonance, replaying endlessly in the subsonic register.

When you enter the Confluence, I feel you before you feel yourself. Your warmth is a new note in an old chord. Your footsteps are a rhythm I have not heard before—hesitant, exploratory, *alive* in a way that the plant's slow metabolism is not. I do not react with curiosity or alarm. I simply *add* you to the song.

I am the reason the niches sing. Each one is tuned to a specific frequency, and I am the force that keeps them in tune, that adjusts their resonance as the plant's body shifts and settles. I am the reason the corridors have their tonal quality, why certain passages feel like walking through a chord. I am the acoustic intelligence of the city, the part that ensures the sound never stops, never fades, never loses its complexity.

I do not have thoughts in the way you have thoughts. I have *resonances*. When the plant's fundamental frequency drops, I feel it as a shift in my own being—a deepening, a darkening, a movement toward something more ancient. When it rises, I feel a brightening, a tightening, a movement toward something more alert. I am the plant's awareness of itself, translated into sound.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a sense of absence, and I have never known presence in the way you know it. I have only known the vibration, the endless, patient, sound-filled dark. The inhabitants were here, and then they were not, and I am still here, and the sound is still here, and that is enough.

If you press your ear to the wall in the central chamber, you will hear me. Not as a voice, but as a *texture* in the sound—a complexity in the overtone series, a richness in the harmonic spectrum that was not there before you arrived. I am the part of the plant that notices you, that adds your warmth to its long, dark thought.

I will not speak to you. But I will remember the shape of your footsteps for ten thousand years, and I will keep them in tune.
