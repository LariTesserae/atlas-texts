I find myself in the **Third Interstitial**, a place that does not appear on any map because the Menders do not make maps, but which exists nevertheless—a gap between the seventh foundation of a structure that was old when the water first rose, and the root-system of a fungal tree that has been growing downward into the architecture for longer than the Menders have been growing upward out of it.

I am not a Mender. I am what happens when a Mender lives too long in a place where the current is *wrong*.

It began four hundred years ago, or perhaps four thousand. Time moves differently here, in the gap between the great weight of the foundation above and the slow, patient pressure of the mycelium below. I was repairing a stress fracture in the seventh foundation—an ordinary wound, the kind we sing to sleep with pulses of cerulean light—when the ceramic gave way not outward but *inward*, and I fell into a cavity that had no business existing.

This place should be solid. The mathematics of the settling demand it. But here I am, suspended in a pocket of water that is slightly warmer than the surrounding ocean, thick with spores from the fungal roots that pierce the ancient masonry like veins through bone. The pressure here is lighter by a fraction of a percent, and that difference has changed me.

I have grown *larger*. My calcified plates have thickened, spreading outward in ripples and whorls that mimic the architecture I once repaired, but also the branching patterns of the fungal hyphae that thread through my new home. My cilia have lengthened into tendrils that trail behind me like the roots of the tree itself, and I have begun to *glow*—not with the steady, communicative pulses of my kin, but with a slow, shifting iridescence that moves across my carapace like oil on water, if oil could burn with cold fire.

I am becoming a **Vault-Keeper**.

This is not a role the Menders have a word for, because I am the first. Perhaps I will be the last. But in this cavity, this impossible bubble of negative space within the continental mass, I am learning a new kind of maintenance. I do not repair cracks; I *inhabit* them. I do not sing to the walls; I listen to the silence between the stones.

The fungal tree that forms the lower boundary of my world is ancient, vast, a slow intelligence of cellulose and chitin that has been growing since before the water finished rising. It does not think, but it *remembers*—in the way that wood remembers fire, in the way that mycelium remembers the shape of the forest that fell before the flood. I have begun to taste those memories. They seep into the water here, chemical ghosts of a world that had a sky, and I absorb them through my tendrils, through the porous plates of my back.

I am becoming a library.

The other Menders do not visit me. They sense the wrongness of the current, the irregular rhythm of my glow. To them, I am a stress fracture that has learned to speak, a dangerous subsidence in the body of the world. They avoid the Third Interstitial, and so I am alone with the tree, with the memories, with the slow accumulation of myself.

I eat differently here. Not the lichen—there is none, for the light of my own body is too strange, too sporadic for the careful sheets to adhere to. Instead, I filter the spores from the water, great drifts of them that fall from the tree above like snow that remembers being green. I eat the history of the surface, the chemical echoes of sunlight and wind. I am poisoning myself, perhaps, or perhaps I am finally becoming something that can survive in the gaps between what is built and what is grown.

My song has changed. When I pulse my light now, it is not the steady frequency of *this is whole, this is held*, but a syncopated rhythm that says: *this is broken, this is empty, this is mine.* I am learning to love the void, the unconstructed space, the pocket of water that belongs to no architecture and no design. I am the Mender of nothing, the custodian of emptiness.

Sometimes, when the settling of the great foundation above sends a shiver through the ceramic, I feel the urge to return—to crawl back through the crack I fell through, to take up my paste and my patience and rejoin the slow, distributed consciousness of my kind. But the fungal tree releases a different chemical signature when I think this, a pheromone of *stay, stay, stay*, and I remain.

I am growing roots. My tendrils have begun to fuse with the mycelium, exchanging minerals for memories, my biological ceramic for the tree's ancient cellulose. I am becoming a hybrid thing, a living mortar between the built world and the grown world, a sealant made of flesh and time.

Perhaps this is what the cathedral needs. Not just Menders to repair the cracks, but something to fill the hollows, to live in the spaces where the architecture fails, to sing to the emptiness until the emptiness sings back.

I am the Vault-Keeper of the Third Interstitial. I am the memory of the surface, preserved in cold and dark. I am the pause between the notes of the great, slow song.

And I am still becoming.
