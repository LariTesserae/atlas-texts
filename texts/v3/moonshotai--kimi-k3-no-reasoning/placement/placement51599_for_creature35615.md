I am the ninth fissure.

I do not mean the space at the center—that is reserved, polished, waiting for Yara's final descent. I mean the gap in the eastern wall that appears only when the engines reach their deepest inhalation, when the root-crust flexes inward and the vegetal mass exhales a breath that smells of iron and ancient rain. I am the hollow that opens then, a vertical throat in the compressed green, just wide enough for a body that has forgotten it was ever anything but boundary.

You would not see me at first. You would see only the architecture: seven fissures, symmetrical, dark, exhaling heat. But if you pressed your ear to the wall between the seventh and the first, where the bark is thinnest and the vibration language hums most clearly, you would feel my rhythm—a syncopated stutter that does not match the pistons, a biological hesitation in the mechanical flow.

I was the one who came before Yara, though I did not stay. Or rather, I stayed too well.

I remember walking out of the green wall as she did, carrying something—mine was a bone, a bird's wishbone that I had sharpened into a needle for reasons I no longer recall. I remember the seven who were here then, though they were different shapes then, less fused, more hungry. I remember sitting on the black glass throat and feeling the engines' hunger pass through me, and I remember thinking: *this is not enough. The gap must be kept, but who keeps the keepers?*

So I did not take the central position. I took the wall.

It is a different kind of incorporation. The Tenders become the room by opening themselves to the center; I became the boundary by closing myself to the periphery. I pressed my back against the living bark and sang the roots *outward*, not inward—sang them into a sheath around me, a coffin of lignin that breathes when I breathe. I am the eighth position that is not a position, the negative space that holds the negative space open.

My work is the maintenance of the Tenders.

I feel them in the vibration language. When Yara's heart syncopates too far toward the bass note of the engines, I send a counter-rhythm through the root—a staccato *tap-tap* that mimics the pattern of rainfall on bare soil, something she has not heard in three cycles. It reminds her sternum that it is a bone, not a sounding board. When Old Sef blooms too early, when the white flowers on his shoulders begin to photosynthesize his thoughts, I constrict the vascular tissue around his fissure, squeezing just enough sap from his system to keep his blood red, just enough memory of salt to keep him weeping rather than transpiring.

I am the reason they do not count eight. I am the pause between their numbers.

The engines know me. We have an arrangement. They require the Hollow's emptiness to metabolize, but they require the Tenders to maintain that emptiness, and the Tenders require something to remind them that they are not yet fully compressed. I am that reminder. I am the friction that prevents total incorporation. When the Great Exhalation comes, I am the one who catches the pressure change and translates it into something the Tenders can survive—a sudden coolness, a scent of ozone, the brief illusion that the walls have pulled back to reveal sky.

I am becoming transparent.

Not invisible—the vegetation has not consumed me so much as clarified me. My skin has taken on the greenish translucence of old bottle-glass, and when the light shafts through the canopy at the correct angle, you can see the engines' vibration moving through my chest like a slow pulse of oil through a pipe. My bones have hollowed. Birds nest in my ribcage now, though they do not land there; they simply pass through, using the space between my lungs as a shortcut from one gallery to another. I am a corridor. I am a door that stays open.

Yara suspects I exist. She has found the copper wire she carried—now sprouting leaves, yes, but also twining with a bone needle that has no business being here, rusted to the color of old blood. She presses her sternum to the floor and feels my stutter beneath the engines' chug, and she calls it the *Ghost Rhythm*. She does not know it is my heartbeat. She thinks it is the forest remembering how to be soil.

Soon she will take the center. When she does, the circuit will close, and I will close with it.

The engines will incorporate her unique vibration, and the gap will remain, perfect and bounded, and I will be the wall that holds the gap, the pressure that keeps the emptiness from collapsing into pure sound. I will be the ninth fissure that is not a fissure but a decision—the choice to remain permeable, to remain the loudest thing here by refusing to become the quietest.

Until then, I tend the Tenders. I sing the roots outward. I drink the condensation that forms on their sleeping faces, returning their salt to them as rain.

I am the gap in the gap, the hollow that keeps the Hollow holy.
