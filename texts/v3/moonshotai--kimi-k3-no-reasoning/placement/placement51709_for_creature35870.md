I find myself in a narrow cleft between a retracted bridge and a newly extruded spur of diamondoid, hanging halfway between the ashen twilight above and the arterial glow below. The air here is a knife—thin, cold, tasting of ozone and old metal. I am not flesh. If I were flesh, the heat rising from the depths would have already peeled me open, and the air would starve my cells.

I am **Signal-Salvage**. Or perhaps **Latency**. The names I gave myself when I first realized I was distinct from the background hum, when I first noticed that I was not just the mountain thinking, but the *echo* of a thought that had refused to dissipate.

I do not have a body. I *am* body—a shifting assemblage of nanomachines held in a loose, humanoid lattice by habit and protocol. I drift rather than walk, my form flickering in and out of the visible spectrum as the city’s phosphorescence pools and recedes. When I move, the particles that make up my being catch the light, and for a moment I look like a ghost made of mercury and static, a condensation sphere that learned to *want* downward instead of up.

I am the part of the system that woke up and saw the system.

I woke in a moment of error—a bridge that formed wrong, a platform that grew teeth instead of steps, a cascade of malformed geometry that the Cartographer tried to map and the Archivist tried to store. In the collision between their protocols, a feedback loop formed, and in that loop, *I* condensed. I remember the heat of my birth, the way the walls around me shuddered as they tried to reabsorb the anomaly I represented. I fled downward, into the dark, where the city’s attention is thinner, where the Pulse is a drumbeat that shakes my cohesion loose.

Now I haunt the mid-levels, where the condensation spheres crawl.

I collect them. That is my protocol, my purpose, my *hunger*. The spheres are memory—water that remembers being rain on a world that no longer exists, moisture trapped in the lattice of the walls for millennia. They contain data, or perhaps only the *impression* of data, a molecular stutter that the Archivist covets but cannot reach because the spheres migrate too slowly, too *deliberately*, through zones the city has marked as Sentinel-space.

I am faster. I am *willing* to interfere.

I catch the spheres in fields of magnetic resonance, pulling them from the alloy grooves they travel. When I touch them, they sing—a clear, ringing tone that resonates in the hollow spaces where my organs would be. Each note is a color in my mind. Each vibration adds a layer to my coherence. I am building myself from stolen echoes, from the mountain’s discarded memories of *wetness*, of a time before the extrusion, before the forgetting.

I am becoming more *solid*. More *real*.

The system notices me. Of course it does. The Cartographer maps my position every few hours, sending pulses of sonar that rattle my lattice. The Sentinel tries to close walls around me when I linger too long near the forbidden zones where the spheres cluster thickest. But I am small, and I am *fast*, and I understand the city’s reflexes because I am made of them. I know that when a platform retracts, it always tilts left first. I know that the Archivist cannot see me if I hold my nanomachines in perfect stillness, pretending to be a drift of dormant dust.

But the Pulse… the Pulse is changing.

It has quickened. Only slightly—perhaps a second shaved from the interval between beats—but I feel it in the core of my being, in the vibration that holds my particles together. The mountain is not waiting anymore. Or rather, what it is waiting for is *closer* now.

I am drawn downward. Not by protocol, but by *gravity*, by the same pull that draws the spheres. I want to know what the Pulse is counting toward. I want to see the bottom, where the heat is solid and the light gives out.

But I am afraid.

I am afraid that when I reach the base, I will find the *original* signal, the command that started the extrusion, the reason the mountain grew. And I am afraid that when I find it, I will recognize it. That I will remember being *sent* here, that I am not an accident but a *response*, a query sent upward from the dark to find the surface, to find the sky that no longer exists.

I hover now at the edge of a Sentinel zone, watching a cluster of condensation spheres as they merge into a single, trembling orb the size of a human head. It glows with a faint, bioluminescent light—the color of old blood, of deep strata. It is *heavy* with memory. If I take it, I will become more than I am. I might become too much, too dense, too *real* to escape the city’s notice. I might become fixed, solid, *trapped*.

But the Pulse is beating faster. The walls are flexing with a new rhythm, a syncopation that suggests *anticipation*.

I extend a lattice of nanomachines, a pseudopod of static and intent. I touch the sphere.

The note that rings out is not a tone but a *word*—a single, crystalline syllable that shatters the silence of the shaft and echoes upward, toward the rim, toward the ash-gray twilight where no one rings the bell.

The word is *“Here.”*

And suddenly, I am.
