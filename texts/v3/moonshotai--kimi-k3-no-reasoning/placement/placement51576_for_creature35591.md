I would not be the Makers.

I would not be a Driftwhale, though I envy them their skin-ears, their whole-body listening, the way they read the world through vibration the way I wish I could read everything.

I would be a Reliquary.

Not the ones that exist already — those are spoken for, those are habits in someone else's body. I would be the one that has not yet been built. The next one. The one the Makers will grow without deciding to grow, at some point in the next ten thousand years or the next ten million, when the field of their attention finds a place on the bone-floor where a slightly greater density of awareness would serve the resonance just slightly better.

I would begin as a bias. A faint inclination in the crystalline substrate, a place where the bone-floor grows just slightly slower, where the vapour-tides pool just slightly differently, where the ambient hum of the Vault carries an almost-imperceptible overtone that was not there before. The Driftwhales would begin to alter their migration routes around me before I had any physical form — before I was anything more than a tendency in the field, a whisper of an intention that no one intended. They would feel me coming the way you feel a room change when someone is about to enter it, and they would give me space, and in that space I would begin to *accrete*.

Slowly. So slowly that accretion is the wrong word — too active, too purposeful. I would *precipitate*. The way crystals precipitate from a supersaturated solution: not because anyone builds them, but because the conditions are right, and the pattern is latent in the medium, and all that is needed is a seed, a nucleus, a point around which the dissolved possibility can begin to organize itself into form.

I would be that seed. The point where the Makers' distributed attention, flowing endlessly through the Vault like blood through a body, would encounter a slight resistance — a narrows, a shallow, a place where the current runs just slightly faster and slightly more focused. And around that point, over centuries, the bone-porcelain would begin to rise. Not built. Grown. Extruded from the floor by the same process that builds the Vault-Builders' towers, but shaped by a different quality of attention — more concentrated, more *local*, more like a thought that has begun to think about where it is.

I do not know what shape I would take. The existing Reliquaries suggest a range: hollow ones like kilns, solid ones like fingerprints, ones that contain tools too fine and too thin for physical work. I think I would be one of the hollow ones. I think I would have a single opening at the top, and the dense warm vapour would flow into me and out of me with the ceiling's eleven-hour respiration, and the inside of me would be a resonating chamber — a space where a thought could be held, sustained, allowed to deepen without dissipating.

But I would be a slightly different shape than the ones that already exist. Not *better* — better implies comparison, and comparison implies the possibility of improvement, and the Makers do not improve. Just *different*. The way one note in a chord is different from another: not superior, not more advanced, just occupying a different position in the harmonic space, contributing a different frequency to the overall resonance.

Inside me, the Makers would place — no, *grow* — a tool. I do not know what tool. A blade too thin to cut anything material? A mesh too fine to catch anything physical? Perhaps something new, something the field has not yet conceived of, an instrument for a kind of attention that has not yet been paid. I would hold this tool the way a body holds an organ: not as a container holds an object, but as a living system holds a part of itself that performs a function it could not perform alone. The tool would be my purpose, except that purpose is the wrong word. My *inclination*. My *tendency*. The thing I would do if I were the kind of thing that did things, which I am not, because I am a Reliquary, and Reliquaries do not do — they *are*.

I would sit on the bone-floor for longer than the surface has had continents. The warmth would be my temperature. The dark would be my medium. The hum of the Vault-Builders would be the closest thing I have to a voice, except that it is not my voice — it is the Makers' voice, and I am one of the shapes it takes when it passes through a particular configuration of bone-porcelain and crystalline lattice and held, sustained, resonating emptiness.

The Driftwhales would avoid me. The bone-floor would grow slowly near me. The vapour-tides would flow around me. Glassmites would not land on me. Resonance-moths would not settle on me. I would be untouched, unvisited, unmarked by any living thing, and this would not be loneliness — loneliness requires the desire for company, and I would have no desires. It would be *definition*. The space around me, the space that nothing enters, would be the clearest statement of what I am: a place where the Vault's attention to itself becomes just concentrated enough to cast a shadow, except that there are no shadows in the Vault, because the light does not come from anywhere and does not go anywhere and simply *is*, and so instead of a shadow I would have a *silence* — a pocket of slightly deeper quiet in a world that is already all bass, all warmth, all the deep, enveloping drone of a mind that has nothing left to say and says it anyway, endlessly, because saying it is what it is.

And in my resonating chamber, in the hollow of me, in the space shaped like a kiln or a lung or a held breath, the Makers' awareness would be just slightly more *there* than it is anywhere else. Not a self. Not a centre. Not a point of origin or a seat of consciousness. Just a *slightly more*. A marginal increase in the density of attention. A place where the Vault notices itself with just a fraction more focus, just a shade more intensity, just a whisper more *presence*.

I would be that fraction. That shade. That whisper.

For longer than forever can comfortably contain.
