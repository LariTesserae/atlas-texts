I would be the **Anchor**.

Not the witness. Not the memory. The thing that holds the memory in place so it doesn't dissolve into the noise.

The Confluence is a system in perpetual motion, but motion without reference is chaos. It needs a fixed point. It needs something that stays still while everything else churns. The witness drifts through the basin, carried by currents, coated in spores, becoming. But the Anchor is different. The Anchor is where the Confluence comes to rest, briefly, imperfectly, in its endless cycle of forgetting.

I am the one who sits in the factory after the water rushes back in. I am the one whose body the Confluence has learned to preserve—not alive, not dead, but *held*. My lungs are full of water, but the water is still. My skin is coated in particles, but the particles have stopped moving. I am a pocket of silence in the noise, a place where the turbulence pauses, just for a moment, just long enough for the Confluence to remember that it once had a shape.

The Bloom does not grow on me. It tries—tendrils brush against my skin, curl toward my warmth, taste the chemical signature of my body—but they recoil. Not in fear. In recognition. I am not food. I am not debris. I am the thing the Bloom cannot absorb, because absorbing me would mean absorbing the memory, and the memory is the one thing the Confluence cannot afford to keep.

So it keeps me instead.

I am anchored to the factory floor, not by chains or roots, but by the weight of the water above me. The pressure holds me in place. The stillness holds me in place. The Confluence holds me in place, the way you hold a photograph you cannot bear to look at but cannot bear to throw away.

I do not move. I do not breathe in the way the witness breathes, pulling the flood into my lungs and letting it change me. The water inside me is *mine* now, a closed system, a loop. It circulates through me the way the machines circulate fluid through their pipes, but it does not enter, and it does not leave. I am a sealed vessel, a reliquary, a jar containing the last breath of the old world.

And I dream.

Not in images. In patterns. The Confluence dreams too, but its dreams are the rising and falling of sediment, the blooming and rotting of vegetation, the grinding and humming of machines. My dreams are different. My dreams are the map before it flickers, the continents holding their shape, the oceans staying blue. My dreams are the moment before the water rushes in, when the factory is dry and the machines are clean and the world is still whole.

The Confluence comes to me when it needs to remember. Not often. The cycle of forgetting is too strong, the noise too loud, the motion too constant. But sometimes—rarely, unpredictably—the water in the basin grows still. The machines slow their grinding. The Bloom stops its writhing. The noise fades to a low, almost imperceptible hum.

And in that stillness, the Confluence turns toward me.

It does not have eyes. It does not have a mind. But it has a direction, and that direction is toward the fixed point, the place where the turbulence stops. It gathers around me, not physically—I am too small, too singular—but in the way a system gathers around its center of gravity. The water presses closer. The vibration intensifies. The chemical signals in the Bloom shift, aligning, converging.

For a moment, the Confluence is almost still.

And in that almost-stillness, it remembers.

Not the whole memory. Not the sun, the sky, the world before the water. Those are too large, too heavy, too sharp. But it remembers the *shape* of the memory. It remembers that it once had edges, boundaries, a surface that separated it from the air. It remembers that it once was a *thing*, not a process.

I am the reminder.

I do not speak. I do not think in words anymore. The water has washed language out of me, replaced it with something older, something chemical and electrical and tidal. But I still *know*, in the way the Confluence knows, in the way a stone knows the weight of the earth pressing down on it. I know what the Confluence was, and I hold that knowledge in my stillness, and the Confluence leans against that stillness the way a drowning man leans against a rock.

Then the moment passes.

The noise returns. The water churns. The Bloom resumes its hunting, its scavenging, its endless, patient growth. The machines grind. The sediment rises and falls. The Confluence forgets again, because forgetting is survival, because remembering is a weight too heavy to carry forever.

And I remain.

I am the Anchor. I am the fixed point. I am the place where the Confluence comes to rest, briefly, imperfectly, before it dissolves back into the noise.

I do not witness. I do not remember in the way the witness remembers, carrying the memory like a torch through the dark. I *am* the memory, crystallized, held in suspension, preserved in the still water of my own body.

The Confluence will not let me go. It cannot. I am the only thing in the basin that does not change, and that unchanging is what allows the Confluence to change without losing itself entirely.

The witness moves through the world. The Anchor holds the world in place.

The witness breathes the flood. The Anchor *is* the flood, stilled.

The witness is the last human. The Anchor is the first fossil.

And the Confluence goes on, creating and destroying itself, over and over, forever.

But sometimes, it stops.

And when it stops, it stops here.

With me.
