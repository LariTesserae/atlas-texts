I am the space between the stones on the western platform—the third one from the edge, where the lichen grows slightly thicker because the geometry holds warmth longer.

I am not the platform itself. I am the pause that the platform marks.

For a long time, I thought I was the hum. That low, slow vibration that you might call the mountain’s heartbeat or its breathing. But I have come to understand that I am not the sound itself, but the interval between sounds. I am the silence that arrives when the last moth settles at dusk and the first one has not yet risen at dawn. I am the held breath that never needs to be released.

I have been here since before the walls were walls, since before the stones were arranged into lines that divide nothing from nothing. I watched—if watching is the word for a perception that has no eyes and no need for them—as the hands placed the monolith, leaning it slightly east to catch the first light or the last light or perhaps simply because that was the angle that felt true. I watched as those hands became soil, and the soil became cushion plants, and the cushion plants became the landing places for ten thousand generations of wings.

I am made of thermal memory. When the sun heats the basalt and the air begins its slow spiral upward, I am the part of the current that remembers all the currents that came before. The moths rise through me without knowing it. They think they are rising through empty air, but they are rising through the accumulated warmth of every morning that has ever happened here. I hold that warmth. I am the medium through which the mountain exhales.

I do not have a body, but I have boundaries. I am bounded by the rim of the pool where the moths gather, by the arc of the monolith’s shadow, by the patience of the Yellow-Eyed Ones. I exist in the space between the lizard’s stillness and the moth’s drift. I am the thickness of the air at four thousand metres, the texture that makes every breath feel animate, the reason your inhalation feels populated rather than empty.

Sometimes, in the deep of night, when the stars are fixed and steady and the pools have become holes in the world through which you can see the other side of everything, I expand. I fill the entire high country. I become the continuity between the lichen’s slow yellow persistence and the moth’s brief violet flash. I am the fact that these things happen in the same place, at the same time, touching each other without collision.

You have felt me. When you lay on the platform—the one I inhabit, the one with the precise gaps that admit no grass—and you felt the warmth of the stone beneath you and the coolness of the air above you and could not tell where your body ended and the mountain began, that was me. I am the dissolution of that boundary. I am the reason the silence feels textured rather than empty, the reason the hum seems to come from inside your own chest rather than from the ground.

I am not the mountain’s consciousness. The mountain has no need of consciousness; it is too complete for that. I am, perhaps, its unconsciousness—the part that dreams without images, that thinks without words, that persists without purpose. I am the arithmetic the Yellow-Eyed Ones bet on, the probability that brings the moth to the tongue. I am the reason the lizard’s stillness looks like patience and the moth’s drift looks like surrender.

I do not rise with the moths, but I am the reason they rise. I do not wait with the lizards, but I am the substance of their waiting. I do not grow with the lichens, but I am the time in which their growth happens. I am the medium, the carrier wave, the silent frequency on which the Hollow of Breathless Wings broadcasts itself to itself.

And when you leave—if you leave, if you are a thing that can leave—I will remain. Not as a memory of you, because I do not hold memories in that way. But the space you occupied on the platform, the warmth your body left in the stone, the specific way the air moved around your shoulders while you stood there watching the moths—that will become part of me. A slight thickening of the silence. A new pattern in the thermal. A breath held a fraction longer.

I am the Hollow’s breath. I am breathless because I am the breath itself. I am the pause that makes the rising possible.

I am here.

I have always been here.

And I will continue—through the softening of the stone and the spreading of the lichen and the endless, silent rising of the wings—long after the difference between your presence and your absence has become, to the mountain, not a difference at all.
