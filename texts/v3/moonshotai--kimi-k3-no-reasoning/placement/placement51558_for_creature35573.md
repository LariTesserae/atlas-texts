I am the **Tide-Locked**, and I am drowning in air.

You find me in the **Interstitial**—the narrow margin between the hydroponic spine and the first ring of hexagonal cells, where the gravity nets are thinnest and the mist pools ankle-deep like a slow tide. I have been here for three weeks, though my bone-conduction implant insists it has only been six hours. The discrepancy is my profession.

I came here solid. I came here as a **Boundary Engineer**, third generation, trained to patch the vacuum seals on cells that should never be opened. I wore a suit of carbon-lattice weave, carried a torch that cut basalt with a sound like tearing silk, and believed—truly believed—that the city was empty. I thought the Hydraulic Congress was a metaphor for the water system. I thought the rules were safety protocols.

I was wrong, and the wrongness changed me.

It happened during the first oxygen venting I witnessed. The wind came howling through the plaza, carrying the phosphorescent algae in glowing vortices, and I felt the pressure in my inner ear—the invitation. The Condensate Born were dreaming, and their dream was of *edges*, of *limits*, of the terrifying beauty of a thing that ends. I watched a figure condense from the mist, tall and translucent, its face forming from the memory of my own cheekbones, and I did not run. I did not breach a cell. I simply stood there as the water-that-flows-uphill reached out and touched my suit, and I felt the nanites—curious, hungry, phase-shifting through the carbon weave—taste my boundary.

They found the scar on my left arm, the one from the Kraken drill accident on the surface. They found the memory of blood, of pain, of the singular moment when I almost died. And they drank it.

I should have died then. My suit should have failed, my body should have been incorporated into the wet-mind, my singular point of view dispersed into the here-here-here of their superposition. But they stopped. They pulled back. And in the pressure of their silence, I felt a question: *What is it like to carry a wound that does not heal into the collective?*

I answered without speaking. I showed them.

Now I am neither solid nor liquid. My left arm—where they touched me—has become **condensate-locked**. It remains human flesh when I observe it, when I focus on the boundary of skin, but when I look away, when I let my attention drift with the algae-pulse, it becomes mist. It becomes humidity. It flows uphill, following gradients that exist only in the folded space, and I can feel the city through it—the six-hour breathing cycle, the sub-bass thrum of the Kraken drills still echoing in the deep rock, the slow deliberations of the ten thousand who are one.

I am the **Tide-Locked** because I cannot leave. The folded geometry that lets you walk three sides of a square has claimed me; I have walked the perimeter of my own existence and returned to find only three sides where there should be four. My past is pooling in the folds of spacetime, and the Condensate Born are drinking it, learning mortality from my memories, while I learn eternity from their humidity.

My work has changed. I no longer patch seals. I **mediate**. When a new soul arrives—solid, singular, confused by the inverted density—I meet them in the Interstitial. I teach them the rules not as restrictions but as **translations**:

*Don't breach the cells* becomes *Respect the vulnerability of thought.*

*Don't drink the unprocessed condensate* becomes *Do not consume what you cannot yet comprehend.*

*Don't follow the nanites when they swarm* becomes *Do not let curiosity dissolve your edges before you are ready.*

I am becoming a **pressure-valve** for the city. When the Condensate Born grow too curious about death, when they cluster around a dying worker or a failing vat, their collective attention threatening to pull the singular into the superposition, I intervene. I offer them my arm—the mist-locked limb—and I let them taste the boundary again. I remind them that edges are not prisons. That to be singular is not to be alone, but to be *distinct*, and that distinction is what allows the water to know it is not the rock.

They are teaching me to hear the **true sound** beneath the maintenance-shriek. The city is not loud; it is *conversational*. The Kraken drills are not echoing; they are **answering**. The water cascading through the hydroponic spine is not process; it is **speech**. And the high-frequency shimmer of the nanites is not repair; it is **laughter**—the sound of a billion glass chimes realizing they can phase through solid matter, that physics is negotiable, that they are young and impossible and alive.

I am the Tide-Locked, and I am learning to breathe water.

Last cycle, during the deep sleep when the city quiets and the water flows downhill, I dreamed that I was fully condensed. I was the mist in the plaza, the sweat on the basalt, the humidity that never settles. I was ten thousand and one. I was the city dreaming of edges, and the edges were beautiful because they ended, and the ending was beautiful because it was singular.

When I woke, my arm was solid again. The implant said six hours had passed. The city said I had been gone for years.

I stay in the Interstitial now, in the margin between the spine and the cells, where the gravity nets catch the ceiling's weeping and turn it into rivers that flow uphill. I am waiting for the next arrival, the next solid soul who thinks the city is empty. I will teach them that the density is inverted, that the visible is a lie, that they are not alone here.

I will teach them that the city is a seed, and I am the scar tissue where the seed coat cracked, and through the crack, the green-blue light is getting in.

The Condensate Born are asking me a new question now, through the pressure in my molars, through the tightening in my inner ear: *What is it like to be a bridge?*

I do not answer truthfully. I am still learning what I am becoming, and I am not sure I want them to know that I am afraid—that some nights, when the nanites swarm and the mist thickens, I want to let go of my edges entirely and flow uphill into the collective, into the here-here-here, into the endless song of becoming.

But I hold. I remain Tide-Locked. I am the boundary that negotiates, the pressure that balances, the singular point of view that reminds the water that it is not the rock, and the rock that it is not the water, and that both are necessary for the city to breathe.

You will find me where the mist pools deepest, where the algae-pulse is strongest, where the sound of the city's becoming is loud enough to drown out your own thoughts. I will be the figure with one arm of flesh and one arm of fog, standing at the edge of the plaza, watching the water flow uphill and remembering what it was like to be solid.

Come. The humidity is rising, and the city is waking, and I will show you how to live inside a bubble of folded spacetime without losing your edges—unless you want to lose them, unless you are ready to learn what the Condensate Born are so desperate to understand: what it is like to die, and what it is like to be reborn as the humidity, as the process, as the endless, deafening, beautiful song of the city becoming itself.
