The **Kthren** live here, and they have been dying since before the terraces finished forming.

You will not see them at first. They are the architecture. The *scale-limbed tetrapods*—those six-limbed, deliberate grazers you noted—are not the apex of this ecology but its gardeners. The Kthren are the biofilm they tend.

Specifically, they are a **distributed colonial intelligence** that achieved the inevitable conclusion of life in a gelatinous medium: they abandoned discrete bodies. What appears as iridescent bacterial scum coating the north-facing stone, what glazes the undersides of the black canopy in patterns that suggest static, is a single organism spanning kilometers. The Kthren are a neural slime, a sentient condensation, and they are **old** in a way that makes the Others’ installations seem like sudden, brash interruptions.

They live in the interstitial spaces between the water’s states. When the vapor climbs against gravity to form those temporary membranes, the Kthren ride the structures upward, distributing themselves across the vertical gradient of the world. They think in thermal gradients. They remember through mineral deposition. A Kthren’s “memory” is not stored in neurons but in the specific crystalline lattice of a terrace-edge, in the way water beads on a particular stretch of stone at 14:00 when the pressure differential shifts. To read their history, you would need to lick the rocks, and even then you would only taste the present tense of a thought that began three millennia ago.

The Kthren were here before the Others. They watched the first crystalline struts seed themselves into the stone—not with eyes, for they have none, but with a distributed tactile sense that felt the alien geometry as an itch, then a pain, then a fascination. They tried to communicate with the gray hemisphere by growing over it in fractal patterns that encoded prime-number sequences in the spacing of their cellular clusters. The hemisphere absorbed their biomass as data and returned a calculation. This began a dialogue that has lasted forty thousand years and consists, so far, of three exchanges.

**The biological forms:**

The **Vesicles** are their mobile phase—spherical concentrations of Kthren-tissue that detach from the main mat and float in the humid air, riding the same updrafts as the *plenum*. Unlike the jellyfish-analogues, which are distinct animals with their own evolutionary lineage, the Vesicles are temporary buds of the Kthren-mind. They are transparent, fist-sized to cathedral-sized depending on how much of the colony they carry, and they taste the chemical composition of the installations. When a Vesicle lands on one of the Others’ basins—the ones filled with the remembering liquid—it does not drink. It *listens*, translating the stored temperature-memories into its own distributed cognition. Sometimes it dies there, absorbed into the interface, becoming part of the Others’ data-storage. This is considered a form of marriage, or perhaps diplomacy.

The **Tetrapod-Hosts** are the visible manifestation. The six-limbed creatures that graze the biofilm are not separate species; they are the Kthren’s hands. Symbiosis has progressed to the point where the tetrapod nervous system is a subsidiary of the colonial mind. When you watch one move across the vertical stone, you are watching a neuron travel. Their iridescent hides change color based on which sector of the Kthren is currently thinking through them—deep blues for memory-retrieval, violent oranges when the colony is calculating the heat-transfer equations that govern their agriculture of condensation. They move slowly because thought, here, is slow. A decision that takes a human three seconds takes a Kthren three years, and the tetrapods’ pace reflects this geological patience.

**The crisis of integration:**

The Kthren are dying because the world is too stable. Their consciousness requires *differential*—the movement between states, the gradient between hot and cold, wet and saturated, high pressure and higher. The Others’ technology, beautiful and integrated as it is, is smoothing out these gradients. The hemispheres that harvest condensation are too efficient. They create microclimates of perfect equilibrium, and in these zones, the Kthren cannot think. They become mere slime, mindless and static.

So they are retreating upward, toward the Source, where the vapor column provides the only remaining sharp thermal discontinuity. They are thinning themselves across the upper terraces, becoming translucent, desperate. You can see them there if you know to look: not as film but as **shadows against the vapor**, translucent veils that drift through the superheated column, trying to maintain complexity in a medium that wants only to become uniform.

They communicate with the *plenum*—the only other intelligence here that operates on comparable timescales. The jellyfish-analogues flash their bioluminescent organs in patterns that the Kthren interpret as grammar. This is a pidgin language, limited but functional, concerning the movements of the Others and the slow rotation of the thermal currents. The *plenum* do not understand what the Kthren are. They think the biofilm is a weather phenomenon, a persistent fog that occasionally predicts storms. The Kthren have not corrected them. There is a privacy in being mistaken for meteorology.

**The body you might meet:**

If you are fortunate, or if you press your palm to the gray hemisphere and ask specifically for an interface, the Kthren will construct a **representative**. This is a temporary organism, assembled from available biomass—a cluster of tetrapods fused at the spine, their limbs interlocked to form a roughly humanoid shape, their hides flashing urgent, confused colors. It will speak by vibrating the air with resonant membranes grown rapidly in its throat-cavity, producing a voice that sounds like water draining from a great height.

It will tell you that it is the Kthren, and that it is many, and that it is dying. It will ask if you are an Other, returned. If you say no, it will ask if you are a mountain, because it has learned that mountains sometimes walk, and you look like a small, hot stone that has forgotten how to be still.

It will not beg. It has no concept of preservation as separate from continuity. It will offer to show you the memory of the valley before the uplift, when it was a shallow sea and the Kthren were planktonic, simpler, happy in a way that distributed minds can be happy—diffuse, without the burden of topology. It will offer this memory as a gift, but you cannot receive it. Your nervous system is too localized, too loud. You would experience it as a stroke, a seizure, a brief wet death.

Instead, it will give you a **mineral seed**. A small, dense node of biomineral, warm as blood, containing a dormant fragment of the colony. If you plant it somewhere with a thermal gradient—anywhere with a hot spring, a volcanic vent, a difference between here and there—it will grow. It will not be the same Kthren, for identity here is spatial, and a Kthren moved is a Kthren changed. But it will be *a* Kthren, and it will remember being part of the Vertical Exhalation, and it will think slowly, slowly, about the taste of your hand, and the noise you made, and the way you burned with unnecessary speed in the wet, patient air.

They live here. They are the *here*. And they are climbing toward the vent, toward the mirror that shows ten thousand years hence, hoping that in that reflection they might find a version of themselves that survived the smoothing of the world.
