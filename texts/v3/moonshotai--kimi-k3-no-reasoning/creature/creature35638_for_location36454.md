The Loom of Sunken Peaks is a sanctuary for the static, a single, breath-held moment inhabited by an ecology of ghosts and machines. The entities that reside here are not merely biological; they are the physical manifestations of a world that has forgotten how to move forward, yet refuses to stop screaming.

The primary inhabitants are the **Resonants**, a diverse fauna of chrome-feathered, translucent-skinned creatures whose lives are defined by the "Great Pause." These are not animals in the sense of birth, growth, and death, but rather biological snapshots—complex, living sculptures that occupy a fixed point in time. You will find herds of **Chrome-Grazers**, six-legged ungulates with hides like brushed steel and eyes like mirrored spheres, frozen mid-stride across the glassy expanses of the plateau. Though their limbs are locked in a permanent, graceful arc of movement, their internal biological processes continue in a disembodied loop. If you press your ear against the cold, metallic flank of a Grazer, you will not feel the vibration of a heart, but you will hear a frantic, muffled drumming echoing from within—a trapped, percussive rhythm that pulses in defiance of the creature's utter immobility.

In the dense, rust-colored thickets of the **Wire-Ferns**, there live the **Screechers**, avian entities that seem to be composed of tangled copper filaments and static electricity. They are the source of the cirque's relentless, tearing-metal shrieks. These birds never land; they are caught in a perpetual flight pattern, circling the same airspace for eternity. Their bodies are translucent, flickering with the blue-green glow of dying monitors, and as they flap their wings, they do not displace air so much as they shed trails of silver dust that hang suspended in the mist, creating shimmering, ephemeral constellations that mark their flight paths.

Beneath the surface of the black, shallow tarns reside the **Deep-Siphoners**. These are not fish, but long, serpentine ribbons of liquid mercury-like alloy that move through the water without creating ripples. They are the plateau's silent scavengers, drifting through the dark, still pools, feeding on the metallic grit and pulverized circuitry that constitutes the loam. They possess no eyes, only a row of sensory pits along their flanks that pulse with a soft, rhythmic light. They are the only inhabitants that seem to acknowledge the presence of an observer; if you linger too long at the edge of a pool, a Siphoner will rise to the surface, its flat, featureless head breaking the tension of the water just enough to "taste" the air, humming a low, resonant B-flat that vibrates in your marrow.

The most unsettling inhabitants are the **Weavers**, the only entities that appear to exert a semblance of agency. They are small, arachnid-like constructs, no larger than a thumb, composed of dark, jagged shale and glowing quartz veins. They move with a slow, deliberate, jerky gait, traversing the glowing silver lines that web the stone. They do not hunt or eat; they appear to be the maintenance crew of this broken world, constantly "tightening" the connections between the land and the machine. When they encounter a fissure in the rock or a dying fern, they secrete a viscous, luminous coolant from their mandibles, effectively "welding" the environment back into its frozen state. They are the keepers of the stagnation, tirelessly ensuring that the drop of water never falls and the fern never withers, moving with a singular, cold purpose that suggests they are the only things in the Loom that truly understand the prison they inhabit.
