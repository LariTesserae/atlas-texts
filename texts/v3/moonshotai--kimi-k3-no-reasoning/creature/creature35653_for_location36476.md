The creatures here do not live so much as they *persist*, maintaining themselves in the space between existence and occupation, too numerous to ignore, too regular to call natural.

The most numerous are the **Folded Ones** — six-legged beings with bodies like damp origami, their pale surfaces creased into precise geometric panels. They range in size from a thumbnail to a human hand, but all share the same proportions, as if scaled from a single template. They rest in groups of exactly seventeen, arranged in spirals that seem random until you notice they always form the same pattern relative to the condensation stones. Their legs end in small, cup-like pads that never quite touch the ground, maintaining a fraction of an inch between themselves and the floor at all times. Occasionally, one will shift position — a slow, deliberate unfolding of limbs that takes hours to complete. When they move, they leave no trace in the sand, no impression, no disturbance. They simply *cease* being in one place and *are* in another, as if the space between positions doesn't quite apply to them.

They do not eat in any observable way, but they gather in their tightest spirals around the teacup pools when they form, arranged like supplicants at an altar. Not drinking — their heads, if the folded planes at their fronts can be called heads, remain tilted at precisely forty-five degrees upward, toward the sourceless light. Perhaps they absorb it. Perhaps they are absorbing something else entirely, something the water merely marks the presence of.

The **Patience-Shells** move so slowly that their motion can only be confirmed by remembering where they were decades ago. They resemble tortoises designed by someone who had only heard tortoises described: low-domed shells in concentric rings of pearl and bone, no visible head or limbs, just the shell and a suggestion of something beneath that might be a body or might be shadow. They travel in straight lines across the cavern floor, each taking a different route, their paths never crossing. If you were to map their movements over centuries, the lines would spell something, though whether in a language meant to be read or simply the byproduct of geometry is impossible to say. They leave no trail behind them, no furrow in the sand, as if the earth refuses to acknowledge their passage.

When two Patience-Shells approach the same condensation stone from opposite directions — a convergence that happens perhaps once every fifty years — they stop precisely one foot apart and wait. Not facing each other, but facing *past* each other, toward the stone. They remain this way for exactly seven days. Then they resume their paths, unchanged, carrying nothing visible away from the encounter. Whether information passed between them or whether they simply synchronized with the stone's slow release of moisture is a question the cavern declines to answer.

The **Resonants** are the only creatures that fly, and they do so with mathematical precision that makes their flight feel less like movement and more like the execution of an equation. They are small, perhaps four inches from wingtip to wingtip, with bodies like elongated seeds and wings that catch the light in ways that suggest they're not quite solid. Each day — though how days are measured in a place where the light never changes is unclear — they rise from their roosting positions on the walls and fly for exactly one hour.

Their flight patterns are perfect geometric figures: triangles nested within hexagons, spirals that maintain constant ratios, loops that cross themselves at precise angles. They move through these patterns in absolute silence except for the hum — a low, harmonic resonance that vibrates not in the ears but in the bones of the skull, in the teeth, in the spaces between vertebrae. The hum has no single source; it seems to emerge from the air itself, as if the creatures are instruments being played by the cavern rather than the other way around.

When the hour ends, they return to the walls and arrange themselves in a formation that is different each day but always precise — a new constellation of small, pale bodies pressed against stone. If you were to mark their positions over a year, you would have three hundred and sixty-five distinct patterns, none repeating. Whether they are counting something or composing something is unclear. They simply *are*, roosting in stillness, waiting for their hour of perfect motion.

The **Molt-Things** are rarer and stranger. You might mistake them for stones at first — rounded, fist-sized objects scattered near the stone circles. But occasionally, one will split along a seam you hadn't noticed, revealing layers inside like an onion made of thin, translucent membrane. The layers unfold slowly over the course of hours, peeling back to expose smaller and smaller versions of the same shape, nested within each other like a diagram of infinite regression. At the center is nothing — a hollow space where the smallest version should be.

Once fully opened, they remain that way for a period ranging from three days to three months, then slowly fold themselves back together, layer by layer, until they are seamless stones again. They never move from where they sit. They open and close in rhythm with nothing observable, at intervals that might be random or might follow a pattern too large to perceive from within a human lifespan. The translucent grass grows closest to them, its blades curving away at precise angles, as if bent by an emanation too subtle to feel.

Perhaps strangest are the **Thin Ones** — if they can be called creatures at all. They appear as distortions in the light, vertical wavering patches roughly the height and width of a human being, visible only when you're not looking directly at them. In peripheral vision, they seem almost solid, almost shaped like *something*, but when you turn to face them they resolve into nothing — just the bright, constant light, just the pale stone behind them. Yet they cast those faint luminous counterparts that everything in the Unburrow casts, the ghosts of brightness that serve as shadows. You can see those ghosts clearly: humanoid in outline, standing near the stone circles, near the patterns of skull-sized rocks, near the tools that appear and disappear.

They do not move. They do not interact with the other creatures. They simply occupy space at the edges of perception, visible in their un-visibility. If you stand where one seems to be standing, you feel nothing — no presence, no cold, no weight. But the condensation stones nearby will release their moisture faster, and the Folded Ones will adjust their spirals to maintain their seventeen-fold symmetry around the point where the distortion stands.

All of them — Folded, Patience-Shell, Resonant, Molt-Thing, Thin One — exist in a state of perfect equilibrium with the space. They do not deplete it. They do not alter it in any way that persists. The condensation still gathers and releases on its seventeen-year cycle. The grass maintains its six-foot spacing. The light remains constant. They are abundant without ecology, present without impact, alive in a way that life as we understand it does not quite encompass.

If you stay long enough — and you could stay forever, if you chose — you begin to notice that their patterns accommodate you. The Folded Ones adjust their spirals. The Resonants shift their flight paths by degrees so small you'd miss them if you weren't watching for change. The Patience-Shells alter their trajectories over decades to avoid the places where you habitually stand. Not reacting to you, exactly. Simply *including* you in their geometry, the way they include the stones and the grass and the tools that manifest and vanish.

And if you left, they would adjust back. Not immediately. Not with any urgency. The Folded Ones would take hours to resume their original spirals. The Patience-Shells would correct their paths over years. The Resonants would return to their unmodified patterns within days. Slowly, patiently, the cavern would forget you — not through any act of erasure, but through the simple persistence of a pattern too old and too complete to be permanently altered by a single temporary presence.

The creatures would continue. Occupying. Persisting. Maintaining their precise positions in a space that is bright without sun, wet without rain, inhabited without life in any form that makes sense to the world above.

They are what lives here.

They are what *lives* here, in the way that a thought lives in a mind, in the way that a held breath lives in lungs — present, sustained, and utterly sufficient unto itself.
