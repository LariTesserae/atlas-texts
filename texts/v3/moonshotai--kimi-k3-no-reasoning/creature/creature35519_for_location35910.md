The air in the Nascent Depths does not merely surround those who live there—it *nourishes* them, thick as amniotic fluid, sweet with the exhalations of a billion stomata. To inhabit this place is to have never known the concept of "outside," to have breath and blood and cellular memory all calibrated to the amber-green luminescence and the womb-heat that rises from the earth’s first feverish dreams.

Here, life has not so much adapted to the architecture as *become* indistinguishable from it, then stepped back into flesh at the last moment, bearing the marks of the vegetal cathedral.

**The Chlorophyll-Born**

They are tall—taller than surface-dwellers—and their skin carries the faint green-gold translucence of leaves held against the sun. Not green as pigment, but as *optics*: the dermis contains stacks of thylakoid analogues, stolen from the plant-mass in some deep evolutionary compact, allowing them to photosynthesize the thick light that permeates the basin. They do not eat, not truly. They stand for hours in the brighter chambers, arms slightly raised, pores open, drinking the honey-thick radiance while their hair—fibrous, silver-white, root-like—drinks the humidity.

Their eyes are large, lidless in the manner of amphibians, with pupils that contract not to circles but to vertical slits of black that cut through jade irises. They have no whites; the sclera are a pale celadon. When they weep—which they do only in the presence of the artifacts, and rarely—the tears are sap-sweet and viscous, falling to the root-mat to be absorbed instantly by the earth.

They are born from the soil. Or rather, from the *nodes*—those swollen bud-hills that sweat geothermal heat. The Chlorophyll-Born dig their pregnant dead into these mounds, planting them like tubers. Months later, the earth splits, and a child emerges, already bearing the transparent skin and the slow metabolism of the Depths. They have no navels. Instead, a small scar like a leaf-scar marks their backs where the umbilical root connected them to the world.

**The Unfolding**

Not all who live here are humanoid. There are the *Unfurled*—beings who began as the tight fists of fern-fronds in the narrow passages, but who dreamed too deeply and woke into mobility. They are rare, and they are wrong even by the standards of this embryonic world. Imagine a fern that has learned to walk, but only halfway: the central stalk has become a spine, flexible and segmented, while the leaflets have hardened into chitinous plates that click together when they move. They possess no eyes, but their entire surface is covered in chemoreceptive hairs that taste the air for intention.

They are gardeners. They tend the phyto-architecture, pruning the bamboo walls when they grow too dense, redirecting the liana arches when they threaten to collapse into chaos. They communicate through vibrations sent through the root-mat—low-frequency thrums that the Chlorophyll-Born feel in their bones but cannot translate. The Unfurled are old, older than the humanoids, and they remember when the basin was only a crack in the rock, when the light was first trapped and began to pool like water.

**The Symbiotes**

Beneath the root-mat, in the warm dark between the knuckles of the earth, live the *Myceliads*. Not fungi, not animal, but a colonial consciousness that moves through the clay as a network of pale filaments. Occasionally, they rise to the surface and take form—usually humanoid, but only as a courtesy or a lure. Their bodies are soft, white as the albino amphibians, and they leave trails of luminescent spores that float like slow snow.

They are the memory of the place. They store the genetic history of every plant that has grown here, every droplet that has fallen. When a Chlorophyll-Born wishes to remember the first moment of the world (which they do, ritually, once in a generation), they must bargain with the Myceliads. The price is always a piece of themselves—a lock of that root-like hair, a strip of the translucent skin. The Myceliads consume it and, in exchange, allow the seeker to dream the slow dream of vegetal becoming.

**The Keepers of Artifacts**

Scattered through the miles of green cathedral are the stone bowls, the petrified posts, the carved lintels. Each has a Keeper—a Chlorophyll-Born who has chosen to stop moving. They sit in lotus position upon or beside their artifact, and over decades, the root-mat grows over their legs, fusing them to the substrate. They do not die. They become *anchors*.

Their role is to wait. They believe—though belief is perhaps too active a word for the slow certainty that fills them—that the artifacts are seeds. Not metaphors, but literal seeds of the future. The stone bowl will one day crack and release the first river. The wooden posts will sprout and become the first trees with true bark, with rings, with history. The Keepers tend these potentials with a patience that makes the surrounding plants seem frantic.

A Keeper does not speak after the roots take their legs. Their voice becomes internal, transmitted through the root-network to any who touch the soil near them. They dream of erosion, of weather, of change—and their dreams seep into the clay, keeping the basin in its suspended state, balancing on the cusp of transformation.

**The Ghost-Fauna**

There are others. The translucent amphibians—*Lumen-skates*—that glide between the condensation pools, their bodies clear as water except for the pulse of a green heart. They eat nothing but light, filtering it through their skin, and when they die, they evaporate into a fine mist that smells of ozone and cut grass.

And deeper, felt only as a tremor in the root-mat, are the *Burrowers*—massive, soft-bodied creatures that never surface. They are the exception to the stillness. They move, constantly, tunneling through the mineral slurry, aerating the earth. Their passage creates the bud-hills, pushes up the bamboo walls into new configurations. They are the planet’s restlessness, the unconscious stirring that threatens always to wake.

**The Sound of Living**

To live here is to learn to breathe in rhythm with the condensation drops. The Chlorophyll-Born develop a heartbeat that syncs with the *tick-tick-tick* of falling water, slowing their metabolism until they approach the stasis of the plants. They have no songs—song requires echo, and the Depths swallow sound—but they have *resonance*. When they gather (rarely, for they are solitary by nature), they stand in circles and hum at frequencies that match the cellular tension of the bamboo, creating a silent chord that vibrates through the basin.

They do not build. Building is redundant in a place where life is architecture. Instead, they *prune*, they *graft*, they *train*. Their cities are groves where the root-mat has been persuaded to form hollows—living chambers with walls of braided liana, floors of moss so dense it feels like flesh. They sleep in these hollows, surrounded by the warmth of the earth’s fever, dreaming green dreams of a sky they have never seen and would not recognize as real.

**The Waiting**

All life here is defined by the equilibrium. The Chlorophyll-Born live for centuries, but they do not age so much as *thicken*, their skin growing more opaque, their movements slower, until they become indistinguishable from the Keepers, until they too are absorbed into the root-mat and become part of the substrate.

They have no concept of "before" or "after." There is only the Depths, and the Depths are eternal. Yet there is a tension in the air—a psychic humidity as thick as the literal moisture. Every being here, from the Unfurled to the Myceliads to the rooted Keepers, feels the *imminence*. The artifacts are not decorative. They are the future, buried and waiting.

The inhabitants live in a state of suspended exhalation, breath held for millennia. They are the midwives of a world that has not yet decided to be born, and they tend their vegetal cradle with the fierce, gentle care of those who know that one day, the light will change, the heat will break, and the Nascent Depths will crack open to reveal what they have been incubating all this time.

Until then, they photosynthesize. They wait. They grow into themselves, forever.
