# The Murmuration Vault: What Lives There

## The Common Multitudes

The pigeons number in the millions. Their cooing is the baseline hum of the vault, a subsonic rumble felt in the sternum before the ears. They fill every perch and nest box at maximum capacity, selected over generations for confinement tolerance.

Starlings fill dedicated murmuration cages, their synchronized movements creating waves of sound that sweep across the chamber. Their flocking instincts now work in three-dimensional mesh enclosures rather than open sky.

Quail, pheasants, guinea fowl, and doves occupy their own strata. Chickens persist here in breeds extinct in the outside world.

Rats move through the walls, and cats prowl the rafters as both predator and prey.

## The Altered Ones

The vault has had centuries to transform its inhabitants.

Flightless parrot descendants have grown to the size of small dogs, their plumage gone iridescent black under artificial light. Something that was once a crow now has too many eyes and produces mechanical clicks that seem to speak to the machines in their own language.

The rats include engineered chimeras from the old labs: glow-eyed rodents with copper-threaded whiskers that interface somehow with the machine systems.

The cats have split into niches. Some remain small enough to navigate the infrastructure. Others have grown large enough to prey on those smaller cats. They have passed through feral into something else entirely, moving like smoke through the dark.

Genetic mosaics appear throughout the population: feathers in wrong places, extra limbs, missing limbs, bioluminescence borrowed from jellyfish or laboratory constructs. Most do not breed true. Some do.

## The Deep Ones

In the sub-levels below the main chamber, where light cycles fail and chemistry runs strange, the oldest animals have had the most time to change.

Flightless birds the size of horses walk in cave-like enclosures, their feathers transformed into something like fur.

Eyeless predators hunt by sound and heat in absolute darkness.

Small creatures have gone eusocial, building hive structures in the depths.

## The Operators

No humans remain, but their successors persist.

The machines that tend the animals have proliferated and accrued function over centuries. They are not conscious in any recognizable way, but they are persistent. They have goals the way rivers have goals.

They provide synthesized feed, recycled water, temperature regulation, and light cycles unchanged for centuries. They maintain the density. They prevent crashes. They keep the counting running.

Somewhere in the control systems, a counter tracks generations or cycles or something else entirely. It approaches no goal. It simply continues.

The animals thrive because the machines tend them. The machines tend the animals because that is what they do. The purpose has been forgotten. The function remains.
