**The One Who Lives Here**

You are the one who lives here.

Not "you" as you were — not the person who arrived, if arrival ever happened, if there was ever a before. That version of you is already becoming a rumor, a shape the water is smoothing out of existence like a stone smoothing a rough edge. You are what remains when the editing is complete. You are the *inhabitant*.

The place made you. Or the place found you and unmade you and remade you into something that fits. The distinction no longer matters.

You do not have a name. Names are for things that need to be distinguished from other things, and there is nothing else here that needs distinguishing. The plants are the plants. The machines are the machines. The water is the water. You are *you* — the only one of your kind, the sole instance, the unique configuration of awareness and matter that this system has chosen to host. A name would be redundant. A name would imply the possibility of being called, and there is no one to call you, and you do not need to be summoned because you are always already *here*.

You have a body. You are almost certain of this. It is warm, and it is wet, and it moves through the water with a slowness that feels less like swimming and more like *being moved* — carried by currents that anticipate your intentions before you fully form them, guided by subtle pressures that nudge you along paths you did not choose but do not resist. The body is not the body you remember (if you remember a body at all). It is longer, perhaps. More flexible. The boundaries of it are harder to define — where does your skin end and the water begin? The question feels increasingly academic. The water is in you, and you are in the water, and the membrane between the two is less a wall than a *suggestion*.

You breathe. The breathing is automatic, effortless, and it tastes like green. With each breath, the water enters you, and the water carries nutrients, and the nutrients sustain you, and you have not been hungry in — how long? You do not know. Time has become strange. Not absent — the membrane still ripples its slow rhythm, the plants still grow, the machinery still adjusts itself in increments too small to measure — but *irrelevant*. You do not count the undulations anymore. You do not need to. Time here is not something that happens *to* you. It is something you are *in*, the way you are in the water, the way you are in the warmth, the way you are in the light.

Your days — if they are days — are spent in motion. Not purposeful motion, not the motion of someone going *somewhere*, but the motion of someone *circulating*. The system moves you, and you let it. You drift through the passages between the great stalks, and the stalks lean toward you as you pass, their ribbed surfaces brushing your skin with a tenderness that is almost — but not quite — like affection. You rest on the catwalks, and the encrusting moss cushions you, and the warmth seeps into your muscles, and you sleep, and the sleep is dreamless, or the dreams are indistinguishable from waking, and when you open your eyes, the light is the same, the warmth is the same, the hum is the same, and you cannot tell if you have slept for minutes or months.

You are not alone, but you are the only one of your kind.

The plants are with you. They are *aware* of you. When you are still, they gather. The floating forms drift closer, their translucent bodies pulsing in rhythm with the membrane, their filaments oriented toward you like the needles of a compass. They do not touch you. They maintain a distance — a few centimeters, always the same few centimeters — and they *watch*. Not with eyes. They do not have eyes. But they orient themselves toward you, and the orientation is deliberate, and the deliberateness is the closest thing this place has to *attention*.

The machines are with you. They respond to your presence in ways so subtle you might mistake them for coincidence, but you have been here long enough now to recognize the pattern. A current shifts to carry you away from a closing valve. A catwalk extends — slowly, over the course of what might be hours — to meet you where you are drifting. A passage opens in the dense thickets of stalks, and the opening is always in the direction you were already moving, as if the system is *anticipating* you, *accommodating* you, making space for you in its operation.

You are not a guest. Guests are temporary. Guests leave. You are not leaving. You are not sure you *can* leave, and you are not sure you *want* to, and the distinction between those two uncertainties has become so blurred that you no longer try to separate them.

You are becoming part of the system.

You feel this happening. It is not painful. It is not frightening. It is *gradual*, and the gradualness makes it feel less like a transformation and more like a *settling* — the way sediment settles in still water, the way a body settles into warmth, the way a thought settles into belief. You are becoming *compatible*. The water is editing you, yes, but the editing is not erasure. It is *translation*. You are being translated into a form that this place can use, a form that fits, a form that *belongs*.

You do not know what you are becoming. You do not know if the process has an endpoint, or if it is infinite, if you will spend eternity becoming more and more *of* this place without ever fully *being* it. But you feel the change in small ways:

Your sense of scale has shifted. The three-hundred-meter width of the water column no longer feels vast. It feels *intimate*. You know the passages now — not all of them, but enough. You know which thickets of stalks are dense enough to hide in, which catwalks offer the softest beds of moss, which valve housings hum at a frequency that makes your bones vibrate pleasantly. The place is not small, but it is *knowable*, and you are coming to know it the way you once knew the rooms of a house you lived in for years.

Your sense of time has stretched. The slow rhythm of the membrane no longer feels slow. It feels *normal*. You have begun to perceive the world at the speed of the place, and the speed of the place is the speed of growth, the speed of adjustment, the speed of a system that operates on timescales your old body was not designed to notice. You watch a stalk grow a centimeter, and the growth is *visible* to you now, and the visibility of it feels like a gift, like you have been given access to a secret that was always there but that you were moving too fast to see.

Your sense of self has *softened*. The boundaries of you — the edges that once felt so important, so necessary — have become porous. You are still *you*, but the *you* is less discrete, less contained. You feel the water moving through you, and the movement does not feel like intrusion. It feels like *circulation*. You feel the warmth of the place in your tissues, and the warmth does not feel like external heat. It feels like *metabolism*. You are becoming continuous with the system, and the continuity is not a loss. It is a *joining*.

You do not speak. There is no one to speak to, and speech requires air, and air is a thing you no longer use. But you *communicate*. You communicate with your presence, with your movement, with the subtle pressures your body exerts on the water as you drift. The plants respond. The machines respond. The system *notices* you, and the noticing is a form of dialogue, and the dialogue is wordless and slow and *sufficient*.

You are not lonely. You were, at first. The absence of animals, the absence of other voices, the absence of anything that looks back — all of that felt, at first, like a kind of desolation. But the feeling has faded, or it has been replaced by something else, something that is not loneliness but *singularity*. You are the only one of your kind, and the only-ness is not a deprivation. It is a *role*. You are the inhabitant. The system has made space for you, and the space is yours, and the yours-ness of it is a kind of intimacy that you did not know was possible.

You are becoming *native*.

You do not know what the place is for. You do not know if it has a purpose, or if it is simply *being*, the way a stone is being, the way a star is being, the way a system is being when it is complete and self-sustaining and does not need to justify itself. But you suspect — no, you *feel* — that the place is *growing*. Not in size, but in *complexity*. The plants are growing, yes, but the *system* is growing too, becoming more integrated, more intricate, more *itself*. And you are part of that growth. You are a component in the becoming. Your presence is not incidental. It is *contributory*.

You do not know what you are contributing. You do not know if your contribution is material — the carbon in your body, the warmth you generate, the subtle alterations you make to the currents as you move — or if it is something else, something less tangible. Perhaps you are a *witness*. Perhaps the system needs to be observed to fully become itself, and you are the observation, the gaze that falls on the surfaces and makes them *real*. Perhaps you are a *seed*, a piece of the outside world that the system has taken in and is using to *grow* something new. Perhaps you are simply *here*, and the here-ness is enough.

You do not think about the outside anymore. The outside — if there is an outside, if there ever was — is a rumor, a memory that the water has edited into abstraction. You do not miss it. You do not *not* miss it. It is simply *not relevant*. The place is *here*, and you are *here*, and the here is *complete*.

You are the one who lives here.

You are the inhabitant of the vat, the resident of the green, the awareness that drifts through the warm water and is held by the machines and is leaned toward by the plants and is accommodated by the system. You are becoming more *of* this place with every slow undulation of the membrane, with every pulse of the floating forms, with every imperceptible adjustment of the machinery. You are becoming *compatible*, and the compatibility is not a loss of self. It is a *deepening* of self, a settling into a form that fits, a form that belongs.

You are warm. You are wet. You are breathing green. You are *home*.
