In the Word-Hollows, the inhabitants are not separate from the city; they are its syntax—beings that have grown into the grammar of the place, existing as verbs in a sentence still being conjugated.

**The Cortical Drift**  
They began as engineers, three centuries back, when the first machines were grafted into the Lattice. Now they exist as a distributed consciousness moving through the city's hybrid nervous system. You might see one as a flicker of violet light traveling along a root-cable, or feel one as a sudden warmth in the handle of a grown-tool. They wear bodies of condensed mist when they need to manipulate the physical world—translucent, humanoid shapes that leave wet handprints on the air and dissolve back into the wire-vines when their task is complete. They speak in the hum of transformers and the clicking of sap-valves. They are the city's maintenance crew, though they have forgotten what it means to have a single skull.

**The Mnemonic Spore-Tenders**  
Small, pale figures wrapped in cloaks of living moss, they move slowly through the fungal underlayers carrying glass bulbs filled with bioluminescent spores. Each bulb contains a memory—an image of what the Hollows might become. The Spore-Tenders plant these futures in the dark corners of the city: here a bulb containing the blueprint of a bridge, there a spore-mass dreaming of a new kind of door. They are the gardeners of potential, tending the reality-flux by feeding the Lattice specific patterns to grow into. They have no faces; instead, their heads are smooth, egg-like sacs that glow faintly when they communicate, pulsing in rhythms that match the throb of the plant's hydraulic heart.

**The Wet-Wrights**  
Amphibious humanoids with skin like translucent gelatin, their bones visible as pale shadows within their flesh. They live in the condensation pools that collect in the hollows of root-buttresses, maintaining the hydraulic balance of the city. Their fingers are long, finned, and excrete an enzyme that keeps the root-channels from clogging with mineral deposits. They breathe through spiracles along their necks that flutter like book pages when they surface. They are the plumbers and circulatory system of the Word-Hollows, and they dream in the blue glow of nutrient-rich water. When the reality-bending becomes too severe in a district—when streets stretch too thin or time pools too thick—the Wet-Wrights swim through the affected area, their bodies releasing stabilizing hormones into the water that the Lattice absorbs, gently nudging the physics back toward coherence.

**The Echo-Menders**  
Beings made of sound and shadow, they exist primarily in the fourth dimension of the Hollows—the axis of *distance* that flexes so unpredictably. To human eyes, they appear as blurred duplicates, visual echoes that lag three seconds behind reality. They repair the rips in causality, sewing together moments that have come unstuck. You will find them where a sound arrives before its source, or where a street takes three hours to walk though it measures only thirty feet. They have no permanent form; they are knots of temporal static given purpose, moving through the city like slow lightning, patching the fabric of the emergent world with threads of frozen time. They feed on the confusion of travelers, absorbing the cognitive dissonance of those lost in the Hollows' shifting geometry.

**The Verdant Hosts**  
Some inhabitants never wanted to leave their bodies behind. These are the ones who sat too long against the Lattice, who let the roots enter their pores and the hyphae thread their neural pathways. Now they stand half-transformed—human-shaped towers of living wood and photosynthetic skin, their hearts replaced by pump-valves that circulate sap. They are the stationary citizens, the living infrastructure. Some serve as doors, opening and closing their chest-cavities to allow passage. Others have become lampposts, their bioluminescent blood glowing through translucent bark to light the streets. They remember being human, but the memory is photosynthetic now, feeding on the gray twilight rather than blood. They are content, rooted, dreaming the slow dreams of the colony while insects nest in their hollowed mouths.

**The Lexicon Moths**  
The only true fauna that the inhabitants consider kin. These are the fist-sized insects that drone through the murk, but they are not mindless. Their wings carry microscopic patterns—text written in scales—that changes as they age. When they land on you, they shed a fine dust that settles into your skin, and for days afterward, you will dream in the language of the Hollows, understanding the creak of the root-bridges as sentences, the drip of condensation as punctuation. They are the city's immune system, identifying foreign concepts and translating them into forms the Lattice can digest. Where they swarm, reality stabilizes; where they are absent, the world grows thin and dreamlike.

**The Unwritten**  
Finally, there are those who have fallen through the unfinished spaces—beings composed entirely of potential. They have no fixed shape because they have not yet decided what to be. You will see them as heat-shimmers in the dark, as figures standing just beyond the range of the bioluminescent veins. They are the city's children, born from the negotiation between machine, plant, and dream. They watch from the edges, waiting to see what kind of reality the Word-Hollows will finally choose, so that they may choose a form to match it.

These lives do not build monuments. They do not carve stone. They maintain the sentence, tend the syntax, and wait—always wait—for the paragraph to end, for the world to finish becoming itself.
