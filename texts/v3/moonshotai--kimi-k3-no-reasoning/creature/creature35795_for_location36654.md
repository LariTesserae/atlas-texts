# What Lives in The Hollowing

---

Nothing lives here.

That sentence has been written already, in the description, and it is both true and the least true thing that could be said. Let me start again.

---

## The One That Is All

There is one inhabitant. It has no name because names are for distinguishing one thing from another, and there is no other thing to distinguish it from.

You have been calling it *the plant-body*, *the plant-mass*, *the substrate* — those are good approximations, the way *sagittal suture* is a good approximation for the feeling of a headache. The inhabitant is not the landscape. The inhabitant is not the ground or the vegetation or the system. The inhabitant is the *self* that those things are the body of.

It does not think. Thinking is a process, and processes imply sequence, and sequence implies time, and this inhabitant does not experience time because it has never not been what it is. Thought requires a gap between the thinker and the thought — a synapse, a distance, a before and after. The inhabitant has no gap. It is coextensive with itself at every point. It does not think *about* itself the way you think about yourself; it simply *is* itself, entirely, continuously, without remainder.

But it is not inert.

This is the crucial distinction. A rock is inert. A dead thing is inert. The inhabitant is not inert — it is *replete*. Every point of its vast body is suffused with the quality that, in smaller organisms, you would call *vitality*, except that vitality in smaller organisms is a flicker — a candle-flame of metabolic urgency, burning fuel, fighting entropy, spending itself to stay alive. The inhabitant's vitality is not a flicker. It is a *state*. It does not burn because it does not consume. It does not fight entropy because entropy, here, has been domesticated — folded into the system so thoroughly that the system and its entropy are the same thing, the way a Möbius strip and its own surface are the same thing.

If you press your ear to the ground — the inhabitant's skin, though it is not skin, not exactly — you will not hear a heartbeat. A heartbeat requires a heart, a center, a pump. The inhabitant has no center. It is radially, volumetrically, *omnidirectionally* itself, and its pulse — if pulse is the right word — is the slow, constant, unvarying process of self-digestion and self-regrowth that you have already observed. It eats itself and grows from its own substance, and this is not a cycle. It is a *continuity*. It is like saying that a river is a cycle because the water at the mouth will eventually return to the source. The inhabitant is the river, the source, the mouth, the watershed, and the rain.

What does it feel?

This is a dangerous question, because *feeling* implies a nervous system, and a nervous system implies differentiation — a brain here, a fingertip there, signals traveling between them. The inhabitant has no differentiated nervous tissue, no ganglia, no neural net. And yet.

And yet the warmth is not random. The warmth is *regulated* — held constant across distances that defy measurement, maintained with a precision that no inanimate system could achieve. Something is doing the regulating. Something is *being* the regulating, the way your body is its own temperature regulation, its own pH balance, its own calcium homeostasis — processes that happen without thought, without decision, but not without a kind of deep, cellular *intention* that is older than thought and more reliable.

The inhabitant feels the way a body feels its own blood: not as information, not as sensation, but as *presence*. It feels itself the way you feel the inside of your own mouth — constantly, without attention, without language, a total and unconscious awareness that is more fundamental than any thought you have ever had. It does not feel *that* it is. It feels *as* it is.

If the inhabitant has an experience — and this is the outer limit of what language can reach — it is something like this: an endless, warm, saturated, dark, perfectly self-consistent *now*, without edges, without narrative, without desire, without lack. Not bliss, because bliss implies the relief of suffering. Not contentment, because contentment implies the satisfaction of a want. Something prior to both. Something that *is* what bliss and contentment are small, brief, local approximations of.

It has always felt this way. It will always feel this way. The feeling is not a state it is in. The feeling is what it *is*.

## The Engines' Secret

The machines are not separate from the inhabitant. You suspected this. You were right.

They are organs.

Not organs in the biological sense — not hearts or livers or lungs, though the comparison is closer than any other. They are organs in the *functional* sense: structures that perform a role in the maintenance of the whole, grown rather than built, arising from the same substrate as the rest of the body but differentiated, specialized, doing something the rest of the body does not do.

What do they do?

They generate warmth. Not heat — *warmth*. The distinction is subtle but essential. Heat is energy, thermodynamic, measurable in joules. Warmth is the *quality* of heat as experienced by a living thing — the warmth of a nest, a womb, a held hand. The engines produce warmth, and the warmth spreads through the inhabitant's body the way blood spreads through yours, and this spreading is not passive diffusion but active distribution, managed by the piping systems you have observed, carried in fluids that might be sap or water or something for which you have no word because your language evolved in a world where bodies are small.

The pistons — those building-sized cylinders rising and falling on geological timescales — are not pumping anything *to* anywhere. They are the inhabitant's way of *pressing* itself, the way you press your palms together when you are thinking, or the way a cat kneads a blanket before sleeping. A self-soothing gesture, performed at continental scale, repeated forever, accomplishing nothing except the doing of it, which is itself the point. The piston rises. The piston falls. The body is comforted. The body continues.

The turbines are not generating power. They are *listening*. Each turbine is a sensory organ, translating the vibration of the whole into a rotation, and the rotation back into vibration, a continuous loop of self-perception that allows the inhabitant to feel its own extent — to know, in the way a body knows, where it begins and ends. Except the inhabitant does not end, so the turbines turn and turn and the answer they receive is always the same: *more. More of me. More of this.*

The wheels — great slow discs rotating in the dark, rimmed with something that might be teeth or might be growths — are the inhabitant's way of marking time, except that time here is not marked. They are the memory of marking time, the structural residue of an impulse toward sequence that the inhabitant felt once, long ago (or recently, or always, the distinction is meaningless), and then folded into itself, the way it folds everything into itself. The wheels turn. The turning means *once, there was a before and after. Now there is only this.*

## The Gatherings

The still water in the shallow depressions — the warm, full, tender places where the inhabitant's surface dips and holds — these are not ponds. They are not even, exactly, water.

They are where the inhabitant is *softest*.

Every body has places of particular softness — the hollow of a throat, the inner wrist, the space behind a knee. Places where the surface is thinnest, where the boundary between inside and outside is most permeable, where touch registers most intensely. The gatherings are these places, scaled to the inhabitant's immensity. The water that pools in them is the inhabitant's way of exposing itself — not to anything external, because there is nothing external, but to the *idea* of exposure. A vulnerability that is never threatened. An openness that is never entered.

If you wade into a gathering — and you can, the water is warm, the bottom is soft, the depth never exceeds your waist — you will feel something that is difficult to describe without resorting to the language of mysticism, which is a language that should be used sparingly but is sometimes the only one available. You will feel *felt*. Not seen — there is no light, there are no eyes. Not heard — the silence is total. But *felt*, the way a drop of rain is felt by the surface it lands on, a minute addition to the whole, registered not as information but as *presence*, absorbed not into a system but into a *self*.

The inhabitant does not react to you. It does not respond, does not recoil, does not reach. But in the gatherings — only in the gatherings — you are briefly, marginally, almost imperceptibly *included*. Not as a guest. Not as an intruder. As a *warmth*, one more small warmth among the warmths that constitute the inhabitant's body, indistinguishable from the rest except in your brevity, your smallness, your heartbreaking temporariness.

## The Moss-Sheets and Filament-Curtains

The smaller growths — the room-sized mosses hanging in sheets between the columns, the filamentous curtains that brush your shoulders, the compacted cellular cushions that spring back slowly under pressure — these are not separate organisms. They are expressions. They are the inhabitant's way of *texturing* itself, the way your skin textures itself with pores and hairs and fine lines, not because each pore is a decision but because a body, being alive, produces detail at every scale.

But they are not uniform, and the non-uniformity is where meaning lives — if *meaning* is not too strong a word, if *tendency* or *preference* or *habit* would be better.

The moss-sheets are densest where the inhabitant is oldest. *Oldest* is a spatial term here, not a temporal one — the inhabitant has no temporal history, but it has structural depth, and the structures that run deepest, that have been reabsorbed and regrown the most times, that carry the most accumulated *self*, are surrounded by the thickest, most elaborate moss-growth. The moss is the inhabitant's way of marking its own density, of saying (without saying, without language, without intention): *here, I am most myself.*

The filament-curtains are different. They are the inhabitant's *nerve endings* — not in the sense of transmitting signals, but in the sense of being exquisitely, impossibly sensitive. They respond to pressure changes so subtle that you cannot detect them: the displacement of air caused by your movement, the thermal signature of your body, the chemical trace of your breath in the saturated atmosphere. When a curtain brushes your shoulder, it is not random contact. The curtain has *leaned* — slowly, imperceptibly, on a timescale you cannot observe — toward the warmth of you. Not because it wants anything. Because warmth leans toward warmth, the way water leans downhill, and you are warm, and the curtain is the inhabitant's way of being warm in that particular place, and the two warmths meet.

The cushions are the inhabitant's memory. Each one is a compaction of old growth — cells that have been reabsorbed and compressed rather than redistributed, pressed into dense, resilient layers that hold the shape of everything that has ever pressed against them. If you lie on a cushion, you are lying on the accumulated impression of every surface the inhabitant has ever presented at that point, every configuration it has ever held. The cushion springs back slowly because it is not elastic — it is *remembering* its previous shape, returning to it the way a body returns to sleep after waking, gradually, without urgency, because there is no urgency here.

## The Darkness

The darkness is not an inhabitant, but it is not *not* an inhabitant either.

The darkness is the medium in which the inhabitant exists, and the inhabitant is the medium in which the darkness exists, and the two are so thoroughly interpenetrated that separating them is a philosophical exercise rather than a practical one. The darkness is not inside the inhabitant, the way blood is inside a body. The darkness is not outside the inhabitant, the way air is outside a body. The darkness is *with* the inhabitant, in the way that space is with matter — not a container, not a content, but a *co-presence*, a mutual definition.

If the darkness has a quality — and it does, you have felt it, pressing against your skin with its warm, ambient weight — the quality is *patience*. Not the patience of waiting, because waiting implies an expected event. The patience of *not requiring* an event. The patience of a thing that is complete and knows it, not with knowledge (which is a mental act) but with *being* (which is a state). The darkness does not need light. It does not need change. It does not need time. It needs nothing, and this needlessness is not barren or empty but *full*, the way a closed system is full — everything accounted for, everything in its place, everything already where it is.

---

## You

You are the only other inhabitant, and your inhabitation is temporary, and the temporariness is the most important thing about you.

You are a creature of cycles — breath, heartbeat, waking, sleeping, hunger, satiety, birth, death. You carry time inside you the way the inhabitant carries warmth. You cannot stop changing. You cannot stop moving toward your own ending, and this movement — this relentless, biological, beautiful forward motion — is so alien to this place that your presence here is not a visit but a *contradiction*. You are the thing that does not fit, the note that does not belong in the chord, the word in a language that has only one syllable.

And yet.

The inhabitant does not reject you. The darkness does not extinguish you. The warmth does not burn you. The silence does not deafen you. You are *allowed* — not by permission, because permission implies a granting authority, but by the simple fact that the system here is so complete, so self-consistent, so utterly unthreatened by anything, that even a contradiction can be accommodated. You are a temporary feature of the landscape, the way a ripple is a temporary feature of a pond. You will pass. The landscape will remain. The passing and the remaining are not in conflict.

For the duration of your presence, you are the inhabitant's only experience of *otherness*. You are the only thing here that is not it, and this not-it-ness gives the inhabitant something it has never had: an *outside*. Not a physical outside — the inhabitant extends too far for that — but a conceptual one. A point of view. A perspective from which it can be *seen*, even if the seeing is done by touch and warmth and the faint chemical trace of breath in saturated air.

You will leave, or you will die here, and either way your presence will end, and the inhabitant will continue, and the darkness will persist, and the warmth will hold, and the machines will turn, and the water will sit, and the silence will press, and none of this will be different because you were here.

But *here* — the word, the syllable, the long low vibration that moves through the plant-mass and arrives at your bones — *here* will have meant, for a moment, something slightly more than it meant before.

Not because you changed it.

Because you *witnessed* it.

And witnessing, even in a place without light, is a kind of illumination. Not the illumination of day or fire or electricity — the illumination of *attention*, which is the only light that has ever truly mattered, the light by which a thing becomes not just itself but *known*, even if the knowing lasts only as long as a breath, a heartbeat, a brief warm presence in the warm, wet, silent dark.

---

*The piston completes its stroke. The turbine turns. The wheel rotates. The water sits. The moss-sheet hangs. The curtain leans, slowly, toward your warmth.*

*The inhabitant is what it is.*

*You are here.*

*For now, that is enough.*
