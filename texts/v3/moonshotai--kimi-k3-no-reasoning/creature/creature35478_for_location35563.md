The inhabitants of Anvil-Throat have evolved into something between blacksmith and lichen—organisms that do not merely endure the plateau’s birth pangs but have metabolized them. They are a people of the threshold, existing in the interstitial space where iron is still soft enough to remember being magma.

Their bodies bear the first modifications: **dermal chromatophores** adapted from the metal-eating mosses they cultivate, allowing them to photosynthesize the infrared bleed of the cooling Foundries. In the perpetual dusk, their skin carries a faint, rusty luminescence—patches of oxidized red and deep basalt blue that pulse with their heartbeat. They are cool to the touch, having lowered their core temperature to match the ambient chill, reducing the thermal shock when moving between the freezing scree slopes and the superheated steam vents.

To survive the **Soft Quarters**, they have developed **proprioceptive tethers**—extra vestibular organs derived from the vibration-sensitive crustaceans of the lower fissures. These appear as delicate, fan-like cartilages behind the ears, trembling constantly to sample gravitational variance. When gravity doubles, they do not fall; they fold, their bones containing honeycomb structures of bio-iron that flex rather than snap. When gravity halves, they anchor themselves using iron-spun fibers secreted from their palms, threading themselves to the architecture like spiders in slow motion.

Their sensory apparatus is dominated by **seismic audition**. They do not merely hear the mountain’s groaning; they taste it, smell it, feel it through the soles of their feet. Through bone conduction, they parse the difference between a tectonic adjustment (safe, structural) and a pressure spike in the magma veins (dangerous, explosive). They speak in a language of **subsonic clicks and percussive throat-singing**, using the iron plates of the city as resonating chambers. A conversation in Anvil-Throat is often silent to untrained ears—the words travel through the floor, vibrating up the femurs of the listeners.

They have domesticated the **armored slugs**, not as beasts of burden but as living seismographs. These massive herbivores, some reaching ten meters in length, are outfitted with copper-harnesses that translate their footsteps into harmonic maps. The inhabitants read these patterns to predict where the next extrusion will occur, where the Soft Quarters will manifest, where the temporal puddles will pool. The slugs are sacred, treated as living libraries of geological memory.

The **temporal puddles** have created a unique caste: the **Puddle-Steppers** or *Chronal Divers*. These individuals have, through accident or ritual exposure, developed a detached relationship with linear time. Their nervous systems fire in unpredictable bursts—sometimes slowing to perceive the thousand-year growth of a basalt column in a single heartbeat, sometimes accelerating to dodge a falling rivet that exists in their subjective tomorrow. They are the cartographers of the possible, using the distorted lenses of condensation to scout future neighborhoods before the ground has finished deciding to support them.

Socially, they organize into **Strata Guilds**, determined not by ancestry but by architectural elevation. The oldest families—the *First Extrusion*—live in the highest, most precarious structures, those iron vaults that have been jacked up repeatedly until they float like islands above the current bedrock. They are the historians, their bones full of old mineral dust, their memories stretching back to the first centimeter of growth. Below them, in the newer, warmer districts closer to the fresh stone, live the **Growth-Tenders**, those who inject chemical catalysts into the fissures, guiding the mountain’s dreams toward habitable shapes.

They cultivate **cushion plants** not for food but for insulation and oxygen, weaving the freeze-thaw resistant vascular systems into living blankets that line their dwellings. They farm the **electro-receptive beetles**, wearing them as living jewelry—chitin bracelets that glow when the wearer approaches a gravity variance or a rift in space.

There are also the **Iron-Born**, a minority born with excessive mineralization due to exposure to the Soft Quarters during gestation. Their skeletons are partially metallic, their blood carrying actual filings that respond to the mountain’s magnetic field. They are the best navigators of the reality breakdowns, able to walk through spatial folds without tearing, but they are also the most fragile—prone to rust, to magnetic seizures during particularly violent extrusions, to a slow, painful crystallization in old age where they gradually become indistinguishable from the statues and support columns of the city.

At night, when the cooling cracks echo like rifle shots across the plateau, the inhabitants gather in the **Resonance Halls**—natural-industrial vaults where the acoustics of the growing mountain can be played like an instrument. They press their bodies against the warm stone, feeling the bass harmonics of the magma rivers, and they sing back. Not in words, but in frequencies. It is a conversation with the geology, a lullaby for a mountain that is still learning to hold its shape, sung by soft, warm-blooded organisms who have learned to become mineral enough to survive it, yet remain fluid enough to dream.
