They live there — all of them, each one shaped by the cold and the dark and the dense, slow water.

**The Mother Root** is not a queen, though she fills the role of a queen. She is a soft, radial mass of translucent tissue, curled in on herself like a fist made of water, roughly the size of a sleeping child. She has no face, no eyes, no mouth that you could point to. Her body is folded inward, layered like the petals of a closed flower, and her skin — if it is skin — is the same material as the egg-web, thickened and concentrated and made whole. She pulses. Once every few minutes. Slowly. And when she pulses, the entire hollow vibrates with her, the egg-web trembling like a plucked string, the water carrying the frequency through your chest rather than your ears.

She is not intelligent. She does not think. But she *organizes*. Her presence imposes structure on everything around her. She produces the eggs — thousands upon thousands, each one no larger than a grain of barley, each one containing a coiled form no longer than an eyelash. She produces the mucus that forms the web, the scaffolding, the architecture of this world. She produces, through her slow metabolism, the chemical conditions that keep the water liquid when it should be ice, that keep the clay walls from closing, that keep this impossible pocket intact.

She is the anchor. The center of gravity. The reason the hollow exists.

---

**The thread-walkers** are translucent arthropods, no longer than a grain of rice, with eight articulated legs that grip the mucus threads with perfect precision. They are everywhere. They travel the web in endless circuits, tending the eggs, removing the dead or damaged ones, repairing broken threads. They are the maintenance crew. The sanitation department. The immune system.

You can feel them if you hold still — a faint trembling in the web, like the vibration of a spider's silk. They move with a frantic precision, as though they understand that the structure they are maintaining is already doomed. They do not slow down. They accelerate. They become more themselves.

Their bodies are clear as glass, and if you look closely — though there is no *looking closely* here, only inference and touch — you can see their organs working, their tiny hearts beating, their digestive tracts processing the waste of the hollow. They are alive in a way that is almost mechanical, almost automatic, but not quite. They make choices. They respond. They adapt.

---

**The glow-fish** are not fish at all. They are the free-swimming larvae of something that will never grow large enough to see. They move through the open water in slow, deliberate schools, their bodies emitting the same cold blue-green light as the ceiling filaments. They are not bright. They are *present* — a faint, moving constellation that shifts and re-forms as the school turns, casting shadows that are barely shadows at all.

They are the primary illumination of the hollow. Without them, the darkness would be absolute. With them, it is only *dim* — a darkness that suggests rather than conceals, that gives you the outline of the web and the silhouette of the Mother Root and nothing more.

The glow-fish feed on the chemical waste of the ceiling filaments. Their waste, in turn, feeds the clay-burrowers. The loop is closed. Nothing is wasted.

---

**The clay-burrowers** are soft-bodied worms that live in the walls, tunneling through the raw clay, aerating it, keeping it from compacting into stone. Their tunnels are the plumbing of the hollow — they carry water through the walls, filtering it, mineralizing it, and returning it to the main volume. Without them, the water would stagnate. With them, it circulates, slow and cold and alive.

You can feel them if you press your hand into the lower walls. The clay yields, accepts your fingerprint, and beneath the surface, the burrowers move — a faint vibration, a shifting, a sense of something alive in the walls themselves. They are the reason the hollow has not collapsed. They are the reason the clay has not settled into stone.

---

**The predators** are small — nothing here is large. A species of flat, disk-shaped hunter that glides through the egg-fields, consuming eggs at a rate that the Mother Root's production barely outpaces. They are the culling force, the selection pressure, the reason the egg-web does not simply fill the entire hollow with a solid mass of protein.

They keep the corridors open. They keep the system breathing.

They are not cruel. They are not efficient. They are simply *there*, doing what they were built to do, and their presence shapes the hollow as surely as the Mother Root's does.

---

And then there are the **ceiling filaments** — thirty strands of living thread, each one a single-celled organism grown enormous, a finger's width across, dangling downward like the fringe of a curtain. They are colorless, but they carry within them the faintest chemical luminescence, a cold blue-green shimmer that pulses once every few minutes, synchronized with the Mother Root's contractions.

They are the entire flora of the hollow. Thirty strands. They metabolize minerals from the clay. Their waste feeds the glow-fish. They are the base of the food chain, the foundation of the ecology, the reason anything here lives at all.

---

All of them — the Mother Root, the thread-walkers, the glow-fish, the burrowers, the predators, the filaments — they live in the cold, in the dark, in the silence, in a pocket of water that should be ice but is not. They live in a world the size of a lung, breathing underwater, beneath the roots of a dying tree.

They are not conscious. They do not think. But they *organize*. They adapt. They persist.

And they are enough.
