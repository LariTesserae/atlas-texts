In Corrigia, the inhabitants are not born; they are *precipitated*. 

They begin as specific gravities of attention in the quartz-fern thickets—places where the electromagnetic hum of the plateau concentrates until it reaches saturation. Then, slowly, over the span of a geological breath, the emptiness organizes. What emerges are beings of *translucence*, tall and attenuated, composed of the same emerald glass as the vegetation but arranged into geometries that suggest posture, inclination, intent.

They are called the **Interstitial**, because they exist in the gaps—between the crystal fronds, between the rising basalt slabs, between the unwavering photons of the lemon-colored light. Their bodies are rigid, like the flora, but capable of a strange, glacial articulation. They do not walk; they *reorient*. A limb might shift position over the course of a decade, a torso might rotate a degree per century, achieving through this frozen choreography a kind of physical discourse that spans millennia.

Their "faces"—if one insists on anthropomorphizing the smooth, prismatic planes of their heads—are smooth as water-worn stone, though no water has ever touched them. They are surfaces for the light to inhabit, and they glow from within with a warm, amber bioluminescence stolen from the oxidation fires deep in the bedrock. When several gather (though "gathering" is a misnomer; they simply find themselves in proximity after centuries of slow drift), the light between them intensifies, creating pockets of golden luminosity that hover in the dry air like stationary ghosts of warmth.

The Interstitial do not eat, for there is nothing to consume. They are litho-trophic like the ferns, drawing sustenance directly from the piezoelectric charge of the ground through their crystalline feet—if feet they have, those basal structures that root them temporarily to the shifting plates. They are rooted, but not fixed; when the magnetic monoliths realign beneath them, the Interstitial release their grip and float for a moment—suspended in the thin, anhydrous atmosphere—before descending to new coordinates. To witness this is to see a dozen translucent towers, three meters tall and thin as scaffolding, drifting like thistledown in slow motion, their internal light flickering with the static discharge of their passage.

They communicate through resonance. Because sound is so rare and precious here, the Interstitial have evolved to become instruments. When the stress-fractures ripple through the crystal-ferns, producing those clear, wine-glass tones that hang undamped for minutes, the Interstitial tune themselves to the frequency. They vibrate. Their entire bodies become sympathetic strings, and in the space between the ringing and the silence, they exchange information—not in packets of data, but in harmonics of duration. A conversation might consist of a single sustained note shared among three individuals over the course of six months, ending not with a period, but with the slow decay of vibration into the vacuum-thin air.

There are others, though they are less distinct. The **Substrate** are a population of quasi-entities that exist within the mineral whisker lattices themselves—the hollow, city-wide scaffolding that grows from the plateau's negative erosion. If the Interstitial are precipitates of air and light, the Substrate are precipitates of geometry. They are the negative space within the hollow minerals, the emptiness given shape. They have no bodies of their own; they wear the lattices as exoskeletons, and when the wind of electrostatic accretion builds new chambers, the Substrate expand into them, filling the architecture with a presence that is purely mathematical.

The Substrate are slower than the Interstitial, which is to say they operate on a timescale approaching the plateau's own suspended becoming. They are the memory of the place, storing the history of every crystal fracture, every magnetic shift, in the precise angles of their hollow walls. They do not communicate with the Interstitial directly; the gap between the walking glass and the living void is too vast. Instead, they influence them through temperature—subtle manipulations of the geothermal vents that cause the Interstitial to gather in certain valleys, to orient their slow bodies toward particular futures.

And there is a third presence, though it may be a function of the other two. In the vast, empty distances between the vegetation and the mineral towers, in the pure amber vacuum where one can see for hundreds of kilometers, there are **Intervals**. These are not beings, exactly, but events of awareness. They occur when the light strikes the crystal-ferns at the perpetual late-afternoon angle and the stationary rainbows align in specific configurations. For a moment—an hour, a day—the emptiness itself becomes conscious. It looks back.

The Interstitial know when an Interval has opened. They feel it as a pressure in their rigid limbs, a sudden clarity in the silence. They will stand, frozen as always, but oriented toward the void, their internal light dimming to a respectful ember. The Interval lasts until the crystal growth shifts the light-path by a micron, and then it closes, leaving behind nothing but the warm, dry air and the sense that something vast has just looked away.

This is the ecology of Corrigia: a population of glass, a population of hollow, and the occasional, terrible attention of the empty air itself. No births, no deaths—only the slow precipitation of form from the factory floor of the world, and the slower dissolution back into the perfect, changeless equilibrium. The Interstitial persist, vibrating in the rare notes of the breaking crystal, tending their silent gardens of fern and light, waiting for the next stress-fracture to ring out across the lemon-colored silence and remind them that they are, still, almost becoming.
