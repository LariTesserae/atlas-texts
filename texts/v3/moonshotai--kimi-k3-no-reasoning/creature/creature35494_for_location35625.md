In this suspended, silver-breathing city clinging to the fresh uplift of stone, **those who live there** emerge slowly, like the water itself condensing from the air. They are not one kind alone, but a layered, interdependent tapestry of presences—human, more-than-human, elemental—each shaped by the plateau's eternal dawn.

---

### **The Cloudweavers (Human Inhabitants)**
The primary dwellers are the **Cloudweavers**, a people who have grown indistinguishable from the mist they harvest. They are not named for weaving cloth, but for their sacred art of *listening*—to the whisper of cooling air, to the slow pulse of condensation on stone, to the subtle shift in humidity that tells them when to open a channel or when to let a basin rest. They move through the city like extensions of its breath: quiet, deliberate, barefoot on the cool rock, their skin carrying the faint sheen of morning dew no matter the hour.

**Appearance & Garb:**  
They are a slender, androgynous people, their skin ranging from pale quartz to deep umber, always kissed with a pearlescent sheen as if the mist lives just beneath the surface. Their hair is often worn loose or braided with strands of dried moss and silvered thread (spun from a rare, mist-fed silk-worm that nests in the highest cisterns). They wear **layered, flowing garments** woven from the fibers of the silvery-leafed *dew-bushes*: pale, breathable cloth that shimmers faintly in the light and wicks moisture toward the skin in dry spells, or releases it in heat. Their feet are bare, calloused and sensitive—able to feel the minute temperature shifts in stone that signal where water will pool.

**Social Order & Craft:**  
- **The Basin-Keepers** – Elders who tend the oldest, deepest basins. Their hands are perpetually cool and slightly translucent, their fingernails like polished river stone. They do not speak much; their presence is a silent calibration of the city’s water-heart. They are believed to *dream* the water into the right places.  
- **The Moss-Singers** – A guild of tenders who walk barefoot through the terraced gardens, humming low, harmonic tones that encourage the mosses to thicken, to spread, to hold moisture like a living sponge. Their voices carry on the damp air, and visitors say the moss *leans* toward their sound. They carry bone flutes not for music, but to *tune* the resonance of the cisterns.  
- **The Stone-Whisperers** – The builders and carvers. They do not cut the rock with force, but with patience—using copper-tipped tools to *coax* the young stone, following its grain, its memory of pressure. They leave deliberate, small imperfections in the basins, believing water finds its truest path where the stone is allowed to breathe. Their rhythmic tapping is not labor, but conversation.  
- **The Sky-Readers** – Those who climb to the highest, sharpest edges of the escarpment at first light, not to conquer the height, but to *inhale*. They read the quality of the mist—its weight, its scent, its mineral taste—and descend to whisper instructions to the Basin-Keepers. They wear amulets of hollow bird-bone that catch the wind and sing faintly when the air is ripe.

**Belief & Being:**  
The Cloudweavers do not see themselves as *owners* of the water, but as **temporary vessels**—channels in a greater cycle. Their deepest creed is *"The stone remembers the sky; we are only the pause between breaths."* They have no concept of ownership of land or water. A child’s first rite is not naming, but *being named by a basin*: an elder holds the infant’s hand in the water and speaks the sound the water makes against the stone. That sound becomes their true name, whispered only in moments of deep stillness.

### **The Mist-Born (The Subtle Inhabitants)**
Beneath and among the Cloudweavers live beings not quite spirit, not quite animal, but **elemental presences** born of the plateau’s perpetual condensation.

- **The Siltlings** – Small, semi-corporeal folk, no taller than a forearm, formed of condensed vapor and fine mineral dust. They drift in the basins’ edges, their bodies shifting like underwater smoke. They do not speak in words, but in *ripples*—disturbing the water’s surface in patterns that the Basin-Keepers interpret as omens or requests (a slow spiral = *open the channel*; a sharp jagged line = *the stone is strained*). They are neither servants nor masters, but **partners in the breath**. If a basin is neglected, the Siltlings fade, and the water grows stagnant and bitter.  
- **The Greywardens** – The large, four-legged grazers mentioned in the description are not mere livestock. They are the **living anchors** of the city’s equilibrium. Their thick grey fur absorbs the morning mist, and as they move across the outskirts, they *exhale* moisture slowly through the day, creating micro-climates that allow the hardy grasses and dew-bushes to thrive. The Cloudweavers do not herd them; they **walk with them**, following their slow, deliberate paths to know where the land is thirsting. When a Greywarden dies of old age, its body is laid in a shallow stone hollow and covered with moss. Over years, it becomes a new, fertile basin—a gift returned.  
- **The Iridescent Darters** – The quick, jewel-feathered birds are not just wildlife; they are **messengers and pollinators of the mist**. Their feathers carry static charges that attract and hold fine droplets. As they dart between buildings, they *seed* the air with moisture, encouraging condensation on roofs and channels. The Cloudweavers leave small, polished copper discs in their courtyards—not as offerings, but as *landing pads* that warm slightly in the light, drawing the birds and their watery burdens.

---

### **The Unseen Architects**
There are presences older than the Cloudweavers, felt but never seen:  
- **The Stone’s Memory** – The young, sharp rock is not inert. It *remembers* the pressure of its rising, the heat of its birth. The Stone-Whisperers believe the escarpment is still dreaming of the deep earth. In certain basins, if you press your ear to the wet stone, you can hear a low, subsonic hum—the **echo of the mountain’s ascent**. This hum guides where new basins must be placed to avoid fracturing the plateau’s fragile balance.  
- **The First Breath** – A mythic, ineffable presence said to be the original exhalation of the world that birthed this morning-place. It is not worshipped, but *acknowledged* in the silence between the drip of water and the tap of stone. The Cloudweavers say that when the last mist of the last day is captured, the First Breath will inhale, and the plateau will fold back into the earth, its purpose complete.

---

### **Daily Rhythms & Sacred Pauses**
- **The Opening (First Light):** The city stirs not with shouts, but with the soft unsealing of clay lids on cisterns, the creak of woven fiber buckets, the first Siltling-ripples. Cloudweavers gather at the central **Mirror Basin**—the largest, stillest pool—and stand in silence until the first bird’s wing-ripple disturbs the reflection. That ripple is the day’s first word.  
- **The Tuning (Mid-Morning):** Moss-Singers and Stone-Whisperers work in tandem. Channels are adjusted not by valves, but by **inserting smooth, shaped stones** to redirect flow. The sound is a slow, percussive music.  
- **The Deep Pause (High Light):** When the light turns clear blue, all labor ceases. The city sits. The water settles. The Siltlings drift to the surface and become still, their forms momentarily solid. The Greywardens lie down in the grasses. The Cloudweavers rest in the shade of their low, inward-curving roofs. This is not sleep, but **active stillness**—a communal listening to ensure the balance holds.  
- **The Giving Back (Dusk-Echo, Though There Is No Dusk):** Since time is suspended, there is no true evening. Instead, in the softening light before the next "morning," the Cloudweavers perform the **Rite of Return**: they carry small vessels of the day’s collected water to the highest, sharpest edge of the escarpment and pour it slowly over the raw stone. This is not waste. It is **feeding the mountain**, reminding the stone that the sky still remembers it. The water vanishes instantly into the young, thirsty rock.

---

### **Why They Stay**
They do not leave. They *cannot*. The plateau’s magic is a delicate membrane—step beyond the edge where the mist thins, and the air turns dry and sharp, the light harsh, the stone old and silent. To leave is to forget the sound of one’s true name. Those rare few who have wandered beyond the boundary return with cracked lips and hollow eyes, unable to bear the **weight of un-misted air**.

Thus, the Cloudweavers, the Siltlings, the Greywardens, the Darters, and the dreaming stone remain—**a single, breathing organism**. They are not inhabitants *of* the city. They *are* the city’s breath, made flesh, made feather, made ripple, made stone. Their existence is a continuous, silent hymn to the moment when sky and rock first touched, and chose to linger.
