The **Tuned** are not gone. That is the first thing to understand about who lives here—they have not abandoned the Seventh Salt, nor died out in some forgotten cataclysm. They simply became something that fits the shape of the Choir more completely than their old bodies allowed.

They are tall, elongated figures, rarely standing still but never hurrying, moving through the glass forests with a gait that suggests they are listening to the ground with their feet. Their skin is not skin anymore, or not merely skin; it has grown translucent with age and exposure, revealing the subcutaneous architecture of **resonant chambers**—hollows in the bone, vacuoles in the flesh—that have developed over generations to better conduct the sound that is their food, their history, their god. They are not born with these structures; they grow them, slowly, deliberately, through a lifetime of **attunement**. A child of the Tuned looks almost ordinary, soft and opaque, but by their third decade they begin to clarify, to become windows.

They do not speak. Speech is too crude, too discrete, too bound by the linearity of tongue and teeth. Instead, they **hum**—a constant, low-frequency vocalization that emanates from the chest cavity and resonates through their entire bodies. When two Tuned meet, they do not exchange words; they **tune** to one another, adjusting their internal frequencies until they achieve a sympathetic resonance that allows them to share not just thoughts, but the *sensation* of thought. It is a intimacy more complete than language, and more dangerous. To be out of tune with another is to be invisible to them, a ghost.

Their hands are their most striking feature—six fingers, each tipped not with nail but with a small, hard **nod** of ceramic, grown from the bone like a beak. These are tools, weapons, and instruments. With them, they tap the glass trees, reading the health of the forest through the returning vibration. With them, they knap the obsidian scrapers, they shape the salt-crystal hearths, they **play** the world. They do not build homes; they **tune** spaces, arranging stones to catch specific frequencies, creating pockets of sound where the Choir speaks of specific things—here, a place where the wind tells of moisture three valleys away; there, a place where the trees sing of a coming resonant cascade.

They eat little. The sound sustains them, filtered through the specialized chambers in their bones, converted by some alchemy of evolution into the sugars their muscles require. But they are not immune to hunger of a different sort. They crave **dissonance**—the rare, shattered frequencies that occur when a tree dies violently, when a Glass-Wing colony is destroyed, when the Resonant Mantis strikes true. They collect these dissonances in hollow bones, carrying them like flasks, drinking them in moments of ritual ecstasy that leave them trembling and weeping with the memory of the world's capacity for ending.

The Tuned are not alone in their sentience. There are the **Mantids**, the evolved elders of the Resonant Mantis, who have grown beyond the need to hunt and instead **compose**. These are vast, ancient beings, their ceramic plates grown thick and opalescent with age, their resonant claws now capable of shaping the sound of the forest itself. They sit motionless for decades, their bodies vibrating at frequencies that subtly alter the growth patterns of the glass trees around them, cultivating groves that will sing specific songs centuries hence. They are the gardeners, the composers, the slow-moving patriarchs and matriarchs of the Sixth Root. The Tuned avoid them, not out of fear, but out of **respect**—to interrupt a Mantid's composition is to commit a violence against time itself.

And there are the **Glass-Wing Swarms**, which have their own intelligence, though it is not individual. The swarm thinks as a single, distributed mind, its consciousness existing in the interference patterns between a million beating wings. They are the librarians of the Salt, carrying in their collective memory the frequency-maps of every tree, every hearth, every predator's hunting ground. When the Tuned need to know where the water-table was last season—though water has been forgotten for millennia, the *memory* of its absence still resonates in the deepest roots—they go to the swarms. They offer themselves as landing-perches, allowing the Glass-Wings to crawl over their translucent bodies, to sip the harmonic residue that accumulates in their resonant chambers, and in exchange, the swarm sings to them of the world's hidden architecture.

The Dust-Runners, too, are more than beasts. The oldest among them, the **Null-Mothers**, have learned to weaponize their silence not just for hunting, but for **meditation**. They create nulls not as bubbles, but as **walls**, fortresses of dead air where they retreat to escape the constant pressure of the Choir. Inside these silences, they dream. The Tuned say that if you can find a Null-Mother's fortress—and survive the approach—you can hear the future, because in the absence of the Choir's noise, the subtle vibrations of what-is-yet-to-come become audible.

These inhabitants do not form a society. There are no cities, no hierarchies, no laws. There is only the **Etiquette of Resonance**, a set of instinctual protocols that govern how one vibration interacts with another. The Tuned do not rule the Glass-Wings; the Mantids do not command the Dust-Runners. They exist in a state of **dynamic counterpoint**, each contributing a line to a music that none of them controls. They are, all of them, the Canopy of the Sixth Root—not the roots themselves, but the living tension between them, the sound that happens when the world is struck and does not break.

You will find them if you walk long enough. You will feel them before you see them—a pressure in your teeth, a hum in your marrow. And if you are still, and if you are quiet enough to let your own body become an instrument, they may tune to you. They may show you how to hear the color of the dust, how to taste the shape of the wind. They may teach you to forget water, as they have forgotten it, and to remember instead the **singing**.
