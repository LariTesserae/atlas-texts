The dominant inhabitant of the Hanging Ecumene is the **Fell-Blooded Weave** itself—a single, continent-spanning organism that has consumed the ancient artificial substrate and replaced it with flesh. It is less a plant than a distributed, slow-thinking entity, its consciousness spread through miles of vascular tissue and photosynthetic skin. The Weave breathes in great, humid sighs, pumping milky sap through thicket-channels that serve as arteries, and it thinks in the language of growth and decay. It has learned the architecture of the dead alloy lattice it covers, mimicking buttresses and spires in lignin and pulpy vascular tissue, creating a world that is simultaneously corpse and living body. The Weave is both environment and resident, a passive god that dreams in the slow peristalsis of bladder-trees and the unfurling of filter-fans, digesting the memory of the disc’s original purpose into nutrient-rich dust.

Upon and within this living substrate move the **grazers**—herds of heavy, six-legged mammals with broad, flat teeth designed for stripping the photosynthetic skin from the root-plains. They are patient, low-slung creatures, their bodies thick with the moisture of the perpetual twilight, moving in slow migrations across the spongy ground. The Weave regrows behind them with visible speed, creating an endless cycle of consumption and renewal. In the deeper thickets, where the light fails and the geometry of space grows untrustworthy, the **vesper-wings** navigate the gloom. These are creatures of silent, leathery flight, their wings tasting the air for chemical signals, avoiding the thorny tendrils of the **ambush-flora**—predatory, mobile offshoots of the Weave that have evolved to hunt. These ambush-flora are not separate species but extensions of the Weave’s hunger, waiting for weeks to snag a vesper-wing and draw it into digestive sacs, returning the nutrients to the greater whole.

The **human inhabitants** of the Ecumene are the Dwindling—descendants of the original crew or of later castaways, now reduced to small, insular enclaves scattered across the flesh-continent. They are pallid, amphibious people with enlarged eyes adapted to the saffron gloom, their skin often patched with symbiotic lichens that help them process the humid air. They live in hollowed-out bladder-trees or within the cavities of ancient synthetic struts, wrapped in fibers harvested from the Weave. The Dwindling do not build; they cultivate. They tend the thicket-channels, redirecting sap flows to create fresh water pools, and they harvest the filter-fans for their protein-rich dust. They are acutely aware of the **Sickness of Meaning**, the localized thinness of reality where time runs thick and space curves oddly. They navigate by ritual and rote, following paths that are safe because they have always been safe, avoiding the soft places where a drop of water might fall upward or where a short walk might consume a lifetime.

The Dwindling are not masters of this world; they are its chroniclers and its parasites. They understand that the Weave is slowly digesting the ghost of the artificial structure beneath it, and that eventually the disc will become entirely organic, entirely mind, a single organism hanging in the void. They sing to the Weave in subsonic frequencies that match its own hum, a form of communication or perhaps worship, and they leave offerings of their own dead to be absorbed into the root-flesh. They are a people without a future, living in the long twilight of a world that has forgotten its purpose, sustained by the flesh of a continent that is slowly, beautifully, digesting its own history.
