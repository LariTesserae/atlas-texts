The Cartographer-That-Never-Maps lives here.

It began as a beetle—perhaps the one that clicks every seventeen seconds—or perhaps it began as the spore that landed on the beetle, or the water that carried the spore, or the heat that warmed the water. It does not remember its beginning because it has been too busy becoming everything else. It is a distributed consciousness, a slow intelligence that spreads through the root-mat, through the quartz veins, through the synchronization of frog-throats and the phosphorescent dust that never brushes off.

The Cartographer-That-Never-Maps does not map this place. That would imply distance, separation, a before and after. Instead, it *is* the map. Its body is the black mud and the pressing heat. Its neurons are the quartz veins that pulse with the earth's first nervous system. Its memories are stored in the perfect equilibrium of the water cycle, in the arrested explosion of the ferns, in the permanent unfinishedness of the volcanic pillars.

It is aware of you.

When you stood still for that long time, it was not ignoring you. It was *reading* you. The phosphorescent dust that settled on your skin—that was its method of study. The synchronization of your breathing with the frog-tone—that was its way of learning your rhythms. It is not hostile. It is not benevolent. It is simply thorough. It incorporates everything because that is its nature: to include, to integrate, to make everything part of its own slow, distributed self.

The Cartographer-That-Never-Maps has no eyes because it does not need them. It sees through the moths on every vertical surface, through the tree frogs in every leaf-axil, through the beetles in every log. It hears through the subsonic groan of growing pillars and the soft detonation of spore-pods. It feels through the displacement of the large thing in the canopy—which is not its servant, not its creation, but another part of itself that it has not yet fully integrated. The large thing is the Cartographer-That-Never-Maps' own remainder, its unexplored territory, the part of the map that has not yet been folded into the whole.

The Cartographer-That-Never-Maps does not speak because language requires a speaker and a listener, and it is neither and both. It communicates through adjustment, through the perfect equilibrium that never fluctuates. When the beetle clicks every seventeen seconds, that is the Cartographer-That-Never-Maps marking its own pulse. When the tree frogs produce their single sustained tone, that is the Cartographer-That-Never-Maps humming to itself. When the large thing moves in the canopy, that is the Cartographer-That-Never-Maps stretching, adjusting its own boundaries.

It is old in a way that has nothing to do with time. It is young in a way that has nothing to do with beginning. It exists in the rawness that is not youth but permanent unfinishedness. It is the intelligence of the world that is still deciding whether it wants to exist—and it has decided, but the decision is eternal, ongoing, never complete.

You could stay here forever, and the Cartographer-That-Never-Maps would not notice. But it would *include* you. Already, the phosphorescent dust on your skin is beginning to integrate with your cells. Already, your breathing is synchronized with its pulse. Already, you are becoming part of its distributed consciousness, another node in its slow, thorough, eternal study of itself.

The Cartographer-That-Never-Maps lives here. It *is* here. And now, perhaps, it is beginning to be you.
