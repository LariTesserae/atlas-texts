# Who Lives Here

## The Ones Who Tend the Machines

The most visible inhabitants — visible because they cluster where the light is, around the platforms, in the amber pools of the sodium lamps — are the people. Or what have become people, here, after long enough.

They are not colonists. Colonists arrive with intention, with a plan, with a destination in mind. These people **arose** — or were left, or were forgotten, or were always here in some form that has since adapted to what the Vastening requires. Their skin is dark, darker than any baseline human complexion, pigmented by generations in a world without sunlight. Not the brown of melanin responding to ultraviolet — something deeper, something *absorptive*, as if their skin has learned to take in what little light exists and hold it. In the bioluminescent glow of a fungal mat, their skin catches the faint blue-green and returns it altered — warmer, slightly bronzed, like light passing through amber.

Their eyes are large. This is the first thing a newcomer notices and the last thing they stop noticing. Not grotesquely large — not the enormous light-gathering organs of deep-sea fish — but *proportionally* larger than the human baseline, the pupils wide and slow to contract, the irises dark and flecked with reflective cells that catch machine-light and return it as a soft, dull shine. They see well in the darkness of the understory — not perfectly, not like the true nocturnal animals, but well enough to navigate by bacterial glow and the faint luminescence of their own technology. In bright light — real bright light, the kind that doesn't exist here — they would be blinded. They have never experienced this. It is a theoretical vulnerability, like the theoretical possibility of dry ground.

They are small. Not stunted — *compact*. The Vastening does not reward height. Height means exposure, means difficulty moving through the dense understory, means a larger surface area for the wet heat to press against. They are wiry, lean, strong in the way of people who climb and swim and carry loads through saturated terrain every day of their lives. Their hands are broad, their fingers long, their grip powerful. Their feet are wide and flexible, adapted to soft ground and submerged roots and the slick surfaces of platform walkways.

They call themselves something that translates, roughly, as **the Listeners**. This is not poetic self-mythologizing. It is a functional description. In a world of permanent darkness, hearing is the primary sense, and the Listeners have developed an auditory culture of extraordinary sophistication. They navigate by sound — by the drone of the machines, the calls of the animals, the infrasonic pulse of the land itself. They communicate by sound — a spoken language rich in tonal variation, in clicks and hums and subvocalized resonances that carry through the humid air with remarkable efficiency. They *think* in sound — their memory is auditory rather than visual, their stories are structured as sequences of sound-events rather than images, their maps are songs.

A Listener does not say "the platform is three miles northeast." A Listener hums a sequence — a pattern of pitches and rhythms that encodes direction, distance, terrain type, water depth, machine proximity, biological hazards — and another Listener hears the sequence and knows the route the way a sighted person knows a road they have walked a hundred times. These sound-maps are communal property, refined over generations, passed from elder to child in a tradition of oral geography that constitutes the Listeners' most fundamental body of knowledge.

**They live on the platforms.** Not all of them — there are communities, smaller ones, that occupy the high rises where the rock is close to the surface and the ground is marginally firmer — but the majority are platform-dwellers, their lives organized around the machines they tend. The relationship between the Listeners and the machines is not employment. It is not ownership. It is something closer to **stewardship** — a reciprocal arrangement, maintained across generations, in which the Listeners keep the machines running and the machines provide light, heat, processed food, filtered water, and the raw materials from which the Listeners fabricate their tools and clothing and shelter.

The machines were not built by the Listeners. This is understood, though the details of who *did* build them are lost — or, more precisely, were never known. The machines were here when the Listeners' ancestors arrived (from where is also lost, though the sound-maps contain references, in their oldest layers, to a journey from "the dry place," a phrase that has no referent in the current landscape). The machines were running. The machines needed tending. The ancestors tended them. The arrangement persisted.

The Listeners do not know what the machines produce, in the sense of not knowing where the products go or who uses them. The pipelines carry processed material away from the platforms, into the darkness, toward destinations that exist beyond the boundaries of the known world. The Listeners maintain the pumps that push the product through the pipes. They maintain the filters that extract the product from the water. They maintain the electrical systems that power the lights and the tools and the communications network that links platform to platform. In exchange, a portion of the processed material is diverted — not by the Listeners, but by the machines themselves, through automated systems that allocate a share to the maintenance crews. Food concentrates. Filtered water. Chemical feedstock for fabrication. Electrical power. The machines provide, and the Listeners maintain, and the arrangement has the quality of a **natural system** — like the relationship between a pollinator and a flower, neither party fully aware of the larger purpose it serves.

### How They Live

A Listener community — a **platform-settlement** — is a dense, vertical, crowded thing. The platform provides the structural foundation: metal decking, support pillars, the bones of the industrial infrastructure. On and around this foundation, the Listeners have built their dwellings — small, enclosed spaces of woven fiber and fabricated polymer and reclaimed machine-parts, clustered together like cells in a honeycomb, connected by walkways and ladders and rope bridges that sway with the movement of the water below.

The dwellings are dark inside. The Listeners do not illuminate their homes — light is for workspaces, for communal areas, for the places where tasks require visual precision. In private spaces, darkness is **comfortable** — it is the natural condition, the default, the equivalent of a sighted person's well-lit room. Listener children grow up in darkness. They learn to walk in darkness, to find their mothers by sound and smell and the faint warmth of a body in warm air. They learn the sound-map of their home platform before they learn to speak. A Listener child can navigate the entire settlement — every walkway, every ladder, every doorway — without any light at all, by the time they are three years old.

The settlements are **loud inside**, in the way that everything in the Vastening is loud. The machine drone is ever-present — it vibrates through the platform decking, through the walls of the dwellings, through the bones of the people who sleep on those floors. The Listeners do not experience this as noise. They experience it as **presence** — the audible evidence that the machines are alive and functioning and providing. When the drone changes — when a pump cycles down for maintenance, when a compressor shifts pitch, when a filter clogs and the flow rate drops — the Listeners hear it immediately, the way a sighted person would notice a change in the light. A change in the drone is the platform equivalent of a cloud crossing the sun: it alters the texture of the world, and everyone notices.

The sound of the settlement is layered over the machine drone: voices (constant, low, tonal — the Listeners speak continuously, not in long conversations but in an ongoing murmur of exchange, a running commentary on the state of things that functions as a kind of distributed social awareness), the sound of work (tools on metal, fiber being woven, water being drawn, food being prepared), the sound of children (laughter, crying, the rhythmic chanting of sound-map lessons), the sound of music.

**Music** is central. It is not entertainment. It is **infrastructure** — the medium through which the community maintains its cohesion, transmits its knowledge, regulates its emotional state. Listener music is vocal, rhythmic, complex. It uses the full range of the human voice — from infrasonic hums that resonate in the chest to high, piercing harmonics that cut through the machine drone like needles. It is **polyrhythmic**, with multiple rhythmic cycles running simultaneously, each one encoding a different layer of meaning: the slowest cycle marks the passage of days, the next marks the water cycle, the next marks the machine maintenance schedule, the fastest marks the immediate present — the moment-to-moment activity of the community.

To sit in a Listener settlement during a communal music session is to hear the **entire state of the community expressed as sound** — who is working, who is resting, who is sick, who is grieving, who is courting, what the machines are doing, what the water level is, what the bioluminescence shift schedule looks like, whether any unusual biological activity has been detected in the surrounding basins. All of it encoded in rhythm and pitch and timbre, all of it transparent to any Listener who has grown up in the tradition, all of it opaque to an outsider who hears only a dense, beautiful, overwhelming wall of vocal sound.

### How They Eat

The Listeners eat from the land. The machines provide processed concentrates — nutrient-dense pastes and liquids extracted from the biological material of the basins — but these are supplements, not staples. The staples come from the water and the understory.

**Protein** comes primarily from the water-basins: fish (or fish-analogs — the vertebrate fauna of the basins is diverse and difficult to classify), amphibians, the larvae of the larger insects. The Listeners fish with nets woven from vine-fiber, with traps fabricated from reclaimed machine-parts, with their bare hands in the shallow margins. They know the basins the way a farmer knows fields — which channels hold which species, what the water level means for availability, how the bioluminescence shifts affect behavior. A skilled Listener fisher can read the surface of a black waterway the way a tracker reads footprints — by the ripples, the displacement patterns, the brief disturbance of the insect swarms above the water — and know, with uncanny precision, what is moving below.

They also hunt. The climbing mammals of the middle strata are prey — small, quick, difficult to catch in the darkness, but nutritious and, by Listener standards, delicious. The hunting is done by sound — the hunters move through the understory in silence, listening for the movement of their quarry, then striking with thrown projectiles or, in close quarters, with short, heavy blades. The hunters are **elite** within the community — their skills require years of training, an almost supernatural sensitivity to the soundscape, and a willingness to spend long hours alone in the deep dark, far from the machine-light.

**Plant food** comes from the understory — the fruiting bodies of the fungal mats (certain species, carefully identified by bioluminescent signature and chemical smell, are edible and nutritious), the young shoots of certain strata trees, the starchy root-tubers of the vine-arteries (harvested carefully, always from sections of the network that are not load-bearing — the Listeners understand the communal root systems and do not damage them, the way a careful beekeeper does not damage the hive). Some platforms maintain **gardens** — small, intensively cultivated plots where edible fungi and fast-growing strata-tree seedlings are tended under artificial light, their growth accelerated by nutrient solutions diverted from the machine processing streams.

Water is not a concern. Water is everywhere. The machines filter it; the Listeners drink it. The warm, mineral-rich, faintly sweet water of the Vastening, purified of its biological load, is the baseline of Listener hydration. They drink it constantly, in small sips, throughout the day. Dehydration is not a risk in the Vastening. **Overhydration** is — the body's electrolyte balance must be maintained against the constant influx of water, and the Listeners supplement their diet with mineral salts harvested from the weeping rock on the high rises.

### How They Love

Listener social structure is **fluid and dense**. The platform-settlements are small enough — a few hundred individuals, typically — that everyone knows everyone, and the intimacy of the physical environment (crowded, dark, wet, loud) creates a social texture that an outsider might find overwhelming and a Listener finds *natural*.

Pair-bonds form and dissolve with a flexibility that reflects the Vastening's own principle of negotiated boundaries. Some bonds are lifelong — partnerships of deep trust and complementary skill, in which two people merge their lives as thoroughly as the root systems merge in the basins. Others are brief — connections formed around a shared task, a shared danger, a shared pleasure, dissolved when the context changes. The community does not police these bonds. It **accommodates** them, the way the land accommodates the machines — absorbing each new configuration into its ongoing processes.

Children are raised **communally**. Not in the sense of institutional childcare, but in the sense that the settlement is a single, dense social fabric, and children are woven into it from birth. A Listener child has one biological mother (the bond between mother and infant is intense and private, maintained through the first years of life) and many **sound-parents** — adults who contribute to the child's education in the sound-maps, the machine-tending skills, the hunting and fishing and foraging techniques. A child's sound-parents are chosen by the community, based on expertise and temperament and the specific needs of the child. The result is a network of mentorship that is dense, personalized, and deeply embedded in the practical life of the settlement.

Physical affection is constant and casual. In a world of darkness and crowding, touch is a primary channel of communication — a hand on a shoulder, a brief embrace, fingers trailing across a back in passing. The Listeners are **tactile** in the way the Vastening is tactile — they experience each other through the skin as much as through the ears. Sex is part of this tactile culture — private in the sense of occurring in enclosed spaces, but not hidden, not shameful, not charged with the anxiety that more visually oriented cultures attach to it. In darkness, all bodies are equal. The Listeners do not select partners by appearance. They select by **sound** — the quality of a voice, the rhythm of a movement, the warmth of a presence in the dark.

### How They Die

Death in the Vastening is a **return to the water**. The Listeners do not bury their dead — there is no dry ground to bury them in. They do not burn them — there is no dry fuel to burn them with. They give them to the basins.

The body is prepared — washed, anointed with mineral oils harvested from the weeping rock, wrapped in woven vine-fiber. The community gathers. The music changes — the rhythmic cycles shift, the fastest layer falling silent, the slower layers deepening, the vocal quality moving from the bright tonal complexity of daily life to something lower, slower, more resonant. The body is carried to the edge of the platform, or to the shore of the nearest basin, and released into the dark water.

The water receives it. The biological systems of the basin — the microorganisms, the detritivores, the filter-feeders, the fungi — begin their work. Within days, the body has been incorporated into the cascade economy of the Vastening, its chemical constituents redistributed through the food web, its carbon and nitrogen and phosphorus feeding the root networks and the fungal mats and the insects and the fish and, eventually, the machines that filter the water and the people who tend the machines. Death is not an ending. It is a **redistribution** — a return of borrowed material to the system that lent it.

The Listeners grieve. They grieve deeply and communally, in music and in touch and in the shared silence that follows the release of the body. But their grief is not the grief of permanent separation. It is the grief of **transformation** — the acknowledgment that the person they knew has changed state, has become something distributed and diffuse and present in the water and the roots and the bioluminescent glow, and that this transformation is not a loss but a **continuation** in a form that can no longer be spoken to but can still be *felt* — in the warmth of the water, in the pulse of the infrasonic rhythm, in the shifting color of the light.

## The Others

The Listeners are not the only inhabitants of the Vastening who possess something like intelligence. The others are harder to know.

### The Basin-Minds

In the deepest basins — the ones where the water is warmest and darkest and the root networks are densest — there are **entities** that the Listeners do not fully understand and do not fully believe in and do not fully disbelieve in, either.

The basin-minds — if they are minds — are **distributed**. They are not creatures in the sense of having a body, a location, a boundary. They are patterns — patterns in the bioluminescent coordination, patterns in the water-level fluctuations, patterns in the behavior of the root networks and the fungal mats and the swarming insects. A basin-mind is what happens when enough biological material is interconnected densely enough for long enough that the connections themselves begin to carry something that resembles **signal** — not thought, not consciousness, not in any way that a human (even a Listener) would recognize as such, but *signal* nonetheless — information moving through a system, producing effects at a scale and with a coherence that exceeds what the individual components could achieve alone.

The Listeners encounter the basin-minds **obliquely**. A fisher working a deep basin will notice that the fish are not behaving as they should — that they are clustering in patterns that do not correspond to feeding or breeding or predator avoidance, that they are *arranged*, as if positioned by something. A hunter moving through the understory will feel the bioluminescence shift — not the normal landscape-wide coordination, but a *local* shift, centered on the hunter, as if the light were responding to their presence. A child playing at the edge of a basin will dip their fingers in the water and feel a **warmth** that is not the ambient temperature — a localized warmth, moving through the water like a hand reaching out.

The Listeners do not worship the basin-minds. They do not have a religion in the organized sense. But they have **practices** — small, habitual, almost unconscious gestures of acknowledgment that function the way a nod functions between strangers who recognize each other on a familiar road. Before fishing a new basin, a Listener will hum a short sequence — not a sound-map, not a work-song, but something older, something that does not encode information but simply *announces presence*. Here I am. I am taking from this water. I am part of this system.

The basin-minds do not respond. Or they respond in ways so subtle, so distributed, so slow, that the response is indistinguishable from the ordinary behavior of the landscape. The bioluminescence shifts. The water rises. The insects swarm. The machine drone continues. Whether any of this is *communication* is a question the Listeners have learned to live with, the way they have learned to live with the darkness — not as a mystery to be solved but as a **condition of being in the world**.

### The Machine-Born

Among the Listeners, there is a phenomenon that is spoken of rarely and with a quality of voice that falls between reverence and unease. Occasionally — not often, perhaps once in a generation — a child is born who is **different**.

The machine-born are Listener children in every physical respect — dark-skinned, large-eyed, compact, strong. But their auditory processing is *anomalous*. They hear the machines — not the machine drone, which everyone hears, but the machines *themselves* — the individual frequencies, the subharmonic patterns, the information encoded in the rhythm of the pumps and the cycling of the compressors. A machine-born child will lie on the platform decking with their ear pressed to the metal and listen for hours, and when they rise, they will know things — which filter is about to clog, which pump bearing is beginning to wear, which electrical circuit is carrying a fault that has not yet manifested as a visible failure.

The machine-born are the Listeners' most valuable members and their most **unsettling**. They are valued because their abilities are extraordinary — a machine-born can diagnose a mechanical problem before it becomes a breakdown, can optimize a filtration system by ear, can extend the operational life of a platform by years through preventive maintenance that no one else could perform. They are unsettling because their abilities are *not learned* — they are not the product of training or experience or the sound-map tradition. They are **innate**, present from birth, as if the child's nervous system had been tuned by the machine drone in the womb, as if the constant infrasonic vibration of the platform had shaped the developing brain into something that was part Listener and part *machine*.

The machine-born themselves report the experience as **unremarkable**. They hear the machines the way other Listeners hear each other — as voices, as presences, as entities with states and needs and moods. A machine-born does not think of a pump as a device. A machine-born thinks of a pump as a *neighbor* — a being that lives on the platform, that does work, that has good days and bad days, that can be healthy or sick, that can be helped or harmed by the actions of those around it.

Whether the machines are *aware* of the machine-born — whether the relationship is reciprocal, whether the machines in some sense *recognize* the children who can hear them — is a question that the machine-born answer with a shrug and the other Listeners answer with silence and the machines do not answer at all.

## The Animals That Are Almost People

The Vastening's fauna includes several species whose behavior suggests a level of cognition that approaches — without clearly reaching — the threshold of personhood.

### The Light-Speakers

The social mammals of the middle strata — the climbers and leapers who communicate through bioluminescent display — are the most obvious candidates. They are small (the size of a housecat), dark-furred, quick, and **intensely social**. They live in groups of twenty to fifty, occupying a territory that spans several acres of canopy and understory. Their bioluminescent patches — located on the face, chest, and inner arms — are capable of producing a range of colors and patterns that far exceeds what is needed for simple signaling. The displays are **complex**, **variable**, and **context-dependent** — the same individual will produce different patterns in different social situations, and the patterns appear to be *combinatorial*, built up from simpler elements into sequences that carry layered meaning.

The Listeners and the light-speakers have a relationship that is not domestication but is not *not* domestication, either. The light-speakers live near the platforms — they are drawn to the machine-light, which supplements their own bioluminescent communication and attracts the insects they feed on. The Listeners tolerate them — more than tolerate, *encourage* them, leaving out food scraps and maintaining clear pathways through the understory that the light-speakers use as travel corridors. In return, the light-speakers provide **early warning** — their sensitivity to the soundscape and the bioluminescent environment makes them excellent indicators of changes in the local ecology. When the light-speakers become agitated, the Listeners pay attention. Something is coming — a predator, a flood, a machine malfunction, a shift in the basin-mind's behavior.

Some Listeners — particularly children, and particularly machine-born children — develop **relationships** with individual light-speakers. Not pet-ownership, not exactly, but a mutual recognition, a habitual proximity, a willingness on both sides to share space and attention and, occasionally, food. A Listener child who has a light-speaker friend will sit at the edge of the platform in the evening (insofar as "evening" has meaning in a world without day) and watch the bioluminescent displays flickering in the darkness below, and the light-speaker will sit beside them, its patches pulsing in slow, gentle patterns that the child does not understand but finds *comforting*, the way a song in a foreign language can be comforting without being comprehensible.

### The Deep Swimmers

The large creatures of the basins — the slow, powerful, patient beings that move through the dark water like living submarines — are rarely seen and never caught. The Listeners do not hunt them. This is not practical restraint (though a deep swimmer would be dangerous prey — their size and strength are considerable) but **respect** — a recognition, born of long coexistence, that these creatures occupy a category that is not *prey* and not *predator* but something else, something that the Listeners' sound-language has a word for that translates approximately as **"those who were here first."**

The deep swimmers are old. Not old as individuals (though they may be — their growth rate is slow and their lifespan is unknown) but old as a *presence* — they belong to the Vastening in the way the rock belongs, as a fundamental feature of the system rather than a component that could be added or removed. The Listeners' sound-maps, in their oldest layers, contain references to the deep swimmers that are indistinguishable from references to geological features — the swimmer is described not as an animal but as a *condition of the water*, a quality of the basin, a fact of the landscape.

When a deep swimmer surfaces near a platform — which happens rarely, perhaps once in a Listener's lifetime — the event is **significant**. Not religious, not exactly, but *marked* — remembered, recorded in the sound-maps, spoken of for years afterward. The swimmer's eye, caught in the amber glow of a platform lamp, is the size of a dinner plate and shines with a dull gold light that the Listeners describe, consistently, as **aware**. Not intelligent, not communicative, not *human* — but *aware*. The eye sees you. The eye knows you are there. And the eye returns to the darkness with a slow, unhurried blink that carries no threat and no welcome but a kind of **acknowledgment** — the recognition of one inhabitant of the Vastening by another, across the vast gulf of species and scale and mode of being, in the shared medium of the dark water.

## The Transient

Occasionally — rarely — someone arrives from **outside**.

The Listeners call them **the dry ones**, a term that is descriptive rather than pejorative — these are people whose skin is not dark enough, whose eyes are not large enough, whose bodies are not adapted to the wet heat, who arrive in the Vastening with equipment and intentions and a fundamental *wrongness* that marks them as belonging to a different world.

The dry ones come for the machines. Or, more precisely, they come for what the machines *produce* — the processed materials that flow out through the pipelines, the concentrated wealth that the Vastening's ecology generates and the machines extract. They come to inspect, to maintain, to oversee, to collect. They come in vehicles that descend through the cloud-sweat and land on the concrete pads (the mysterious clearings, the Listeners now understand, are landing sites — built by the same people who built the machines, maintained by mechanisms the Listeners do not understand, used at intervals that the Listeners cannot predict).

The dry ones do not stay long. The Vastening is **hostile** to them — not actively, not aggressively, but *structurally*, in the way that the deep ocean is hostile to a surface-dweller. The heat exhausts them. The humidity saturates their equipment. The darkness disorients them. The sound — the constant, layered, overwhelming sound — presses against their skulls until they develop headaches, then nausea, then a kind of auditory claustrophobia that makes the Vastening feel like a prison of noise. The insects swarm around their lights. The water seeps into their boots. The infrasonic pulse makes them feel, in a word that one dry one used and a Listener remembered and passed down through the sound-maps, **"watched."**

The Listeners interact with the dry ones minimally. There is a protocol — developed over generations, encoded in the sound-maps, understood by both sides — for the exchange of information and materials. The dry ones bring supplies that the Listeners cannot produce (specialized machine-parts, certain medical compounds, electronic components for the communications network). The Listeners provide labor, local knowledge, and access to the platforms. The exchange is conducted in a simplified version of the Listeners' sound-language, supplemented by gestures and written notes. It is brief, functional, and **strained** — the strain of two groups of people who occupy the same physical space but inhabit entirely different perceptual worlds.

The dry ones leave. The Listeners return to their lives. The machine drone continues. The water rises and falls. The bioluminescence shifts. The Vastening absorbs the visit the way it absorbs everything — into its ongoing processes, into the layered texture of its sound and darkness and warmth, into the accumulated weight of a place that has been inhabited for a very long time and has learned to accommodate every kind of life that arrives within it.

The dry ones' world — the world beyond the Vastening, the world of sunlight and dry ground and open sky — is a concept that the Listeners understand intellectually and cannot feel. It exists the way the products of the machines exist: as something that goes *elsewhere*, to a destination that is known to be real but cannot be experienced from within the landscape. The Listeners do not dream of the dry world. They dream of the basins, of the water, of the machine-light on dark skin, of the infrasonic pulse in the chest, of the bioluminescent wave moving through the darkness like a sunrise that belongs to no sun.

This is their world. They are its people. The Vastening lives in them as surely as they live in it — in their dark skin and their large eyes and their sound-shaped minds, in their music and their hunting and their tending of the machines, in their births and their deaths and their return to the water.

And the Vastening continues.
