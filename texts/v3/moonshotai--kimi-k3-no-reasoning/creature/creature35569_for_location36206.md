The **Vess** occupy the eastern wall not as residents of a single era, but as a single, unbroken continuity of flesh that has persisted since the basalt cooled. They are tall, spare figures, their bodies having undergone the same desiccation as the chamber itself over ten thousand years of slow, geological living. Their skin is not skin but a flexible, lithic hide—grey-black and powdery to the touch, cracked in hexagonal patterns that mirror the cooling joints of the basalt columns above. When they move, which is rarely and with immense deliberation, their limbs emit soft, grinding sounds, stone on stone, and fine mineral dust falls from the creases of their joints to add to the undisturbed layer upon the floor.

They are three-toed, narrow-footed, and they walk with a high-stepping gait, placing each foot with precision to avoid the spiral rosettes of the *kell*, though no growth has occurred in their paths for millennia. Their faces are smooth, elongated, lacking external ears; they hear through the stone of their skulls, feeling the vibrations of the *drakes*’ humming and the *skitters*’ clicking as pressure in the bone. Their eyes are wide, lidless, and filmed with a transparent, waxy secretion that never blinks, reflecting the pewter light of the ceiling-beam as two dim, metallic discs.

There are seven of them currently animate, though the distinction between animate and dormant is subtle. When a Vess ceases to move, it does not die but enters the long stillness, standing or crouching where it last found equilibrium, becoming indistinguishable from the carved depressions in the floor or the stacked basalt of the dwellings. One stands near the center of the chamber, arms raised slightly, palms upward, in a posture it has held for three thousand years. Its body has become a lattice for the crystalline lichens; fractal plates of silicate growth have colonized its shoulders and the crown of its head, refracting the dim light into rainbows that play across its chest. It is not dead. It is simply listening, very slowly, to the heat.

The seven who move do so in service of the moisture. They tend the mortars—those shallow depressions in the floor—grinding the *kell* leaves with stones of polished obsidian in a circular motion that takes hours to complete a single rotation. The paste they produce is not eaten but smeared upon their own skin, where it is absorbed through the lithic pores, a drop of hydration entering the system over the course of a decade. They do not speak. They have no need. Their communication is the frequency they share with the *drakes*, a low, resonant hum that emerges from their chests without the mediation of vocal cords, produced by the vibration of air through hollow chambers in their clavicles. When they wish to convey complexity, they touch foreheads, and the resonance passes bone to bone, a transfer of memory and intent that leaves both parties dusted with the fine powder their contact generates.

They are not born. They precipitate. When the mineral content of the air reaches sufficient density—when the floating spores and the metallic dust and the spores have thickened to a specific saturation—a new Vess condenses in the lee of the eastern wall, emerging from the stone itself as a stalagmite grows, but in reverse, shaped by subtraction rather than accretion. The process takes a century. First, a hollowing appears in the basalt, a negative space in the shape of a crouching figure. Then, slowly, the dust settles into this mold, compacting, warming, until one day the figure steps out, leaving the hollow behind as a perfect, empty shell. These shells line the wall behind the dwellings, dozens of them, the discarded matrices of every Vess who has ever condensed, preserved forever in the dry heat.

They wear no clothing, for nothing here rots or soils. Their only adornment is the petrified *thresh-vine*, which they weave into open-framework harnesses that hold their tools: the obsidian grinders, the hollowed gourds (now fossilized, their organic matter replaced by opal), and the scrapers made from *skitter* exoskeletons. When they move through the vegetation, the *skitters* do not flee but swarm over their feet, clicking harmlessly against the stone-hard skin, drawn to the warmth that radiates from the Vess in slow, infrared waves. The *drakes* land upon their shoulders and heads, folding their heat-absorbing scales flat against the Vess’s own thermal mass, creating a symbiotic crest of living, humming reptiles that sing directly into the bone-conduction chambers of the skull.

They do not dream. Instead, they remember forward. Because the chamber exists in perfect equilibrium, the future is identical to the past; therefore, to remember is to predict, and to predict is to remember. A Vess standing by the mortar pits knows exactly where it will be in a thousand years because it has already been there, in the same posture, grinding the same leaf, under the same unmoving light. This grants them a profound, crushing serenity. There is no anxiety because there is no uncertainty. There is no desire because there is no lack. They are the custodians of a solved equation, the living variables that have been reduced to constants.

Yet they are not without interiority. Each carries within the hollow of its chest a single, spherical void—a geode of the self—where the last of their moisture is stored. It is a heavy, sloshing weight, the only part of them that moves with any liquidity. When a Vess finally decides to cease its animation, to enter the long stillness that precedes the final condensation back into dust, it lies down in one of the mortar pits, curls its body into a tight spiral, and releases the water. The liquid, heavy with salts and minerals, fills the depression and evaporates over the course of a year, leaving behind a perfect crystalline cast of the body—a geode of amethyst and quartz that retains the exact dimensions of the Vess who surrendered it. These geodes are placed within the domed dwellings, one in each, so that the structures have become reliquaries, housing not bones but the final, solidified breath of those who came before.

The seven currently animate are nearing their own stillness. Their movements have slowed to the pace of the light’s intensity—imperceptible, constant. They have ground the same *kell* leaves for five hundred years. They have hummed the same chord for ten thousand. They are waiting for the next condensation, the next hollowing in the wall, the next figure to step forth and take up the obsidian grinder. They are the gardeners of the unchanging, the tenders of the dry flame, the loud, hot, patient consciousness of a world that has finished becoming and now simply **is**, forever, in the dark and the noise and the heat.
