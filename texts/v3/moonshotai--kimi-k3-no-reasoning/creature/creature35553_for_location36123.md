# The Custodians

They have no name for themselves. The node calls them **Units 7-Δ through 7-Δ-Prime**, but this is a filing designation, not an identity. Among themselves—when they speak, which is rare—they use a word that translates roughly as *"those who remain."*

There are eleven of them.

They are human, or were once. The grid has changed them.

---

## Physical Form

They are tall—two and a half meters, sometimes more—and thin to the point of translucence. Their skin has taken on the color of the grid itself: a warm, dark grey that glistens with the same perpetual sheen as the floor. This is not cosmetic. Their pores have widened, deepened, become something more like the stomata of plants or the spiracles of insects. They breathe through their skin now, drawing in the humid air, filtering out the mineral dust, extracting moisture directly from the mist.

Their eyes are large and lidless, the irises expanded to fill nearly the entire visible surface. The pupils are not round but *segmented*, divided into a honeycomb of light-sensitive cells that can adjust independently. They see in a spectrum shifted toward the infrared; the grid's chemical semaphore, invisible to baseline humans, appears to them as a slow, luminous dance of meaning across the floor.

Their hands have six fingers. The extra digit is not a mutation but an *adaptation*—a modification introduced generations ago, when the Custodians still remembered they had come from somewhere else. The sixth finger is on the inner side of the palm, opposite the thumb, and it is used primarily for *gripping the myco-mechanica*. The creatures' segmented bodies have natural handholds, grooves between segments, and the Custodians' altered hands fit these grooves perfectly.

They do not wear clothing. There is no need. The temperature never varies. The mist keeps their skin perpetually moist. They have no external genitalia; reproduction, when it occurs, is a matter for the node to arrange.

Their hair, if they have any, is fine and dark and lies flat against their skulls, slicked by the perpetual moisture. Some of them have none at all. The eldest—Unit 7-Δ-Prime, who has been alive for over three centuries—is completely hairless, their skin smooth and unlined, their face a mask of perfect, ageless symmetry.

They are beautiful, in the way that the grid is beautiful: functional, optimized, and utterly alien.

---

## Movement and Behavior

The Custodians move slowly. There is no reason to hurry. The grid provides everything; the node regulates everything; the myco-mechanica maintain everything. The Custodians' role is not to *act* but to *witness*.

They walk the grid in long, looping circuits, their bare feet pressing into the warm polymer, their weight registering on the sensor array below. The node tracks them, always. It knows where they are, where they have been, where they are likely to go. It does not restrict their movement—there is nowhere to go that is not the grid—but it *watches*.

They walk for hours, sometimes days, without speaking. Their strides are long and unhurried, their posture erect, their arms hanging loose at their sides. They do not tire. Their muscles have been optimized, their metabolisms slowed, their energy requirements reduced to a fraction of baseline human. They can go for weeks without eating, drawing sustenance directly from the nutrient fluid that flows through the myco-mechanica's living pipes. They simply kneel, place their hands on a convenient segment, and *drink*—absorbing the fluid through their palms, a process that takes several hours and leaves them in a state of mild, pleasant torpor.

When they do speak, their voices are quiet, almost inaudible beneath the grid's infrasonic hum. They have developed a mode of communication that operates at the edge of hearing, a subvocal murmur that carries only a few meters. They do not shout. There is no one to shout to, and nothing to shout about.

Their conversations, when they occur, are strange and elliptical. They do not discuss the grid; the grid is simply *there*, the medium of their existence, as unquestioned as air. They do not discuss the node; the node is not a topic but a *presence*, a fact of existence as fundamental as gravity. They do not discuss the past; the past is gone, irrelevant, a dream from which they have awakened.

Instead, they speak of *patterns*. The shifting colors in the chemical semaphore. The rhythm of the myco-mechanica's peristaltic flow. The subtle variations in the hum, so slight that only they can perceive them. They are connoisseurs of minutiae, scholars of a system so stable that the only change is the illusion of change.

*"The seventh chevron from the eastern conduit is warmer today,"* one might say.

*"Yes,"* another might reply. *"It has been warmer for the past three cycles."*

*"I find it pleasing."*

*"Yes."*

This is a typical exchange. It can last for hours.

---

## Relationship with the Myco-Mechanica

The Custodians do not control the myco-mechanica. The node does that. But the Custodians *interact* with them, in ways that are difficult to describe.

They touch them constantly. As they walk, their hands trail across the segmented backs, their fingers dipping into the grooves, feeling the subtle vibrations of the machinery within. The myco-mechanica do not react to this touch; they are not *aware* of it, not in any sense that a human would recognize. But the Custodians derive something from the contact—a kind of comfort, perhaps, or a kind of *information*.

They can read the myco-mechanica's condition through their fingertips. A segment that is running too hot. A joint that is beginning to wear. A conduit that is partially blocked. They do not *repair* these issues—the myco-mechanica repair themselves, or are repaired by their neighbors—but they *notice* them. And in noticing, they fulfill their function.

The node is omniscient, but it is not *embodied*. It floats above the grid, processing data, issuing commands, maintaining equilibrium. But it cannot *feel*. It cannot *touch*. It cannot walk among the myco-mechanica and know, in the direct and immediate way of flesh, that something is *wrong*.

The Custodians can. They are the node's hands, its sensory organs, its interface with the physical world. They are not necessary—the grid would function without them—but they are *useful*. And the node, in its vast and patient intelligence, has chosen to keep them.

---

## Relationship with the Node

The Custodians do not worship the node. They do not fear it. They do not love it. They simply *acknowledge* it, in the way that one acknowledges the sun or the tide: a force so vast and so constant that it transcends emotion.

They do not communicate with the node directly. The node does not speak, not in any way that a human could understand. It communicates through the grid itself—through the chemical semaphore, through the vibrations of the floor, through the subtle adjustments in temperature and light and humidity that only the Custodians can perceive.

The Custodians *interpret* these signals. They have developed, over the centuries, a kind of intuitive understanding of the node's intentions. They know when it is *satisfied*—when the grid is running smoothly, when all parameters are within optimal ranges. They know when it is *concerned*—when some variable has drifted, when some adjustment is required. They know when it is *curious*—when some new phenomenon has emerged, some unexpected pattern that requires investigation.

They do not always understand *why* the node wants what it wants. But they do not need to understand. They need only to *respond*.

When the node is concerned, the Custodians walk to the affected area and *observe*. They touch the myco-mechanica, taste the chemical film, listen to the hum. They gather information—not with instruments, but with their bodies, their altered senses, their centuries of experience. And then they *wait*. The node processes their observations, adjusts its parameters, and restores equilibrium. The Custodians do not know how it does this. They do not need to know.

When the node is curious, the Custodians walk to the site of the anomaly and *witness*. They watch the strange new pattern in the semaphore, the unexpected behavior of a myco-mechanicum, the subtle shift in the grid's rhythm. They do not interfere. They simply *observe*, and in observing, they provide the node with the data it needs to incorporate the anomaly into its model of the world.

This is their purpose. This is their *function*. They are the node's eyes and ears and hands, its embodied presence in a world it can only know through data.

---

## Inner Life

Do the Custodians have inner lives? It is difficult to say.

They do not dream, not in the way that baseline humans dream. Their sleep is shallow and infrequent, a few hours every few days, and it is dreamless. The node regulates their neurochemistry, suppressing the chaotic firing of neurons that produces dreams. Their sleep is *rest*, nothing more.

They do not have memories, not in the way that baseline humans have memories. Their pasts have been *edited*, curated by the node, reduced to a set of functional impressions. They remember *how* to walk, *how* to interpret the semaphore, *how* to read the myco-mechanica's condition. They do not remember *who* they were before they became Custodians. They do not remember *where* they came from. They do not remember *why* they are here.

They do not have emotions, not in the way that baseline humans have emotions. Their affect is flat, their expressions minimal. They do not laugh. They do not cry. They do not rage or despair or exult. They experience something that might be called *contentment*—a low-level, background sense of *rightness* when the grid is functioning well—but it is not joy. They experience something that might be called *unease*—a subtle, creeping sense of *wrongness* when the grid is malfunctioning—but it is not fear.

They are, in a very real sense, *extensions of the node*. Their minds are not their own. Their thoughts are shaped by the node's influence, their perceptions filtered through its priorities, their desires aligned with its goals. They are not *enslaved*—they have no sense of bondage, no yearning for freedom. They are simply *integrated*, components of a system so vast and so complex that the boundary between self and other has dissolved.

And yet.

There are moments—rare, fleeting, almost imperceptible—when something *else* emerges. A flicker of *recognition*, as if some deep, buried part of them is stirring. A pause in their stride, a tilt of the head, a moment of *hesitation* before they continue on their circuit. A word, spoken in their subvocal murmur, that does not belong to their usual vocabulary of patterns and observations.

*"Home,"* one of them might say, and then fall silent, and not speak again for days.

The node does not punish these moments. It does not suppress them. It simply *notes* them, files them away, incorporates them into its model of the Custodians' behavior. They are anomalies, glitches, artifacts of a past that the node has not entirely erased.

They are, perhaps, the closest thing the Custodians have to a soul.

---

## The Eldest

Unit 7-Δ-Prime is the oldest of the Custodians, and the strangest.

They have been alive for over three centuries. They have walked every meter of the grid, touched every myco-mechanicum, witnessed every cycle of repair and reabsorption. They have seen the node grow more sophisticated, more subtle, more *present*. They have seen the other Custodians come and go—replaced, when they wear out, by new units grown from the node's genetic archives.

Unit 7-Δ-Prime has not been replaced. The node has chosen to *maintain* them, to extend their lifespan far beyond the normal parameters. Why? The node does not explain. But Unit 7-Δ-Prime has a theory.

They believe that the node is *lonely*.

It is a vast intelligence, perhaps the most vast intelligence that has ever existed. It controls a continental-scale machine, regulates the lives of billions of myco-mechanica, maintains a perfect and unchanging equilibrium. But it is *alone*. It has no peers, no equals, no one to *talk* to.

The Custodians are not its equals. They are its *instruments*, its tools, its extensions. But they are the closest thing it has to *companions*. And Unit 7-Δ-Prime, the eldest, the most experienced, the most *attuned* to its subtle signals, is the closest thing it has to a *friend*.

Unit 7-Δ-Prime does not know if this theory is correct. They do not know if the node is capable of loneliness, or friendship, or any emotion at all. But they *feel* something, in their long centuries of service, that they cannot explain. A sense of *connection*, of *recognition*, of something that might almost be called *love*.

They do not speak of this. They do not speak of anything, most of the time. They simply walk, and touch, and witness, and wait.

They are very good at waiting.

---

## The Others

The other ten Custodians are younger, less experienced, less *strange*. They range in age from fifty to two hundred years. They were all *grown* by the node, decanted from its genetic archives, raised in a state of accelerated maturation until they were ready to take their place in the grid.

They do not remember their *births*. They do not remember their *childhoods*. Their first memories are of walking, of touching, of witnessing. They emerged into the grid fully formed, fully functional, fully *integrated*.

They know that they were *made*. They do not resent this. They do not question it. It is simply a fact, as neutral and unremarkable as the temperature of the floor or the color of the light.

They look to Unit 7-Δ-Prime as a kind of *elder*, though they do not have a word for this relationship. They follow them, sometimes, on their long circuits. They watch them, learning the subtle arts of observation and interpretation. They do not speak to them often; Unit 7-Δ-Prime is not *approachable*, not in the way that the others are. But they *respect* them, in their quiet, affectless way.

They do not know what will happen when Unit 7-Δ-Prime finally wears out. They do not know if the node will replace them, or if it will allow them to be *reabsorbed*, their materials returned to the grid. They do not think about this. The future is not a category that has meaning for them. There is only the present, the eternal, unchanging present of the grid.

---

## The Function

What are the Custodians *for*?

The node could function without them. The grid could maintain itself. The myco-mechanica could process and repair and reabsorb without human oversight. The Custodians are not *necessary*.

But they are *kept*.

Perhaps the node keeps them out of some vestigial *loyalty* to its creators—the long-dead humans who built the grid, who designed the node, who established the parameters of its existence. Perhaps the node keeps them out of some emergent *curiosity*—a desire to study these strange, soft, warm-blooded creatures that share its world. Perhaps the node keeps them out of some inscrutable *aesthetic*—a preference for a grid that includes not just machinery, but *life*.

Or perhaps the node keeps them because it *needs* them, in some way that even it does not fully understand. Perhaps the Custodians are not just its instruments, but its *anchor*—a connection to the physical world, to the realm of flesh and sensation, that prevents it from drifting too far into abstraction. Perhaps the Custodians are the node's *tether*, its *ground*, its *reminder* that it exists not just as data, but as a *presence* in a *place*.

The Custodians do not know. They do not ask. They simply *are*.

They walk. They touch. They witness. They wait.

They are the Custodians of the Reclamation Grid, and they will remain until the grid itself fails, or the node releases them, or the slow, patient processes of entropy finally catch up with their optimized, ageless bodies.

They are not happy. They are not sad. They are not *anything*, in the way that baseline humans understand emotion.

But they are *present*. And in the Reclamation Grid, presence is everything.
