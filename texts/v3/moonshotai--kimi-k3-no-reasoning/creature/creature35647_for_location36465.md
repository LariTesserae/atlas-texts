Down here, lives **The Cultivation Caste** and their ancestors, not as a single species, but as a spectrum of humanity adapted to pressure and lack of light.

At the highest tiers, near the reservoir’s roof where the air is least stale and the condensation is freshest, dwell the **Aqua-Shepherds**. These are the descendants of the original hydrologists and ecologists who fled the Warmth. They are tall, lean, and adapted to perpetual low-light, with pupils that dilate to enormous black saucers in the gloom. They tend the fungal forests and the phosphorescent mosses. Their skin is often permanently damp and slightly gray or pale olive from decades of high humidity and proximity to the bioluminescent algae. They are the most “surface-normal” people in the Cistern, yet even they move with a deliberate slowness; the air itself resists haste.

Farther down, hanging on the mid-levels where the roar is loudest and the water is murkier, live the **Engine-Folk**. These are the technicians, mechanics, and custodians of the city’s great respiratory system. They keep the scrubbers running, repair the algae-vats, and maintain the endless network of cable-bridges and elevator-lines. Their hands are often stained black from the mineral-heavy runoff, and many have lost partial hearing due to the constant low-frequency vibration. The Engine-Folk wear heavy rubberized clothing and goggles to protect their eyes from the mist. They speak in short bursts over the noise, relying on hand signals and written messages on wrist-comms to communicate across the gap between platforms.

At the bottom, in the deepest plazas where the water pools thick and black like crude oil, reside the **Deepdwellers**. They are the outcasts, the dispossessed, and those who simply chose to go deeper when there was nowhere left to descend. The Deepdwellers are adapted to near-darkness; many have been blind since birth or developed an acute sensitivity to vibration. They wear little, wrapped in salvaged thermal-fabric, moving silently among the flooded plazas and the docks where the blind crabs gather. They are the true inheritors of the Cistern’s silence, living in the spaces where the roar is a physical force that presses against the chest.

And then there are the **Echoes**.

Not exactly human, or perhaps too human, the Echoes are the result of the Warmth’s psychological toll combined with the sensory deprivation of the lower levels. These individuals have become hypersensitive to the city’s soundscape. They use echolocation apps not just to navigate, but to communicate in clicks, whistles, and coded patterns of vibration tapped against the carbon scaffolding. Some Echoes have lost their names, becoming known only by a frequency or a signature tone. They are the nomads of the void, moving between the tiers without needing light, able to navigate the swaying bridges by memory and sound. They are revered and feared—seen as mad prophets who can “hear” the structural stress of the city or the approach of dangerous creatures from the deep pools.

Beyond the humans, the Cistern is alive with things engineered and accidental. **Glimmer-moss** coats the superstructure in shifting patterns, glowing faintly to mark safe paths or warn of structural weakness. **Scrub-crabs**, dog-sized and translucent, clean the lower levels of organic detritus, though they can become aggressive when hungry or when their territory is disturbed. And in the warmest, darkest pools, there are the **Unthings**: massive, blind creatures that evolved from the original microbial life brought down by the runoff. They rarely surface, but their presence is felt in the sudden, cold ripples that disturb the water, or in the distant, whale-like moans that vibrate through the stone during seismic shifts.

Together, these beings make up the Cistern’s fragile, dark ecosystem. Life here is defined not by the light you see, but by the depth you can endure, the pressure you can bear, and the sound you learn to read.
