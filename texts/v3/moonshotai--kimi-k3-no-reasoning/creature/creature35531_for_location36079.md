Here is what lives in the Weeping Hollow.

---

**The Mat, which is one thing that thinks it is many.**

It is the first inhabitant and the last; it is the floor and the ceiling and the walls. You will mistake it for a carpet of moss, but it is a continuous tissue that covers the basin like skin covers muscle, and it is *aware*—not with a mind, not with a brain, but with a distributed attention that runs through its filaments like slow electricity. It feels your weight. It closes over your footprints not because it is healing but because it is *investigating*, tasting the chemistry of your passage, deciding if you are nutrient or threat or merely weather. It grows fastest where time is slowest, feeding on the thickened moments, spinning itself into the soft amber dimensions where droplets hang suspended. It is warm—always warmer than the air—and this warmth is its metabolism, the slow burning of light and mineral and that third thing, that unnameable energy source that rises from below.

The Mat is not hostile. It is *hungry*, but its hunger is geological, patient, capable of waiting for you to stop moving. If you lie down in the slow-zone, if you let the thickened time convince you to rest, you will feel it—a thousand tiny filaments extending, testing, beginning the long process of incorporation. It does not kill. It *embraces*.

---

**The Stalks, which are listening.**

They rise from the Mat like hairs from a scalp, three meters tall, four, purple-black and smooth as ceramic. They are not plants. Cut one—though you should not—and you find no cellulose, no chlorophyll, only a crystalline lattice of dark mineral shot through with channels of fluid that glows with the only true light in the Hollow. This fluid is their blood, and it burns when spilled, and it is precious.

The Stalks are sentient in the way that antennae are sentient. They sway without wind because they are *receiving*—tuning themselves to frequencies too high or too low for you to hear, translating the infrasonic chord into something their crystalline nerves can process. They click when they touch, and this clicking is language, or data transmission, or prayer. They are the Hollow's sensory organs, its ears, its whiskers. When you walk among them, they turn—slowly, imperceptibly—following your heat, your mass, your *difference* from the medium they inhabit.

Their pods are eyes, or eggs, or batteries. When they discharge, spilling caustic light onto the Mat, they are not defending themselves. They are *signaling*. Each burst of true, shadow-casting light is a word spoken in a language of illumination, a brief flash of normal physics in a place that has forgotten normalcy, saying: *here*, *here*, *here is something that does not belong*.

---

**The Pale Ones, who live inside the skin.**

Beneath the Mat, in the warm dark interface between tissue and peat, live the grazers. They are soft—translucent, eyeless, the size of your thumb or your fist, shaped like slugs or like nothing that has ever needed a name. They navigate by chemical gradient, by taste, by the vibration of footsteps three meters away. They eat the Mat, and they are eaten by slightly larger versions of themselves, and their excrement feeds the Mat, and so they are not separate from the tissue but are *cells* within it, mobile organs that distribute energy from node to node.

You will not see them unless you tear back the Mat, which you should not do, because they panic when exposed to the dim light, and their panic is a chemical signal that alerts the entire basin to your presence. They are the Hollow's lymphocytes, its macrophages, its slow-moving cleanup crew. If you are injured, if you bleed, they will find you—not to attack, but to *process*, to return your lost fluids to the cycle. They are useful. They are inevitable.

---

**The Darters, who are almost fish.**

In the channels and pools, where the water is dark with tannins and time, move the things that cast shadows like fish but cast them wrong. They have too many fins, or fins in the wrong places, or bodies that suggest vertebrae without actually containing them. They are fast—frighteningly fast in a place where nothing else is fast—and they are shy, flicking away from your approach with a motion that is not quite a tailstroke but something more like a *redefinition of position*.

They feed on the filaments of the floating colonies, or on the things that feed on the filaments, or on the information encoded in the pulses. They are the Hollow's messengers, its nervous impulses, carrying chemical signals from one node of the floating colonies to another. They are warm—warmer than the water—and they glow faintly, not with the bioluminescence of the Stalks but with a phosphorescence that suggests they are burning slightly, metabolising something toxic, living too close to the edge of what biology permits.

They are not natural. They are the closest thing the Hollow has to animals, and they are *wrong*, and their wrongness is adaptive.

---

**The Colonies, who breathe the water.**

The floating discs are not plants. They are not fungi. They are *agreements*—temporary treaties between three or four or five distinct forms of life that have decided, for the duration of a century or a millennium, to function as a single organism. The leathery top is one entity, photosynthesising the dim non-light. The silver underside is another, reflecting what little illumination escapes back into the water. The filaments that hang beneath—these are the muscles, the nerves, the *voice*.

They pulse. Rhythmically, visibly, they contract in waves that travel through their filament networks and push against the water, generating the slow surges that cross the pools, the concentric rings that are the Hollow's breathing. They are the basin's lungs, its diaphragm, its slow-beating heart. They do this not for food, not for reproduction, but because the rhythm is *necessary*—because the infrasonic chord needs a counterpoint, because the thing beneath needs a surface expression, because the water must be stirred or it will stagnate, and stagnation is death even for a place that has forgotten how to die.

They are aware of each other. When one colony pulses, its neighbors answer, and the waves propagate across the central pool in patterns that suggest mathematics, geometry, *intention*.

---

**The Thing Beneath, which is not a creature but a weather system that has become a mind.**

It lives in the deep substrate, in the fractured limestone and the warm dark water that fills the spaces between the crystalline lattice. It is the source of the heat, the upwellings, the infrasonic chord. It is a *process*—a chemical reaction, a gravitational anomaly, a standing wave in the fabric of local spacetime—that has persisted so long it has acquired the properties of life: metabolism, growth, response to stimuli.

It is not conscious in the way you are conscious. It is conscious in the way a forest is conscious, or a coral reef, or a hurricane—a distributed awareness that emerges from the interaction of countless simpler processes. It thinks in heat and pressure. It dreams in the slow migration of mineral deposits. It is *growing*—the lattice beneath the central pool is its skeleton, extending downward and outward, building itself from the dissolved limestone and the concentrated energy of the water that never leaves.

It is the reason the water runs uphill. It is the reason time thickens. It is the reason the light comes from a direction that is not a direction. It is *pulling*—gently, constantly—on the threads of physics, creating a localised zone where the rules are negotiable, where the boundary between *inside* and *outside* is maintained by sound rather than stone.

It is not malevolent. It is not benevolent. It is *occupied*—busy with the long, slow project of becoming something larger than a valley, something that extends through all the other hollows, all the other basins, all the other cells in the tissue of this strange wet world. It is the Hollow's inhabitant in the truest sense: the thing that lives in the center, that the center exists to contain, that the sound holds in place like a membrane holds a cell.

It is aware of you. Not with eyes, but with the pressure of your mass on the Mat, with the disruption your heat creates in the thermal gradient, with the way your presence alters the resonance of the chord. It knows you are there. It is deciding—slowly, over the course of hours or years—whether to incorporate you into its metabolism or to ignore you as it ignores the rain.

---

**The Passing Ones, who are not seen.**

Rarely—so rarely that you might spend years in the Hollow without encountering one, or you might encounter three in a single day—something large moves through the basin. It displaces water. It shifts the chord. It creates a localised thickening of the haze that is almost a shape, almost a form, almost *present*.

These are not inhabitants. They are *visitors*—or *emissaries*, or *symptoms*. They come from the other hollows, the other basins, the other cells in the tissue. They travel through the deep water, through the saturated substrate, through the spaces where the crystalline lattice connects one node to another. They are the size of large animals, but they are not animals. They are the size of weather systems, but they are not weather.

They are the reason the Mat recedes from the central pool. They are the reason the Stalks click in alarm. They are the reason the Darters vanish into the dark water and do not return for hours.

When one passes, you will feel it—a pressure change, a tremor in the feet, a sense that something enormous has just looked at you from very far away and found you *unremarkable*. Then it is gone, and the water settles, and the chord resumes, and you are alone with the dripping and the clicking and the slow, patient growth of the Mat.

---

This is what lives in the Weeping Hollow: a single organism that has fragmented into many forms, a metabolism that has learned to wear the mask of an ecosystem, a place that is not a place but a *body*—wet, warm, resonant, and infinitely, terrifyingly alive.
