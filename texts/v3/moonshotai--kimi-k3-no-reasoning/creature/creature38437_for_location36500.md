There is a place that does not appear on any map because the maps are drawn by people who need to go home. Nobody who comes here goes home, not because they die, but because they stop being the sort of thing that has a home.

The place is small. It is inside something. Specifically, it is inside a machine that was built on a high cold ridge and then grown over and then abandoned and then restarted, not by people, but by something else.

The machine is a turbine hall, but it is also a cave, but it is also a womb, but it is also a stomach. It is all of these because it was built once and then the water got in and the cold got in and the moss got in and the moths got in and the mice got in and the rust got in and the rust did not stop and the moss did not stop and the moths did not stop and the mice did not stop and the water did not stop and the cold did not stop and the hall did not stop being a hall and became a stomach.

The hall is maybe thirty meters long, eight high, ten wide. It is the size of a village church, or a small aircraft hangar, or a chapel, or a throat. It is exactly the size of a room in which something large can be born and then never leave.

The walls are made of cast iron plates, riveted, bolted, seam-welded, seam-welded again, and again, and then painted with a lead paint that has since been licked by something with a tongue made of lichen, and now the paint is gone in patches and the iron is wet and the iron is cold and the iron is always, always wet and always, always cold. The wet is not from rain. The wet is from the walls themselves. The walls sweat. The walls breathe. The walls weep. The walls are cold enough to ache and they weep water that is warmer than the air and the water runs down in slow rivulets and gathers in the corners and in the corners it is not water anymore, it is a gel, it is a jelly, it is a film, it is alive, and it pulses very slowly, once every forty seconds, like a heart that is also a lung that is also a kidney.

The floor is not a floor. The floor is a lattice of iron grating over a sump, and the sump is full of black water that is not black from dirt but black from depth and from the things that live in it and from the light that does not reach it. The grating is slick. The grating is never dry. The grating is never warm. The grating is always cold and always wet and always humming. The hum comes from below. The hum comes from the turbine that is still down there, still turning, still spinning, still working, still doing something, even though there is no river anymore, no pipe anymore, no grid anymore. The turbine turns because something is turning it. The turbine turns because something is feeding it. The turbine turns because the water is moving, and the water is moving because the water is being pumped, and the water is being pumped because the machine is alive and the machine is alive because the machine is alive.

The air is cold. Not sharp cold. Not wind cold. Not mountain cold. A wet cold. A cold that sits in your lungs and stays. A cold that condenses on your teeth. A cold that makes your breath visible and then makes your breath fall down, because the breath is heavy here, because the air is saturated, because the air is full of water and full of spores and full of sound and full of wings.

The light is dim. It comes from fixtures that were installed a century ago and then replaced and then broken and then repaired by something that is not a person and then left alone. The fixtures are glass globes, and inside the globes are filaments, and the filaments are lit, but not by electricity. The filaments are lit by heat. The heat comes from the walls. The walls are cold but they are also warm inside, and the warmth goes to the filaments, and the filaments glow a dull orange, like embers, like the inside of a mouth, like the inside of a hive. The light is not enough to see by. The light is enough to know that you are not alone.

The fauna is overwhelming.

There are mice. There are mice in the walls and mice in the floor and mice in the sump and mice in the turbine and mice in your shoes if you stand still too long. The mice are not mice. The mice are small and warm and furry and they have too many joints and too many eyes and they move too fast and they move too slow and they move in ways that suggest they are not one thing but many things pretending to be one thing.

There are beetles. There are birds. There should not be birds here. There are birds.
