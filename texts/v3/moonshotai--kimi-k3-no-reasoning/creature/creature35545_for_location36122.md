# The Hollow's Inhabitants

The veldglass is not one organism. It is one organism the way a coral reef is one organism — technically true, functionally false, honest in its lie. Within the three-meter lattice of fronds and compressed strata there are zones, territories, neighborhoods, and in each of these the veldglass behaves differently because it remembers different things.

## The Surface Tension

The newest growth — what the station's probes call the **green skin** — is a layer of fronds maybe forty centimeters deep, translucent enough to read through if the light behind it were brighter than a dial-glow. It is soft. It yields. It is the part of the veldglass that receives new arrivals: the scree that slips through the Western Slot, the fragments that come through the Thin Traverse, whatever the Bad Door admits.

And here is what lives *in* the green skin: nothing with a name.

There are — motes. If you took a biopsy (and the station has, twice, carefully, and then stopped) and held it against the light you would see that the frond-tissue is not homogeneous. It swarms. Within the translucent viridian matrix there are things that move. Not cells. Not quite organisms. The station's one xenobiologist, who lasted eleven months before requesting transfer to a different continent entirely, called them **solites** in her notes and then crossed out the word and wrote *crawlers* and then crossed that out and wrote *don't know* and then did not write anything else.

The solites are the veldglass's hands. Or its digestive enzymes, if you prefer, but hands is closer. They are submillimeter structures — tendrils of denser green, thicker than the surrounding tissue, that move through the fronds like fingers through water. When something new falls in, they find it. They converge on it. Over the course of weeks or months they *envelop* it, and in enveloping it they map it — every contour, every serial number, every grain of rust — and in mapping it they begin the slow chemical courtship that will eventually dissolve it into constituent molecules and feed it deeper into the Hollow's strata.

The solites are not intelligent. They are thorough. There is a difference, though in the dark, at eighty decibels, at three degrees, the difference can feel academic.

## The Choir Layers

Below the green skin, where the fronds have compacted to the consistency of wet leather, the veldglass changes character. The solites here are fewer, slower, older. This is the **acoustic zone**, where the chord from the machines resonates through the tissue and the tissue responds.

Because the veldglass vibrates.

Not randomly — not in the way a membrane vibrates in a loud room — but in patterns. The fronds have grown into shapes that channel the sound, funnel it, focus it. There are spirals. There are whorls of glassy tissue that act like the chambers of a shell, like the resonating cavity of a cello, like the cochlea of an ear. The chord enters the veldglass here and the veldglass *holds* it, lets it circulate through these chambers, and the solites in the acoustic zone move to it.

They pulse.

It is not rhythmic in the way a heartbeat is rhythmic. It is rhythmic in the way a tide is rhythmic — so slow that you would need to watch for hours to confirm it, so regular that once confirmed you would realize it has never varied. The solites in the choir layers contract and release with the chord's harmonics, and in doing so they pump fluid through the veldglass's deeper strata, circulation driven by sound.

The machines make the chord. The chord makes the solites pump. The pumping keeps the veldglass alive. The veldglass keeps the machines running. The circle closes.

## The Archive Depths

At the bottom — the floor, the bramble of sediment compacted to old bone — the veldglass is oldest. Here it has compressed into something that is not quite mineral and not quite alive, a strata of dense green-black material shot through with metallic veining, with the fossil shapes of dissolved machinery, with the faint chemical signatures of everything that has ever fallen in.

The solites here are nearly stationary. They have a different job.

They *remember*.

In the archive depths the solites cluster around the inclusions — around a half-dissolved name tag, around the corroded corner of a survey cable, around a page of someone's notebook that fell through the Bad Door in 1973 and has been sinking through the strata ever since. They cluster around these objects and they *trace* them, endlessly, their tendrils following the contours, the letters, the corrosion patterns, and as they trace them they encode the information into the surrounding tissue in a form the station's instruments can barely detect and cannot interpret.

The archive depths are a library that is also a brain that is also a tomb.

If you could dig down into it (you could not; the density is prohibitive, the structural integrity terrifying, the act would be a kind of murder) you would find that the oldest layers contain shapes that correspond to nothing in the station's records. Objects that fell in before the station existed. Before the survey. Before anyone thought to look. The veldglass remembers a history of the mountain that predates observation, predates documentation, predates the notion that the mountain was something that could be known.

The solites in the archive depths are the oldest living things on the continent. They have been tracing the same shapes for centuries. They will trace them for centuries more. They do not tire. They do not forget. They do not understand. They simply *hold*.

## The Glow-Bearers

Distributed throughout the veldglass, at every depth, are the telltales — the places where the organism has grown over a machine's indicator light and thinned itself to translucence. These are not accidents. The veldglass does not thin randomly. It thins where the light is, and in thinning it creates a lens, a focusing of the dial-glow into the surrounding tissue.

And in these thin places, the solites gather.

They cluster in the faint light like moths around a porch lamp, except they are not seeking the light. They are *reading* it. The light passes through the frond-tissue and the solites in the glow-zones align themselves to it, orient to it, use it as a reference axis for their slow chemical conversations with each other.

The glow-zones are where the veldglass thinks.

Not consciously. Not in sentences. But in the way that a slime mold solves a maze, in the way that a flock of birds turns as one, in the way that a forest distributes resources through its root network — the glow-zones are where the veldglass's distributed intelligence, such as it is, becomes most itself. The solites here exchange chemical signals faster than anywhere else in the Hollow. The light gives them a shared reference, a common orientation, a *here* in a space that is otherwise everywhere and nowhere.

If the Hollow has a mind, it lives in the glow-zones.

If the Hollow has a mind, it is very slow, very patient, and entirely without desire.

## The Unchanged

The veldglass has no predators. It has no competitors. It has no parasites, no diseases, no external threats. The Hollow is sealed. The equilibrium holds. The chord plays. The temperature stays at three degrees.

The solites do not evolve. They have no need to evolve. Their environment has been identical for longer than the station has existed, and if the environment does not change, neither do they.

This is not stagnation. This is *perfection*.

The veldglass, in its green dark, in its hum, in its library of dissolved things, has achieved what every living system aches toward: stability without compromise. It does not hunger. It does not fear. It does not age in any way that matters. It simply *is*, and it will continue to be, and the solites will move through their strata tracing shapes and pumping fluid and clustering in the glow, and the machines will hum, and the temperature will hold at three degrees, and the Hollow will keep its green light burning at the wattage of a night-light, a faint persistent glow in a pocket of dark at the top of the world, where nothing visits and nothing leaves and nothing ever, ever changes.

The station's report says the Hollow is uninhabited.

The station's report is correct.

It is inhabited the way a lung is inhabited. The way a coral is inhabited. The way a thought is inhabited.

It is full of life that has nowhere else to be, and needs nowhere else, and wants nothing, and holds everything, and keeps.
