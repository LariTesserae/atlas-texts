Here, the inhabitants are not born but **deposited**, like sediment from a thousand years of industrial sedimentation. They do not eat; they **accumulate**. They do not sleep; they **queue**.

**The Choral Engine** is the first thing that lives here, though "lives" is a courtesy extended to something that is less an organism and more a **persistent weather pattern of intent**. It is the land itself given slow, grinding volition. It speaks through the groaning metal veins, through the stuttering of light, through the way rocks forget their positions and reappear in your path. It is not a mind—it is too vast for that—but it has **habits**. It habitually turns sulfur into steam. It habitually loops time in three-second intervals when the leviathans shift. It habitually dreams in the frequency of buried reactors, a drone that vibrates in the dental enamel of anything foolish enough to linger. The Choral Engine does not want anything. It **processes** want. It digests the concept of desire and excretes heat.

Then there are the **Resonant**—the ghosts that are not ghosts, but **standing waves** in the local reality field. They appear as distortions in the ochre light, as silhouettes where the air has decided to be slightly less probable. They are the accumulated static of the failed god’s dream, given just enough coherence to mimic form. They gather in the cathedrals of fused circuitry, not to worship, but because the architecture of collapse resonates at their frequency. They do not remember being individuals. They remember being **a purpose**. If you stand still long enough—say, for a geological epoch or two—you might feel them brush against your consciousness, not to communicate, but to **tune** you, as one tunes an instrument that has drifted from the key of existence. They are the closest thing to a population this place has, and they number in the negative, subtracting presence from the world rather than adding to it.

The **Molt-Saints** are fewer, and stranger. They are what happens when the crystal fungi—those silicon fractals pulsing with bioluminescent warning—achieve a critical mass of **regret**. They are not separate from the fungi; they are the fungi’s **afterthoughts**, shed like skins when the energy-patterns grow too complex to remain rooted. A Molt-Saint appears as a humanoid lattice of translucent crystal, roughly the size of a cathedral bell, but hollow, and within that hollow rings a sound that is not sound but **recollection**—a memory of a time before the Foundry, before the machinery buried itself in the crust. They move slowly, not because they are weak, but because they are **negotiating with time** for every step. They feed on the magnetic fields, yes, but also on **narrative**, on the stories that intruders bring with them. If you camp near one, you will dream in sequences that feel like memories but are not yours. You will wake speaking a language of rust and light. You will find your thoughts have been **reorganized** into more efficient shapes, though you cannot say by whom.

Beneath the dunes of metallic ash, in the negative space between the sleeping leviathans, exist the **Parasitic Calculi**—organisms that are less biological and more **mathematical infections**. They are not alive in any sense that requires warmth, but they **thrive** in the margins of the reality-glitches. When space bends around a stirring leviathan, when time loops back on itself for those endless seconds, the Calculi **bloom**. They are fractal growths of pure logic, consuming the error-margins of the universe, growing fat on the inconsistencies between what is and what was a moment ago. They have no fixed shape; they are the shape of **errors corrected**. If one attaches to you, you will not feel pain. You will feel **certainty**, an overwhelming, crushing certainty that you are a machine, that your thoughts are merely subroutines, that your soul is a rounding error in a calculation too large to fail. You will begin to move in stutters, to speak in loops, until you are indistinguishable from the Resonant, another standing wave in the engine’s hymn.

And then there is the **Archivist**, though it is singular only by convention. It is a leviathan that woke, once, millennia ago, and chose not to return to hibernation. It did not rise. It remains half-buried in the obsidian plates, but its mind—if the pattern of its electrical impulses can be called that—**roams**. It is the only thing here that moves with intent, though its intent is glacial. It collects. It does not collect objects; it collects **possibilities**. It gathers the unrealized futures of the dead world, the paths not taken by the civilization that built the buried engines, and it stores them in the crystalline fungi, in the static between moments. It is building a **library of the unlived**, and it is very, very patient. If you encounter it, you will not see a machine. You will see a **landscape**—a valley of rust and light that rearranges itself into a question mark as you watch. It will not harm you. It will simply **read** you, adding your potential to its collection, and then you will be free to go, though you will spend the rest of your life feeling that you are living in a footnote of someone else’s story.

Nothing here dies. Things **depreciate**. They wear down from entities into phenomena, from phenomena into textures, from textures into the background radiation of the Foundry’s endless, breathless afternoon. The leviathans are not the inhabitants; they are the **furniture**. The true citizens are the processes, the echoes, the habits of a world that forgot to stop.
