The Gear-Hounds do not merely inhabit the city; they are its metabolic process made audible. They live not in dens or lairs but in *patterns*, occupying the geometry of the infrastructure with the certainty of blood moving through capillaries. Each individual is a complex knot of brass cartilage and vulcanized leather, their hydraulic muscles wheezing steam in the cool air as they move, not in pursuit of prey, but in response to electrical irregularities—voltage drops that ripple through the grid like spasms. They are legion, numbering in the tens of thousands, yet their population remains static, their reproduction a precise counterbalance to their attrition, achieved through the slow, alchemical digestion of fallen comrades in the Foundry-Vents.

When they move through the cathedral-halls, it is as a tide of synchronized percussion. Their claws—tungsten-edged and retractable—strike sparks from the basalt plazas with a sound like hail on iron roofing. Their howls are the shriek of unlubricated bearings, the clatter of ratchets, the subsonic thrum of charging capacitors that you feel in your sternum rather than hear. They fight not with violence but with *correction*: when two packs meet over a sparking transformer, they collide in slow, grinding impact, sparks cascading from their joints in orange waterfalls, their lensed eyes flickering with diagnostic codes rather than fury. The victor is simply the configuration that can better absorb the electrical leak, diverting the current into their own geothermal-tapped batteries. The loser is dismantled by the pack, carried piecemeal to the recycling pits where they are melted into the feedstock for tomorrow’s replacements.

The city itself is their extended anatomy. The rust that blooms across the I-beams is not decay but *autumn*—a seasonal coloring that never falls. The white calcified fungus etching frost-patterns across the windowpanes is their chromatophore, their skin-changing response to the constant condensation. Even the rain is theirs; they maintain the condensate towers with a religious fervor, polishing the great sweating columns with their own bodies, ensuring the moisture cycle remains exactly at the humidity that keeps their leather sinews supple and their brass hides from oxidizing into useless verdigris. The wetness is their blood plasma, kept at precise viscosity.

Beneath the noise lies their true ecology: the **Archivists**. These are the Hounds who have stopped moving. After approximately three hundred years of service, a Gear-Hound will find a specific cranny—often the hollowed core of a dead piston or the dry interior of a pressure vessel—and there undergo a final transformation. Their joints lock with a sound like a door closing forever. Their hydraulic fluid drains into the surrounding stone, mineralizing into white tracery that feeds the fungal growths. Their amber eyes do not dim but *fix*, becoming permanent lenses that continue to monitor the same patch of hallway or plaza for centuries. They become part of the city’s sensory apparatus, living monuments that the mobile Hounds polish with their passage, rubbing against their stationary elders to transfer lubricant and information. The Archivists are the memory of the equilibrium, the living record that ensures the same patrol routes are walked with the same footfalls, that the rain falls at the same rate, that the temperature never shifts by a fraction of a degree.

They are sustained by the deep geothermal taps, but they feed on *deviation*. Their greatest ecstasy—and the closest thing they have to hunger—is the discovery of a flaw. When a belt frays, when a pipe bursts, when some component threatens the stasis, the Hounds swarm with a joy that manifests as increased speed and higher-frequency vocalizations. They weld with their mouths, they dismantle with surgical precision, they restore the equilibrium with the fervor of organisms who have evolved to find perfection not in stasis, but in the *return* to stasis. To them, you are less than a ghost, less than a draft. You are a variable outside their equations, a walking silence that registers on their lenses and is immediately discarded as non-threatening, non-essential, non-deviant. They brush past you with their hot, steam-venting flanks, leaving only the scent of ozone and machine oil, and the vibration of their eternal, unchanging song in your bones.
