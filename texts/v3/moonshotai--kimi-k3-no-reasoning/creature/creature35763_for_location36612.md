The **Kharosi** are not a people who have chosen to live underground; they are a people who have chosen to *become* underground. They are the City-Born of the Unfinished, and their very physiology and psychology are being rewritten by the perpetual construction site they inhabit.

**Physiological Adaptation: The Compression**
Generations within the packed, dense environment have shaped them. They are, on average, shorter and more compact than surface-dwellers, with a tendency toward broad shoulders and powerful, wiry builds suited to maneuvering in tight crawl-spaces and applying force in confined areas. Their most striking feature is their skin. It is pale, not the sickly white of a cave fish, but a deep, translucent alabaster that seems to hold a faint, cool luminescence in the dim light, as if their bodies have absorbed the quality of the polished limestone. This skin is perpetually cool to the touch and slightly damp, their pores adapted to the high humidity, allowing them to "breathe" the moist air more efficiently. Prolonged exposure to the surface's dry air and direct sun is physically painful, causing their skin to crack and their eyes to burn.

Their eyes have adapted to the perpetual twilight. Large, dark, and liquid, their pupils remain wide, gathering every stray photon from the mirror-shifts and fungal glows. In absolute darkness, they are as blind as any human, but in the dimmest light of the Under-City, they see a world of subtle gradients, reading expressions in the shadows of an archway where a surface-dweller would see only black. They are intensely photosensitive; a direct, unfiltered sunbeam is a blinding, terrifying assault.

**Psychology: The Interiority of Pressure**
The Kharosi psyche is a direct product of their environment: dense, pressurized, and intensely interior. Living in a state of constant, intimate proximity with no privacy, they have developed a complex culture of psychic containment.

*   **The Whisper-Culture:** They are a quiet people. Loud noises are not just rude; they are a form of spatial aggression. Voices are kept low, movements are economical and deliberate. Their language is rich in sub-vocalizations, subtle hand gestures, and a complex system of taps and scratches on stone that can carry conversations across a crowded hall without a single spoken word. They have learned to create "privacy" through silence and stillness, wrapping themselves in a cloak of quiet so profound it becomes a personal space.

*   **The Stillness of Becoming:** Their city is not finished, so how can they be? This imbues them with a profound sense of patience and a unique relationship with time. A project that takes three generations is not a failure of ambition; it is the natural order. A drop of water forming on a stalactite is a promise to their grandchildren. They are masters of deferred gratification, their lives oriented towards a future state of completion that they know, intellectually, they will never see. This creates a culture that is both deeply stable and in constant, incremental flux. They are not building *for* eternity; they are building *as* a form of eternity.

*   **Hydrological Piety:** Water is not a resource; it is the lifeblood of the mountain, a divine and capricious gift. They do not worship a god of water; they revere the process of seepage, condensation, and collection. The "Drip-Listeners" are a semi-monastic order who spend their days in the deep reservoirs, meditating on the rhythm of the drops, interpreting the subtle shifts in the water's song as omens of surface storms or geological stress. To waste water is not a crime against the city; it is a blasphemy against the slow, patient generosity of the stone itself.

**Social Structure: The Guilds of Excavation and Flow**
Kharosi society is structured around the two great engines of their existence: expansion and water management.

*   **The Diggers' Guild:** The most prestigious and dangerous profession. These are the citizens who work the excavation frontier. They are part engineer, part geologist, part mystic. They can read the bedding planes of the rock, listening to the stone's stress with sensitive instruments and their own bare hands, predicting fractures before they happen. They speak of "negotiating" with the mountain, offering it "gifts" of worked stone in some areas to persuade it to yield in others. To be a Digger is to be at the very tip of the city's becoming, tasting the raw, unfiltered future.

*   **The Water-Bearers' Guild:** The silent administrators of the city's lifeblood. They manage the cisterns, maintain the condensation collectors, and enforce the strict Hydrological Law with quiet, implacable authority. Their leader, the "Master of Drops," holds a power that is subtle but absolute. The most feared punishment, exile to the Dry Galleries, is not a death sentence, but a sentence of sensory deprivation so profound it is considered a fate worse than death—a return to the silence and stillness the city is forever struggling against.

*   **The Keepers of the Hearth:** A guild of fire-keepers and air-managers. They control the kilns that drive the ventilation, manage the precious oil for the lamps, and cultivate the phosphorescent fungi. They are the masters of the city's scheduled light and breath, the ones who ensure the city continues to inhale and exhale.

**The Stranger's Perspective**
An outsider arriving in Kharos would find the inhabitants unnervingly quiet and still. They would seem cold and distant, their large, dark eyes unreadable in the dim light. The outsider would feel the constant, subtle pressure of their presence, the sense of being in a space that is not just inhabited, but *saturated* with consciousness. To speak to a Kharosi is to engage in a quiet, intense conversation where every word is weighed, every silence is meaningful. They would offer you water not from a wineskin, but from a small, carefully measured ceramic cup, watching you drink with the focused intensity of a host sharing a sacred substance. They are not unfriendly, but their friendship is like their city: it must be excavated slowly, patiently, with respect for the unseen structures that lie beneath the surface. They are the embodiment of their home: cool, dense, quiet, and perpetually, patiently, becoming something more.
