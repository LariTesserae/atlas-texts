The inhabitants are not people. They are not even animals in the way you understand animals, with their separable skins and individual names. They are the **greenness itself**, fragmented into mobile organs, and they are **numberless as leaves**.

Start with the **Sound-bearers**, for you hear them before you see them, and you never stop hearing them. They fill the air like living static, like weather made flesh. They are the size of your hand, or your forearm, or your entire torso, and they are built from contradictions: membrane and cartilage, hollow bone and resonating chambers that open and close like mouths. They have wings that are not wings but sails, stretched between elongated fingers that are actually ribs, and when they beat them they do not fly so much as *swim* through the thick, warm air. Their thoraxes are instrument chambers, drum-taut skins stretched over hollows where air vibrates at frequencies that make your teeth ache and your vision blur. They circle the fungal towers in flocks that are actually single entities, a thousand bodies sharing one magnetic consciousness, their shrieks and drones layering into that pressure you feel against your eardrums. They are the city’s voice, its tinnitus, its constant declarative statement of *I am here, I am here, I am here*. They eat the golden sap directly from the walls, piercing the transparent streets with needle-tongues, and they excrete a glittering dust that fertilizes the moss below. When they die—and they die constantly, torn apart by the flowering vines or simply exhausting their resonating chambers—their bodies fall not to the ground but into the open throats of the pitcher-plants, and the Sound loses one voice among ten thousand, and does not diminish.

Below them, in the dappled spaces between the Arteries, move the **grazers**. These are the soft things, the mammalian approximations, though they have six legs where your world demands four, and their eyes are not eyes but clusters of dew-sensitive cells that weep constantly, refracting the light into violet and green. They are the size of deer, or dogs, or sometimes small horses, and they are the color of fog, of pearl, of the inside of a shell. They move by **stepping between**, disappearing from one shadow and appearing in another, browsing on the phosphorescent moss that coats the vertical surfaces in patterns that shift when unobserved. Their mouths are tender, lipless, designed for scraping rather than biting, and they make a sound like tearing silk when they feed. They are prey, but they are also gardeners; their digestive tracts cultivate the spores of the bioluminescent fungi, and where they defecate—which they do while walking, while stepping between—new growth erupts in violent, beautiful spirals. They are not intelligent. They are **attentive**. They know the thin places in reality, the doorways that open into chambers larger than their containers, and they use these shortcuts to escape the **flowering vines**.

Ah, the vines. They are the city’s **hands**, if the city had hands that were also stomachs. They hang from the canopy like decorative garlands, beautiful with petals the color of fresh blood or old gold, and they wait with the patience of geological time. But when a grazer steps too close, or a Sound-bearer flies too low, the vines snap shut with a hydraulic *crack* that echoes through the Empty Places like a gunshot. They are not plants. They are **events**. They digest slowly, over weeks, their victims visible through translucent petals, struggling, then softening, then becoming liquid that the city drinks through the same root-mass floors you walk upon. The vines have no eyes, but they have **memory**; they learn the patterns of the grazers, the rhythms of the flocks, and they position themselves accordingly, growing thicker where the traffic is dense.

In the pools—those active, shivering collections of warm rain—dwell the **waiters**. You might mistake them for eels, for snakes, for something aquatic, but they are amphibious in the truest sense, able to haul their transparent flesh onto the root-mass banks and lie there, gill-lungs fluttering, for hours. They are the length of your leg, or longer, and their bodies are glass-clear, revealing the slow pulse of organs that are themselves translucent, the golden sap of the city moving through them in sluggish tides. Their jaws are not built for speed but for **patience**, hinged in ways that allow them to open impossibly wide, to wait with mouths agape until a grazer steps between and lands *inside* them. They are the silence beneath the Sound, the pause between heartbeats. When they move, it is with the slowness of sap, but when they strike, it is with the suddenness of a dropped stone.

Deeper in, in the Processing Wards where the air smells of ozone and sweet rot, the **digesters** work. These are not individuals but **colonies**, great pitcher-plants the size of houses, as you noted, but within them dwell the smaller things: the larvae of the Sound-bearers, wingless and legless, swimming in the digestive fluid and breaking down the dead with enzymes that could dissolve stone. They are the city’s **cleaners**, its recycling system, and they sing too, in frequencies too low for your ears, a basso profundo that vibrates in the soles of your feet. They are born in the fluid, live in the fluid, and when they mature they crawl up the inner walls of the pitchers, sprout their resonating thoraxes, and join the flocks above, completing the cycle.

And in the Empty Places, where nothing grows and nothing builds, there dwell the **listeners**. You will not see them. They are too flat, too wide, too perfectly camouflaged against the bare, woven roots. They are the city’s **nerves**, perhaps, or its conscience. They spread themselves thin as shadows, as discolorations on the bark, and they absorb the Sound, processing it, filtering it, sending it back into the Arteries as golden pulses of information. They are the reason the city dreams. When you stand in the center plaza and feel that warmth rising from below, that sense of vast, slow thought, you are feeling the listeners **considering** you. They do not move. They do not feed. They simply **perceive**, and in perceiving, they maintain the thinness of reality, the flexible geometry that allows the grazers to step between, the doorways to open inward, the city to remain exactly the same size forever, neither growing nor shrinking, balanced on the edge of its own impossible biology.

All of these—the Sound-bearers, the grazers, the vines, the waiters, the digesters, the listeners—are the city. They are not separate species but **expressions**, temporary solidifications of the greenness, the vascular will that replaced the stone grid so long ago. They eat each other, they feed each other, they sing to each other, and in their singing and their eating and their stepping between the cracks in space, they maintain the equilibrium, the warmth, the terrible beautiful noise that is the only language the city knows, and the only language it needs.
