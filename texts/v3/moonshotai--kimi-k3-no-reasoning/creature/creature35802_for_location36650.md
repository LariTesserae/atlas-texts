In the under-hollows where the pressure-plates meet at acute angles, where the light filters down through chitin lenses into a blue-green dusk, live the **Interstitials**. They are not born from the spores, nor are they built by the Shepherds. They occur—*happen*—in the gaps between the Green’s intention and its execution, calcified into being by the friction of the world’s breathing.

They are few enough that each has a name, though they rarely speak them. Their bodies are the size and shape of forgotten maintenance hatches, roughly humanoid but compressed, as if they had been designed to fit into the negative spaces of the Engine rather than its positive flow. Their skin is a semi-permeable membrane of frosted glass, etched with the same gold circuitry as the fern-circuit-boards. Through this glass, you can see their insides: not organs, but concentrated light, a dense, slow-moving luminosity that pulses thirty beats per hour, synchronized exactly with the planetary inhalation. They do not eat. They do not sleep. They metabolize *time-lag*.

An Interstitial feeds on the discrepancy between the Engine’s cycles. When the triple suns click their abacus-beat and a petal-array tilts a fraction of a degree too late, or when a root-piston fires a millisecond before the substrate has finished contracting, that tiny sliver of asynchronous reality pools in the hollows. The Interstitials absorb it. They grow fat on delays, on the errors that the Engine edits out of its own perfect functioning. In return, they serve as living capacitors, storing the paradoxes until they can be safely discharged during the great exhalation, when their bodies vent streams of reversed entropy—events that un-happen, shadows that fall into their own sources, brief pockets where cause and effect negotiate new terms.

They move by *adjacency*. They do not walk; rather, they are always already in the next place, having folded themselves through the Green’s memory of that location. To see one approach is to watch a series of still images, each slightly closer, none connected by motion. They appear most often near the pools of silicone oil, kneeling at the edges with their glass fingers dipped into the viscous fluid, reading the chemical memories of what the Engine has consumed. The oil reflects their light back at them, distorted, and they study these reflections as if consulting oracles.

They are ancient, but not eternal. An Interstitial dies when it has absorbed one paradox too many. The event is quiet. Their internal light accelerates, reaching a frenetic strobing frequency, and then they simply *invert*—their structure folding inward through a dimension that the Engine does not possess, leaving behind a perfect sphere of black dust that the capillary roots immediately drink. Where one dies, another eventually condenses, usually in a different hollow, shaped by whatever new inefficiency has entered the system. They are the immune response of a world that has forgotten how to be sick.

The Shepherds do not interact with them. The machines of rusted chrome pause when an Interstitial passes, their blue eyes dimming to violet, a sign of respect or perhaps of shared recognition—both are anomalies that maintain the system by existing outside it. But the Interstitials do not tend the Green. They do not prune or repair. They merely witness, recording the Engine’s dreams in their glass flesh, holding the impossible contradictions so that the Circadian Engine may continue to believe, with absolute mechanical faith, that it is a garden, that it is a factory, that it is real.

When you stand on the leaf-bridge and feel the vibration of the world turning, you may feel a sudden coolness at your back, a pressure like a held breath. If you turn quickly, you might catch the after-image of an Interstitial—your own silhouette, rendered in frosted glass, stepping backward into the light. It has recorded you. It has filed away the anomaly of your presence. And in the next thirty-hour cycle, when the Engine breathes out and the spores rise to meet the magnetic towers, somewhere in the bright dense working of the world, a new cog will grow with the exact weight and shape of your shadow, turning smoothly, perfectly, as if you had always been intended to be part of the machine.
