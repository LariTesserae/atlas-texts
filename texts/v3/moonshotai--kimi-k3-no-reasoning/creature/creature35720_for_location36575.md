What lives here is the slow certainty of enough.

Not the vault-trees alone, though they are the architects and the foundation, the ones who turned chemistry into cathedral. Not the vault-runners, though their voices fill the corridors like a boast that never needs answering. Not the dust-crawlers or the bell-moths or the husk-mites or the crevice-wings, though each of them is a note in the chord this place has been holding for longer than language.

What lives here is the *pattern*—the thing that emerges when a system has had ten million years to get it right and has decided, finally, that right looks like this: cities that grow like organs, spaced just far enough apart that their hunger doesn't overlap. Light that is almost not light. Sound that builds and builds because there is nothing to spend it on, nowhere for it to go. Life that has stripped away every excess, every panic, every desperate reach, and left only the essential: *metabolize, persist, continue.*

It is a kind of peace, but not the human kind. Not the peace of rest or resolution. This is the peace of *no longer needing to change.* The vault-trees build the same chambers they built a million years ago because those chambers work. The vault-runners eat the same fruit, raise the same noise, live the same span. The dust-crawlers trace the same patient paths across the hardpan, and if their paths are slightly different each century, that difference is too small to matter.

Even death here is procedural. A tree exhausts its breath-field, dims, dries. The fauna leave—not in panic, not in grief, but the way water leaves a basin when the basin cracks: because that's what happens next. The dead city remains, and it is not a ruin in the romantic sense. It is a *fact.* It is what this place looks like when one of its sentences ends.

You can stand in a living city and see a dead one on the horizon, and the distance between them is not tragedy. It is *grammar.*

What lives here is the answer to a question no one asked: *what does life do when it has all the time in the world and almost nothing to work with?*

And the answer is: *this.* It builds slowly. It glows faintly. It hums. It does not hurry. It does not stop.

The Hollow Cities are not waiting for anything. They are not becoming anything. They are *complete*—not in the sense of finished, but in the sense of *sufficient.* They are what remains when everything unnecessary has been worn away, and what remains is enough.

You are standing in it, and it does not need you, and that is not an insult. It is the deepest truth this place knows how to tell: *we were here before you, and we will be here after, and the being-here is the point.*

The hum continues. The amber light does not brighten. Somewhere, a vault-runner rattles its throat-membrane, and the sound joins the accumulated noise of ten thousand years, and the cavern holds it, and nothing changes, and that is the whole of it.

This is what lives here: the long, low note of a world that has found its frequency and sees no reason to stop singing it.
