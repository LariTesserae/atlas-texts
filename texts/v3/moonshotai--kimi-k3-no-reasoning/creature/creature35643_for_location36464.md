# The Howling Borough

---

## What Lives Here

Nothing built this place. Nothing *could* build it—there are no hands here, no tools, no intention. And yet it is unmistakably a **city**: dense, structured, zoned, thrumming with the urgent energy of a million inhabitants crammed into a warm, low basin where the horizon is never visible because something is always growing in front of it.

The inhabitants are the city itself.

---

## The Citizenry

### The Tower-Kind

The skyline belongs to the **columnar giants**—vast, braided organisms that are not quite trees, not quite vines, but something the valley invented in its frenzy. They rise in fused bundles of vascular tissue, each "strand" a separate plant that has surrendered its autonomy to the collective structure. They do not grow *toward* light; they grow *toward each other*, tangling into load-bearing knots that hold for days, sometimes weeks, before the internal pressure becomes too great and the whole tower **detonates outward** in a shower of sap, splinters, and spore-clouds thick enough to blot the sun.

These collapses are not failures. They are **reproduction**. The wreckage seeds the next tower.

Within the hollow cores of older columnars live entire **secondary communities**: pendant moss-curtains that filter the damp air, shelf-fungi that glow a dull amber, and translucent bladder-plants that inflate with captured humidity and then **pop**—a percussive *crack* that echoes through the valley like gunfire, adding to the ceaseless roar.

### The Road-Makers

There are paths here, but they are not cleared. They are **grown**. A species of low, fast-spreading rhizome-mat produces a hard, dark surface—almost like bark, almost like skin—that other plants seem to avoid. These mats form branching networks across the valley floor, connecting tower-clusters the way roads connect districts. They pulse faintly, carrying chemical signals from one end of the valley to the other. Where two mats intersect, the tissue thickens into a **node**—a swollen, warm hump that oozes nutrient-rich sap, attracting a riot of opportunistic growth: ferns unfurling in seconds, carnivorous bladders snapping at spores, fungi blooming in concentric rings of impossible color.

These nodes are the valley's **public squares**—chaotic, overgrown, reconfigured hourly.

### The Screamers

The deafening sound has many sources, but the loudest inhabitants are the **hollow-reed colonies** that colonize every vertical surface. These are thin, jointed stalks—each one a separate organism, but growing in such dense clusters that they function as a single instrument. Wind passes through them, and they **howl**. Not a whistle, not a moan—a full-throated, multi-octave **roar** that shifts in pitch as the reeds grow, their lengths changing minute by minute. A grove that sang bass at dawn shrieks soprano by dusk.

Beneath this, the **sap-geysers** provide percussion: overpressurized vascular systems in the larger tower-kind rupture periodically, sending geysers of sticky, sweet fluid thirty feet into the air with a sound like a cannon firing underwater. The valley floor is slick with this exudate, and where it pools, new life erupts within minutes—pale, translucent shoots that have never existed before and may never exist again, born of whatever chemical cocktail the geyser happened to carry.

## The Strange Physics of the Place

This is where the valley stops obeying.

**Gravity is inconsistent.** Not absent—*inconsistent*. A fallen branch may lie on the root-mat floor for an hour, then slowly, deliberately, rise and drift sideways, catching on a tower-wall and rooting there. Water in the shallow canals sometimes flows *uphill*, pooling in elevated root-basins that should not be able to hold it. The plants do not seem to notice. They have adapted to a world where **down is a suggestion**.

**Time is uneven.** Growth here is already impossibly fast, but it is not *uniformly* fast. A fern at your feet may go through its entire life cycle—unfurl, sporulate, wither—in the time it takes a nearby vine to extend a single inch. Standing still is dangerous: you may find yourself **anchored** by roots that were not there a moment ago, while the structure you were leaning against has already collapsed and been reabsorbed.

**Boundaries dissolve.** Two tower-kind may grow into each other and **merge**—not graft, but genuinely *fuse*, their vascular systems intertwining until it is impossible to tell where one organism ends and another begins. This happens at every scale. The entire valley may, in some biological sense, be a **single organism** arguing with itself.

---

## The Texture of Life Here

To move through the Howling Borough is to be **touched constantly**. Fronds brush your face. Tendrils curl around your wrist—not aggressively, just *curiously*, tasting your chemistry before losing interest. The ground yields underfoot like flesh. The air is so thick with spores and moisture that breathing feels like **drinking**.

The warmth is biological—**metabolic heat**, generated by the sheer mass of growing, respiring, decomposing tissue. It radiates from the root-mat floor, from the tower-walls, from the composting undersides of fallen fronds. The valley is warm the way a body is warm.

And the sound—the sound never stops. It is layered: the bass drone of the reed colonies, the mid-range groan of straining wood, the high shriek of splitting bark, the staccato pop of bladder-plants, the wet percussion of sap-geysers, the rustle-crash of a tower collapsing three districts over. It is not music. It is not noise. It is the sound of **life refusing to be quiet**, a roar so total that silence, if it ever came, would feel like death.

---

## Who Thrives Here

The successful inhabitants share certain traits:

**Speed.** Slow things are buried. The valley rewards those that can grow, adapt, reproduce, and die within hours. Longevity is a liability—a slow-growing hardwood would be smothered before it reached knee height.

**Loudness.** Not metaphorically. The plants that dominate are those whose growth processes generate the most **physical vibration**—snapping, hissing, booming. Sound is a byproduct of metabolic violence, and metabolic violence is the valley's only currency.

**Flexibility.** Rigid things break. The tower-kind survive not because they are strong but because they are **woven**—thousands of individual strands, each expendable, each replaceable. When a section fails, the others compensate. This is not design. It is the evolutionary logic of a world where **collapse is constant**.

**Indifference to physics.** The most successful species are those that have stopped "expecting" gravity to work consistently, that root equally well on ceilings as on floors, that can reorient their growth direction in seconds when the local rules change.

---

## The Feeling of the Place

The Howling Borough is not hostile. It is not welcoming. It is **indifferent in the way that only something overwhelmingly alive can be**—too busy growing, dying, screaming, merging, collapsing, and regrowing to notice anything as small and slow as a visitor.

It is a city with no citizens, only **tissue**. A metropolis with no architecture, only **growth patterns**. A wilderness with no silence, only the **deafening, warm, wet roar** of a billion living things doing what living things do, stripped of every constraint—no predators, no seasons, no stone to anchor them, no laws to hold them.

Only growth. Only sound. Only the **turbulent, golden-green now**.
