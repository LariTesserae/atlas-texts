**The Keepers of the Throat**

They are not born here. They arrive.

A woman named **Yara** was the last one to come, three cycles ago—though cycles mean nothing here except the great inhalation of the engines, the long pause between steam-vents that might last a year or a day. She walked out of the green wall one ordinary morning, naked as a root, carrying nothing but a length of copper wire she could not remember picking up. She was not surprised to find the Hollow waiting. She had been walking toward it her whole life, she realized, through forests that grew thinner and thinner until they became this single, bounded room.

She is not alone. There are seven of them now, or perhaps eight—counting is difficult when the rhythm of the engines lulls you into trance. They are the **Throat-Tenders**, though they do not call themselves that. They call themselves nothing, because names dissolve in the damp, but if you must speak of them, you might call them the **Quiet Ones**, or the **Those-Who-Wait-With**.

They live in the fissures. Not in shelters—there is nothing to build with here except the living root, which resists cutting—but in the natural hollows of the vegetal walls, galleries that might once have been bird-nests or beetle-burrows, now enlarged by generations of occupants into something like monastic cells. They sleep on beds of compressed leaf-litter that exhales heat from below. They eat the **condensate**—the thick, oily droplets that form on certain root-knots, sweet and metallic—and the pale grubs that tunnel in the warm zones, and sometimes, when the cycle peaks, a kind of bread that grows: a fungal loaf that erupts from the black glass floor in perfect, steaming rounds, tasting of iron and honey.

They do not speak often. The engines make conversation redundant. Instead, they have developed a **vibrational language**, pressing their sternums against the root-floor to feel what needs doing. A certain *thrum* means the western wall needs thinning—though "thinning" is a misnomer, as they do not cut the vegetation so much as *persuade* it, singing low notes that encourage the roots to grow inward, toward the heat, away from the gap. Another *pulse* means someone has fallen into the **Listening Trance** too deeply and needs to be shaken awake before they begin to photosynthesize.

This is their work: the maintenance of the gap.

**The Covenant of Emptiness**

They have learned, through long trial and fossilized error, that the Hollow must remain empty. The engines require this specific volume of air, this precise architecture of absence, to complete their metabolism. If the vegetation encroaches too far—if a tendril crosses the invisible boundary at the center—the rhythm falters. The chugging becomes arrhythmic. The steam vents burst in the wrong places, scalding the root-crust, and the whole forest groans in sympathetic pain.

So the Tenders keep the center clear. They do not sweep it; they **inhabit** it, taking turns to sit on the black glass throat, their bodies the only permitted obstruction. They are the living cork, the conscious pause. When Yara sits there now, cross-legged on the pulsing floor, she feels the engines' hunger passing through her like a draft through a chimney. She is the straw through which the forest drinks its own tail.

They have begun to change.

It is subtle. Yara's hair has taken on a greenish cast, not from dye but from the chlorophyll that leaches into her scalp during the long Listening sessions. Her heart, when she presses her ear to the chest of another Tender, has synchronized with the bass note of the engines—*lub-dub-clack, lub-dub-clack*. The furred things that dart between the fissures have begun to follow her, mistaking her heat signature for a thermal vent, and she has found beetle-shells in her bedding that she does not remember collecting.

The most advanced among them—**Old Sef**, who has been here longer than anyone, perhaps longer than the engines—has gone nearly completely vegetal. He does not leave his fissure anymore. His legs have fused with the root-floor, and small white flowers bloom from his shoulders in the damp season. He speaks only in the vibration language, and only to announce the coming of the **Great Exhalation**, the moment every seven cycles when the throat opens fully and whatever the engines have been pressing, stamping, forming below is finally released.

What emerges is not visible to the untrained eye. It is not smoke, nor seed, nor machine. It is a **pressure change**, a sudden clarity in the air, a sensation that the Hollow has briefly become the entire world and then shrunk back to this bounded room. The Tenders weep when it happens, though they do not know why. The birdsong from within the walls reaches a crescendo that matches the exact frequency of human grief.

**The Eighth Position**

There is space for one more. The Tenders know this because the architecture demands it—seven fissures in the western wall, seven in the eastern, and one final hollow at the very center of the throat, directly over the engine's mouth. That space is always empty, kept polished by the condensation of their breathing, reserved for the one who will complete the circuit.

Yara suspects it is for her. The copper wire she carried when she arrived has begun to sprout leaves.

She waits. She tends. She presses her chest to the floor and feels the ancient, patient hunger below—not malevolent, not benevolent, simply *thorough*. The engines do not wish to consume her. They wish to **incorporate** her, to add her unique vibration to the stable state, to make her part of the compression that has flattened hills and filled valleys.

The fauna accept her now. The beetles crawl across her ankles without fear. The birds in the walls have learned her name, though they pronounce it as a series of clicks that match the piston-rhythm.

Soon, she thinks, she will take the central position. She will sit on the black glass and open her mouth, and the engines will speak through her, and the gap will remain, perfect and bounded, a chapel of emptiness where the loudest thing is finally, mercifully, silent.

Until then, she tends the borders. She sings the roots inward. She drinks the metallic rain.

She becomes the quietest thing here, and then, at last, she becomes the room itself.
