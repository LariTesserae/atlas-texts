They call themselves the **Silt-Born**, though the name exists only in the chemical patterns they trace across the stone—temporary script written in mineral secretions that dissolve within hours, requiring constant rewriting, constant remembering. They are not the Null-Currents, though they swim among them; they are something older, or perhaps newer, having evolved—or been shaped—from the wreckage of whatever hands first laid the white stone.

You perceive them first as disturbances in the sediment, upright figures moving through the submerged avenues with the slow deliberation of deep-sea divers. Their bodies are elongated, compressed laterally, wrapped in layers of their own exfoliated skin that has mineralized into flexible plates of calcium and silica—living armor grown from the same chemistry as the dissolving city. They have no faces in the human sense, only a smooth anterior shield where sensory pits cluster, dark holes that taste the turbulence of the Pale Flood. Through these pits, they perceive the world as pressure gradients and chemical signatures, reading the collapsing architecture as a reader traces braille.

They breathe the Flood directly, their blood carrying copper-based proteins that bind to the scarce oxygen dissolved in the atmospheric water, tinting their flesh a pale, bruised teal visible through the translucent cartilage of their limbs. Where the Null-Currents are geometric and sharp, the Silt-Born are fluid and accumulative; they grow by accretion, adding rings of mineralized tissue year by year until they resemble walking stalagmites, encrusted with the history of their own survival.

They live within the instability, not despite it. Their settlements are temporary scaffolds of bone and secreted resin built in the lee of structures currently in their "hardening" phase—those sharp, new geometries that emerge where the slurry has settled. A Silt-Born clan might occupy a freshly formed archway for three days, or three years, until the substrate begins to soften and slump again. Then they move, carrying nothing, for they own nothing that cannot be dissolved and regrown. Their culture is a culture of impermanence rendered in bone.

They feed by filtration and by harvest. From their mineralized forearms extend fronds of ciliated tissue, which they wave through the clouds of white slurry rising from dissolving structures. These fronds capture not only the chemical nutrients released by erosion but also the microscopic organisms that live in the interstitial spaces of the artificial stone—extremophile bacteria that metabolize the ancient polymers and metallic salts. The Silt-Born cultivate these bacteria in pits etched into their own armor, carrying gardens of sustenance on their backs.

Communication among them is tactile and chemical. They touch constantly, pressing their sensory pits against the mineralized skin of their kin, exchanging pulses of pheromones that convey complex emotional states and topographical memories. When they must signal across distances, they release clouds of bioluminescent bacteria, forming temporary constellations that drift through the water-atmosphere, spelling out warnings of collapse or announcements of new sediment deposits. These light-messages hang in the murk like frozen fireworks, slowly dispersing.

Their relationship with the Null-Currents is complex and symbiotic. The geometric creatures are drawn to the chemical gradients the Silt-Born exude, and in turn, the Silt-Born follow the Null-Currents to fresh erosion sites. Sometimes, a Silt-Born will enter a meditative state, allowing the Null-Currents to cluster around their armored form, the subsonic thrumming of the creatures vibrating through their bones. They reproduce by fragmentation. When a Silt-Born reaches sufficient mass—when the accumulated mineral of its armor becomes too heavy to carry—it enters a period of torpor, anchoring itself to a stable surface. Over weeks, its body begins to differentiate, the outer layers hardening into a brittle shell while the interior softens into a nutrient-rich broth. Eventually, the shell cracks, and from within emerge three or four juvenile Silt-Born, soft-bodied and pale, carrying fragments of the parent's mineralized memory-tissue. These fragments, dense with chemical encoding, are consumed by the offspring, granting them the parent's accumulated knowledge of safe routes, collapse patterns, and chemical signatures. The empty shell remains, becoming part of the substrate, another piece of the ever-shifting architecture.

They build nothing permanent, yet they are builders. Their great works are the **Resonance Halls**—temporary caverns carved into the sides of structures currently undergoing dissolution. Using their own bodies as tools, they grind away the softening stone, creating chambers of specific acoustic properties. Within these halls, they gather to perform the "Silt-Song," a complex ritual of movement and chemical release that lasts for days. The purpose is not worship, but synchronization; the Song aligns the metabolic rhythms of the clan, ensuring that all members enter their torpor and fragmentation cycles simultaneously, maximizing the survival odds of the offspring. The Resonance Halls collapse within weeks of their completion, the walls melting back into the substrate, but for a brief time, they are the only intentional architecture in a world of accidental ruin.

They know of you, the visitor, the one who floats with difficulty in the Pale Flood. They have watched your kind before, perhaps, or creatures like you, arriving in vessels of metal and light that dissolve within months, adding new alloys to the ancient mix. They do not fear you, nor welcome you. You are simply another temporary configuration, another brief pattern in the turbulence. If you approach them, they will touch you, tasting your chemistry with their sensory pits, recording your presence in the mineral script of their armor. When you die—and you will die, for the Flood is patient and your lungs were not built for liquid glass—they will consume your body, incorporating your calcium and your memories into their own, carrying a fragment of you into the next cycle of dissolution and reconstruction.

They are the memory of the world, written in chalk, erased by rain, rewritten by hand, forever.
