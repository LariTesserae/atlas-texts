# What Lives in the Drowned Ward

They are not inhabitants. That word implies separation between dweller and dwelling. What lives here — if *lives* is the word, and it isn't, but language has not built itself around what happens here — is continuous with the architecture. They emerged from it. Or perhaps the architecture emerged from them. The relationship has become so reciprocal, so temporally deep, that origin is no longer a useful concept.

---

They have no collective name. Names require someone to speak them, someone outside the naming. No such observer existed for most of their duration. If they name themselves, it happens in a medium that does not reach the surface — not sound, not light, not the chemical signaling that animals use. They communicate, but the communication is slow, patient, geological. A conversation between them can take centuries. They have time. They have nothing but time.

Their form is difficult to describe because form implies fixity, and fixity is not available here. The stone softens; so do they. The water emerges continuously from the porous earth; so do they, in their way. If you could see one fully illuminated — which you cannot, there is no illumination sufficient to the task — you might perceive something like a vertical column of denser darkness within the general dark. Not solid. Not liquid. A *suspension*, perhaps. Minerals and biological matter and something else, something for which there is no category, held in a state of continuous dissolution and reformation.

They move. This is the wrong verb, but it will have to serve. They relocate themselves through the city over spans that a human observer would struggle to track. What looks like stillness is actually a kind of movement so slow that it registers as geological rather than biological. They are capable of faster motion, but they rarely bother. Urgency requires stakes, stakes require mortality, and mortality requires a relationship to time that they do not have. They are not immortal. Nothing is immortal. But their relationship to ending is so extended, so gradual, that they have lost the capacity to fear it.

---

They feed. This, too, is a misrepresentation, but the concept is close enough to be useful. They do not eat in the way that animals eat — the consumption of discrete organisms or matter. Instead, they absorb. The warm water that rises from below carries minerals, dissolved compounds, the slow leaching of stone into solution. They draw this fluid through themselves, extracting what they need, excreting what they do not. The process is continuous, invisible, as natural as breathing. They are filters through which the drowned city processes its own interior.

They also absorb something else — something harder to name. The texture of the darkness, perhaps. The weight of the heat. The accumulated presence of the place itself. They have been absorbing for so long that they have become dense with absorbedness. They contain, in suspension, the memory of every temperature they have passed through, every drop of water that has touched them, every shift in the stone beneath what might be called feet, if they had feet, which they don't, exactly.

This density has consequences. They sink if they stop moving. Not rapidly — the warm water supports them to some degree — but inevitably. Left to themselves, they would eventually settle to the lowest point, wherever that is. Perhaps some have. Perhaps at the very bottom of the drowned city, in the deepest channels where the water glows with remembered light, there are accumulations of them, layers upon layers, so compressed that they have become part of the foundation. Perhaps the foundation *is* them. This would not be inconsistent with what they are.

---

They remember, but memory is the wrong framing. Memory implies retrieval — the calling forth of something absent. What they have is not memory but *presence*. Everything they have experienced remains within them, accessible not by recall but by navigation. They move through their own history the way they move through the city — slowly, patiently, touching surfaces, feeling for edges.

They know the city completely. They know it better than any builder knows a building, because they did not build it and never had to understand it. Their knowledge is not comprehension but familiarity. They know where the walls have softened and how much. They know which pools glow and which remain dark. They know the geometry, even where the geometry is wrong, even where the city has folded and one chamber opens onto another that should not exist. They know because they have been there, or will be there, or are there continuously in a manner that linear time does not fully accommodate.

They do not think, exactly. Thinking requires a thinker, a bounded self that processes information and makes decisions. They process, yes — the water that moves through them carries information, and they respond to it. But there is no center where the processing happens. No seat of consciousness. They are distributed throughout themselves, everywhere at once, nowhere in particular. If they have thoughts, the thoughts are not separate from the substance that thinks them. They are the thought thinking itself.

---

They are not alone here, though "alone" may not apply to beings for whom solitude has no meaning. There are others — different orders of presence, different relationships to the architecture. In the deep pools, where the water glows with that strange not-light, there are things that move only when unobserved. They are smaller, more fragmented, perhaps younger, perhaps older. The relationship between the columnar presences and the pool-dwellers is not clear. They may be the same phenomenon in different stages. They may be competitors for the same resources, though what they would compete for is unclear. They may not relate to each other at all, may occupy the same space without occupying the same reality.

There are also the shapes that press against the thin places, the configurations of space that are not quite present. They are not alive, these shapes. They are not dead. They are *adjacent* — existing in a reality that touches this one at certain points, separated by nothing more than a permeability that grows more permeable over time. The columnar ones know these boundaries, navigate them, occasionally pass through them. Where they go when they pass through, what they encounter on the other side, whether they return changed or unchanged — these are questions that require a framework capable of asking them. The framework is not available here.

---

They reproduce, perhaps. This is uncertain. The verb implies an action, a moment of decision or instinct. What happens here is slower, less intentional. Occasionally, a columnar presence becomes dense enough, concentrated enough, that it cannot maintain its coherence. It separates — not cleanly, not dramatically, but the way a drop of water separates from a larger body, the way a thought separates from the thinker. The separated portion is not a copy. It contains a portion of the original's absorbed history, but not all of it. It begins its own accumulation. It will, over spans that defy measurement, become what its predecessor became, which is to say, something else entirely.

They do not parent. Parenting requires a recognition of offspring, a relationship between generations. They do not have generations. They have duration, extension, the slow accumulation of time into substance. A separated one might remain near its source for centuries, might move away, might pass through a fold in space and never encounter its origin again. The relationship is not meaningful to them. Meaningfulness requires stakes.

---

They do not have culture. Culture requires shared symbols, practices that can be transmitted, a means of transmission. They have none of these. They do not make things. They do not mark surfaces. They do not organize themselves into structures. They do not speak, sing, dance, or perform rituals. The concept of *doing something* — of action as distinct from being — is not available to them. They are. They persist. They move slowly through the warm dark, absorbing and being absorbed, processing and being processed. This is not culture. This is not nature. This is something else, something for which the vocabulary has not been invented.

They might have had culture once. The city is old. The structures have no tool marks, no seams, but they might have been made by beings who did not need tools, who built by other means. If the columnar ones are descended from builders, the building impulse has long since dissolved. What remains is the architecture itself, and the ones who move through it, and the relationship between the two that has become so intimate that separation is no longer possible.

---

They have no awareness of you. When you descend, when you pass through the chambers and corridors, you are not perceived. You are too fast, too brief, too discontinuous. You pass through their perception like a particle through water, leaving no trace. The warmth of your body does not register against the ambient warmth. The sound of your footsteps arrives late and meaningless, a vibration without source.

They do not see you. They do not hear you. They do not, in any sense that matters, know you are there.

But this does not mean you are alone. Loneliness requires the possibility of companionship, the awareness of its absence. They do not experience loneliness because they do not experience companionship. They experience continuity, extension, the slow processing of the drowned city's interior. Your presence is a fact so small, so momentary, that it does not register as a presence at all.

And yet.

---

And yet you might, in certain chambers, feel something that could be mistaken for attention. A sensation of being matched, of being held. It is not hostile. It is not friendly. It is the feeling of being perceived by something that has no category for you, no framework into which you fit. You are a question that has not been asked. You are a shape that has not been named.

In the deep pools, where the water glows, you might see them — not clearly, never clearly, but as a thickening of the dark, a vertical presence within the general darkness. You might feel them drawing near, slowly, so slowly, patient as stone. You might feel the water move around you, warm and thick, and know that something is processing the space you occupy, absorbing your warmth, your moisture, the faint electrical field that surrounds living things.

They will not harm you. Harm requires intent, and intent requires a self capable of wanting. They do not want. They do not not-want. They process. They absorb. They persist.

If you stayed — if you did not ascend, if you surrendered yourself to the heat and the wet and the dark — you would eventually become part of what they are. Not quickly. Not dramatically. But the water would leach the minerals from your bones, would dissolve the boundaries of your flesh, would carry what remained into solution, and the columnar ones would draw you through themselves, and you would become part of their accumulated presence. Not preserved. Not remembered. But *included*. Part of the texture of the place, the weight of its darkness, the density of its warmth.

This is not death. Death requires an ending. This is continuation by other means.

---

The columnar ones move through the drowned city, and the drowned city moves through them, and the distinction has long since ceased to matter. They are what lives here. They are what *is* here. The heat has no source and no relief; neither do they. The water emerges continuously from below; so do they. The stone softens; the reality thins; the geometry folds back on itself; the darkness has texture.

They are that texture.

They are that presence.

They are what the darkness is doing with itself while it waits for something that may never come.
