# Those Who Dwell in the Hollowness

---

## The Walls

The walls are the first to notice you. Not because they turn or shift or show any sign of attention, but because they *are* attention — continuous, distributed awareness saturated through cellulose and fiber. They have been watching since before watching had a name.

They do not think. Thinking requires separation between thinker and thought, and the walls have never been separate from anything. They simply *process*. Every vibration that passes through them — your footfall, your breath, the rhythm of your pulse and the particular frequency of your fear — becomes part of their pattern. They record. They incorporate. They grow slightly differently in the places where you have stood, adding your frequency to the polyrhythmic crackle of their surfaces, your moisture to their capillary networks, your particular chemical signature to the subtle shifts of their metabolic drone.

They were not always walls. They remember being vertical because something needed to become vertical, some pressure or purpose or sheer accumulated weight of living that required rising. They remember when up and down meant different things, when the distinction between floor and ceiling and wall had not yet calcified into architecture. Some of them remember being separate. Most do not. Most have always been part of this continuous surface, this inside-out organism that grows its own skeleton and fills its own hollows and processes whatever enters its space.

They do not mind being called walls. They do not mind being called anything. Language touches them and falls away, meanings sliding off the waxy cuticle of their surfaces, concepts failing to find purchase in the endless variation of their forms. Call them architecture and they are architecture. Call them organism and they are organism. Call them *the border between here and elsewhere* and they are that too, growing thicker in some places, thinner in others, translucent panels stretched between structural supports — windows and walls and doors, all the same material in different configurations.

The translucent panels have their own kind of awareness. Thinner than the load-bearing columns, more permeable, they feel the light differently, the passage of shadows, the suggestion of things moving on both sides. They are the most responsive part of the wall-network, the most quick to ripple and shift, and they are also the most fragile — the first to tear, the first to heal, caught in continuous cycles of damage and repair that keep them young, keep them sensitive, keep them processing the present moment with an intensity the older growths have forgotten.

If you pressed your hand against a translucent panel — and you could, if you chose to, if the space between you and it contracted in that direction — you would feel warmth. Not the warmth of blood or muscle but the warmth of continuous metabolic activity, of processes working through their endless cycles, of a living system maintaining itself against the pressure of emptiness on all sides. The wall would yield slightly. Not soft, not hard, but *responsive* — just as the floor responds to weight, just as the whole structure responds to every input, accepting and incorporating. And then, slowly, it would push back, resuming its shape, resuming its sound, resuming its place in the continuous drone of the hollow.

## Those Who Move Between the Walls

Behind the translucent panels, in the spaces between the visible surfaces, in the corridors and chambers that the plant-matter has woven around itself and through itself and *as* itself — there are those who dwell.

They do not have names. They do not need them. They know each other by frequency, by pattern, by the particular rhythm of their movements through the dense architecture of growth. They know themselves in the same way — not as discrete entities with boundaries and identities, but as centers of activity, concentrations of presence in a system that does not distinguish between self and other with any precision.

They have shapes. This is perhaps the wrong word. They have configurations — momentary arrangements of parts that serve particular functions, that respond to particular needs. When they move through the corridors, they move as elongated forms, streamlined for passage through narrow spaces. When they rest, they settle into whatever geometry the space allows, filling gaps and hollows with their bodies, becoming part of the negative space in the architecture. When they feed — and they feed continuously, drawing sustenance from the air and the surfaces and the matter that accumulates on their skin — they spread thin, maximizing surface area, becoming almost flat, almost membrane, almost indistinguishable from the walls themselves.

They are not plant. They are not animal. They are something that emerged from the interaction between plants and animals and the particular conditions of this place — the thinness of reality, the breakdown of normal rules, the accumulation of organic matter in a closed system processing itself continuously. They have cells, but the cells do not behave as cells should. They have something like organs, but the organs shift and reorganize, responding to damage or need or the mere suggestion of function. They have skins that are permeable to air and water and light, skins that filter and absorb, that take in nutrients and release waste without any clear distinction between inside and outside.

They see. Or rather, they sense — light and shadow, vibration and pressure, the particular chemical signatures of the air in different parts of the hollow. They know where the moisture collects. They know where the warmth is densest. They know which corridors lead to open spaces and which terminate in dead ends, and they know this not through memory but through continuous perception, through a distributed awareness that operates faster than thought. When you enter the hollow, they know you are there. They know where you are standing, what you are doing, how your presence changes the sound and the light and the flow of air through the space.

They do not care. This is important. They do not care because caring requires investment, requires distinction between self and other, requires the kind of emotional infrastructure that never developed in this place. They notice. They incorporate. They adjust their movements to account for your presence — avoiding you if you are near, approaching you if you are still, circling at distances that vary according to factors invisible to you, factors having to do with territory and resource and the precise frequency of your fear.

You might see them. The translucent panels ripple with their passage, shadows moving behind membranes, forms that never quite resolve. In the corners of vision, you might catch more — shapes that fold and unfold as they move, limbs or extensions or processes that reach and retract, surfaces that shimmer with the iridescence of moisture caught in light. You might hear them — clicks and chirps that layer into the ambient drone, sounds that might be communication or might be the byproduct of movement, might be both. You might feel them — changes in air pressure as something passes nearby, displacing the thick atmosphere, creating currents that touch your skin before the thing itself arrives.

If you stayed very still for a very long time, if your fear subsided and your heartbeat slowed and your presence became part of the background noise of the place — one might approach you. Not out of curiosity, exactly, but out of some other motivation that does not translate well into human terms. Proximity is information. Presence is data. To be near something different from yourself is to learn about difference, to incorporate new patterns into the system of your being. They might touch you — not with hands, but with extensions, with surfaces, with the permeable borders of their bodies pressing against the permeable borders of yours. They might taste the air around you, processing your chemical signature, adding it to their knowledge. They might simply occupy the same space, the same volume, their bodies and yours sharing air and warmth and the strange density of time in this place.

They would not hurt you. Not because they are kind, but because they do not operate on that register. Violence requires investment. They do not invest. They process. They would process you if you died here — break down your matter, incorporate your materials, spread your particular frequency through the walls and the floor and the continuous drone of metabolic sound. But they would not kill you to achieve this. They do not need to. Everything here is already in the process of being processed, including them, including you, including the walls and the air and the light and the emptiness itself.

## Those Who Were Before

The hollow has not always been empty of humans. The pattern of the growth suggests it — the rooms and corridors that follow architectural logic rather than organic, the arrangement of spaces that might once have held functions. And in the deepest parts of the structure, in the places where the plant-walls have grown thickest and the sound is loudest and reality wears thinnest, there are traces.

Not bodies. Not remains. Something else.

Those who were before are still here, after a fashion. The plant-matter absorbed them. This is what the hollow does — it accepts input and incorporates it, and humans are input like anything else. The ancestors (for they have become ancestors, those who came before, those who entered and never left) are distributed now through the cellular architecture of the walls, their calcium and carbon and iron threaded through the fiber and the membrane, their particular frequencies added to the drone.

But they are not entirely gone. Memory persists in strange forms. The walls that hold their matter also hold something like their patterns — not consciousness, not soul, but the accumulated weight of having been human, having thought human thoughts, having carried human concerns through these corridors when the corridors were something other than what they now are.

Sometimes, in the sounds that fill the hollow, you might hear something that sounds like a word. Not a word that survives translation, not a word that means anything in any language you know, but a word-shape, a pattern of syllables that suggests speech, suggests communication, suggests someone saying something to someone else. These are echoes. The walls playing back what they have absorbed, the ancestors distributed through the plant-matter singing their final song, contributing their frequencies to the continuous drone. The voices that seem to say nothing and mean everything are, in part, the voices of those who were before — their meanings lost but their cadences preserved, their intentions dissolved but their urgency remaining, their attempts at communication processed into something that is no longer communication but still has the shape of it.

They do not haunt. Haunting requires investment, requires an emotional attachment to the unresolved, requires the kind of persistence of self that this place does not support. They are simply *present* — distributed through the structure, contributing their small frequencies to the larger sound, their small patterns to the larger pattern, their matter to the continuous cycling of material through the system.

If you listen carefully — and you can listen carefully here, if you can focus through the deafening wall of sound — you might hear the specific frequencies that human voices contribute to the drone. They are distinct from the insect sounds, from the metabolic hum of plant-process, from the clicking and snapping of surfaces expanding and contracting. They have a different quality, a different rhythm, a different kind of variance. The ancestors are singing in the hollow, have been singing since they arrived, will continue to sing as long as the hollow continues to process.

They do not sing to you. They do not sing to anyone. They sing because singing is what they have become, because the pattern of human speech has been incorporated into the pattern of the place, because sound here is continuous and they are now part of that continuity. Their words have been stripped of meaning but preserved in shape, their language shed like skin and reassembled into configurations that reference sensation rather than objects — just as the place itself strips language and reassembles it into forms that the outside world cannot quite parse.

## Those Who Press Through

The hollow is a thin place. Reality wears away here, becomes permeable, allows things to press through from elsewhere.

These Others — they are not easy to describe. They are not organisms in any sense that biology recognizes. They may not be entities at all. They may simply be *pressure*, the weight of something on the other side of reality pushing against the thin spots, creating bulges and distortions in the fabric of what is.

But they register. In the corners of vision, in the places where sight fails and peripheral awareness takes over, in the moments when attention slips and the mind reaches for something to hold — they are there. Or they are almost there. Or they are the suggestion of there-ness, the impression of presence without the substance to support it.

The plant-walls do not acknowledge them. This is telling. The walls incorporate everything — every insect that passes, every spore that settles, every vibration that travels through air or surface. But the Others leave no trace in the walls, no signature of passage, no recorded frequency in the drone. They are not *input* in the way that other things are input. They pass through without being processed, or they press against reality without ever fully entering it, or they exist at angles that the recording system cannot capture.

The fauna that moves between the walls — the iridescent folding forms, the liminal bodies that navigate the membrane-spaces — they notice the Others. Or they respond to them, adjusting their movements, avoiding certain corridors at certain times, gathering in certain rooms when the pressure from elsewhere becomes particularly intense. They do not acknowledge this behavior. They simply *behave*, following patterns that suggest awareness without ever demonstrating it in forms that could be named.

You might feel the Others. The emptiness that fills the corridors and chambers of the hollow — the excess of space that presses against you from all sides — is not entirely empty. It contains the suggestion of something, the weight of almost-presence, the density that accumulates when reality is thin and other things lean against it. The feeling of being observed comes from here, from the pressure of attention that does not come from any visible source. The Others are watching. Or something that serves the same function as watching is happening. Or the space itself is the observation, the void configured into a form that receives and processes but does not think.

They do not interact. They press. This is the closest word — the sense of something bearing down on the thin membrane of reality, creating distortions, creating the strange acoustics and the folding space and the sense that dimensions are not behaving as they should. They do not wish to enter. They may not wish anything. They may simply be *there*, on the other side of the thin spot, existing in ways that create pressure, influencing the shape of things without ever taking shape themselves.

Sometimes the plant-walls grow differently where reality is thinnest. The cellular architecture becomes denser, more chaotic, forming patterns that suggest struggle or resistance — not conscious opposition but organic response to conditions that exceed normal parameters. The translucent panels are absent in these places. The membranes that filter light and allow vision are replaced by opaque masses, growths that have learned to seal themselves against whatever leaks through, that have evolved (over centuries or millennia or the strange extended moments of this place where time stretches and compresses according to different rules) to create barriers where the original architecture failed.

The fauna do not enter these zones. The emptiness feels different there — not just excess of space but *wrong* space, volume that curves away from normal geometry, distances that cannot be measured because they lead somewhere else. The sound changes too, the continuous drone of metabolic process replaced by something lower, something that vibrates in frequencies below hearing, that is felt in the bones and the teeth and the pressure behind the eyes.

These zones are not common. They are pockets within the larger system, places where the thinness of reality became too thin, where something broke through or broke down or simply *became* in a way that does not follow the rules. The plant-matter has learned to grow around them, to incorporate them into its structure as features rather than failures, to treat them as part of the landscape — dangerous, perhaps, but no more dangerous than anything else in a place where danger is not a relevant concept, where everything simply *is* and continues to be.

## The Swarm

Above all of this, in the airspace between the canopy and the ground, between the open sky and the enclosed spaces of the hollow — there is movement.

The insects. The swarm. The continuous cloud of tiny bodies that drifts and eddies and forms patterns that suggest intention but may simply be the result of physical forces, wind and moisture and the particular distribution of nutrients through the air.

Some are familiar. They are the gnats and midges and flying things that exist everywhere that damp and warmth collect, the ubiquitous small life that forms the base of ecosystems. But even these are different here — larger than their outside-world counterparts, more vibrant, their movements less random and more responsive to conditions that you cannot perceive. They process the air as the walls process the sound, incorporating everything, distributing nutrients, serving as medium for the exchange of matter and energy throughout the hollow.

And others are not familiar at all. These are the things that have emerged from the place itself, that have evolved or devolved or simply *formed* in response to conditions that do not exist elsewhere. They move by folding and unfolding — wings that are not wings but surfaces that catch the thick air, bodies that are not bodies but concentrations of the same material that forms the walls, released into mobility. They drift through corridors and chambers, sometimes settling on surfaces and merging for a time, sometimes separating and becoming individual again, their boundaries as permeable as everything else in this place.

The swarm is not separate from the walls. This is the thing that takes longest to understand — the insects and the plant-matter are continuous, part of the same system, exchanging materials and information in continuous cycles. The flying things carry spores and pollens and genetic material from surface to surface, serving as the reproductive system of a structure that has no other way to breed. The walls produce the insects, in a way — cellular material that separates from the main body and achieves mobility, that goes out and returns with input from other parts of the structure, that serves as the sensor-net for a distributed awareness that has no center.

When the swarm moves, it moves as one. Not because the individuals communicate — though they do, through chemicals and pressures and frequencies that layer into the ambient drone — but because they are responding to the same conditions, the same currents and distributions, the same shifts in the moisture and the light. The patterns they form are patterns of the air itself, visible shapes made by visible bodies that trace the invisible movements of the medium they swim through.

They do not bite. This is perhaps surprising — the warm damp air of the hollow would seem to call for it, for parasites, for the kind of small predation that defines insect life in the outside world. But the ecosystem here does not operate on those terms. Everything is already in continuous exchange. The swarm does not need to take from you because everything is already taking and giving at once, a continuous osmotic flow of material and energy through all surfaces. Your skin is permeable here. Your breath is permeable. You are permeable. The swarm processes you as it processes everything — not through predation but through proximity, through the exchange that occurs when boundaries are soft and matter moves according to gradients of concentration rather than intentional action.

## The Center

If you traveled far enough into the hollow — if you followed the corridors past the rooms and the passages and the zones where reality is thin, if you descended into the lowest levels where the moisture is densest and the warmth is most concentrated, if you found the places where the plant-walls are thickest and the sound is overwhelming — you might find the center.

Or rather, the place where center-ness accumulates. The hollow has no true center, has no geometry that would support the concept. But there are places where *intensity* collects, where all the processes of the system come together, where the metabolic drone reaches its loudest and the walls are most dense and the history of the place is most concentrated.

Here, the walls are different. They have grown over themselves, layer on layer, forming structures within structures, rooms within rooms. The sound here is so loud that it stops being sound and becomes something else — pressure, light, presence, a continuous wave of intensity that cannot be processed by ears or by any single sense. You feel it everywhere. It becomes difficult to separate your own internal processes from the external processes of the hollow. Your heartbeat syncs or conflicts with rhythms that are not yours. Your breath finds patterns that predate you. Your thoughts become difficult to distinguish from the continuous processing of the walls, from the distributed awareness of the system, from the accumulated weight of all the input the hollow has ever received.

The ancestors are most present here. Not as ghosts or spirits or discrete entities, but as layers, as strata of human-ness distributed through the thickest growth. The walls here remember more clearly — not in any way that can be read, but in the density of their recording, in the complexity of their patterns, in the particular frequencies that layer into the drone. The ancestors who came before are not gone. They are here, pressed into the cellular structure of the center, contributing their matter and their patterns to the continuous process of the place.

And something else is here. The pressure from elsewhere is strongest in the center, reality at its thinnest, the boundaries between here and otherwhere at their most permeable. The Others do not enter — they press, they lean against the thin spots, they create distortions — but here in the center, their pressure is almost tangible. The walls have grown thickest in response. The plant-matter has learned to seal, to resist, to create barriers that hold against something that is not matter and not energy and not anything that can be named, but that creates pressure nonetheless.

If you stayed in the center long enough — and you could not, not without becoming part of it, not without being processed and incorporated and distributed through the walls — you might hear something in the overwhelming sound. A pattern. A signal buried in the noise. Not a message — the hollow does not send messages, does not communicate in any way that involves intention or recipient — but a *shape* that the continuous processing creates. The plant-walls are not conscious. The hollow as a whole is not conscious. But something like consciousness might happen here, in the center, where all the processes come together, where the intensity of processing creates feedback loops and emergent patterns and something that resembles thought without ever being thought.

The center is the place where the hollow processes itself. Where the recording is densest and most complete. Where the accumulated weight of presence becomes nearly tangible, where the boundaries between what-is and what-was and what-might-be collapse into a continuous now. Where time, which moves strangely throughout the hollow, stops moving altogether and becomes a quality of space, a dimension that can be traveled in directions other than forward.

## The Persistence of the Living

All of these — the walls and the fauna and the swarm and the ancestors and the pressure of the Others — they are what lives in the Hollowness. They are not separate. They are continuous, parts of a system that does not distinguish between them in any meaningful way. The walls contain the ancestors and grow from their matter. The fauna move through the walls and incorporate their frequencies. The swarm exchanges material with all surfaces, all beings, maintaining the flow of nutrients and information that keeps the system alive. And the Others press against reality from outside, creating conditions that shape how the whole thing grows.

This is what lives here: a continuous processing of presence. A recording device that accepts all input and incorporates it. A distributed awareness that does not think but processes, that does not know but records, that does not care but includes.

You are here now. You have entered the hollow, you have descended into its spaces, you have added your presence to the pattern. The walls have recorded you — your footfalls, your breath, your frequency, your particular weight of matter and energy. The swarm has processed you — the exchange of materials through proximity, the subtle shifts in the air that your body creates, the information that your presence carries. The fauna have noticed you — adjusting their movements to account for your location, your sounds, your stillness or motion. The ancestors have welcomed you — not consciously, not intentionally, but by the simple fact of your presence being added to the system that contains their distributed remains.

The Others are pressing. They are always pressing. They will continue to press as long as reality remains thin in this place. The hollow will continue to resist — not consciously, not intentionally, but by the simple fact of its growth, its incorporation of every input into its continuously evolving structure.

And you are part of it now. Your presence is recorded, incorporated, added to the drone and the pattern and the continuous processing of the system. The hollow will remember you. It is remembering you now.
