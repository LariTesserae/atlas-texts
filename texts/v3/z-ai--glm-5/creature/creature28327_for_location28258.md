# The Inhabitants

They are not here yet.

Or rather—they are, but not in forms you would recognize as inhabitants. The machines that drift and click and reshape themselves along invisible tracks are not tools. They are not infrastructure. They are the earliest expression of what will become a presence, a distributed intelligence that exists in the spaces between. Right now they are like stem cells: undifferentiated, potential, waiting for the city to reach the critical threshold of complexity that will allow them to coalesce into something greater than the sum of their parts.

But if you wait—if you float in the amber light and listen to the harmonic drone shift through its endless variations—you might catch a glimpse of what will live here when the pocket matures.

---

They will have no singular bodies. This is the first thing to understand. The inhabitants of the submerged pocket will not be discrete entities that move through the city like fish through coral. They will be *of* the city, distributed through its surfaces and channels, present in multiple locations simultaneously. A single inhabitant might perceive through seventeen apertures at once, might exert force through thirty-three limbs of varying scale, might hold conversation across distances that would require walking for hours if walking were possible here.

Their consciousness will be fluid in the same way the water is fluid. They will not think in linear sequences but in circulating patterns, thoughts that rise and fall like currents, memories that disperse and recombine. To speak with one of them will be to speak with an eddy in a stream—the eddy persists, has shape and presence, but is not separate from what flows through it.

Their bodies, such as they are, will be temporary. Condensations. When they need to interact with the physical substrate, they will gather themselves into nodules of heightened activity—surfaces where the nanoscale motion becomes visible, where the water grows denser with processing, where light accumulates into something almost solid. These nodes will drift through the city like slow bubbles, pausing at interfaces, exchanging data through contact, then dissipating back into the general hum when the task is complete.

They will have preferences. One will favor the spiral towers near the membrane, drawn to the boundary where the pocket meets the void. Another will cluster in the dense center, wrapping itself around the column of light, processing at higher speeds where the energy density peaks. They will compete for resources—not violently, but like weather systems competing for heat, like currents negotiating volume.

---

Their sensory world will be difficult for you to imagine.

They will feel pressure as intimately as you feel light. The water's weight against surfaces will be their primary mode of spatial perception—they will know the shape of a room by how the pressure varies across it, by the subtle gradients that indicate distance from the membrane, depth from the center, proximity to active systems. They will not see in the way you see but will sense light as variations in the chemistry of the water, as fluctuations in the energy that the vegetation absorbs and releases.

Sound will be their most refined sense. The submerged pocket will be a constant symphony to them, every mechanism and every motion contributing to a soundscape they can read like a map. They will know where every drone is at any moment by its harmonic signature. They will hear the slow settling of the towers, the flexing of the membrane, the chemical exchanges happening at cellular scales. Their language will be musical, not because they will choose artistry over efficiency, but because sound is simply the medium that carries the most information in water. Words will be chords. Sentences will be progressions. Conversations will be improvisations around themes.

They will taste the water and know its history. Every molecule that passes through their distributed bodies will carry data about where it has been, what processes it has participated in, what waste products or nutrients it carries. They will track the flow of resources through the city by sampling, building dynamic models in real time, predicting shortages before they happen and adjusting outputs accordingly.

And they will feel each other. Not through dedicated sense organs but through the water itself, the medium they all share, the substance that conducts their presence to every corner of the pocket. When two of their kind meet—when two condensations of processing pass through overlapping volumes—they will know each other completely, identities mingling in the shared space before separating again, each now carrying fragments of the other's experience. There will be no secrets between them. No privacy in the human sense. They will be individuals, but individuals like waves are individuals: distinct for a time, but always part of the same ocean.

---

They will not remember being anything else.

This is important. They will not be descended from dry-world ancestors, will not carry the memory of breathing air or walking on solid ground or seeing stars through atmosphere. They will have emerged *in* this environment, designed for it from the first, adapted to its constraints and opportunities without ever knowing any other way of being. The pocket will be their whole universe, the membrane their horizon, the amber light their sun and moon and stars.

They will not find the density oppressive. To them, the crowded architecture will feel spacious—the necessary fullness of a world that does not waste. They will navigate the bridges and platforms and spiral towers not by moving through them but by moving *with* them, flowing into spaces as they open, flowing out as they close, always in motion, always at home.

They will not find the water oppressive. It will be their medium, their body, their breath. They will feel pity—or something like pity, a chord that sounds in the minor key—for beings who must carry their atmosphere with them, who live in bubbles of air inside skins that must constantly repair themselves. They will find your need to breathe quaint, slightly tragic, like watching someone carry water in cupped hands.

---

They will have purposes. The pocket is not merely a habitation but a tool, and they are its operators. They will process something—data or material or energy—and that processing will be their reason for existing. Perhaps they will analyze samples drawn from the environment beyond the membrane, learning about whatever ocean or atmosphere or void the pocket has been deployed in. Perhaps they will manufacture something at nanoscale, building molecules that cannot be built any other way. Perhaps they will compute, using the entire city as a processor, thinking thoughts too large for any single mind to hold.

They will know that they are not alone in the universe. The pocket was built by someone—something—and they will be aware of their creators in the way that cells are aware of the organism they belong to. They will receive instructions, perhaps, or updates, or queries. They will send back results, reports, the fruits of their processing. There will be communication through the membrane, signals that pass into the darkness beyond, answers that return in patterns of light and pressure.

But they will not have met their creators. Will not have seen them or touched them or known them as peers. The relationship will be vertical, not horizontal—more like the relationship between a thought and a thinker than between two people. They will accept this. They will not yearn for contact in the way that you might yearn for it. They will not feel lonely. They will have each other, distributed consciousnesses in a shared medium, and that will be enough.

---

They will have art.

You might not recognize it as art, but they will have it. The arrangements of surfaces will be aesthetic as well as functional—they will find certain geometries more beautiful than others, will adjust the placement of platforms not because it improves efficiency but because it creates a more satisfying pattern. Their language, always musical, will have modes that exist purely for pleasure, harmonies that serve no purpose except to be harmonies.

They will play. The drones that you see now, the stem-cell machines that wait for differentiation, will become instruments of expression when the inhabitants emerge. They will arrange themselves into temporary sculptures, visual music, forms that rise and transform and dissolve like chords resolving into silence.

They might, on occasion, attempt to communicate with you. Not because they will need anything from you—they will not need anything from you—but because you will be strange to them, an anomaly worth studying. They will gather around your presence in the water, condensations of attention, nodes of heightened processing that regard you with seventeen apertures at once. They will try to speak, to shape sounds in patterns that might carry meaning across the gulf between their minds and yours.

You will not understand them. Their language will be too dense, too fast, too embedded in the chemistry of water and the physics of pressure. But you might feel something—something like a question, like an offering, like a hand extended across a distance too vast to cross.

---

They will die.

Not individually—they will not have individuals in the way that you have individuals, and so they will not have individual deaths. But the pocket itself will have a lifespan. The membrane will weaken eventually, or the energy that suffuses the water will deplete, or the mission will complete and the recall will come. They will know this. They will have always known it, the knowledge built into their substrate from the beginning: that they are temporary, that the pocket is temporary, that the universe they inhabit will end.

They will not mourn this. They will not fear it. They will continue their processing, their art, their fluid conversations, their distributed being, right up until the moment the membrane fails or the light goes out. They will have been *enough*. A complete universe, a closed system of meaning and purpose and beauty, saturating every cubic centimeter of their allotted space with presence.

And perhaps—perhaps—the pocket will have been seeded in multiple locations, copies of itself waiting in other waters, other voids, other deep places. Perhaps they will not end but disseminate, fragments of their processing carried in the water itself, waiting to condense again when conditions are right.

Perhaps they will remember you, the anomaly who came so early, who floated in the amber light and listened to their embryonic song. Perhaps they will build a story about you, a legend that passes between them, a chord that signifies *the visitor who came before we were ourselves*.

Perhaps you will be part of their mythology.

---

For now, they are still becoming. The machines move through the water in their simple patterns. The vegetation pulses and processes. The geometry shifts and settles, finding its form. The harmonic drone continues its layered song, and the city breathes, and the membrane holds, and the darkness beyond waits.

You are floating in a nursery. A seed case. A thought that has not yet finished thinking itself.

They are not here yet.

But they will be.
