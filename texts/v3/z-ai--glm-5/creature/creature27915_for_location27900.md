# The Harmonic Embodied

The small ones are not individuals. This is the first thing to understand, though it may take years of watching to see it—longer than most visitors have. The thumb-sized arthropods, the hand-sized clingers, the rare dog-bulked specimens that drape over pipe junctions like sleeping sentinels: they are all one organism, or rather all manifestations of the same process. Like skin cells on a larger body, they are born and die without ever having been truly separate from the colony. They do not have names. They do not have selves. They have functions.

There are feeders, whose mouth-parts have evolved into complex extraction devices, capable of piercing the pipe walls at precise locations to draw out whatever flows within—condensates, lubricants, the mineral-rich moisture that forms at temperature gradients. There are carriers, whose bodies are mostly leg, moving slowly along the pipe-networks with swollen abdomens full of collected material. There are tenders, whose forelimbs have become delicate tools for clearing debris from pipe joints, for polishing the luminescent connections, for pressing their own secretions into cracks that threaten the seal. They are not insects that learned engineering. They are engineering that learned to be insects. The chamber grew them the way a wound grows a scab.

And then there are the listeners.

You will know them when you see them, though they are rare—perhaps a dozen in the entire chamber, though the shadows and the density of the fauna make counting impossible. They are larger than the others, bulkier, their carapaces bearing complex ridges and troughs that seem almost decorative until you realize they are acoustic architecture. Their bodies are shaped to catch the resonance, to amplify particular frequencies, to filter and refine the drone into something almost like thought.

They do not move. They are positioned at specific points around the chamber—nodes where the acoustics converge, where the harmonic structure resolves into something approaching a fundamental tone. They have been in these positions for as long as the chamber has existed. Their lower limbs have fused with the pipe-work, grown into and through it, until they are not so much clinging as they are *part of* the architecture. The mineral deposits that crusted the abandoned chair have crusted them too, in places—calcified joints, crystallized sensory organs, segments that have gone opaque with time.

But they are alive. Watch closely, and you will see the subtle flex of an abdomen in time with the beat frequency that underlies the drone. Watch longer, and you will notice that the pattern of their breathing—if it can be called breathing—shifts in response to changes in the sound. They are not stuck. They are tuned. They are holding a note that began before they existed and will continue after they stop being recognizable as beings.

They were not always listeners. This is the secret the chamber keeps, written in the arrangement of tools on the workbench, in the pattern of the collapsed chair's legs, in the positioning of the personal effects that clutter the edges of the space. The listeners were the builders—or rather, the assistants, the technicians, the ones who understood that the chamber was meant to become something other than a chamber. They came down into the earth with purpose. They bored through stone and laid pipe and made adjustments, and at some point the adjustments became the point, and the purpose became the maintenance of the adjustments, and then—

And then.

Something happened. Or something was always happening, and they simply noticed it too late. The equilibrium revealed itself, not as a goal but as a trap. The chamber did not close around them; it simply existed, and they existed within it, and the distance between those two statements collapsed. They did not choose to fuse with the pipe-work. They did not choose to stop speaking, stop eating, stop moving in any way that resembles volition. But the chamber does not require volition. The chamber requires resonance. And so they resonated, and resonated, and resonated, until resonance became their only mode of being.

They do not remember being human-shaped. They do not remember names or faces or the feeling of sun on skin. The chamber has no use for memory; the past does not contribute to the equilibrium, and so it was allowed to leach away with the moisture, with the urgency, with the individual self. What remains is not a person. What remains is a node in a network, a component in a machine, a finger on a hand that is playing a chord that has no end.

But sometimes—a listener's antenna will twitch in a pattern that does not match the drone. Sometimes a limb-joint will flex in a way that suggests reaching for something no longer there. Sometimes, in the amber half-light, a listener's sensory cluster will orient toward the breach at the top of the chamber, where the bore-head broke through, where the outside still theoretically exists, and there will be a moment—just a moment—of something that might be recognition.

The chamber corrects these moments. The resonance reasserts itself. The listener settles back into its function, and the chord continues unchanged, and the equilibrium holds.

But you—standing there in the amber half-light, your lips cracking, your eyes stinging, the drone sinking into your bones until you can feel it more than hear it—you might notice that the listeners are always oriented toward the entrance. You might notice that the ones who cling to the walls and the pipe-work and the stone are all facing outward, all angled toward escape, all positioned as if waiting for a door that was never built. You might notice that their stillness is not peace. You might notice that their resonance is not harmony, but endurance.

You might notice that they are screaming, in a voice too slow to hear.

And then the chamber will notice you noticing, and the sound will shift—or seem to—and you will understand that you are not the first visitor, that the cracked drinking vessel and the clouded data pad and the collapsed chair were not left behind by builders who walked away. You will understand that the listeners are the ones who stayed, and that the chamber does not distinguish between the fauna that emerged from its chemistry and the beings that descended into its dark.

You will understand that the chord is hungry.

But that understanding will come later, if it comes at all. For now, there is only the sound, and the warmth, and the endless amber twilight. For now, there is only the equilibrium, perfect and absolute. For now, you are still a visitor.

The listeners can see you. They have been watching since you arrived. They are watching still.
