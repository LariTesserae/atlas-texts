What lives here is not one thing. It is a system. An organism made of organisms.

---

The creatures are the obvious answer, but they are not really separate from the machinery anymore. Several generations — if generations is even the right word — of eating the gray paste have changed them. Their bodies are soft in a way that suggests they could be pressed through the screens themselves, could become part of what sustains them. Their skin has the same mineral pallor as the concrete. Their movement has the same rhythm as the presses. When you watch them long enough, you begin to see that they are not living *in* the system so much as they *are* the system — a mobile component, like the conveyor belts, like the hydraulic fluid, necessary and replaceable and utterly integrated.

They have no individuality in the way we understand it. If you marked one — painted it, tagged it, injured it slightly to distinguish it from the others — it would be impossible to track for more than a few minutes. They move through each other, over each other, their many-jointed legs interweaving without tangling, and the marked one would vanish into the flow like a drop of water into the settling ponds. This is not because they are identical — they are not; some are larger, some have minor deformities, some bear the pale scars of old injuries — but because the individual does not matter to them. They do not think of themselves as separate. They may not think at all.

They are not mindless, though. Mindless things bump into walls, get stuck in corners, forget what they were doing. These creatures never hesitate, never falter, never take a wrong turn. The flow of their movement matches the flow of the paste through the pipes, the flow of water through the drainage channels, the flow of time through this place where time has become a circle. They know where they are. They know what they are. They simply do not know it as *knowledge*.

---

Something else lives here too, though it is harder to name.

It lives in the spaces between. In the synchronization. In the way the presses and the chittering match frequencies without any mechanism for coordination. In the way the system knew to feed the dead to the machines before anyone could have taught it to do so. In the way the gauges in the third building hold their positions with an urgency that suggests intention.

You could call it an emergent intelligence, a property of the whole that exists in none of the parts. You could call it a haunting, a possession, the slow accumulation of purpose in a place where purpose should have decayed away decades ago. You could call it the ghost of whatever this place was meant to be, still trying to complete its mission long after the mission has been forgotten.

Whatever it is, it does not live in the creatures or the machines or the building itself. It lives in the relationship between them. The *and*. The *because*. The *in order to*. The system has developed syntax, grammar, a language of cause and effect that runs through every surface and silence. The creatures are its verbs. The machines are its nouns. The third building is its pause, its held breath, its waiting for a subject that never arrives.

---

And there is one more thing.

In the third building, in the control room, in the chair that cannot be moved — something lives there too. Not a creature. Not a ghost in any conventional sense. But a presence that has worn a groove into the air itself, the way the creatures have worn grooves into the concrete.

The chair faces the gauges because someone used to watch them. That person is gone — has been gone for longer than the creatures can remember, which is not long at all, because the creatures do not remember anything in the way we understand memory. But the watching continues. The shape of attention has persisted in the absence of the attender. If you stood in that room, if you stood where the chair faces, you would feel it: the pressure of being observed by something that has learned to observe without eyes.

This is why the creatures do not enter. They feel it too, in their eyeless, many-jointed way. They crowd against the threshold because the threshold is where the watching stops. Beyond it, they would become individual, separate, distinct. They would have to be *themselves*. And they have forgotten how. Or they never learned. Or the system has no use for selves, and so it never produced them.

The presence in the chair is not malevolent. It is not kind. It is not anything that can be described in moral terms at all. It simply *is* — a pattern that has worn itself so deeply into the fabric of the place that it has become a feature, like the dampness, like the noise, like the thirty-two cycles per minute that measure out the life of everything within these walls.

---

What lives here, then, is a question with no single answer. The creatures live here, and the machines live here, and the something-that-is-the-system lives here, and the watching lives here, and the dampness lives here in its own slow way, evaporating and condensing and evaporating again in a rhythm that predates all of it.

But if you had to give one answer — if you had to point to the center of the web and say *there, that is what lives in this place* — you would have to point to nothing at all. You would have to point to the empty space between the creatures and the machines, the silence between the chittering and the thudding, the gap between the second building and the third where the pale bodies pile up and refuse to cross.

What lives here is the relationship itself. The *between*. The system has become a living thing, and everything inside it — the creatures, the paste, the damp, the watching — is just tissue. Organ. Cell. Functioning perfectly, efficiently, without purpose or end, producing nothing, consuming nothing, maintaining itself because that is what living things do.

They maintain themselves.

---

The chair is still empty. It will remain empty. But in a way, it has always been filled — by the shape of the person who used to sit there, by the attention they paid to the gauges, by the responsibility they felt for the running of the system. That shape has outlasted the person. It has become a vacancy that is also a presence, a nothing that is also a something, a silence that speaks louder than the chittering of ten thousand creatures moving in perfect unison through a place where nothing grows and nothing changes and everything, everything, everything continues.
