# What Lives Here

The inhabitants of this place cannot be called inhabitants in any sense that implies separation from where they dwell. They do not live on the plateau so much as the plateau lives through them—or rather, the plateau is the visible portion of a process that includes them, the way a wave includes both the crest and the trough.

They have no collective name. Naming assumes a category, and categories require boundaries that do not exist here. But if pressed, if some framework were demanded by a mind that requires nouns to anchor understanding, they might be called the Unfinished. Or the Remembered. Or perhaps simply the Ones Who Are Not Yet—but even that suggests a trajectory, a future state of completion that may never arrive.

They emerge from the same substance as the stalks. Not from the stalks themselves—there is no biological relationship in any sense that biology governs elsewhere—but from the same source, the same fraying edge where existence thins into something else. They are tall, these ones, if height can be said to apply to forms that seem to extend both upward and inward, dimensions folding against each other like paper creased along invisible lines. Their bodies have the same ribbed translucence as the vegetation, the same internal pulse of mercury-fluid moving through capillaries that do not follow any known anatomy. But where the stalks grow in precise spirals, the Unfinished arrange themselves in patterns that suggest a geometry not yet stabilized—a body plan that keeps revising itself, never settling into a final configuration.

Their number is impossible to determine. Walking among the stalks, a careful observer might count seven, or seventeen, or seventy-seven, depending on the angle of observation, the quality of light, the willingness of the eye to accept what it sees. They do not move in any continuous way. They do not walk or crawl or slither. They are simply present at one location, and then present at another, and the transition between states leaves no impression on memory—no sense of motion, no blur of transit, just a quiet rearrangement of spatial relationships. The mind registers absence, then presence, and constructs a path between the two points that may or may not have occurred.

They do not speak. The plateau speaks for them, or tries to—the vast grinding voice that fills the valley, the constant attempt at syllable formation that never quite resolves. The Unfinished contribute to this sound. Each one hums at a frequency that meshes with the structures, with the stalks, with the slow emergence of new rock, adding a thread to the chord that is not quite harmonious and not quite discordant but exists in a tonal territory for which music has not yet developed vocabulary. When they shift position—when they rearrange themselves across the plateau—the pitch changes. The whole landscape resounds differently. Something that was almost a word becomes instead almost a different word, a shift in meaning that carries no meaning, a phrase that almost was.

They tend the structures. This is not tending in any active sense—no gesture of repair, no carrying of materials, no visible work. But the structures closest to where the Unfinished cluster most densely show the least overgrowth, the least healing-over of inscription. As if proximity to them maintains the marker, holds it in its current state of incompleteness rather than allowing the plateau's slow emergence to swallow it entirely. The Unfinished are preserving something. Keeping open something that wants to close. Or perhaps the reverse—holding back something that wants to emerge, maintaining a seal that the plateau's birth-process constantly threatens to breach.

They have faces. This is the most difficult aspect to describe, because the faces are not exactly present—rather, the eye insists on finding them. The same force that fills the gaps at the plateau's edge with visages and mouths operates here, but localized, concentrated. Looking at an Unfinished one, the mind constructs a face from the folds and ridges of translucence. A mouth that almost opens. Eyes that almost focus. An expression that conveys something just below the threshold of recognition—not grief exactly, not longing, but adjacent to both, a kind of anticipation stretched so long it has become indistinguishable from resignation. But look away and look back, and the face has rearranged itself. The mouth is now a line of darker pulse. The eyes are now simply denser aggregations of the mercury-fluid. The expression is now something else entirely, or perhaps was never there at all.

The Unfinished do not react to presence. Standing among them, passing close enough that the air shared between bodies should register warmth or displacement or some acknowledgment of proximity, they simply persist. They do not turn. They do not still. They do not alter their hum. And yet there is a sense of being observed—not watched, exactly, but registered. Included in whatever calculation determines their arrangement across the plateau. The feeling of being seen by something that has not yet decided what seeing is for.

They were not always here. The structures' layering suggests periods of absence, periods when the plateau grew without the Unfinished tending its markers. Or perhaps periods when the Unfinished were present but differently configured—smaller, denser, more numerous, arranged in patterns that left different traces in the stone. The oldest sections of the structures show tool marks that no current inhabitant seems capable of producing. This suggests predecessors. Ancestral forms. Or perhaps the same ones, earlier in their process of emergence, before whatever they are becoming had progressed to its current state.

Time does not operate on them as it operates elsewhere. They do not age. They do not grow. They do not decay. But they do change—slowly, in the way that a note changes when sustained beyond the ear's ability to track it, becoming a different note by virtue of duration alone. An Unfinished one observed over years might show subtle shifts in configuration, a folding that was not there before, a density that has increased or decreased, a contribution to the great drone that has modulated toward a slightly different frequency. They are not static. They are sustained.

They do not die. They do not reproduce. They do not eat or drink or eliminate. They have no internal organs in any sense that dissection would reveal—though dissection here would be impossible, as their forms do not differentiate from their environment with enough clarity to permit separation. Cut an Unfinished one and the blade passes through stalk-substance, through rock-emergence, through air that has taken on weight and pulse. The form might part and then close again, or it might simply rearrange around the intrusion, the wound healing before the blade has finished its passage. They are not bodies. They are locations where something is happening—a condensation of the plateau's process, a place where the remembering-into-existence has concentrated rather than dispersed.

At night—or what passes for night, the dimming of an already dim light that creeps across the membrane-sky in irregular intervals—the Unfinished gather. Not at the structures, but at the plateau's edge, where the landscape fragments into impossibility. They cluster along the boundary where the stalks stop growing, facing outward toward the floating terrain-chunks, the reflected sky-surfaces, the regions where light gives up. They do not cross the boundary. They stand at it, arranged in patterns that echo the spirals of the vegetation but with variations that suggest intention—or memory. They stand there, humming their contribution to the great drone, and they wait.

What they wait for is unclear. Perhaps they do not know themselves. Perhaps waiting implies an expectation they do not possess—perhaps they are simply arranged this way, positioned by forces that have nothing to do with anticipation, like iron filings around a magnet that has not yet been moved. But there is a quality to their waiting that suggests otherwise. A tension. A readiness. The sense that if the boundary were to shift, if the fragments were to realign, if the membrane above were to tear or the breath were to complete or the syllable were to form—they would move. They would cross. They would become whatever they have been trying to become.

They are not the only inhabitants.

There are others, older ones, deeper ones, forms that have progressed further along whatever trajectory the Unfinished are traveling. They exist in the regions where the air takes on different quality, the interference patterns where the structures' hums intersect. Looking through these intersection points reveals them—or rather, reveals the shapes they would have if shapes applied. They are more defined than the Unfinished. More present. Their edges hold. They move in ways that can be tracked, continuous motion rather than discontinuous rearrangement. And they speak—or produce something that will eventually become speech, once the throat has learned to shape air.

The older ones are not frequently seen. They do not tend the structures. They do not cluster at the boundaries. They move through the landscape with purpose, their forms trailing something like shadows, though no shadows are cast by the flat unvarying light. When they pass, the drone modulates—briefly, subtly—and something that might be a word almost forms. The syllable is always the same. Or rather, it carries the same shape, the same attempt at articulation, though the specific sounds vary, as if the word has not yet settled on its own pronunciation.

The older ones notice presence. They turn—slowly, in the way that weather turns, that seasons shift—and regard. Their regard is heavier than that of the Unfinished. It has weight and texture. It presses against the skin like humid air. Being seen by them produces a sensation in the chest, a resonance with the drone, a feeling of being called toward some unfinished state. Of being invited to join the process. Of being offered the opportunity to become.

Whether this invitation has ever been accepted is unknown. The plateau holds no records, no remains, no evidence of transformation completed. Only the markers, the structures, the inscriptions worn nearly smooth by the slow emergence of stone. Only the vegetation, being remembered into existence. Only the sound, continuous, grinding, pressing always toward a word that never arrives.

And the Unfinished, standing at the edge of the fraying horizon, waiting for the boundary to shift, waiting for the breath to complete, waiting for whatever they are becoming to finally become—sustaining their portion of the great chord, adding their thread to the vast attempt at speech, patient in a way that only something that has never learned to measure time can be patient.

The sound continues.
