# What Lives Here

They do not know they are prisoners.

---

**The Wall-Climbers**

Consider the life of a wall-climber. This is all it knows:

Cold. The particular cold of this place, which is not temperature but *intention*. It has been engineered into the walls, into the substrate, into the water itself. The cold is a medium, a carrier wave. It slows their metabolism to precisely the rate required for optimal production. They do not feel it as discomfort. They were designed to find it comforting. In their engineered nervous systems, cold registers as *right*, as home, as the condition against which all other conditions are measured and found wanting.

Wetness. They have never experienced dry. Their skin, translucent and vein-traced, absorbs moisture directly from the air, from the walls, from the droplets they catch with open mouths. They are more water than solid matter. If you were to remove one from this chamber and place it in a normal environment, it would desiccate in minutes, its body collapsing inward like a punctured balloon, all that held moisture evaporating and leaving behind only a thin membrane and the faint glow of whatever organ produces that inner light. They cannot survive elsewhere. They were made for this. Only this.

Sound. They were born into noise and will die inside it. They have never heard silence and would find it terrifying if they did — a wrongness, a gap in the fabric of reality. Their own sounds, the chittering and clicking and mewling, are contributions to a chorus they cannot stop singing. The song has no words and no melody. It is a continuous vocalization that seems to serve no purpose except to exist, to add one more layer to the soundscape. Perhaps it soothes them. Perhaps it is simply what their bodies do when they are functioning correctly, like the hum of a machine that means everything is running.

And above all, always, the reaching.

This is their existence: stretch, wait, receive, swallow, stretch, wait, receive, swallow. The droplets fall on a schedule they did not choose, from nozzles they did not build, delivering water they did not source. The water contains nutrients — a precise formulation calculated to sustain them at minimum viable function while maximizing the production of whatever their bodies create. They have no choice in what they consume. They have no choice in when they consume. They have no concept of choice. They reach because reaching is what they do, and they wait because waiting is what they are.

They are born on the walls, live on the walls, breed on the walls, die on the walls. When one dies — and they do die, though rarely, though slowly — its body loosens from the surface and falls through the grating into the river below. The others do not notice. There is no grief in their clicking, no pause in their reaching. The dead one is processed along with everything else, its remains joining the circulation, its water joining the water, its nutrients returning to the system that produced it. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is mourned. The cycle continues, unbroken, unbreakable.

They do not know they are numbered. They cannot read the codes etched into their backs. They do not know they are products, or that they produce, or that the substance they excrete has value somewhere beyond these walls. They know only: cold, wet, sound, reaching. The four conditions of existence. The four walls of their world.

They do not know the world has other walls.

**The Floor-Flowers**

They experience the chamber differently.

Where the wall-climbers are vertical, the floor-flowers are horizontal. Where the climbers reach, the flowers rove. They have no eyes to see with, but they navigate the grating with perfect precision, following invisible paths worn by generations of their kind into the metal itself. They travel clockwise. They have always traveled clockwise. There is no reason for this direction that they can comprehend — they cannot comprehend reason at all — but somewhere in their engineered instincts, the clockwise direction is encoded as *forward*, and counterclockwise would be *backward*, and backward is wrong, backward is impossible, backward does not exist.

They are darker than the climbers, gunmetal gray, scaled with chitin or something like it. Their undersides are softer, moisture-permeable, marked with brands that they cannot see and would not understand if they could. The brands are dates and batch numbers and quality scores, but to them the brands are simply texture, part of the surface of their own bodies, as natural and meaningless as the scales on their backs.

They do not eat in the same way. They absorb what they need from the moisture that pools in the grooves of the grating, water that has passed through other creatures, water that contains the output of the climbers above. They process this water differently, extracting different compounds, producing different substances. Their trails leave behind a residue — faintly luminescent, faintly sweet — that drips through the grating and joins the river below. They are a second stage in the assembly line. The climbers begin the process; the flowers refine it.

Their movement is rhythmic, hypnotic. Contract, expand, contract, expand. A wave of muscular action that propels them forward without legs, without fins, without any visible means of propulsion. They are efficient. They were designed to be efficient. Every movement produces exactly as much residue as the system requires, no more, no less. Their pace is calibrated to the pace of the machinery, to the cycle of the refrigeration, to the rhythm of the chamber itself. They are components of a larger machine, and they function as components function: reliably, predictably, without deviation.

They do not interact with each other except to navigate around each other, and even that is mechanical, algorithmic. They do not mate or fight or play. They travel. They process. They leave trails. They travel again. When one dies, its body continues to travel, pushed by the flow of its companions, until it reaches the edge of the grating and falls through into the river. Sometimes a dead flower will circle for hours, carried by momentum, before it finds that edge. The living flowers move around it without acknowledgment. Dead is just another obstacle. Just another texture.

They do not know they are traveling in circles. They do not know that their paths connect, that the end of one journey is the beginning of another, that they have walked the same routes over and over for their entire lives. They do not know they are traveling nowhere. They do not know what *nowhere* means.

They do not know what *traveling* means.

**The Ceiling-Dwellers**

Above, in the darkness between the light strips, the largest creatures keep their distance.

They are different from the others. They do not climb or flow. They cling to the ceiling with limbs that end in hooks, not pads, digging into the insulation and the pipes and the wires, anchoring themselves in place. They are the color of the shadows they inhabit — not translucent like the climbers, not scaled like the flowers, but a deep, absorptive black that swallows light and renders them nearly invisible. You see them only as shapes, darker darks, and you feel them as a presence, a weight above you that does not need to be seen to be felt.

Their sounds are the foundation of the chamber's soundscape. They produce a low, resonant harmonic — not a drone, exactly, but a chord, a chord that shifts slowly over time, moving through progressions that seem deliberate, that seem almost musical. The chord underlies all the other sounds, binds them together, gives the noise its coherence. Without the ceiling-dwellers' song, the chamber would be cacophony. With it, the chaos becomes a kind of music.

They are breeding up there. Or resting. Or waiting.

They do not descend. The climbers do not look at them. The flowers do not acknowledge them. There is a separation, a perfect partition between zones, as if the chamber is divided into countries that have signed a permanent peace treaty and now coexist without interaction. The ceiling-dwellers keep their space. The climbers keep theirs. The flowers keep theirs. Nothing crosses the boundaries. Nothing needs to.

They are larger than the others. You cannot see them clearly, but you can see their shapes shifting, their hooks repositioning, their bodies turning in the dark. They are perhaps half a meter in length, perhaps more. They move with a deliberation that the others lack — not the frantic reaching of the climbers or the mechanical flowing of the flowers, but something slower, more purposeful. They are thinking, or something like thinking. They are waiting, or something like waiting.

For what, you do not know.

They do not come down. They do not need to. The moisture they need condenses directly onto their bodies from the saturated air, pooling in the folds of their skin, running into mouths that open only to drink and close again. They do not need the nozzles. They do not need the grating. They have adapted to the ceiling as a separate environment, a separate system, a separate life.

They may be the originals. The first iteration. The prototype from which the others were derived. Or they may be something else entirely — a different product line, a different phase of production, a different purpose that you cannot discern from where you stand. They have markings too, you are certain. They are catalogued too. They are part of the system too. But they are also apart from it, hovering above, watching (if they can watch) the endless cycling below.

They have been there longest. They will be there after everything else stops. They do not know they are waiting. They do not know what waiting is.

They sing, because singing is what they do.

---

**The Chamber Itself**

Is the chamber a creature?

It processes. It circulates. It maintains equilibrium. It takes in energy — the electricity that runs the machinery, the water that feeds the nozzles — and it produces something, though you cannot see the final product, only the intermediate stages. It grows nothing, but it sustains everything within it. It dies if the power fails, if the water stops, if the balance is disrupted. It lives as long as the balance holds.

It has no brain, but it has logic. It has no will, but it has purpose. It was designed, built, filled, and left running. It does not know it exists, but it persists, and persistence is a kind of existence, a kind of life.

The creatures inside it are its cells. Its organs. Its systems. They work together without knowing they work together. They produce without knowing what they produce. They cycle without knowing they cycle. They are the chamber's body, and the chamber is their world, and neither knows the other exists.

This is what lives in the Condensation Chamber: a single organism made of thousands of parts, none of which know they are parts. A factory that has forgotten it is a factory. A life that has forgotten it is alive.

It is waiting for something.

It does not know what.

It will wait forever.
