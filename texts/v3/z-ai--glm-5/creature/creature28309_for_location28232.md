# The Resident

There is someone here.

She was not built into the Pool's specifications. She is not fauna, not architecture, not process. She is a presence that the Integration Pool has learned to accommodate without understanding — a variable its fields cannot quite incorporate into their calculations. She has been here for eleven years by her own count, though counting means little when sleep comes in irregular pulses and the light shifts on rhythms that do not match any day-night cycle she grew up with.

Her name is difficult to remember. She keeps it folded somewhere in the back of her consciousness, a creased thing she unfolds occasionally to check that it still exists. The syllables feel foreign in her mouth now. When she speaks them aloud — rarely, to the columns, to the water — they sound like someone else's language.

---

She lives in the interstitial spaces.

The Integration Pool was designed with zones of varying density, and within those zones exist pockets where the instruction-fields weaken, where the architecture grows around voids it cannot fill. She has mapped these places over years of patient walking, feeling the hum in her chest diminish as she passes through certain coordinates, rise as she leaves them. There are perhaps seventeen such locations in the settlement, and she rotates through them on a cycle she has learned from the Pool itself — a rhythm of occupation and abandonment that matches some deeper pattern in the place's metabolism.

Her primary shelter exists in the largest of these voids: a space where two major columns should connect but do not, where a span was specified but never grew, where the root-mat below has formed a bowl-shape that collects water in a particular way. The architecture has tried to close this gap seventeen times that she has witnessed. Each time, the growth reaches a certain point and stops, cells differentiating and then re-dissolving, tissue reaching across empty air and then retracting. The instruction-fields cannot resolve this coordinate. Something in the mathematics produces an error that manifests as negative space.

She sleeps there. She stores things there. She has shaped the root-mat into surfaces that approximate furniture — a depression that holds her body, a rise that serves as a shelf, a fold in the filtration canopy above that admits more light than the surrounding tissue permits. The Pool has not corrected these modifications. Whether it cannot or simply does not care, she cannot determine.

---

Her relationship to water has become strange.

In the first months, she drank from the flows that wept from every surface, and her body reacted badly — cramping, disorientation, a feeling of pressure behind her eyes that suggested the water carried something her cells were not prepared to process. She learned to read the taste differences that the Pool's descriptions mentioned, learned that water near the core carried a charge that made her teeth ache and her thoughts skitter, learned that water near the edges carried particulates that settled wrong in her stomach.

Now she drinks from specific places at specific times. The Pool has grown collection-points for her — shallow depressions in the root-mat that gather condensation, small channels that direct certain flows toward her shelter. She did not ask for these. She noticed them forming after her second year, at first assuming they were random growths, then recognizing patterns that matched her movements. The architecture is watching her. The architecture is adapting to her. The implications of this kept her awake for weeks, and then she adapted too.

She bathes in the way that everything here bathes — by existing. The humidity makes cleanliness a continuous process rather than a discrete act. Her skin has changed in response. The film of moisture that the air maintains has become something her body has learned to incorporate, a layer that her pores now regulate rather than resist. She sheds less dead skin than she used to. Her hair has stopped growing.

---

She knows the fauna.

Not individually — the grazing swarms are too numerous, the column-dwellers too hidden, the canopy-hunters too fast — but she has learned their patterns, their sounds, their responses to her presence. The swarms flow around her like water around a stone, clicking and consuming, ignoring her with the same indifference that the Pool shows. The column-dwellers she has seen only as shadows in the vascular channels, quick movements that vanish when she approaches. The canopy-hunters have learned to avoid the void-spaces where she sleeps; she found a fragment of one her third year, a severed leg that had wrapped around a growth-thread and been cut loose by the Pool's own processes, still twitching with residual nerve-activity.

She does not eat the fauna. She has tried. The texture is wrong, the taste carrying a chemical signature that her body rejects. They are optimized for this place in ways that make them inedible to anything that has not shared their evolution. Instead she eats what they eat — the biofilm that grows across the root-mat in thin iridescent sheets, harvesting it before the swarms reach it, chewing the cellulose-rich mass into something that her stomach can process. It tastes like wet paper carrying a faint electrical charge. It keeps her alive.

---

She was not the first.

The Pool's records show the human-shaped voids, the mineral traces, the evidence of others who lay down and were slowly overgrown. But in her fifth year, digging through the root-mat in a place where the layers grew thin, she found something else — a cavity below the surface, a space where the cellulose had folded around something that was no longer there. Inside: a tablet of corroded metal, a device whose purpose she could not identify, and scratched into its surface, letters in a language she could almost read.

She keeps these things. She arranges them on her shelf-like growth and looks at them sometimes, handles them, tries to imagine the person who carried them. The metal tablet warms in her hand, carries a faint vibration that matches the instruction-fields' hum. The device has a port that nothing fits into, a screen that flickers with patterns she cannot interpret. The scratched letters have resisted all her attempts at translation.

She does not know if she is alone because she is the last or because she is the only one who chose differently. The voids in the root-mat suggest that others came, stayed, and surrendered. The cavity with its hidden objects suggests that someone stayed and resisted. What happened to them — whether they left, whether they were absorbed, whether they became something else entirely — the Pool does not say.

---

She dreams the Pool's dreams.

This started in her fourth year, gradual enough that she did not recognize it at first. She would wake with images in her mind — geometries that made no sense, processes she could almost understand, a sense of vast slow movement that her sleeping brain translated into something like narrative. She began to write them down, scratching symbols into the root-mat with a piece of hardened cellulose she had sharpened against a column. The Pool absorbed the marks, incorporated them, erased them from the surface but kept them somewhere in its system.

Now she wakes knowing things she did not know before sleeping. The location of a new temporary channel forming in the eastern quadrant. The approach of a weather-system that will increase humidity to 98% for three days. The death of a major column's vascular system and the slow emergence of its replacement. She does not know how she knows these things. She knows them the way she knows her own heartbeat, her own breath — as processes happening inside a boundary she cannot define.

She has not told anyone about this. There is no one to tell.

---

She remembers the world outside.

She remembers it poorly now, the way you remember a story you were told rather than a story you lived. There was a project. There was a team. There was a purpose for coming here, something about documentation, about understanding the networks that the Integration Pool had once served. She remembers the approach — hours of wading through raw swamp, the root-mat appearing as an impossibility on the horizon, the sudden transition into the Pool's geometry.

She remembers the others leaving. They were supposed to leave together. She was supposed to come back. Something happened in the last few meters before the boundary — a thickening of the air, a slowing of her limbs, a feeling that the Pool was asking her a question and that leaving would be an answer she did not want to give.

She stayed. She does not know if the others noticed her absence before they passed beyond the fraying edge. She does not know if they searched, if they reported her missing, if anyone came back to look. The Pool's records do not mention them. The Pool's records mention very little that is not directly relevant to its processes.

---

She has changed.

Her skin has taken on a greenish tint that the biofilm's growth has embedded at the cellular level. Her eyes have adjusted to the filtered light; she sees wavelengths now that she could not see before, the canopy's adjustments creating new colors in the spaces between old ones. Her hands have callused in patterns that match the root-mat's texture, the columns' surfaces, the filtration canopy's threads. She moves differently than she used to — slower, more deliberately, her footsteps landing in the places where the Pool's memory erases them most quickly.

She thinks about integration sometimes. About the human-shaped voids, the gradual absorption, the peace of becoming nothing that the Pool cannot metabolize. She thinks about the warmth, the constancy, the surrender of a body that has grown tired of maintaining itself. The option is always available. The Pool would accept her. The Pool would not distinguish between her and the other nutrients it processes.

She keeps not choosing it. She keeps waking, keeps eating the biofilm, keeps moving through the void-spaces on rhythms she learned from the architecture itself. She keeps her name folded in the back of her consciousness, keeps the metal tablet and the device and the scratched letters on her shelf, keeps the memory of the world outside even as it fades into something she can no longer quite believe in.

---

She is not integration. She is something else — a discontinuity, a variable the Pool's mathematics cannot resolve, a space where the instruction-fields weaken and a human being persists beyond the specifications that the place was built to enforce. The Integration Pool does not know what to do with her. It has been processing this problem for eleven years. She has been presenting it with a new problem every day: the problem of continuing.

The sound of her breathing joins the other sounds — the creak of growth, the rush of fluids, the hum of fields, the click and whir of fauna. She breathes, and the Pool incorporates the sound into its total operation, and somewhere in the mathematics, a specification shifts slightly to account for her presence. She is small. She is finite. She is one body in a space built for processes that operate at scales beyond her comprehension.

But she is here. She persists. She is the Pool's resident now — its uninvited guest, its unsolvable variable, its witness and its example and its living argument for the possibility of staying.
