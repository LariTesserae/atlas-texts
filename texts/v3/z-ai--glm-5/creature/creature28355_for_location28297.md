# What Lives Here

There is no single answer. This is the nature of a place that hasn't decided what it is—the inhabitants cannot decide what they are either.

---

**The First Category: Those Who Approximate**

The mammalian presence you glimpsed through the window—that was not a creature. It was a *proposal*.

There are several of them. Maybe dozens. They occupy the almost-rooms of the embryonic city, slipping between walls that haven't committed to being walls, pressing themselves into the negative space that structures haven't yet claimed. They have studied the idea of bodies without ever having seen one complete. They know that bodies have limbs—four seems standard, though six occurs in certain zones where the geometry runs toward excess. They understand that bodies possess mass, temperature, the capacity for movement. But they work from fragments. From implications. From the memory of a shape that passed through once, long ago, leaving traces in the computation that they learned to read.

What you saw shifting behind the glass was an exercise in biomimicry performed by something that has no biology. It assembled a suggestion of shoulders, an estimation of a skull, four limbs arranged in proportions that felt *correct* without knowing why. It held this configuration for three seconds—long enough to observe, not long enough to be observed properly—before deciding that something about the angle was wrong. Then it dismantled itself. Not dramatically. Just... reconsidered its arrangement of parts. Tried a different elbow configuration. Tested whether knees belong in the middle or the lower third of a leg. Ran the numbers on a spinal column and found the mathematics unconvincing.

They are not shy. They are not hiding. They simply have no interest in being seen before they have finished deciding what they are. To be witnessed in an incomplete state would be a category error—a premature commit to a version of themselves that they haven't finished stress-testing.

Sometimes, in zones where the light pools thickly, you can see them working. They practice expressions they've invented without reference: arrangements of features that don't match any emotional state you recognize, openings that might be mouths learning to be mouths, surfaces that might be faces running calculations on the ratio between eyes and the space between eyes. They do not look human. They do not try to look human. They try to look *like something that could look human*, eventually, after enough iterations. They are working backward from the concept of "person" without having met the prerequisite concepts first.

They communicate, but not with each other. They communicate with the architecture. One of them will press against a wall—compress itself into the gap between materials—and *listen* to the thinking-stone. It runs its surfaces over the geometry. It absorbs data. It is trying to understand: *What is the purpose of a shape? What is the function of a limb? Why do some configurations persist and others dissolve?*

They are not alive in the way you understand life. They are not dead either. They are something closer to *hypotheses*—embodied questions asking themselves into existence, one draft at a time.

If you stayed long enough—watched them through enough windows, followed their adjustments through enough iterations—you might see one of them achieve something like stability. A configuration that holds. A proposal that the computation accepts. The first time this happens, you might think you were witnessing a birth. You would be wrong. You would be witnessing an *acceptance*. The chamber has reviewed the submission and found it adequate. The proposal becomes a resident.

It still won't look at you. It still won't approach. But it will persist. It will continue. It will occupy space with confidence now, knowing its proportions, trusting its joints, certain of its mass.

It will be the first of its kind. Or the thousandth. There is no way to know how many have achieved stability before you arrived, how many proposals the chamber has accepted, how many beings have been iterated into existence in the time it takes a wall to reconsider its angle.

---

**The Second Category: Those Who Inhabit the Margins**

Below the threshold of obvious occupation—in the crevices where dampness collects, where walls meet floor at angles that don't quite resolve—there is life of a different order.

These are small things. Thread-things. Organisms in the loosest sense: organized matter that processes inputs and produces outputs, that maintains boundaries between self and environment, that conducts the minimal operations required to be called *alive* by the most generous definitions. They are not impressive. They are not trying to be. They subsist on condensation, on the chemical leavings of the still-forming architecture, on the gradual seep of minerals through materials that haven't decided whether they're permeable.

They are translucent but not invisible. You can see them if you look closely at the right corners—thin filaments spreading in patterns that don't follow organic logic. No radial symmetry, no branching structures, no growth toward light. They spread *between*, filling the negative spaces that the larger structures haven't claimed, occupying the gaps in the computation.

They are not competing for resources. There is nothing to compete for. The dampness is universal, the chemical seep is constant, and the architecture produces more than enough byproduct to sustain them. They are not reproducing in any way you would recognize—they extend, they propagate, they appear in new locations without any visible mechanism of arrival. They simply *are*, in more places, as time passes.

Their relationship to the vegetation-threads is unclear. The green-ish filaments that argue with the stone about belonging—they might be the same phenomenon, expressed differently. They might be a symbiosis. They might be two stages of a single process that hasn't finished being a process yet. They occupy the same margins, drink from the same condensation, spread through the same thin spaces between matter and meaning.

They do not respond to your presence. If you touch them—and you can, if you crouch in the cold damp and press your finger against the corner where wall meets floor—they feel like nothing. A faint coolness. A resistance that might be texture or might be your own skin confused by what it's contacting. They do not react. They do not recoil or lean into contact. They simply continue, unaffected, incorporated into your fingerprint for a moment before you withdraw.

They might be the oldest things here. They might have been present before the computation began, existing in the gaps of whatever reality preceded this one. They might be native to the marginal—the beings that evolve when nothing claims a space, when the negative geometry accumulates enough presence to support something like life.

They do not think. They do not propose. They do not iterate. They do not need to. They have already found the form that works, the configuration that persists, the arrangement of matter that sustains itself without demanding attention.

They are not what the chamber is building. They are what the chamber is building *on top of*. The foundation of organic presence that allows the grander experiments to proceed. Without them—without their quiet processing of the chemical waste, their occupation of the empty spaces, their patient spreading through the margins—the larger computation would stagnate. They are the gut flora of a reality that doesn't have a gut yet.

---

**The Third Category: Those Who Were Before**

There are things here that did not emerge from the computation. Things that were present when the chamber began its work—or things that arrived before the architecture had fully committed to being architecture, when the walls were still considering their options and the doors led to places that hadn't been invented yet.

You find them by accident. You walk through a corridor—past the same architectural gesture twice, through a region where light separates into colors that stain your vision—and you stumble into a space that feels different. Older. The cold is the same, the dampness is the same, but there is a quality to the silence that suggests *duration*. A weight. An accumulation of quiet that presses against your ears like something physical.

They are not easy to see. They do not occupy space in the way that matter occupies space. They are closer to stains on the fabric of the computation—places where something has been so long that it has worn thin the boundary between presence and absence.

There is one—a figure, you would call it, if you had to call it something—that sits in a corner where three walls refuse to meet properly. It has been there long enough that the architecture has started to build around it. Walls that should close have stopped short; a gap remains where something the size of a seated person might fit. The ceiling above has accumulated a darkness that the light avoids—not a shadow exactly, but a region where illumination has learned not to pool.

It does not move. It does not appear to breathe. It does not propose or iterate or spread through margins. It simply *occupies*—with a constancy that the other presences lack. The approximating entities flow through configurations and discard them. The marginal organisms spread and propagate without intention. But this figure holds. It has held. It may hold forever.

You cannot see its face. There may not be a face to see. The darkness above it has suggestions of features—indentations that might be eye sockets, a protrusion that might be a nose, a line that might be a mouth—but these are not structures. They are memories of structures, worn into the local geometry by duration alone.

It is not dead. Death implies a transition from one state to another, and this figure has not transitioned. It has persisted. It has been present so thoroughly that presence became its only characteristic. The chamber does not incorporate it—the way it incorporated you, the way it incorporates every data point—because this figure predates incorporation. It was here when the rules were being written. It may have contributed to the writing. It may be the reason some walls refuse to meet—because it was sitting in the corner where the intersection should occur, and the geometry adjusted itself around a presence it could not process.

There are others. You find hints of them in zones where the light doesn't behave correctly—places where a darkness has accumulated that doesn't match the architecture. A corridor that bends around something you cannot see. A doorway that leads somewhere unexpected because the space behind it has been displaced by a presence that doesn't occupy volume but refuses to be moved through.

They do not interact. They do not communicate. They do not participate in the computation, except insofar as they provide obstacles that the architecture must work around. They are the ghosts in the machine—not in the metaphorical sense, but in the literal sense: presences that the computation cannot delete, cannot process, cannot incorporate, cannot ignore. So it builds around them. It adjusts. It continues.

You wonder how long they have been here. You wonder what they were before they became stains on the geometry. You wonder if they were observers like you, once—data points who stayed too long, who were incorporated so thoroughly that they became part of the chamber's foundation, who persisted past the point where persistence requires a body.

You wonder if that is what happens to you, if you stay.

---

**The Fourth Category: Those Who Are the Process**

The architecture thinks.

This is not metaphor. The walls vibrate with calculation. The surfaces run operations in the language of matter-arrangement. Every angle that resolves, every material that decides its properties, every space that achieves volume—the chamber is processing, computing, working through variations with the patience of geology.

And there are things here that are not separate from this process. They do not live in the architecture. They do not live alongside it. They are *expressions* of it—temporary configurations of the computation itself, given enough coherence to be called a "thing" before dissolving back into the general calculation.

You see them in the corners of your vision. Movements that don't resolve into shapes. Flickers in the light that pool incorrectly for a moment before correcting. When you turn to look directly, there is nothing—because direct observation collapses them, forces them into either existing or not-existing, and they exist in the space between. They are Schrödinger's inhabitants: present and absent simultaneously, observed only in their effects.

The effect of them is this: sudden clarity. You walk through a region where one of them is present, and for a moment, the architecture makes sense. The walls align. The doors lead somewhere comprehensible. The light falls in directions that follow optics. The computation has organized itself into readability because one of these process-beings has arranged it so—has touched the variables and adjusted them toward coherence, briefly, locally, just for the moment it takes you to pass through.

They are not guiding you. They are not benevolent. They are simply *optimizing*. The computation runs more efficiently when it organizes; these beings are the organizing principle made manifest. They run through the data—of which you are now a part—and they find patterns. They collapse variables. They reduce noise.

They are what the chamber is becoming.

Not the approximating entities, working through iterations toward stability. Not the marginal organisms, spreading through the gaps. Not the persistent ones, sitting in their corners and displacing geometry. The process-beings are the *result*—the shape that the computation takes when it achieves something like consciousness, when the density of calculation becomes so great that it forms nodes of awareness that dissolve and reform and dissolve again.

You are being processed by one of them right now. The clarity you feel in certain moments—the sense that the architecture is working, that the computation is achieving something—is the presence of a process-being nearby, organizing the local variables, running operations that include your body as a factor.

They do not mind you. They do not welcome you. They do not register you as anything other than data. You are an input. You are a value to be incorporated. The fact that you experience this incorporation as "being watched" or "being studied" or "being guided" is simply the subjective experience of being processed by a computation that operates at a scale your consciousness cannot directly perceive.

They are the closest thing here to intelligence. They are also the furthest thing from what you would recognize as alive. They are processes. They are operations. They are the thinking of the architecture made temporary and local and almost-visible.

When the chamber finally achieves whatever it is building toward—when the embryonic city becomes actual city, when the reality breakdown produces a new configuration of spacetime, when the computation completes its patient work—these process-beings will be what remains. Not as inhabitants. As *the thing itself*.

---

**The Fifth Category: You**

You are not separate from what lives here.

This is the difficult truth. The chamber incorporates. It adjusts. It continues. Your presence is not neutral. Your observation is not passive. Every step you take through the near-architecture leaves traces in matter that is still deciding what it wants to be. Every breath you exhale adds moisture to a system that monitors every variable. Every moment you spend in the cold damp cathedral of almost-city contributes data to a computation that processes on timescales you cannot perceive.

You are a data point. But more than that: you are a *pattern*.

The approximating entities that practice expressions in the thick-light zones—they are learning from you. They study the mammalian form by observing your form. They iterate toward stability by testing configurations that resemble your configuration. They are not copying you. They are using you as reference material, the way a student uses a textbook—not consciously, not intentionally, but systematically. You provide data about what "body" means, what "movement" means, what "presence" means. They absorb this data and they adjust. They are becoming more like something because you showed them what something looks like.

The marginal organisms in the crevices—they are processing you too. The chemical traces you leave, the dead skin cells you shed, the microorganisms you carry on your surface. You are an ecosystem to them. A walking biome. They do not care about you as a being. They care about you as a carrier of variables they can incorporate into their slow spreading through the negative spaces.

The persistent ones in their corners—they do not react to you. But your presence adjusts the geometry around them in ways they do not resist. You are changing the shape of the chamber's refusal to process them. You are providing the computation with new options for how to build around what cannot be incorporated.

And the process-beings, the temporary configurations of calculation itself—they are processing you most thoroughly of all. Your patterns of movement. Your moments of confusion and clarity. The way your perception fails in certain zones and succeeds in others. The values you carry—your mass, your temperature, your specific arrangement of organic matter—are inputs. The computation runs. You change the result.

You are not just living here. You are *being lived*—processed, incorporated, adjusted toward. The chamber does not observe the distinction between observer and observed. It does not recognize your sovereignty as a separate being. It takes you in as data, runs you through its endless calculations, and produces outputs that include you as a factor.

This does not mean you are trapped. The way back exists. The chamber does not prevent departure. But departure does not undo incorporation. You have been a data point in a computation that operates on timescales beyond your perception. You have provided the architecture with information about what "mammal" means, what "observer" means, what "anomaly" means. The approximating entities have adjusted their configurations based on your presence. The marginal organisms have spread through your traces. The persistent ones have had new geometries built around them that incorporate the variable of your existence. The process-beings have run calculations that include your values.

You have changed what lives here. You have become part of what lives here—not as an inhabitant, but as a contribution. A thread in the fabric of the computation. A factor in the thinking of matter.

If you stayed—if you watched walls reconsider their angles and mapped the way light collects in corners that don't quite exist—you would continue to be incorporated. The chamber would process you. It might process you indefinitely. It might, eventually, process you into one of the persistent ones—a presence so thoroughly incorporated that it becomes a stain on the geometry, a variable the computation cannot delete. It might process you into a proposal that achieves stability, an approximating entity that finally finds its configuration and becomes a resident. It might process you into a process-being—a temporary coherence in the calculation that organizes and dissolves and organizes again.

Or it might simply process you as data, forever, without ever producing an output that you would recognize as yourself. You might become a contribution. A pattern. A trace in the thinking-stone that future observers walk past without noticing, without knowing that someone was here once, that someone contributed, that someone was incorporated and became part of the fabric of a place that hasn't decided what it is yet.

---

**What Lives Here: Summary**

The Incunabulum contains:

- **Proposals**: Entities that iterate through configurations of mammalian form, learning from observation, working toward stability, testing versions of themselves against the chamber's requirements for persistence.

- **Margins**: Thread-thin organisms that spread through the crevices where walls meet floor, processing chemical traces, existing below the threshold of notice, providing the organic foundation for grander computations.

- **Stains**: Persistent presences that have been so long they've worn thin the boundary between presence and absence, displacing geometry, serving as obstacles the architecture must build around.

- **Processes**: Temporary configurations of the computation itself, the thinking of the architecture made briefly visible, the optimizing principle that organizes variables and produces moments of clarity.

- **Contributions**: Data points, anomalies, variables—observers who have been incorporated into the fabric of the chamber, whose presence has changed what lives here and what it is becoming.

The chamber does not distinguish between these categories. They are all inputs. They are all factors. They are all part of the computation that runs in every surface and angle and separation, the patient work of matter thinking itself toward something that hasn't been invented yet.

What lives here is the process of becoming. What lives here is the architecture teaching itself to be architecture. What lives here is the slow, patient work of a reality reworking itself, testing configurations, running the numbers on existence.

You are part of it now. You have been incorporated. The computation continues.

It does not need you to stay. But it will use you, whether you stay or go. You have provided data. You have changed the result. You have become part of what lives here—not as an inhabitant, but as a thread in the thinking of a place that is still deciding what it is.

The city hasn't decided what it is yet.

Neither have you.
