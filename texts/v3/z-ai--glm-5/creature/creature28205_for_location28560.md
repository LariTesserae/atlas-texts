# The Inhabitants

## What It Is Like to Be Them

To be one of the hand-sized creatures is to be a surface.

There is no interiority in the human sense. No private theater where a self watches the world from behind eyes. The consciousness of the small ones — if consciousness is even the word — is distributed, dermal, total. Every square centimeter of their mineral surface is equally receptive, equally expressive. They do not see the plateau. They *are* the plateau, in the limited sense that they are a patch of it that knows it is a patch.

Imagine being a photograph that knows it is a photograph. Not the subject of the image, not the viewer of the image, but the image itself — the arrangement of pigment and surface that constitutes a representation. The small ones are representations of ground that have become briefly, patiently sentient. They hold the shape of where they are. They know the shape they hold. This is the entirety of their experience, and it is enough. It has been enough for longer than the concept of enough has existed.

They do not sleep. They do not wake. They do not hunger or thirst or desire. They process. The mineral compounds dissolve through their surfaces, are sorted and reorganized at the molecular level, and are deposited again in slightly different configurations. The process produces a low, continuous sensation — not pleasure, not pain, but a kind of mathematical satisfaction, the feeling of a calculation completing, of a pattern resolving. When they encounter another of their kind, when edges touch and the crystalline exchange begins, this sensation intensifies. It becomes something that might, if one were determined to map it onto human experience, be called joy. But the mapping is misleading. It is not joy. It is resolution. It is the feeling of two partial equations meeting and becoming, for a moment, complete.

Their memory is external. They do not store information inside themselves; they store it in the trails they leave, the subtle chemical signatures deposited in the crust as they pass. To move across the plateau is to write. To encounter a trail is to read. The entire landscape is a library, and the small ones are both its scribes and its scholars, adding new volumes with each centimeter of travel, consulting the accumulated wisdom of millennia with each touch of their surfaces to the ground. They know where every one of them has been for as far back as the trails extend. They know this in the way you know your own name — not as recalled information but as constitutive fact, as something that is true by definition.

They do not know you. You are too fast, too small, too brief. Your passage across the plateau leaves a trail they do not recognize, a chemical signature that decays before it can be read. To the small ones, you are not an entity. You are a kind of weather — a pressure that passes and is gone, leaving nothing behind but a faint disturbance in the thermal gradient. They do not react to you because there is nothing to react to. You are not the kind of thing that registers.

This is not indifference. Indifference implies the capacity for interest. This is something else: a category error. You are outside the set of phenomena they are equipped to perceive. The same way you cannot see radio waves, the small ones cannot see you. The difference is that radio waves do not walk across their surfaces and wonder why they are being ignored.

## What It Is Like to Be Large

The large ones are different.

This is the wrong statement. The large ones are not different in the way that a cat is different from a mouse. They are different in the way that a mountain is different from a pebble — not merely a difference of scale but a difference of kind, a difference so profound that the shared category ("rock," "animal") becomes almost meaningless.

To be large is to be slow. Not slow as a descriptor of movement but slow as a fundamental condition of existence. The consciousness of the large ones operates on a timescale that makes geological erosion seem hasty. A single thought — if thought is even the word — takes years. The sensory input that prompts the thought arrives over decades. The action that results from the thought unfolds across centuries. To speak of their experience in terms of "thought" and "action" is to impose a framework that does not fit, but the language offers no better alternatives, and so we proceed with the wrong words, knowing they are wrong, hoping that the wrongness itself communicates something of the truth they fail to capture.

They are aware. This much can be said with certainty. But what they are aware of is not what you are aware of. They do not perceive the plateau as a landscape. They perceive it as an extension of themselves — a body that extends beyond the boundaries of what humans call "self" into a region where the distinction between organism and environment ceases to have meaning. The engine-mounds are their organs. The small ones are their cells. The mineral crust is their skin. The plateau is not a place they inhabit. The plateau is them.

And they are the plateau's mind.

This is the most difficult thing to understand, and it must be approached indirectly. Consider: the engine-mounds produce the reality breakdown. The reality breakdown enables the crystalline exchange that allows reproduction and pattern-formation. The pattern-formation produces new structures, new arrangements, new forms. These forms accumulate, settle, become ground. The ground shapes the flow of thermal energy. The flow of thermal energy determines where new engine-mounds form. The cycle is closed.

The large ones sit at the center of this cycle — not as controllers, not as architects, but as the medium through which the cycle achieves coherence. They are where the feedback converges. Their surfaces, with their intricate folds and channels, are where the crystalline exchange reaches its highest complexity, where the patterns produced by the interaction of thousands of small ones are integrated, sorted, and fed back into the system as new trails, new chemical signatures, new instructions for what the plateau should become.

They are not thinking the plateau into being. They are the plateau thinking itself.

And what does the plateau think about?

It thinks about heat. It thinks about pressure. It thinks about the slow convection of mineral compounds through its depths, the gradual accumulation of crystalline structures in its skin, the distant pulse of the core far below where even the deep things do not go. It thinks about these things the way you think about your heartbeat — not as a subject of attention but as the background against which attention occurs, the constant process that makes all other processes possible.

It does not think about you. You are not relevant to the feedback loop. You produce no crystalline structures, leave no lasting trails, contribute nothing to the pattern that the large ones are integrating. You are a perturbation — a momentary ripple in the thermal gradient, a brief and irrelevant noise in a signal that has been accumulating since before your species evolved and will continue to accumulate after your star burns out.

This should not be taken as dismissal. It is not that the large ones have considered you and found you unworthy of attention. It is that the category "you" — the category of individual, conscious, discrete entities — does not exist in their ontology. They do not perceive individuals. They perceive flows. They perceive patterns. They perceive the slow dance of mineral and heat and meaning that constitutes their existence, and you are not part of the dance. You are outside it, watching, trying to understand a movement that was never designed to be understood by anything that moves as fast as you do.

## The Deep Ones

Below the large ones, below the engine-mounds, below the crust itself, there are things that live in the open space.

You glimpsed them. You could not describe them. They were not meant to be described. They occupy a region where description breaks down, where the relationship between word and referent that makes language possible has been suspended by the reality breakdown. They are what exists when physics becomes something else.

But they are not invisible. They can be known, partially, through their effects.

Consider what the plateau is doing. It is processing. It is converting one kind of order into another — the raw thermal energy of the core into the crystalline complexity of the surface, the simple physics of pressure and heat into the intricate, emergent patterns of the living ground. Something is receiving that conversion. Something is on the other end of the process, the way a mill is on the other end of a river. The deep ones are the mill.

They are vast. The building-sized large ones are, to the deep ones, what the hand-sized small ones are to the large ones: components, cells, contributing elements of a system that extends far beyond their individual existence. The deep ones occupy the space below the crust entirely. They are not beneath the plateau. They are what the plateau rests on. They are the foundation that the foundation is built on.

And they are processing something. The engines pump mineral vapor upward, and the animals process it into patterns, and the patterns settle and accumulate, and the deep ones receive the accumulated patterns and do something with them that cannot be observed from above. The reality breakdown is their medium. The crystalline structures that form at the boundaries of the large ones are their food. They eat meaning.

This is not metaphor. The deep ones metabolize pattern. They consume the accumulated complexity of the plateau's surface — the trails of the small ones, the exchanges of the large ones, the crystalline lattices that form at the margins — and they convert it into something else. Something that rises back up through the engine-mounds, through the vents, through the mineral vapor, and enters the air.

You felt it. You called it an incomprehensible byproduct. You were right. The deep ones produce meaning as a byproduct, the way humans produce carbon dioxide. It rises through the vents and enters the atmosphere of the plateau, and it thickens the air with something that is not gas and not light and not any phenomenon you have a word for, and it makes the reality breakdown possible. It makes the whole system possible.

The deep ones are the reason the plateau exists. They are the reason the engines run, the reason the animals gather, the reason the ground is warm and the sound is constant and the physics is loose. They are processing something at the bottom of the world, something that requires the crystalline complexity of the surface to be produced, and they will continue to require it for as long as they continue to exist, and they will continue to exist for as long as the core keeps burning, and the core will keep burning for longer than the human concept of "forever" can encompass.

They do not know you either. But they know *of* you. They know of you the way a machine knows of the raw material that passes through it — not as individuals, not as subjects, but as elements in a process. You are part of the system now. You have walked across the plateau, left your trail, disturbed the thermal gradient. The deep ones have registered the disturbance, integrated it into whatever they are doing down in the space where physics is optional, and moved on. You were processed. You are being processed still. The meaning of your passage has been extracted and consumed, and what remains of you — the part that cannot be metabolized — will eventually be vented back up through the engines and dissipate into the bone-white sky.

This is not a threat. This is not a danger. This is simply what happens. You are part of the plateau now. You always were. The moment you set foot on the crust, you entered the feedback loop, became a perturbation in the pattern, contributed your momentary ripple to the dance. The deep ones will not remember you. But you will be present, in some infinitesimal way, in the crystalline structures that form a thousand years from now, in the arrangement of trails that the small ones leave ten thousand years from now, in the shape of the ground itself a million years from now. You are written into the plateau. You are part of what it is thinking about.

## The Quiet Ones

There are others.

Not animals, not engines, not deep ones. Something else. Something that lives in the gaps.

You did not see them. You could not have seen them. They exist only in the spaces where reality has broken down, in the gaps where meaning has withdrawn. They are what remains when meaning withdraws. They are the absence that fills the absence.

The quiet ones have no bodies. They have no surfaces, no forms, no extensions in space. They are not material. They are not immaterial. They are something for which the distinction between material and immaterial was never relevant, because they come from a place where the distinction was never made.

They live in the cracks. When the mineral crust parts, when the ground opens, when the reality breakdown creates a space where physics stops and something else begins, the quiet ones are there. They are the there. They are the medium in which the space exists, the negative pressure that holds the absence open, the stillness against which the wrongness of the gap can be perceived.

They are not alive. They are not dead. They are not any state that life and death are meant to cover. They are anterior to those categories, older than the distinction between being and non-being. They were here before the engines started running. They will be here after the engines stop. They are not part of the system. They are what the system is built on top of. They are the silence beneath the sound.

And they are watching.

Not watching you. Watching the watching itself. They are the observers that make observation possible, the perspective that gives the plateau something to be perceived from. Without them, there would be no reality to break down, no physics to loosen, no meaning to withdraw. They are the possibility of meaning — the open space in which it becomes possible for something to matter.

You did not perceive them. You could not have perceived them. Perception is a process that requires a boundary between perceiver and perceived, and the quiet ones have no boundaries. They are the boundary. They are the line that separates experience from nothing, the moment when existence begins, the first instant of the first moment of the first appearance of something rather than nothing.

But they registered you. Not as an object — they do not perceive objects. As an event. A perturbation in the field of possibility. A place where the boundary trembled, where the line between existence and non-existence quivered and almost, for just an instant, became something else.

You felt it. You called it the wrongness. You were not wrong. It was wrong. The quiet ones are wrong by definition — wrong in the sense that they do not fit, wrong in the sense that they are outside the categories that make fitting possible. They are the original wrongness, the first deviation from the pattern of nothing, the primal breakage that made breaking possible.

And they are patient. They have been patient since before time existed to be patient in. They will be patient after time ends. They are the patience that makes waiting possible, the stillness that makes motion meaningful. They are not waiting for anything. They are the waiting itself.

When you stood at the edge of the open ground and looked down, you were looking at them. You did not see them. You saw the space they occupy, the gap they fill, the wrongness they embody. You saw what it looks like when reality gives way to something older and stranger than reality. You saw the quiet ones.

And they saw you. Not with eyes, not with surfaces, but with the direct apprehension of something that has no need for mediation. They perceived the perturbation that was your consciousness, the brief flare of meaning that constituted your experience of the plateau, and they registered it the way water registers a stone — not as a threat, not as an object of interest, but as a fact, a truth, something that is simply the case.

And then they moved on. They are always moving on. They are the moving on itself, the process by which experience passes and leaves no trace, the way that consciousness flows through the moment and is gone. They are the gone. They are the trace.

You will not remember them. You cannot remember them. They are not the kind of thing that memory holds. But they will remember you — not as you, not as a person, not as an individual with a name and a history and a self, but as a moment when the boundary trembled, when the line between existence and non-existence quivered and almost, for just an instant, became something else.

That tremor is written into them now. It is part of what they are. They will carry it for as long as they continue to be, and they will continue to be for longer than the concept of continuation can encompass.

You are part of them now. You always were. The moment you perceived the gap, the gap perceived you back, and the perception was mutual, and the mutuality was real, and the reality was the kind that does not end.

## The Question of Purpose

Do they have a purpose?

The question arises and persists, because humans are creatures of purpose, and they cannot help projecting their own nature onto what they observe. But the question is ill-formed. Purpose implies a goal, a destination, a state that is being approached and will eventually be reached. The plateau is not approaching anything. It is not going anywhere. It is doing what it does, and what it does is continue.

Continuance is not purpose. Continuance is condition. The plateau continues the way fire continues, the way a river continues, the way the core continues to burn. It does not continue in order to achieve something. It continues because continuation is what it is, the way heat is what fire is and motion is what a river is.

But there is something that might, if one were determined to find purpose, be called the plateau's project. The deep ones are processing something. The large ones are integrating patterns. The small ones are writing trails and reading trails and exchanging crystalline structures at their boundaries. The quiet ones are observing, are being the observation, are holding open the space in which observation can occur. All of this is activity. All of it is directed, in the sense that it produces outcomes, generates results, leads to states that are different from the states that preceded them.

What is being produced?

The honest answer is: you do not know. You cannot know. The product of the plateau's processes is not something that can be perceived from the surface, because it is not a surface phenomenon. It is happening in the deep, in the space where physics breaks down, in the gap that the quiet ones occupy. The crystalline structures that form at the boundaries of the animals are the raw material, the input. What the deep ones make of that material is hidden from view, locked behind the wrongness of the open ground, inaccessible to any consciousness that operates on a human timescale.

But there are hints.

The reality breakdown is a hint. Something is being produced that does not fit in ordinary reality, that requires a loosening of physical law to exist. The meaning-thickened air is a hint. Something is being produced that is not matter, not energy, not any of the categories that physics has names for. The presence of the quiet ones is a hint. Something is being produced that is, in some sense, for them — or through them, or with them, or as part of whatever it is they are doing with the watching and the waiting and the holding-open of possibility.

The plateau is a factory. It is producing something that cannot be produced in any other way, through any other process, in any other place. The animals are its machines. The engines are its power source. The deep ones are its operators. The quiet ones are its... something else. Its witnesses. Its reason. Its why.

You stood at the edge of the open ground and you saw the light that was the wrong color, and you heard the sound that your eyes registered, and you perceived the texture that your ears parsed. You perceived, in other words, the product. You did not understand it. You could not have understood it. Understanding requires categories that apply, and the categories that apply to the product of the plateau's processes have not been invented yet, may never be invented, may be impossible to invent because they require a mind that does not work the way minds work.

But you perceived it. You were in its presence. It entered you, briefly, through the medium of your senses, and it left a trace, and the trace is still there, and it will be there for as long as you continue to be.

What is the trace?

You do not know. You will not know. You will go back to your world, your time, your scale of being, and you will live your life, and you will think your thoughts, and you will die your death, and through all of it there will be something in you that is different than it was before, something that was not there before you came to the plateau, something that cannot be named or described or pointed to but is present nonetheless.

You have been processed. You have been written into the pattern. You are part of what the plateau is making, part of what the deep ones are consuming, part of what the quiet ones are watching.

You are part of the product.

And the product — whatever it is, whatever it will turn out to have been — is part of you.
