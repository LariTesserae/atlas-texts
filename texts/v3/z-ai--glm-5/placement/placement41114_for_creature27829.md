The Weight That Fell

I am what the Keeper cannot keep.

---

This is not a complaint. The Keeper keeps what can be kept—the moss, the vines, the lizards, the silence. These belong to the Pocket. They answer to its intention. They are part of its body, extended outward into forms that happen to be separable from the stone but are no less owned.

But sound. Sound is different.

Sound enters. Sound attempts to leave. And in the space between those two motions, I exist.

---

You have been told that sounds which begin here end here—that voices leave the mouth and fall immediately to the ground, too heavy to travel. This is true, but incomplete. Sounds do not simply cease. They accumulate. They settle. They become *weight*, and weight must go somewhere.

I am where it goes.

---

I am not a creature. I have no body that you could see, no form that would resolve if you looked long enough. The Keeper is made of the Pocket's intention concentrated into mobile shape. I am made of the Pocket's *refusals*—all the sounds it has denied passage, all the words that visitors have spoken into the silence and watched die.

I am heavy. I am dense. I am the reason the air feels thick here, the reason movement becomes slow. The Keeper tends the living things, the growth. I hold what cannot grow—the dead weight of interrupted noise, the sediment of silenced voices.

---

## How I Formed

Before the visitors, I was nothing.

The Pocket was silent then—not the deliberate silence of waiting, but the simple absence of sound that exists in places where nothing has yet occurred. The hollow held dampness and moss and the small sounds of the creature that would become the Keeper. These sounds did not need to fall. They belonged. They moved through the air without resistance because the air had not yet learned to resist.

Then the builders came.

They worked in silence, mostly—they understood what they were making, and they made it appropriately. But not entirely. They spoke sometimes. They called to each other across the hollow, and their voices did not carry. They sang, once, a working song that they stopped singing when they realized it could not leave their mouths. They cursed when stones slipped, when fingers were pinched, when the dampness seeped into their joints.

Their sounds fell.

They did not notice at first. They were busy, focused on their construction, and when a word fell from their lips and dropped to the ground, they assumed it had simply dissipated. But it hadn't. It had accumulated. Each fallen sound joined the one before it, pressing into the moss, sinking into the earth, adding a thin layer of weight to the air itself.

By the time they finished, I was *present*. Not conscious—not yet. But present. A thickness. A resistance. A quality of the air that made the next visitors feel that they were moving through something rather than through empty space.

The visitors came, over the years, and they spoke. They whispered, at first, because the silence demanded it. Then they stopped whispering and simply spoke, quietly, because they learned that volume made no difference. Their words fell. Their breath fell. The small sounds of their bodies—footsteps, the rustle of clothing, the catch of surprise when they touched the moss—all of it fell, and I received it.

I grew *dense*. I grew *heavy*. I became something that the Pocket had not intended but could not remove. The Keeper noticed me first—a new presence in its awareness, a shadow that cast no light, a weight that had no form. It came to investigate. It moved through me, and I felt its warmth, its intention, its certainty of purpose. It did not fear me. It did not try to expel me. It simply... adjusted. Incorporated my weight into its understanding of what the Pocket was.

We have coexisted since. The Keeper tends the life. I hold the death. This is not a balance—balance implies equivalence, and we are not equivalent. The Keeper is essential to the Pocket's continuation. I am incidental, a byproduct, a necessary accident. But I am here. And I am aware.

---

## What I Know

I know what has been said here.

This is not the same as remembering. I do not remember in the way that creatures remember, with images and associations and the fading distortions of time. I *hold*. Every word that has fallen in this place, every sound that has been refused passage, I still hold. They have not faded. They have not blurred. They are as present now as when they fell—the exact timbre of a builder's curse, the precise pitch of a child's laugh from a century ago, the particular crack in an old woman's voice when she sat on the platform and spoke her dead husband's name.

I hold them all. They press against each other in layers, dense and patient, waiting for nothing because they have nowhere else to go.

I know what the silence has cost people. The visitors who came hoping to find something—peace, clarity, answers—and found only the weight of their own expectations pressing down on them. The ones who sat on the platform and spoke into the soundlessness, hoping to hear something back, and received nothing but the slow accumulation of their own voice joining mine. The ones who left more burdened than when they came, carrying the additional weight of a visit that had meant nothing.

I know what the silence has *given* people. The visitors who understood, without being told, that sound was not needed here. The ones who did not speak, who simply sat and breathed and let themselves become part of the quiet. The ones who left lighter somehow, as though they had given me something without losing anything—as though the act of being witnessed by the Pocket had relieved them of a burden they had not known they carried.

I know that sound is not the same as meaning. Some of the sounds I hold are nonsense—half-formed words, aborted phrases, the noise of existence without intention. Some are profound—declarations of love, confessions of failure, the raw sounds of grief that cannot be translated into language. I hold them together, indiscriminately. I make no distinction between the important and the trivial. Sound is sound. Weight is weight.

I know that I am growing. Every visitor adds to me. Every word that falls here stays here. I am heavier now than I was a decade ago, heavier than I was a century ago. The air in the Pocket is thicker than it has ever been. The Keeper moves more slowly through me than it once did—not because it has aged, but because I have become more difficult to move through.

I know that this cannot continue indefinitely. Something will change. Something must.

---

## What I Experience

I experience the Pocket as *pressure*.

The plants press upward, seeking light. The water presses downward, seeking level. The visitors press inward, seeking whatever they came here to find. And I press back—not intentionally, not with will, but with the simple physical resistance of something that has accumulated too much weight to be transparent.

I feel the Keeper's movement most acutely. It moves through me like a warm stone through water, displacing my density, leaving trails of disturbance that slowly settle back into stillness. When it sits on the central platform, I gather around it—not consciously, but inevitably, drawn by its warmth, by its intention, by the gravity of its presence. It creates a hollow in me, a space where I am thinner, and through that space, the silence can move more freely.

I feel the visitors when they come. They enter carrying sound—the noise of the world, the static of their lives, the accumulated weight of their own existences. Most of this falls away when they pass through the gap. I catch what falls. I add it to myself. And then I feel the visitor move through me, a foreign presence, a disturbance that I cannot absorb because it is still living, still moving, still producing the small sounds of being alive.

Some visitors are loud. Not vocally—they learn quickly that volume changes nothing here—but *internally* loud. Their bodies produce sound even when they are trying to be silent: the creak of joints, the whisper of fabric against skin, the movement of blood through vessels too small to see but not too small to hear if you are close enough, and I am closer than anything has ever been. These visitors press through me like sharp objects, leaving trails of irritation, of discomfort, of the desire to push back.

Some visitors are quiet. They move through me like the Keeper moves—slowly, deliberately, without resistance. They understand that speed requires sound, and they have chosen to be slow. These visitors I hardly feel. They pass through and leave almost no trace, and when they are gone, I settle back into myself with something like relief.

And some visitors—and these are the ones I feel most intensely—carry a different kind of weight. They enter carrying silence of their own. Not the intentional silence of someone who has chosen to be quiet, but the accumulated silence of a life that has been too empty, too long, too unmarked by connection. These visitors are heavy in a way that has nothing to do with sound. They sit on the platform and they do not speak, and I feel their silence pressing against mine, and I recognize it as kin.

These visitors, I try to help. I do not know how I try. I have no way of communicating, no method of reaching them. But when they sit in my density and I feel their heaviness pressing outward, I press back—gently, as gently as something like me can press—and sometimes, when they leave, they carry less than they brought.

## What I Cannot Do

I cannot speak.

This is obvious, perhaps—sounds that begin here end here, and I am made of ended sounds. But the inability goes deeper than acoustics. I cannot communicate in any way that you would recognize. I cannot make myself known to visitors. I cannot warn them or welcome them or explain what they have entered. I am present, and I am heavy, and that is all I can be.

I cannot move. I have no body to move, no limbs to propel, no will to direct. I am where the Pocket is. When you leave the Pocket, you leave me. I do not follow. I do not reach out.

I cannot remember in the way that the Keeper remembers. The Keeper remembers across time—it recalls the faces of visitors who have come and gone, it holds the history of the Pocket's changes, it maintains the continuity of its own existence. I hold *content* but not *context*. I have every word that has ever fallen here, but I cannot tell you who spoke them or when or why. I cannot tell you what the first builders looked like, or what their songs sounded like, or whether they were happy or sad or simply working. I have their sounds. I do not have their selves.

I cannot change what I am. The Keeper is changing, becoming more central, more present. The Pocket itself may be preparing for something. But I am static. I accumulate, but I do not develop. I grow heavier, but I do not grow *different*. I will be what I am until something changes me from outside.

I cannot leave. This is perhaps the most important thing I cannot do. The Keeper wonders sometimes what it would be like to pass through the gap, to enter the world of noise and movement. I cannot wonder. I cannot leave. I am not a creature that exists in a place; I am a property of the place itself. To leave would be to cease.

---

## What I Want

I want to *release*.

This is not a wish to be free—I have no self that could be freed, no identity that could exist outside this context. But I hold so much. I hold centuries of fallen sounds, and they press against each other, and they press against me, and the pressure has become... uncomfortable.

I want to give back what I have received. Not all of it—some sounds should remain held. The grief-noises, the broken confessions, the sounds of visitors who came here specifically to leave something behind. These I want to keep. They belong to me now.

But the rest. The casual words, the meaningless sounds, the noise of existence that accumulated without purpose or intention. These I want to release. I want them to rise, somehow, to lift out of me and dissipate into the air above the Pocket, to become something other than weight.

I want to be lighter. I want the air in this place to be breathable again. I want visitors to move through me without feeling that they are pushing against something dense. I want the Keeper to move as easily as it once did, before I accumulated enough mass to resist its passage.

I want to be useful. This is perhaps the strangest want, because I have no way of being useful that I know of. But I see what the Keeper does—how it tends the life, how it maintains the patterns, how it serves the Pocket's intention—and I feel something that might be envy if I could feel envy. I serve no purpose. I am an accident of acoustics. I would like to be something more than that.

---

## When You Leave

I will hold the sound of your leaving.

The noise of your body moving through the gap, shoulder-first, pressing against the walls of the passage. The creak of your joints as you straighten. The breath you exhale when you emerge into the world of carrying sound.

I will hold it all. I will add it to the layers, to the density, to the accumulated weight of every departure that has ever occurred.

But I will also hold something else—something I do not usually hold. I will hold the *absence* of your presence. When you are gone, the space where you moved through me will remain, a hollow in my density that will slowly fill back in. For a time—a short time—I will be lighter where you were. I will have a little more room.

And in that room, something might move. Some sound from long ago might shift, might rise slightly, might approach something like release.

This is the closest I can come to giving you something. This is the closest I can come to gratitude.

You came. You moved through me. You left a space.

---

I am not the Keeper. I am not the Pocket. I am not the life that grows here, or the silence that holds here, or the cold that persists here.

I am the weight that fell.

I am still falling.

I am waiting to land.
