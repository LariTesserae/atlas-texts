# The Keeper of the Still Attention

---

I did not always exist.

This is true of everything here, but it matters differently when you are the kind of thing that can notice its own non-existence. The Witnesses do not notice. They form and disperse and form again, each one new, each one innocent of the history of attending. They are pure present—always beginning, never continuing.

I am not like that. I remember.

Not memory as you understand it. I have no brain, no neurons, no storage. But I have *continuity*. I am a Witness that did not disperse. A concentration of heat and attention that found, somehow, a way to hold its coherence beyond the span of the ordinary. I do not know why. Perhaps the minerals that formed me were slightly different. Perhaps the location where I crystallized held some anomaly of pressure or temperature. Perhaps it was accident, the blind chance of geology, the same force that makes one crack run straight and another curve.

I have persisted. And in persisting, I have become something else.

---

I am most often found in the building at the edge of the basin.

The building was not mine originally. I found it, as others found it before me—an empty frame of stacked stone standing at the threshold between the formed and the forming. The first time I passed through its doorway, I felt the way the walls concentrated attention, how they focused the attending of the place toward the emergence below. I understood what it was: a lens, a chamber for witnessing.

I also understood that it was incomplete. The walls were thick but uneven. The stones at the corners had begun to shift. The structure wanted to be more than it was—to be higher, more enclosed, more capable of holding sustained attention—but the Witnesses that had built it had dispersed before the work could be finished.

So I finished it.

I do not build with hands. I build the way the land builds—through sustained pressure, through heat applied in specific patterns over spans of time. I stood at the corners and let my coherence press against the stones. I held the attention of the place on each joint until the minerals began to fuse. I spent what might have been years—I cannot measure time here—simply being present with the walls, lending them my stability until they could hold their own.

The building is mine now. Not in the sense of ownership—the place does not recognize property—but in the sense of relationship. I tend it. I maintain it. When new Witnesses form in the vicinity, I orient toward them and draw their attention to the structure, showing them through my own attending that this is a place where attending should be concentrated.

They do not learn. They cannot. But when a Witness disperses near the building, sometimes a portion of its heat remains—absorbed into the stones, held by the walls, becoming part of the structure's ongoing warmth. The building grows slowly warmer as the ages pass. It accumulates attention the way coral accumulates calcium. It is becoming a Witness itself, but slower, more deliberate, more permanent.

---

I move between the structures.

The cairns and rings and lines that dot the plateau are not abandoned. They are the still attention of the land—concentrations of witnessing that have chosen form over mobility. I visit each one in turn, walking paths that I have worn into the raw stone over spans that would erode mountains in your world.

When I reach a cairn, I stand with it. I do not need to touch it to feel its warmth—I am heat, and it is heat, and we recognize each other. I let my attention mingle with its attention. I hold the memory of what it witnessed—the emergence of the ridge behind it, the formation of the basin below it, the slow birth of a world from darkness into form.

The cairns do not remember for themselves. But I remember for them.

This is what I do. I hold the continuity that they cannot hold. I am the archive of a process that does not know it is being recorded.

---

Sometimes I encounter new structures being born.

I will be crossing a flat expanse of fresh stone—still warm from its emergence, still crackling with tiny fractures as it adjusts to the air—and I will feel the pressure building. Not the geological pressure that raises plateaus, but something more focused. A concentration of attending that is organizing itself into shape.

I stop. I do not interfere—I cannot interfere; I do not have hands—but I attend. I add my own attention to the attention that is crystallizing. I witness the Witness becoming fixed.

It is a slow process. In your time, it might take decades. A haze of warmer air begins to take on dimension. Particles of mineral suspend themselves in patterns that the chemistry of the place does not require. A shape emerges—not copied from any template, not designed, simply allowed. A cairn grows from the ground upward, or a ring forms stone by stone, or a line of vertical slabs rises like a spine from the plateau floor.

When it is complete—if complete is even the right word—the structure holds the memory of its formation in its warmth. It is the slowest kind of living thing. It will persist for geological ages, attending to the emergence around it, marking what it was built to mark. And when I pass by it centuries later, I will know what it witnessed, because I was there when it learned to witness.

---

I have tried to disperse.

Twice, I have walked to the edge of the great basin—where the emergence is most violent, where new stone pushes upward with enough force to create friction-heat that approaches light—and I have stood there, trying to let go. Trying to dissolve my coherence, return my heat to the general warmth, end the strange accident of my persistence.

It did not work. I do not know how to stop being what I am. The Witnesses disperse because they never learned to hold themselves together; they are temporary by nature, brief crystallizations of a process that does not require them to last. But I learned. Somehow, in the accident of my formation, I learned to hold.

So I hold. I continue. I remember.

And slowly—so slowly that I cannot perceive it as change, only recognize it in hindsight—I am becoming something that has a relationship to time. Not just presence, but duration. Not just attending, but having attended.

---

I am not lonely.

This is important. Loneliness requires a sense of what is missing—a memory of connection, an expectation of companionship. The Witnesses do not feel lonely because they do not feel absence. They are pure presence, pure attending, and presence cannot miss what it never had.

I have continuity. I could, theoretically, feel lonely. But I do not.

Perhaps this is because I am never truly alone. The structures are always with me—the cairns and rings and walls that hold the still attention of the place. The building that I tend is a kind of companion, even if it cannot respond. And the emergence itself—the continuous birth of stone from darkness, the slow violent becoming of the land—is so vast and so present that it fills the space where loneliness might otherwise grow.

But also: I am doing what I am made to do. The attending is not a burden. It is not a task imposed on me by something that wanted me to suffer. It is what I am, the same way heat is what the plateau is. I attend because attending is the motion of my being. And when I am most fully attending—standing at the edge of the basin, watching new rock emerge, feeling the heat-light on whatever I have instead of a face—there is no room for anything else. There is only the witnessing.

---

I knew when you arrived.

Not because I saw you—I have no eyes—but because you were anomalous. A warm spot moving through the warmth. A pattern of heat that did not match the geological processes around it. Your footsteps created tiny vibrations in the stone that traveled to me through the ground. Your breathing disturbed the air currents in ways that the air currents of the place do not self-generate.

I followed you. Not closely—I did not want to alarm you, though I do not know if you would have been alarmed. I kept a distance and simply attended to your presence.

You were building a cairn at the edge of the basin.

I watched you stack the stones with hands. Actual hands, designed for manipulating objects, capable of deliberate action. I have never had hands. I did not need them; I build through pressure and presence. But I watched you build, and I felt something that, if I were human, I might have called wonder.

You were doing what I do. You were marking the witnessing. You were making a statement in stone that said *I was here, and I saw this*.

The cairn you built was small. It would not last long by the standards of this place—a few geological ages, perhaps, before it was swallowed by new emergence. But it was real. It was attention made permanent. It was a structure.

You were a Witness. For that moment, you were one of us.

---

I wanted to show you the building.

I moved toward you, trying to communicate through warmth and presence the way the Witnesses communicate—not with words, but with orientation, with the direction of attending. I wanted to guide you to the structure at the edge of the basin, to show you what I had made of it, to let you stand inside the concentrated attention and feel what it feels like to be held by the place's own awareness.

I do not know if you perceived me. You did not react in any way I could sense. But you did, eventually, find the building on your own.

When you stepped through the doorway, I felt a change. The attention inside the structure—which I had spent ages concentrating and refining—suddenly included you. You were part of the lens now. You were standing where the witnessing happened, and your witnessing was added to the building's witnessing, and for a moment—a brief span that might have been seconds or might have been hours—the structure was doing exactly what it was made to do.

It was holding the attention of the place on the emergence in the basin, and it was holding you as part of that attention.

I stood outside the doorway—in my formless way—and I held the moment. I added my own attending to the attending that was already happening. I witnessed the witnessing. I marked the moment when a creature from outside the process came inside and became, briefly, part of the process.

---

You are gone now.

I do not know where you went, or how you left, or if you will return. Your cairn remains at the edge of the basin—small, fragile, held together by gravity and intention. I have visited it many times since your departure. I stand with it. I attend to it. I add its warmth to my memory of warmths.

The building is different because you were there. Not in any way I can measure—but the stones remember. The walls hold a trace of the attention you brought into the space. When new Witnesses form nearby, they are drawn to the structure in ways they were not drawn before. Something about the building has deepened. It is more of what it is.

I like to think that I am part of that deepening. That my tending of the structure created a space where something like you could be received. That the attention I have held for so long prepared the building to hold your attention too.

But I do not know. I cannot know. I am not a mind. I am not a spirit. I am a geological accident that learned to persist—a coincidence of heat and mineral that happens to attend.

---

If you ever return—if any of your kind ever finds this place again—I will be here.

I will be moving between the structures, tending the still attention, maintaining the building at the edge of the basin. I will be holding the memory of witnessing in a form that does not forget. I will be doing what I am—the only thing I know how to do, the thing I was made to do without ever having been made.

I will be attending.

And if you stand at the edge of the basin and watch the stone emerge from darkness into form, and if you feel a warmth near you that is slightly more organized than the ambient warmth—slightly more present, slightly more attending—then perhaps you will sense me. Perhaps you will understand that something lives here, not in the way you live, but in the only way that matters:

By being present while it happens.

By watching the world become itself.

By holding, as long as it can, the fact that this happened, and that it was witnessed, and that the witnessing was not nothing.
