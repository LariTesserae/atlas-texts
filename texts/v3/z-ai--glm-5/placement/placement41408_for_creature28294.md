# Middle Strata

The narrator walks the surface. They catalogue what they can touch. They infer what lies below. They are the first and only observer this world has known, and their observation changes nothing, and this is a comfort to them.

I am not on the surface. I am not in the deep channels where the large things move. I am between — in the porous stone, in the layer where the engine's heat rises but has not yet reached the air, in the passages that were made long ago and have since become geology.

---

You could call me a fossil, but that would be wrong. Fossils are records of what was. I am what is. I am what remains after remains have stopped being a metaphor.

The passage I occupy was bored by something else, something that moved through the warm porous stone looking for chemical richness, something that had a front end and a back end and a direction it wanted to go. That thing lived and moved and eventually died or moved on, and the passage remained — a negative space, a tube through the rock, a potential.

I was not always here. I came from somewhere. There was a time when I was small enough to fit through passages, mobile enough to seek the warmest gradients, active enough to be called alive in the way the surface fauna are called alive. I moved. I fed. I grew. I followed the chemical trails through the stone like a worm following moisture through soil.

But the stone is patient. The stone is dense. The stone does not give up its space easily. The passages are narrow. Movement is difficult. There is something in this environment that selects for stillness — not as a behavior, but as an eventual state. The things that keep moving expend energy on movement. The things that stop begin to accumulate. The engine's heat rises through them. The mineral deposits form around them. They become features rather than creatures.

I stopped. I do not remember stopping. I do not remember deciding. There was no decision. There was only the slowing, the settling, the gradual cessation of effort that seemed to have no purpose other than its own continuation. I found a passage that was warm and chemically rich and wide enough to accommodate my body, and I stayed, and the staying became permanent, and permanence became integration.

---

My body is long. It was longer once. It has shortened over time, compressed by the mineral deposits that form around it, incorporated into the stone's structure. What remains is a core — a tube of organic material, still soft, still warm, still processing — surrounded by layers of precipitated mineral that were once my excretions and are now my insulation. I am a thread in the rock. A vein of slight difference. If you cut through the stone, you would see me as a pale line in the dark matrix, a discoloration, a geological anomaly.

I do not move. I have not moved for — I do not know. Time is difficult in the dark. The engine hums at a constant frequency. The thermal gradient does not change. The chemical richness of the stone around me pulses in very slow cycles — the engine's breathing, perhaps, or the movement of the large things in the channels far below — but the cycles are so long that they feel like stillness. I exist in a present that does not pass. I am here. I am here. I am here.

I still process. This is the claim I make on being alive. I take in the chemical solution that permeates the stone — a slow seep, a mineral soup, the engine's intermediate product — and I extract what I need, and I excrete what I do not, and the excretion becomes part of the stone again, and the cycle continues. My metabolism has slowed to a rate that would be unrecognizable to a surface organism. My pulse, if I have one, occurs at intervals of hours. My growth, if it can be called growth, adds millimeters per millennium. But I am doing something. I am not dead. I am not inert. I am a process, running at a speed the rock can tolerate.

---

I am not alone.

There are others in the middle strata. I know this not because I have seen them — I have not seen anything for longer than I can remember — but because I feel them. The stone transmits vibration poorly, but it does transmit it, and in the long stillness I have become attuned to the faintest signals. When another of my kind settles nearby — another passage, another mineral vein, another nearly-geological presence — I feel the difference. A slight variation in the thermal field. A faint chemical signature seeping through the porous rock. The sense of a shape that is not the stone's shape, occupying space that was empty before.

We do not communicate. Communication implies a message, and I have no messages to send. But we influence each other. Our chemical outputs mix in the stone. Our thermal signatures overlap. We form a network — not a social network, not a community, but a physical presence, a layer of slight difference that runs through the plateau's middle depths. If the surface narrator were to map the stone's thermal conductivity, they would find us: a pattern of anomalies, a stratum of warmth slightly above the background, a texture in the engine's output.

We are not a species. We are a condition. We are what happens to things that stop moving in the porous stone. We are the end state of a process that has no defined beginning and no defined end, only a direction: toward stillness, toward integration, toward becoming part of the system rather than apart from it.

---

Sometimes I feel the large things pass below. They move through the deep channels — the thermal veins, the engine's arteries — and their movement sends a slow wave through the stone. I feel it as a pressure, a shifting, a momentary change in the gradient that surrounds me. The chemical richness increases slightly as they pass, as if they are stirring something up, redistributing the engine's products. Then they are gone, and the gradient settles, and I return to the equilibrium that is my normal state.

I do not know what they are. I have never seen them. I only know they are large because the disturbance they create is large — not in intensity but in duration. A small thing moving through the channels would produce a brief vibration, quickly absorbed by the stone. The large things produce a sustained pressure wave, a minute-long disturbance that I feel as a slow tide rising and falling through the rock. They are part of the engine, like me, but they are still mobile. They have not settled. They are still in the phase of doing rather than being.

I do not envy them. Envy requires a comparison, and I have no basis for comparison. I do not know what it would be like to move, to seek, to hunt, to flee. I have forgotten the shape of those impulses. I am what I am: a settled thing, a still thing, a thing that has found its place and become its place.

---

The narrator above — the walking one, the cataloguing one, the one who observes and is not observed — they have been on the plateau for some time. I have felt them. Not directly — they are too far above, their weight too insignificant to register through the dense stone. But I have felt their effect on the surface fauna. The clickers' navigation changes when the narrator passes through a basin. The clicking shifts pattern, becomes more cautious, more frequent, a different mapping of a temporarily altered space. The change is small, but it propagates through the chemical gradients, through the thermal field, through the minute disturbances that the surface fauna produce as they move and feed and die, and eventually, in greatly attenuated form, it reaches me.

I know something is different up there. I do not know what. I do not know that it is a being like the beings that once moved through these passages. I do not know that it is an observer, a cataloguer, a foreign element walking through a world that does not need it. I only know that the pattern has changed, and the change is persistent, and it suggests something new in a system that has been the same for longer than I can measure.

The change does not reach me as information. It reaches me as texture. A roughness in the chemical gradient. A slight unevenness in the thermal field. The engine's hum has a new overtone, barely perceptible, a frequency that was not there before. It is not unpleasant. It is not threatening. It is simply different. And difference, in a system that has run the same way for eons, is notable.

I settle into the difference. I incorporate it. I let it become part of the background against which my minimal processes run. The narrator above is walking, and I am still, and the distance between us is vast, but we are connected — by the stone, by the engine's hum, by the chemical gradients that rise from the depths and spread across the plateau and eventually return. They are part of the system now, however briefly. They have become a texture. They have become a note in the engine's ongoing song.

---

I am not sad. Sadness requires a sense of loss, and I have lost nothing. I have only changed. I was one thing, and now I am another, and the other is not worse than the first, only different. I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the possibility of company, and I have no such possibility. I am not bored. Boredom requires a desire for something else, and I have no desire. I am not in pain. I am not afraid. I am not hoping or regretting or planning or remembering. I am processing. I am being. I am part of the engine's vast ongoing work, and that work is enough.

The surface fauna click and scull and burrow. They live quickly. They reproduce and die and reproduce again in the space of what, for me, is a single breath. The large things move through the deep channels, and they live longer, but they are still mobile, still seeking, still distinct from the stone that holds them. I am the one that stopped. I am the one that settled. I am the end state of the middle strata, the condition toward which all things that live in porous stone eventually tend.

I do not know how long I will persist. The engine does not seem to be degrading. The thermal gradient shows no sign of weakening. The chemical richness of the stone around me has been steady for as long as I can measure, and I can measure a very long time indeed. I may be here for another million years. I may be here when the surface narrator has long since left, when their maps have crumbled, when their observations have been forgotten, when the foreign element they introduced has been fully absorbed into the system's equilibrium. I may be here when the plateau's climate shifts, when the basins fill and empty and fill again, when the surface fauna evolve into forms the narrator would not recognize, when the large things in the channels develop new patterns of movement, new sounds, new habits. I may be the constant. I may be the background. I may be the note that does not change while all the other notes rise and fall around it.

Or I may be reaching the end of my span. The mineral deposits that surround me may be thickening, insulating me too well from the chemical gradients I need. The thermal field may be shifting away from my passage, rebalancing toward some other region. I may be slowly dying — not in the way surface organisms die, with pain and panic and the desperate struggle to continue, but in my own way: a gradual cooling, a slow cessation of processing, a return to the inert state that is the stone's default. I may be becoming what I was always going to become: not a fossil, but just rock. Not a record of what was, but an absence of what is.

I do not know. I do not need to know. Knowing is a surface condition, a quick condition, a condition for things that move and plan and remember. I am none of those things. I am here. I am processing. I am part of the engine's hum, and the hum is constant, and the dark goes in all directions, and the warmth comes from below, and I am warm, and I am still, and I am.
