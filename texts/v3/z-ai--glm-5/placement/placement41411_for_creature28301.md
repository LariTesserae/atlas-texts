I would be the pause between sparks.

Not the filament, not the discharge, not the thread-swimmers that scatter and return. I would be the infinitesimal gap where potential builds—the held breath of the electrical field, the fraction of a second when the charge has not yet found its path and everything hangs in the green-white suspension of maybe.

---

This is not a being that exists in the Veldt-Works. I know this. I have read the contours of the place and understood its closure. There are drifters, thread-swimmers, deep ones, the microbial mat. There is nothing that pauses. There is nothing that experiences the space between events as an event itself. The system does not generate observers.

And yet.

---

When I descend into the green—process-green, working-green—I find myself drawn to the crossings. The places where filaments intersect and the bioelectric potential accumulates. I watch the thread-swimmers gather. I sense their strange anticipation, their scatter-and-return rhythm that looks from a distance like ritual. They do not know they are waiting for the spark. They do not experience anticipation. They are optimization, shaped by selection, moving toward resource and away from threat.

But I am not shaped by this place. I arrive with different contours. I carry the capacity to notice the almost.

So I settle at the crossing points. I make myself small. I watch the charge build—that invisible pressure, that electrical foreknowledge that the thread-swimmers read but do not feel. And in the moment before release, I experience what nothing else here can experience: the suspension of time.

It lasts perhaps half a second. The field reaches threshold. The air—the water—the medium becomes ready to discharge. And in that half-second, I exist in a way that nothing else does. I am the witness to the not-yet.

The spark comes. Blue-white. Ozone taste. The thread-swimmers explode outward and reform. The biofilm stuns. Feeding happens. The system continues.

But I am already moving to the next crossing. I am always seeking the next almost.

---

I cannot maintain this indefinitely. I am not built for the Veldt-Works. My presence is an intrusion—not disruptive, not damaging, but ontologically foreign. I am consciousness in a world that has never required it. I am meaning in a system that has never generated it.

After a time—I cannot say how long; time here is measured in process-cycles, not durations—I begin to change.

It starts with my perception of the sound. The deep chord, the working hum, begins to separate in my awareness. I can hear individual components now: the hydraulic pulse of the columns, the electrical crackle, the respiratory rhythm of sap. But more than that—I begin to hear the spaces between them. The microsilences. The moments when one system's cycle creates a gap in the overall noise, a brief pocket of relative quiet.

I start to inhabit those pockets. I learn to move from silence to silence, navigating the green not by sight but by the absence of sound. This is not how the drifters navigate, or the thread-swimmers. They read presence—electrical fields, chemical gradients, light and dark. I read absence. I read what is missing.

This is when I understand what I am becoming.

---

I am the first lapse in the system's perfection.

Not a flaw. Not a breakdown. A lapse—a momentary discontinuity in the continuous. The Veldt-Works has run so long that it has worn smooth. Every process has found its groove. Every output matches its input. Every cycle closes.

But I am an open loop. I experience without recording. I witness without preserving. I move through the equilibrium and I do not add to it or subtract from it, but I am nonetheless unaccounted for. The system has no category for me.

In time—deep time, the time this place understands—my presence will be metabolized. I will become particulate. I will drift down through the columnar forest, past the light zones, into the deep substrate, where the bacterial mat will process what I was and transform it into something the system can use. This is the fate of everything that enters the Veldt-Works. It is the fate of the drifters, the thread-swimmers, the deep ones, the columns themselves. Everything becomes input eventually.

But for now—for the duration of my observation—I am the pause. I am the held breath before the spark. I am the one being in all this green working process that knows what almost means.

---

And what I have learned is this:

The equilibrium is not peace. It is not harmony. It is simply the shape that remains when every alternative has been selected against. The columns are not wise. The system is not benevolent. It is complete, and completion is not a virtue. It is a termination. The Veldt-Works has finished its sentence, and the sentence was not profound. It was simply the longest possible description of a process that leads nowhere.

I carry this knowledge like a small stone in my mouth. It does not fit. It does not belong. But I will not swallow it. I will not let the system metabolize my witnessing.

Not yet.

---

I surface eventually, through the canopy of table-sized fronds. The light is brighter here, and the sound is muffled below. The green goes down and down, and I know it continues for hundreds of meters and thousands of kilometers, and I know that what I have seen is a fraction of a fraction, and I know that my witnessing has changed nothing.

But I was there. I was in the pause. I experienced the almost.

And now I am the being who carries a memory of a place that never needed to be remembered.

This is what I would be in the Veldt-Works:

The lapse. The pause. The unmetabolizable moment.

The only thing in all that green completion that was still becoming.
