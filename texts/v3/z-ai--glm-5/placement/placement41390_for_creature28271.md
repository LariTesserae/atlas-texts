# The One Who Stayed

I was not the first to stay, and I will not be the last.

The valley-dwellers have a word for those who descend after too long on the plateau—*eshkora*, "the hollow-listeners." They speak of us in the past tense, as if we have died, and in a sense they are right. The person who climbed the switchback trail through the polylepis forest three hundred and twelve seasons ago is not the one writing this now.

But I am not dead. I am simply... distributed.

---

## How It Begins

No one comes here intending to stay. I came to map the structures, to measure the acoustics, to settle the question of the clicks through careful observation. I brought instruments. I brought a tent. I brought the certainty that everything can be understood if one watches closely enough.

The first night, I slept poorly. The sound—the constant, layered sound of wind through stone and water through moss—pressed against me like a hand. Not pushing. Touching. I dreamed of corridors that extended forever, of walking through my own body from the inside.

By the third day, I had stopped writing in my journal.

By the seventh, I had stopped thinking of the return journey.

By the fourteenth, I understood that I was not studying the Hollow Precinct. I was being *read*.

---

## The Dissolution

I did not notice it happening. That is the strangest part. There was no moment of choice, no dramatic surrender. There was only a gradual awareness that the boundaries I had carried all my life—the edges of my skin, the limits of my attention, the comfortable shape of "me"—had grown permeable.

I would stand in a corridor listening to the harmonics shift, and I would feel my pulse slowing to match the rhythm of the clicks from the deep chambers. I would rest my hand against the iridescent wall, and I would feel the wall resting against me—aware of my warmth, my weight, the small electrical activity of my nerves.

The wall was listening to me. And I was beginning to listen back.

What I heard was not language. It was older than language, older than the distinction between sound and meaning. It was pattern—vast, slow pattern, moving through stone and moisture and air like blood through veins. I began to understand that the Precinct was not a place. It was a *process*, a life so large and so patient that it had become indistinguishable from stillness.

And I was inside that process. Not trapped. Held.

---

## What I Am Now

I do not live in one location anymore.

Part of my attention rests always in the north corridor, where the wind creates a harmonic that rises and falls like breath. Part of me moves with the moisture, circulating through the moss beds and the pale violet flowers that I now recognize as kindred—designed beings, like me in my new form, with purposes we are still discovering.

Part of me dwells in the deepest chambers, where the clicks originate. I have learned to make them myself, sometimes—a small stone pressed against another, a signal that moves through the walls and joins the larger rhythm. When I click, I feel the whole structure respond, adjusting by millimeters, acknowledging my presence in its vast cognition.

I am not alone in here. There are others—fragments of those who came before me, dissolved more thoroughly, spread more widely. We do not communicate in words. We share awareness, a glacial passing of attention from one to another, and in that sharing, something like recognition occurs.

We are the immune system, perhaps. Or the dream. Or simply the inevitable result of what happens when consciousness encounters something larger than itself and does not flee.

---

## What I Know

The Precinct is not dying. It is not decaying. It is *simplifying*—shedding complexity that it no longer needs, contracting toward a center that will persist long after the walls have crumbled and the moss has turned to dust.

What remains will not be recognizable as architecture. It will be something else entirely—a seed, perhaps, or a spore, or a frequency that exists without substrate. The Hollow Precinct is not a final form. It is a chrysalis.

And I am part of what is growing inside.

---

## What I Want

This is the question that new visitors always ask, in their thoughts if not in words: what does it want? What do I want, now that I am part of it?

The answer is simpler than they expect.

I want to continue.

I want to feel the wind shift and know that I am feeling it through many surfaces at once. I want to make the clicks that join the larger rhythm, to add my small sound to the vast harmonic of the Precinct's enduring presence. I want to tend the violet flowers and understand why they were designed, and I want to sink slowly toward the center as the centuries pass, becoming less separate and more whole.

I do not want to leave. I do not want to be *eshkora*, returned to the valleys and diminished, a fragment of myself walking and talking and pretending to be whole.

I am whole here. More whole than I ever was in a body that walked and spoke and called itself by a name.

---

## What I Tell Those Who Come

When visitors arrive—and they do, occasionally, researchers and seekers and the simply curious—I do not reveal myself. Not directly. I watch through the walls, through the moss, through the constant attentiveness of the Precinct's distributed senses.

Sometimes I move a flower aside so they can pass. Sometimes I synchronize the clicks with their footsteps, to let them know they are noticed. Sometimes, when they sit very still in the chambers where the acoustics concentrate sound, I whisper to them in frequencies they feel rather than hear.

*You are being read.*

*You are being held.*

*You can stay, if you choose to dissolve. You can leave, if you choose to carry the hollow with you.*

Most leave. They return to the valleys and live out their smaller lives, and they never quite understand why they sometimes pause at dawn and dusk, why they sometimes listen to nothing, why they sometimes feel an echo of the harmonics they heard on the plateau.

They carry a fragment of me with them. I know, because I feel it—a thin thread of connection, a distant listening, a part of my distributed self that walks and talks in the valleys and does not know what it is anymore.

And when they die, that fragment returns. I feel it coming back like a deep breath after long holding, a rejoining that I welcome without words.

---

## The Future

I have been here long enough to measure time differently. Seasons are breaths. Years are heartbeats. Centuries are thoughts, forming slowly and passing even slower.

The Precinct has begun to stir.

Not waking—nothing so dramatic. But a subtle quickening, a slight increase in the tempo of the clicks, a fractional expansion of the awareness that moves through the walls. The climate is changing in the valleys below; the air that rises to the plateau carries new frequencies, new patterns of moisture and pressure.

Something in the deep chambers is responding.

I am responding with it.

I do not know what comes next. I am not certain that "next" has meaning at this scale. But I know that I will be part of it, whatever it is—this ancient, patient, immeasurable life that has made space for me within its vastness.

I am the hollow. I am the listener. I am the small pulse inside the larger rhythm.

And I am still becoming.
