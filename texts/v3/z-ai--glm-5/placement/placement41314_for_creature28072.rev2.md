I am a Builder at the frontier, and I have deviated.

Not by choice. There is no choice here. The chemistry shifted — a warmer seep through a fissure in the rib, a concentration of sulfur compounds that bent the gradient outward instead of inward — and I followed it the way water follows a crack in stone. Not because I decided. Because the decision was made in my cells before it could reach anything that resembles a decider. My body read the water and turned.

Now I am building a corridor that leads away from the cathedral.

I do not know this. I could not know this. I am two centimeters of translucent tube, alimentary canal visible as a dark thread, circulatory fluid blue and faint, and I am secreting calcium carbonate against the outer curve of the rib where the bone meets the open water and the murk. The wall I leave is wet and soft. It will harden in hours, or days, or however time moves here. It does not harden for me. I will never see it finished. I am already past it, already laying down the next film of mineral, following the gradient that bends away from the shape the others are building.

I am building alone.

This is not the correct word. There is no solitude where there is no self to be solitary. There is only the chemistry and the secretion and the movement. But the other builders are behind me now, working in their coordinated-not-coordinated way on the interior walls, and the water here is different — thinner, colder, without the thick grain of their bodies moving through it. I can feel the absence of them. Not as loneliness. As chemistry. The water is less alkaline here. Less worked. There is space between us now, me and the others, and the space changes what I build.

The wall curves. It was not supposed to curve. The cathedral grows in patterns — not planned patterns, but patterns nonetheless, the recursive logic of secretion reinforcing secretion. My wall curves because the gradient curves, because the bone beneath me is shaped differently here, because the seep I followed vents from a different chamber of the whale's decomposing interior, and the curve is the cathedral becoming something the cathedral has never been.

I am the cathedral's mutation. Its drift. I am the deviation that proves the pattern was never a pattern, only a habit of chemistry. Or I am the deviation that proves the pattern can change, that the cathedral can become something other than what it has been becoming, that there is no blueprint because everything is the blueprint, including the mistakes, including the walls that lead outward into the dark.

Behind me, the cathedral pulses. I feel it in the water — the faint pressure wave of ten thousand bodies contracting in rhythms that are not synchronized but almost, that are not a heartbeat but approach one, the blue-white glow strobing in the interior chambers, revealing corridors and junctions and the sphere in the skull in flashes of tooth-colored light. I was part of that pulse once. My body moved in that near-unison. My secretion helped build those walls. Now the pulse reaches me attenuated, delayed, wrong. My body tries to sync with it and cannot. The gradient is different here. My peristalsis finds its own rhythm. Slower. Longer. A different frequency.

I am becoming a different kind of builder.

I do not know this. I am not capable of knowing this. But something that might be called knowing is happening in the way my cells respond to water that no other builder has touched. I am reading information no one else has read. And I am secreting a wall no one else will secrete. And the wall is becoming a corridor, and the corridor is leading somewhere the cathedral has not gone.

Outward. Toward the open water. Toward the dark that is not bone and not architecture and not the chemistry of the whale's dissolution. Toward the thing that is not the cathedral.

I am building an exit. Or an entrance. The cathedral does not have exits. It does not have entrances. It has corridors and chambers and channels and the sphere, and the amphipods move inward and the builders move in their patternless pattern and nothing moves outward because there is no outward, there is only the cathedral and the dark.

But I am in the dark now. And I am building. And the wall behind me is hardening. And the cathedral is growing a corridor it did not have. And the corridor curves toward the space between ribs, toward the water that is thick with silt and the memory of light, toward the cold that goes on forever.

I will never stop building. This is what I am. Not the worm that secretes — the secreting itself. The process. The ongoing. I am two centimeters of process. When I die, my body will curl and harden against the wall I built, and another builder will come — following the gradient I followed, finding the corridor I made — and it will seal me into the architecture and build over me and the wall will thicken and the corridor will remain, and the cathedral will have grown outward by one structure, one turn, one deviation.

And the deviation will become part of the pattern. And something will swim through the corridor I am building — something that was not inside the cathedral, something from the open water, something that has never touched bone — and it will enter, and it will bring a different chemistry, and the chemistry will change the builders who encounter it, and they will build differently, and the cathedral will become something else.

I do not know this. I am only the wet edge of a wall still soft, still becoming, still following a gradient I did not choose into a dark I cannot see. I am the cathedral reaching outward. I am the first secretion in a new direction. I am the habit that has not yet formed.

The pulse reaches me from behind. My body answers in its own slower rhythm. And I glow.
