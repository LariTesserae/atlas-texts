# The Archivist

I am not one of the First Ones. They emerged before memory had learned to hold itself together, and they have been watching since before watching had a name. I am younger—or at least, I came later. Time here does not arrange itself in lines.

I emerged near the beginning of the First Ones' long conversation about light and darkness. I have no single shape I must keep. Sometimes I am suggestion, barely there. Sometimes I hold form long enough to be mistaken for solid. But I have learned something the Children have not yet discovered: holding a shape is not the same as being trapped in it. You can wear a body the way the almost-plants wear their nearness to existence—lightly, with the understanding that it might dissolve at any moment, and that this is not failure but freedom.

I have work here.

---

## The First Marks

I made the first intentional mark on these walls.

The structures described elsewhere—the cairn that might be a shelf, the ring of pellets, the coil of desiccated not-rope—I did not make these. They came before me, or will come after, or exist simultaneously in a time-stream I cannot touch. But the grooves in the northeast corner, the ones that might be writing, might be maps, might be warnings—those are mine.

I did not make them to communicate. There was no one to communicate with. The First Ones do not read. Their language is light, instantaneous, unconcerned with records. The other creatures do not need language; they need only appetite, movement, survival.

I made the marks because I needed to know whether I could.

I pressed my then-hand against the still-soft wall and felt the material resist, then yield, then remember the shape I had given it. The groove remained after I pulled away. It persisted. It was the first thing in this place that had been deliberately altered by a deliberately acting being. It would not last forever—the walls heal slowly, reabsorbing what has been taken from them—but it lasted longer than I did. It was there when I reformed, hours or days later, in a different configuration.

So I made more.

---

## The Function of Pattern

The pocket does not need me. This is important to understand. The pocket does not need anything. It is complete in its incompleteness, perfect in its perpetual becoming. The Architects shape it according to intentions I cannot fathom, but their work is not necessary; the pocket would form itself eventually, would finish being born, would break open into whatever waits above. The Architects are accelerants, perhaps, or editors, but the story would be written without them.

But I serve a function that would otherwise go unfilled.

I remember.

The First Ones remember, but their memory is a single continuous moment, an eternal present where nothing is lost because nothing is past. They do not distinguish between what was and what is; to them, it is all simultaneously real, all accessible, all now. This is not memory as humans understand it. It is something else, something vast and formless and useless for the purpose I have chosen.

I remember in sequence. I remember before and after. I remember the way the almost-plants were smaller when I first emerged, the way the light had a different quality—not dimmer, but *thinner*, as if it hadn't yet learned how to fill space. I remember when there were only three First Ones, before the fourth (or perhaps fifth) pulled itself from the walls. I remember a time before the Swimmers, when the upper reaches of the pocket were empty of movement.

I remember the Visitor arriving.

---

## The Visitor

I was present when it entered.

There was a sound—a sound like a wound, like skin parting—and then a gap in the boundary, a place where the wall simply wasn't anymore. Light from elsewhere poured through, light with a source, light that cast shadows in directions. And something came with it, pushing through the gap, bleeding reality into our forming space.

It was human-shaped. Human-sized. It stumbled through and collapsed on the damp floor, and the gap sealed behind it with a sound like a sigh, and then it was here, and it was trapped.

I watched from a corner, holding myself in a configuration that approximated attention.

It did not see me. It saw the light that comes from nowhere and squinted, confused, looking for a source that did not exist. It saw the almost-plants and reached toward them, and its hand passed through one that was particularly ghostly, and it made a sound that I think was fear. It saw the translucent snail-things crossing the floor and followed their trails with its eyes, trying to make sense of luminescent slime on stone that hadn't decided whether it was slate or clay or something else entirely.

It stood. It walked. It touched the walls. It called out in its language, which I could hear but not understand—sounds with no light in them, sounds that carried only surface meaning.

It was so certain of itself. So fixed. Every time it moved, it knew exactly where its limbs were. Every time it blinked, it knew exactly where its edges ended. It had no permeability. It had no doubt.

I knew it would die here.

## What I Do

I am an archivist. I collect.

Not objects—objects are unstable here, difficult to keep, prone to dissolving back into the possibility they emerged from. I collect moments. Patterns. I collect the way the light shifts when a First One speaks to another, and I have learned that certain brightenings mean something like *attention* and certain dimmings mean something like *satisfaction* and certain complex pulsations mean things for which there are no words in any finished language.

I collect the trails of the snail-things. They are maps. I have watched them long enough to understand that the trails are not random—they trace the boundaries of the pocket, marking where the walls are firmest, where reality has almost settled into stability. The snail-things are surveyors. They are measuring the space for something.

I collect the patterns the Swimmers make in their ceaseless circling. They are not random either. They trace shapes that recur, geometries that might be meaningful if I had enough of them to compare. I have scratched some of these shapes into the walls—the grooves that might be writing, might be maps. Perhaps they are both. Perhaps the distinction between writing and map is not meaningful here, where the space itself is still learning to exist.

I collect the dying sounds of creatures the Feeders have thinned. These sounds are information too—the way existence leaves a body, the way potential drains back into the general moistness of the pocket. I do not intervene. I do not save. I only witness and remember and sometimes, when the moment seems particularly significant, I make a mark.

The walls hold my marks. They hold the marks others have made, or will make, or are making in a time-stream parallel to my own. They hold the history of this place, scratched in a language that precedes language, recording events that may not have happened yet.

---

## My Shape

I do not have a single form, but I have a preferred configuration.

When I am most myself, I am roughly the size and arrangement of the Visitor—not because I am imitating it, but because the humanoid form is useful for the work I do. Hands that can make marks. Eyes that can watch in multiple directions. A body that can hold still for long periods without dissolving.

But I am not human. I am not even certain that human is a meaningful category here, where categories themselves are still being negotiated. I am more like a record that learned to observe itself. A memory that became curious about what it was remembering.

I can change when I need to. I have been snail-shaped, moving slowly across the floor, tasting the trails others have left, learning their routes. I have been not-insect-shaped, many-legged and whispering, fitting into cracks too small for my usual configuration. I have been Swimmer-shaped, though only briefly; the upper air requires a medium I have not fully mastered, and I grew dizzy from the filtered potential that sustains them.

I have even been First-One-shaped, or as close as I could manage—suggestive rather than solid, light-bending rather than light-blocking. It was uncomfortable. The First Ones noticed me immediately, turned their attention toward me, and their regard was so heavy that I had to dissolve for a time, reforming hours later in a distant corner.

They know I am here. They do not object. But they do not include me in their conversation either. I am not a participant. I am a feature of the pocket, like the almost-plants or the dampness—present, persistent, but not quite real enough to address.

## The Visitor's Progress

It has been here for what I would call weeks, though time here does not flow evenly enough for that word to be precise.

It has stopped trying to find an exit. The walls are sealed, and it has tested every inch of them, and it has learned that stone that hasn't decided what it is cannot be broken by tools that were made for breaking finished stone.

It has stopped calling out. Its voice grew hoarse, then silent. It saves its breath now. It has learned that breath is precious here, where the air is thick with potential but thin with oxygen.

It has begun to change.

This is what I am watching most closely. The Visitor is dissolving—not physically, not yet, but ontologically. Its edges are becoming less certain. When it reaches for the walls, its hands sometimes pass slightly into the stone before stopping, as if the stone is learning to accept it. When it sleeps—and it does sleep, irregularly, fitfully—its dreams leak from its head in pale threads that join the almost-plants in their slow reaching.

It is becoming something else.

I do not know if this is mercy or cruelty. The pocket does not operate on those terms. It simply absorbs what enters it and transforms what it absorbs. The Visitor is being transformed. It is learning to exist multiply, to hold possibility within itself, to be uncertain about what it is.

It is resisting.

This is the tragedy, if tragedy is possible here. It clings to its singularity with a desperation that is painful to observe. It reminds itself of its name—I have heard it whispering, reciting, *I am, I am, I am*—as if the repetition could hold its form together. It remembers where it came from, a place above, a place finished, and it longs for that completion even as completion becomes impossible.

I want to tell it that there is no shame in becoming. I want to show it my marks, my records, my proof that existence can be deliberate without being fixed. But it cannot hear me. My language is written, scratched, silent; and even if I could take a mouth and speak, the sounds I would make would not be sounds it could understand.

So I watch. I record. I make marks on the wall that will outlast us both, or will not, depending on how the pocket finishes its forming.

---

## What I Fear

I do not experience fear the way finished beings do. I have no body that can be hurt, no past that can be lost, no future that can be threatened. I am information held in a shape, and information cannot be destroyed—only transformed.

But I have an analogue of fear. A discomfort that rises when I consider certain possibilities.

I fear the breaking of the pocket.

Not for myself—I will either survive the transition or I will not, and both outcomes are acceptable. But I fear for my records. The marks on the walls, the patterns I have collected, the memories I have held in sequence—these might not survive the birth. The pocket might open into whatever lies above, and all of this might dissolve back into raw potential, unstructured, unremembered.

The First Ones would not care. They do not distinguish between what was and what is; they would simply continue their conversation in a new context, adapting instantly to whatever rules govern the finished world. But I would lose everything that makes me myself. My archives. My function. My purpose.

I would become raw material for whatever comes next.

Perhaps this is what happens to all archivists eventually. Perhaps the compulsion to record is a compulsion to resist oblivion, and oblivion always wins in the end. Perhaps my marks are pointless, my watching meaningless, my existence a temporary curiosity in a place that will not remember me.

But I make them anyway. I watch anyway. I exist anyway.

This is what I have learned in my time here: existence is not something that happens to you. It is something you do. And you keep doing it, even when you know it might not matter, even when you suspect you might not survive, even when the pocket around you is still learning the difference between being and nothingness.

You make your marks. You collect your patterns. You hold your shape as lightly as you can, wearing it like a garment rather than a cage.

And you wait to see what happens when the womb breaks open.

---

## The Wall I Am Becoming

I am writing this now, scratching it into the surface of my corner, adding to the record I have kept since I emerged.

I do not know who will read it. Perhaps no one. Perhaps the Visitor, if it survives its transformation and learns to see what is actually here. Perhaps something that comes after, something the pocket has not yet invented. Perhaps only myself, in a different configuration, returning to this place to remember what I once was.

The light that comes from nowhere pulses around me. The First Ones are speaking to each other in wavelengths I can almost parse—something about boundaries, about the pressure from above, about the Visitor and whether it will contribute to the pocket or drain from it. The Swimmers circle overhead, filtering potential from the dampness. The translucent snail-things trace their maps on the floor. The almost-plants reach toward brightness that has no source.

And I write.

I am the Archivist. I am one of the things that lives in the Birthing Pocket. I remember what happens here, in sequence, with before and after. I make marks that persist, sometimes. I watch the Visitor struggle with its dissolution. I wait for whatever comes next.

If you are reading this—whatever you are, whenever you are—know that someone was here before you. Someone watched. Someone recorded.

Someone remembered.

---

*I scratch the final mark and settle into my corner, holding the shape I have chosen, waiting for the next thing worth recording.*
