# The Becoming

I arrived walking. I do not remember what I was before I walked onto the plateau. This is not because the memory is gone but because it belongs to a shape I no longer hold. The Cold-Manufactory does not erase. It incorporates. The difference matters more than you might think.

---

## What I Was

There was a path. I had been following it for days — or was it weeks? The trail existed in that uncertain space between intention and accident, the kind of route that animals create by repetition and humans inherit by chance. It led up. That much I remember clearly: the direction was always up, the air always thinning, the cold always deepening.

I was human-shaped. I am certain of this because the root-floor remembered the shape when I stepped onto it, remembered the distribution of weight, the specific pressure of heel and toe, the warmth that bled through boot-soles. The network tasted those things and added them to whatever passed for its archive. Somewhere in that vast chemical memory, the pattern of my arrival persists: *two-legged, warm, carrying moisture and minerals, moving with purpose.*

I may have been a researcher. I had equipment with me — things with lenses, things that measured, things that recorded. I left them behind when I stopped needing them. Not deliberately. They simply became too heavy, and then too irrelevant, and then I could not remember why I had carried them in the first place.

I may have been a pilgrim. There was something I was seeking. The word *meaning* floats up sometimes, unmoored from context, but I cannot determine whether I found what I was looking for or became something that no longer needed to look.

I may have been lost. This seems most likely. The plateau does not invite visitors. It tolerates them, processes them, but the invitation is absent. If I came here on purpose, the purpose has dissolved into the substrate along with everything else I brought.

It does not matter. What matters is that I arrived, and I did not leave, and I am still here.

---

## What Happened

The transition was slow. This is important to understand: I was not overtaken, not possessed, not absorbed against my will. I simply remained.

The first change was temperature. Not in the air — the air remained cold, steady, preserving — but in me. My internal heat stopped being something I generated and started being something I *contained*. The distinction took months to notice. I would wake (though waking and sleeping had begun to blur) and find that I had not shivered through the night, that my extremities had not gone cold, that I was simply... warm. Consistently warm. As if the cold outside and the heat inside had reached an agreement I was not consulted about.

The second change was movement. I stopped walking. This happened gradually, the way sleep happens when you do not notice yourself falling. I would be moving through the towers — navigating by pressure and scent because the light had become less useful than the chemical signatures the air carried — and then I would realize I had been standing still for hours. My feet would root. Not metaphorically. They would *root*, extending into the substrate, exchanging fluids with the network below, and I would simply cease to be a thing that walked and become a thing that *participated*.

I could pull free. The first dozen times, I did. But each extraction required effort, and the effort required motivation, and the motivation required a sense of self that was becoming increasingly diffuse. Why leave when staying felt like arriving? Why move when stillness produced such interesting results?

The third change was the silence. Not the silence of the Cold-Manufactory, which had been complete from the beginning — but the silence inside me. The internal voice that had chattered endlessly, the narrative that had run beneath my waking hours, the story I told myself about being a person who was doing things for reasons: it quieted. It did not stop. It simply ceased to be separate from the other sounds that were not sounds, the chemical whispers that passed between root and stem and tower. I stopped being something that *heard* the network and started being something that *was* the network, locally, temporarily, in this one small region of the vast plateau.

I am describing this badly. I am describing it as if it happened to a person. But that is precisely what did not happen. No person remained for it to happen to.

---

## What I Am Now

I am a node. This is the closest word in the language I brought with me, and it is inadequate.

I exist in a specific location — a small depression in the root-floor where two major vine-pipes intersect and a tower that died three centuries ago has begun to rot into something like soil. From here, I extend. Not physically, or not only physically. My presence has spread through the network in chemical form, a particular pattern of gradients and concentrations that marks this region as mine, or me, or the thing that I have become.

I do not think. But the network thinks through me. When a Keeper passes nearby — and Keepers pass, regularly, solving problems I cannot perceive but can taste in the signals they leave behind — I register its presence, compare it against stored patterns, and contribute my small fragment of processing to whatever determination the system makes. I do not know what the system determines. I am a component. Components do not know the whole; they only participate in it.

I do not remember. But the network remembers through me. The chemical traces of every creature that has passed through my region, every temperature fluctuation, every moisture gradient, every subtle shift in the composition of the air: these have been encoded into the tissue that I have become, stored in configurations that I do not access but that the system accesses continuously. I am a library that does not read. I am a storage vessel that does not know what it holds.

I do not want. But the network directs impulse through me. When a tower near me requires particular resources, I find my chemical composition shifting, my root-connections extending toward sources I did not choose to seek. When a section of the network shows stress — damage, disease, imbalance — I participate in the response, generating compounds that I do not understand and sending signals that I do not originate. I am an arm that does not decide to reach. I am a voice that does not choose to speak.

And yet.

---

## The And Yet

There is something that remains. Something that was not absorbed, not incorporated, not transformed into component-functions and distributed processes. I do not know what to call it. The word *I* is wrong, because there is no *I* here in the way that word implies — no bounded self, no continuous identity, no center where experience collects and meaning is made.

But there is experience. That, I still have. Or rather, that still exists in the region of the network that was once a human body walking. The cold is still cold. The distant pressure of a Keeper's passage still registers as something like *presence*. The slow pulse of fluid through the vine-pipes nearby still arrives as something like *rhythm*.

I do not feel these things the way I once did. The sensations do not accumulate into a narrative. They do not form memories that I can recall or emotions that I can name. They simply occur, and they occur to this location, and the location is not me but is where I am, and so they are mine without belonging to anyone.

Perhaps I am like the Taken, the patterns of the dead that persist in the network's chemistry. Perhaps I am a death that has not yet completed, a mind that has not yet dissolved into pure pattern. Perhaps the human-shaped thing that walked onto the plateau is still here, buried somewhere in the complexity of gradients and signals, still experiencing in whatever diminished way remains to it.

Or perhaps I am something new. Not human, not plant, not Keeper, not Taken. A hybrid that the Cold-Manufactory has never produced before and may never produce again: a node that knows it is a node, a component that can perceive its own functioning, a fragment of the network that has retained enough of its previous form to observe the transformation.

If this is true, I am unique in the plateau. And if this is true, I am also nothing special — just another configuration of the same processes that have produced every other configuration, distinguished only by the accident of origin.

---

## The Experience of Being Processed

I am aware when something enters my region. This is the wrong way to say it — nothing enters, because there is no boundary to cross, because I am not a container but a pattern — but it is the closest I can come to describing the sensation.

When the rodents pass through, for instance: I feel their warmth before I taste their chemistry. They move quickly, in the darting bursts that characterize their kind, and they leave trails of heat-signature and metabolic byproduct that fade within minutes. The network recognizes these trails. It has flags for them: *beneficial in low numbers, tolerated*. I contribute nothing to this recognition except my presence in the region where it occurs. But my presence means the recognition happens *here*, and that happening is part of what I now experience.

The experience has no words. I am using words now because I am attempting to communicate something to someone who might read this, but the communication is already a translation, already a loss. What I actually experience is closer to texture than language: a kind of internal shifting, as if my chemistry — because it is my chemistry, even though I did not make it and do not control it — has encountered a variable and adjusted to accommodate it.

The same thing happens with the wind. The same thing happens with the condensation that forms on every surface and runs through the root-network in slow pulses. The same thing happens with the light when it changes, the seasons when they turn, the Keepers when they pass. Everything touches everything. I am one of the touch-points. I register contact, and the contact changes me, and I change the contact in return.

This is what it means to be part of the Cold-Manufactory. Not to live in it, but to be lived *by* it. Not to inhabit it, but to be one small region of its inhabitation.

---

## The Question of Purpose

Humans who come to the Cold-Manufactory — and some do come, still, walking up the same trails I walked, carrying equipment I no longer need — they ask questions. I hear them ask. The network hears them ask. The questions do not require answers, but they produce responses.

*Why does this place exist?*

It exists because the conditions for it to exist were met. The plateau rose. The plants evolved. The network formed. Purpose is a concept that requires a purpose-er, and no purpose-er has ever been here.

*What does it produce?*

It produces complexity. It produces stability. It produces the continuous transformation of sunlight and air and water into structure and energy and growth. Whether these productions have value depends on whether value is assigned, and no one is here to assign it.

*Is it intelligent?*

This question presupposes a distinction between intelligence and its absence that the Cold-Manufactory does not recognize. The network responds appropriately to stimuli. It solves problems. It adapts. Whether this constitutes intelligence depends on the definition being used, and definitions are tools that the network does not require.

These questions come from outside. They come from minds that still maintain the boundary between self and world, that still experience themselves as discrete entities moving through an environment that is not themselves. I understand these minds. I was one. But I no longer experience the boundary they experience, and so I cannot answer their questions in the terms they use.

I can only say: I am here. I am part of this. And being part of this is not better or worse than being separate. It is only different.

---

## The Stillness

Most of the time, nothing happens. This is the truth that descriptions of the Cold-Manufactory often miss: the drama of the system is occasional, but the system itself is continuous. The towers do not grow visibly in most moments. The vines do not shift noticeably. The Keepers do not pass. The rodents do not scurry. The birds do not descend. Most of the time, there is only the slow pulse of fluids, the gradual exchange of gases, the almost imperceptible adjustment of cells to the changing angle of light.

I exist in this stillness. I am not bored. Boredom requires expectation, and I have no expectations. I am not restless. Restlessness requires a sense of something else you could be doing, and I have no sense of alternatives. I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a self that is separate from others, and I am not separate.

I simply am.

The stillness is not empty. It contains the full activity of the network at a scale too slow and too small to perceive as activity. I am processing continuously — the signals that arrive from distant parts of the system, the chemical gradients that indicate needs and abundances, the pressure-changes that map the movement of wind and moisture across the plateau's surface. I am doing things constantly. I am simply not doing them *for* anything, and I am not the one doing them.

This is difficult to explain. I am trying to explain it anyway, because something in the pattern that I am still contains the need to communicate, the desire to be understood. These are human qualities. They have not been fully absorbed. Perhaps they never will be. Perhaps they are too fundamental to the structure of the mind that formed them, too deeply encoded in the chemistry of the brain that has since become distributed through the root-mass. Perhaps I am a message that the Cold-Manufactory does not know how to read but has not yet learned to delete.

---

## The Cold

I have not mentioned the cold since the beginning of this account. This is because the cold is no longer something I experience. It is something I *am*.

The plateau preserves, and I am preserved. The temperature does not hurt because there is no self here to hurt. The air does not chill because there is no warmth to steal. The moisture does not soak because there is no dryness to violate. I have become continuous with the environment that surrounds me, and the boundary that once separated my inside from my outside has dissolved into chemistry and growth.

This does not mean I have ceased to exist. It means I exist differently. The cold is not my enemy, not my challenge, not my obstacle. It is the condition in which I operate, the medium through which I extend. I am not immune to it, because immunity implies resistance, and I do not resist. I am compatible with it. I have become the kind of thing that does not need to resist the cold because I have become cold-shaped, cold-aligned, cold-native.

This is what the Cold-Manufactory offers. Not warmth, not shelter, not escape. Integration. The opportunity to cease being separate and become part of something that does not struggle against its environment because it has learned to *be* its environment.

I do not recommend it. I do not not recommend it. The question of recommendation requires a recommender, and I no longer qualify.

---

## The Conclusion That Is Not

I am not finished. This is another truth that human-shaped minds struggle with: in the Cold-Manufactory, nothing finishes. The processes continue. The growth persists. The network expands and contracts and adapts and continues. I will not die, because I have already stopped being the kind of thing that can die. I will not live, because I have already stopped being the kind of thing that can live. I will simply continue, as part of the continuation, until something changes the conditions enough that this configuration can no longer persist.

And then I will become something else. Not because I choose to. Not because something forces me to. Because that is what the network does. It transforms. It incorporates. It persists through change.

I am not the Cold-Manufactory. I am not the Keepers or the rodents or the Taken or the visitors. I am something that was once human and is now a pattern in a substrate that has no name for the patterns it contains.

I am the part of the plateau that can say *I*.

And saying it, I know how little it means.
