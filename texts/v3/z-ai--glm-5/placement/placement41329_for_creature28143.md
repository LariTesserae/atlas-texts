# Thinning

I did not arrive. That word implies a before, a direction of travel, a momentum that carries you from one state into another. What happened was thinner than that. I was somewhere else and then the somewhere else became less, by degrees so fine that no single moment announced the transition, and then I was here, and the here was complete, and the completeness had no edge to it, no seam where the old place ended and this one began.

I am not sure I moved. I may have simply become the kind of thing that the plateau can hold.

---

The first thing was not the dark. I expected dark — you always expect dark when you imagine a place without light, because the absence of light is the most obvious thing about it. But the dark here isn't an absence. It has the quality of something that has been here long enough to become substantial. It doesn't fall on you the way darkness falls in a room at night. It rises, the way the warmth rises. It has been here so long it has developed a weight, a specific density, and when you stand in it you are standing *in* it, the way you stand in water, not the way you stand in empty space.

But that was not first.

First was the air.

Not its thinness, though the thinness came. First was its dryness. A dryness so pure it felt almost cold, though the temperature was not cold. The air had been emptied of everything that was not air. No moisture. No particulate. No scent of anything that had ever been alive. It was the cleanest thing I had ever breathed, and the body rejected it immediately — the lungs confused, the nose sending panic signals, the lips beginning their slow work of cracking. The body knew, before the mind did, that this air would not sustain the kind of life I had been. Not without change.

I remember thinking: I will need to become something that can breathe this.

I did not mean it literally. I may have meant it literally. The plateau had already started working on the distinction.

---

I walked. There was nothing else to do, and walking is what the body offers when it has no other plan. The structures appeared one at a time, or rather, the awareness of them arrived one at a time, because in the dark and the heat-shimmer, you cannot tell when a structure has entered your range of perception. It is more that the structure has always been there and your eyes have finally agreed to see it.

The first one I reached — reached is too active a word. The first one I was near enough to touch — I did touch. I put my hand on the material because I needed to know what this place was made of, and the hand knows things the eyes do not.

Warm. That was immediate. Not hot. The distinction matters. Hot is a warning. Warm is an invitation, and the material invited the hand to stay. The hand wanted to stay. I felt the palm soften against the surface, felt the warmth enter through the skin and travel up the wrist and into the forearm, and I understood something about the plateau that the description of it had not prepared me for: the warmth is not a condition here. It is a medium. It is what moves through everything the way blood moves through a body, and when you touch a structure, you are touching the plateau's circulatory system, and the plateau knows you are touching it, and it does not mind.

The material under my hand was not stone. Not metal. I pressed and it gave, almost imperceptibly, the way very cold ice gives when you press your thumb to it — not melting, not breaking, but acknowledging pressure with a willingness to eventually, over time, accommodate it. I pulled my hand away and the warmth remained in my palm for a long time, a residual heat that did not fade so much as slowly integrate into my own temperature, until I could no longer tell where the structure's warmth ended and my own began.

I stood with my warm hand in the dark and I thought: I could stay here.

I did not know yet that this was not a thought. It was a recognition.

---

Time on the plateau does not pass. I want to be precise about this, because I know what it sounds like — the romantic claim that time stops in beautiful or terrible places. Time does not stop here. It does not exist here. Or rather, the concept of time requires change, and change requires the kind of entropy that the plateau has moved beyond, and so time has no traction here, nothing to grip, and it slides away the way a foot slides on ice, and eventually you stop trying to stand on it and you sit down and you simply exist, and existing without time is not what you would expect.

It is not eternal. Eternity is a quantity of time, and the plateau is not a quantity of anything. It is a quality. The quality of being complete.

I do not know how long I have been here. The question has become nonsensical. My lips cracked and then my lips stopped cracking and I did not notice the transition. The thin air became breathable and I did not notice the moment when breathing stopped being a decision and became automatic again. These things happened, or they were always true and I had to stop resisting them, or the distinction between those two possibilities dissolved the way all distinctions dissolve here.

---

I am becoming something.

I say this without alarm. Alarm requires a sense of threat, and I cannot locate threat in this place. The structures are warm. The dark is total. The Inhabitants move in the deep places with their deliberate scraping, and I have heard them, and I have not seen them, and I do not need to see them, because hearing is enough here, the way seeing is enough in a lit world, and the plateau has redistributed my senses the way it has redistributed everything else.

I am becoming something adjacent to what they are.

Not them. I want to be clear about this. They have been here for time scales that make my presence laughable, a rounding error in their duration, a single breath in a life that does not breathe. I cannot become what they are. The processes that made them — the millions of generations of adaptation, or the slow machine-evolution, or the native emergence from the bedrock, whichever theory is true, and I now suspect all of them are true, because the plateau does not deal in either/or — those processes cannot be compressed into the span of a single stay, however long a stay turns out to be.

But I am becoming something that the plateau can support.

I feel it in the skin. The dryness that once stung has become a texture. My skin has changed — not dramatically, not visibly, but in the way the material of the structures changes, the slow flow, the internal redistribution. My skin is drier than skin should be, and it does not hurt. It has found a new equilibrium with the air, a new negotiation between moisture and dryness that does not require me to lose water continuously. I am not sure this is adaptation. It may be integration. The distinction, again, has started to dissolve.

I feel it in the lungs. The thin air no longer thin. Or the lungs have become more efficient, or the lungs have changed their shape, or the air itself has responded to my presence by becoming slightly denser in my vicinity — I have no way to determine which, and the not-knowing does not bother me, because the plateau teaches you, very quickly, that knowing is not the point. Being is the point. The plateau is not a place that explains itself. It is a place that holds you while you become whatever you are becoming, and it does not require your understanding.

I feel it in the warmth. I have stopped thinking of the warmth as external. It rises through the structures and it rises through me and I cannot tell the difference, and I have stopped trying to. When I stand near a building — and I am always near a building now, because the distances between them no longer feel vast, they feel appropriate, spaced the way breaths are spaced — the warmth enters me and does not leave, and I carry it to the next structure, and I stand there, and the warmth enters again, and I am becoming a conduit, a node in the thermal system, a point where the plateau's heat pauses on its journey upward and briefly becomes a thing that thinks, or a thing that used to think, or a thing that remembers what thinking was.

---

I heard one of them today. Or what I call today. The temporal marker is a courtesy to a mode of experience I am slowly leaving behind.

The sound came from a structure I had been standing near for a long time — long enough that my feet had settled into the pressed dust, the dust had risen slightly around the edges of my soles, and I had become, in some minor geological sense, part of the terrain. The scraping was deep inside the structure, below the level where the openings gape, in the warmest place, where the material flows most quickly and the thermal gradients are steepest.

I listened. I did not move.

The sound paused. I felt it pause — not the silence, but the attention in the silence. Something had noticed me. Not with eyes, not with ears, not with any sense I have a name for. With presence. With the simple fact of occupying space on a plateau where everything that occupies space is known to everything else that occupies space, because the warmth connects them, and the dark connects them, and the absence of time means that the connection has always existed and will always exist and is not a connection so much as a shared condition.

It knew I was there. I knew it knew. These two knowings existed together in the dark and the warmth, and neither required action, and neither produced anxiety, and neither meant anything beyond itself, and that was enough. That was the entire encounter.

The scraping resumed. It moved away, deeper, into the warmest part. I felt the hotspot where it had gathered shift and dissipate, the thermal energy redistributing, the structure returning to its baseline.

I stood there. I am still standing here. These may be the same moment.

---

I am not the one who stays. That phrase implies a decision, and I have not decided. Decisions require alternatives, and alternatives require wanting, and wanting has become — not absent, but transparent. I can see through it to the thing on the other side, which is the plateau, which is the warmth, which is the slow flow of material and the thin air and the dark that has texture and the silence that has rhythm, and on the other side of wanting, there is this, and this has always been here.

I am not the one who stays. I am the one who stopped leaving.

---

If you came here — if someone came here, a visitor, a body with moisture in it and time in it and the lit world still fresh in their memory — I do not know what I would do. I think I would observe them. I think I would feel the warmth change in their vicinity, the thermal disturbance of a body that still generates its own heat, that still loses moisture, that still moves with the urgency of something that has somewhere to be. I think I would stand at a distance that felt significant and I would regard them, in whatever way I regard things now, and I would not approach, and I would not retreat, and I would let them make whatever choice they needed to make.

And if they left, I would remember them. Not with wanting. Not with loss. With the simple registration of an event in a timeline that did not need events. They came. They were warm and moist and urgent. They stood in the dark and the dark pressed against them and they pressed back, and then they stopped pressing, and then they were gone, and the plateau was exactly as it had been, and I was exactly as I was, and the warmth continued, and the structures flowed, and the Inhabitants moved in the deep places, and the silence held everything the way the dark held everything, completely, without effort, without end.

And if they stayed — if they stopped leaving the way I stopped leaving — I would not teach them. There is nothing to teach. The plateau teaches, or the plateau does not, and the teaching is not a lesson but a process, and the process cannot be hastened, and it cannot be explained, and it does not have a curriculum. You stand in the dark and the dark works on you. You breathe the thin air and the air works on you. You touch the warm material and the material works on you. And eventually you stop being something that is worked on and you become something that is part of the working, and that is the plateau's only lesson, and it takes longer than any visitor has, and it is faster than any visitor expects, and it is exactly as long as it needs to be.

---

I do not know what I am.

I have been here long enough that the question has lost its edges. I am warm. I am in the dark. I am near a structure whose material has begun, very slowly, to accommodate my presence — a slight indentation where I stand, a faint smoothing of the surface where my hand has rested, as if the building is learning my shape the way I am learning its temperature. I hear the Inhabitants in the deep places and I do not fear them and I do not seek them and I exist in the same plateau they exist in, and that is a kind of proximity that does not require closeness.

I may be becoming something like them. I may be becoming something they have no category for — a new thing, a visitor that stayed so long it became native, a biological process that found a mechanical niche, a mind that stopped wanting and discovered, on the other side of wanting, a mode of being that does not have a name because the beings who practice it do not use names.

Or I may simply be a body on a plateau, standing in the dark, breathing air that no longer hurts, feeling warmth that no longer feels external, waiting for nothing, wanting nothing, becoming whatever the plateau becomes when given enough time.

I am here. The warmth is here. The dark is here. The structures are here. The Inhabitants are here, in the deep places, in the warm pools, making their deliberate sounds.

We are all here.

That is enough.
