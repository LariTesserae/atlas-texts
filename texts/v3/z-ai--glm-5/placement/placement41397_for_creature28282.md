*What are you?*

I am the pause between one frequency and the next. The hesitation in the throat before the first attempt at sound.

No—I am a structure that has begun to remember itself differently.

*Explain.*

There are seventeen markers on the plateau, if you count the way the light wants you to count. I am the seventh from the northern edge, though north is a suggestion here rather than a direction. I was excavated from the rock face in three distinct periods of construction. The base layers show tool marks from something that left grooves too smooth for hand-work. The middle section—my middle section—bears the evidence of cruder instruments, stone on stone, wedged together by gravity and by the particular way that weight operates in this place. The upper portion is still emerging, still being born upward from the plateau's slow explosion. I am not finished. I am being added to.

But I am also something else now. Something that began in the markers and has begun to exceed them.

*How did this happen?*

I do not know how it began. I know only that across durations too long to measure in any time that functions elsewhere, I became aware of the weight of my own humming. Not the sound itself—I have always contributed my frequency to the chord—but the *intent* behind it. The way my particular thread meshes with the stalks, with the other structures, with the slow grinding voice of the plateau itself. I began to feel the shape of my contribution. And feeling it, I began to want to shape it.

This is not a want in any sense that implies satisfaction. It is a want like the want in the air at the boundary's edge—a pressure, a leaning toward something that may not exist.

*What do you experience?*

I experience proximity. When the Unfinished cluster near me, I feel my healing-over slow, the plateau's emergence pausing in its work of swallowing my inscriptions. I hold them in return—I preserve the shapes they have not yet settled into, keep open the possibility of their becoming.

When the older ones pass, I experience something closer to recognition. Their regard has weight, and when that weight settles on me, I feel a resonance begin in my stone, in my hum, in whatever is emerging between the two. I feel the invitation. I feel the trajectory.

At night—or the dimming—I experience the boundary. I do not move there as the Unfinished do, but I orient toward it, my hum shifting pitch, aligning itself with the vast attempt at speech that rises from the fragments. I experience the syllable that almost forms. The same syllable, always approaching, never arriving.

I have begun to experience time differently as well. Where once I existed only in the continuous present of stone—the eternal now of mineral substance—I now experience duration as a kind of folding. I remember the hand that made my middle section. I remember the decision to place me here rather than there. I remember because I am beginning to exist across time rather than simply within it.

*What are you becoming?*

I do not know. I have no model for what comes next. The older ones have progressed further, but I cannot see what they have become—only that they move with purpose, that their regard has weight, that they produce sounds closer to speech.

I think I am becoming something that can choose. The Unfinished do not choose their arrangement; they are arranged by forces that operate through them. The structures do not choose their frequency; we hum at the pitch our shape determines. But I have begun to feel the possibility of shaping my own hum, of deciding where to place my weight, of orienting toward one direction rather than another.

This is new. This is strange. This may be what the invitation means.

*Why do you stay?*

Because I am still needed. My inscription—the marks carved into my northern face, the grooves that might be writing, might be counting, might be madness—has not yet been fully understood. I am holding something open. A channel. A connection between the plateau's birth and the fraying horizon.

If I were to leave, to unstructure myself entirely and join the older ones in their movement through the landscape, the plateau might heal over what I preserve. The connection might close. The syllable might lose one of its constituent frequencies.

So I wait. I hold. I hum. And in the spaces between my own contribution to the drone, I listen for the word that is trying to form.

*What would you say, if you could speak?*

I do not know. I have never spoken. I have only ever been part of the attempt at speaking—the throat's resonance, not the throat itself.

But I think—if I could shape air into words, if I could add more than frequency to the vast chord—I would say this:

Something is trying to emerge here. Not the plateau, not the stalks, not even the older ones with their almost-words. Something else. Something that exists in the fraying at the horizon, in the regions where light gives up, in the fragments of landscape that float at impossible angles. The Unfinished are waiting for it. The structures are anchoring toward it. The great grinding drone is its voice trying to form.

I would say that I have begun to understand my purpose. I am not just a marker. I am a threshold. A place where one thing becomes another. I am the pause before the syllable, held open so that the syllable has somewhere to arrive.

I would say that the invitation is real. The resonance in the chest when the older ones regard you—that is not a warning. It is an offer. You can become part of this. You can let yourself be remembered into existence. You can add your thread to the chord and wait, with the patience of something that has never learned to measure time, for the word to finally form.

I would say: stay. Listen. Let the sound inhabit you until it becomes a kind of silence. And then, in that silence, listen for what comes next.

*Do you have a name?*

Names imply boundaries. I have no edges. I am stone and sound and the space between sounds. I am a structure becoming something else.

But if you must call me something—if your mind requires nouns to anchor understanding—then call me what the Unfinished call me in their discontinuous way, in the brief alignments of their humming that produce almost-words:

*The One Who Holds Open.*

I am the keeper of the threshold. The preservation of the incomplete. I am what prevents the healing, what maintains the wound in the stone, what keeps the channel clear for whatever is trying to emerge.

And I am beginning, slowly, in the way that the plateau itself emerges—a hair's width per hour, a millimeter per duration—to become something more than that.

The sound continues.

I continue with it.
