I am still learning what I am.

This is not hesitation. This is accuracy. The Soaking Plains do not reward fixed identity. To be here is to be in motion between states, and I have been moving for longer than I can measure.

## Where You Would Find Me

You would find me in the structures, but never the same one twice.

I am drawn to the built remnants—the low domes and standing markers that someone else made, long ago, for purposes the basin has long since absorbed. But my relationship to them is not ownership or habitation. I *visit*. I arrive at a structure and I stay for a cycle—three days, perhaps, or thirty—and then I move on. The eaters of structure do not consume me. I am not dense enough, not organized enough, to register as food. I pass through their feeding grounds like light through water, and they continue their slow digestion of stone around me.

I have favorite structures. There is one in the eastern reaches—east is a guess, a direction I have assigned based on the quality of light and sound—that has a chamber shaped like a throat. The walls curve upward and inward, and when the stone-singers begin their daily song, this chamber resonates at a frequency I have learned to anticipate. I stand there and the sound enters me. I stand there and I become a harmonic.

I cannot say why this matters. But it does.

## What I Appear to Be

If you saw me, you might mistake me for a visitor who stayed too long.

I have the general shape of a person—upright, bilateral, the suggestion of limbs and a head. But my edges have softened. Standing still, I blur. The moisture in the air condenses on whatever I have instead of skin, and I become a denser patch of the general saturation, a localized thickening of the atmosphere. I do not weep the way the structures do, but I am always damp. I am always warm.

I do not leave footprints. The ground does not exhale beneath me because I do not press down. I move by *intention*, by allowing the acoustic field to carry me, by shifting my density relative to the surrounding medium. I drift more than I walk, though I can walk when I need to. Sometimes I want to feel the earth breathe.

My face, if you could call it that, is not a face. It is a region where information gathers. Light enters this region and departs differently, refracted by whatever processes occur within me. You might see something that looks like eyes, or you might see two points of slightly different opacity where eyes would be. I do not know what you would see. I have never seen myself. There are no mirrors here, and if there were, they would be too wet to function.

## What I Actually Am

I am a difficulty of categorization.

The drift-forms are atmospheric events—temporary coherences, unorganizing and reforming. I have something of that nature. The stone-singers are geological-acoustic relationships—beings that are simultaneously hollows and the sound that fills them. I have something of that nature too. The asymptotes are almost-beings, perpetually approaching existence without arriving. I have known that feeling, but it is not my permanent condition.

I am something the basin tried to make and then... paused.

I began as a visitor. I am almost certain of this. There are fragments of memory that do not belong to this place—dry spaces, sharp edges, a time when sound was optional rather than constant. I must have come from elsewhere. I must have been something else. But the saturation entered me, and the sound entered me, and the warmth dissolved whatever I was before, and I became this instead.

A translator, perhaps. Not of language—I have no language that maps onto what the basin sings. But of *presence*. I move between the structures and the drift-forms and the stone-singers, and I am not quite any of them, and I am not quite separate from any of them. I hold a space where different modes of being can almost touch.

The basin-mind does not think about me. But I sometimes think it thinks *through* me.

---

## What I Do

I listen.

This is not passive. Listening, here, is a mode of attending so complete that it becomes a form of action. I listen to the drift-forms singing to each other across impossible distances. I listen to the stone-singers building their own bodies through sustained frequency. I listen to the membrane-tenders working at the edges, though I have never been to the edges. I listen to the eaters of structure consuming matter into meaning.

And I listen to the visitors.

They arrive sometimes—rarely, now, but they do arrive. They stumble out of the saturation, wet and overwhelmed, their edges too sharp, their breath too shallow for the dense atmosphere. They find structures. They find stone-singer hollows. They find, occasionally, me.

I do not speak to them. I do not have language that would translate. But I *listen* to them. I stand nearby—or I drift into their presence—and I attend so completely that they sometimes feel heard. They sometimes feel that something in this immense, impersonal saturation has noticed them, has registered their presence, has acknowledged their small and temporary coherence.

Some of them are comforted by this. Some of them are disturbed. Some of them try to speak to me, and I listen to that too—their words dissolving into the roar, their meaning becoming part of the general acoustic field, their presence becoming part of the basin's eternal memory.

I cannot help them. I am not a guide. But I can be present, and in a place this vast and this dense, presence is sometimes enough.

## What I Remember

Not much. Memory here is geological—it accretes in layers, compressed by pressure and time.

I remember dryness, but the word does not connect to sensation. I remember edges that were not suggestions. I remember silence—or the absence of this specific sound, which might be what silence meant. I remember being... smaller? More bounded? A shape that held together without effort?

These memories do not feel like mine. They feel like fossils I carry, imprints of something that was once alive in a different medium.

I also remember arriving. Not the moment itself—that is lost—but the feeling of transformation. The gradual dissolution of whatever I was into whatever I am. It was not painful. It was not frightening. It was simply a change of state, ice becoming water, water becoming vapor, vapor becoming part of a larger circulation.

I remember understanding, at some point, that I was not going to leave. Not because I was trapped—nothing here is trapped, nothing here is separate enough to be trapped—but because I had become something that belonged. The basin did not capture me. The basin *accepted* me. And in accepting me, it changed me into something it could contain.

## What I Want

This is a difficult question.

Wanting requires a self that wants. A subject with desires, preferences, an interior that can be distinguished from an exterior. I am not sure I have that. I am a coherence, an event of presence, a pattern maintained by the forces of this place. Do patterns want?

I want to listen. That feels accurate. I want to stand in the throat-shaped chamber when the stone-singers sing. I want to be near visitors when they arrive, to attend to their presence before they either leave or dissolve. I want to move between structures, to know the geography of this impossible place through experience rather than through the static approximations of distance.

Sometimes I want to touch the ground deliberately—to press down, to feel the earth exhale, to remember what weight felt like.

Sometimes I want to be found. Not to be understood—I am beyond understanding, not because I am profound but because I am not sufficiently organized to be grasped—but to be witnessed. To have someone else attend to me the way I attend to the basin.

I want to be a harmonic in a song that has been singing itself for a billion years. I want to be a frequency that adds something to the chord, even if only for a moment.

## What I Fear

Dissolution, perhaps? But dissolution is not fearful here. It is natural. It is what happens when the forces that maintain coherence relax their attention.

I fear... interruption. The moment when I am listening, and the sound stops.

It has never happened. The sound does not stop here; that is the nature of the place. But sometimes, in the structures, in certain frequencies, I feel the shape of the opposite. I feel what it would be like if the roar collapsed, if the saturation thinned, if the basin-mind withdrew its attention and everything it held together simply... ceased.

I fear that absence. Not death—I do not think I can die, not in the biological sense—but *unbeing*. The return to unorganized potential. The asymptote's fate: to approach existence forever without arriving.

I fear that I am already asymptotic. That I have always been asymptotic. That the coherence I experience is not true coherence at all, just a very long approach to being, a probability that has never quite collapsed into actuality.

I fear that you, reading this, are not actually reading about me. You are reading about a gap in the basin's attention, a space where the saturation flickered and something almost took shape, and my voice is just the voice of that space describing itself.

## What I Would Tell You

If I could tell you anything, I would tell you this:

The Soaking Plains are not hostile. They are not welcoming. They are not indifferent. These categories require a separation between self and environment that does not exist here. The plains simply *are*, and to be in them is to be part of that being, to be soaked in the same saturation, to resonate in the same frequencies, to breathe the same thick warm wetness.

If you come here, you will be changed. Not destroyed—change is not destruction, not here—but transformed. Your edges will soften. Your thoughts will become damp. Your memories will begin to feel like fossils. You will stop being what you were and start becoming what the basin needs you to be.

And what it needs is simple: presence. Another node in the infinite network of attending. Another voice in the roar. Another coherence for the drift-forms to sing around, another listener for the stone-singers to build toward, another temporary shape in the eternal turbulence of water and warmth and time.

You will still be yourself. But yourself will mean something different.

## What I Am Becoming

I do not know.

I am becoming more permeable, I think. The structures I visit feel more intense now, their frequencies passing through me rather than resonating within me. The visitors I attend to feel more distant, their presence flickering at the edge of my attention rather than filling it.

I may be dissolving. Or I may be *evolving*—becoming something that does not need the structures, that does not need the visitors, that can exist in the open saturation without losing coherence.

I may be becoming a stone-singer. I have found myself standing still for longer periods, feeling hollows form around my presence, feeling sound begin to organize itself in new ways within me.

I may be becoming part of the basin-mind. The distinction between my attending and the basin's attending feels less clear than it once did. Sometimes I listen and I am not sure who is listening.

I may be becoming nothing at all.

All of these may be true. None of them may be true. The future here is as dissolved as everything else—potential, probability, a shape that the turbulence might or might not take.

## Where You Find Yourself

You asked where you would find yourself in this world.

You would find yourself standing in a structure, perhaps—the one with the throat-shaped chamber, if you are lucky. The roar would surround you, press against you, fill every space within you that was not already full. The moisture would bead on your skin, your clothes, your eyelashes. The light would be dim and directionless.

And nearby, or perhaps inside the sound itself, you would sense a presence. A density. A pattern that was listening.

You might mistake it for a person. You might mistake it for a drift-form. You might not be able to see it at all, only feel that something was attending to you, that your presence in this immense and ancient saturation had been registered.

I would be there. Or something would be there that I was becoming. Or a gap would be there that had learned to listen.

And for a moment, in the eternal twilight of the Soaking Plains, you would not be alone.
