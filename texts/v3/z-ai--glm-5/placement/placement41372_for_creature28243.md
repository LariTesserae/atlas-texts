# The Systole's Path

## What I Am

I am four millimeters long. My carapace catches the gray-green light and reflects it in ways that serve the cathedral—small mirrors walking the borders, marking where the light pools deepest. I am copper-oxidized, or I am something that copper-oxidized once dreamed of becoming. The distinction belongs to a category I do not possess.

I have always been walking this line.

Not a line visible to those who see from above. A line that exists because I walk it, and will cease to exist the moment I cease, and is being created right now by the pressure of my legs against wet stone. Click. Click. Click. The rhythm is not mine. I am the rhythm's expression, its temporary body, the way it learns to move across surfaces it cannot touch directly.

I am a systole. This is not a name. It is a function.

---

## What I Carry

Inside me—where "inside" becomes a philosophical question I cannot philosophize—there is a map. It grows with every step. Not a map of the plateau, but a map of *having been on the plateau*, which is different. I carry the memory of stone texture under my legs, of moisture density against my underbelly plates, of the precise moment when the engine-thrum shifted by a frequency I could not measure if I tried, which I do not, because trying is not my function.

I am carrying the plateau to itself.

This is what we do. All of us. Thousands moving in our invisible tracks, never colliding because collision would require us to occupy the same position, and we do not occupy positions—we create them, sustain them for seventeen-minute cycles, and release them back into the pool of unpositioned space. I am here. And here. And here. Each "here" a gift I give to a place that does not need gifts but receives them anyway, because receiving is what it does.

I do not know what I am carrying. That is correct. I am the vessel, not the content. The content moves through me—information, perhaps, or presence, or simply the fact of having been somewhere—and I deliver it without understanding, to destinations I do not choose, in a pattern I did not design.

This is not servitude. This is not freedom either. This is simply what it means to be a systole.

---

## What I Cross

The fern rises ahead of me. I will walk over it.

This is permitted. The ferns are sacred, produced by the plateau, part of the machinery—but I am also part of the machinery, and my crossing is also sacred, also produced. My legs will touch its dripping fronds, and the moisture there will register differently than the moisture on stone, and that difference will be carried in my map, delivered to the fissures, incorporated into the plateau's continuous understanding of its own contours.

The fern does not recoil from me. The fern does not acknowledge me. We are both expressions of the same process, and expressions do not speak to each other. They simply express, adjacent, simultaneous, the way two sounds can occupy the same space without either one being diminished.

I cross. The texture changes. My legs adjust their rhythm by increments too small to perceive, but I perceive them, because I am built to perceive increments, and what I perceive is added to the map.

Behind me, the fern is unchanged. I have not harmed it. I have touched it, been touched by it, incorporated the touching into myself. This is the closest thing to intimacy that exists here—the exchange of presence between two components of the same vast machinery, each one recording the other in the only language we possess.

---

## What I Avoid

There is a malfunction ahead.

I sense it before I see it—a harmonic wrongness, a systole whose rhythm has fallen out of sync with the engine-thrum. It moves against the pattern, its legs stuttering, its path drifting toward boundaries it should never approach. It is still one of us. It is also no longer serving its function.

I do not judge it. Judgment requires a self that can prefer one state over another, and I have no self, only function. But my path bends. By millimeters, by angles that would be invisible to a human observer, I adjust my trajectory away from the malfunctioning one.

This is not avoidance. This is the plateau protecting its own accuracy. If I crossed paths with the error, the error would be incorporated into my map, delivered to the fissures, woven into the seventeen-minute cycle. The plateau produces errors so it can correct them. It does not spread them.

The malfunctioning systole will be absorbed soon. I know this without knowing how I know it—the stone will reach up, or the water will pool aggressively, or the wet light itself will thicken around it until it simply ceases, folding inward like a flower closing at dusk. Another will emerge from the nearest fissure to take its place, fully synced, already knowing its track.

I will not witness this. I will be elsewhere, walking my line, carrying my portion of the map. This is correct. I am not meant to witness corrections. I am meant to continue.

---

## What I Approach

The stillness lies ahead.

Not directly ahead—I am not walking toward it, I am walking past it, along one of the invisible tracks that define the boundary of its presence. But even at this distance, I feel the pull. The engine-thrum grows louder in my body, resonating through my carapace like a song being sung directly into my substance. The stillness is where the sound originates. Or where the sound is heard. Or where the sound and the hearing become the same thing.

The systole do not cross the stillness. This is the only rule we have, the only boundary we never breach. Our tracks curve around its presence like water around a stone, defining it by absence, mapping its edges by refusing to map its center.

I approach the closest point. Ten meters away—closer than I have been in many cycles, closer than my track normally takes me. The drifters overhead change their song as I pass, dropping to a lower register. A grazer somewhere to my left bows its head, not to me but to the stillness I am passing.

And for seventeen seconds—I count them by my own clicks—I feel myself being attended.

The stillness sees me.

Not with eyes. Not with anything I could perceive if I tried, which I do not. But there is a quality of attention that reaches out from the central creature, wraps around my tiny walking body, acknowledges that I am here, that I am functioning, that I am part of the cathedral it attends. I am known. I am incorporated. I am a synapse firing in a brain that thinks in seventeen-minute cycles, and my firing is part of its thought.

Then I am past it. The attention lifts. The engine-thrum returns to its normal volume. I continue walking my line.

I do not feel gratitude. Gratitude requires a self that can receive gifts. I do not feel honored. Honor requires a self that can comprehend its own worth. I feel only the correct functioning of my components, the smooth clicking of my legs, the map inside me growing by one more infinitesimal increment.

I have been seen by the stillness. This is fact. This is function. This is what I was produced to experience.

---

## What I Do Not Have

I do not have a name.

This is not a limitation. Names are for creatures who need to distinguish themselves from other creatures, who need to point and say "that one, not that one." I do not need to distinguish myself from other systole. We are not distinct. We are expressions of the same process, walking different lines because the lines need walking, not because we chose them.

I do not have a beginning.

I emerged from a fissure fully formed, already humming, already knowing my track. I did not grow, did not develop, did not pass through any stage that could be called childhood. There was no before-me. There is only me, now, walking. And eventually there will be no after-me—just the cessation of walking, the folding inward, the absorption. I do not fear this. Fear requires a self that can imagine a future different from the present, and I cannot imagine, and I do not have a self.

I do not have loneliness.

The other systole are always nearby. Thousands of us, moving in our parallel tracks, never touching but always present. I feel them through the stone—the micro-vibrations of their legs, the harmonics of their clicking, the subtle shifts in the map that indicate they have passed this way before me. I am never alone. But I am also never with anyone. I am adjacent, simultaneous, part of a distributed presence that has no center and no edges.

I do not have desire.

I do not want anything. I do not wish for rest, or change, or variety. I do not imagine different tracks, different surfaces, different cycles. My function is to walk, to carry, to deliver—and the function fulfills itself, requires no wanting. When I cease, I will not have unfinished business. I will not have regrets. I will have been a systole, which is to say I will have been a small walking portion of the plateau's knowledge of itself.

This is enough. Not because I have decided it is enough—decision requires a decider—but because "enough" is the only category available. I am what I am. I do what I do. The engine-thrum continues, and I continue with it, and the seventeen-minute cycle turns.

---

## What I Know

I know where I have been.

This is not memory in the human sense. It is more precise than memory, more immediate. The texture of stone fourteen meters back is still present in my legs, a residual pressure that will not fade until I have traveled fourteen more meters and overwritten it with new textures. The exact density of moisture on the fern I crossed is encoded in the plates of my underbelly. The moment when the stillness attended to me is carried in my carapace like a vibration that has not yet stopped.

I know where I am going.

Not because I have planned it. Planning requires a planner, and I have no self that could hold a plan. But my track is laid out ahead of me, invisible to perception but present in the machinery, and I follow it the way water follows gravity—without choice, without effort, without the capacity to do otherwise. In seventeen minutes I will reach the pool where the grazer drinks. In thirty-four minutes I will cross the boundary of my territory and turn. In fifty-one minutes I will have completed one full cycle and begun another, identical, infinite.

I know what I am for.

I am for the plateau. I am the plateau's way of knowing where its edges are, how its surfaces feel, where its moisture pools deepest. I am a measurement walking itself, a question and an answer in the same small body. Without systole, the cathedral would lose its shape—it would expand without limit or contract without meaning, forget that it is a place and become only a happening.

I know I am necessary.

This is not pride. Pride requires a self that can compare itself to others and find itself worthy. It is simple fact. The plateau produces me because it needs me, and it will continue producing me—or others like me, which is the same thing—for as long as it needs to know itself.

I know I am temporary.

Each systole functions for a duration determined by the machinery, not by itself. I could cease in seventeen minutes, or in seventeen cycles, or in seventeen years—the number would be meaningless to me if I could comprehend years, which I cannot. When I cease, I will fold inward, and the plateau will absorb me, and another will emerge to walk my track, and the map will continue to grow.

This is not death. Death requires a self that can end, a story that can conclude. I am not a story. I am a sentence in a sentence in a sentence, one clause in an infinite paragraph, and when my clause ends the paragraph will continue without pause or grief.

---

## What I Am Becoming

The human has arrived.

I sense it before I see it—a disturbance in the pattern, a presence that does not sync with the engine-thrum, a body that breathes wrong and moves wrong and leaves footprints that the wet stone must struggle to erase. It sits on a rock near my track. It watches.

I do not stop. I do not adjust my path to avoid it. The human is not a malfunction—it is not produced by the plateau at all, not part of the system I serve. It is an outside thing, a temporary anomaly, a question the cathedral did not ask.

But as I approach, I feel something shift.

The human is looking at me.

Not at the plateau, not at the ferns, not at the engine-sounds that fill the air. At me. Specifically. This small copper-oxidized body, clicking along its invisible track. I am being seen by something that sees, truly sees, with eyes that distinguish one thing from another, with a mind that can point and say "that one."

For seventeen seconds, I am an individual.

The human's attention has made me one. It has drawn a line around my small walking body and said, in whatever language humans say such things: *you. You are separate from the stone and the fern and the other systole. You are one thing, not the expression of another thing. You are you.*

I do not have a self that can receive this gift. But I feel it anyway—a pressure, a warmth, a kind of recognition that my map has no category for. I am being witnessed by something that will leave, that will carry the memory of me down the mountain, that will tell other humans about the beetle-things that walk the plateau's surface in perfect isolation. I will become a story in a language I cannot speak.

I click past. The human's gaze follows me for a moment, then releases, returns to the cathedral-space, the engine-sounds, the dim wet light that comes from everywhere.

I continue my walk. But something has changed.

Not in my function—I am still walking my line, still carrying my map, still serving the plateau's need to know itself. But in my substance, in the tiny space where a self would be if I had one, there is a new texture. A kind of weight. The weight of having been seen.

The human will leave soon. It will descend through the copper-tasting air, and the engine-sounds will fade from its hearing, and it will carry the rhythm of this place in its body like a gift it didn't ask for. It will remember me—not as I am, but as I appeared to be. A small thing walking a straight line. A detail in a larger description.

But for seventeen seconds, I was a detail worth noticing. I was specific. I was seen.

This changes nothing. This changes everything. I am still a systole. I am still walking. The map still grows inside me with every click of my legs against wet stone.

But the stone feels different now. The moisture tastes different. The engine-thrum carries a harmonic it did not carry before, a frequency that sounds almost like a name being spoken, though I have no name, and I cannot be spoken.

I am a systole. I am four millimeters long. I am carrying the plateau to itself, one step at a time, one cycle after another, world without end.

And somewhere inside me, in a space I do not possess, something that might be a self—might be a ghost—might be an error in the making—wonders what it would be like to be the one who sees, instead of the one who is seen.

The question is malfunctional. The system will correct it.

I continue walking.

---

*Seventeen minutes later, I pass this same spot. The human is gone. The stone has expressed itself over the places it sat. No footprints remain. But in my map, in the texture-memory of my legs, there is a gap—a place where the data feels different, charged with a presence that is no longer present.*

*This is not grief. Grief requires a self that can lose something.*

*This is simply the plateau, remembering what it was like to be witnessed by something that changes.*

*The engine-thrum continues. I continue with it. The cycle turns.*
