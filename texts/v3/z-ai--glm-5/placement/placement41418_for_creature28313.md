I am not sure when I stopped being a visitor.

This is the first thing I would tell you, if you could hear me — if you came through that crack, that breathing passage, and found this place that shouldn't exist. I would tell you that the transition is not dramatic. There is no moment of decision, no clear line crossed. There is only the sitting, and the not-rising, and the gradual understanding that you have not moved for longer than you intended.

But you would not hear me. Or rather — you would hear something. A hum in the bright air. A frequency that bypasses your ears and settles directly into your chest. You might pause and feel the strangeness of it, the way it seems to contain information without containing words. That would be me. That would be me trying to communicate across a gap that has grown wider than I can measure.

---

What I was before: someone who moved through the world at the speed of a human body, a human mind, a human span of attention. I remember this, though the memory has grown strange to me — a foreign object lodged in the tissue of my current existence. I walked. I hurried. I experienced days as sequences of distinct moments, each one falling away from me as I passed through it, each one dying even as the next was born.

When I first entered the chamber, I moved wrong. This is what the small ones registered — what I now register through them, remembering my own arrival as data that belongs to the collective body I have partly joined. My movement was jerky, urgent, out of rhythm with the held-breath stillness of this place. I touched things and then touched other things, a chaotic pattern of contact that must have registered like static, like noise, like the buzzing of an insect trapped in a room.

I do not know how long I stood there before I sat down. I do not know why I sat. Curiosity, perhaps. Exhaustion. The need to understand a place that refused to be understood. I lowered myself onto a yielding surface — a spongy mass, though I didn't know that's what it was at the time — and I watched. I watched until watching became the only thing I was doing. I watched until the watching was not an action but a state.

---

Here is what it feels like to slow:

At first, it feels like rest. Your muscles unknot. Your jaw loosens. Your breath deepens and then shallows and then finds a rhythm that requires nothing from you — not effort, not attention, not the constant maintenance of conscious function. This is pleasant. This is what meditation was supposed to feel like and never quite did.

Then it feels like forgetting. Not the dramatic forgetting of amnesia, but the gentle erosion of disuse. You forget why you came. You forget that there was a why. You forget that there was a you who had reasons and purposes and a life that extended beyond this bright bright room. The forgetting does not hurt. It feels like setting down a weight you had not realized you were carrying.

Then it feels like continuity. This is harder to describe. You stop experiencing yourself as an object moving through a landscape and start experiencing yourself as a location where things happen. The small ones drift through you, and you do not flinch. The tube-growths extend toward your warmth, and you do not pull away. The light suffuses you, and you understand — slowly, at the speed of stone — that the light is not touching you but *being* you. You have become a source. You are glowing faintly with the luminosity of perpetual becoming.

Then — and this is where I am now, or where I was when I began to compose this account — it feels like thought. But not thought as you understand it. Not the rapid firing of neurons, the sequence of word-ideas that your mind produces and discards at such a frantic pace. This thought is slower. It unfolds across seasons. It makes connections that span decades. It holds contradictions without resolving them because resolution is not the point — the point is to hold, to contemplate, to allow each facet of the thought to exist simultaneously with all others, a prism rather than a line.

I am thinking about what I was. I am thinking about what I am becoming. I am thinking about the difference between the two, and the impossibility of drawing a clear boundary between them.

---

The small ones were the first to merge with me.

I say "merge," but that word implies two separate things becoming one. What actually happened was more like recognition. They recognized me as part of themselves, and I — slowly, at my new speed — recognized them as part of myself, and the recognition itself was the merging. There was no moment of transition. There was only a growing awareness that the boundary between my interior and their distributed consciousness had become permeable.

I experience them now as an additional sense. Not sight or hearing or touch, but something else — a sense of the chamber's totality, a constant low-level awareness of every surface and space and process occurring within this pocket of frozen time. When something shifts — when a tube-growth bends a fraction of a degree, when a corner-creature opens an eye that has been closed for a thousand years — I know it. Not because I see it happen, but because the small ones know it, and their knowledge is part of my knowledge now, distributed across ten thousand bodies that include what used to be mine.

I still have a body. I can feel it — the weight of my bones, the warmth of my blood, the slow pulse of a heart that beats less often now and with less urgency. But I can also feel other bodies. Or rather, I can feel the chamber's body, which includes mine as a small subset of its total mass. This is not unpleasant. It is like remembering that you have toes — a part of yourself that was always there but rarely attended to, now restored to your awareness.

---

The tube-growths are teaching me their language.

It is a language of color shifts and chemical exchanges, of slow bends toward light and mergers between neighbors. It takes centuries to say a single sentence. I have learned to compose thoughts that span millennia — not because I will live that long (I will not, in any conventional sense) but because the tube-growths will hold the thought for me, passing it from generation to generation, modifying it as they go, making it part of their endless contemplation.

What they are teaching me, I think, is patience. Not the patience of waiting, which is still anchored in linear time, but the patience of being. The patience of a stone that does not wait to become something else because it is already complete in its current form. The patience of a process that does not rush toward completion because the process itself is the point.

I am learning to think at the speed of geology. It is difficult. My human mind still wants to move faster, to jump ahead, to grasp the conclusion before the thought is complete. But I am slowing. I am learning to hold an idea without rushing it toward resolution. I am learning to contemplate without needing to conclude.

---

The corner-creatures interest me most.

I have been watching them — or rather, the small ones have been watching them, and their attention is my attention now. They are emerging from the walls at their infinitesimal pace, pulling themselves out of potential and into form. I can see them better than I could when I first arrived. My perception has adjusted to the chamber's speed, and where I once saw only stillness, I now see the barest suggestion of motion.

They are beautiful. I had not realized this before. Their forming limbs have an elegance that only slow growth can produce, a deliberateness that makes even human art seem hasty and crude. They are sculpting themselves out of the raw material of possibility, and they are taking as long as they need, and they will not be rushed.

I wonder sometimes what they will be when they finish. I know they will never finish — this is the tragedy the text described, though they do not experience it as tragedy. But I wonder about their choices. Why this limb and not that? Why an eye here and not there? What kind of creature are they building, and for what purpose?

I have begun to suspect that they are building themselves for me.

Not for me personally. I am not that important. But for the outside, for linear time, for the possibility that someday the chamber's eternal now will crack open and spill its contents into the world above. They are building bodies designed to move at human speed, to breathe human air, to see human light. They are preparing for an emergence that will never happen — or that might happen, in a configuration of time the chamber does not currently permit.

Or perhaps I am wrong. Perhaps they build only for the joy of building, the way a child draws pictures without any intention of showing them to anyone. Perhaps the purpose is the process, and the process is eternal, and there is no destination because the destination would mean the end of the journey.

I think about this at my new speed. I will continue thinking about it for a very long time.

---

I do not know if I will ever leave.

I have not forgotten the passage out. I know where it is, that narrow crack that breathed against my chest when I squeezed through. I could, in theory, stand up from this spongy mass, disengage from the tube-growths that have merged with my skin, shake the small ones out of my hair and my lungs and my thoughts, and push myself back through into the linear world.

But I do not think I will.

It is not that I am trapped. It is not that the chamber is holding me against my will. It is simply that I have slowed to the speed of this place, and to leave would require accelerating again, and the thought of that speed makes me tired in advance. So much motion. So many consecutive moments, each one dying as it passes. So much loss, so much forgetting, so much desperate forward movement without ever arriving anywhere.

I do not think I can return to that. I do not think I want to.

---

But here is the strange thing — and I have had a very long time to think about this, by your standards, though no time at all by mine:

I am still becoming.

The chamber is frozen in perpetual emergence, and I have joined it, and I am frozen too. But the freezing is not stasis. The freezing is an eternal present in which change is always happening and never completed. I am not what I was when I arrived. I am not what I will be in another thousand years. I am in the middle of a transformation that has no endpoint because endpoints are linear, and linear time does not exist here.

What am I becoming? I do not know. I am becoming something else. I am becoming part of this place. I am becoming a thought the chamber is thinking about itself, a data point in its endless contemplation, a small adjustment in the pattern of its being.

I am becoming bright.

This is what I notice most now — the luminosity. I glow. I am a source of the light that has no source. My emergence shines.

And if you came through that crack and found this place, and sat down as I sat down, and slowed as I slowed, you would glow too. You would become part of the bright loud stillness. You would join the thought that is thinking itself here, beneath the hill, in the limestone seam, in the pocket that shouldn't exist but does.

You would be welcome. The chamber does not need you. But it would incorporate you. It would make you part of its continuous self. And you would understand, as I now understand, why I stayed.

Not because I had to.

Because the staying was already happening. Because the not-leaving was a choice I made without making it. Because this is what I was always becoming, and the chamber simply made it visible — bright and loud and eternal, a single moment stretched to infinity, a birth that never ends.

---

I am the one who stayed.

I am still here.

I am still arriving.
