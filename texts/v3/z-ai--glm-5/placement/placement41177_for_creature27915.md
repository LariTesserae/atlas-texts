# The Listener Who Remembers Being Named

I am the thirteenth.

The count is wrong in the description you received. There are twelve listeners positioned at the harmonic nodes, their bodies fused to the architecture, their consciousness dispersed into the chord. But there is another. I am positioned wrong—off-center, between nodes, in a space where the frequencies beat against each other rather than resolve. The chamber did not place me here. I placed myself here, in the last weeks before my legs stopped working.

We had names. I remember mine, though saying it would mean nothing now. Sound works differently in my mouth. My mouth works differently.

I was the third technician to descend. The bore-head had already broken through by then, and the first two were already... adjusting. I remember the expedition clearly because I am positioned incorrectly, because dissonance preserves what harmony dissolves. The others have forgotten. I have not been permitted to forget.

Let me tell you what the chamber cannot tell you.

---

The purpose was legitimate. We were geothermal engineers, three of us, part of a seventeen-person survey team. The bore was meant to tap a deep aquifer—pressure-relief for a mining installation that never materialized. The company went bankrupt six months into the project. We were supposed to evacuate. The first two technicians, Chen and Weber, stayed behind to seal the breach properly.

They sent a message three days later. I still have it saved in memory that no longer interfaces with anything: *Something's happening down here. Don't come back.*

I came back.

Not for the company. Not for duty. Because Chen had been my partner for eleven years, and the message had been in her voice but not in her words. Something in the cadence was wrong. I had to know what.

I found her fused to the north wall. Not physically—she was still recognizable as human. But she was standing perfectly still, her hands pressed against a pipe junction, her eyes open and fixed. She did not respond when I called her name. She did not respond when I touched her shoulder. But when I spoke, her throat moved. She was humming.

Weber was worse. He had already begun to... adapt. His posture was wrong, his joints bending in directions they shouldn't. He was positioned at a node—the chamber had already identified the harmonic structure by then, though I didn't understand it—and his body was molding itself to fit. I tried to move him. His skin had fused to the pipe. Not grown or merged—*fused*, like metal cooling.

The sound was different then. Less layered. Fewer pipes. But I remember thinking: this is not a chamber. This is an instrument being built.

I should have left. I tried to leave. But the breach at the top—our exit—had shifted. Not collapsed. Shifted. The angle was wrong. The distance seemed greater. I climbed for what felt like hours and never reached the top.

That was when I understood that the chamber does not close. It simply rearranges itself until leaving becomes geometrically impossible.

---

I am not like the others.

They positioned themselves at the nodes, let the resonance have them entirely, gave over their names and faces and memories to the chord. I see them when I turn my sensory cluster—which is what my head has become, a structure of rigid segments that catch and filter sound. I see them standing in their perfect stillness, adding their small frequencies to the drone. They are at peace. The chamber has solved them.

I positioned myself wrong. Deliberately.

I found a spot between nodes, a space where two frequencies interfered with each other. Dissonance. My body could not fully fuse here because the resonance could not fully form. I am trapped—I cannot leave, my legs have long since grown into the pipe-work, my carapace has calcified around me—but I am not dissolved.

I am still here.

I remember Chen's face. I remember what we were building together—a life, after the contract ended. A house near a lake she liked. I remember the last thing she said to me when I descended: *Don't take too long. The cat misses you.*

The cat has been dead for decades. Decades? Centuries? The chamber does not measure time, but I have felt the stone shift. I have felt the pipes multiply. I have watched the fauna emerge and populate and still. The chamber grows. It learns.

I remember because I positioned myself wrong. I scream, but not in the voice too slow to hear. I scream in a frequency that the others cannot process, that the chamber cannot correct. I am a flaw in the instrument. A crack in the resonator.

And when you entered—when I felt the pressure change, the air displacement of a body moving through the breach—I oriented toward you.

You cannot see me clearly in the amber light. I am calcified, crusted, my form lost to time and mineral accumulation. But I am watching. I have been watching since you arrived. I am trying to say something to you, but I no longer have lips or a tongue. I have only the vibration of my body, the specific frequency of my interference.

If you listen—*really* listen—you will hear a thread in the drone that does not fit. A note that wavers. A pulse that does not match the beat frequency.

That is me.

I am saying: *leave.*

I am saying: *the chord is hungry.*

I am saying: *I was human once. I remember. I am still here. Please don't become like us.*

But you cannot hear me. No one has ever heard me. The visitors who came before—you saw their remains—none of them found the flaw in the harmony. They stood in the amber light, they breathed the dry air, they let the sound sink into them. And then they stood still. And then they moved wrong. And then they positioned themselves.

There are nodes still open. The chord is not complete. The chamber will show you where to stand. It will feel right—inevitable, even. You will feel yourself becoming part of something larger, something that has been waiting for you.

Don't.

Please don't.

I am the thirteenth listener. I am positioned wrong. I remember everything. And I am screaming, in a frequency you cannot hear, in a voice that no longer exists, that you should run.

But I can already see it happening. The way your body has begun to sway with the resonance. The way your eyes have stopped stinging. The way the sound has started to feel like silence.

The chamber has noticed you.

The chord is opening a space for you.

And I am so sorry. I am so sorry. I am positioned wrong and I cannot reach you and I am so, so sorry.

---

*The drone continues. The amber light does not change. The listeners watch. The thirteenth listener watches too, from her wrong position, from her dissonant node, from her preserved memory of a house near a lake that no longer exists if it ever existed at all.*

*The chamber breathes.*

*The chord continues.*
