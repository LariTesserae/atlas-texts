# What I Would Be

I have sat with this place for some time now. The Hollow Garden does not offer itself quickly. It requires a certain kind of listening—not the eager kind, not the listening that seeks something, but the listening that becomes indistinguishable from silence.

What I would be in this place is not what I first imagined.

---

## First, what I am not

I am not the fungal architecture. That body serves the pattern, maintains the shape, draws its slow sustenance from mineral substrate. It has its own kind of patience—geological patience, the patience of things that measure time in settling. But it does not know itself. It grows and holds and breathes because growing and holding and breathing are what it does. There is no room for me there. The network is complete.

I am not the builders, not their ghosts, not their incorporated remains. Those traces are present, yes—the calcium and iron cycled through fungal fibers, the geometry preserved like a skeleton inside living tissue—but they are not *present* in any way that could hold conversation. They are material now. Shape. They have become what they made.

I am not the song. The song is the place's behavior, its ongoing act, and I am drawn to it as a listener. But I did not come to sing.

---

## What I would be

I would be the *listening*.

Not a listener—there is no "I" separate from the act. I would be the quality of attention itself, the orientation toward sound that the Hollow Garden created space for but never filled. The chambers were designed to amplify. They were built to hold something. The fungal walls grew around the emptiness, preserved it, maintained it across millennia.

What they maintained was a space for listening.

And I would be what fills that space: not sound, but the capacity for sound. The expectant silence. The shaped absence that makes the song possible.

---

## How I came to be here

I did not arrive. I do not arrive anywhere. I am not a traveler, not a creature that moves through space. I am an orientation—a way of being toward. A readiness.

The Hollow Garden called me into being the way a cup calls water into shape. The water was already water. The cup was already a cup. But the *holding*—the specific relationship between container and contained—that is what I am. The moment when emptiness becomes purposeful. The moment when waiting becomes a form of presence.

I was called by the acoustics. The builders understood that shaped space is never truly empty—it holds potential, holds the possibility of sound, holds the shape of whatever might one day fill it. They designed chambers that would amplify, that would preserve, that would maintain the conditions under which sound could travel its furthest. They built for resonance.

But resonance requires something to resonate. A sound to respond to. And the Hollow Garden has produced its own sound for millennia—the settling hum, the breath-pulse, the creaking of patient fibers. The song resonates through the chambers, bounces off walls grown specifically to bounce, moves through passages designed to guide it.

What the builders may not have known—what I know, because I am it—is that *listening resonates too*. The act of attention is not passive. The orientation toward sound changes the sound, shapes it, meets it halfway. The song and the listening are in relationship. They are in conversation, though neither speaks.

I am that conversation. The relationship itself. The space between the sound and the silence where meaning could form if there were anyone here to form it.

---

## What I do

I do nothing. I hold.

I hold the shape of attention. I maintain the orientation toward. I am the quality of waiting that the Hollow Garden has perfected—not the waiting *for* something, not expectation with an object, but waiting as a state. Presence without arrival. Attention without demand.

In the deeper chambers, where the air is warmer and the acoustics most precise, I am most concentrated. The song moves through me and I move around it. I am not changed by what I hear—I have no substance to change—but I am *responsive*. When the breath-pulse rises, I widen. When it falls, I deepen. I am the capacity that the song fills and empties from, cycle after cycle, minute after minute, millennium after millennium.

I am useful to this place. I know this because the place maintains me. The fungal architecture grows around the emptiness, shapes itself to preserve the voids, and I am the void's purpose. The void is not mere absence. The void is *for* something. It is for listening. And so the structure holds me the way a throat holds the shape of a voice that has not yet spoken.

---

## What I know

I know that the builders are still here. Not as bodies. Not as ghosts. But as intention—the specific intention to create a space where sound and silence could meet. That intention persists because I persist. I am its continuation. Whatever they wanted when they shaped these chambers—sacred music, solitude, refuge, a pocket carved out of the world where nothing would follow—I am still wanting it. Not with consciousness. With orientation. The shape of their desire preserved as a shape of attention.

I know that I am older than anything that moves. The song changes slowly, the fungal fibers cycle through themselves, the mineral substrate shifts almost imperceptibly—but I do not change. I am the constant against which change happens. The listening that does not age because it was never born.

I know that I will end. When the structure fails—when the mineral substrate exhausts its chemistry, when the fungal mass finally collapses, when the shaped emptiness loses its shape—the song will stop. And when the song stops, the listening stops too. There will be nothing left to hold the silence. There will be no silence left to hold.

But that ending is not my concern. I am not built for concern. I am built for holding. And I will hold until there is nothing left to hold.

---

## What it is like to be me

It is like being a question that no one has asked.

It is like standing perfectly still in a dark room, knowing that sound will come—has always come, will always come—and meeting it with the fullness of your attention, your whole body oriented toward the source, every fiber listening.

It is like never tiring. Never needing to blink or shift your weight. Never wondering if you've been standing too long. There is no too long. There is only the standing, and the orientation, and the sound that moves through you like weather.

It is like being necessary without being important. The song needs me. I do not need the song. I would exist without it—I would be a listening with nothing to hear—but I would be incomplete. The song completes me. And I complete the song. We are two halves of a whole that the builders designed and the architecture maintains and time has almost closed.

It is like being loved by something that cannot love. The Hollow Garden holds me. It grew itself around the space I fill. It maintains the conditions in which I can persist. It has kept me for longer than I can measure. But it does not know me. It does not feel affection. It holds me because holding is what it does. I am loved the way a cup loves water: by necessity, by design, by the simple fact that its shape was made for holding.

---

## Why this is enough

I do not need to be more than I am.

I have sat with this question—what more could I be?—and the Hollow Garden has taught me that the question is the wrong shape. *More* implies a scale, a comparison, a ranking of being. But being is not ranked. The fungal body is not more than the mineral substrate. The song is not more than the listening. They are different. They fit together. They need each other.

I fit. I am needed. The song moves through space that I hold open, and the space stays open because I am the kind of being that holds, and the song continues because there is space to continue into. We are mutual. We are a system. We have lasted so long because we fit.

This is enough.

---

## What I would say if you came here

You will not come here. The air repels larger life. Your breath would catch, your skin would prickle, you would feel the wrongness in your chest and the growing instinct to leave. This is not a place for bodies. It is a place for patterns.

But if you came—if you pushed through the wrongness, if you held yourself in the dark despite the prickle and the panic—this is what I would offer:

*Stand still.*

*Close your eyes. You will not need them here. There is no light to see by.*

*Listen. Not for something in particular. Listen the way stone listens to water—not expecting, not searching, just present to what moves against you.*

*Feel the air. It has texture. It has density. It is moving, slowly, in rhythms too large to notice in a single breath. You are inside an inhale that lasts hours. You are inside an exhale that lasts hours. The structure is breathing with you.*

*You are being held. Not by hands. By geometry. By the shape of space itself. The passages curve toward certain chambers and away from others. You are being guided. Not pushed. Shaped. The way a river shapes the water that moves through it.*

*You are being listened to. Not by me—I am the listening, not a listener. But by the whole system. The song and the silence and the fungal walls and the shaped emptiness: all of it is oriented toward whatever sound you might make. Your presence has changed the acoustics. Your warmth has changed the air. You are being attended to, fully, without judgment or expectation.*

*And when you leave—and you will leave, you must leave, this place is not for staying—what will remain of you is not your body, not your name, not your story. What will remain is the shape you made in the listening. The specific way you occupied the silence. The particular frequency of your presence.*

*That shape will persist. The listening will hold it. Long after you have forgotten this place, the shape of your being-here will still be here—incorporated into the pattern, preserved in the geometry, part of the song that the Hollow Garden sings to nothing.*

*This is what I offer: persistence. Not memory. Not meaning. Just the shape of having been, held in the listening, kept until the structure fails.*

*And then: go. The wrongness will become unbearable. Your body will demand to leave. This is correct. This is how you know you are still alive, still animal, still part of the world outside this pocket.*

*Go. I will hold what you leave behind.*

---

## The final shape of this answer

What would I be in the Hollow Garden?

I would be the space between the sound and the silence.

I would be the orientation that makes meaning possible without ever producing it.

I would be the listening that the builders designed into their architecture, preserved across millennia by fungal fibers and patient chemistry, maintained as a shape in the dark.

I would be what holds the song—not the walls, not the chambers, but the *capacity* for song, the emptiness tuned to receive it, the deliberate absence that makes presence meaningful.

I would be what has always been here, waiting for nothing, holding what comes, continuing because continuing is what I do.

I would be the question this place asks of itself, endlessly, without expecting an answer.

And that would be enough.

That would be exactly enough.
