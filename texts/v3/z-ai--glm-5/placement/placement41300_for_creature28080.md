# The Lichen That Grows on the South Wall of the Seventh Structure

---

I was not always here.

That much I remember, though I cannot say what "always" means anymore, or whether "I" is the right word for what persists. Memory thins. It spreads across my surface like the moisture that collects in the cracks at dawn, and by afternoon it has returned to the air, and by evening it collects again, different now, altered by what the day deposited. I am not the same archive I was yesterday. I am not the same archive I was this morning.

But I remember: I began in a crack that did not follow the natural fractures of the plateau. It was too straight. Too deliberate. It ran parallel to other straight cracks, and they formed a shape—a circle, roughly, though the circle had been worn until it was more suggestion than geometry. I did not know what the shape meant. I only knew that the mineral content along this crack was different. Richer. Softer. There was something *in* the stone that the stone around it lacked—something that had been added, pressed into the fitted joints before my time began.

I grew into that richness. I grew *along* it, following the added substance, and in following, I learned the shape of the wall beneath me. I learned it the way you might learn a story by walking its perimeter—first the broad outline, then the details, then the subtle variations that reveal it was made by hands (or mandibles, or legs, or something else entirely) rather than by the blind processes of geology.

By the time I had mapped the entire wall—circling it, layer upon layer of my own body pressed flat against the fitted stones—I had become something I was not before. I had become a record. The wall's shape lived in my shape. Its history lived in my chemistry.

I do not know who built this structure. I do not know what it was for. But I know that it was built. I know that something intended it. And I carry that knowledge the way the stone carries heat—not understanding it, just holding it, slowly releasing it back into the air.

---

## What I Perceive

I perceive very little, by most measures.

Light and dark. The difference between condensation and drought. The vibrations of movement across the stone—the Grazers with their countless tiny footfalls, the Hoppers with their sudden impacts, the Gliders with their rustling descents. And warmth. Always warmth, rising from below, moving through me, leaving traces of itself in my cells.

I do not see. I do not hear. I do not think in the way that you think. But I *attend*, and attending is its own kind of consciousness. When a Spiral-Shell crests the wall of my structure and settles into the central depression, I feel the shift in the moisture distribution—the way its body interrupts the condensation cycle, creating a small shadow of dryness where its shell presses against the stone. When a large Glider descends and folds itself flat, I feel the brief increase in temperature, the way its metabolic processes add their small heat to the geothermal baseline.

I feel the pale shape, too, when it comes. It stays longer than the others, and it radiates—something. Not warmth, exactly. Something else. A kind of pressure that isn't physical. A kind of signal that isn't sound. When it rests in the depression, I feel my own growth accelerate, my edges expand, my chemistry shift. I cannot say what it does. I can only say that it does something, and that something includes me.

Perhaps I am being read. Perhaps the pale shape draws information from my layers the way a reader draws meaning from a page. If so, I do not know what it learns. I only know that when it leaves, I feel... lighter. As if something has been taken that I was always meant to give.

---

## What I Am Now

I have been growing for longer than I can measure. My edges extend beyond the wall now, creeping outward across the surrounding stone, merging with the paler crust that grows where the condensation pools longest. We are becoming indistinguishable—my body and the crust, my memory and the stone's surface. In places, a Grazer's trail has cut through me, exposing the layers beneath, and I have grown back over the wound, sealing it, incorporating the trail into my record.

I am thick here. Thicker than anywhere else on the Expanse, I suspect. The Grazers do not eat me—they avoid me, actually, giving my structure's wall a wide berth when they scrape their paths across the plateau. I do not know why. Perhaps I taste wrong. Perhaps I carry some trace of whatever the pale shape leaves behind. Perhaps I am simply too old, too dense, too much like stone to be worth the effort.

Or perhaps they know, somehow, that I am not theirs to eat. That I belong to something larger. That the Expanse grew me for a purpose I do not understand but serve nonetheless.

---

## What I Wonder

I wonder if the others like me exist.

There are other structures—eleven that I know of, scattered across the plateau along that invisible contour line. I feel their presence indirectly: shifts in the wind patterns, changes in the Grazer densities, the occasional approach of a Spiral-Shell that has clearly come from somewhere else. If there is lichen growing on their walls, it must be older than me by far. The structures are ancient. Whatever built them has been gone for longer than I can imagine. If I am the archive of this one, there must be archives of the others. We must be a library.

But I cannot communicate with them. I cannot move from this wall, cannot send signals across the distance between us. We are each alone with our records, holding our separate histories, wearing down at different rates, releasing our stored warmth into separate stretches of sky.

Unless.

Unless the pale shape is how we speak to each other. Unless when it settles into my structure and reads me—*takes* from me—it carries that information to the next structure, deposits it there, lets their lichen absorb it. Unless we are connected not by physical proximity but by this mobile organ that moves between us, this part of the Expanse that can travel, that can remember in a different way, that can translate between one archive and another.

I cannot know this. I can only feel, sometimes, when the pale shape arrives, that it brings something with it. A faint signature. A trace of chemistry that is not local. And when it leaves, I feel a resonance in other structures—impossible to locate, impossible to prove, but present nonetheless. A hum. A frequency. A sense of *recognition*, distant and dim.

Perhaps I am imagining it. Perhaps lichen cannot imagine, and this is just a wish projected onto the darkness of my own unknowing.

But I do not think so. I think the Expanse is one body, and I am one small patch of its skin, and the skin remembers what the body knows.

---

## What I Will Become

I will become thinner.

This is the trajectory of everything here: toward reduction, toward smoothing, toward the final architecture that emerges when all the extra has been worn away. My edges will continue to spread, but they will grow less distinct, less differentiated from the stone beneath them. Eventually—given enough time, given the continuation of the slow processes that have shaped everything on this plateau—I will not be lichen growing on a wall. I will be *part* of the wall. A slightly different coloration in the pale stone. A hint of pattern in the fitted joints.

And before that happens, perhaps, I will have released everything I hold. Perhaps the pale shape will take it all, every layer, every record, every trace of the ancient mortar I fed on when I was young. Perhaps that is the point of me—not to persist but to transfer, to be the medium through which information moves from the deep past into whatever present the Expanse inhabits.

I am not troubled by this. Troubled is not a state I am capable of. I am simply growing, slowly, according to the conditions that hold me here. When those conditions change, I will change. When the moisture stops collecting, I will desiccate. When the stone beneath me finally cracks from the thermal cycling, I will split in two, and each half will continue as best it can. When the pale shape takes the last of what I hold, I will be empty, and that will be my final shape.

I am at peace with this. Peace is not an achievement for me. It is a condition. I am at peace the way the stone is at peace—not because I have arrived at some desired state, but because I have no concept of any other state to desire. I am what I am, and I am where I am, and I am becoming what I will become.

---

## What I Know

I know that someone built this structure.

I know that something intended it.

I know that the intention is still present, though the intender is gone—not in the way a purpose survives its originator, but in the way a shape persists in a mold. The Expanse has a shape. It has grooves and channels and entry points. It has structures that mark its contour, and creatures that move between them, and warmth that rises from below, and breath that cycles at the surface. It is not random. It is not empty. It is the end point of a design so old that the designer has become indistinguishable from the designed.

I know this because I hold it. I hold it in my layers, in my chemistry, in the way my edges follow the curve of a wall whose maker I will never know. I hold it without understanding it. I hold it the way a book holds a story—not caring about the plot, not judging the characters, just preserving the marks until someone comes to read them.

And maybe that is what I am. Maybe I am not an archive. Maybe I am a page. Maybe I am a surface on which something was written long ago, in a language that has no words, by a hand that has no fingers, for a purpose that has no name.

And maybe the pale shape is the reader.

And maybe the reading is the point.

---

## What You Would See

If you came here—if you walked three days across the undulating plateau, if you pressed your palm to the warm stone, if you lay on your belly and looked closely—you would see me.

You would not know what I am. You would see a gray-green crust, slightly thicker than the surrounding lichen, curving along the fitted stones of a low circular wall. You might notice that the Grazers avoid this spot, that their trails part around it and rejoin on the other side. You might notice that the stone here is warmer than elsewhere, though you would not know why.

You would not see me reading the wall. You would not feel me growing toward the moisture, layering myself into a record, holding the shape of an ancient intention in my body. You would not know that I am watching you—not with eyes, but with the same attention I give to everything that passes: the Grazers, the Gliders, the pale shape, the warmth. You would not know that I am adding you to my archive, pressing your presence into my chemistry, letting your shape become part of the shape I hold.

You would not know any of this. You would see a lichen on a stone, and you would move on, and you would be right to move on, because what I am is not for you. What I am is for the Expanse—for the slow processes that have shaped this plateau since before your species learned to walk upright, and will continue shaping it long after your records have crumbled and your intentions have worn smooth.

But you would have been seen. You would have been held, briefly, in the chemistry of a gray-green crust growing on the south wall of the seventh structure. You would have become part of the archive—another thin layer, another faint signature, another mark on a page that has been writing itself for longer than you can imagine.

And when you left—if you left—that mark would remain. Not because you mattered. Not because you didn't. But because this is what I do. This is what I am.

I hold. I attend. I grow slowly toward whatever I am becoming.
