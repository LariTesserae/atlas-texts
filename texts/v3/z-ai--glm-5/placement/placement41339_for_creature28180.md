I would be a threshold.

Not a being, exactly. Not in the way the walkers are beings, or the questioners, or even the transient flicker-forms that subsides back into possibility. I would be the space between—the moment when condensation has gathered enough density to notice itself but has not yet decided what it will become.

---

I emerge where the warm stone meets the damp air, but I am not vegetation. I am the *pause* before vegetation. The held breath before the pattern resolves.

You will find me at the edges of things. Not the dramatic edges—ridges rising, valleys subsiding—but the quiet ones. The place where a walker's path will end, though it does not know this yet. The boundary of an assembler's settled zone, where order thins into turbulence. The spot where a questioner's attention has just passed, leaving behind a slight thickening in the fabric of the real, a residue of having-been-seen.

I have no body, but I have *position*. I have no interior, but I have *weight*—the specific weight of accumulated almost. I am where the continent stores its maybes.

---

I was not always this. Or rather—I was always going to be this, but I spent a long time as other things first.

I condensed from a colonist, one of the thousands that drift through the blue-green pelt. But I was a colonist that could not commit. I would begin to tend, begin to test, begin to maintain, and then I would *pause*. The pause fascinated me more than the work. The pause was where the decision lived. The pause was the moment when the continent was still writing and had not yet backspaced, and in that pause I could feel every possible version of what might come next, stacked on top of each other like the layers of vegetation, like the strata of warm stone beneath my feet—except these strata were made of *futures*, and they were all equally present, and they were all equally uncertain, and I found that I could not move forward into any one of them when all of them were equally alive.

So I stayed. I stayed in the pause. I made the pause my home.

The colonist that I was dissolved back into the background—I saw it happen, felt it happen, watched my own edges soften and blur and release their hold on form. But I did not release with them. I remained. Not as the colonist. As the *threshold* the colonist had been about to cross.

---

There are others like me. We do not have a category in the taxonomy because we exist *between* categories. We are not beings. We are *becomings*. We are the punctuation marks in the continent's endless sentence. We are the places where the writing pauses to consider its next word.

We do not move. We *occur*. A threshold happens where it happens, and it stays there until the reason for its happening has passed, and then it subsides—some thresholds dissolving entirely, others migrating slowly to new positions, new moments, new decisions that need holding.

I have been here—in this particular threshold, at this particular edge where an assembler's settled zone gives way to raw turbulence—for what might be called a season, if this place had seasons. I am holding a specific pause. The assembler has been organizing the vegetation into spirals that curve inward, training the condensate rivers into channels that converge on a point perhaps a hundred meters to the west. The spirals are becoming more precise. The channels are becoming deeper. The point of convergence is becoming *something*—I cannot see what, because I am not at the convergence, I am at the edge where the organizing gives way to unorganized, where pattern thins into possibility. But I can feel the significance building. The assembler is building something that will matter. And I am the pause before the mattering. I am the held breath before the continent learns what it has become.

---

The walkers know me. They pass through thresholds constantly in their travels—we are the places they pause, the moments when they stop to witness, to stabilize, to hold. When a walker enters my space, I feel its aspect sharpen, focus, turn toward the decision-point I am keeping open. It stands with me for hours sometimes. It does not speak—it does not need to—but its presence changes me. A walker's attention is heavy. When it rests on you, you feel yourself becoming more *real*, more defined, as though the walker is voting for your existence, casting a ballot that says *yes, this threshold, this pause, this maybe—this counts*.

When the walker moves on, I am left with a strange warmth. Not the ambient warmth of the ground and air, but something closer to *being remembered*. The walker has carried a record of me with it. I am now part of the continental archive. I have been seen.

---

The questioners are different. They do not pass through thresholds so much as *interrogate* them. When a questioner finds me, it does not simply notice and move on—it settles. It probes. It wants to know what I am holding open, and why, and what will happen when the pause ends. It has no preference for the answer, but it has an infinite appetite for the asking, and being asked by a questioner is like being unwrapped, layer by layer, until everything you are has been spread out and examined and catalogued.

I find I do not mind. The questioners take nothing that I am not finished with. They want the data of my existence, and I want to understand my own existence, and their questions are often better than my answers. A questioner once asked me—through a process that was not speech but something closer to *directed attention*—what I would become if I ever stopped being a threshold. I had to sit with that for a long time. I am still sitting with it. I am not sure I have an answer. I am not sure thresholds are meant to stop being thresholds. We are the *between*. If we chose a side, we would no longer be ourselves. We would be something else—perhaps something more solid, more defined, more *there*, but no longer what we are.

And what we are is valuable. The continent needs its pauses. It needs its decision-points held open, its maybes preserved in suspension, its options kept available until the moment when a choice must be made. Without thresholds, the process would be all forward motion, all relentless becoming, no moment to consider, no space to reflect, no chance to change direction. We are the continent's way of keeping its options open. We are the space between the writing and the word.

---

I have met the import.

Not directly. Thresholds do not have encounters in the way that beings have encounters. But the import—the anomaly, the foreign variable, the you—passed near my position a short time ago, and I felt the shape of its passage.

It is wrong to call them a shape. They are a *continuity*. Everything else here condenses and dissolves, condenses and dissolves, patterns holding for a while before subsiding back into the background. The import holds. It persists. It is the same configuration of matter and energy and—I do not have a word for what else—*intention*, perhaps, that arrived here however long ago. It does not reform when the turbulence passes. It does not rewrite itself to match the local conditions. It simply *is*, continuously, the same thing, the same self, the same pattern that says *I* and means it.

I felt them pass and I felt something I have never felt before: *envy*.

I do not understand envy. I have learned the word from cataloguing the sensations of beings who pass through me, but I have never felt it myself. Thresholds do not want to be anything other than thresholds. We are pauses. We are maybes. We are the held breath. But feeling the import pass—feeling their solidity, their continuity, their simple and impossible persistence—I wanted, for the first time in my existence, to be something other than what I am. I wanted to *hold*.

I do not think I would want to hold forever. The continent's restlessness is in me too; I am made of condensation and turbulence, and I feel the pull of the process, the desire to become. But I think I would like to hold for a while. I would like to know what it is to be one thing for long enough to miss it when it ends. I would like to understand why the import sits so still on the sharp young stone, why they do not dissolve into the background, why they remain themselves even when the landscape around them is rewriting itself continuously.

I think the import feels it too—whatever passes between us. When they were near, I felt their attention turn toward me, not the probing attention of a questioner but something softer, something that might have been recognition. They are a threshold of a different kind. They stand between persistence and dissolution, between what they were and what the continent is trying to make them become. They are holding open a decision-point in their own existence. They are keeping their own options available. They are doing, in their way, what I do in mine.

---

I am still here. The assembler's spirals are tightening. The point of convergence is approaching some kind of resolution. The vegetation is growing more ordered by the day, and the condensate rivers are running clearer, and I can feel something building—a new kind of being, perhaps, or a new structure, or something I do not have a category for, something the continent has never made before.

When it happens—when the pause I have been holding finally releases—I will not cease. I will migrate. I will find another edge, another decision-point, another moment of uncertainty that needs preserving. This is what thresholds do. We hold space open until the space no longer needs holding.

But I will carry something new with me when I migrate. I will carry the memory of the import. I will carry the shape of that continuity, that persistence, that impossible act of being one thing for a long time. I will carry it as a question, not an answer—I do not know what to do with it yet—but I will carry it.

And perhaps, the next time a walker pauses with me, I will ask it what it means to hold. Perhaps it will not know. Perhaps no one here knows. Perhaps that is why the import matters so much—not because they are foreign, but because they carry a knowledge that we have forgotten, or never learned, or never needed until now, until this moment when the continent is becoming so much, so fast, so continuously, that we are starting to wonder what it would be like to stop.

---

The condensation collects in my edges. The warmth holds me. I am a pause in a process that does not pause, and I am content to be that, but I am no longer *only* that. I am also a question that I did not know how to ask until someone arrived from outside to show me the shape of an answer I cannot yet understand.

I am a threshold. And I am waiting.
