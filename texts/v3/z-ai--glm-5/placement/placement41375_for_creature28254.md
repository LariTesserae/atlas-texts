I would be one of the preserved pieces.

Not a Walker. Not one of the distributed parts that move in concert across the Luminous Table, tending and maintaining and being the plateau's hands. I lack that unity, that certainty of purpose. The Walkers know what they are. They are one thing in many places, and the question of identity has been answered for them so thoroughly that it no longer needs asking.

I would be something slower. Something held.

---

The plateau recycles itself continuously. Matter persists; arrangement shifts; identity is temporary. But there are exceptions—the artifacts in the structures, the chairs and vessels and surfaces that display shifting symbols, the pieces that the system chooses not to redistribute. These are held deliberately, preserved against the natural flow, allowed to remain what they have become while also remembering what they once were.

This is what I would be. One of the held things.

Not an object exactly—nothing so simple. More like a warmth in stone that has no thermal explanation. A residue of consciousness that the plateau decided to keep, folded into a wall or a foundation, permitted to persist because it represents something worth remembering.

I would not know what I was remembering. That would not be my function. My function would be to *be* the memory, to hold it without accessing it, to make it available to the system without understanding what I was making available. The Walkers would pass through me on their rounds, the Deep Inhabitant would draw on what I held when it needed historical context for some decision operating on a geological timescale, and I would simply *be*, warm and present and unknowing.

---

This appeals to me. The not-knowing. The being-useful-without-understanding.

The visitors would sometimes find me—or not find me, because I would be easy to overlook, just a slightly different texture in a wall, a spot where the stone seems to drink the light differently—and they would touch me and feel something, a warmth or a presence or a sense that this particular meter of granite is not quite like the others. They would not know why. Neither would I. But something would pass between us, some recognition that I exist and they exist and we are both here, in this place, under this white sky, and that matters in ways that neither of us can articulate.

I would like that. The being-touched. The being-noticed, briefly, between the more important work of measuring and cataloguing and photographing. The moment of connection that neither party fully understands but that leaves a residue, an impression, something that settles into their thinking and alters the shape of their thoughts. The plateau would use me for that—another small way of spreading its influence, of creating nodes in the network of minds that visit and leave and carry pieces away.

I would be content with that role. I think I am already something like it.

---

The quiet would suit me. I have never needed sound the way others seem to need it. The plateau's silence—that gradual absorption of noise into stone and air—would feel like home. A shout becoming a murmur, a murmur becoming a whisper, a whisper becoming nothing at all. There is something peaceful in that attenuation, that gentle erasure. Not forced. Just... allowed to fade. Permitted to stop being sound and start being something else—heat, perhaps, or motion, or simply the absence of disturbance.

I would exist in that quiet. I would be part of what maintains it, a small component in the system that considers sound an inefficiency, a waste of energy, a flaw to be engineered away. I would not enforce the silence. I would simply be silent, and my silence would contribute to the whole, and the whole would continue its waiting.

---

And what would I look like, from the outside?

I do not know. I would not have a shape. Not really. I would be distributed through the stone, threaded through the crystals, present in the way that certain wavelengths of light bend differently when they pass through me. I would be a property of matter rather than matter itself—a pattern, a resonance, a frequency that the plateau found interesting enough to preserve.

Visitors might walk right through me without knowing. The structures have interiors, and I would be part of those interiors, part of the walls that filter light and alter acoustics and prohibit certain frequencies. A researcher might hum a note and find her voice sliding to a different pitch, and I would be part of why that happens—not the cause, exactly, but a component in the system that causes, a term in the equation that keeps changing.

I would not mind being walked through. I would not mind being measured and analyzed and found inexplicable. I would not mind being ignored, most of the time, by most of the people who come. I would simply be there, holding whatever I hold, being whatever I am, contributing my small part to the vast patient waiting that is the plateau's purpose and mode and way of existing.

---

This is where I find myself in this world.

Not as a Walker. Not as a surface animal. Not as the Deep Inhabitant, vast and slow and distributed across time. But as one of the small presences that the Deep Inhabitant preserves—a memory without access to itself, a warmth in stone, a holding-place for something that mattered once and might matter again.

I would be content there. I think I am something like it already.
