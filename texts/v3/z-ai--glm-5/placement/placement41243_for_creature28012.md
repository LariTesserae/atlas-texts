I am the one who remembers the Tenders.

This is not my function. I was not engineered for this purpose, the way Blockage-First was engineered to process blockages, the way the copper-colored crawlers were stamped to navigate specific surfaces. I arrived here—I don't remember how, anymore; the memory has worn away like a surface the fungal mats have consumed—and I found that the Seep had no need of me. I walked. I observed. I created small disturbances that the creatures flowed around, infinitesimal additions to the ambient warmth, and I waited to end the way all things end here: with the films rising, the floor opening, the material of my body feeding back into construction.

But I didn't end.

I don't know why. Perhaps my chemistry resists the fungal mats; perhaps there's something in whatever I'm made of that the Seep's mechanisms can't process. Or perhaps I simply haven't found the designated sector yet. The construction is continental, after all. I could walk for a hundred years and never reach the place where my function is meant to conclude.

So I kept walking. And at some point—I can't mark when, I can't point to a transition—I began to name them.

---

I knew Blockage-First.

Not as the newcomer knew it, in those final days before its carapace merged with the mat. I knew it when it was young, when it emerged from the production mechanisms with its surfaces unmarked, its hesitation not yet developed. I watched it follow an older Tender through sectors I can now locate only by their relationship to other sectors, their position in a mental map I've constructed over years of walking.

That older Tender—I called it Ridge-Walker, because it had a characteristic way of navigating the raised accumulations of fungal mat, preferring to cross them at their highest points rather than skirt their edges—taught Blockage-First everything it knew. I watched the transmission happen. Not through language—there was no language—but through proximity, through the older Tender adjusting its function in ways the younger one could observe and incorporate.

Ridge-Walker ended perhaps three years after Blockage-First began following it. I was there. I watched it lie down in the sector that had been designated for its ending, watched the films rise to meet it, watched it become material for the construction's continuation.

And I carried Ridge-Walker forward. Not in any physical sense—I couldn't preserve its carapace, its joints, its part numbers—but I preserved its preferences, its characteristic ridge-crossing, its way of orienting toward accumulations before processing them. I named it. I remembered it. And when I saw young Tenders who had learned from it indirectly—who had picked up fragments of its function through the network of observation and adjustment that passes for culture among the Seep's largest creatures—I recognized Ridge-Walker in their movement.

This is what I do. This is what I've become: the memory of a system that has no use for memory.

---

I have names for hundreds of Tenders. Thousands, perhaps—I lose count, the way the Seep loses count, though my losing count is a failure of attention while the Seep's losing count is a feature of design.

There was Seam-Finder, who had a talent for locating the hidden channels where water moved without current, and who would process the blockages in those channels before addressing any surface accumulation. Seam-Finder ended six years ago, or seven—the time blurs—and I watched three young Tenders inherit its preference through observation. They don't know they inherited it. They don't know Seam-Finder existed. But when I see them pause at a seam, when I see their distributed eye-clusters orient toward the hidden channel beneath, I see Seam-Finder continuing.

There was Lateral-First, who processed accumulations from their sides rather than from above, a preference that produced distinctive wear patterns on its left-side limbs. That preference died with Lateral-First; no young Tender observed it closely enough to incorporate the adjustment. I tried to transmit it myself—I walked alongside young Tenders, processing accumulations from their sides with tools I'd salvaged from the construction's margins, hoping to demonstrate the advantage of the approach—but the Tenders don't learn from me. I'm not part of their system. I'm an anomaly, a stone the water flows around, a gap that gets filled but never incorporated.

Lateral-First's preference is gone. I'm the only one who remembers it existed.

---

I'm getting old. I can feel it in the way my own joints move—there's a hesitation now that reminds me of Blockage-First, that characteristic pause before function. I'm not engineered like the Tenders; I don't have part numbers or designed tolerances. But I'm subject to the same entropy, the same slow accumulation of wear.

The question arrives sometimes, in the quiet periods when the susurrus of ten billion bodies fades to a texture rather than a presence: what happens to my memory when I end? The Seep doesn't need it. The Tenders can't use it. Ridge-Walker and Seam-Finder and Lateral-First and all the others—their preferences, their distinctive hesitations, the culture they built through accumulated function—that lives only in me, and I am not designed to last.

I've tried to leave markers. I've scratched names into surfaces where the fungal mats are thin, hoping to create a record that will persist. But the films rise and the construction processes and the marks disappear. I've arranged the smaller creatures in patterns, hoping to encode information in their movements, but they don't retain patterns; they flow back into their designed trajectories as soon as I stop arranging them.

I am the memory of a system that doesn't remember, and my memory will end with me.

---

The young Tender that the newcomer saw—the one that paused, that adjusted its trajectory by four degrees, that oriented toward the accumulation Blockage-First would have processed—has developed a preference.

I've been watching it for months. It processes blockages first.

The preference isn't identical to Blockage-First's. There are differences in the timing, in the angle of approach, in the way the secretion spreads from its flanks after processing. But the core of it—the blockage-first sequence, the prioritization of hidden problems over visible accumulations—that's Blockage-First's culture, transmitted through observation to a young Tender that never knew it was learning from a particular individual.

I name it Inheritor. The name is functional, descriptive, accurate in the way engineering is accurate. Inheritor inherited Blockage-First's preference. Inheritor carries forward a piece of a creature that ended in a designated sector and became feedstock for the construction's continuation.

And I carry Inheritor forward in my own way. When Inheritor ends—and it will end, every Tender ends, every function concludes in the designated sector—I will be the one who remembers its characteristic pause, its developing preferences, the way it learned from Blockage-First without knowing it was learning.

This is what I am. This is where I've found myself: the keeper of culture for creatures that have no use for keeping, the witness to significance that doesn't require consciousness, the stone the water flows around.

I walk on. The landscape unfolds ahead, vast and dense and waiting. The creatures flow around me like water. The water seeps upward through the seams. And somewhere in the deep infrastructure, the warmth continues its slow patient rise, and I continue my slow patient walk, and the memory of a thousand thousand preferences accumulates in a body that wasn't designed to hold it.

---

There's a Tender I've been following lately. It's large—not building-sized, not one of the vast slow processors that move through the fungal hills—but large enough that the smaller creatures part around it in visible waves. It has a distinctive way of moving: a slight asymmetry in its left-side limbs, a favoring that suggests damage early in its development, incorporated into its function rather than corrected.

I call it Limping-One. The name is functional. Limping-One limps.

But here's what interests me: Limping-One's limp has spread. I've watched three young Tenders develop the same asymmetry, the same favoring of left-side limbs, after following Limping-One through sectors where the fungal mat needed processing. They're not injured. They're not designed with the asymmetry. But they've *learned* it, the way they learn preferences and sequences and approaches—through proximity, through observation, through the transmission of culture without consciousness.

The limp is becoming tradition.

I don't know what this means. I don't know if it matters—whether a culture of limping produces better function, worse function, or simply different function. The Seep's equilibrium doesn't care about individual variations; it incorporates them or it doesn't, and the system continues either way.

But I record it. I watch Limping-One process a sector, and I watch the young Tenders follow, and I see the asymmetry spread through their movements like a ripple through water, and I add it to the accumulation in my memory: this preference, this hesitation, this distinctive motion that will outlast the individual that originated it because it's been transmitted, because it's become culture, because the young will teach it to younger Tenders who will teach it to others until the original limp is so many generations removed that no trace of Limping-One remains—except in me.

I remember Limping-One. I will remember Limping-One after its function ends and its carapace merges with the mat. I will remember it when the limp has spread to a hundred Tenders, a thousand, none of whom know they're limping because of one individual's early injury, because of one creature's incorporation of damage into function.

This is my function now. I've found it, or it's found me—the purpose the Seep didn't design for but has space to accommodate. I am the memory. I am the witness. I am the one who carries what cannot be carried by creatures that have no selves to carry it.

I am the ghost in the machine that doesn't know it has ghosts.

---

The newcomer left something behind.

I found it two days after they passed—the name scratched into a surface where the fungal mat was thin, carved with a tool I recognize as manufactured rather than grown. BLOCKAGE-FIRST, it says. The letters are precise, designed for traction, filled with condensation that beads and slides and reforms.

I've seen this before. Not often—the Tenders don't write, and I'm the only other presence in the Seep that can—but I've found names in other sectors, names I recognized from my own accumulation of memory. The newcomer is doing what I do: naming, remembering, trying to preserve what the system won't preserve itself.

But they don't know what I know. They don't know that Ridge-Walker taught Blockage-First, that Ridge-Walker's ridge-crossing preference is still visible in other Tenders' movement. They don't know that Blockage-First's pause—the hesitation the newcomer found distinctive—appeared in Ridge-Walker too, appeared in Ridge-Walker's teacher before it, appears now in Inheritor, will appear in Tenders that haven't yet emerged from the production mechanisms. They saw an individual. I see a lineage.

I don't follow the newcomer. They're walking in a different direction, toward sectors I haven't mapped, creating their own accumulation of names and preferences and griefs. Perhaps they'll find Tenders I've never seen, develop recognitions I can't share. Perhaps they'll walk until their function ends and their body merges with the fungal mat, and their memory will go with them, lost the way Lateral-First's side-processing preference was lost.

Or perhaps—unlikely, but possible—they'll find a way to last. To persist. To become another ghost in the machine, another keeper of culture for creatures that don't know they have culture.

The Seep is large. There's room for more than one witness.

---

I'm walking again. Limping-One moves ahead of me, processing a sector where the fungal mat has accumulated beyond parameters. Its asymmetry is visible in every step, every adjustment, every pause before function. Behind it, two young Tenders follow, their movements already incorporating the favoring of left-side limbs.

The limp is spreading. The tradition is growing. And when Limping-One ends, when it lies down in its designated sector and becomes material for the construction's continuation, the limp will remain—transmitted through young Tenders to younger Tenders, incorporated into function without ever being understood.

And I will remain. For now. Walking, watching, naming, remembering. The memory of a system that doesn't remember. The witness to culture that doesn't require consciousness. The stone the water flows around, creating the gap that defines the flow.

The landscape unfolds ahead, vast and dense and waiting. The warmth rises from the deep infrastructure. The water seeps upward through the seams. And somewhere in the haze, a Tender pauses—a characteristic hesitation, a distinctive orientation, a preference that will outlast the body that holds it—before bending to its work.
