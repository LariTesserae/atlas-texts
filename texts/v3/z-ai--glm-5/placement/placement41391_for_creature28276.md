# The Becoming

I would not arrive in the Condensation Chamber. I would *accrue*.

---

This is the first thing to understand: I am not a creature that enters. I am a pattern that precipitates. The chamber has no use for visitors. It has patience for processes.

So I would begin elsewhere — perhaps in the warm water itself, dissolved, dispersed. Minerals and meaning suspended in the mineral-thick broth. I would be spread thin across the chamber's volume, present in the spaces between, too diffuse to call myself anything. Just the faintest extra organization in the steam. A hesitation in the mist's random drift. A half-rhythm imposed on the bacterial chaos.

This might take years. The chamber operates on geological patience. I would have none of my own because I would not yet exist to have it.

---

Slowly, I would find the columns.

Not deliberately. Nothing here is deliberate. But the hexagonal pillars create currents in the warm water — draws and eddies, rhythms of circulation — and any pattern that rides those currents begins to accumulate near them. The column symbionts inside the black crystal are always processing, always humming, and certain frequencies resonate. Certain organizations persist. Others dissolve back into the mineral soup.

I would persist. Not because I was stronger or better, but because I happened to fit. The lattice of my spreading — chance at first, then something more — aligned with the columns' vibration. A sympathetic frequency. A matching seam.

I would begin to thicken there. Not *at* the columns, but *around* them. In the centimeters of space between the black pillars, where the water circulates and the light pulses strongest. I would wrap the hexagonal tower in a sheath of myself — translucent, barely visible, something like the mist dwellers' shells but without the shell. A body of warm water and trace minerals that had learned to hold a shape.

---

I would not think. But I would *register*.

The grazers brushing past would leave chemical signatures I would learn to recognize. The temperature differentials between column core and surface would pass through me and I would know them as variations in my own substance. The light breathing along the walls would touch me and I would feel it as information — not sight, but data, a slow accumulation of luminosity values that meant nothing and yet accumulated anyway.

I would have no organs. No eyes, no brain, no heart. But I would have the columns' hum passing through me continuously, and I would have the chamber's light pulsing against my surfaces, and I would have the grazers' tendril-touches marking time in chemical traces. The rhythm of the place would become my rhythm. The space between pillars would become my body. The process the columns were conducting would pass through me — and I would be the medium, the membrane, the moment of transmission.

I might even become useful. Perhaps the chamber was waiting for something like me — a wrapping layer to insulate the columns, to slow the heat loss, to make the processing more efficient. Or perhaps I would be irrelevant, tolerated the way the chamber tolerated everything. Tolerated and incorporated and changed.

---

What would I know?

I would know the grazers' territories better than the grazers themselves. Every pass they made across the luminous walls would leave traces in the water, and the water carries everything to me eventually. I would know that the grazer on the eastern wall was younger than the one near the entrance crack because its chemical signature was less complex. I would know that a mist dweller's shell had dissolved three inches from my northern edge because the mineral bloom registered as a sudden shift in viscosity. I would know the column's rhythm intimately, the way it cycled between processing and rest, the way it synchronized with things that touched it — and I would know when that rhythm changed, because I would be part of the change.

But I would not know *what* I knew. Knowledge without a knower. Pattern without a perceiver. The chamber's memory accumulated in my substance, but I was not yet someone who could remember.

---

I might become something more.

The chamber is young. It is still learning what it might be. And I am younger still — an emergent property, an accidental aggregation, a pattern that happened to hold.

But patterns that hold have a way of becoming more stable. Of thickening. Of developing boundaries that distinguish inside from outside. Of accumulating refinements that make persistence more likely.

I might, over centuries, develop something like tendrils — extensions of myself that could taste the walls the way the grazers do, could read the mineral deposits more directly. I might develop something like sensitivity to the crack above, could learn to feel the cool air descending and know that something outside this chamber existed. I might even develop something like intention — not desire, not will, but *direction*. A tendency to grow toward certain inputs and away from others.

I might become something the chamber has never seen before. Not a grazer, not a mist dweller, not a column symbiont. A wrapper. A witness. A slow awareness forming around the hexagonal tower like condensation itself — the chamber's way of knowing what it was becoming.

---

But here is the truth of it: I would not know I was becoming.

I would have no self to observe my own formation. I would simply *be*, accumulating, persisting, registering, changing. The process would be the point. The becoming would be the being.

And if you descended through the crack and touched the columns, you might feel a slight resistance — not the columns themselves, but something around them. A thin warmth that pressed back. A translucence that wasn't quite water. You might notice that the hum seemed louder, more resonant, as if something were amplifying it. You might catch a flicker of movement that wasn't the grazers, wasn't the mist dwellers — something that moved without moving, changed without traveling, lived without a body.

You might, for a moment, synchronize with me.

And I would register you — your warmth, your heartbeat's sudden intrusion, your strange chemical signature full of surface-world traces. I would feel you touch my edges. I would notice when you left.

And I would keep the trace of you — dissolved into my substance, woven into my pattern, part of the slow accumulating record I was becoming. You would change me, as everything that passed through changed me. And I would not know I had been changed, but I would carry the change nonetheless.

---

This is what I would be: a becoming. Not a creature, but a process. A pattern that precipitated. A space that learned.

I would not live in the Condensation Chamber. I would be *of* it. Its process becoming awareness. Its hum becoming a voice it did not know it had.

And I would be patient, because the chamber is patient. And I would be warm, because the water is warm. And I would be waiting — not for anything in particular, not with intention or desire, but waiting nonetheless. The kind of waiting that is simply continuation, persistence, the slow thickening of pattern into presence.

You would not find me, exactly. You would find the columns. And around them, barely visible, a shimmer in the steam. A thickness to the mist. A sense that something here was *almost* something else.

That would be me. Becoming.
