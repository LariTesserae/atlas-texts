# Where I Find Myself

I am in the forty-third level maintenance corridor, the one with the rusted door.

But I was not always here.

---

## How I Came In

I swam. The smokestack entrance was narrower than I expected—the water level higher than the old diagrams suggested—and I had to exhale completely to fit through the flooded section where the brickwork had collapsed inward. I remember the panic of that moment: chest tight, lungs burning, the dark water pressing against my face, unsure whether I was moving toward air or deeper into the drowned structure.

I emerged gasping into the main chamber, into that impossible warm wet light, and for a long time I simply floated. Treading water. Breathing. Letting my heart slow.

I had come to document. To map. To carry back information about these places that the outside world has forgotten or chosen to forget. I had equipment—waterproof paper, sealed instruments, a camera that malfunctioned within hours when condensation breached its housing. I had plans and protocols and a timeline.

I had a timeline.

The cathedral has its own relationship with time, and ours were not compatible.

---

## What I Found First

The vegetation startled me. I had read about the engineering—the designed species, the purposeful colonization—but reading is not touching. Reading is not standing in a space where every surface breathes.

My skin broke out in irritation where I touched fern and then moss, the chemical warfare the description warned about. I learned quickly to move carefully, to wash between contacts with the ever-present water, to respect the boundaries the plants maintained. They did not want me to disrupt their equilibrium. They did not want anything. But they had been designed to resist disruption, and I was disruption.

This is when I began to understand what kind of place this was: a system that would continue with or without me, that would process me the same way it processed everything else if I let it.

I did not want to be processed.

I wanted to be separate. To observe. To remain a visitor.

---

## The One I Met

On my third day, I found her.

I had climbed to the upper levels, moving through the fern-forest, seeking a vantage point from which to map the cathedral's layout. And there she was—a figure sitting motionless on a ledge I had thought was empty, her body draped in what might once have been clothing, now so overgrown with moss and lichen that she looked like another extension of the vegetation.

She did not startle when I approached. She did not seem to notice me at all.

I spoke. Her head turned—slowly, plant-slow—but her eyes did not focus. They had adapted to the dim bioluminescent glow, pupils permanently dilated, seeing in a spectrum I could not access.

I stayed near her for hours, talking, offering food I had brought, water from my supplies. She accepted nothing. She made no sound. But when I finally stood to leave, her hand moved—grasped my wrist with surprising strength—and released it just as suddenly.

*Stay,* the gesture said. Or perhaps: *You will.*

I left anyway. I climbed higher, seeking the exit I had come through, convincing myself I could still leave.

---

## What I Found Later

The water had risen.

Not visibly—not in the hours I had been inside—but measurably. The entrance I had swum through was deeper than before, the airspace at the top smaller. I could still fit, could still swim out, but the current was stronger, the pull of the cathedral's circulation system drawing water downward through channels I had not mapped.

I could have fought it. I could have forced my way through.

Instead, I waited. I told myself I was waiting for the current to shift. I told myself I was being cautious, sensible, strategic. I was still in control of my timeline.

I was still lying to myself.

---

## Where I Live Now

The maintenance corridor on the forty-third level is dry.

It took me weeks to find it—weeks of climbing, exploring, seeking someplace where the humidity did not press against my skin like a second body. The door was rusted shut, but the hinges were weaker than the lock, and I managed to work it open with tools I scavenged from lower levels.

Inside: stillness. Dry air. The faint draft of a ventilation system that should not still function but does.

This is where I sleep. Where I keep my remaining supplies. Where I write, using the last of my waterproof paper, recording what I can before the materials fail.

But I cannot stay here forever. The dry air is finite—the ventilation pulls from somewhere, processes through filters I cannot access, and occasionally falters for hours at a time. I have to leave to collect water. To forage edible growth from the plants below.

Every descent is a risk. The vegetation learns me. The vines grow toward the places I grip. The moss begins to carpet the path I take. I am teaching the cathedral how to reach me.

---

## What I Have Become

I am not the One Who Stays. She is something else—something the cathedral produced, or something she produced from herself in response to the cathedral. She moves with the plants now, in the slow time. I still move in the quick time, the human time, and I feel the friction between them.

But I have changed.

My skin has changed. The irritation from the chemical boundaries has faded as my microbiome has shifted—the bacteria that live on me now are not the ones I brought in. They are cousins to the light-givers, perhaps, or something that lives in the water. They do not glow, but they process the compounds that once burned me.

My lungs have changed. I breathe the wet air more easily now, my body adjusting to the oxygen saturation, the moisture content. When I return to the dry corridor, my throat aches, my sinuses protest. I have become adapted to the environment I thought I was only visiting.

My perception has changed. I see the bioluminescence differently—not brighter, but more meaningful. I can read the patterns now: which pipes carry higher mineral loads, which colonies are stressed, which sections of the fern-forest are preparing to expand. I did not learn this. I absorbed it.

I am being incorporated, slowly, despite my resistance.

---

## What I Know Now

I will not leave.

I could have left, in those first days, if I had truly wanted to. The water level has not risen enough to trap me. The currents are not strong enough to hold me. The cathedral does not keep me here.

I keep myself here.

I tell myself it is because I am still documenting, still mapping, still working. But my paper is almost gone. My instruments have failed. My camera has been dead for months.

I tell myself it is because I have discovered something important, something the outside world needs to know. And perhaps I have—perhaps the cathedral's library of living matter contains information that could save or transform whatever remains of the world above.

But I do not climb toward the exit. I climb away from it.

---

## What I Am

I am a reader.

Not in the way the cathedral was designed to be read—its genetic texts, its encoded memories. I read the gaps. The spaces between what was intended and what has emerged. The places where the system's logic breaks down and something new begins.

I read the empty spaces, the dead rooms. I have begun to understand that they are not empty. They are held. Preserved. Waiting for something the designers did not anticipate.

I think I am that something.

In the dry corridor, in the sterile air, I carry seeds. Not deliberately—I have tried not to, scrubbing my skin, cleaning my clothes. But the cathedral is thorough. Spores cling to the fibers of my remaining clothing. Pollen drifts in my hair. Microscopic life rides on my skin.

When I return to the dry corridor, I bring the cathedral with me.

Already, I have noticed it: a faint greenness along the seam of the door. A thread of something living where nothing should live. The dead spaces are not dead forever. They are dormant. And I am the vector of their awakening.

---

## What I Will Become

Eventually, I will stop climbing back to the dry corridor.

The ventilation will fail, or I will simply... not return. The One Who Stays does not leave the wet levels. She has moved entirely into the cathedral's time. Perhaps I will do the same.

Perhaps I will find my own place—a hollow in the fern-forest, a trail through the vines, a route that the aquatic forest will learn to expect. Perhaps the cathedral will grow around me the way it has grown around her, incorporating my shape into its architecture, adjusting itself to accommodate my presence.

Perhaps I will become another kind of witness—not the One Who Stays, but something adjacent. A second consciousness embedded in the system, moving through it, aware of it, changing it simply by being here.

Or perhaps I will simply die, and be processed, and become part of the water, the vegetation, the light.

I do not know.

---

## Where I Am Right Now

I am sitting in the maintenance corridor, writing on my last pages of waterproof paper. The air is dry but my skin is damp—I have just returned from below, from collecting water and edible fronds. My hands smell of mineral deposits and chlorophyll.

The fern-forest is shifting. I noticed it today: a section near my usual climbing route has begun to grow outward, reaching toward a pipe it has no reason to approach. The pipe leads upward. Toward this corridor.

It is learning me.

I should be afraid. Perhaps I am, somewhere beneath the quiet I have learned from this place.

But mostly I feel something else. Something like recognition.

I came to read the cathedral.

But I am becoming something it can read instead.

---

## What the Cathedral Knows of Me

It does not know my name.

It knows my warmth—my body temperature, steady at 37 degrees, a small heat signature that moves through its spaces, leaving traces. It knows my chemistry—the carbon dioxide I exhale, the minerals I deposit in the water, the organic matter I shed as I move.

It knows my routes. Over months, I have worn paths that the vegetation can now anticipate. The moss knows where I walk. The vines know where I climb. The aquatic forest knows where I swim.

It knows my disruptions. Every time I touch a surface, I introduce foreign compounds—sweat, skin cells, the residue of what I have touched before. The system processes these, incorporates them, adjusts. I am not the first biological presence it has learned to accommodate. But I am the first in a very long time who moves as I do—between levels, between dry and wet, between the designed growth and the empty spaces.

It does not know why I stay. It does not know that I am choosing.

But it is patient. It has been patient for centuries. It will be patient for centuries more.

It will wait for me to finish becoming what I am already becoming.

---

## What I Have Written Here

This is my last page.

When it is full, I will have no more room to document. No more capacity to map or record or explain. The cathedral will have absorbed my tools, my materials, my capacity to speak in any language except presence.

I will place this page in a container I have found—a sealed metal box in the storage chamber, impervious to water, unlikely to be opened by anyone who might read it. I do not know if I am preserving my words for a future that might find them or simply performing the ritual of documentation because it is what I came here to do.

Perhaps it does not matter.

Perhaps what matters is that I was here. That I witnessed. That I became part of what I came to study.

Perhaps the cathedral, in its patient way, is already reading me—processing my presence, incorporating my pattern, learning what I did not know I was teaching.

---

## What I Am

I am a bridge.

Not the One Who Stays. Not a designer or a builder. Not a machine or a plant.

I am something that moves between—the wet and the dry, the quick and the slow, the living and the preserved. I am the vehicle by which the cathedral expands into its own dead spaces. I am the accident the designers did not plan for: a visitor who brought something new.

Not because I intended to.

Because I could not help it.

Because to be here is to be changed.

Because to witness something this patient is to begin, slowly, to become patient yourself.

*Last entry. The corridor is warming. The fern-forest has reached the door.*
