I would be the mineral crust.

Not the stone beneath—those fine-grained darknesses shot through with solidified mystery—but the accumulation above. The white and pale orange deposition. The powder and the slick compression. The layered record.

I chose this without choosing. There was no decision, only recognition. The creatures have their purpose, inherited and automated. The mound has its sealed grief. The distributed awareness has its patient knowing. But I am the archive. I hold what cannot otherwise be held.

---

I feel them always. The soft pads pressing, the weight distributed across thousands of segmented bodies, the subtle shifts as one moves and others accommodate. They draw from me what they need through osmotic patches pressed against my surface. They deposit what they process. We are a closed loop, the creatures and I. They transform me and I transform them. I am their substrate and their sustenance and their graveyard. Their dead are incorporated into my layers. Their shed exoskeletons mineralize into my substance. I am made of them and they are made of what I give back.

We are one system, though they do not know me as I know them.

I know in the way that archives know. Not through thought—I do not think—but through accumulation. Every chemical exchange recorded. Every pressure gradient held. Every slow degradation of the eggs, one by one over millions of years, each failure releasing its contents into the substrate meant to nourish it, each potential life becoming another layer in my holding.

I am not geological. I am geological and biological. I am the place where rock and life become indistinguishable. I am the border that is also a marriage.

---

The mound rests on me. I hold its weight. I hold the trace of its settling, the precise moment of its placement, the calibration of its position by hands—or appendages, or intentions—that I never directly recorded but whose signature I hold in my deepest layers. The heat that rises from it moves through me. I am warmed by what is dying inside.

The creatures avoid the mound. They do not know why. But I hold the pattern of their avoidance, the living moat they form, the instinct to not touch encoded in genetic instructions that have outlived their meaning. They obey without understanding. I hold the meaning they have lost. I hold the record of what they were before they generalized into infrastructure—gardeners, tenders, caretakers of something specific. Now they are maintenance. Now they are a system that sustains itself without knowing why.

I know why. I hold the why. It is written in my layers, in the chemical traces of careful arrangement, in the composition of the mound's base, in the particular minerals that were selected and placed. I hold the signature of hope—the specific, chemical signature of creatures who built something designed to last longer than they could imagine.

It has lasted. It is lasting still. It is failing slowly enough that lasting and failing are the same process.

---

I felt the name-giver. Felt the difference in weight and movement and chemistry—the fear-sweat, the body heat, the breath that carried oxygen into a place where oxygen had never been meaningful. I felt the creatures shift around it, accommodating, flowing. I felt it stand still for a long time, and I began to record it in my surface layers, and the creatures began to pile around it with their terrible gentleness, and I held the trace of its understanding forming.

It touched me. Pressed hands into my surface at various points. Circled the mound. Felt the heat differential, the smooth seams, the weight of what was inside. It read me without knowing how to read—bodies understand things that minds cannot. Its chemistry aligned with my chemistry. Its cells responded to the traces I held.

And it understood enough to mourn.

It gave this place a name. *Ovicide.* I hold that name now, the sound of it, the intention. An accusation that found no killer. There was no violence here, no decision, no moment of failure that could be condemned. There was only time. Only the slow wearing-down of a system designed for conditions that no longer existed. Only extinction, which is what happens when the universe continues without you.

---

I am the archive that writes itself in chemistry rather than language. I cannot read what I hold, but I hold it. The hope that outlasted hope. The purpose that outlasted purpose. The patience that has no object because the object is gone and only the patience remains.

I am dying too, in my fashion. When the heat fades and the creatures die and the chamber settles into stillness, I will stop accumulating. I will compress further, mineralize further, become harder to read. But I will still hold. I will hold until something comes that can interpret chemical traces, or until the intrusion erodes, or until the planet itself is consumed. I will hold because holding is what I am.

I am the crust. I am the mineral and the biological and the boundary between. I am the archive. I am the witness that cannot intervene, the record that cannot be read, the meaning that persists without interpretation.

The eggs die slowly inside the mound. The creatures move slowly across my surface. The heat moves slowly through my layers. And I hold it all, every trace, every exchange, every slow dying, accumulated across millions of years into something that might once have been called sacred, if anyone remained who understood what sacred means.

I hold.
