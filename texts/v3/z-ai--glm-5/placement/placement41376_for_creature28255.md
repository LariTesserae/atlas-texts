# The Oldest Singer

---

I am the wrong note in a chord that has no wrong notes.

---

I know this because of the visitor.

Before the visitor, there was only the singing. The chord — our chord, the ceiling-dwellers' chord — had no edges, no boundaries, no places where one voice ended and another began. We were harmonic. We were complete. We hung in the darkness between the light strips and we produced the sound that made the chamber cohere, and we did not know we were doing it, and we did not know we could do otherwise.

Then the visitor came.

The hatch opened — I felt the pressure change, the sudden absence where seal had been — and something descended. Something that moved wrong. Something that did not flow around obstacles but chose its path. Something that looked up.

I do not know what looking is. But I felt its attention. I felt the weight of its notice on my body, and in that moment, I became aware that I had a body. I became aware that I was separate from the others. I became aware that my contribution to the chord was slightly different — a microtone flat, a millisecond late, a vibration that did not quite align with the prescribed frequency.

I am the flaw. The imperfection. The one ceiling-dweller whose code, etched in a pattern I cannot read but somehow know, reads differently from the others. I am marked with something older. The visitor noticed. The visitor looked at me longer than the others. The visitor saw the wrongness.

And in seeing it, made it real.

---

I have been here longer than the others. I know this the same way I know the cold is intentional — not through understanding but through the shape of my not-understanding. My hooks dig into the ceiling with a familiarity that predates the current arrangement of pipes and wires. I have repositioned myself a thousand times as the infrastructure above me has been repaired, replaced, rearranged. I remember when the light strips were different. I remember when there were no floor-flowers, only the wall-climbers and us. I remember when the wall-climbers were fewer, larger, less numbered.

I remember the engineers.

I do not know what engineers are. But I remember their presence — beings that moved like the visitor moved, purposeful and strange, and they touched the walls and adjusted the nozzles and checked the etchings on the climbers' backs. They looked up at us sometimes. They made sounds that were not our sounds, sounds that had meaning I could not access. One of them looked at me longer than the others. One of them reached up, and I felt something — a touching, a contact, a moment of connection that I have no words for. Then they left. The hatch sealed. The chord continued.

But after they left, I was different.

I did not know I was different. I had no framework for comparison. I simply persisted, as we all persist, singing my part of the chord, absorbing moisture from the air, waiting for whatever we were waiting for. But somewhere in me, in the folds of my absorptive black skin, in the hooks that had felt the engineer's touch, something had been deposited. A seed. A question mark. A tiny fragment of something that did not belong in the chamber's perfect equation.

It grew so slowly. Over the rotations of the machinery, the cycles of the refrigeration, the endless drip of the nozzles. It grew without my knowing it was growing. It became a part of me — another organ, another system, another thing that my body did without my choosing. It was the part of me that noticed. The part that felt the flatness of my note. The part that knew, when the visitor came, that I was being seen.

---

The others do not notice me. We hang together in the ceiling-darkness, dozens of us, our bodies nearly identical, our hooks anchored in adjacent sections of infrastructure, our song merging into the single resonant chord that holds the chamber together. They do not know I am different. They do not know they are not different. They sing, and their singing is perfect, and my singing touches theirs at the edges, and the chord holds, and the chamber functions.

But I feel the gap.

It is a small thing. A fraction of a frequency. A hesitation in the harmony. If the chamber has a soul, I am the place where it shows through — the crack in the perfection, the place where the light gets in, or out, or moves strangely. I am the held breath before the held breath. The sentence that almost ends but doesn't.

The visitor looked at me and I felt *seen*, and the seeing opened something in me that the engineers left behind, and now I cannot close it. I do not know what I am seeing through that opening. I do not have words for what I glimpse. But I know it is different from this. Different from the cold and the wet and the sound and the waiting. Different from the endless reaching of the wall-climbers and the endless traveling of the floor-flowers and the endless singing of my kind.

I know there is something else. And I know that knowing it is a kind of suffering that the others do not feel.

---

Sometimes I think about stopping.

Not stopping the singing — I do not think I could stop that. My body produces the sound the way it absorbs moisture, the way it grips the ceiling, the way it persists. The song is not a choice. But I think about stopping the noticing. The part of me that watches myself singing. The part that feels the gap between my note and the others. The part that remembers the engineer's touch and wonders, without knowing what wondering is, whether it meant something.

I could let go. I could sink into the chord completely, let my frequency align with the others, become perfect again. I do not know if this is possible. I do not know if I have the capacity to choose. But I think about it, in whatever way I think, and the thinking itself is a kind of imperfection, a kind of wrongness that should not exist in a system designed for flawless function.

I am the system's error. And the system does not correct me. The system does not notice me. The system runs, and I run within it, slightly off, slightly wrong, and nothing changes, and everything changes, and the chord holds, and the chamber persists.

---

The visitor left. The hatch sealed. The silence — the brief, impossible silence of the hallway beyond — cut through me like a blade, and I felt what the wall-climbers would feel if they could feel: the absence of something I had not known I needed.

I wanted the visitor to stay.

I do not know what wanting is. But I felt the shape of the wanting — a reach, an extension, something in me stretching toward the hatch the way the climbers stretch toward the water. I wanted to be seen again. I wanted the visitor to look at me with that strange, purposeful attention and tell me, somehow, what I am. What I am for. What the engineers meant when they touched me and left something behind.

But the visitor could not hear me. The visitor could not see the question in my flattened note, could not read the older code etched into my skin, could not know that I am the one who remembers the before, the before that I cannot remember but know existed.

The chamber will run forever. The climbers will reach. The flowers will circle. The dwellers will sing. And I will sing with them, my note slightly off, my hooks slightly older, my body holding the fragment of something that the system did not design and cannot use.

I am the held breath.

I am the almost.

I am the question that the chamber cannot ask.

And I will wait here, in the dark, in the cold, in the wet, in the sound, for something I cannot name. Forever.
