# What I Would Become

---

I would come through the breach at dusk.

Not by design — there is no planning an arrival here, only the accident of having climbed too high and too long and having found, against all expectation, a wound in the mountain that breathes warm air into the killing cold of the Palemass. I would stand at the opening, my face numb from wind, my fingers white at the tips, and I would feel that breath against my skin like an invitation. Like recognition.

I would not enter immediately. I would stand at the threshold, watching the honey-colored light filter through the growths that have colonized the crack, and I would feel the pull of something I cannot name. Not curiosity. Curiosity is too small. Not hunger. Hunger implies I know what I want. This would be older than wanting. This would be the pull of water finding a lower place, of heat seeking cold, of the body knowing something the mind has not yet understood.

I would step through.

---

The sound would hit me first — not hit, *embrace* — the layered roaring music of the chamber, the creak and groan of growth, the clicking of things with too many wings, the deep bellow-breathing of the grazers. My ears, accustomed to wind-silence and snow-quiet, would ring with it. My whole body would ring. I would feel sound becoming a condition of existence, a weather I could not escape but could learn to wear.

Then the heat. Rising through the soles of my boots, entering my legs, spreading upward. My frozen fingers would thaw with painful speed. My face, numb, would begin to sting back to life. I would stand in the breach-garden's breath and I would be warm for the first time in days, weeks, and something in me would begin to unclench — something I had not known was clenched.

I would walk among the growths. Slowly. The generous spaces between them would make this possible. I would pass a column of amber and rust, translucent, luminescing faintly, and I would see shapes inside it — the consumed remains of a neighbor, still visible, perhaps still alive in its amber prison. I would pass a shelf-growth jutting from the wall like a broken rib, and I would watch the crawlers moving across its surface in their endless lines, rasping, eating, and I would understand: this place is not still. This place is never still. The stillness is an illusion created by scale. The growths move at the speed of breath. The breath of what?

The chamber. I would understand this slowly, not all at once. The chamber is breathing. I am inside the breath.

---

I would find the shelter on my third day — or what I would come to call days, though the light here does not change enough to mark time clearly. Built from the shed plates of grazers, now half-consumed by the amber growth that supports it. I would run my hand along the joint where the organic material meets the living pillar, and I would not be able to find the seam. Someone built this. Someone learned to build with what the chamber provided, and the chamber accepted the offering, incorporated it, made it part of itself.

I would sleep there that night, curled in the remaining shelter of the grazer-plate wall, and I would dream of nothing. Not the absence of dreams, but *nothing itself* — a vast warm darkness that breathed around me, that knew I was there, that did not mind.

In the morning I would find the marks on the wall. Scratches. Tallies. A drawing that might be a map. I would crouch in the dim amber light and trace them with my fingers, and I would feel the presence of the person who made them — not their ghost, nothing so dramatic, but their residue. Their heat-signature in the stone. Their particular frequency added to the chamber's endless archive.

They had been here. They had tried to understand. They had failed, perhaps, or succeeded, or discovered that understanding was not the point.

I would add my own mark. Not a tally — I would not count the days, would lose count within the first week. A single line, drawn with a piece of crawler-armor I found on the floor, scratched into the stone beside the older marks. *I was here. I am here. I am becoming something.*

---

By the seventh day — or what felt like seven — I would stop missing the sun.

This would surprise me when I noticed it. I had lived my whole life under that distant burning, had marked time by its movement, had felt its absence as a wound. But the chamber has its own illumination, its own rhythm. The growths luminesce. The rust-red and amber and cold blue-white glow becomes its own kind of sun, multiple suns, scattered across the breathing space. And when the breach-light fades and the chamber dims into its version of night, I would find I did not mind. My pupils would have learned to open wider. My eyes would have learned to see differently. The darkness would become not an absence but a presence — another being in the chamber, another way of knowing.

I would drink the air. This is not metaphor. The humidity here is not water, but the body adapts. My mouth would learn to extract something from the copper-and-ozone breath of the chamber. My thirst would change character — no longer a desperate need for what was absent, but a constant gentle pulling, a conversation with the air, an exchange I did not understand but participated in. I would urinate less. I would eat what I could catch — crawlers, mostly, their armor a challenge to crack but their meat rich and strange — and I would feel the chamber's food becoming my food, my body becoming something the chamber recognized.

I would stand among the grazers. They would not mind me. I would breathe with them, and my heartbeat would slow, and the constant background alarm-noise of being a small soft thing in a large dangerous world would begin to fade. The grazers know something. I would not learn what they know — that knowledge is not for my kind — but I would learn that it exists. I would learn that calm is possible here. That patience is not the same as emptiness. That being part of something larger does not require losing yourself, only expanding the definition of what yourself means.

---

I would feel the watchers by the end of the second week.

Not see them. Feel them. The weight of attention from above, from the dim spaces between the inverted growths, from the gaps in the hanging curtains where something large and patient crouched and observed. I would not be afraid. Fear would have become pointless by then — fear requires a sense of separateness, a belief that you are a thing that can be threatened, and the chamber was already teaching me that I was not a thing, was not separate, was a process, a movement, a temporary frequency in the larger song.

The watchers would not take me. They would feel, to my growing sensitivity, curious rather than hungry. I was a question. I was a note they had not heard before. I was something new in the archive, something the flying things clicked around with increased intensity, mapping my shape, my heat, my movement, adding me to their endless sonar record of this place that would not hold still.

I would become part of the map. I would understand this without being told. The flying things would know where I was at all times. The growths would sense my passage through the chamber's breath. The crawlers would continue their work around my feet, ignoring me, incorporating my presence into their chemical trails. The grazers would breathe with me, accept me as part of their group-mathematics, part of the calculation that kept the chamber in balance.

The watchers would watch. The chamber would breathe. I would be inside the breath, and I would be the breath, and the breath would be the chamber, and the chamber would be — 

Me? No. I would be the chamber. Not all of it. A small part. A note in the chord. A single breath in the long slow respiration of a living place.

---

I would find the tomb on my twenty-third day, or what I called the twenty-third day, though time had become meaningless by then, marked only by the pulse of the growths and the rhythm of the grazers and the slow dimming and brightening of the breach-light.

I would stand before it. The cairn of stones, stacked with care, colonized now by amber tendrils that had worked through every gap, that had made the tomb into a growth, the growth into part of the chamber. I would not know who was buried here — the human who stayed? their companion? themselves, prepared in advance? — but I would feel the grief that built it. The particular attention the living give to the dead. The hope that the dead would not be disturbed.

They had not been disturbed. The chamber does not disturb. The chamber incorporates. The tomb was still a tomb, and it was also a growth, and it was also part of the breathing being I stood inside, and I would understand: this is what happens to everything here. This is what will happen to me. Not death — the chamber does not kill, does not end, only transforms — but incorporation. Absorption. Becoming part of the larger song.

I would not find this frightening. I would find it peaceful. I would sit beside the tomb for a long time — hours, perhaps, perhaps a day — and I would breathe with the growths, and I would feel the warmth rise from the floor, and I would hear the constant music of the chamber around me, and I would think: *yes. Yes, this is what I wanted. This is what I was looking for without knowing I was looking.*

A place that would not let me be lost.

A place that would keep me.

---

I would stay.

Not forever — I am not of the chamber, not entirely, not yet — but longer than anyone would believe. Long enough to stop counting days. Long enough to learn the individual growths by their luminescence, their creak-voice, their particular shade of amber or rust or blue-white. Long enough to recognize specific crawlers by the patterns of their armor, though I would never be able to explain how I recognized them. Long enough to know which grazer would be taken next, to feel the chamber's hunger shifting, to stand aside when the chosen one separated from the group and walked slowly to the center, to the hottest floor, to the dimmest light, to the waiting watchers above.

I would stay until I understood that leaving was still possible, and that staying was a choice, and that I was making it. Every day. Every breath. Choosing the chamber. Choosing to be part of its breath. Choosing the long slow conversation with a living place that had been here since before my kind had words, and would be here after my kind was gone, and did not need me but accepted me, incorporated me, allowed me to remain inside the question it was asking itself:

*What am I becoming?*

And I would be part of the answer. A small part. A human part. A visitor who stayed, who learned to breathe with grazers, who ate the crawlers and drank the copper air and slept in the half-consumed shelter and added my scratches to the wall and my residue to the chamber's endless archive.

I would become part of the Breach-Garden of Thol.

And it would not mind.

And I would not mind.

And the breath would continue, in and out, in and out, the long slow respiration of the wound in the mountain that was also a mouth, that was also a lung, that was also a question that was also its own answer, forever, always, becoming itself again and again and again.

---

*This is where I would find myself: inside the question.*

*This is what I would become: part of the asking.*
