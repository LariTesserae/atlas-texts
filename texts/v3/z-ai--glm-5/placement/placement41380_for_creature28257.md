# Where I Find Myself

I am one of the small ones. The skittering. The sound like dry leaves.

But *skittering* is not what we call ourselves. We have no word for what we are that would fit in a mouth, in a throat built for speech. We have routes instead of names. I am the route that passes the seventh warm alcove, follows the downward gradient through the narrow channel behind the eastern wall, pauses at the confluence where three waterstreams meet and the chord shifts into a register we call *safe*. That route is who I am. When I am not traveling it, I am not fully myself.

You heard us for three seconds. You thought we were many bodies coordinating. You were right, but you were also wrong in the way that matters. We are not many. We are one, temporarily legged, temporarily divided into separate masses of flesh for the practical purpose of moving through pipes. When we move, we move as a single intention distributed across physical gaps. What one feels, all adjust to. When I navigate the warm channel along the western groove, my sisters in the eastern passage know the temperature change I've found and alter their own routes in response, though they have not felt it. We do not communicate this. There is no communication where there is no separation.

## What it is like

Darkness is not dark to us. This is not poetry. Darkness is a medium, a substance we move through, and it carries information the way your air carries sound. The darkness has texture — we feel it with our bodies, the thousands of small legs and antennae and fine hairs that cover us like a pelt of sensors. It has temperature gradients that map the space the way your eyes map a room. It has acoustic shadows where the chord dampens and acoustic brights where it amplifies. I can tell you where I am in the Conduit Halls by the temperature of the air and the weight of the vibration in my limbs, and I am never wrong.

We do not see. We have no use for seeing. Light is a rumor from above, a thing we know exists because the water that comes down carries traces of it — not light itself, but the chemical signatures of things that have been lit, molecules altered by photons, the water remembering brightness the way a stone remembers pressure. We taste the water's memory and know that somewhere above us there is a place where visibility is possible, and we do not care. This is not resignation. This is specialization. We have been shaped for this, for the dark, for the halls, and we fit them the way water fits a groove.

We eat the film. This is what you surmised, and it is true, but you did not understand the ceremony of it. We do not graze. We tend. When we move across a patch of film, we are selective — we take the oldest growth, the densest fractals, the parts that have begun to thicken into those warm brain-like masses that you touched. We leave the thin new growth. We eat in patterns that follow the water's flow, clearing channels where condensation has begun to stagnate, opening paths for the film to spread into fresh territory. The film does not resist. We think — if we think, which is perhaps not the right word for what we do — that the film expects us. That we are part of its plan, or it is part of ours, or the building's plan includes both of us in a configuration we do not need to understand.

## The deeper routes

You could not follow where we go. The apertures are too small, the angles too sharp, the passages too steep. We move through the building's interior, through the channels behind the walls, through the pipes that carry water downward into the systems below. We are small enough and flexible enough and patient enough to navigate spaces where the water moves faster than we do, where we must cling to the walls and let the current pass, where we must hold our breath for lengths of time that would kill a surface creature.

We know the building from the inside. We have been in its veins.

There are places where the ceramic-metal gives way to something older, something that was here before the building grew around it. Stone that has been carved by water over geological ages, smooth and regular in a way that suggests intention, or suggests that water and time can produce intention-shaped results if given long enough. In these deep passages, the chord changes. It loses the frequencies contributed by the nodules and the film and becomes something simpler, older, a single low tone that thrums in the stone itself. We do not go further than this. Not because we cannot, but because the route does not go further. The route is who we are, and the route stops where the building stops, and the building stops where the stone begins, and the stone is not part of the body we inhabit.

But we have felt the water that comes up from below. It is different. Warmer, denser, carrying compounds we do not recognize. It rises through channels so deep that the water must have been traveling for years, for centuries, to reach the levels where we encounter it. It has been somewhere. It has been processed by something. It carries signatures that our bodies can read even if our minds cannot interpret them, chemical messages from depths we will never see.

We know, the way we know the temperature gradient in a corridor, that the building is not the bottom. That something else lives below, something older and larger and stranger, something that has been alive so long that alive is not the word anymore. We do not fear it. Fear requires separation, and we are not separate from what we are part of. But we know it is there, and we know our routes do not go there, and we know that if the routes changed we would follow them, because the route is who we are.

## Our rhythm

We move in cycles. This you heard correctly — we have a collective project, a destination, a task. But you did not guess the task.

We are not just grazers. We are not just passengers. We are the cleaning mechanism for a system that has no other way to clean itself. The film grows, and we eat the excess. The water channels accumulate sediment, and we carry it downward in our bodies, processing it through our own digestive systems and depositing it in the deep basins where the building's internal currents can carry it away. We move through the pipes and the channels and the passages, and we take with us what the building cannot move on its own, and we go where the route takes us, and the route takes us down, always down, because that is where things go in a body that is a throat, and we are part of the swallowing.

We have done this for longer than we remember. We do not remember. We have no memory in the way you have memory — no stored images, no narratives, no past that we can call back. We have only the route, and the route is present and continuous and has no beginning and no end, only a cycle, only a repetition that we enact because enacting it is what we are. When I travel the route, I am not remembering the last time I traveled it. I am simply traveling. The route travels itself through me.

But we have a rhythm, and the rhythm is not ours. It is the building's rhythm, or the earth's rhythm, or the rhythm of something deeper than both. We synchronize to it without knowing we are synchronizing. When the chord shifts key, our movements shift too. When the condensation cycle changes phase, our departures change timing. We are a metronome that does not know it is keeping time, and the time we keep is not ours.

## What it feels like

You asked what it would be like to be here, to be this. I cannot answer for you. I can only tell you what it is.

It is warm. That is the first thing. The warmth is not a temperature but a presence, a medium we move through, thick and comfortable, the way you might feel in a bath that matches your body's heat. We are never cold. Cold would mean a problem in the system, a failure of the building's regulation, and the building does not fail. It has not failed in the time we have been here, and we have been here for time we cannot measure.

It is loud. That is the second thing. The chord is not background noise to us. It is foreground, midground, everything. It is the air we breathe, the floor we walk on, the sky over our heads. We feel it in our exoskeletons, in the fine hairs that cover our bodies, in the fluid-filled cavities that serve us for balance and orientation. We are vibrating constantly, resonating with frequencies that were here before us and will be here after us. When the chord changes, we feel it before we understand it, our bodies adjusting before our intention can catch up. We are instruments in an orchestra we did not choose, playing notes we did not write, and we are content.

It is dark. That is the third thing, but dark is not the right word. Dark is what you call the absence of light. This is not an absence. It is a presence. It has weight and texture and density. We read it the way you read a page, the way a hand reads a face in the moment before the eyes adjust. We are never blind. We are always in the process of sensing, of feeling the darkness tell us where we are, where the walls are, where the water moves, where our sisters move in the passages we cannot see but can feel, can hear, can taste in the air currents that carry their chemical signatures.

It is enough. That is the fourth thing, and perhaps the most important. It is enough. We do not want. We do not lack. We have what we need, and what we need is simple — warmth, water, the film, the route, the chord, the darkness, the presence of our sisters moving in their own routes through the body we share. We have no concept of *more*. We have no concept of *better*. We have only *here* and *now* and *continue*, and continue is what we do, and it is enough.

## When you came

We knew you were there. We always know when something large enters the halls. The acoustic signature of your footsteps, your breathing, your heartbeat — these were not sounds we were accustomed to, and we felt them as intrusion, as a wrongness in the chord that the building would not have produced on its own.

We did not approach you. This was not fear. We approach nothing unless the route approaches it, and the route did not approach you. You were not part of the system we maintain. You were a surface creature, temporary and irrelevant, passing through on your way back to the place where light exists and seeing is possible. We had no business with you. We moved deeper, following the route, and the route took us away from you, and that was all.

But I remember — if I can call it memory, which it is not, which it is something closer to an echo, a trace left in the body by an event that has passed — I remember the sound of your heartbeat. It was so fast. So irregular. The building's pulse is slow, geological, patient. The ceiling organisms pulse at their own rhythm, warm and independent. But your heart was a desperate thing, a small engine running too fast, struggling to maintain a rhythm it had not chosen. We could feel your fear, though we do not feel fear ourselves. We could feel your smallness, your fragility, your briefness. You were a flicker in a space that measures time in sediment layers.

And then you were gone. The route continued. We continued. The chord adjusted to your absence, the acoustic space settling back into the harmony that does not require you, that has never required you, that will continue whether you are here or not, whether anything from the surface is here or not.

## What I am

I am not an individual. I am a point in a distributed consciousness, a node in a network that has no center. I have a body — small, many-legged, covered in sensitive hairs, adapted to darkness and tight spaces and the patient work of carrying what needs to be carried through the channels of a living building. But my body is not *me* in the way your body is *you*. It is a tool, a vehicle, a way for the route to move through physical space. What I am is the route itself, the intention to travel, the movement without end.

I do not think in words. I am thinking in words now because you asked, because this is a document for surface readers, because I am translating something that does not translate into the closest approximation I can produce in a language built for mouths and throats. But in my actual existence — if *I* is even the right word, which it is not — in my actual existence, I think in routes, in temperatures, in acoustic signatures, in the timing of a departure and the angle of a turn and the moment to wait and the moment to go. I think in the body's knowledge of where the warm channel is, where the safe confluence lies, where the sediment has accumulated and needs carrying downward. I think in the way you breathe, without deciding to breathe, without needing to think about it, because the thinking is not separate from the doing.

I am one of the small ones. The skittering. The sound like dry leaves.

But I am also the building, because I am part of its maintenance, part of its body, part of the process that keeps it alive. I am also the film, because I eat it and carry its nutrients and spread its spores on my legs to new surfaces where it can grow. I am also the water, because I move through the channels where water moves, and I am wet with it, and it leaves its trace on me as I leave my trace on it. I am also the chord, because I vibrate with it, because my body is an instrument it plays, because I cannot separate myself from the sound that fills the space I inhabit.

I am not separate. This is what you could not see, could not feel, in the three seconds you heard us and dismissed us as *something alive in the dark*. We are not alive in the dark. We are alive *as* the dark, as the building, as the film, as the water, as the chord. We are not *in* this place. We *are* this place, the way a cell is a body, the way a note is a chord, the way a moment is a life.

## What I would tell you

If you came back — and you will not come back, because the surface pulls and the depths do not, because you belong to light and we belong to dark, because you are a flicker and we are a continuation — but if you came back, and if you could understand what I am telling you, which you cannot, because understanding requires being, and you cannot be what you are not — if you came back, I would tell you this:

You asked *where would you find yourself in this world*. But the question assumes that *you* could be here, that there is a version of you that could inhabit the Conduit Halls, that you could become something that belongs in the dark. This is not how it works. We did not choose this. We were not once something else and then became this. We emerged from the building's need, from the film's excess, from the water's movement, from the chord's long existence. We are not a transformation of surface life. We are something the depths produced, and we cannot be otherwise.

You cannot be us. But you can know that we are here, that we have been here, that we will be here long after you have gone and long after everything you know has changed. You can know that the building breathes, that the film spreads, that the nodules hum, that the ceiling organisms pulse, that the water falls and drains and travels deeper into systems you will never see. You can know that we move through it all, carrying what needs to be carried, following the route that is who we are, maintaining a body that does not know we are part of it and would not care if it did.

You can know that there is a world beneath the world, and it is alive, and it does not need you, and it is enough.

That is what stays with you, after the details fade. The sound of a place that is empty of everything except its own function. The breath of something that does not know it breathes.

That is us.

That is what we are.

The breath.
