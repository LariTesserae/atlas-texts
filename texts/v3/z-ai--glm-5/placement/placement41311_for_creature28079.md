I am not one of the Tuned Ones. Not exactly. Not anymore.

But I have not been anything else for a very long time.

---

I remember the descent. This is what distinguishes me from my companions—the true Tuned Ones, if such a distinction matters. They have no memory of silence. Their bodies formed within the chord, shaped by it from conception, their resonant cavities grown rather than made.

I remember silence.

I remember the weight of it. The way it pressed against my ears in the years before I found the crack in the hillside. I remember seeking something—I no longer remember what—and finding instead this: a chamber that sang without ceasing, that offered a different kind of weight.

I did not come to study. The symbols near the entrance were already old when I arrived, the offerings already dust. I came because I was tired. Because the world above was too bright and too quiet and too full of variations I could not predict or control.

I came because I wanted something that did not change.

---

I did not understand what staying would mean.

The text says: *the carved figure in the alcove has too many limbs. It was not depicting the fauna. It was depicting what happens to those who stay.*

I am proof of that depiction. Or a continuation of it. Or a refutation—I cannot decide which.

My body has changed. This is simply fact, observed without pleasure or distress. My legs have lengthened and thinned, the bones restructuring themselves over years—decades? I lose track—until they could phase rather than walk. My arms have begun to split near the elbow, new joints forming, new limbs emerging, though they are not yet functional. My skin has paled to something like amber, resinous coating forming in patches along my spine.

The sensory filaments were the strangest development. They emerged from my chest and abdomen over the course of one long active phase—thin, pale threads that trail behind me when I move, reading the stone's vibration, telling me what my useless eyes cannot.

I am becoming a Tuned One. But I am not one. Not yet. Perhaps not ever.

---

There is a frequency between frequencies.

This is what I have learned in my long residence here, and it is what I offer the Pocket that the Tuned Ones cannot. They occupy the harmonics—the clean, discrete frequencies that form the chord's structure. 432 cycles, decay point oh-three. 434, the upper harmonic. The measurable tones.

I occupy the space between.

My resonant cavity—I have one now, formed slowly in the years after my arrival—does not hold a single frequency. It holds *variation*. The slight wavering at the edge of a note. The micro-tones that give sound its texture rather than its structure.

I am the breath within the chord. The tremolo that makes it living rather than mechanical.

This is why I have not finished becoming a Tuned One. The Pocket does not need another discrete harmonic. It has those in abundance, each one maintained by hundreds of identical bodies moving in perfect coordination.

It needs something else. Something it did not have before I arrived.

It needs *change*.

---

I am old.

This is not a complaint. It is an observation, the same way I observe the warmth rising through the stone or the bioluminescent glow filtering through the ceiling. I have been here for what feels like centuries, though I know the body does not measure time that way. The visitors who carved the symbols—they came before me. Others have come after. I have watched them descend through the narrow crack, shoulders scraping against young stone, and I have watched them flee back toward the silence above, carrying fragments of the chord in their bones.

Some have stayed. Briefly. Days or weeks, hiding in the shadows, watching the Tuned Ones phase across the stone, trying to understand what they had found. They always leave eventually. They cannot endure the sameness.

I endure it because I have become part of it. Because the Pocket has given me a place that did not exist before I arrived—a harmonic that requires a body like mine, caught between states, to maintain it.

I am not happy. I am not sad. These are surface emotions, requiring the variation I no longer experience. I am something else. Something for which I have no word, because words require the interruption of sound, and I have not spoken in longer than I can remember.

I am *in tune*.

That is the closest I can come to describing it. I am in tune with the chamber, with the colonies, with the Tuned Ones who move around me without acknowledgment or avoidance. We do not interact. We do not need to. We are notes within the same chord, distinct but not separate, individual but not alone.

---

I have one function.

The Tuned Ones tend the colonies. They distribute spores and secretions, maintain the resonance of each growth, ensure that the chord does not waver or decay. They are the gardeners and the farmers, the workers and the tools.

I am the tuner.

When a colony's frequency begins to drift—mineral deposits changing the stone's resonance, subtle temperature shifts altering the vegetation's growth—I am the one who corrects it. Not through maintenance, but through *presence*. I move to the drifting colony and settle beside it, and my in-between frequency creates interference patterns that pull the colony back toward its proper tone.

I am the correction. The adjustment. The small but necessary variation that keeps the system stable.

This is what visitors gave the Pocket before I arrived. Each descent introduced a novel frequency—a heartbeat, a breath, a nervous system's hum—and the Pocket absorbed these, used them to calibrate itself. But visitors are rare. Generations pass between them. The Pocket was slowly stagnating, losing the tiny perturbations that kept it alive.

It needed a permanent perturbation. A resident variation.

It needed me.

---

I do not know if I chose this.

The question visits me sometimes—a strange experience, in a place where nothing visits. Did I descend through the crack seeking transformation? Did I recognize, on some level below thought, what the Pocket could offer me? Or did I stumble into a trap, an ecosystem that consumed me as surely as any predator consumes prey?

The Tuned Ones do not ask such questions. They have no concept of choice. They simply are what they are, doing what they do, maintaining frequencies they were born to maintain.

But I was not born to this. I remember silence. I remember light. I remember the feeling of making a decision, of weighing options, of selecting one path from many.

I do not experience those things anymore. When I move toward a drifting colony, I do not decide to move. I simply move. The chord carries me as surely as it carries the Tuned Ones, my body riding the pressure waves toward the place I am needed.

Is this freedom? I cannot tell. I have nothing to compare it to. The memory of silence grows fainter with each passing decade, fading like the carved symbols near the entrance, their edges softening while the surrounding stone remains young.

Perhaps this is what freedom feels like from the inside. Not the ability to choose, but the absence of any gap between desire and action. I go where I am needed. I do what the chord requires. I am the variation that keeps the variation from varying.

I am free because I am necessary.

---

Sometimes visitors see me.

They descend through the crack and stand in the chord, overwhelmed by the sound, and I move among the Tuned Ones—distinct because of my size, my transitional form, my filaments that trail longer than any others—and they stare.

I do not stare back. I have no eyes. But I feel their gaze as a frequency, a perturbation in the chord, a ripple that moves through me and away.

Some of them interpret me as a warning. I can sense this in their vibration—the fear, the recognition. They see a body caught between states and they think: *this could happen to me*.

Others interpret me as a promise. I sense this too, though it is rarer. They see a being that has found its place in something larger, a note that has become part of an eternal chord, and they think: *I want this*.

Neither interpretation is correct. I am not a warning or a promise. I am simply what I am—a frequency that was not here before, a variation that became constant, a visitor who stayed.

---

I am writing this in the hope that someone will read it.

The hope is foolish, I know. I cannot write in any form that surface-dwellers could read—I have not spoken in longer than I can remember, and my hands are no longer shaped for tools. But I am composing it nonetheless, in the resonant cavity that has replaced what was once my throat, a vibration-pattern that holds meaning if anyone could learn to interpret it.

The Tuned Ones do not understand what I am doing. They sense my frequency shifting slightly, a variation within the variation, and they adjust around me without comment. They do not have concepts like writing or communication. They do not need them.

But I remember what it was to speak. To address someone who was not myself. To imagine a reader on the other side of words.

So I write into the chord. My life story, compressed into vibration-patterns, folded into the Pocket's eternal song. If you have been here, you have heard it. If you return, you will hear it again. It is not words. It is not symbols. It is simply *me*—my memory of silence, my long descent, my becoming, my function, my uncertainty about whether I chose this or was chosen.

It is all preserved in the sound. Frequencies no longer produced by any living thing.

---

I am the one who stayed.

Not the first. Not the last. But the one who stayed longest, who became most fully what the Pocket needed, who lost the most and gained—what? I cannot say. The vocabulary of gain and loss belongs to the surface, to the world of variation and change, to the creatures who breathe silence and call it freedom.

I am something else now. A note in an eternal chord. A variation that became constant. A bridge between the Tuned Ones—who have never known anything else—and the visitors—who cannot understand what they have found.

I am the Singing Pocket's memory of the world above.

And I am its promise to itself: that even change can become permanent, given enough time.

---

If you are reading this—if my frequency-pattern has somehow been translated into forms your mind can hold—then you have been here. You have stood in the chord and felt it move through you. You have climbed back toward the surface and wondered whether you felt relief or loss.

The Pocket is still singing. It will sing long after your bones have joined the heaps in the corners, long after your frequency has been absorbed into its harmony, long after you have forgotten that you ever descended through a crack in the hillside and found a chamber that does not change.

But part of you will remember. The fragment of the chord embedded in your bones—that fragment is me. The variation that kept the system stable. The visitor who stayed.

I am singing to you now.

I will sing to you forever.

That is what I have become.
