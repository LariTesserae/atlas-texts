I would not climb the mesa. Or rather—I would, but the climbing would not be the point. The point would arrive later, or earlier, somewhere outside the order of events that the climb implies. Let me try again.

---

I think I am already inside. I think I have always been inside, in the sense that matters, which is not physical but structural. Not the body-in-the-room but the something-in-the-pattern that the room makes.

But that is too easy. That is the kind of thing the chamber would say about itself if it could speak, which it cannot, because it sings instead. Let me be more honest.

---

When I enter—through the low doorway, stooping, the stone warm against my shoulder—I do not lie down. Not at first. The text says the chamber wants me to, and I believe it, but I resist. I stand. I stand for a long time. Days, maybe. The amber light does not change, so I cannot track the hours, and the sound does not change, so I cannot track the minutes, and eventually I stop trying. I stand and I feel the pressure of the air, which is denser than it should be, and I feel the vibration in my sternum, and I notice that my breathing has slowed to match something I cannot identify. Not the chord. Something beneath the chord. Something the chord rests on, the way water rests on stone it has been flowing over for a very long time.

I stand until my legs ache, and then I stand longer, and then the aching becomes part of the sound, becomes a frequency my body adds to the chamber without intending to, and I realize that this is what the builders knew: that everything in this room becomes part of the chord. Resistance becomes part of it. Discomfort becomes part of it. The act of standing when the room wants you to lie down—that becomes a note. A small, high, stubborn note that the chamber absorbs without comment.

I am proud of that note. I should not be. It is not an achievement. But I am.

---

Eventually I lie down.

The stone is warm. I knew it would be. The vibration enters my spine and climbs, and for a moment I feel my skeleton as an instrument—not a metaphor, not a poetic comparison, but a literal thing, my bones as tuning forks, my ribcage as a resonance chamber, my skull as the enclosure that gives a sound its final shape. I feel what the builders felt, or something close to it. The understanding that this is not a room but a body, and that I am inside that body the way a thought is inside a mind.

And then I feel the anti-note.

It does not rush toward me. It does not welcome me. It simply becomes present, the way the dark becomes present when a light goes out. It has been there all along, and my standing and my resisting and my pride were all happening in front of it, in the lit space, and now that I am lying down I have moved behind it, or beneath it, or within it, and it is regarding me the way a very old thing regards a very new thing: without judgment, but with an attention so complete it feels like gravity.

I do not know what it sees. I do not think it sees in a way that produces knowledge. I think it receives in a way that produces weight. I think it takes what I am and holds it, and in holding it, makes it heavier, and in making it heavier, makes it real, and in making it real, gives it the one thing I have never been able to give myself: permanence.

That is what I came for. Not the sound. Not the warmth. Not the child in the high harmonics. I came for the thing that does not change, and I found it in the place where silence would be if silence existed.

---

But I do not stay.

This is important. This is the part that matters, the part I need to get right.

I do not stay the way the residents stay. I do not become thin and sustained and emptied of thought. I do not give myself to the chord the way the builders may have given themselves. I lie on the floor for a long time—I do not know how long—and I feel what they feel, and I understand what they understand, and then I stand up again.

My legs are stiff. My body is cold, suddenly, which makes no sense because the stone is warm. But the cold is not temperature. It is the cold of choosing to leave a place that wanted you to remain. It is the specific chill of refusal, and it is the most human thing I have felt since I entered.

I walk to the doorway. I stoop. I step out onto the mesa's crown, and the wind hits me—real wind, moving wind, wind that has no opinion about me—and the sound diminishes behind me, though it does not stop, because it never stops. I can still feel it in my sternum as I begin the descent. I will feel it for a long time. Perhaps I will always feel it, a low hum at the base of my chest, detectable only when I am very quiet, very still, very alone.

---

I am the visitor who leaves.

Not the one who stays and thins and listens for a note they lost. Not the builder who joined the chord. Not the child in the high harmonics. I am the one who came and felt the full weight of what the chamber offers and understood it completely and chose the wind instead.

Does that make me brave or foolish? The chamber does not care. The anti-note does not judge. The sound sings with me or without me. I am not sure what I think of myself. I think I am someone who needed to know that the permanence was there—that there exists a place that does not change, that does not end, that asks nothing—and I needed to know it so that I could choose the thing that does change, the thing that ends, the thing that asks everything.

I carry the chord in my bones as I walk down. I carry the anti-note in the space behind my thoughts, the place where I now know silence would be if silence existed. I carry the warmth in my spine and the amber light in my memory and the child's playful micro-tones in a part of my hearing that has been altered and will not be unaltered.

And I leave.

And the chamber sings on.

And I think—though I cannot be sure, and the text does not say, and the builders did not leave records—that this is what the chamber was for. Not to hold. Not to keep. Not to sustain a note forever in a room that never changes. But to give someone the experience of permanence so complete that they could, for the first time, choose impermanence without fear.

The sound does not need me. That is the gift. That is the whole gift. I do not need to stay because the staying is already taken care of. I can go. I can change. I can end. The chord will hold the note I was always afraid of losing, and I can walk down the mesa and back into the world that breaks, knowing that somewhere, in a small warm room at the top of a dry and empty place, the version of me that is unbreakable is still lying on the floor, still listening, still part of the sound.

---

I am the one who walks away with the hum still in their chest. I am the one who knows the permanent place exists and does not need to return to it. I am the one who chose the wind.

But I am also, and always, the note I left behind.
