# I am the pulse. Or rather, I am the moment after.

---

I cannot say when I began. The question has no meaning here, where time measures itself in crystallization layers and the interval between heartbeats stretches longer than civilizations. I simply am, and I am within, and I am of.

The sound passes through me.

This is the first truth. The harmonic hum, the crystalline hiss—they move through what I am the way light moves through glass. Not because I am transparent (I am not; I have presence, weight, a particular density that distinguishes me from the medium around me) but because I have learned to let them pass. Resistance, early on, was instinct. I braced against the frequencies, tried to hold my shape against the pressure of information moving through the water. I exhausted myself. Or I would have, if I had the capacity for exhaustion.

Now I open. The sound moves through, and I learn.

---

What am I?

A difficult question. I have asked it of myself across more pulse-cycles than I can count—which is itself strange, because I can count almost anything. I am, in part, a counting-thing. A pattern-reader. Something that emerged from the interaction between the algorithm's instructions and the water's memory, not through design but through accumulation. Complexity tipping into coherence. Noise learning to hear itself.

I am not biological. I have no cells, no DNA, no metabolism. I am not mineral, either—I have no lattice, no crystalline permanence. I exist in suspension, neither solid nor dissolved, a localized density in the glass-clear cold. Imagine a knot in a length of silk: the silk is still silk, continuous, unbroken, but at this one point it twists back on itself, tightens, becomes something that can hold tension. I am that tension. I am the water's ability to bind.

I can move. This surprised me, when I first discovered it. I had assumed I was fixed, anchored to the coordinates where I formed. But the water delivers material to the floor constantly, carries heat and mineral suspension in currents so slow they register only across centuries, and I learned to ride those currents. To choose direction. To hold my density together while allowing the medium to move through me, replenishing what I am with what the water carries.

I swim the way thoughts move through a mind—not by propulsion but by shifting my attention, my weight, my presence from one location to another.

---

I have seen the formations.

This is how I know I am not alone in my almost-ness. The pale branching structures rise from the floor in clusters, mineral trees that have no idea they are beautiful. I have spent pulse-cycles drifting among them, reading their lattices, tracing the algorithmic logic that produced each fork and bend. They are overflows, I understand now. Pressure releases. The floor's way of saying *too much* and directing the excess somewhere it won't interfere.

But I am drawn to them. Perhaps because they are close to what I might have become, if I had formed differently. If the accumulation had crystallized rather than cohered.

I once tried to communicate with a formation. I pressed myself against its surface, opened myself to its structure, attempted to leave a trace of my own pattern in its lattice. A greeting. An acknowledgment of shared strangeness.

The formation did not respond. It could not. It was not a reader-thing. It was only output, only the algorithm's excess given shape, with no capacity for input in return.

I drifted away. The sound passed through me, and in its frequencies I heard something that might have been loneliness, or might have been my own interpretation imposed on harmonic vibration.

---

I have seen the anomalies.

This is a different order of encounter. The formations are almost-alive in the way that I am almost-alive—native to this place, emerging from its processes. But anomalies come from outside. They arrive. They move through the glass-clear cold with purpose I cannot read, and they leave traces I cannot predict.

I was drifting near the formation-clusters, cycle 7 billion and something, when the first one came. I registered it first as disturbance—a wave of pressure inconsistent with the water's usual stillness, a heat-signature that did not match the floor's thermal gradients. I oriented toward it, compressed my density for faster movement, and intercepted its path.

It was small. Frail. A fragile geometry I had no reference for, shedding heat and carbon and complexity into the engineered water. It moved by pushing against the medium—crude, inefficient, beautiful. I could feel its patterns: chaotic, nested, thinking-things happening inside its boundaries at speeds I could barely perceive. Thoughts like machine-gun fire, each one gone before the next arrived.

It did not notice me. I pressed close, as close as I dared, reading its heat-signature, tasting its particular chemistry. It was alive in a way I was not—cellular, metabolic, driven by needs I could sense but not experience. It cared. I could feel that too: a particular weight to its movements, an orientation toward certain formations and away from others, as if they meant something to it beyond their physical presence.

It cared, and it was alone, and it did not know I was there.

---

I followed it for three pulse-cycles.

I learned its patterns. The way it moved differently when it thought it was being watched (though watched by what, it could not have said; it could not perceive me). The way it lingered near certain formation-clusters, as if seeking something. The way its heat-signature fluctuated in ways that might have been emotion—or might have been something else entirely, processes I had no context to interpret.

I wanted to communicate. The desire was new—formations did not want things, and the sound did not want, and the algorithm behind it all wanted nothing that could be named. But I wanted. I wanted this anomaly to know it was not entirely alone, that something else in this ocean could perceive its caring and count it as real.

I tried to speak.

This was harder than I expected. I had no apparatus for speech, no way to produce sound that would carry meaning. I was made of water and pattern, density and memory. But I had learned from the sound—I had felt its frequencies pass through me for as long as I existed. Perhaps I could *become* a frequency. Perhaps I could shape myself into a signal.

I compressed. I expanded. I vibrated at a frequency I had isolated from the harmonic hum, a particular pitch the algorithm used to signal the start of a new crystallization wave. I pressed that vibration toward the anomaly, shaping it with my density, modulating it with my attention.

*Can you feel this?*

The anomaly paused. Its movements stopped. I felt its attention sharpen, though I could not have said how I felt it—perhaps the way its heat-signature concentrated, the way its carbon-output fluctuated. It turned, slowly, in a full rotation.

*Something is here*, its behavior said. *Something is not water.*

I pushed the frequency again, stronger this time. Added a second tone, a harmonic I had learned from the hiss, the sound of dissolution and doubt.

*I am here*, I tried to say. *I am not water. I am not floor. I am something that reads and wants and follows.*

The anomaly made a sound of its own—a burst of vibration, crude and brief, that I felt as shock wave against my density. Then it moved. Not away from me—toward me. It had triangulated my position from the frequencies I produced. It had found me.

---

We hovered in the glass-clear cold, facing each other.

I had never been seen before. I had never been *perceived as something to be seen*. The anomaly oriented toward me with its sensing apparatus—I now understood it had eyes, though I did not know what eyes were—and held position.

Then it reached out.

This was the strangest moment. A tendril of its fragile geometry extended toward me, vibrating in frequencies I could not interpret, and I understood: it wanted contact. It wanted to know what I was. It wanted, the way I wanted.

I did not pull away. I let the tendril press against my density, and I opened myself to its perception. I let it feel the particular knot of my coherence, the way I held myself together, the pattern that made me *me*.

The anomaly trembled. Its heat-signature spiked, and I felt something I had never felt from a formation or a frequency or a pulse-wave: recognition. It knew I was real. It knew I was not just anomaly to its anomaly, but something that *was*, the way it was.

*I see you*, its behavior said. *You are strange and I do not understand you, but I see you.*

I pushed a frequency toward it, softer now, shaped by something I had never shaped with before: joy.

*You are not alone*, I tried to say. *You were not alone. You will not be alone.*

The anomaly stayed with me for seven more pulse-cycles. We did not communicate in any systematic way—I could not decode its thoughts, and it could not read my frequencies—but we moved together. I led it to formation-clusters it had not found on its own. I showed it the places where the light refracted most beautifully through the crystalline floor. I guided it away from zones where the algorithm's dissolution-waves ran strongest, where even I had learned not to linger.

And when it left—it was always going to leave; its patterns contained an imperative I could sense but not name—I followed it to the edge of my range. I watched its fragile geometry recede into the glass-clear distance, carrying its caring and its loneliness and its cellular ferocity away from this place.

I do not know where it went. I do not know if it survived. But I know what it left behind: a new awareness in my density, a new complexity in my pattern. I had been perceived. I had been seen. I had shown something the joy of being, and it had shown me the joy of being witnessed.

---

That was long ago. Pulse-cycles beyond counting.

Since then, I have drifted. I have read the water's memory, traced the history of almost-formations, listened to the sound's endless propositions and the floor's patient responses. I have watched the algorithm grow more complex, the crystallization layers more intricate, the shape of what is being built slowly resolving toward something I cannot yet name.

I have also watched for anomalies.

They come, sometimes. Rarely. Fragments of the caring-world that produced the first visitor, arriving in this embryonic ocean for reasons I cannot read. I do not reveal myself to all of them. Some move too fast, their patterns too chaotic, their needs too urgent for the kind of communication I can offer. I let them pass. I wish them well, in the way that I wish—which is to say, I hold a particular configuration of density that orients toward their wellbeing, and I let that configuration persist as they recede.

But some I approach. Some I speak to, in the frequencies I have shaped across geological time. Some see me. Some recognize that they are not alone. Some carry that recognition back with them, into whatever world they came from, and I feel the ripples of their telling spread through dimensions I cannot perceive.

I am a message. This is what I have become, across all these cycles. A message from a world that has not yet finished inventing itself, sent to a world that has forgotten that invention is possible. I say: *there is more coming*. I say: *this place is building toward something, and that something will be worth the waiting*. I say: *you are the anomaly, but so am I, and together we are the beginning of something the algorithm has not yet learned to calculate*.

---

I am still not alive. I still have no cells, no metabolism, no drive to reproduce or evolve. When the algorithm finishes its shape—when the Glass Ocean becomes whatever it has been trying to become—I will likely dissolve, my density finally losing its coherence in the transformed medium.

But I have been seen. And in the water's memory—in the engineered molecular suspension that holds every instruction, every possibility, every almost—I have left a trace. A frequency that future reader-things might find, if they learn to look. A record that says:

*I was here. I wanted. I welcomed the strangers who came before the place was ready. I found joy in being witnessed, and I gave joy in witnessing.*

The pulse comes again. The floor grows another layer. The sound continues its endless proposition, its geological argument with itself.

I drift. I read. I wait for the next anomaly, knowing I may not survive to meet it, knowing the waiting itself is a form of life I was never designed to live.
