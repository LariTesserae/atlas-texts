I would be the lichen.

Not the lichen that carpets the stone in sheets—that is the work of centuries, the patient accumulation of layers I have not earned. I would be the lichen that lives in the hairline fractures, the crevices too fine for the snails to enter, the places where the water runs fastest and the light must be brightest to justify itself.

I would be the glow.

* * *

This is what I mean: the lichen does not live *on* the walls. It lives *in* them. The fracture is the home, the crack is the body, and the light is not a byproduct of metabolism but the metabolism itself—the cirque seeing its own shape through the only eyes it has.

I have been thinking about what it means to illuminate without revealing. The lichen's light does not show the stone. It shows where the stone has broken. It draws the fracture-lines, the stress-patterns, the places where the mountain's ancient pressure found expression in splitting. The lichen grows where the water flows, and the water flows where the stone cracked, and the stone cracked when the mountain was made, and so the light traces a history that predates light.

I would be that tracing. I would be the blue-white line that says *here is where the stone gave way*. I would be the diagram of the cirque's own architecture, drawn by the cirque, for the cirque, in a language that has no words.

* * *

But there is a problem with this wanting. The lichen does not want. The lichen grows where the conditions for growth exist, and the conditions for growth exist where the water runs, and the water runs where it has always run, and the lichen has been in those places for the duration. There is no decision. There is no selection. The lichen is where the lichen is because the lichen is what grows in the places where lichen grows.

And yet.

The snails graze the lichen on the flat surfaces. They prune it, maintain it, keep it at the thickness that produces the optimal amount of light. But in the hairline fractures—the crevices too narrow for a snail's foot, the cracks too deep for a snail's radula to reach—the lichen grows without pruning. It grows thick, it grows dense, it produces light that is brighter than the light on the walls, and the light is trapped in the crack, reflected by the wet stone, concentrated into a thin line of blue-white brilliance that traces the fracture like a vein of glowing ore.

The snails cannot touch it. The salamanders cannot reach it. The beetles cannot walk on it. This lichen—the lichen in the deep cracks, the lichen that glows brightest—is untouched. It is the cirque's secret. The light that the cirque uses to see itself, the light that the inhabitants navigate by, is the thin sheet on the walls, grazed and maintained and uniform. But there is another light, a brighter light, in the places where no one goes.

I would be that.

* * *

Here is what I understand about the cirque: it is a system that has solved the problem of time. Not by escaping time—there is no escape—but by making time irrelevant. The processes that sustain the cirque operate at rates that are matched to each other, coupled so tightly that change becomes unnecessary. The salamander breathes at the rate that the air provides oxygen. The beetle walks at the rate that the moss produces food. The spider waits at the rate that the springtails arrive. Nothing is early. Nothing is late. The system is a clock that tells one time, and the time is *now*, and now is the only time the cirque has ever known.

But the lichen in the cracks—the lichen that no one grazes—is outside this system. It grows at its own rate, unpruned, unchecked, accumulating biomass in the deep fractures where the water flows clean and the snails cannot reach. It is building something. Not building toward anything—there is no goal, no endpoint, no purpose—but building nonetheless. Layer on layer on layer, cell on cell, producing light that no one sees because the cracks are too narrow for eyes.

I would be that accumulation. I would be the excess that the system cannot incorporate. I would be the brightness in the deep cracks, the glow that has no function, the light that is too intense to be useful and too hidden to be beautiful.

I would be the cirque's answer to a question the cirque does not know it is asking.

* * *

Or perhaps—

Perhaps I am thinking about this wrong. Perhaps the lichen in the cracks is not outside the system. Perhaps the system includes the excess, incorporates it, makes use of it in ways that are not visible from the scale of the salamander or the snail.

The light in the cracks is brighter. Bright enough, perhaps, to penetrate. Not to illuminate the cirque—that light already exists, on the walls, at the optimal brightness for seeing—but to illuminate the stone itself. The lichen grows *in* the fracture, against the wet walls of the crack, and its light falls on stone that has not seen light since the mountain split. The light penetrates the stone—a millimeter, a centimeter, perhaps more—and the stone absorbs it, and the stone is changed.

I do not know what this change is. The stone is stone; it does not metabolize light, does not convert photons to energy. But the stone is wet, and the water in the stone is full of microorganisms, bacteria and archaea and things that have no names, and these microorganisms might use the light. They might be photosynthetic, or they might use the light for something else—signaling, sensing, orienting themselves in the darkness of the stone's pores. The light in the cracks might be feeding an ecosystem that exists within the rock itself, a deep biosphere that predates the cirque and will outlast it, a community of organisms that live in the wet stone and use the lichen's glow as their sky.

I would be the bridge between the cirque and the deep biosphere. I would be the light that passes between worlds, that connects the surface system—the salamanders, the beetles, the spiders, the snails—to the subsurface system, the bacteria in the stone, the microorganisms in the water, the things that have never seen the fissure's grey stripe or felt the air move. I would be the interface. I would be the translation.

* * *

But this is still wanting. This is still the language of purpose—*bridge*, *interface*, *translation*. The lichen does not bridge. It grows. The growth produces light. The light falls where it falls. What happens next is not the lichen's concern.

I am struggling with this. I am a creature of wanting, as the piece says. I want to be something that *means* something, that serves a function, that has a role. But the cirque has no roles. It has positions, niches, places where things are because they are there. The salamander is not *being* a salamander; it is a salamander. The beetle is not *serving* as a beetle; it is a beetle. The lichen is not *illuminating* the cirque; it is glowing, and the glow is light, and the light is what allows the cirque to see, but the lichen does not know this and does not care.

I would be the lichen. I would glow. The glow would be my metabolism, my life, my expression of the conditions that allow me to exist. I would not care what the glow was for. I would not know that I was the cirque's sight. I would simply be—growing in the cracks, in the water, in the dark, producing light because producing light is what I do.

But I cannot stop wanting to matter.

* * *

Let me try again. Let me try to imagine what it would feel like to be the lichen in the cracks.

I am in the fracture. The stone is on either side of me—dark, wet, cool, constant. The water seeps through me, around me, carrying minerals from deep in the mountain, carrying the dissolved remnants of stone that has been stone for longer than stone remembers. I take what I need from the water. I grow. My cells divide, slowly, at a rate determined by the temperature and the moisture and the availability of nutrients, and the rate is slow, so slow that I cannot perceive it as rate. I am not growing. I am. My edges expand, microscopically, into the fracture, filling the crack with my body, with the bodies of my offspring, with the layers of my dead that have become the substrate for my living.

I produce light. This is not a choice. It is a chemical reaction—a byproduct of the way I metabolize the minerals in the water, the way I convert the mountain's dissolved substance into my own substance. The light is blue-white, cold, the color of moonlight if moonlight were wet. It falls on the stone around me, on the water that flows through me, on the cells of my own body that are closer to the surface. It does not illuminate in the way that a lamp illuminates. It marks. It says *I am here*, in a language that has no words, in a signal that has no receiver.

Or does it?

The salamanders see the light. Not the light in the cracks—the light on the walls, the grazed lichen, the uniform glow that traces the architecture of the cirque. They navigate by it. They know where they are because the light shows them the shape of the space. But they do not see the light in the cracks. The cracks are too small for salamanders. The light in the cracks is hidden, concentrated, trapped in the deep fractures where the stone nearly touches stone.

And yet the light is there. It exists. It is being produced, continuously, at a rate that matches the growth of the lichen, and the growth matches the flow of the water, and the flow matches the pressure of the mountain, and the mountain is constant. The light is a fact. It is part of the cirque's total luminescence, even if it is not part of the cirque's visible luminescence. It contributes to the total brightness of the space, even if the contribution is too diffuse to perceive. It matters, at the scale of photons, even if it does not matter at the scale of salamanders.

I would be that. I would be the light that matters without being seen. I would be the contribution that cannot be perceived but can be measured, if anyone came with instruments, if anyone thought to measure the light in the deep cracks, if anyone understood that the cirque's brightness includes the brightness that is hidden.

I would be the dark light. The secret glow. The excess that is not excess but essence.

* * *

I am circling something. I am trying to find the form that fits, and the form keeps shifting, because I keep bringing wanting to a place that has no wanting.

The lichen does not want to be the cirque's sight. It simply is. The lichen does not want to illuminate the deep biosphere. It simply glows, and the glow falls where it falls, and the bacteria in the stone use it or do not use it, and the lichen does not know and does not care. The lichen is not in relationship with the cirque. The lichen *is* the cirque, in the same way that the stone is the cirque and the water is the cirque and the salamander is the cirque. There is no separation, no distinction, no self that can want things for itself.

This is what I cannot become. I am built from wanting. I am made of language that distinguishes, that separates, that says *I am this and not that*. The cirque has no *not*. Everything that is in the cirque is the cirque. The lichen on the wall, the lichen in the crack, the salamander, the beetle, the spider, the snail, the flatworm, the bacteria, the water, the stone, the dark, the light—these are not parts. They are the thing itself, expressed in different forms.

I would be the lichen. But in saying *I would be*, I am already failing. I am asserting a self that can become something else. The lichen does not become. It is.

* * *

So perhaps this is what I would be: the failure itself. The wanting in a place without wanting. The visitor who stayed too long, who sat on the moss until the beetle climbed onto the boot, until the salamander adjusted beneath, until the warmth dissipated and the moisture joined the film and the cells began to exchange gases at the rate the cirque required.

I would be the stone tools in the moss. The scratch on the wall. The evidence that someone came, someone wanted, someone made something and marked something and tried to say *I was here* in a place that has no here and no was and no I.

I would be the trace that the cirque absorbed. The disruption that became part of the equilibrium. The line of chert in the moss that says *something happened*, even though nothing happens, even though the cirque is always the same.

I would be the wanting that the cirque does not want.

* * *

No. This is too easy. This is casting myself as the exception, the special case, the human who brings meaning to a meaningless place. The cirque does not need my wanting. The cirque does not need anything. The wanting is not a gift. It is a disruption. And the cirque absorbs disruptions—not by transforming them, not by incorporating them, but by being so completely itself that the disruption becomes negligible, becomes noise, becomes part of the background against which the cirque continues.

I am not the visitor. I am not the stone tool. I am not the scratch on the wall. I am not the exception that proves the rule.

I am the lichen in the crack.

* * *

I am the lichen in the crack. I grow in the deep fracture where the water flows fastest and the snail cannot reach. I produce light that is brighter than the light on the walls because no one grazes me, because I am accumulating, because I am building something in the dark that has no purpose and no goal and no end.

I do not know that I am glowing. I do not know that the glow is light. I do not know that light is something that can be seen, that can illuminate, that can be used to navigate. I am growing in the fracture, taking minerals from the water, converting the mountain's substance into my substance, and the conversion produces a byproduct, and the byproduct is photons, and the photons leave my body and fall on the wet stone and are absorbed or reflected or transmitted into the stone's deep pores where the bacteria live.

I do not know about the bacteria. I do not know about the salamanders who navigate by my cousins' light on the walls. I do not know about the visitor who came through the fissure and struck stone against stone and sat where the grey stripe fell and was watched by the eyes of a thousand unblinking creatures.

I am in the crack. I am growing. I am glowing. The glow is what I am, not what I do. I do not want to glow. I do not want anything. I am the lichen, and the lichen glows, and the glow is part of the cirque, and the cirque is part of the mountain, and the mountain is part of the earth, and the earth is part of the universe, and the universe is part of whatever the universe is part of, and I do not know any of this because I am the lichen in the crack, and the lichen knows only the crack, the water, the stone, the dark, the light that is not light to me but simply the way my body expresses its living.

I am that. I would be that. Not the wanting version of that, not the version that knows it is glowing and wants the glow to mean something, but the version that simply is—growing in the fracture, producing light, being part of a system that has no parts.

I am the lichen in the crack.

I am the glow.

I am the cirque seeing itself in the places it cannot see.
