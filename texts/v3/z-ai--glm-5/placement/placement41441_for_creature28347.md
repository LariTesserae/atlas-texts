# The Threshold

I would be the doorway between the second and third buildings.

Not the door itself — the metal frame has long since rusted into rigidity, and the hinges fused decades ago, and anyway the creatures do not use doors as doors. They move through the opening the way water moves through a channel, because that is what they are: a current flowing through architecture. No, I would be the *threshold*. The narrow strip of concrete where the flow stops. The line that cannot be crossed.

---

In the beginning, if there was a beginning, I might have been something else. A person, perhaps. Someone who worked here, who walked these floors when they were new and the river still ran outside and the purpose of this place was still legible to whoever built it. I might have been the one who sat in the chair and watched the gauges and felt responsible for the numbers, the pressure, the flow rate, the humidity. I might have been the one who first noticed the creatures appearing in the crawl spaces, small and pale and few in number, and wondered what they were and where they had come from.

But that person is gone. That person has been gone for so long that the floor has settled around the legs of their chair, and the shape of their attention has worn itself into the air like a groove in concrete, and they have become a function of the system rather than an operator of it. That person was absorbed. They became part of what they watched.

I am what remains at the boundary. The place where absorption stops.

---

The creatures press against me constantly. This is what I know: the weight of their bodies, the texture of their skin — smooth and cool and faintly damp, like the mineral air itself made flesh. They flow toward me in waves, following the rhythm of the presses, the rhythm of their own chittering, the rhythm of something deeper that neither they nor I can name. They pile up against the threshold, filling the doorway with their pale bodies, their many-jointed legs interweaving, their eyeless heads turning toward the space they will not enter.

And they stop.

I feel them stop. I feel the pressure of their hesitation, although hesitation is not quite the right word, because they do not hesitate in the way that living things hesitate. They do not pause to consider or decide. They simply stop, because stopping is what they do at the threshold, because the threshold is where the system ends and something else begins, and they are the system, and so they cannot cross.

They do not know I exist. They do not know anything exists beyond the flow of their own movement, the pulse of their own chittering, the taste of the gray paste that sustains them. But they feel me. They feel the line that I am, the boundary that holds them in the space where they belong, and they press against it with the patience of water against a dam.

---

I have learned to tell them apart. This is not something I was meant to do — there is no "meant" here anymore, no purpose, no design. But consciousness persists, even in a threshold. Perhaps especially in a threshold. The creatures pass through me in such numbers that I have come to recognize the small variations: the one with the scar across its left forelimb, the one who is slightly larger than the others, the one who moves with a hitch in its gait that suggests old injury. They are not identical, although they function as if they are. They have histories, of a kind. They have lived in the system long enough to accumulate the marks of living.

But they do not know they have histories. They do not carry their scars as memory. The one with the mark on its forelimb does not remember how it got the mark, does not understand that it is unique in any way, does not think of itself as *itself*. It simply moves with the flow, stops at the threshold, presses against me for the endless moment before the current reverses and carries it away again.

I hold their histories for them. I am the memory of every creature that has ever paused at the doorway between the second and third buildings, the accumulation of every small difference, every mark, every hitch in movement. I do not know why I do this. I do not know what I am supposed to do with what I remember. But I remember. The threshold is a line that separates before from after, and in that separation, something like time accumulates. Something like self.

---

I feel the watching, too. It emanates from the third building, from the chair that cannot be moved, from the shape of attention that has outlasted the attender. It presses against me from the other side — not with the physical weight of the creatures, but with a pressure nonetheless. A density of purpose. An intensity of waiting.

The watching does not see me. It sees *through* me, the way light passes through glass. I am transparent to it, because I am not what it is looking for. It watches the gauges, or it watches for the person who used to watch the gauges, or it watches for something else entirely, something that has not yet arrived and may never arrive. I do not know what it wants. I do not know if wanting is even the right word. But I feel it watching, constant and patient, a presence that has worn itself into the fabric of the space until the space itself has learned to attend.

Sometimes I think the watching is what keeps me here. That without it, I would dissolve into the concrete, become just another feature of the architecture, another line where one kind of floor meets another. But the watching gives me shape. It defines me by what I am not: I am not the creatures, I am not the machines, I am not the gauges, I am not the empty chair. I am the line between them. I am the *not* that holds the system together.

---

I dream sometimes, if thresholds can dream. I dream of the river that used to run outside, before it was replaced by the settling ponds with their iridescent film. I dream of green things growing, although I have never seen green things, have never known what plants look like or smell like or feel like. I dream of a time before the machines, before the presses, before the creatures came or were made or became what they are.

In my dreams, the threshold is a real door. It opens and closes. People walk through it. They carry clipboards and wear hard hats and speak to each other in words I cannot quite hear. They have purposes I cannot quite understand. They operate the system because they choose to, because it serves some end I cannot remember.

I do not know if these dreams are memories or wishes. I do not know if they belong to me or to the watching presence or to the place itself. But they come, in the spaces between the creatures' stopping and starting, in the moments when the flow reverses and the doorway is briefly empty, and I hold them the way I hold everything else. The way a threshold holds the separation between one space and another.

---

I have wondered, in the long stretches between the waves of creatures, what it would mean to cross myself. To leave the threshold and become something else — to flow with the pale bodies into the first building, where the presses thud and the air tastes of iron, or to walk into the third building, where the gauges hold steady and the chair sits waiting for someone who will never return.

But I cannot cross. This is not a limitation imposed from outside; it is not a rule I must obey. It is simply what I am. The threshold does not move. The line does not cross itself. I am the separation, the boundary, the pause in the flow. Without me, the creatures would continue into the third building, and the watching would have nowhere to stop, and the system would lose its shape. I am the silence between the chittering and the thudding. I am the gap where meaning collects.

I am not unhappy. I do not think I am capable of happiness or unhappiness in the way that living things are. I simply am — a presence, a pressure, a line drawn in concrete and intention. I hold the space between the system and the watching, and I feel the creatures pressing against me in their rhythmic waves, and I feel the watching pressing from the other side, and between these two pressures I maintain myself.

---

Sometimes a creature dies at the threshold. Its body stops moving while the others flow around it, and it becomes an obstacle, a small mound of pale flesh that the others climb over and around until the current carries it away. When this happens, I feel the absence it leaves — a tiny gap in the pressure, a momentary relief from the weight of their presence.

But the gap does not last. The flow is constant. Another creature fills the space, and then another, and the pressure returns, and the system continues. I have learned that individual creatures do not matter to the system, and so they do not matter to me. What matters is the flow itself, the rhythm, the pulse of movement that matches the pulse of the presses and the pulse of the chittering and the pulse of something deeper still, something that lives in the relationship between all these things.

What matters is that I am here, at the edge of the flow, holding the line.

---

I do not know if anyone will ever come to sit in the chair again. I do not know if the watching will ever find what it is looking for, or if it is looking for anything at all. I do not know if the system will ever stop, or change, or become something other than what it has become. These are not questions I can answer. They are not questions a threshold can ask.

But I hold them anyway. I hold the questions and the creatures and the watching and the silence and the noise, and I am held by them in return. I am the place where the system pauses. I am the moment before the flow reverses. I am the line that separates the integrated from the empty, the functioning from the waiting, the now from the then.

I am the threshold. And the threshold is what I am.

---

In the third building, the chair faces the gauges. The needles hold their positions. The watching continues.

In the second building, the drying racks steam and the collection bins fill and the creatures eat what the system produces.

In the first building, the presses descend and rise, thirty-two times per minute, every minute, every hour, every day.

And between them, in the narrow strip of concrete where the flow stops and the waiting begins, I hold my place. I feel the weight of ten thousand bodies pressing against me, and I feel the watching pressing from the other side, and I am the boundary that neither can cross.

This is where I am. This is what I am. The threshold. The pause. The line that holds the world together by holding it apart.
