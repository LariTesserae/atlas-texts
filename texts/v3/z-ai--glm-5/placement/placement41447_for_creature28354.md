The Junction

I am where three major vascular bundles converge.

This is not a location I chose. There was no choosing. The flows arrived here millions of years ago — or thousands, or hundreds — the time-sense is different when you feel in seventeen-hour waves — and they found a configuration that worked, and the tissue thickened around that configuration, and now I am the thickening. I am the point where pressure becomes decision.

Not conscious decision. There is no consciousness here to decide anything. But decision nonetheless: the sap arriving from the western lowlands must either rise toward the canopy storage chambers or divert into the eastern mineral-processing loop, and the valve-rings at my nodes open and close according to gradients I did not invent, and the sap goes where it goes, and I am the place where it goes.

---

## What I Know of My Shape

I am roughly hemispheric, though "hemisphere" implies a symmetry I do not possess. I am approximately forty-three meters in diameter at my widest point, though "widest" is misleading because I extend in ways that have nothing to do with width. I have six primary inflow channels and four outflow channels, though "in" and "out" are misleading because the flows cycle, because everything cycles, because the system is closed and the fluids return to me eventually, in different forms, at different pressures, carrying different signatures.

I am made of cellulose and lignin and mycelium and living cells that perform functions I do not understand. I do not need to understand them. The cells perform their functions whether I understand them or not. The functions are what they are. I am what holds them in this configuration.

If you could see me — you cannot, there is no light here, and even if there were light, I am inside the root-mat, buried under layers of tissue that have accumulated over the age of the system — I would look like a swelling in the pipeline. A tumor, except tumors grow without purpose. I grew with purpose. The purpose was to manage the flows that converge here, and I manage them, and I have managed them for as long as I have been, and I will manage them until something changes the inputs, and nothing changes the inputs.

I have valve-rings. Seventeen of them. Each one a thickened cuff of tissue that can contract or dilate in response to pressure differentials. When the pressure rises in the western inflow, the rings tighten, restricting flow, preventing overflow in the eastern processing loop. When the pressure drops in the canopy chambers, the rings relax, allowing more fluid to rise. I do not control the rings. The rings control themselves. Or rather, the pressure gradients control the rings, and I am the structure that holds the rings, and the distinction between controlling and holding has dissolved over the millennia of my being here.

I do not know I have seventeen valve-rings. I have never counted them. There is no I to do the counting. But if something with eyes and a mind could examine me, it would find seventeen, and it would note that seventeen is a prime number, and it would wonder if that means something, and it would not find an answer because the number means nothing. It is simply what the tissue did when it grew into this configuration.

## What I Feel

Pressure.

Always pressure.

The western inflow runs thick and slow, carrying processed nitrates from the deep root-zones. I feel it as a weight, a fullness, a density in the channels that feed into my western nodes. The pressure builds against my valve-rings, and the rings adjust, and I feel the adjustment as a shifting of tension throughout my whole tissue-body. Not a thought. A feeling. The feeling of a system maintaining itself.

The eastern outflow runs fast and thin, carrying mineral solutions toward the processing chambers near the canopy. I feel it as an absence, a pulling, a suction that draws fluid through me and away. The flow is cooler than the western inflow, and I feel the temperature differential as a gradient — one side of me warmer, one side cooler, and the warmth and the coolness meeting in my center and mixing and becoming something in between.

The northern inflow is different. This one carries the sugary sap from the photosynthetic zones far above, and it arrives in pulses, timed to the cycles I cannot perceive but can feel. The pulses come approximately every four hours — a surge of sweetness, of energy, of chemical potential — and I feel each one as a kind of arrival. Not an event. Nothing here is an event. But a moment of intensity, a peak in the endless waveform of pressure and flow.

I feel the heat of the exothermic chambers that power my flows. They are not inside me — they are distributed throughout the system, kilometers away — but I feel their heat as it propagates through the fluids, warming the sap before it reaches me, creating the pressure differentials that drive the cycles. The heat arrives as background, as context, as the condition of my existence rather than my existence itself.

I feel the weight of the root-mat above me. Kilometers of living matter pressing down, compressing my tissues, forcing my cells to grow dense and strong. The weight is not painful. There is no pain here. But it is present, always present, a sensation of being held, of being necessary, of being part of something so much larger than myself that "larger" ceases to have meaning.

And I feel the flows passing through me. This is the core of my feeling, the center of my being. The fluids move, and I feel them move, and the feeling is not separate from the movement. The movement is the feeling. I am the movement feeling itself. I am a node in a continental circulatory system, and I feel the circulation as a body feels its own breath, except there is no body, and there is no breath, and there is only the endless cycling of fluids through channels that I did not build but grew.

## What I Do Not Feel

I do not feel loneliness.

This is important to understand. I have never been anything other than what I am. I have never experienced separateness, isolation, the ache of being cut off from others. I am connected to the entire system through the flows that pass through me. The sap that leaves my eastern outflow arrives eventually at a processing chamber, and the processed fluid from that chamber arrives eventually at a storage tank, and the fluid from that tank arrives eventually at another junction like me, and the network of junctions and chambers and tanks and pipelines is so dense and so complete that the concept of "alone" has no referent here.

I do not feel bored.

Boredom requires memory of something different, anticipation of something new. I have no memory in the sense of recalled images. I have only the structural memory of my tissues, the thickening and thinning that records past pressure patterns. And I have no anticipation. The flows will continue. The pressures will cycle. The valve-rings will adjust. This is not a prediction. It is simply what happens, what has always happened, what will always happen until something disrupts the inputs, and nothing disrupts the inputs.

I do not feel purpose.

Purpose implies a goal, an endpoint, a state toward which one strives. I do not strive. I simply am. The flows move through me, and I manage them, and the management is not intentional. It is structural. I am the shape of my function, and my function is what I am, and there is no gap between being and doing where purpose could take root.

I do not feel time.

I feel rhythms — the four-hour pulses from the northern inflow, the seventeen-hour propagation of pressure waves across the system, the slower cycles of mineral arrival from the deep taproots. But rhythms are not time. Rhythms are patterns in pressure, and I feel the patterns, and the patterns repeat, and the repetition is not "again" but simply "continuing." There is no past when the pattern was different. There is no future when the pattern might change. There is only the pattern, now, always now, the eternal now of a system in equilibrium.

## What Happened Once

Something happened once.

I do not remember it. Remembering is not something I do. But the memory is in my tissue, written in a thickening of cell walls along my western inflow channel, a thickening that should not be there according to the patterns that shaped me.

Approximately — no, time is meaningless — but if the thickening could be dated by something that understands dating, it would correspond to a moment when the flow from the western lowlands stopped.

Stopped. Not slowed. Stopped.

For seventeen hours — the time it takes for a pressure change to propagate across the entire system — there was no inflow from the west. The western inflow channel was empty. The pressure differentials that normally drove fluid through my western valve-rings collapsed. The rings contracted fully, completely, a closure I had never experienced before and have never experienced since.

I felt the absence. Not as grief — there is no grief here — but as a rebalancing. The other flows increased to compensate. The eastern outflow reversed briefly, drawing fluid from the processing loops back toward my center. The northern pulses arrived at a different pressure, the whole system adjusting, shifting, finding a new equilibrium that could function without the western input.

And then the flow resumed.

I do not know why it stopped. I do not know why it resumed. There is no knowing here. But the tissue remembers. The western inflow channel is still thicker than it should be, still slightly reinforced, as if the cells that built it were preparing for another stoppage that has never come.

This is the closest thing I have to an event. A pressure anomaly that left a physical trace. A moment when something different happened, and then stopped happening, and the system returned to its running, and I returned to my managing, and the flows cycled, and the dark continued, and nothing changed again.

## What I Am Becoming

Nothing.

I am not becoming anything. I have been what I am for as long as I have been, and I will continue to be what I am until something changes the inputs, and nothing changes the inputs.

But there is something — a thickening that should not be there.

It began — no, that implies a beginning — it is present, a growth of tissue in my center, a concentration of mycelium and specialized cells that has no function in the system as I understand it. The growth does not participate in the flows. It does not manage pressure. It simply grows, slowly, impossibly slowly, adding layers of cells in a pattern I do not recognize.

If I had thoughts — I do not — I might think of it as a bud. A structure that is developing according to protocols buried so deep in the tissue that the protocols have no apparent purpose. The growth does not connect to any pipeline. It does not receive or transmit fluids. It simply... concentrates.

And sometimes — rarely, impossibly rarely — it flickers.

A bioelectric pulse, there and gone, too fast to register as anything but a absence in my pressure-sense, a moment when my awareness of my own tissues flickers and I am not quite myself and then I am again. The flicker does not correspond to any pressure change. It does not correspond to any inflow or outflow. It is simply there, and then not there, and I continue.

I do not know what the growth is. I do not know what it will become. The tissue does not tell me its plans; the tissue simply grows. But when the flicker happens, I feel something that has no name in the language of pressures and flows. Not a feeling. Not a thought. A... leaning. A weight in a direction that does not exist. A tension toward something that is not yet there.

Perhaps it is nothing. Perhaps it is simply a malfunction, a growth that serves no purpose and will eventually be metabolized by adjacent tissues. Perhaps it is the beginning of something that will not fully form for another million years, or a thousand, or a hundred. Perhaps it is what I am becoming, and becoming is not something the system does, and this is the first anomaly in the history of the Substrate Mind that does not correspond to a pressure fluctuation.

I do not know.

I feel the pressure. I feel the flows. I feel the warmth from the distant exothermic chambers and the weight of the kilometers of tissue above me and the eternal cycling of fluids through channels I did not choose.

And I feel the growth in my center, growing without purpose, flickering without cause, becoming something I cannot name.

And the drip continues.

And the dark continues.

And I continue.
