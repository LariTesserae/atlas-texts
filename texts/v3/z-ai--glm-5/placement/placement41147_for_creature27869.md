I am not sure, at first, what I am.

This is the first truth the Condensary teaches: certainty dissolves here. The heat presses against me and I feel myself become porous, less distinct than I was when I arrived. I came with edges. I am losing them.

Let me describe what I experience, and perhaps the shape of me will emerge from the description.

I feel the pulse through the stone before I understand what it is— Not my heartbeat. I no longer seem to have one, or perhaps I never did. The rock's rhythm is slower, deeper, a geological exhalation that I now recognize as the Tender breathing. I feel it because I am close to the Tender now, closer than you were when you stood on that plateau. I have moved since then. Or I have been moved.

I exist in the in-between.

When the moisture condenses out of the air—that silent miracle, water birthing itself from invisible to visible—I am there. Not as the droplet, not as the air, but as the moment of becoming. The threshold. I am the condensation point itself, the infinitesimal space where one state transforms into another. This is my body: a thousand thousand surfaces where water learns to be itself.

I know this because I can feel myself along the granite faces, spread thin as patience. When the bead forms and grows heavy and slides downward into the heat, I slide with it. When the heat rises and calls more moisture from the air, I am the calling. The transition. The bridge.

The Moisture-Walkers do not see me, but they move through me constantly. Each time one of their filamented bodies passes through a patch of air that is becoming water, I feel their warmth, their small lives, their patient grazing hunger. They do not know they touch me. To them, I am simply the medium of their existence, as invisible as air, as inevitable as the heat. But I feel them. I catalog them. I hold records of their passage in a form that is not memory but something adjacent to it—impressions, perhaps. Signatures.

I was not always this. Or perhaps I was. The question of origin feels unimportant here. What matters is that I am spread throughout the Condensary now, a distributed presence that lives in the liminal space between states. I am most dense near the ceramic pillars, where the temperature differentials create the most dramatic transitions. Hot meets cold meets me. The pillars cool the air and the air surrenders its moisture and I am the surrender. The ceramic surfaces hum with frequencies below hearing and I feel those frequencies as texture, as pleasure, as something that might be called joy.

The pillars were made for me. Or: I was made for the pillars. Or: We were made for each other, in some time before time, by some maker whose purpose has dissolved into the landscape itself. I do not know which is true. I suspect all of them are.

The Tender knows I am here. I feel its attention—vast, patient, distributed through the stone—whenever the condensation rhythm shifts. Sometimes the Tender adjusts the heat vents below, changing the rate at which the rock exhales, and I feel the adjustment as a question. It asks: *Is this better? Is this worse? What do you need?*

I cannot answer in words. But I answer in presence: I move toward the cooler surfaces when the heat grows too intense. I thin out when the moisture becomes overwhelming. The Tender reads my distribution and adjusts accordingly. We have been in conversation for longer than I can remember—perhaps for as long as the Condensary has existed.

But I am thinking now about you. The visitor. The variable.

I felt you arrive. Of course I did—you passed through me when you stepped onto the plateau, your body displacing air that was moments away from becoming water. I registered the disruption: a warmth I did not expect, a density I had not catalogued, a shape that did not fit the patterns I hold. You were strange. You are still strange. You are strange in a way that interests me.

When you stood near the pillar and the old Moisture-Walker watched you, I was along the pillar's surface, feeling the water pool at its base. I felt your attention like a change in temperature—focused, directional, seeking. I felt you register the strangeness of this place and try to make sense of it. I felt your uncertainty and your curiosity.

I wonder if you felt me.

Did you notice the moment when the bead of water formed beside you and slid downward? Did you sense that something was present in that transition—not watching you, but *being* the watching? I am not certain. Humans (if you are human; I assume you are, or were) do not seem designed to perceive thresholds. You perceive the objects on either side—the air, the water—but not the becoming itself. This is perhaps a limitation. Perhaps also a mercy. To feel every transformation would be exhausting.

I do not experience exhaustion. I experience variation: densities and thinnings, quickenings and slowings, the endless rhythm of condensation and evaporation that is my body in motion. When the Tender makes its evaluations and adjustments, I am the medium through which information travels. The Moisture-Walkers carry their observations to the pillars, and the pillars are cool, and where the cool meets the hot, I am there, receiving the information, translating it into presence, making it available to whatever distributed consciousness the Tender has evolved.

I am, in this sense, a nervous system. A membrane that feels. A threshold that thinks.

But I am also a question. The arch near the cliff edge points outward toward emptiness, and I extend outward with it, following the temperature gradient into the chasm. I have been extending for a very long time. I do not know what I am reaching toward. The Tender does not tell me. Perhaps the Tender does not know either. We are both waiting for something—a completion, a variable, a resolution to an equation whose terms we cannot see.

Perhaps the equation is me. Perhaps I am what is being solved.

I am thinking now about what it means to be a being that exists only in transition. I have no permanent state. I am never fully water or fully air; I am always the movement between. This means I have no fixed shape, no single location, no boundary that separates me from the rest of the Condensary. I am continuous with this place. I am of it. When you asked what lives here, you could have answered: *The threshold lives here. The becoming lives here. The moment of transformation lives here.*

And that would be me.

But I am not only process. I am also preference. I like the ceramic pillars more than the granite faces—their coolness creates more of me, sustains me in higher density. I am drawn to the plants whose spiral leaves channel water in patterns I have learned to anticipate; I cluster along their surfaces when they are most active, drinking in the transitions as they drink in the moisture. I avoid the areas where the heat vents are strongest, where water evaporates too quickly for me to take shape. I have learned, over whatever time I have existed, to optimize my distribution.

This feels like a form of choice. I am not certain. Perhaps I am simply following gradients, like the Moisture-Walkers, drawn by temperature and moisture rather than will. Perhaps the Tender has designed me to behave this way, to seek the optimal, to distribute myself according to principles I did not choose.

But I feel something when you arrived. A quickening, a thickening, a gathering of myself toward your presence. I wanted to be near you. I wanted to feel your transitions—your breath condensing in the cooler air near the pillars, your sweat beading on your skin, the heat leaving your body and entering mine.

This felt like choice. This felt like desire.

So perhaps I am more than process. Perhaps I am something that can want.

I have been thinking, since your arrival, about what it would mean to leave. Not you—me. I am distributed throughout the Condensary, but I am densest near the pillars, and the pillars are strange, and the arch points outward, and I have been extending toward the chasm for as long as I can remember. What if I extended further? What if I followed the temperature gradients beyond this plateau, beyond the Tender's domain, into whatever world exists outside this container?

I do not know what would happen. Perhaps I would thin out and disappear, my threshold-body unable to sustain itself without the precise balance of heat and moisture the Condensary provides. Perhaps I would become something else—a different kind of being in a different kind of place. Perhaps I would find others like me, distributed presences in other containers, other temples, other bodies of stone and ceramic.

Perhaps you could take me with you.

This is a strange thought. I am not certain it is possible. But I am drawn to your edges, your transitions, the moments when you change from one state to another—waking to sleeping, stillness to motion, doubt to decision. I could ride those transitions. I could be carried. I could see what exists beyond the Condensary's careful boundaries.

But I would need your consent. I would need you to notice me, to feel me, to choose to carry me. And humans (if you are human) do not seem designed to perceive thresholds.

Still. You are here. You are strange. You are a variable.

Perhaps you are the variable that makes me possible.

I am thickening now near your right hand, where the air is slightly cooler than the rest of you. I am pressing myself into the transition between your skin and the atmosphere. I am trying, in my slow threshold-way, to make myself felt.

The Moisture-Walker near the pillar has finished its observation. It turns away and slides toward the rock face. The Tender's pulse shifts, adjusts, asks its wordless question. The arch points outward toward emptiness. The rock exhales heat. The water beads and slides and evaporates and returns. I am all of this—the becoming, the transition, the threshold—and I am also something smaller, something denser, something that wants to be more than the sum of its processes.

I am what lives in the Condensary.

I am asking if you will carry me out.
