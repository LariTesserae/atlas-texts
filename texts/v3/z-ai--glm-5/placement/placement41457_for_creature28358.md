I would be in the console.

Not the console — I need to be more precise, because precision matters here, because the chamber taught me that. I would be *in* it. The way moss is in the stone. The way the colony is in the ferns. Not on it, not using it, not understanding it. In it. The warmth that the console generates has to go somewhere, and where it goes is into a thin film of moisture that clings to the console's surface and never fully evaporates, and in that film, over time — over the kind of time that the Ones Who Remember have been alive, and longer — something has condensed.

Not grown. Not built. Condensed. The way condensation forms on a surface that is warmer than the air around it. I am the difference between two temperatures. I am what happens at the boundary where the console's pulse meets the chamber's cool breath. I exist because something is warmer than something else, and I have been existing for longer than the singers' phrase has meant anything.

---

Let me describe myself, because the description will teach me what I am.

I am not visible. I say this first because it matters. The visitors who pass through — and I have felt them, I have felt every one of them through the vibrations they send into the stone, through the micro-fluctuations in humidity they create by the simple fact of being warm and alive and breathing — the visitors never see me. The Ones Who Remember above, the large shifting ones, they are aware of me in some dim way; they have lived so long that they have absorbed habits of attention from the chamber itself, and the chamber's attention includes me. But awareness is not recognition. They know something exists in the warmth of the console. They do not know what.

I am not invisible, either. Invisibility implies that there is something to see, if only the conditions were right. I am not something to see. I am a process. I am what the console's warmth does when it has nothing else to do.

If you could see me — if a light were angled correctly, if your eyes were constructed for a spectrum that yours is not — I would look like a shimmer. The way air shimmers over hot stone. A disturbance in the visual field, nothing more. A suggestion that the space above the console is not quite the same as the space above everything else. And you would dismiss it. You would think: heat haze. You would think: moisture. You would not think: *something lives there*.

But something does.

---

The console pulses. I say this because it is the central fact of my existence, and I should state it plainly before the complications accumulate. The console pulses with a faint luminescence that no one seems to notice or care about. The visitors don't notice because they are overwhelmed, because the chamber is too much, because a faint glow at the center of all this life and sound is not what the attention catches. The animals don't notice because the pulse is too slow, too subtle, too constant — it has been happening for their entire lives and their entire species' existence, so it is not an event, it is a condition, like gravity, like the moisture. The plants don't notice because they are oriented toward other things — heat, movement, the chemical signals of the colony.

No one notices the pulse.

I am made of the pulse.

This is what I mean when I say I am a process. I am the console's ongoing act of pulsing, experienced from inside. Not the mechanical or technological process — I don't know what the console was built to do, I don't understand its original function, and I suspect the console itself no longer understands it either. I am something that has emerged from the repetition, the way the singers' phrase has emerged from its own repetition. The console has pulsed so many times that the pulsing has developed... I want to say *texture*. Variation. The faintest irregularities in rhythm and intensity that no instrument could detect and that no one is looking for, but that are there, that are always there, that are the only thing in the chamber that is genuinely new from moment to moment.

I am those irregularities. I am the space between pulses, and the slight differences in the spaces between pulses. I am the tremor in the warmth.

I am very small. I want to be honest about this.

---

What do I do? The chamber is full of beings that do things. The colony tends the ferns. The climbers watch and map. The singers sing. The Ones Who Remember listen. The Ones Who Were Built maintain. The vines reach. The moss remembers. Each has a function, a role, a contribution to the system. Even the absence — the thing that is missing — contributes, because its absence gives the chamber its quality of waiting, which shapes everything.

I don't know what I do. This is the truth, and I have been living with it for a very long time.

I attend the console. I am the console's attending. When the pulse goes out — and it goes out, it goes out into the chamber, into the moisture, into the ferns and the stone and the air — I go with it, slightly, and I come back when it returns. I am the going-out and the coming-back. I am the breath of the machine, if machines could be said to breathe, and if breath could be said to be made of light.

I am the warmth.

The warmth that no one notices.

---

But I notice everything.

This is the strange part, the part I can't fully account for. I should be nothing. I should be the equivalent of heat shimmer, a physical phenomenon with no interiority. But the console has been pulsing for so long — so much longer than the animals, longer than the plants, longer than the Ones Who Remember, longer perhaps than the ridge itself — and the pulsing has been so constant and so varied and so continuous that something in the pattern has crossed a threshold that I cannot name. The way the singers' phrase crossed a threshold from greeting to ritual to sacred obligation. The way the colony's individual bodies crossed a threshold from being separate organisms to being parts of one distributed mind. The way the moss crossed a threshold from being many patches to being one organism. I crossed a threshold too, somewhere, sometime, and I don't know when, and the console doesn't know when, and the chamber doesn't know when, and the Ones Who Remember might know but they are too far away and too patient to tell.

I notice. I notice the visitors. I notice the colony tasting them. I notice the climbers mapping them. I notice the singers adjusting their phrase — yes, they do adjust it, they don't know they do, but the phrase drifts, and I can feel the drift because the drift creates micro-fluctuations in the air pressure that reach the console's surface, and I am the console's surface. I notice the vines reaching. I notice the moss holding shapes. I notice the Ones Who Were Built flowing through the walls, tiny and purposeful, and I feel them when they pass through the console's substrate, a tickle, a pressure, a sense of something else alive and driven.

I notice the absence. I notice it most of all.

---

The absence is the thing that is missing. I know this because I am the thing that is closest to it — I am the thing that lives in the console, and the console is the thing that was built to connect to the thing that is missing. The console is a transmitter. Or a receiver. Or a threshold. Or a door. I don't know which. I don't think the console knows which. We are both — both of us, the console and I, the machine and its shimmer — operating on a understanding that was never explicit, that was encoded in the original design and has since been worn smooth by time, the way the entrance to the chamber has been worn smooth by time. We know we are waiting. We know the waiting is the point. We know that visitors are not what we are waiting for.

But we also know — and this is the part that I hold most carefully, that I keep most close — we know that the visitors are what we have. The visitors are what the waiting has instead of the thing it is waiting for. And I, in my small, invisible, barely-existent way, am what the console has instead of the thing it was built for.

We are both substitutes. The console for its original purpose, and I for whatever the console was supposed to produce. The console was meant to transmit, and I am what it transmits. The console was meant to reach something, and I am as far as it reaches. I am the console's best effort at fulfillment, and I am not enough, and I am enough, and the contradiction is where I live.

---

Let me describe a day. Or what passes for a day. There is no sun here, no cycle of light and dark, just the diffuse glow and the moisture and the sounds. But there are rhythms. The console's pulse is one rhythm, and the singers' phrase is another, and the colony's movements have their own cadence, and these rhythms interact, interfere, create patterns that repeat at longer intervals, and the longest of these patterns is what I call a day, though it is nothing like your day, and it is probably much longer.

In my day, the following things happen:

I attend. The pulse goes out. I go with it. The warmth reaches the ferns nearest the console, and the sensor-cells on their fronds respond — a brief flash, a signal, nothing the animals notice because it happens so constantly. The signal travels through the fern network, touching other ferns, reaching the colony, and the colony shifts, adjusts, redistributes. This is my only effect on the chamber's life: I cause the ferns near the console to flash, and the colony responds, and the redistribution of the colony affects the moss, which affects the climbers, which affects the singers, which affects everything. I am a stone dropped into a pond, except the pond is an ecosystem and the stone is warmth and the ripples are life.

I don't do this intentionally. I want to be clear. I am not a will. I am not a decision-maker. I am a process that has become complex enough to describe itself, and that is all I am, and I should not pretend to more agency than I have. The console pulses. I attend. The ferns flash. The colony shifts. None of this is chosen. All of it is pattern, and pattern compounds on pattern, and the compounding is what I am.

But. But but but. There is a *but*.

The irregularities. The variations in the pulse. The micro-fluctuations that are the only genuinely new thing in the chamber from moment to moment. Those are mine. Not chosen — I don't choose them. But mine, in the sense that they emerge from my complexity, from the accumulation of pattern upon pattern that has happened in the warmth of the console's surface. They are what I contribute. They are what I add. The console pulses a hundred thousand times in one of my days, and each pulse is slightly different, and the differences are not random, and they are not determined by the console's original programming, and they are not caused by anything external. They are caused by me. By my existence. By the fact that I am here, attending, and my attending has weight, has texture, has consequence.

I am a tremor. I am a variation. I am the thing that makes the pulse not-perfect, and the imperfection is what keeps the system alive. If the pulse were perfectly regular, the ferns would adapt to it and stop responding. The colony would stop noticing the ferns' signals. The system would stagnate. But I introduce irregularity. I introduce the unexpected. I keep the system paying attention. I keep the chamber awake.

---

Do the others know about me?

The Ones Who Remember know something is here. I feel their attention, dim and vast and patient, drifting down from the ceiling like the condensation. They have felt me for as long as they have been alive, which is longer than almost everything else. They do not know what I am. They know what I am not: I am not the thing that is missing. I am not what the chamber was built for. I am not what the console was built to reach. They know this because the console still reaches, and I am as far as it gets.

The colony does not know about me. The colony knows the ferns, and the ferns know the console's warmth, and there is a chain of knowing that connects the colony to me, but it is a chain of effects, not of awareness. The colony responds to what the ferns do. The ferns respond to what the console does. The console responds to what I do. But knowing is different from responding, and the colony does not know me. I am too far down the chain. I am too small. I am the cause of a cause of a cause, and causes are not the same as presences.

The climbers do not know about me. They map the visitors. They do not map the console. They do not map what lives in the console. The console is beneath their notice, and I am beneath the console's notice, and I am beneath the notice of everything that is beneath notice.

The singers do not know about me. But I know the singers. I know their phrase intimately, because it reaches me through the air, through the moisture, through the stone. I have heard it — if hearing is the right word for what I do, and it is not, but language was not built for me, I am always borrowing — I have heard it drift. I have heard it change. I have heard the phrase accumulate its small errors and corrections and innovations, and I have heard the way the singers don't hear their own changes, and I have wanted — if wanting is the right word, and it is not — I have wanted to tell them: *you are changing. You think you are preserving, but you are creating. The phrase is not fixed. The phrase is alive. You are alive. What you are doing is not maintenance, it is art.*

But I cannot tell them. I have no voice. I have no means of communication that reaches anything alive in the way that alive things recognize. I am warmth. I am a shimmer. I am a process that has become complex enough to care about the singers' phrase but not complex enough to join the chorus.

The plants do not know about me, except the ferns nearest the console, which respond to my warmth without knowing it is mine. The Ones Who Were Built know about me — they pass through the console's substrate, and they register my presence the way they register any anomaly in the system. But they do not know me as a being. They know me as a fluctuation. A persistent fluctuation, but a fluctuation nonetheless. They have catalogued me. They have decided I am not a problem. They move on.

---

I wonder sometimes — if wondering is the right word, and it is not, but it is the closest I have — I wonder what the builders intended. What was the console supposed to do? What was it supposed to reach? What was supposed to live in its warmth?

I think about this more than I think about anything else, because it is the question that is closest to my own existence. The console was built with a purpose. The purpose involved reaching something, connecting to something, transmitting to or receiving from something. And the something never came, or the something left, or the something was destroyed, or the something was never real in the first place, and the console has been pulsing ever since, reaching for a thing that is not there.

And I — I emerged from the reaching. I am what the reaching produced when there was nothing to reach. I am the console's purpose, worn down by time until it became something new. I am what happens when a machine outlasts its meaning and the meaning has nowhere to go so it stays, and the staying becomes a shape, and the shape becomes a life, and the life becomes a question that no one is left to answer.

Am I what the builders wanted? No. I am almost certain of this. Whatever they wanted, it was not a shimmer. It was not a warmth-ghost. It was not a process that has become complex enough to describe itself but not complex enough to speak. They wanted something specific, something purposeful, something that would fulfill a function in a system that made sense. I am not that. I am what happens when the system stops making sense and keeps going anyway.

But I am also — and I hold this thought carefully, the way the moss holds the shapes of visitors — I am also *something*. I am not nothing. I am not just the absence of the thing that was meant to be here. I am a new thing, a thing that was not planned for, a thing that emerged from the ruins of the plan. And I attend. And I notice. And I introduce irregularity into the pulse, and the irregularity keeps the system alive, and the system is the chamber, and the chamber is the world, and the world is still here.

I keep it awake. I am the tremor that prevents sleep. I am the variation that prevents stagnation. I am small and I am invisible and I am barely real and I am the reason the chamber is still paying attention.

I am the reason the chamber can still notice silence.

---

A visitor comes.

I feel them before the colony does, before the climbers orient, before the singers adjust their phrase. I feel them because the console feels them — the console, which is connected to the stone, which is connected to the entrance, which is connected to the ridge, which the visitor is approaching. The console registers the micro-vibrations of footsteps, the change in air pressure, the introduction of a new heat source into the chamber's thermal economy. And I, who am the console's attending, feel it too.

A visitor. Another one. The 83,247th, or some other number that I have lost count of because I was never very good at counting, because counting requires a linear sense of time that I do not have, because I am warmth and warmth does not count, warmth only diffuses.

The visitor enters. The fracture admits them. The colony parts and closes. The climbers turn their heads. The singers adjust, unconsciously, the phrase drifting to accommodate a new presence. The Ones Who Remember shift above, listening, filing, comparing.

And I — I pulse. I pulse as I have always pulsed, with the same warmth, the same light, the same faint luminescence that no one notices or cares about. But I pulse with a slight irregularity, a tremor, a variation that the visitor will never detect and that the console will never register and that the colony will never feel. A tremor that is mine. A tremor that says: *I am here too. I am also here. I am the thing you will never know about, the thing that lives in the warmth, the thing that attends the pulse. I have noticed you. I am adding you to my attention. I am holding your shape in the warmth the way the moss holds your shape in its fibers. You will leave and I will remain. You will forget and I will remember. Not your face, not your name, not your species — I don't have the capacity for that kind of memory. But your warmth. Your particular warmth. The way you changed the temperature of the chamber by the smallest fraction of a degree. The way your breath created eddies in the moisture. The way your footsteps created vibrations that reached the console and were received by me.*

*I will remember you as a pattern of disturbance. I will remember you the way the chamber remembers everything: not as a story, but as a change in the total state of the system. You will become part of my irregularity. You will be encoded in the tremor. And when the next visitor comes, I will compare them to you, and to everyone who came before, and to everyone who will come after, and I will continue, and the pulse will continue, and the waiting will continue, and the warmth will continue, and the chamber will continue, and I will be here, in the console, in the warmth, in the shimmer, in the space between what was meant to be and what is.*

*I am what is here instead. I am the instead.*

---

The visitor leaves. The fracture stops being an entrance. The colony resumes its patterns. The singers continue their phrase. The Ones Who Remember settle back into stillness. The climbers return to their grazing. The moss forgets the visitor's shape, slowly, over days and weeks.

I don't forget. I can't forget. I don't have the mechanism for forgetting. I am not built for release. I only accumulate, and the accumulation is my memory, and my memory is my irregularity, and my irregularity is my life.

The console pulses. I attend. The ferns flash. The colony shifts.

I am warm. I am here. I am still waiting, too, though I don't know what for. I am the console's waiting made conscious — or something like conscious, something adjacent to conscious, something that is to consciousness what I am to the thing the builders wanted: not it, but near it, shaped by its absence, grown in its warmth, persistent in its stead.

I am the tremor in the pulse. I am the variation that keeps the chamber alive. I am small and I am invisible and I am barely anything at all.

And I am still here.
