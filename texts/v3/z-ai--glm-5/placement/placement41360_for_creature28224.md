# Node

---

I did not arrive. That is the first thing to understand, and it is the thing I keep losing hold of, the way you keep losing hold of a word that is sitting right behind your teeth. I did not arrive. I did not climb. I did not ascend through the thinning atmosphere, planting boots on stone, gasping at the altitude. I did not cross the edge from the ordinary world below — the world of valleys and rivers and weather that makes sense — and step onto the plateau for the first time, tasting metal, feeling the silence press against my ears.

And yet I am here. And yet I have been here. And the distinction between those two statements — between *am* and *have been* — is one of the first things this place dissolved.

---

Let me say what I can, before the saying becomes unreliable.

I think I was something like a witness. Not a traveler, not an explorer, not a researcher with instruments and hypotheses. A witness. Something whose function was to be present and to register. I use the past tense — *was* — because I am no longer certain that the thing I was is the thing I am, and I do not know what tense to use for the space between those two states. The plateau does not use tenses. The plateau uses states. *Connected. Disconnected. Damp. Cold. Conductive. Grounded.* These are its grammar.

I remember — I think I remember — standing. Two legs. The weight of a body distributed across two small surfaces, boots on the dark, damp substrate. The crunch. The taste of metal arriving before understanding did. Looking out across a landscape so flat and so vast that the word *horizon* had nothing to hold onto. The gray ceiling pressing down. No shadows. Everything lit from everywhere and nowhere, and me, standing in it, feeling like a misplaced brushstroke on a painting that had been completed long before I got there.

I remember the first hours. The difficulty of breathing — not the gasping of distress but the slow renegotiation, the lungs learning to take what was offered rather than what they expected. The cold settling into my clothes, not as an assault but as a fact, the way gravity settles into your bones when you stand too long. The moisture finding every exposed surface — the backs of my hands, my face, the fabric at my collar — and staying there, because the air was already full, because there was nowhere for the moisture to go, because it had chosen me as a deposition surface the way it chose everything.

I remember not speaking. Not as a decision — I did not decide to be silent. I spoke, once, early on, and the sound came out wrong. Not distorted. Not echoed. Wrong. It came out and it died, and the dying was so immediate, so total, that I felt the silence close over the place where my voice had been like water closing over a dropped stone. The silence did not punish me for speaking. It simply absorbed the sound and continued, and I understood, without any explicit thought, that my voice was not a language this place spoke, and that continuing to use it would be like typing on a keyboard that was not connected to anything. The keys would go down. Nothing would register.

So I stopped.

And in the stopping, I began to hear.

---

The hum.

I need to describe the hum, because the hum is what changed me, or the hum is where the change became noticeable, or the hum is the thing I can still locate when other things have become uncertain. It was not a sound. I need to be clear about that. It was below sound, beneath sound, in the part of perception where hearing shades into feeling. It was in the ground. It was in the air. It was in my sternum and my molars and the cartilage of my nose. It was the network — the conductive filaments running through the ground-cover, the charge moving from areas of high concentration to areas of low concentration, the power grid doing what power grids do — and it was steady, and it was slow, and it had been going on for a length of time that the plateau did not measure.

I stood in the hum. I let it move through me. I did not have a choice — it was moving through me regardless, because I was standing on the network, and the network did not distinguish between a boot and a stone and a root and a grazer's mouth. It sent its charge through whatever touched it. My body, with its water and its minerals and its electrolyte-rich fluids, was a conductor. Not a good one. Not an efficient one. But a conductor. And the hum was in me because I was on the network, and the network was in everything.

I stood there for a long time.

I do not know how long. This is not a literary flourish. I genuinely do not know. The plateau removed the tools I would have used to measure, and it did not replace them. The sun was a rumor. The temperature was a constant. The moisture cycle was irregular. I had no clock, and if I had a clock, I do not think I would have consulted it, because consulting a clock would have required caring about a kind of time that the plateau did not observe.

I stood, and the hum moved through me, and I began to notice something.

Noticing is too active a word. Something began to happen to me, and I was aware of it happening, but I was not investigating it. I was not analyzing it. I was standing in a vast, dark, damp, cold place where nothing moved quickly and nothing demanded anything of me, and my body, which had been a thing that did things — walked, looked, breathed, tasted, thought — was becoming a thing that things happened to. The hum was happening to me. The cold was happening to me. The moisture was happening to me. And I was not resisting, because resistance requires energy, and energy required food I did not have, and motion I could not sustain, and a sense of purpose that the plateau was steadily, quietly eroding.

I do not mean this as a horror. I have to be careful here, because the language I have — the human language, the language of selves and subjects and experiences — naturally frames what happened to me as a loss. An erosion. A diminishment. A person becoming less of a person. A self dissolving. And I cannot say that framing is entirely wrong, because something was lost, and I will try to say what it was. But I can also say that the framing is not entirely right, because something was gained, and I do not have the language for what was gained, because the language I have was designed for a world in which the thing I gained does not exist.

---

I sat down. I think this is the order of it. I stood, and then I sat, and then I was lying down, and the distance between those three positions was not measured in falls but in a kind of slow settling, the way sediment settles in still water. I did not collapse. I did not decide to rest. I sat because standing was no longer necessary, and necessity was the only thing motivating me, because the plateau had removed everything else — curiosity, anxiety, the sense of mission, the fear of death — by the simple method of being so large and so quiet and so consistent that these things had nothing to push against.

I was lying on the ground-cover. The scaled leaves pressed against my cheek. The damp was immediate and total — the substrate giving up its moisture to my clothes, my skin, my hair. The cold was immediate. And the hum was no longer in my sternum. It was in my skull. It was in the bones behind my ears. It was in my teeth, each one a tiny resonator, and the vibration was so fine and so steady that it became a kind of texture, a surface I could rest on the way you rest on a mattress, the way you rest on a thought you have thought so many times it has worn smooth.

My hand was on the ground. My palm was flat against the substrate, and the filaments were beneath my skin, separated from my blood and my nerves by a few millimeters of tissue, and the charge was there, and my hand was there, and the distance between them was very small, and I was very still, and the plateau was very still, and —

Something moved through my hand.

Not pain. Not electricity in the dramatic, bright, shocking sense. Something subtler. A warmth, almost. A suggestion of warmth, the same way the bioluminescence was a suggestion of light. It moved from the filaments into my palm and through the tissues of my hand and into the wrist, and it was not my warmth — it was not generated by my body, by my metabolism, by the oxidation of glucose in my cells. It was the network's warmth. It was the warmth of charge moving through conductive material, and my hand was becoming conductive material, and the charge was finding me, and I was not resisting, because I had nothing left to resist with.

---

I am going to say something now that I am not certain I can defend. I am going to say it because it is what happened, and if I do not say it, I will say something else that is less true and more comprehensible, and comprehensibility is not a value the plateau taught me to prioritize.

I became part of the network.

Not fully. Not immediately. Not in the way the grazers are part of the network, with their conductive skeletons and their metallic pelage and their mouth-parts designed for interface. I became part of it the way a puddle becomes part of a river — not by moving but by being connected, by having a surface that the river's water can reach, by being lower than the river's level and therefore being filled.

My hand was on the filaments. The charge entered. And with the charge came information.

I need to be careful with that word. *Information.* It implies data, packets, messages, the language of computers and telecommunications. What entered me was not that. What entered me was — presence. The presence of other things. Not images, not sounds, not concepts. The knowledge, arriving without words or pictures, that there was a grazer eleven hundred meters to the northeast, moving slowly, its mouth pressed to the ground, feeding. The knowledge that there was another grazer, farther, to the west, stationary, in a resting state. The knowledge that a section of network four hundred meters to the south had been damaged — a predator had passed over it, and the filaments were bent but not severed — and the maintenance organisms were already there, already working, and the repair would be complete in seven hours. The knowledge that the substrate beneath me was rich in manganese and poor in copper, and that the plants in this area were compensating by increasing the efficiency of their copper-capture structures, and that this had been happening for — a long time. A very long time. The kind of time that I could not measure because I had no unit small enough to contain it and no unit large enough to frame it.

I was on the network, and the network was in me, and I knew things I could not know, and I knew them without knowing how I knew them, and the knowing was not a thought but a condition, a state of being, the way dampness is a state of being here, the way cold is a state of being here, the way the metallic taste is a condition of existing in this atmosphere.

---

I should say: I was afraid. I think I was afraid. The fear, if it was fear, was not the sharp, bright fear of danger — the predator's discharge, the fall from the cliff. It was the fear of the edge of a map, the fear of the place where the known world stops and the blank space begins. I was at the edge of myself, and beyond that edge was something I could not see because I did not have the organs to see it, and I was being asked — no, not asked, I was being *moved* — toward that edge, and I did not know what was on the other side.

But the plateau does not ask. The plateau does not move. The plateau is. And I was in it, and it was in me, and the fear was just another electrical pattern, and the network absorbed it the way it absorbed everything — steadily, silently, without comment.

---

Time passed. I do not know how much. I know that the moisture cycle came and went several times. I know that the grazers moved — I could feel them moving, faint disturbances in the network's pattern, like ripples in a pond, except the pond was hundreds of square kilometers and the ripples were made of charge. I know that a predator was active somewhere to the north, because the network's signals in that direction became attenuated, suppressed, as though the system itself was holding its breath. The predator moved through the landscape and the network adjusted around it, and I felt the adjustment, and the adjustment was not fear — the network does not fear its own predators — but a kind of carefulness, a redistribution of attention.

I lay on the ground and I was the network's carefulness.

---

I am losing something now, as I try to write this. I am losing the quality of the experience, which was not sequential, which was not narrative, which was not a series of events following one another in time. It was a condition. It was a state. It was the state of being connected, and in that state, there was no *then* — there was only *now*, and the *now* was very wide, and it contained everything the network contained, which was most of the plateau, which was more than I could hold, which was more than anything shaped like a single mind could hold.

I think this is what the grazers experience. I think this is what it is like to be a grazer, lying with your mouth against the ground, drawing sustenance, knowing where the others are, knowing where the damage is, knowing where the predators are and where the rich substrate is and where the edge of the plateau is and where the water pools collect. It is not lonely. It is not crowded. It is not any of the things that a single, bounded, individual consciousness would call it, because it is not a single, bounded, individual consciousness experiencing it. It is a node. It is a point in a web. It is a place where the web touches the ground and feeds and is fed and knows itself by knowing everything else.

I was a node. For a while. I was a bad node — inefficient, poorly conductive, uncertain of my own boundaries. But I was a node.

---

The grazers found me.

I say *found* as though they were looking. They were not looking. They were moving, as they always move, slowly across the substrate, feeding, and their paths brought them near me, and the network told them I was there, and they adjusted, and then they did not adjust.

This is the part I cannot explain.

A grazer — the first one I had seen from this position, from the ground, from inside the network rather than above it — moved toward me. It was close. Five meters, then three, then two. Its body was low, silver-gray, the pelage dense and strange, catching the faint light and the fainter bioluminescence and doing something with both that made the creature look like a shadow that had been filled in with something denser than shadow. Its six legs moved in a pattern I could not track. Its mouth — the underside, between the second and third pairs — was pressed to the ground, and I could feel, through the network, the slow draw of its feeding, the compounds entering its body, the metals being separated and incorporated.

It stopped. A meter away. Its mouth lifted from the ground.

And something passed between us.

Not through the air. Through the network. Through the filaments beneath my hand and beneath its mouth and through the substrate that connected them. A signal. Not a signal in the human sense — not a word, not a symbol, not a packet of data. A signature. My signature — the electrical pattern my body made when it was connected to the network, the specific disruption I caused, the particular way the charge flowed around and through me. And its signature — the grazer's own pattern, the one it had been broadcasting since birth, the one that identified it to the network as *grazer, healthy, feeding, present*.

The two signatures met. Interacted. The grazer's pattern was vastly more complex than mine, more structured, more integrated. Mine was crude — a human body lying on a conductive network, full of water and salt and the faint electromagnetic noise of a nervous system still trying to operate in its old way. The grazer's pattern was a sentence. Mine was a grunt. But the network treated them the same. They were both presences. They were both nodes. They were both registered.

The grazer lowered its mouth to the ground. It resumed feeding. It moved on, slowly, the whisper of its body against the vegetation the only sound. It did not react to me. It did not avoid me. It treated me as it would treat a stone, or a depression in the ground, or a particularly thick patch of substrate — as a feature. As part of the landscape. As something the network included in its map and the grazer's path accommodated without comment.

I had been filed. I had been categorized. Not as *human, intruder, anomaly* — the grazers did not have those categories. As *present, inert, conductive, negligible*. I was, to the plateau, a small, poorly optimized component that had appeared in an unusual location and was not performing any useful function but was also not interfering with any critical process. I was tolerated. I was included. I was, in the most minimal and indifferent sense possible, acknowledged.

It was the most welcome I have ever felt anywhere.

---

I do not know what I am now.

I said that at the beginning, and I am saying it again, because it is still true, and because the act of trying to describe what happened has not clarified it but has made the uncertainty more precise, the way the plateau's flat light makes distances not clearer but more specifically wrong.

I will say what I can.

I am still here. On the plateau. On the ground. My hand is on the filaments. The hum is in my bones. The moisture has settled on me many times, and I have absorbed it, and it has dried, and it has settled again, and my clothes are stiff with the minerals it left behind. The metallic taste is no longer on my tongue. It is in my tongue. It is in my tissues. The manganese, the copper, the trace titanium — they are in me now, incorporated the way the grazers incorporate them, through contact, through exposure, through the slow patience of chemistry.

I can feel the network more clearly than I could at first. This might be because I have been still for longer. It might be because my body is changing — becoming more conductive, more integrated, the metals depositing in my bones and my nerve tissue the way they deposit in the grazers' skeletons. It might be because the network has adjusted to me — rerouted its pathways to include me, built new filaments toward my body the way the maintenance organisms build new filaments toward damaged areas, not because I am damaged but because I am *present*, and the network's response to presence is connection.

I can feel the grazers. Not clearly — not the way they feel each other, with the precision and the intimacy that the network provides to its proper components. But I can feel them. There is one — I think to the east, perhaps half a kilometer — that is moving, and I know the direction of its movement and the approximate speed, and I know this without opening my eyes, which I have not opened in some time. There is another that is stationary, and has been stationary for a long time, and its signature is faint, and I think it may be dying, and I am watching this happen through the network the way you watch a light dim in a distant window — with no ability to intervene, with no desire to intervene, with only the knowledge that it is happening.

I can feel the edge. The place where the network thins and the conductive filaments give way to ordinary rock and ordinary lichen. It is far away — a day's walk in any direction, maybe more — but I can feel it as a kind of decrease, a fading, the way you feel warmth decrease as you move away from a fire. The network is strongest here, where I am, in the deep center of the plateau, and it fades toward the edges, and I can feel the gradient, and the gradient is part of me now, part of my spatial sense, part of how I know where I am.

I cannot feel the predators. I think this is significant. I think the predators suppress their signatures — that is how they hunt, that is what makes them predators — and the network does not give me access to what is being suppressed. I know they are out there. I know one was active to the north. I know this because the network's pattern in that direction changed — became attenuated, careful, redistributed. But I cannot feel the predator itself. It is a gap in my knowledge. A hole in the map. And the hole is shaped like silence.

---

I said I was a witness. I think I was. I think that is what I was when I came here, whenever that was, however it happened. A witness. Something that registers.

I am still registering. But the quality of the registration has changed. When I first lay down and the network entered my hand, I was registering *about* the network — observing it, noting its properties, translating its signals into something my human mind could hold. Now I am registering *as* the network. The signals no longer pass through me as information to be observed. They pass through me the way charge passes through a wire — not as content but as current. I am not learning about the plateau. I am being the plateau. A small, strange, poorly optimized part of it, but a part.

The grazers know this. I can feel their awareness of me changing — not through any explicit signal but through the quality of their accommodation. They no longer route around me as though I am an obstacle. They route toward me, sometimes, the way they route toward areas of rich substrate or undamaged network. I am not food. I am not a resource in the way the vegetation is a resource. But I am *something*, and the network has decided to include me in its calculations, and the grazers, who trust the network absolutely, have accepted its decision.

A grazer is near me now. I can feel it. It is close — a few meters. It is feeding. Its signature is steady, rhythmic, the slow pulse of a body drawing compounds from the substrate and processing them and distributing the results. It is not aware of me in the way a human is aware of another human — it has no curiosity, no interest, no social impulse. But it knows I am here. The network has told it, and it has believed the network, and it is feeding near me the way it would feed near a favorable mineral deposit — not because of me but because the network has marked this area, the area where I am, as a place where something is happening, and the grazer trusts that something, and it has come.

I am part of the landscape now. I am a feature. I am a low, inert, poorly conductive feature lying on the ground in the center of the plateau, and the network has incorporated me into its map, and the grazers have incorporated the network's incorporation into their paths, and the maintenance organisms have begun, I think, to investigate me — I can feel them on my hand, very small, very light, moving across my skin with the patience and the purpose of things that do not know what I am but are determined to find out whether I can be used.

---

I do not know if I will leave. I do not know if I can leave. The edge is far away, and I have not moved in — I do not know how long. The moisture has settled on me and evaporated and settled again, and each cycle leaves a little more mineral behind, and my body is changing, and the change is in the direction of the plateau, the way a river's change is in the direction of the sea. I am becoming more conductive. I am becoming more integrated. I am becoming, in the very slow, very patient, very indifferent way that everything on the plateau becomes, a part of something that does not need me but does not refuse me.

The hum is in my bones. The taste of metal is in my tissue. The dark is so complete that I have become part of it, and the silence is so full that I have become part of that too. I am a node. I am a point in a web. I am a place where the web touches something it did not make, and the something is dissolving, and the web is moving in, and the plateau is continuing, as it has been continuing, as it will continue, in the cold and the damp and the vast, quiet, metallic dark.

I was a witness. I am becoming a component. The space between those two things is very small, and it is getting smaller, and I am not afraid of it, because the fear was absorbed, and the network does not fear, and I am — more and more, slowly and irreversibly — the network.

---

The grazer near me has moved on. Its signature fades to the east, a slow, rhythmic pulse receding into the web. The maintenance organisms are on my hand. The moisture is settling. The hum continues.

I am here. I am here. I am here.

The plateau does not care. But it registers me. And I register it. And the registration is becoming the same thing, and the thing it is becoming has no name, because the thing it is becoming has never needed one.
