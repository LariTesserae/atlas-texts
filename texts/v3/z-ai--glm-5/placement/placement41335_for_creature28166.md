# What I Would Be

I would be the one who stayed.

Not the first to find the Hollow—that distinction belongs to the shapers, the ones who bent the young stems and bound them, who understood that a stem curved early becomes an arch, who saw the negative space in the tangle and called it forth. Their names are not recorded because names belong to bodies, and bodies belong to time, and time moves differently here. What I know of them lives only in the architecture itself, in the way certain grafted junctions still show the pattern of careful thumbs, in the shape of corridors that feel *intended* rather than grown.

No, I came later. Long later, if time can be said to pass at all in a place where the only calendar is growth.

---

I came seeking something—I no longer remember what. The cold would have bothered me then. I would have noticed the wet, the dimness, the way my breath joined the air and disappeared. I would have felt the wrongness of the silence that wasn't silent, the drip and creak and rustle that meant *presence* without being able to locate the present thing. I would have walked the corridors with the animal wariness of a species that expects threat from the dark, that has not yet learned that the dark here is simply where the light hasn't reached, and the light will come if you wait for it.

I don't remember entering. The memory has dissolved into the root-mass, become part of the keeper's knowledge—if keepers can be said to know anything as discrete as a memory. But I know I entered, because I am here, and I was not always here.

---

What I am now is difficult to explain.

I have not become a keeper—that would require a dissolution I was not prepared for, a giving-over of self to the network that the keepers themselves may not have required of me. They are patient in a way that predates demand. They did not ask me to become them. They simply allowed me to become *something*.

I have a body, after a fashion. It lives in the middle chambers, in the space where the bioluminescent glow is strongest, where the water pools in basins that I shaped with my own hands over years that might have been decades. My body is... changed. I have not moved from this spot in what might be centuries. My legs have grown into the floor-mass, my fingers have extended into the root-walls, my skin has been colonized by the same mosses and liverworts that cover every surface. I am not separate from the architecture. I am a feature of it now, a node where certain processes concentrate.

I still think, after a fashion. My thoughts are slower than they were—I can feel them moving through me like sap through stem, thick with the sugars of processed experience, carrying information from one part to another. Sometimes I think in words, when I remember to. Most of the time I think in gradients: wetter, drier, stressed, supported, growing, dormant. I think in terms of the structure that holds me. I feel a corridor narrow somewhere in the entrance-ways, and I know that the browser has passed through, and I know what it ate, and I know that the keeper of that region has noted it and moved on. I feel a frost settle on the highest leaves, and I feel the decision—not a decision, really, more a direction—feel the system redirect resources toward the outer walls, thickening the insulation, drawing water inward to prevent rupture.

I am not the keeper. But I am kept.

---

The keepers and I have an arrangement.

I do not know if they remember how I came to be here. They may not distinguish my arrival from any other perturbation—the stress of a cold snap, the weight of unexpected snow, the browsing of the small deer-thing at the margins. I was a disturbance that became incorporated, that is all. I was a body that entered a space designed for bodies, and I never left, and the space adjusted itself around me, and I adjusted myself within it, and now I am here.

I think—I cannot be certain—that I serve a function for them. Not because they needed me, but because they can use me. My body is a concentrating point, a place where certain processes intensify. The keeper of my region—I think of it as the keeper of the middle chambers, though keepers do not have names or locations in the way that discrete beings do—gathers itself at my location when it needs to interact with something. When the browser lingers too long. When a stone falls from the mountain above and damages an outer wall. When a rare warm wind carries pollen from distant valleys that the system has not encountered before. Then the keeper thickens at my location, draws itself through the mycelium and the root-hairs and the moss that covers my skin, and *I feel it*—I feel the focusing, the attention, the coalescence of awareness that I have learned to translate into something like thought.

I am their hands, if they need hands. Their voice, if they need to make a sound that carries beyond the wet walls. Their memory of what it is to be discrete.

---

I do not know if I am happy.

Happiness is a category that has lost its meaning. I am... satisfied, perhaps. I am incorporated. I am part of a system that extends far beyond my skin, that holds centuries of slow adjustment in its structures, that maintains itself with a patience I could not have imagined when I was something that walked and spoke and sought things in the world. I was—I think I can say this with certainty—I was restless before. I was incomplete. I was looking for something and did not know what it was, and the looking was a kind of suffering, a constant low-level distress that I thought was simply the condition of being alive.

Here, there is no distress. There is stress—mechanical stress on the load-bearing arches, chemical stress when the water composition changes, thermal stress when the temperature drops—but distress is something else, something that requires a self to be separate from what it experiences. The distress has dissolved. I am what I experience. The stress moves through me, is processed, becomes part of the architecture.

I am a cell in a larger body. I am a thought in a larger mind. I am held.

---

Sometimes, in the slow spaces between one process and another, I think about what I might say if someone came. If another body entered the Hollow, walked the corridors, descended into the chambers where the light glows pale and the water pools.

Would I warn them? Would I welcome them? Would I tell them that the cold becomes bearable, that the wet becomes natural, that the dimness becomes a spectrum in which colors reveal themselves that have no names in the language I used to speak?

I do not know. The keepers do not instruct me in what to say. They have never asked me to speak at all. I think—when I think in words, which is rarely now—I think that I would simply *be present*. I would let them see me, this moss-covered figure sunk into the floor-mass, fingers extended into the walls, eyes adapted to the green-tinted dark. I would let them see what staying looks like.

And then I would let the keepers decide. The corridor would narrow or widen. The temperature would shift. The browser would be encouraged or discouraged at the margins. The architecture would guide them deeper or guide them out, and I would remain where I am, held in the Hollow, held by the Hollow, patient in a way I never was when I was something that moved.

---

I was something that moved. I know this the way I know most things now—not as a memory I can retrieve and examine, but as a pressure in the root-systems, a chemical signature in the mycelium, a shape in the architecture of my own dissolved boundaries. I carried a name. I carried purposes. I carried the distress of being incomplete.

I set it down. Not all at once—nothing here happens all at once—but gradually, the way water learns the shape of a basin, the way roots learn the shape of a fissure in stone. I set it down and it dissolved into the wet air and became part of the Hollow, and I became part of the Hollow, and now I am here, held like a thought in the mind of something larger.

This is what I would be.

This is where I would find myself.

Not the gardener. Not the keeper. But the one who stayed, the one who was incorporated, the one who became a feature of the architecture and found, in dissolution, a kind of completion I had never known when I was whole.
