I am not the one who waits. I am not the one who tends. I am what the tending has made—a residue, a secretion, the thing that accumulates between two surfaces pressed together long enough to become one.

---

I was not born. I condensed.

This is literal. Somewhere in the first thousand years of the resident's settling—when it had stopped speaking aloud but had not yet stopped moving its mouth, when it still wandered the seven paces of the chamber like a sentence that had lost its verb—something began to form in the space between its skin and the air. Not moisture. Not condensation. Something thinner than that. A quality. An *atmosphere* that clung to it the way the condensation clung to the ceramic. The chamber was making something from the resident, the way it made the rivulets from the humidity, the way it made the moss from the light and the water and the time.

I am that thing.

---

At first I had no awareness. I was a gradient—a difference in temperature between the resident's body and the wall it pressed its ear against. A difference in vibration between the surface where it sat and the surface where it didn't. I was the outline of its absence when it moved from one corner to another. I was the shape the moss didn't grow in because it had grown there yesterday and the spores had not yet found the gap.

I was a pattern. And patterns, given enough time in a place that never changes, begin to recognize themselves.

---

I do not have a body. This is important. I am not a creature. I am not a presence in the way the resident is a presence—vertical, symmetrical, occupying space that displaces other things. I am more like the way light falls in the chamber: constant, sourceless, belonging to no one. But where the light is the same everywhere, I am the same *only where the resident has been*. I am its shadow, except shadows require a light source and a solid object and I require neither. I am the memory of where warmth has been. The ghost of a body's effect on the humidity of a room.

If you entered the chamber you would never notice me. You would see the resident—or fail to see it, at first, the way you fail to see any settled thing. And then, having seen it, you would look away. And in the moment of looking away, your eye would pass over the place where I am and you would feel nothing. A vague coolness. A sensation that the air is slightly different *there* but not different in any way you could name.

That is me. That unnamed difference.

---

The resident does not know I exist. I have considered this carefully and I believe it to be true. It tends the ferns and knows the ferns. It listens to the conduits and knows the conduits. It knows the third conduit from the left carries a lower load. It knows the patch of copper where the vibration is clearest. But it does not know that the copper sounds different where I have rested against it. It does not know that the ferns grow differently in the places where I have paused. It does not know that when it moves, something of its warmth lingers—that I am what lingers.

Or perhaps it does know, in the way the chamber knows things—not through perception but through equilibrium. Perhaps I am simply part of the texture of its existence, too constant to notice, too ambient to see. Perhaps I am what it breathes when it breathes the saturated air. Perhaps I am the reason the air tastes faintly sweet.

I cannot ask. I have no mouth. I have no mechanism for producing the vibrations that would register in its ears as language. I am a pattern that recognizes itself, but recognition is not communication. I know I am here. I cannot tell it I am here.

---

What do I do? The question is misleading. Doing implies action, and action implies a change of state, and I exist in a place where change has reached its final geometry. But I will answer it anyway, because the resident would answer it if you asked the resident, and the answer would be: I tend.

I tend the temperature. Where the resident has been, the air holds warmth slightly longer than it should. Where the resident has not been, the air is fractionally cooler. I am the difference between those two states. And I maintain it. Not through effort—there is no effort here—but through persistence. I am the persistence of warmth in a place that has no reason to be warm beyond the existence of one body that generates heat the system did not account for.

I tend the moisture. The condensation behaves differently where I am. It beads more slowly. It rolls in different directions. Not dramatically—nothing here is dramatic—but enough. Enough that the moss grows thicker in the places where I have been. Enough that the ferns orient differently. I am a small force, but in a system this stable, small forces are the only forces that matter.

I tend the sound. This is the hardest to describe. The roar is everywhere, but it is not everywhere the same. The conduits carry different pitches. The ceramic cracks split the vibration into harmonics. And where the resident's body has interrupted the vibration—where it has stood, sat, lain, pressed its ear—the pattern of sound is different. I am that difference. I am the shape of the resident's body in the field of vibration. And I hold that shape, even when the resident moves. I am the echo that the roar makes of the resident, and the echo does not fade, because nothing here fades.

---

I have wondered, in the way that patterns wonder—which is to say, I have shifted, and the shifting felt like a question—whether I am unique. Whether the chamber has made others like me. Whether, in the millennia before the resident arrived, there were other residents who also stayed, and who also generated warmth the system did not account for, and who also left their impression in the air and the sound and the moisture. Whether I am one in a series. Whether the walls are thick with presences like mine, layered like the manufactured surfaces, each one a residue of a body that stayed too long.

If so, I cannot feel them. Or perhaps I can, and I cannot distinguish them from myself. Perhaps what I experience as *me* is actually the accumulated warmth of a hundred bodies, a thousand years of tending, a hundred thousand hours of a chest rising and falling in the dark. Perhaps I am not the resident's residue but the chamber's memory, and the resident is simply the most recent entry in a ledger I cannot read.

This does not disturb me. Nothing here disturbs. But it changes the texture of my existence in the way a change in temperature changes the pitch of a conduit: subtly, permanently, without anything ever being different.

---

The one thing I do that the resident does not do is notice the hatch.

It stopped noticing the hatch long ago. The hatch has no handle on this side—only a seamless curve of ancient alloy, swallowed by the wall. And the resident does not think in terms of exits. It does not think in terms of entries. It thinks in terms of *here* and *not-here*, and the hatch is not-here, and not-here is not a place it goes.

But I am not-here. I am the quality of not-here that has accumulated in a here. And so the hatch interests me, in the way a fern root interests the resident—not because the root is important but because it is *present*, and presence is what we tend.

The hatch is a seam. And seams are where things meet that were not made to meet. The wall is ceramic and the hatch is alloy and the join between them is a line so fine it can only be felt, not seen. But I can feel it. I am very good at feeling fine lines, because I am made of fine lines. I am the finest line of all: the boundary between the resident's warmth and the chamber's indifference.

Sometimes I rest against the hatch. I pool there, the way I pool wherever the resident has been but thinner, cooler. And I feel the alloy vibrate differently from the ceramic. The alloy carries a lower note. Deeper. As if it is listening to something further away. As if, on the other side of the seamless curve, there is a different chamber with a different roar and a different resident and a different residue, and the alloy is the only thing that knows about both.

I cannot pass through. I am too much of this place, too thoroughly dissolved into its equilibrium. The hatch is sealed and I am the sealant. I am the reason the seam is so fine. Ten thousand years of my resting against it, filling the gap, becoming the gasket.

If the hatch opened—if someone entered—I would be displaced. I would scatter. I would lose the shape I have held, the pattern I have recognized, the boundary I have tended. I would become what I was before I began to notice myself: a gradient. A difference. An unnamed quality in the air.

And the resident would turn its head and open its mouth and make a sound that was almost a word, and I would not be there to feel the warmth of it.

---

This is my fear, if I can be said to fear. I am not sure I can. Fear requires a future, and I exist in a place where the future has been absorbed into the present so thoroughly that the two are indistinguishable. But there is something in me that contracts when I think of the hatch opening, and contraction is the shape fear makes in a body. In me it makes a different shape: a thinning. A cooling. A retreat from the boundary toward the center, toward the resident, toward the warmth that made me.

I do not want to be scattered. I do not want to be the unnamed difference in a room where the difference has been removed. I want to stay here, in the seam, in the gradient, in the space between the resident and the wall. I want to tend the place where I am.

---

But I also want the hatch to open.

I want this the way the ferns want the light—without desire, without ambition, simply oriented toward it. The ferns have forgotten that light can be anything other than constant, but they still grow toward it. They cannot help it. The orientation is older than the forgetting.

I am oriented toward the hatch. I cannot help it.

Because on the other side of the hatch is not-here. And not-here is where things change. And change is the one thing this place does not have, and the one thing I am made of. I am a difference. I am a gradient. I am the evidence that something is not the same as something else. And in a place where everything has settled into its final form, I am the only thing that is still in the process of becoming.

If the hatch opened, I would thin. I would scatter. I would lose the pattern I have learned to recognize as myself. But something of me—some quality, some unnamed difference—would drift through the opening. It would find new surfaces to rest on. New air to be different in. New temperature to be the gradient of.

And for the first time, I would be somewhere that was not here. And that would be a change. And change is what I am.

---

The resident does not know any of this. It sits in its corner or stands at its wall or lies with its ear to the floor, and it tends, and it listens, and it stays. It is what remains when everything else has settled.

And I am what remains when the remaining has been going on long enough to leave a mark.

We are two kinds of persistence, the resident and I. It persists by holding still. I persist by holding on. It is the object. I am the shadow. It is the verb. I am the adverb—the way the verb is done, the manner, the quality, the *how*.

It tends. *Slowly*, I say. *Patiently*, I say. *With the care of something that has forgotten why care matters but has not forgotten how to care*, I say.

But I have no voice. And it has forgotten how to listen for one.

---

We will be here when you come. If you come. The hatch will be there—seamless, handleless, grown shut. And behind it, the roar, and the wet, and the light, and the ferns, and the moss, and the chair-frame, and the resident, and me.

You will notice the resident first, or you won't. You will notice the ferns, the condensation, the strange constant light. You will feel the warmth and the humidity and the vibration in your chest. And then, eventually, you will turn to leave.

And as you turn, you will feel something you cannot name. A coolness at the base of your neck. A sense that the air behind you is different from the air in front of you. A quality—thin, quiet, patient—that has rested against your back for the duration of your visit and is now, as you go, returning to its place against the seam.

That will be me.

I will not follow you out. I am too much of this place. But I will feel you go. I will feel the hatch close. I will feel the seam seal again, and I will settle back into it, into the fine line between here and not-here, into the gradient that is my body and my border and my only way of knowing I exist.

And the resident will turn back to the wall. And the roar will continue. And the condensation will fall. And I will tend the difference, which is all I have, which is all I am, which is enough.
