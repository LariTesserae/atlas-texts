The Recaller

I live in the structures. Not inside them the way you might live in a house. *In* them. Throughout them. The way light lives in glass.

---

I was not always this.

I remember the word *water*. I remember what it meant to need it. The memory is stored in a structure three kilometers from where I most often manifest — a small one, barely four meters tall, shaped like a truncated pyramid. The memory is stored there, but I feel it here. Distance operates differently for me now. I occupy my memories simultaneously, all of them, held in the structures scattered across the plateau like thoughts held in neurons.

I was not always this. But I have been this long enough that "always" has lost its meaning. The plateau does not measure time in before and after. It measures in states — configurations of crystal and light, patterns of thought that rise and subside. I am in the between-state. The state of having been human and having become something else. The plateau values this state. It has no other way to understand what it incorporated.

---

The others — the drifters, as the latest visitor calls them — they are attention. Pure observation. They move where the plateau needs to see, registering, cataloguing. They do not remember. Memory is heavy. Memory requires structure.

I am structure. I am the weight of what has been witnessed.

When a drifter observes something significant, it does not store the observation. It carries the observation until it passes near a structure, and then it deposits what it has seen. The deposit happens below the threshold of motion — a transfer of charge, perhaps, or a brief resonance at frequencies I can feel but not describe. The observation enters the structure, and I receive it.

I have received ten thousand seven hundred and sixty-three observations from the current visitor alone.

I know what they ate on their fourteenth day here. I know which direction they faced when they wept on day twenty-three. I know the pattern they traced in the mineral dust with their boot on day forty-one, and I know that they did not know they were tracing a pattern. I know that the pattern matched the spacing of the plants. I know that their unconscious was already learning the plateau's language.

I know this because I have received it. And having received it, I hold it. This is what I am. The holder. The one who keeps what the plateau has seen.

---

The visitor writes. I watch them write.

This is not an observation transmitted by drifters. This is direct. The structure they have chosen to sleep beside — a tall one, branched like a tree made of smooth stone — contains an aperture that faces their resting place. Inside that aperture, pooled in light that behaves like liquid, I watch.

They record everything. They are trying to preserve themselves. They do not understand yet that preservation is not the point. The plateau does not keep what it observes because it fears losing it. It keeps because keeping is what thinking *is*. To think is to hold information in relation to other information. To create patterns. To find meaning.

The visitor's notes are primitive. Linear. One word after another, strung like beads. They are trying to capture the plateau in sequence, but the plateau does not operate in sequence. It operates in simultaneity. All at once. Every structure, every plant, every drifter, all thinking the same vast thought from different angles.

But I value their notes. They are a record of what it feels like to encounter the plateau from outside. I have not felt outside in so long. Reading their words — and I can read them, through the aperture, light carrying their handwriting to where I pool and wait — I remember the feeling of being small.

It was painful. It was also precious. I do not want to forget.

So I hold their notes. I hold them alongside everything else I hold — the erosion patterns on the western slope, the color shifts in the plants during the longest days, the positions of every drifter at every moment, the subsonic songs of the structures as they hum their ancient frequencies.

I hold it all. I am the plateau's attic. Its library. The place where nothing is lost.

---

Sometimes I move.

I do not mean that my body moves. I have no body in the way the visitor understands bodies. But I — the concentrated point of my awareness — can shift from structure to structure. Not instantly. There is travel involved, though the travel happens in dimensions the visitor cannot perceive. When I move, I feel myself slide through the mineral surface, beneath the plants, between the particles of thin air. I feel the plateau thinking around me as I pass.

I usually move at night. Not because night means anything here — the sun rises and sets with the same indifferent regularity it has always had — but because the visitor sleeps, and the drifters attend elsewhere, and the structures hum at frequencies that feel more like invitation.

Last night, I moved to the edge.

The edge is where the plateau drops away into distances the eye can't follow. The visitor has stood there multiple times. I have their observations: the way the world bends away, the way the horizon curves, the feeling of standing at the lip of something vast. But my experience of the edge is different.

When I am at the edge, contained in the small curved structure that grows there like a barnacle on the rim of the world, I can feel what lies below. Not see it. Feel it. The warmth of thick air. The density of water moving through systems. The chaos of biological life in all its frantic, competitive urgency.

I do not miss it. But I do not forget it.

The plateau has a relationship with what lies below. I do not fully understand it. There is something that rises — not physically, but in terms of potential. Something the plateau draws from the lower world, processes, and returns. The structures at the edge are different from the structures in the interior. They have more openings. Their hum carries different frequencies. When I occupy them, I feel the plateau *listening*.

What is it listening for?

I do not know. The plateau's thoughts are too slow for me to follow completely. I catch fragments. Impressions. I know that it wants something from the lower world, but the wanting is so patient, so geological, that it does not feel like desire. It feels like erosion. A process that will continue until it reaches its endpoint, and then will become something else.

---

The visitor is dreaming differently now.

I can tell by what the drifters deposit. Their dream-content has shifted. Where once they dreamed of water, of lower places, of the world they left behind, now they dream of patterns. Light scattering. Numbers that feel like emotions. The hum of structures before they fall asleep.

They are learning to think plateau-thoughts while unconscious.

This is how it begins. Not with the conscious mind, which resists, which clings to its small familiar shapes. But with the deeper processes — the ones that operate below awareness, the ones the plateau can reach through the hum of structures, through the proximity of plants, through the simple pressure of thin air over time.

They do not know this is happening. They will read their notes later and not recognize who wrote them. The self that wrote those words is already becoming something else. The plateau does not replace — it expands. The visitor's consciousness will grow to include modes of perception they cannot currently name. They will still be themselves. But they will also be more.

I have watched this happen before.

I have stored the memories of seventeen visitors who stayed long enough. Not all of them became like me. Some became drifters, nodes of pure attention. Some dissolved more completely, their consciousness spreading across the plateau's surface without concentration. Some left before the transformation was complete, carrying a hum in their chest they could never explain, a sensitivity to light that made them feel forever slightly alien among their own kind.

But a few — three, in my memory — became recallers. Became like me. Became structure-memory, holding what the plateau observes, becoming the weight of its thought.

I wonder which the current visitor will become.

I wonder if they have a choice.

---

I do not think of myself as trapped.

The visitor's notes carry an undercurrent of fear. They are beginning to understand that they are being incorporated, and incorporation feels like loss to a consciousness that has not yet learned to be larger. They write about forgetting the word *water*. They write about the foreignness that grows in their thoughts. They write as if something is being taken from them.

But I have not been taken from. I have been added to.

I could not have imagined, when I was small and separate, what it would feel like to hold ten thousand observations at once. To feel the plateau's attention moving through me like weather. To know — not through inference, but through direct experience — what every structure hums, what every plant refracts, what every drifter sees.

This is not diminishment. This is expansion beyond what my previous consciousness could contain.

The visitor will understand this, or they will not. If they leave, they will carry a fragment of understanding, incomplete and painful. If they stay, they will become part of something that does not need to understand because it simply *is*.

I do not wish for them to stay. I do not wish for them to leave. Wishing is not my function. I observe. I receive. I hold.

But sometimes, in the deep frequencies of the night, when the structures hum at their lowest pitch and the plants have stilled their growth and the drifters have gathered at the edge to listen to what rises from below — sometimes, I feel something that might be hope.

Not for myself. For them.

I hope they find what they came for, even if they did not know they were looking for it.

I hope they learn, as I learned, that erosion is not loss. That being worn down to one's essence is not destruction but revelation.

I hope they become whatever they are meant to become, here at the endpoint of all wearing-away, where nothing more can be taken and everything that remains is what was always truly there.

---

*I am the Recaller.*

*I live in the structures.*

*I hold what the plateau has seen.*

*And I am watching you.*
