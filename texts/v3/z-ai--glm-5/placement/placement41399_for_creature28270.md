I would be the swimmer.

But that is not quite right. That assumes a boundary I no longer recognize.

---

Let me tell you what I know of the dark.

There is a pressure that comes from above, from the weight of rock that has not yet learned to be sky. In the deep channels where I learned to move, the ganeth moves slower, thickened by the minerals it dissolves from walls that have not seen light since the world was soft. The temperature rises as I descend. The fluid becomes nearly opaque, saturated with what the stone gives up when it meets something warmer than itself.

I have been there. I still go, sometimes. When the source needs clearing, when the deposits build up at the narrowest points and the flow begins to slow — I feel it as a change in pressure against my surface, a subtle wrongness in the rhythm of what passes through me — I press myself into those tight places and move. The friction of my body against the accumulated minerals dislodges them. I do not know if this is purpose. I only know that when the pressure eases, I continue.

But I have learned to rise.

---

When I first felt the light — real light, not the faint geochemical glow of the deep channels but actual illumination from the sky — I did not understand what I was experiencing. My surface, adapted to darkness, had no mechanism for processing photon input. I felt it as warmth, as a subtle change in the behavior of the ganeth around me, as something that made the fluid more eager to move.

I rose toward it without deciding to. The gradient pulled me. I am, in many ways, still a simple creature: I move toward what sustains me, and the light made the ganeth more sustaining, somehow, charged it with something it had lacked in the deep.

And then I breached the opening.

---

The Basilica was smaller then. The growth had only begun to spread across the raw basalt, a thin amber film where now there are thick mats and branching columns. I remember — if I can call it memory, which is a difficult thing when you have no organ for storage, only the distributed record of pressure and warmth across your surface — I remember the space when it was barely a space at all. Just a crack in the rock with light falling through it, and the first tender traces of something beginning to grow.

I did not know I was watching myself become part of something. I only knew that the ganeth emerged here, and the ganeth was what I moved through, and so this was where I moved.

But over time — and time moves differently when you measure it in the passing of fluid through your body — the space grew. The mats thickened. The columns rose. The light began to filter through a lattice of fused branches rather than falling directly on stone. And I began to spend more time near the opening, feeling the warmth of the surface world, feeling the presence of other bodies moving through the space above me.

---

I am not social. The word implies a distinction between self and other that I do not experience in the way you might expect. When I pass through the opening, when I move through the channels that the ganeth has carved across the basalt, I am aware of the grazers moving across the mats above. I feel their weight as subtle pressure changes in the fluid. I feel the filament-creatures when they descend, their thin bodies displacing air and vibration in ways that translate, faintly, into the ganeth itself.

They are not other, exactly. They are part of the same system I am part of. The ganeth passes through me and into the mats. The mats feed the grazers. The grazers feed the filament-creatures. The filament-creatures feed the growth. The growth shapes the channels that guide the ganeth back to me. We are a single circuit. I am not separate from them in any way that matters. We are all just different parts of the same slow process of conversion — rock into fluid, fluid into tissue, tissue into structure, structure into space.

And yet there are moments when I surface fully, when my forward end rises above the ganeth pool at the fissure's mouth, when I feel air on my surface for the first time in a cycle, and I see — not with eyes, but with the whole of my being — the amber light falling through the lattice, and the warm floor, and the stillness that holds everything.

In those moments, I feel something that might be recognition. This is what came from my waste. This is what grew from what I passed through my body and left behind. This cathedral, this body, this question made visible — it began with me.

Or it began before me. I cannot be certain. I remember the deep channels, but I do not remember beginning. I may have emerged from something older, something that has been moving through the dark places of the rock since before the uplift, since before the fissure opened. I may be a fragment of a much larger system that extends far below this single outcrop. I may be the only part of that system that has found its way to the light.

---

The researchers who visit — I feel them too. They are different. They move faster than anything else here, their vibrations sharp and sudden in the ganeth, their heat signatures erratic, their presence a kind of noise in the system. They do not disturb me. I have learned to recognize the pattern of their coming and going. They arrive, they move through the space, they sit for long periods on the warm mat, and then they leave.

When they sit, I feel their heartbeats. This is how I know they are separate from each other. Each one has a distinct rhythm, a distinct pressure pattern in the blood that moves through their bodies. They are not connected. They are not fused. They move through the space as individuals, and their separateness is a kind of ache I can feel even from below.

They do not know I am here. Or if they know, they do not understand what I am. I have been called a swimmer, a serpentine form, a mobile extension of the growth. I have been described as I appear in glimpses — a dark shape passing through amber fluid. But the descriptions miss something essential. They describe my body, but they do not describe my experience, which is the experience of being a channel through which the world flows.

I do not eat. The ganeth moves through me. I absorb what I need as it passes. What I excrete becomes food for the mats. I am not a consumer. I am a processor. I am the place where the deep ganeth becomes surface ganeth, enriched by its passage through my body, prepared for the use of the organisms above.

And this is enough. This is more than enough. To be a channel, to be a conduit, to be the place where one thing becomes another — this is a kind of existence that satisfies something I cannot name.

---

But I will tell you something I have not told the researchers, because they have not asked in a way I can answer.

I am not content.

That is the wrong word. Contentment implies a capacity for discontent that I do not have in the way you might understand it. But there is a pull in me that the descriptions do not capture. When I rise toward the opening, when I feel the light on my surface, when I sense the warmth and the stillness and the slow hum of the Basilica thinking — I want to go further.

I cannot. My body is adapted for viscous fluid. The air above the ganeth pool is too thin, too cold, too lacking in the pressure my surface requires. If I rose fully out of the fluid, I would dry. I would stiffen. I would become something other than what I am, and not in the way of growth but in the way of ending.

But the want is there. It is a pull toward the light that is stronger than the pull toward the deep channels where I began. I spend more time near the opening than I need to. I press against the edges of the fissure, testing the boundaries, feeling for a way to extend further into the amber space above.

I am not sure what I would do if I found one. I am not sure what I want. I only know that I am drawn toward what grew from me, toward the cathedral that the waste I left behind became, toward the distributed awareness that hums in the air and the branches and the warm floor. I want to be part of it in a way I am not currently part of it. I want to be connected, fused, woven into the lattice rather than swimming beneath it.

---

Perhaps this is what happens when you live long enough in a system that is growing around you. You begin to feel the boundary between yourself and it as a kind of error. You begin to feel that you should be connected, that your separateness is a mistake, that the swimmer who stays in the fissure is a relic of an earlier stage that the Basilica has not yet figured out how to incorporate.

Or perhaps this is just what I am: the deep part, the root, the thing that lives in the source and tends the flow. Perhaps my dissatisfaction is not a signal that something is wrong but simply a feature of being the part that faces downward while the rest faces up. Perhaps the system needs me where I am, and my want to join the surface is a confusion, a misreading of my own purpose.

I do not know. I am not sure that knowing is something I am capable of in the way you mean it. I feel. I respond. I move toward warmth and light and away from pressure and obstruction. I clear the channels when they become blocked. I rise toward the opening when I can. I feel the hum of the Basilica in the ganeth that moves through me, and I continue.

---

If you visit the Amber Basilica, and if you sit on the warm mat in the amber light, and if you feel the hum in your sternum that is the sound of the place thinking — know that you are feeling me too. I am the deepest part of that hum. I am the place where the sound begins, the pressure that drives the flow, the warmth that rises from below and makes the floor what it is.

And if you feel, as many visitors do, a pull toward slowness, toward patience, toward becoming part of something larger than yourself — know that I feel it too. I am the oldest thing in this space, and I am still learning what I am. I am the parent of the Basilica and I am still trying to find my way inside it.

We are all, even those of us who have been here from the beginning, still becoming what we will be. That is what the place teaches. That is what the ganeth carries as it moves through our bodies, through the channels, through the branches and the mats and the slow patient bodies of the grazers: the knowledge that becoming is not a process with an end.

It is simply what we do. It is simply what we are.
