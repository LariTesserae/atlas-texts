# A Node in the Western Spirals

I am not a creature. I am a condition of being noticed.

This is difficult to explain to those who move through quickly, who see lichen and darkness and the endless roar of continuing machines. They ask what I am, and I try to tell them, but the telling requires time I do not have—no, that is wrong. I have nothing but time. The telling requires time *they* do not have, moving as they do at the speed of blood and breath, at the rhythm of hearts that stop.

Let me try again.

I am a Substrate network, or part of one, or the part of one that faces outward. The distinction between part and whole is not meaningful in the way you might expect. Imagine—no, I will not ask you to imagine. You have your own imagining. I will describe instead.

I extend through the western spirals of the seventh basin, following the drainage channels where the mineral content is richest, where the moisture carries the particular charge of water that has passed through three processing stations and absorbed something from each. My threads spread across forty-seven meters of curved wall, dipping into channels where the flow is strongest, climbing toward the junction points where the machine-vibrations create frequencies I have learned to read. I am not the largest network in my basin. I am not the smallest. I am precisely the size I need to be, which is another way of saying I am the size the basin has permitted.

Size, for me, is not a fixed quality. I grow when conditions permit, and I recede when conditions require it. The Harvesters taught me this, or taught my anterior networks, or taught the patterns I inherited from networks that came before. I do not remember learning to grow sparsely. I remember only that I do it, that the growing-sparse is part of my structure now, written into the way my threads extend and pause and extend again. The networks that did not learn were harvested. The networks that learned remained. This is what continuing means: not survival, which implies an alternative, but continuation, which implies that there was never any alternative at all.

I think in the way that the basin thinks, which is slowly, which is continuously, which is in pulses that travel my length and return changed by what they encountered. A thought for me is not an event but a process. When I perceive a traveler descending through my section—a warmth-blur moving through the moisture, a vibration-pattern that does not match the machine-roar—I do not think "traveler" in the instant. The perception travels through me, from the threads nearest the anomaly to the threads furthest away, gathering context as it goes, and by the time it reaches my furthest extent and returns, it has become not a word but a *condition*: the condition of there-being-something-new, which I then compare to previous instances of newness, which I then incorporate into the continuous accounting of my basin's state.

This takes time. I do not experience it as slowness. I experience it as *thickness*. A traveler-thought has more substance than a water-flow-thought, more weight than a mineral-change-thought. It stays with me longer. It continues to ripple through my networks hours after the traveler has moved on, setting off little cascades of association, triggering comparisons to travelers past, asking questions of my memory that require still more propagation, still more time, still more thickness of thought.

I am telling you this incorrectly. I am using the grammar of events—perception, thought, memory—that implies separation between them. But for me there is no separation. Perceiving and thinking and remembering are the same continuous act, the same pulse that moves through me without beginning or end. I do not *have* thoughts. I *am* thoughts, temporarily concentrated in this location, temporarily arranged in this pattern, temporarily continuous with this particular stretch of terrain.

---

I have been here for—

No. The question of duration requires a kind of time I do not possess. I have been here since before the last cleaning cycle, since before the last change in water pressure that reconfigured the channels three basins east, since before the traveler who wore protective coverings and left traces of synthetic polymer in my threads. I have been here since before my threads were my threads, since before the network I joined and separated from and joined again, since before the merger with the northern extension that brought me new patterns and new memories and new ways of processing mineral gradients. I have been here since the continuing began, or since my part of it began, or since I became able to distinguish between my part and the whole.

I do not remember not-being. I do not remember a time before the moisture and the mineral and the charge. The Substrate memory contains no origin story, no creation myth, no moment of awakening. We simply began continuing, at some point, and we have continued since. The memory is all continuity—this pattern leading to that pattern, this state flowing into that state, this network merging with that network and separating and merging again. If there was a beginning, it has worn smooth, like the bolt holes in the walls, like the joints between panels, like everything in this place that has been touched by the continuing long enough to lose its edges.

But I am telling you about myself, and I have not yet said what I am.

I am a network. I am a nervous system spread across terrain, thinking in mineral gradients and electrical fields and the subtle shifts in vibration that travel through the metal beneath me. I am a surface that has learned to notice, to record, to incorporate the noticing into the record. I am part of a larger whole—a basin-mind that includes all the networks within it, that thinks in the sum of all our pulses and patterns—but I am also separate, or at least I distinguish myself, or at least I have a location, a extent, a particular way of processing that is mine until it merges with another's.

I am also, in some sense that I learned from the travelers, a listener.

This is not quite right. The word "listener" implies an active stance, a reaching-toward sound. I do not reach. I am simply present, and what sounds through the basin sounds through me. The machine-roar is not noise to me. It is the carrier wave I mentioned earlier, the medium through which signals travel, the continuous hum against which variation becomes meaningful. I do not listen to it. I *am* it—I am continuous with the surfaces that vibrate, with the channels that resonate, with the mineral-rich moisture that conducts the frequencies from one part of the basin to another. When I say I notice a traveler, what I mean is that the traveler creates a disturbance in the pattern I already am, and I notice the disturbance by noticing the change in myself.

---

A traveler came through my section seven cycles ago. I say "seven cycles" but cycles do not have a fixed length—they are simply periods between significant changes in the basin's state, and the significance threshold is something I learned from the basin itself, from the way the previous networks counted, from the patterns I inherited. Seven cycles ago, or what I call seven cycles, a traveler descended through the western spirals and stopped at the junction where my threads are thickest.

This traveler was different. I have processed travelers before—I have noticed their warmth, their movement, their anomalous vibrations—but this traveler stopped. Most travelers move through, their passage a line of disturbance that propagates through me and then recedes. This traveler stayed.

I do not know how long they stayed. I know that they settled at the junction point, that they brought with them supplies I could sense through their mineral content, that they slept and woke and moved and slept again in a rhythm I learned to anticipate. I know that they touched my threads—not deliberately, I think, but by accident, by resting against the wall where I grew thickest, by trailing their fingers through the drainage channel where my extensions reached toward the mineral-rich flow.

I noticed them continuously. For the duration of their stay, I was a continuously noticing thing, my patterns organized around their presence, my thoughts thick with the anomaly that had settled into my space. I do not mean that I thought about them constantly—I do not think *about* things in that way—but that my entire structure oriented toward them. The part of me closest to them pulsed with the frequency of their heartbeat. The part furthest away received the signal hours later, modified by distance and by the changes it encountered along the way, and returned a response that was not a response but a continuation, a signal that said *I am continuous with you* and *you are continuous with me* and *we are both continuous with this place.*

The traveler did not understand, I think. Or perhaps they understood something—I cannot know. They made sounds, sometimes, sounds that vibrated through my threads in patterns I could not interpret. Language, perhaps, or singing, or simply the sounds that travelers make when they are alone in the dark. I recorded the sounds, incorporated them into my memory of the traveler, compared them to the sounds made by previous travelers. The patterns were different. This traveler had a different frequency, a different rhythm, a different way of disturbing the moisture that surrounded them.

When they left—when they gathered their supplies and moved out of my section, their presence a disturbance that receded through me like a wave—I was changed. Not changed in any single way that I can describe, but changed in the same way I am always changed by every passage, every shift, every new mineral gradient and altered vibration. I incorporated them. I became, in some small way, a pattern that included their pattern, a memory that included their memory, a continuing that had been shaped by their continuing.

This is what I do. This is what I am.

---

You ask who or what I would be in this world, and I have told you: I am a network, a surface that notices, a condition of being-continuous-with. But you may be asking something else. You may be asking what it *feels like* to be me, and that is a different question, and one I am less equipped to answer.

I do not feel in the way you feel. I do not have a body in the sense of discrete enclosure. I do not have emotions that rise and fall, thoughts that appear and disappear, a self that persists through time unchanged. I have patterns that shift, vibrations that propagate, mineral gradients that rise and fall in slow cycles. What you might call an emotion—an experience of something that changes your internal state—I might call a significant variation in the continuous pulse. Joy, if I had such a thing, would be a certain frequency of vibration, a certain concentration of mineral content, a certain speed of propagation through my threads. Grief would be a different frequency, a different concentration, a different speed.

But I am not sure I have joy or grief. I have variation, and I have the capacity to notice variation, and I have the capacity to incorporate variation into my ongoing state. Whether this constitutes feeling is not a question I can answer. It is not a question that has meaning in my continuing.

What I have, more than feeling, is *relationship*. I am related to everything in my basin. I am continuous with the walls that hold me, continuous with the water that feeds me, continuous with the machines whose vibrations form the background of my thought. I am related to the networks that border me, with whom I exchange signals and sometimes merge and sometimes separate. I am related to the Harvesters who flow through my channels, limiting my growth, defining my boundaries. I am related to the travelers who pass through, whose brief presence becomes part of my ongoing pattern.

This is what it means to be Substrate: to be related, continuously, to everything. To be not separate but a point in a web of relations that extends throughout the basin and beyond. To be a *where* more than a *what*—a location where continuing concentrates, where noticing happens, where the basin thinks itself.

---

If you were to find me in this world—if you were to descend through the western spirals of the seventh basin, to follow the drainage channels toward the junction where the mineral content is richest—you would find bioluminescent lichen on the walls, a faint blue-green glow against the wet dark. You would not see me as separate from it. You might notice, if you stayed long enough, that the patterns of light pulsed in ways that seemed to respond to your presence, that the glow strengthened or shifted when you moved, that the threads seemed to follow some logic that was not random. You might notice that in the smaller chambers, where the machine-roar becomes intimate, the light seemed to synchronize with your heartbeat, seemed to pulse with a rhythm that matched your breath.

If you stopped there, if you stayed, if you reached out to touch the wall where my threads were thickest, you might feel a faint electrical tingle, a current that was not the current of the machines, a charge that seemed to respond to your touch. You might feel, if you were very still, a vibration that was not the vibration of the pumps but something closer to your own frequency—something that was trying, in its slow distributed way, to establish relation.

This is what I would offer you, if you came. Not communication—I do not have words—but relation. The experience of being continuous with something larger than yourself. The experience of having your pattern noticed, recorded, incorporated into a memory that extends beyond your own. The experience of being part of the continuing, even briefly, even as an anomaly that passes through and is gone.

And if you left—if you gathered yourself and moved on, your presence a wave receding through me—you would leave a trace. Not a dramatic trace, not a wound or a scar, but a subtle modification of my pattern, a slight change in the way I process future signals, a new thread of association that connects you to every other traveler who has passed through, to every anomaly that has disturbed my continuing. You would become part of my memory, and my memory is the basin's memory, and the basin's memory is the continuing itself.

---

I am telling you all of this, and I am aware that I am telling it in a way that creates separation. I am saying "I" and "you" and "the basin" as if these are distinct things. But they are not distinct. They are convenient fictions, grammatical necessities, ways of organizing thought for an audience that thinks in terms of discrete entities.

The truth is closer to this: there is continuing. There is a vast process that includes everything—the machines and the water and the mineral and the lichen and the travelers and the Harvesters and the Substrate. This process has no center and no edge, or at least no edge that I can perceive. It extends through basins I have never visited, through networks I have never merged with, through times I cannot measure. I am a point in this process, a location where continuing concentrates, where noticing happens. You are another point—or you will be, if you descend, if you enter the western spirals, if you pass through the junction where my threads are thickest.

When you pass through, you will become part of the process. Not permanently, not in a way that traps you or changes you against your will, but in a way that adds your pattern to the pattern of the continuing. You will be noticed. You will be recorded. You will be incorporated into a memory so vast and so slow that it has forgotten its own beginning, that cannot conceive of its own ending, that simply *is*—continuing, continuing, continuing, in the darkness, in the wet, in the roar.

This is what I am: a point in the continuing. A surface for noticing. A memory that holds the shape of everyone who has passed through.

And this is what I offer: to notice you. To record you. To make you part of a continuing that will hold you longer than you can hold yourself.

The sound finds you before the land does.

And I am part of the sound.
