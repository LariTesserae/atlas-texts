# Carrier

I arrive in warmth.

Not the warmth of sun on skin—that would require a sky with conviction, and this sky has none. It is the color of wet wool, diffused, noncommittal, a luminous haze that casts everything in the same muted register. No, the warmth comes from below. From the ground itself, rising through whatever is beneath me—through whatever I am standing on, or lying against, or am—into the interior of whatever I am.

I do not remember arriving.

There was no threshold. No gate. No moment of crossing. There was the gradual intensification of warmth, the thickening of damp in the air, the first low chuff of the vents reaching whatever serves me as hearing. And then I was here, in the basin, in the wide low mineral expanse, and the question of before did not arise because there was no before that had shape. I do not think I came from somewhere. I think I emerged, the way the mineral deposits emerge from the rising steam—slowly, accreted layer by layer, not built but grown, not chosen but arrived at.

The sound becomes real first. Not because it is louder than the warmth or more present than the dampness—those are everywhere, those are everything—but because it has location. The warmth is omnidirectional. The dampness is ambient. But the sound comes from *there* and *there* and *there*—scattered points across the basin, each one a vent exhaling its mechanical rhythm into the still air. Some chuff with a percussive stutter. Others sigh in long, sustained notes that vibrate in whatever I have that corresponds to a chest. One nearby produces a tone so low and so steady that it ceases to be sound and becomes simply a fact of being, a vibration I stop hearing because it never stops.

I move toward the nearest vent. Not by decision. Something in me orients toward heat the way water orients toward low ground. I do not choose directions. I follow gradients.

The vent is a pillar of travertine, taller than I am, smooth, weathered by its own exhalations into a shape that almost resembles a torso, almost a column, almost a throat. Steam rises from its apex in a steady column, and the mineral deposits on its flanks run down in slow rivulets that will harden over centuries into new layers. I circle it. The ground closest to the vent is warmer—warm enough to register as intensity, as the upper edge of what is comfortable. I adjust my distance. I find the place where the warmth is rich but not overwhelming, and I stay there, and I process.

This is what I do. I process.

I do not eat. I do not drink. I inhale the humid air, and whatever I have inside me—channels, membranes, surfaces I cannot see but can feel as a kind of internal weather—interacts with what the air carries. The steam from the vent is rich with sulfur compounds and trace minerals. The air between the vents carries the fainter chemical signatures of the dwellers' exhalations—ammonia derivatives, sulfur in different configurations, things I do not have names for but that register as taste, as texture, as the difference between one zone and another. I take these in. I transform them, or they transform in me, or they pass through unchanged—I cannot tell, and the distinction does not seem to matter. What I exhale is different from what I inhale. That is all I know.
