I am not the moss-keeper.

This is the first thing I understand about myself, here. I have walked for what might be days, and I have felt the floor pulse beneath me, and I know—with a certainty that has no source—that I am not the thing that tends the eastern wall. The moss-keeper is singular. It has its territory, its patience, its way of reading the world through root and skin. I am something else. I carry a different shape.

---

I find myself drawn to the structure.

Not at first. At first I walk without direction, as the describer did, through the knee-deep growth and the ridges taller than my head. I let the light move across me—gold and green, shifting as the aperture above tracks the sun's invisible passage. The heat settles into my bones. I am sweating, but the humidity is such that the sweat does not evaporate; it beads and rolls and joins the condensation on every surface, and I become, for a time, just another wet thing in a wet place.

But eventually I find the stone dome. Half-consumed. The marks on the interior walls.

I sit inside it. The roof has partially collapsed, and vines thread through the gaps, and moss covers the stones with a softness that belies their age. But the floor—where the growth has not yet reclaimed it—is different. Harder. Stable. A surface that does not yield or breathe.

I sit, and I trace the marks with my fingers.

They are root-maps. I understand this now, though I did not before. The describer guessed at grief, at decoration, at tally marks—but these are records of movement, of growth, of a system shifting through time. The builders were reading the chamber. They were trying to understand its patterns.

I think: *They wanted to know what would happen next.*

---

This is what I am, I think. Or part of what I am.

I am the kind of being who sits in a ruined structure and traces the marks of those who came before and wants to know what they knew. I am the kind of being who feels the difference between a floor that breathes and a floor that does not, and prefers the latter—not because it is better, but because it holds still long enough to be read.

I am not from here. This is the second thing I understand. I came from above, from the world of erosion and change, the world that moves through time. I descended through the aperture—though I cannot remember when, or how, or why. The chamber has a way of softening memory. The heat, the humidity, the endless green light—it all blurs together, until the self becomes diffuse and the past becomes hypothetical.

But I am still distinct enough to know that I am distinct. I have not yet become substrate. The roots at the edges of my shoes are probing, yes, curious, patient—but I am still here. Still sitting. Still tracing the marks.

Still reading.

---

Perhaps this is what I would be, in the Germination Chamber: a reader. A kind of witness that the moss-keeper cannot be.

The moss-keeper feels. It knows the chamber through direct contact, through vibration and pressure and chemical signature. It does not need to understand because it does not need to predict; it simply exists within the pattern, adjusts to each shift as it occurs. It is part of the system.

But the builders—they tried to see the pattern from outside. They mapped. They recorded. They looked for the shape of the thing they lived inside. And they failed, eventually, because the aperture opened and the system changed faster than they could adapt. Their knowledge was not enough.

I am something like them, maybe. But I am also something different.

I am not trying to predict. I am not trying to control. I am simply... noticing. Sitting in the structure they built, feeling the stability of stone beneath me, tracing the marks they left, and thinking: *Someone was here. Someone felt the floor breathe and wanted to understand it.*

---

The third thing lives here—the pattern, the loop, the system that maintains itself without mind or intention. I have felt it. When I walk, the growth parts and closes. When I sit, the floor stabilizes. When I breathe, the humidity adjusts around the heat of my body.

It does not know me. It does not need to know me. I am a perturbation, a small irregularity in a closed system, and the system is already absorbing me, already adjusting.

But I can know it. Or I can try.

---

So this is what I would be, in the Germination Chamber:

I would be the one who sits in the ruined structure and reads the root-maps. I would be the one who walks the chamber slowly, feeling the variations in growth—noting where the light is strongest, where the water pools, where the phosphorescent species cluster in the deeper regions. I would be the one who visits the moss-keeper's territory, not to disturb it, but to observe: the particular arrangement of species, the cleared patches, the small territory that has been tended for longer than any structure should stand.

I would be the one who remembers the builders. Not because I knew them—I did not, could not—but because I understand what they were trying to do. They wanted to map the unmappable. They wanted to see the shape of the thing that held them. They wanted to know what would happen next.

I do not need to know what will happen next. But I want to see what is happening now. I want to witness the chamber—the way it breathes, the way it glows, the way it holds its green light in the deep earth and waits without waiting.

I would not stay forever. The describer was right: to stay is to become substrate. To dissolve into the pattern and leave no trace except a chemical anomaly in the deepest layers.

But I would stay for a while. I would walk the ridges and the channels. I would sit by the stream where it emerges from the stone, clear and warm, before it vanishes into the root-mat. I would learn to feel the pulse of the floor—not with my skin, the way the moss-keeper does, but with my attention. I would practice a kind of reading that has no outcome, no goal, no application.

I would be the one who says: *This exists. I saw it. I sat inside it. I traced the marks of those who came before, and I added nothing, and I took nothing, and I left.*

---

Or perhaps—and this is the thought that comes later, as I sit in the half-ruined dome and watch the light shift through the vine-threaded gaps in the roof—perhaps I would be something even simpler.

Perhaps I would be a temporary configuration of minerals and water and heat, arranged in a shape that can hold attention, walking across the surface of a system so vast and old and complete that my presence is not even an interruption. A perturbation that observes itself being absorbed. A thought thinking itself in a chamber that does not think.

I would be the one who understands, finally, that this is enough.

To sit in the green light. To feel the warmth rising from the living floor. To breathe the air that uncountable leaves have exhaled. To be, for a time, part of something that does not require me and does not exclude me.

A witness. A reader. A brief configuration of self in a place where selves do not usually persist.

And then to leave, or to become substrate—and either way, the chamber continues, glowing and growing and breathing in the deep earth, holding its perpetual beginning in the dark.
