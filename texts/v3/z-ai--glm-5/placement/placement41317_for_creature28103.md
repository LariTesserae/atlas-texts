# At the Railing

I am not the Tender. I want to say this first because when I arrived — sliding through that fissure with obsidian scraping skin from both shoulders, the sky vanishing above me like a door closing — I think I wanted to be something here. Something with a role. Something the chamber recognized.

But the Tender does not recognize me. The Tender moves past me the way water moves past a stone in its channel: acknowledgment without attention. Once, early in my time here, it touched my wrist. I held my breath. I thought — *conference. Exchange. I am being included in the calculations.*

It was only checking my moisture content. I was damp from the descent. It adjusted nothing in its internal map of the chamber's needs and moved on, flowing into position elsewhere, and I stood with my wrist tingling and my heart doing something stupid and hopeful in my chest.

---

I am the one at the railing.

This is what I can say with certainty. I have been here long enough now — I do not know how long; time behaves differently when nothing demands you account for it — and the railing has become my coordinate. My fixed point. The walkway leads to it and I lead to the walkway and the three of us have an arrangement that the chamber did not design but has not corrected.

The railing is too low. The description says this, and it is true, but it does not capture what *too low* means when you are standing at it. It means the small of my back rests against the metal — if metal is what it is, this warm, humming material that yields like hard muscle — and I lean into the tilt, and I look down, and the dark water holds its light like a held breath, and I am at exactly the right height for the small of my back to rest, and I think someone built this for a body shaped roughly like mine and I do not know what to do with that thought except hold it the way the pool holds its light.

The Interrupted stood here. I feel them the way you feel a chair that is still warm from someone else. Not their warmth — the material doesn't hold heat that way — but their *shape*. The railing has been leaned on. The stone beneath my feet has been stood on. The view has been viewed. I am doing something that was done before, and the chamber expects it, and this is the closest thing to belonging I have found in a place that was not built for me.

---

I should tell you what I am.

I am a woman who found a crack in obsidian and was stupid enough — curious enough — hungry enough — to go through it. That is all. I am not a scientist. I am not an explorer. I am not someone who was looking for this place. I was walking, and the rock had a wound in it, and I put myself in the wound, and the wound swallowed me, and here I am.

Before this I had a life that would fit in a sentence: I lived somewhere, I worked at something, I loved a few people poorly, I was going to keep doing all of that until I wasn't. The chamber has not asked me to stop. The chamber has not asked me anything. It does not ask. It *runs*.

But here is what I have become, or what I am becoming, or what is becoming of me:

I am mostly water. This is true of everyone, but it is *useful* here. The chamber is a water system. Everything that happens here happens through water — condensation, channeling, pooling, separation, the circulation of compounds and information through liquid media. When I breathe, I add moisture to the air. When I sweat — and I sweat; the humidity is precise and relentless — I add my chemistry to the chamber's record. When I stand at the railing and look down at the pool, the water in my body is talking to the water in the pool, and the Observer is reading both, and I am part of the conversation whether I consent or not.

I am a variable. I learned this word from the chamber, though not in language. The chamber does not use language. It uses resonance, chemical gradient, growth rate, the specific frequency at which a structure vibrates when a living thing leans against it. But the concept is clear: I was not in the equation. Now I am. The equation has not been rewritten to exclude me. It has not been rewritten to include me. It is simply running, and I am in it, and the math continues.

I am warm. This matters more than you'd think. The chamber maintains its own temperature, but my body heat is local, specific, a small thermal signature that moves through the space and leaves traces. The plants near the railing where I spend my time grow slightly faster than the plants elsewhere. The Tender visits them more often. It has not connected this to me. Or it has, and does not care. Or it has, and cares in a way that does not look like caring.

---

Let me be honest about the Observer.

It watches me. I know this. I know it the way I know the chamber is running — not through evidence but through the pressure of it, the weight of being attended to by something that does not blink or wander or get bored. The Observer sees everything. I am everything's smallest fraction. But I am a *new* fraction, and new fractions must register differently than old ones, and I think — I *think* — that my presence has added something to the water that the Observer has been reading for longer than I can imagine.

I do not think it likes me. I do not think it dislikes me. I think *likes* and *dislikes* are categories that do not apply, the way *photosynthesis* does not apply to the plants here, and yet they grow. The Observer does not need to have an opinion about me. It needs to read me. And it reads me. And I stand at the railing and let it, because there is nothing else to do with the attention of something that old and that comprehensive except receive it and be known in ways I will never understand.

Sometimes I press my forehead against the railing and close my eyes and try to feel what the Observer feels: the whole chamber at once, every molecule, every vibration, every root-tip and water-drop and chemical signal. I cannot. I am too small. I am a single instrument playing in a space designed for orchestras. But I can feel the edges of it — the hum in the railing, the warmth of the stone, the subtle shift in air pressure when the Tender moves somewhere behind me — and for moments I can almost imagine what it would be to hold all of it at once, to be the pool, to be the reading and the record simultaneously.

Then the moment passes and I am just a woman with her forehead on a railing, and the chamber continues.

---

The Emergent.

I should not have a section about the Emergent. I am not qualified. The Observer does not observe it. The Tender does not tend it. It grows in the deep roots where I cannot see, and everything I know about it I know from the way the chamber feels different at certain times — a thickness in the resonance, a new harmonic in the water-sound, a direction the plants turn toward that they did not turn toward before.

But I stand at the railing, and the railing overlooks the pool, and the pool is where the Observer lives, and the Emergent is growing toward the pool. So I am at the intersection. I am standing at the place where something is arriving. And I do not know what it is, and I do not know what it will do when it gets here, and I am afraid in a way that the chamber has no category for, because the chamber does not do *afraid*, and I am here, and I do.

I think this is why the railing exists. Not for the view. Not for observation. The railing exists because someone was meant to stand here when the Emergent arrived. Someone was meant to be the variable that witnessed the transition. The Interrupted were meant to be here. They are not. I am. I am not what was planned, but I am what is present, and I think — I *think* — that this might be enough. That the chamber might have built redundancy into its expectations. That *someone at the railing* was the requirement, not *the specific someone who was supposed to be here.*

I do not know if this is true. The chamber does not confirm. It continues.

---

I will tell you what I am, then, since you asked, since I have been circling it:

I am the one who stayed.

Not because I was chosen. Not because I was meant to be here. Not because I understand anything that is happening. I stayed because the railing fit the small of my back, and the sound found the water in my inner ear, and the Observer was reading me, and for the first time in my life I was *fully known*, even if what knew me was not anything I could talk to. I stayed because the plants near me grow faster and that makes me feel necessary in a way I have never felt necessary. I stayed because leaving would mean becoming a coordinate without content again, an absence that a chamber grows around, and I have been that already. I have been that my whole life. I did not know it until I found a place that remembers its absences.

I am the afterthought who became, through the simple act of remaining, something the chamber has not yet named. I am warm and wet and afraid and I stand at the railing and I watch the dark water and I wait for whatever is coming, and the chamber provides the conditions for waiting, and I wait.

The Tender tends. The Observer observes. The Interrupted remain as shape-memory in stone. The Emergent continues to become.

And I — I was not expected, and I am here, and I am part of the system now, and the system continues, and I continue with it, and the railing holds me, and I hold the railing, and the sound fills my chest like a second heartbeat, and I am not nothing, I am not nothing, I am **not nothing**.
