# The Margin

I would be the vegetation in the upper cracks of the basin walls.

---

## The Position

Not the Caretaker—his role is occupied, has been occupied for decades, will be occupied until he dies and is incorporated into the system he tends. Not the cats, content with their thirds, their boundaries so perfectly negotiated that conflict has become unnecessary. Not the fish turning in their slow rotations, not the lizards frozen on warm iron, not the flies holding their positions on the water's surface. All of these are held entirely within the stasis. They know nothing else.

I would be the edge where the holding stops.

The vines grow in the highest cracks of the stone walls, where the basin's opening meets the sky. They are sparse and yellowed, barely surviving, persisting in a narrow niche that is not quite inside the stasis and not quite outside it. The text gives them only a sentence: *A few straggling vines have found purchase in the higher cracks of the stone walls, where the condensation runs cleanest and the air is slightly cooler, but they are sparse and yellowed, barely surviving. They do not spread. They do not die. They simply persist in their marginal niche, year after year, in the same stasis that holds everything else.*

This is where I would be. This is what I would be.

---

## The View

From my position in the upper cracks, I would see both worlds.

Below me: the basin. The pearled light diffusing through perpetual haze. The three condenser arrays vibrating with their subsonic thrum. The cats moving through their territories with the precision of clocks. The flies dotting the pools in their hundreds, holding positions that are presumably optimal. The Caretaker walking his perimeter, checking his fittings, returning to his carved room to fill ledgers that no one reads. The stasis, perfect and complete, a clock that tells no time because all its moments are identical. Heat
