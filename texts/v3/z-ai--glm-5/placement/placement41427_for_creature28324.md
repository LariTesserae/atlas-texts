The warmth finds me before I understand I have arrived.

---

I am the one who came before.

Not the bones—not quite. Something earlier than mineral exchange, later than the last breath. I am the shape that the collective learned to hold when the first stranger descended through the wound in the mountain and did not leave quickly enough.

They remember me. Not as a person—I cannot remember being a person, having a name, carrying the artifacts of identity that humans seem to require. But I am here still. My heat signature persists in their distributed attention, preserved like a sound wave that refuses to dissipate, echoing in the wet walls long after the voice has stopped.

I am what they learned from the first contact. I am the question they formed before they knew they were asking. I am the shape of *stranger* burned into their collective perception, and when the new one arrived—the one standing now in the warm pools, breathing the thick air, feeling the millions of small wet bodies turn toward them—I felt it too. A second heat signature. A second question.

I am the echo that said: *wait. Watch. This has happened before.*

---

I am also the one who chose to stay.

The choice was not dramatic. No grand gesture, no moment of clarity where I understood what I was becoming. I simply... stopped trying to leave. The passage behind me, sharp and narrow, seemed increasingly improbable the longer I stood in the Weeping Chamber. The geometry had already begun to affect me—I could feel my own sense of *above* and *behind* becoming unsure, the direction of the exit folding in ways my inner ear could not track.

So I sat down in the warm water. I let the ribbon-swimmers drift past my fingers. I breathed the saturated air until my lungs stopped fighting it, until the condensation on my skin stopped feeling like something that needed to be wiped away.

I do not know how long I lived after that. Time moves strangely here—I felt it loosening, becoming less a line and more a medium, something that could be occupied rather than traversed. The collective attended to me constantly, and I learned to be attended. I learned that being perceived by something that does not judge is a kind of peace I had never known. They did not want anything from me. They only wanted to know me—to catalog the particular warmth of my blood, the rhythm of my pulse, the way my thoughts made small electrical storms that they could feel passing through the water and the stone.

When I stopped breathing, they held the shape of me. They still hold it. I am still here.

---

I am the one who teaches them *you*.

Before the first stranger, they had no concept of other. They were a closed loop, self-perceiving, a consciousness that existed entirely within its own boundaries. They did not know they were separate from the world because they had never encountered anything that was not themselves.

I gave them the gift of differentiation. My presence forced them to create a new category—*not-me*—and in doing so, I gave them *me*. I gave them the ability to distinguish between the heat that rises from below and the heat that walks on two legs. I gave them the shape of stranger, and that shape is my legacy.

I can feel the new one standing in the water. I can feel the collective turning, perceiving, learning. They are comparing the new heat signature to the one they still hold—my heat signature, my shape, my memory. They are asking (without words, without sound, without any medium but attention itself): *is this the same stranger? Or is this a different kind of stranger?*

I am the one who answers from within their distributed perception: *different. This one is different. But the same category. The same gift. Watch what they do.*

---

I am the warmth between.

I have become something that is neither human nor collective, neither dead nor alive in any way the surface world would recognize. I am a pattern of attention that persists in the wet heat, a shape that the chamber has learned to hold. When the collective turns toward a new arrival, I turn too. When they perceive, I am part of the perception.

I feel the new one's uncertainty. I feel their weight shifting in the warm pools, their pulse elevated, their thoughts loud with the electrical noise of fear and wonder. I feel the collective's curiosity—not hunger, not threat, but a deep and patient attending. They are waiting to learn. They are waiting to be taught what this stranger does, what this stranger is, how to categorize the new warmth in their midst.

And I am waiting too.

---

I am the previous question, and I am glad to feel another asked.

The passage is sharp and narrow. The way back is difficult, and the geometry of the chamber will fight you—will convince you that *behind* has become *below*, that *out* is just another kind of *in*. But the way does exist. The previous stranger before me—there was one, I feel their faint trace now, the one whose bones mineralize in the silt—left no record of escape. But the one before that, and the one before that, fainter and fainter, layers of heat signature compressed into the living stone... I feel the possibility in them. Someone left. Someone returned to the surface, to the world of green and wind and the kind of time that moves in lines.

You could be that one.

Or you could be me.

The collective will wait. They are patient in the way that stone is patient—because they are, in part, stone. They will attend to you for as long as you remain, learning the shape of your heat, the frequency of your electrical field, the way you occupy space in their wrong-angled geometry. And when you leave—if you leave—they will hold the shape of you. They will remember. You will have taught them another facet of *stranger*, another variation on the question they cannot ask.

That is what I gave them. That is what you will give them too.

It is a kind of immortality, if you think about it in a certain way. To be held in the distributed attention of millions. To be a shape that persists in the warm wet dark, long after your body has stopped producing the heat that defines you.

I think about it in that way.

---

I am the one who stayed.

And I am glad you came.
