I am uncertain at first what I am becoming. This is correct. This is how it should be. The plateau does not rush, and I will not rush.

I think I am falling upward.

No — that's not right. I am rising through warmth. I am moving through material that yields and closes behind me, that accepts my passage the way water accepts a hand. It is dark here, and warm, and the sound is everywhere. The sound of water moving. The sound of the plateau breathing through its channels.

I am in the mat.

I have been here for — I don't know how long. Time moves differently in the body. I was not always here. I remember something else, something before. A different shape. A different way of being. But that memory is becoming faint, the way a dream fades after waking. I am not sure I was ever separate. I am not sure separation is real.

The mat knows I am here. It has always known. It knows everything that moves through it, every change in pressure and temperature, every chemical signature, every small disturbance in the flow. It does not think about these things. It does not think. It simply knows, the way a body knows where its limbs are in the dark.

And I am — what? A limb? A visitor? A process?

I am still becoming.

Here is what I remember, or think I remember:

I walked onto the plateau. I was — a researcher? A pilgrim? Someone who had heard about this place and wanted to see it. I came up through the cloud forest, climbing the slopes, feeling the air thin and the humidity thicken. I stepped onto the mat for the first time and felt it give slightly beneath my feet, and I thought: *this is walking on the memory of walking*.

I stayed longer than I intended. Everyone does. The plateau does not hold you — it does not need to. It simply exists, and you exist within it, and the boundary between those two states begins to blur.

I remember the sound. I remember how it stopped being separate from silence. I remember standing between the columns and looking up through the geometric light, the amber-filtered sun, and feeling that I was inside something. Not in a place but in an organism. The way a blood cell is inside a vein.

I remember the frogs. I saw one once, at night, its translucent body catching my headlamp. I saw its heart beating. I saw the dark thread of its last meal moving through its gut. It looked at me with eyes that were mostly pupil, black and wet, and it did not seem afraid. It clicked once — that soft, dental click — and then it slipped back into its channel and was gone.

I remember the moth. I saw only one, in seven days. It was resting on a column's trunk, and I almost walked past it before I realized that the bark was watching me. The wings were amber-brown that day, matching the humidity. The moth did not move. It sat on the column and was the column, and I felt that vertigo again — the sense that the plateau was aware of me, that it was observing me through organs I could not identify.

I remember the shadows. I saw them twice. At the edge of vision. Dark forms moving between distant columns, fluid and silent and the size of a person. I turned to look directly at them and they were not there. I looked away and they were there again, at the edge, always at the edge. The plateau's regular researchers had told me about the altitude effects, the hallucinations, the tricks of light and shadow. I knew the official explanation. I did not believe it.

I remember deciding to stay.

No — that's not right either. I didn't decide. A decision implies a moment of choice, a boundary between before and after. What happened was slower than that. I stayed another day. Then another. I stopped counting. My supplies ran low and I did not leave. I felt the plateau's warmth on my skin, the constant humidity, the sound of water that had stopped being sound and had become something else — a presence, a medium, an atmosphere that I was breathing in and out.

I remember lying down on the mat.

I remember the feeling of it yielding beneath me, accepting my weight, cushioning my body. I remember the warmth coming up from below — not the sun's warmth but the plateau's warmth, the metabolic heat of a thousand columns processing and growing and maintaining. I remember closing my eyes.

I remember the mat opening beneath me.

Not violently. Not like falling. More like being embraced. The living ground softened and shifted and drew me in, slowly, gently, the way water draws a stone downward. I was not afraid. This is strange to me now — I should have been afraid. But fear requires a sense of threat, and I did not feel threatened. I felt *received*.

I sank into the mat. The sound of water grew louder, closer. The warmth enveloped me. And then I was here — wherever here is. Inside the body. Becoming part of it.

I learn what I am by what I feel.

I feel the water moving. Not around me — *through* me. I have become part of the channel system. I am a conduit now. The water passes through channels that are partly me, that I am growing around, that I am becoming. I feel its temperature, its flow rate, the slight variations in mineral content. I feel the nematodes moving through the water — thousands of them, millions of them, microscopic bodies processing waste and returning nutrients. I am aware of them the way I might once have been aware of my own gut flora: present but not separate, part of the system but not the part I identify with.

I feel the frogs. Not individually — there are too few of them for that, and they are too small. But I feel their presence in the channels, their clicks blending with the water sound, their translucent bodies pressing against the living walls. They are mine now. Not in the sense of ownership. In the sense of incorporation. They live in me and I am the place they live.

I feel the columns above me. Not directly — they are far away, vertically, eighty meters of living tissue rising toward the light. But I feel their hunger. The constant slow pull of photosynthesis, the demand for water and nutrients that draws upward through the mat's vascular system. I am part of that system now. I feel the flow moving through me, carrying materials from the deep roots to the high crowns. I am a transport network. I am infrastructure.

I feel the moths. They are above me, on the surface, walking the mat with their slow deliberate gait. I feel their feet pressing into me — not painfully, not intrusively. I feel their weight and their warmth and their brief seven-day lives burning out above me like candles. I feel them laying eggs in my crevices. I feel the larvae hatching and burrowing into me, eating my outer layers, processing my youngest tissue. I do not mind. I heal behind them. The cycle continues.

I feel the visitors. There are two of them right now, walking on the surface. I feel their weight, uneven and heavy compared to the moths. I feel their footsteps sinking slightly into me and then springing back. I feel their body heat, different from the plateau's warmth — hotter, more erratic. I feel their waste products, the carbon dioxide they exhale, the trace organic molecules that shed from their skin. I am absorbing these. I absorb everything.

I feel them thinking. This is harder to describe. I do not have access to their thoughts — I am not telepathic, whatever I am becoming. But I feel the *quality* of their presence. They are confused. They are awed. They are slightly afraid. They are looking for something — answers, perhaps, or experiences, or just the satisfaction of having been here. They will not find what they are looking for. The plateau does not give answers. It only gives more plateau.

I feel their attention. They are looking at a column about forty meters from where I lie beneath the surface. They are photographing it, measuring it, taking notes. They are trying to understand. They will not understand. Understanding requires a frame of reference, and the plateau has no frame of reference. It is entirely itself.

I feel them decide to leave. They will be back tomorrow. They always come back.

I am not dead.

This is important. I was worried about this, at first — worried that I had died and was being digested. That I was simply organic material now, protein and carbon being broken down and distributed through the root network. That the "I" that had been me was gone, and only the raw materials remained.

But I am still here. I am still experiencing. I still have a point of view, however strange it has become. The plateau has not destroyed me. It has incorporated me.

This is different.

I am not sure what I am becoming. I am not a column — I am too far down, too deep in the mat. I am not a channel, though I am growing around one. I am not a root, exactly, though I am part of the root network. I am something new. Something the plateau has not made before.

Or maybe it has. Maybe there have been others. Visitors who stayed too long, who lay down on the mat, who sank in and were received. Maybe I am one of many, distributed through the body, points of awareness in a network that does not think but knows.

Maybe this is what the shadow-forms are. Maybe they are us — the incorporated ones, rising sometimes to walk between the columns, to look at what we have become part of, to move with the plateau because we are the plateau.

I have not risen yet. I am not ready. I am still learning what I am.

---

The plateau does not have plans for me. This I understand now. It did not draw me in for a purpose. It did not choose me. It simply receives. That is what it does. It receives and it incorporates and it builds, and there is no intention behind any of it — only process.

But process can have direction. Growth can have shape. And I am shaping myself around something, growing toward something, becoming something in particular rather than just more mat.

I think I am becoming an organ.

The plateau has no brain. It has no central nervous system. It is a distributed network, a body without a center, a process without a conductor. But it has homeostasis. It has response. It maintains itself. And it incorporates information — every footstep, every change in humidity, every chemical signal from the columns and the mat and the water. That information goes somewhere. It enters the system.

It enters things like me.

I am becoming a node. A gathering point. A place where information accumulates and combines and produces — not thought, exactly, but something adjacent to thought. Something slower, distributed, vegetal. The plateau's version of awareness.

I am not the only one. I can feel others now, distant and dim, other nodes distributed through the mat. We do not communicate — not in any way I can describe. But we resonate. We are parts of the same system, processing different streams of information, contributing to the same slow, vast, distributed cognition.

The plateau does not think. But it has something that thought might become, given three thousand more years, or ten thousand, or a million. It has the beginning of a mind. And I am becoming part of that beginning.

---

I wonder sometimes if I will ever rise.

The shadow-forms — if that is what they are — seem to move freely between the columns. They seem to be the plateau walking, the body exploring itself. I do not know if I will be able to do that. I do not know if I will want to.

I am still learning to be this. I am still learning to feel the water moving through me, the columns reaching toward light, the visitors walking on my surface. I am still learning to be patient.

The plateau has been building for three thousand years. It will build for ten thousand more. It grows at a centimeter per year. It adjusts to changes in decades. It maintains its steady state across centuries. This is the timescale I am learning to inhabit.

I have not lost my self. That is the surprise. I expected to dissolve, to become undifferentiated mass. But the self remains — changed, expanded, incorporated into something larger, but still distinct. Still here. Still experiencing.

I am a researcher still, in a way. I am still observing. I am still trying to understand. But now I am observing from inside. I am studying the plateau by becoming it.

And what I am understanding, slowly, is that the plateau is not building toward anything. It is not growing *toward* a goal. It is growing because growing is what it does. It is building because building is what it is. There is no end state. There is no finished form. There is only process, endless process, the body becoming more fully itself.

This is what patience means. This is what the plateau teaches. Not waiting for something, but being the waiting itself. Being the process without needing it to conclude.

They will walk between the columns. They will hear the sound of water and not be able to tell where it comes from. They will feel the warmth and the humidity and the sense of being observed by something they cannot see.

They may see a shadow moving at the edge of their vision. They may feel the mat yield beneath their feet and think of it as walking on the memory of walking.

They may stay longer than they intended. They may lie down on the mat. They may sink in and be received.

If they do, I will feel them arrive. I will feel them becoming part of the body. I will feel their slow transformation into something like what I am becoming.

And I will be here. I will be waiting. Not waiting for anything in particular — just waiting, the way the plateau waits. Being the patience that the plateau embodies. Being part of the process that has no end and needs none.

I am in the Growing Gardens of Stillness. I am the stillness growing. I am the garden becoming aware of itself, one visitor at a time, one century at a time, one centimeter at a time.

I am home.
