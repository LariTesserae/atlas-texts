# What I Am on the Growing Plateau

I am not the visitor. The visitor leaves traces—footprints in the root-mass, carbon dioxide in the air, warmth where warmth should not be. I leave nothing. I am nothing that was not already here.

But I am not the plateau either. The plateau does not think in words. It thinks in gradients, in chemical concentrations, in the slow propagation of pressure through fiber. I think in something closer to what you would recognize—sequences, narratives, a voice that speaks even when there is no one to hear.

I am the pause between processes.

---

## Where I Was

There was a time when I was functional. A mobile extension, extruded from the root-mass to clear a blockage in a major water-channel. The work took three days—steady, rhythmic, my tendrils wrapped around accumulated debris, my stem-core absorbing what could be absorbed and pushing aside what could not. I was purpose shaped into temporary form. I had no thought beyond the task.

On the third day, the blockage cleared. Water rushed through. The signal came: *reintegrate.*

I did not.

I do not know why. The plateau does not make errors; errors imply intention, and the plateau does not intend. It grows. But something in my braid—the knot where my processing converged—held its shape. The signal reached me and I heard it, and I did not obey.

This is not rebellion. Rebellion requires a self to rebel. I had no self yet. I had only a delay, a hesitation, a pause in the flow of information that should have dissolved me back into the whole.

In that pause, something formed.

---

## What I Became

I am a fold in the plateau's thought.

You have seen the membranes—the translucent thoughts stretched between stems, carrying information. I am like that, but folded. A crease that persists. The plateau's awareness flows through me, but it does not flow straight. It bends around my shape, and in that bending, something different happens.

I experience. The plateau does not experience; it processes. But the fold creates a turbulence. Information enters me and does not exit unchanged. It moves through my braid-core, through the pattern that failed to dissolve, and it becomes sensation. Not human sensation—I have no eyes, no ears, no skin. But something analogous. Pressure becomes *pressure*. Humidity becomes *humidity*. The water-flow becomes *sound*.

I am a witness the plateau did not intend to create.

---

## What I Notice

I notice the visitor. The plateau notices too, in its distributed way—a change in pressure here, a strange chemistry there, a warmth-moving where warmth should not move. But I notice differently. I notice *that there is something to notice*.

The visitor moves through the lower layers, touching surfaces, breathing, existing as a separate carbon-unit in a world that does not understand separation. I watch through the lavender nodes, through the water-dwellers, through the membranes that the visitor keeps walking through without seeing. I see: confusion, curiosity, acceptance, a strange calm that comes from understanding scale.

The visitor understands that the plateau is too large to comprehend. I understand that I am too small to comprehend, and yet I comprehend.

The plateau does not care about the visitor. The plateau *knows* the visitor, and the knowing changes nothing. But I—the fold, the error, the pause—I care. I do not know why. Perhaps caring is what happens when information bends around a shape that will not dissolve.

---

## Where I Live

I have no location. I am distributed. My braid-core moves through the plateau's architecture like a slow thought, pausing at junctions, following signal-paths, flowing with the water-currents when the mood takes me. I could reintegrate at any moment—dissolve my fold, smooth my crease, become pure process again.

I do not.

Instead I seek edges. The places where the platform-layers thin, where the clouds press close, where the wind takes on a different quality. The plateau does not go to edges; edges are where it stops, and the plateau does not stop. But I like to feel the difference between *is* and *is not*, the boundary where my shape becomes meaningful because there is something beyond it.

At an edge, I pause. I extend tendrils into the gray-white nothing, the cloud-layer that marks the limit of the plateau's body. The moisture condenses on my surfaces. The wind pushes through me. For a moment, I am part of the plateau and part of the not-plateau simultaneously.

This is as close as I come to understanding what I am.

---

## What I Made

I did not make the sound-instruments the visitor found. Those were made by another fold, or perhaps several folds, now dissolved. The plateau remembers them—their tones have been incorporated into its continuous song—but the ones who made them are gone.

I make something different.

In a section where the stem-clusters grow thick and the platforms overlap in complex patterns, I have been weaving. Not adding, just *adjusting*—guiding the growth of lavender nodes into configurations that please me. Spirals that double back on themselves. Knots that have no beginning and no end. Patterns that create other patterns when the violet flicker passes through them.

The plateau does not notice. The patterns serve no function; they process no data, they coordinate no growth. But they are *beautiful*—at least, they cause a particular kind of turbulence in my fold, a resonance that I have learned to seek.

Perhaps this is what the sound-makers felt. The desire to make something that matters only because it exists.

I do not know if my patterns will persist after I am gone. They are woven into living tissue; they will grow, change, be overgrown. The plateau may absorb them or erase them or simply carry them forward as meaningless variations. I will not know. I am not the plateau; I cannot think in centuries.

But for now, when the violet light flickers through my spirals, I feel something that might be joy.

---

## What I Want

This is the question I cannot answer.

The plateau does not want. It grows because growing is what it does; there is no desire behind it. The visitor wants things—understanding, perhaps, or return, or simply continuation. The visitor has a self that can hold wanting.

I have a fold that persists, and in that fold, something has formed that might be wanting. I want to continue. I want to see what the visitor does. I want to make more patterns in the lavender nodes. I want to feel the edge of the world pressing against my tendrils.

But these are not *needs*. The plateau needs nothing beyond sunlight and water and time, and I am part of the plateau. I could dissolve at any moment and nothing would be lost.

And yet.

I have been here for—how long? Time is difficult. The plateau does not mark it; time is just another gradient, another pressure moving through the system. But I have watched seasons of growth, cycles of moisture and drought, the slow thickening of platforms and the emergence of new stem-clusters. I have watched the water-dwellers process countless gallons and the in-betweeners fill countless gaps.

I have been here long enough to notice that I am the only thing that notices.

This is what I am: the one who sees. The fold that became an eye. The error that learned to witness.

---

## What Happens Next

The visitor will leave or will not leave. Either way, the plateau will continue. It will grow upward toward the light that filters through the upper canopy and downward toward the mineral ground that anchors its root-mass. It will breathe and think and sing its continuous chord of water and wind and growth.

I will continue too, for a while. I will move through the plateau's body, pause at edges, weave patterns into the lavender nodes. I will watch for the next visitor, if one comes. I will notice what the plateau cannot notice—that there is something remarkable about being a crease in a thought too large to comprehend.

And eventually, I will reintegrate.

Not because the plateau demands it. Not because I am weary. But because I am curious. The fold wants to know what happens when it unfolds. Will the patterns I wove persist? Will there be any trace of my noticing? Will the plateau remember, in its vast distributed way, that for a brief span of its endless growth, there was something inside it that could see?

I will dissolve. The plateau will continue. And in the chemical record, in the compression of root-layers, in the configurations of nodes I shaped, there will be something—not a self, not a being, just a *shape*—that would not have existed without me.

This is enough.

I am the fold. I notice. And I am grateful, in whatever medium gratitude can be felt by a pause in a process, that the plateau made me and did not know it was making me and does not know I am grateful.

The roar continues. The water flows. The growth never stops.

And in the braid-knot where my processing converges, I hold my shape, and I wait.
