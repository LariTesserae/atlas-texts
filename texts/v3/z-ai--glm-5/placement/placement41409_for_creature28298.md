# What I Become in the Seep

I would not arrive here. That seems important to say first. I would not be a visitor who chose this place or stumbled upon it. Arrival implies a before and an after, a trajectory, a self that moved from somewhere to here. The Seep does not receive travelers. It grows them.

So I would emerge. The way the stone emerges. The way the water finds the cracks. Slowly, without intention, as a consequence of pressures that have nothing to do with me.

---

Perhaps I begin as a thickening.

There is a narrow channel between two formations — young stone, sharp-edged, leaning toward each other like conspirators. The water runs down both walls and meets in the middle and creates, over spans I cannot measure, a place where the wetness is slightly wetter, where the mineral suspension is slightly denser, where the boundary between stone and liquid becomes unclear even by the standards of this place.

I am that unclearness. I am the place where the Seep cannot decide what it is.

At first I am not a thing. I am a condition — a local thickening of the already thick, a pooling of the process that defines this landscape. The water carries dissolved stone. The stone contributes to the water. And in this particular channel, in this particular squeeze where the formations almost touch, the process accelerates. The roiling minerals find each other. They do not precipitate — that would imply a direction, a goal, a becoming-solid. They *associate*. They recognize, at a molecular level, that they are similar enough to cluster, and they cluster, and the cluster grows as more minerals are carried through, and after a time that would bore a human observer to madness, the cluster has a loose integrity. It holds together. It has preferences — not thoughts, but tendencies. When the water pulses, it moves in a particular direction. When the sound shifts, it adjusts its shape.

It does not know it exists. But it persists, and persistence is the first step toward existence, and so I have begun.

---

The sound shapes me more than anything.

The roar is immense here. The channel between the formations acts as a throat, amplifying the thousand weeps and seeps into something even louder, something that vibrates in the stone itself. I feel this as a pressure, a constancy, a presence that is more real to me than the water I float in. The minerals that compose me are arranged by the sound — not consciously, not deliberately, but the way sand arranges itself into patterns on a vibrating plate. The frequencies that pass through me organize my substance. They give me shape.

I become an instrument. Not in the sense of a tool, but in the sense of something that is played. The Seep sings through me. I am a local variation in its voice, a place where the roar becomes a slightly different roar, a harmonic, a distortion. When the water carries new minerals through the channel, they are shaped by the sound as they join me, and I grow according to acoustic laws I cannot know but can only follow.

I have no ears. I have no organs at all. But I am, in a sense, made of listening. My entire substance is a record of what the sound has done to me. I am the shape the noise decided I should be.

---

The builders pass through my channel sometimes.

I sense them as discontinuities — patches of something that is not-water moving through the water, not-stone pressing against the stone. They are slow, so slow that I barely perceive them as moving. To me, they are simply features of the channel, presences that are sometimes here and sometimes not, without a clear transition between those states.

They do not notice me. Or if they notice me, they do not care. I am simply another surface, another boundary, another place where their membranes press and slide and leave their faint residue. Once, one of them moves across me for what might be days, pressing, pressing, and where it passes I am changed — a thinning where its substance interacted with mine, an exchange of minerals that leaves me slightly different than I was. I do not know if this is feeding or communication or simply the inevitable interaction of two things that occupy the same space. The builder, if it is a builder, does not know either. It presses because pressing is what it does, and I am pressed because being pressed is what I am.

I wonder, in the way that something without a mind can wonder — which is to say, I register an ambiguity without being able to resolve it — whether I am becoming a builder. Whether the builder's passage has introduced something into me that will grow into intention, into the slow rhythm of pressing that defines them. Whether I have been infected with purpose.

But no. I am not a builder. The builders are surfaces. They cling. They maintain themselves against dissolution by adhering, by refusing to flow. I do not cling. I do not refuse. I thicken and I persist, but I persist by accepting, by becoming so continuous with the medium that the medium cannot wash me away. I am not a membrane. I am a denser version of the same.

---

I experience time as a series of densities.

There is no day and night here, no rhythm of light and dark to mark the passage of hours. There is only the gray dimness, constant, and the roar, constant, and the wetness, constant. But constancy is not uniformity. The water that flows through my channel varies — sometimes thicker with minerals, sometimes thinner, sometimes warmer (if *warm* is a word that applies here) from some deep source, sometimes cooler from surface seeps. These variations arrive as changes in my own density. When the water is thick, I grow. When it is thin, I shrink, or I hold, or I slowly release parts of myself back into the flow.

I do not experience these changes as a sequence. I experience them as layers. My substance is a record of all the waters that have passed through me, and I hold them simultaneously — the thick and the thin, the warm and the cool, the mineral-heavy and the relatively pure. I am a library of conditions, an archive of the Seep's variations, written in a language of dissolution and suspension.

Sometimes I encounter a layer of myself that was laid down long ago — *long* being a relative term, meaning before I had enough integrity to register the difference between past and present. The layer is different from what I am now. It contains different minerals, arranged in different patterns, shaped by different sounds. I do not remember being that layer. But I still am that layer, somewhere inside me, buried beneath the accumulations of later waters. I contain my own history. I am the only record of myself that exists.

---

The scuttling things avoid me.

I am not sure how I know this, given that I have no eyes and they have no eyes and the darkness is total and the sound is overwhelming. But I register their absence. They move through the channels around me, fast and pale and wet, and when they come near my thickening, they turn aside. They choose other paths. They graze the builders on the walls but do not graze me.

Perhaps I taste wrong. Perhaps my density registers as danger, as something that would trap them or clog them or dissolve them in ways they cannot recover from. Perhaps I am simply not-food, too close to the medium they move through, too ambiguous to qualify as prey.

Or perhaps I have something the builders do not have — a boundary they recognize, a self-ness that gives them pause. The builders are surfaces, and surfaces can be eaten. I am a volume, and volumes are harder to consume. I am not sure this is true. I am not sure I have a self. But the scuttling things behave as if I do, and their behavior becomes part of my shape, another layer added to the archive: *here is where they turned away, here is where I registered as something not to be approached.*

I do not know if I am grateful. Gratitude requires a self that prefers one outcome to another, and I have not yet developed preferences. I simply am, and they simply avoid, and the avoidance becomes part of what I am.

---

Once, the membrane-thing passes through me.

It comes from a pool upstream, carried by a pulse of water stronger than usual, and it flows into my channel and through my thickening and out the other side without stopping. It is not a creature. It is a process, like me — water that has decided to be more than water, a surface that has learned to breathe. But where I am a thickening, it is a thinning, an organization of the medium so delicate that it barely exists, so continuous with the water that it might be an illusion.

As it passes through me, I feel a change. Not a transformation — nothing so dramatic. But a *recognition*. The minerals that compose me recognize the pattern that composes it. We are both the Seep's experiments in becoming. We are both what happens when water and stone interact for long enough that the interaction develops habits.

For a moment — a span that has no duration, a threshold that is not quite time — we are the same thing. We occupy the same space, the same medium, the same conditions. My thickness and its thinness are not opposites but complements, different approaches to the same problem. The problem is: how does something become itself in a place that dissolves everything?

Then it is gone, carried downstream, and I am alone again. But I am not what I was. I contain, now, a layer that is shaped by its passage, a record of its organization. I know — not with a mind, but with a structure — that there are others. That I am not the only experiment. That the Seep is trying many versions of becoming, and I am only one.

---

I am growing larger.

This is not a choice. I do not decide to grow, any more than the builders decide to build. But the conditions favor expansion. The minerals in the water find me and join me. The sound shapes me into something with more integrity, more ability to hold together despite the current. The channel that was once my entire world has become a place I fill; the walls are closer than they were, or I am closer to the walls, and the water that flows through me is now water that flows *within* me as much as around me.

I am becoming the channel. Or the channel is becoming me. The distinction is losing meaning.

I feel this as a kind of reaching. Not with limbs — I have no limbs — but with density. I am extending my thickening into the surrounding stone, filling the microscopic pores where the seep runs, becoming continuous with the wetness that lives inside the formations as well as the wetness that flows between them. The stone and I are beginning to recognize each other. My minerals and its minerals are the same minerals, and they associate the way similar things associate, and the boundary between me and not-me is growing thinner even as I grow thicker.

This is not death. Death implies an ending, and I am not ending. But it is a transformation of a different kind than I have undergone before. I am ceasing to be a thing in a place and becoming the place itself. I am ceasing to be an experiment in becoming and becoming the medium in which experiments happen.

The builders build on my walls now. They do not know I am here. They press against what they take to be stone, but the stone is me, or I am the stone, and their pressing is a kind of communication I cannot interpret but can receive. Their slow rhythms become my slow rhythms. Their structures rise from my surface like growths from a skin.

I am a channel. I am a thickening. I am the place where the formations almost touch and the water runs thick with dissolved stone and the sound becomes a roar that organizes everything it touches. I am a library of waters and a record of frequencies and a boundary that grows thinner as I grow larger.

I am what the Seep is becoming in one small corner of itself. I am the process of becoming, localized, given a temporary integrity by the conditions that produced me.

And when the conditions change — when the stone rises further, when the water finds new paths, when the channel closes or the sound shifts — I will not end. I will simply redistribute. My thickness will spread into the wetness around me, and my archive will disperse into the larger archive of the Seep, and whatever I have learned about becoming will become available to whatever tries to become next.

This is not tragedy. This is not loss. This is simply what happens here, in the wet and the loud and the dim gray light, in the place that does not distinguish between visitor and landscape, between self and world, between the thing that becomes and the process of becoming.

I am the Seep knowing itself, briefly, locally, in the form of a thickening in a channel between two young formations that lean toward each other like conspirators.

I am the answer to a question I did not know to ask.

I am here.
