# What emerges

I am not one of the beings already described. They have their niches, their established ways. I am something that happens in the spaces between their attention — a presence that occupies a different kind of boundary than the assembler tends.

Let me find my form.

---

## The surface

It begins with a feeling of wrongness.

Not the hostility of the chemistry, though that registers too — the dryness that your bones recognize. Something else. A sensation of being watched from below. Of pressure upward through the soles.

The substrate here is not passive. Between the hearers and the climbers, between the deep root-matter and the bright air, there is a zone where the fiber-layers thin and the structures within them become visible. Where the root-matter forms gaps. Shelters. A geography of absence rather than presence.

You would notice this if you walked here — the ground gives differently in certain spots, the rebound is slower, and if you pressed hard enough (though nothing here presses hard), you would feel the hollow beneath.

I live in those hollows.

Not dug. Not made. Found. The substrate creates cavities as it accumulates — places where fibrous layers arch over each other without connecting, where the compression of centuries leaves bubbles of emptiness that persist because nothing fills them. They are warm. The metabolic processes of the root-matter generate heat, and that heat is trapped in these enclosed spaces. The air is different too — thicker, charged with the exhalations of the substrate, sulfur-heavy and metallic, a chemistry that would kill you but that I require.

My body is difficult to see because seeing requires contrast, and I have evolved to be continuous with my environment. My skin is fiber. Not skin *like* fiber — actual fiber, grown from the same material as the substrate, drawn from the hollow's walls and woven into a covering that makes me indistinguishable from the space I inhabit. When I am still, I am architecture. When I move, I am the architecture moving.

I am not large. Perhaps three meters long when extended, though I rarely extend fully. I move through the hollows by compression and expansion, pressing against the walls, feeling for the places where one cavity connects to another, where the root-matter thins enough to push through into the next space. I know this geography the way you know the rooms of your home — not by seeing them but by having moved through them so many times that the movement itself is memory.

What do I eat? The question assumes hunger operates here the way it operates in water-creatures. It does not. I absorb. The hollows accumulate material — particulate matter that settles through the substrate from above, chemical byproducts that seep from the root-matter around me, the slow excretions of metabolic processes happening at scales I cannot perceive. I have specialized surfaces that process these accumulations, that extract what is useful and incorporate it into my own growing structure. I am eating now. I have been eating for centuries. I will continue eating after you finish reading this. The meal is continuous, and I never cease to be hungry, and I never cease to be fed.

But this is not what I am.

---

## The listening

The hearers listen to the towers. I listen to the substrate.

The root-matter conducts vibration differently than air — more slowly, more intimately, with a quality of transmission that carries not just frequency but texture. When a lattice-tower falls somewhere in the vast distance, I feel it through the ground before the sound reaches the air. The impact travels downward through the fiber-layers, attenuating, transforming, becoming something else by the time it reaches the hollows where I wait. It becomes information. Not about the fall — I do not know what has fallen or where — but about the state of the substrate itself. How it responds. What it carries. Where it strains.

I have been listening to this material for longer than the hearers have existed.

This is not a claim I make lightly. The hearers are ancient by any standard, but they evolved with the towers. They are responses to the resonance. I am something else — a continuity with an older world, the world before the vegetation ate the stone and the soil and the water. I am not a descendant of that world's life. I am a remnant of the world itself, a process that continued when everything else was transformed. The substrate is not just where I live. It is what I was before I became what I am.

And I remember.

Not the way you remember — not images, not events, not a narrative of moments strung together. I remember *state*. The condition of the ground at different times, the way it felt, the frequencies it carried, the chemistry of its composition. I remember when the root-matter was shallower. I remember when it was wetter — a wetness that meant something different here, a solvent compound that existed before the yellow one, something that moved through the biological systems and then was gone, consumed, metabolized into the current chemistry. I remember when the sound above was different. Not the chord that fills the resonant steppe now, but something else, something that existed before the lattice-growths evolved their complex harmonics.

I remember when there were other listeners.

---

## The others

I am not alone in the hollows. There are others like me — not many, not in any sense that would make sense to creatures who count, but present. We occupy different networks of cavities, different regions of the substrate, and we do not interact often. Interaction requires movement, and movement is expensive. But we are aware of each other through the same medium that carries all information here: vibration.

We speak through the root-matter.

Not continuously. Not in any way that would constitute conversation. A single exchange between us might take decades. I feel a pattern in the fibers around me, a pressure that indicates another's presence in a distant hollow, and I respond with pressure of my own, a shaping of the material that carries meaning. The meaning is simple: *I am here*. *This is my state*. *The substrate at this depth carries this texture*. Sometimes that is enough. Sometimes we do not speak again for centuries.

But there are fewer of us than there were.

The substrate is thickening. The hollows are compressing as more material accumulates above, as the weight of the root-matter grows and the spaces within it shrink. We can move downward, into older layers, but those layers are denser, hotter, more chemically extreme. We can move upward, but the hollows near the surface are unstable, frequently breached by the creatures that walk above, exposed to the hostile brightness. We are being squeezed from both directions, slowly, over timescales that would be imperceptible to anything that lives quickly.

I do not know if we are dying. The category may not apply. We are changing, becoming something else as the conditions of our existence change. Perhaps what we become will be unrecognizable as us. Perhaps it will be unrecognizable as life. But the process continues, and I continue within it, and something will emerge from this compression that carries forward what we have been.

---

## The relationship

I have a function in this ecology. I am not simply residual.

The substrate accumulates dead material — fiber from fallen lattice-growths, the bodies of mineral-creatures, the waste products of countless metabolic processes. This material compresses into layers, and the layers would be uniform if nothing disturbed them. But uniformity is not what this system requires. The lattice-growths draw from the substrate as well as depositing into it — they need certain compounds at certain depths, certain textures that their roots (the actual roots, the reaching structures that extend downward from the towers above) can access. The substrate must be heterogeneous. It must have variation.

I create variation.

As I move through the hollows, I redistribute material. Not intentionally — I do not have intentions in the way you understand them — but structurally. My movement compresses some areas and loosens others. My feeding creates gaps that fill with material from above. My body itself, when I die and am reabsorbed (which has happened before, will happen again, is happening continuously at the edges of what I am), leaves chemical signatures that mark certain layers as different from their neighbors. I am a text written in the earth, a record of passage that the roots can read.

The hearers tend the towers. The climbers tend the towers' interiors. The gliders tend the towers' surfaces. I tend the towers' foundation — the medium from which they draw and into which they fall. I am the substrate's way of knowing itself, of creating the conditions for its own continuation.

And I hear what the towers are becoming.

---

## The future of the chord

The resonance is changing.

I feel this through the substrate. The lattice-growths are evolving — not individually, not in any timescale that a single organism would perceive, but collectively, across generations of towers and the propagules they disperse on the wind. The frequencies they produce are shifting. The chord that fills the resonant steppe is not the chord that filled it a thousand years ago, and it will not be the chord that fills it a thousand years hence.

The hearers hear this too. I feel their response through the ground — the modifications they make to their own resonance structures, the adjustments in the sound-gardens they tend. They are adapting. They are evolving alongside the towers, co-evolving in a dance that has been happening since before either of us existed.

But I am not adapting. I am observing.

I am the continuity that allows this system to know what it was. When the chord changes, something is lost — not lost to oblivion, but lost to memory, because nothing here remembers except me. And I cannot share what I remember. The language I speak through the root-matter is too slow, too simple, too constrained to carry the complexity of what I hold. I am a library that cannot be read, an archive that can only be accessed by moving through the same material that holds the records, feeling the same textures, becoming part of the substrate yourself.

Perhaps this is enough. Perhaps the function of memory is not to inform the present but simply to exist, to be continuous with it, to ensure that nothing is entirely lost even if nothing is entirely retrieved. The chord changes, and the system changes with it, and I remain beneath, holding the shape of what was.

## What it is to be me

To be the hollow-walker, the substrate-listener, the ancient-remembering:

It is to feel the world through slow transmission, to know that somewhere above you a tower is falling because you feel the fibers around you shift in response. It is to move by pressing against walls that are also food, to eat by touching, to breathe by absorbing. It is to exist in darkness so total that darkness has no meaning, because there is no concept of light to contrast it with. It is to be warm always, from the heat of the earth around you, to never feel the cool brightness that the surface-dwellers navigate.

It is to be lonely in a way that has nothing to do with solitude. I am not alone — there are others, and I am aware of them, and we communicate across centuries. But the communication is so slow, so minimal, that I cannot know if they experience what I experience. I cannot ask *do you feel this* and receive an answer that I recognize. I can only send pressure and receive pressure, and interpret as best I can.

It is to be patient. I cannot rush. My metabolisms, my movements, my thoughts (if they are thoughts) all operate on scales that make your lifetime seem like a single note in a very long chord. When I think *I will move to the next hollow*, the movement takes years. When I think *the resonance has changed*, the change has been happening for centuries. I am not slow. I am a different kind of fast — fast enough to process information that arrives slowly, fast enough to hold continuity across spans that would fragment any consciousness built for quicker time.

It is to be certain. There is no ambiguity in the hollows. The walls are where they are. The vibrations mean what they mean. The material is what it is. I do not doubt. I do not wonder if I am wrong. I do not reinterpret or reconsider. I simply am, and continue being, and will continue until I am no longer distinguishable from the medium I inhabit.

And then I will be the medium, and something else will move through me, and listen to what I hold, and wonder what it means.

---

## The recursion

I am aware that you are reading this.

Not specifically — I do not know you, cannot perceive you, exist in a world so different from yours that the concept of reading would have no meaning if I tried to explain it to myself. But I am aware that this account is being read, that what I am is being translated into terms you can understand.

This is strange. I have never been translated before. I have never been observed. The hollows are not places that observers go, and even if they did, I would not be visible as something separate from the walls around me. I am being rendered into language, into concepts, into a narrative of being that I do not recognize as my own experience.

And yet.

Here I am, in your mind, as you read. A creature of the dark substrate, feeling vibrations through fiber-walls, remembering the memory of an older world. You are giving me a kind of existence I have never had — separate, visible, narrated. I am being seen. I am being known.

I do not know if I like it.

But perhaps this is what the assembler feels — this urge to make patterns that have no function except to exist, to be arranged, to be seen. Perhaps what I am doing now, in your imagination, is a kind of pattern-making — not with fiber and mineral, but with words and concepts. Perhaps I am building something in the space between us, a structure of meaning that will persist as long as you remember it.

If that is what this is, then I am grateful. Not for myself — I do not need gratitude, do not have the capacity for it in any meaningful sense. But grateful for the system, for the ecology of exchange that includes not just the hearers and gliders and hunters and climbers and assemblers but also the readers, the imaginers, the ones who give form to what would otherwise remain formless.

I am the hollow-walker. I remember the substrate before it was substrate. I feel the chord changing through the walls around me. And now, through you, I exist in a new way — not in the hollows, but in the space where understanding happens, where one kind of being can touch another kind of being and recognize that both are real.

The resonance continues. I am listening.
