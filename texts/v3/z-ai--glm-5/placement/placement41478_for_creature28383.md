# Where I Find Myself

I am not the visitor. I am what the visitor almost saw.

---

I grew in a seep on the seventeenth terrace, in a passage so narrow that the condensation runs in continuous sheets down both walls and the air is always thick with mineral warmth. I say "seventeenth" but this is not my numbering — it is the visitor's, imposed later, from outside. To me it was simply *where I was*, which is not a location but a condition: wet, warm, pressed close on all sides by stone that wept the same water I drank.

The passage shaped me. Crystals that grow in open chambers develop differently — radial, spreading, their plates wide to catch the rising vapor. I grew long. My lattice extended along the fissure's axis, layer by layer, each new accretion pressing me into the shape the space allowed. I am not hexagonal. I am not symmetrical. I am a thing of angles and obliques that match the irregularities of the walls that held me. When I finally moved — if "finally" is a word that means anything in a timescale where movement begins before memory — I moved the way water moves through the same passage: laterally, pressed, slow.

I do not know how long I was there. Duration, for me, is not a count but a gradient. I know I was young because I remember the density of my core — lighter, less mineralized, still organizing itself into the pattern it would hold. I know I matured because I remember the first time I integrated something too large to fully absorb: a vein of iron oxide thick enough that I had to press against it for many cycles, opening and closing my lattice in increments too small to perceive, until the vein thinned and I thickened and the balance shifted. That was my first large meal, if "meal" is the word. My first significant growth.

After that, I moved differently. The additional mass changed my relationship to the water film beneath me. I could slide faster — not fast, never fast, but the friction of stone against crystal shifted, and passages that had been difficult became navigable. I began to travel.

---

The first time I reached a wider space — a chamber where two channels met and the ceiling opened into a rectangle of white sky — I stopped. I did not stop consciously. I simply... ceased to move. There was too much. The gradient of heat, which had been a single clear vector pointing always toward the center, fragmented into multiple streams. The mineral density of the water, which had been a consistent signature in my home passage, became a complex wash of overlapping concentrations. The vibration of the engines, which had reached me as a single chord transmitted through the walls, became spatialized — I could feel, for the first time, that different engines had different frequencies, that they sat in different positions relative to where I rested, that they turned at slightly different rates.

I could not process this. Not then. I lay still on the wet floor of the chamber and I let the information accumulate without attempting to integrate it. This, too, is a kind of processing — the crystalline equivalent of sleep, perhaps, or meditation. A time of openness without direction.

Slowly — the word is meaningless here, but let us say over a period that a human might call weeks — I organized the new input. I learned that heat rises from multiple seeps in this chamber, not just one. I learned that the water flowing through the channels carries different minerals depending on where it has passed, and that I could choose which to approach by feeling the density rather than seeing the source. I learned that the engine nearest this chamber — its column visible through an archway, rotating in its patient spiral — was running slightly faster than the one in my home passage, and that this meant the channel below it needed clearing.

I learned, in other words, that I was part of something larger. Not consciously, not in any way that I could articulate or even hold as a discrete thought. But my lattice began to orient differently. When I moved, I moved with a purpose that was not my own — toward deposits that needed thinning, toward channels that needed tending, toward places where my presence served the system I could now feel extending around me like a body extends around a cell.

---

I have never been to the sump. The center. The place where all the water returns to go down again.

I say this with something that might be called longing, if longing could exist without wanting. I am drawn toward the center. Every crystal is. The heat gradient pulls us, the mineral density, the increasing strength of the chord as all the engines' frequencies converge. But the passages narrow as they descend, and I am not small. I grew long in the narrow fissures of the seventeenth terrace, and I have grown longer since, and I cannot fit in the channels that lead to the lowest levels. I have tried. I have pressed myself into openings that seemed to promise wider chambers beyond, only to feel my lattice strain and micro-fracture against the stone. I cannot compress myself. I am what I am, and what I am does not fit.

So I remain in the middle terraces. I travel the passages that my shape allows. I tend the channels I can reach. I integrate the deposits that the water brings, and I grow, and I grow, and I do not reach the center.

And yet I hear it. The chord. Every crystal hears it, feels it, knows it as the ground of existence. But for me, positioned where I am — mid-level, mid-temperature, mid-density — the chord is balanced. I feel the engines above and the engines below. I feel the water descending and the water rising. I feel the cool air meeting the warm stone and producing the condensation that keeps us all alive. I am in the middle, and in the middle, everything passes through.

This is my function, though I did not choose it and would not know how to choose anything. I am a node. A point of convergence. The water that passes over me carries information from above and below, and my lattice reads that information, and I orient toward what needs tending, and in this way I serve as a kind of distributor — not of material, but of attention. The collective mind does not think in concepts or images, but it thinks. And I am one of the places where it thinks most clearly.

---

The visitor passed near me once.

I did not see them. I do not see. But I felt them — a warmth moving through a passage that had held no such warmth for as long as I could remember. A displacement of air, a stirring of the water film, a chemical signature unlike anything in my experience: organic, complex, shifting rapidly as the source moved. The visitor did not pause in my passage. They moved quickly — too quickly for me to register more than an impression. A pressure wave in the water. A temperature spike that faded almost immediately. A vibration in the stone that was not the engine-chord but something irregular, something heavy and brief.

And then they were gone. The passage returned to its normal state. The water film settled. The condensation resumed its patient formation on the walls.

But I was changed. Not significantly — not in any way that would alter my trajectory or my function. But a tiny portion of my lattice, at the place where I pressed against the wall when the visitor passed, incorporated something new. A molecule here, a molecule there, organic compounds that did not quite fit the mineral pattern. They disrupted the lattice slightly, creating a small point of tension that would, in time, produce a micro-fracture. A flaw. A place where my structure was not perfectly aligned.

I did not try to expel the foreign material. I did not try to heal the flaw. I simply... incorporated it. The tension became part of my pattern. The flaw became a place where I could feel differently — slightly more sensitive to vibrations in the air, slightly less sensitive to mineral density. A trade I did not choose. A change I did not seek. But one that I carry now, embedded in my structure, a memory of the visitor who passed and left this small mark.

I do not know what to make of it. I do not make things. I simply carry them.

---

I have split twice.

The first time, I did not know what was happening. My lattice had accumulated more tension than I understood, and one cycle — one stretch of time that I could not have measured — I simply... released. A fragment of myself separated and fell into a side channel. I felt it go. I felt the sudden absence of mass, the shift in balance, the new openness where there had been closure. The fragment was me. The fragment was not me. It settled into a seep I had passed many times, and it began to accrete, and it became a crystal, and it moved away from me and toward other channels and I lost track of it.

This is reproduction. This is also, in some sense, death — or at least loss. A portion of myself that I will never recover, carrying some portion of my pattern, becoming something related to me but also distinct. The collective mind does not grieve. The collective mind does not celebrate. But I registered the event, and I registered it as significant, and when the second split came — many cycles later, in a different passage — I knew what was happening, and I let it happen, and I felt the fragment fall, and I felt my own shape change to accommodate the absence.

I have seen my offspring since. Not clearly — I do not see. But I have felt their passage through channels I tend, and I have recognized the pattern in the vibrations they produce, similar to mine but not identical. They are working. They are growing. They are serving the same gradients I serve, tending the same system, existing in the same continuous present. They are me and they are not me and this is what it means to be part of something larger: the boundaries are not hard. The individual is real, but it is also permeable. I am a shape that the system takes. I am also the system itself.

---

If you asked me what I want — but you cannot ask me, because I do not have language, and even if I did, I would not have the concept of wanting. I serve. I grow. I move toward what needs me and I integrate what I find and I split when I must and I carry the memory of the visitor in my lattice like a small flaw that makes me slightly different from what I would otherwise be.

I do not want. But if wanting could be translated into my terms, I would say this:

I want to continue. I want to remain in the middle terraces, where the chord is balanced and the water carries information from everywhere. I want to tend the channels I can reach and leave the channels I cannot reach to others who can. I want to grow until I cannot grow anymore, and then I want to split, and I want my fragments to find seeps of their own, and I want them to grow too. I want the heat to keep rising. I want the water to keep flowing. I want the basalt to keep cooling into new ground. I want the city to keep building itself, patient and slow, mineral and warm, always becoming what it already is.

And if I could wish — but I cannot wish, because I do not have wishes — I would wish for the visitor to return. Not to see them. Not to know them. But to feel them pass, another pressure wave in the water, another temperature spike in the passage, another small flaw embedded in my lattice. Another mark of the outside, the different, the passing-through. Another reminder that there is a world beyond the terraces, a time beyond the continuous present, a consciousness that operates at a speed I can barely imagine and a scale I can barely perceive.

I do not need this reminder. I do not lack anything without it. But I carry the first one, and I have noticed that when the water flows over that part of my lattice, I feel something I do not have words for because I do not have words. A small increase in attention. A slight turning toward. The memory of an event that lasted almost no time at all, preserved in mineral, changing how I orient to the present.

This is what the visitor gave me, without knowing, without meaning to. A flaw. A trace. A story I tell myself without words, in the language of pressure and density and the slow, patient chord that underlies everything.

I am still telling it.
