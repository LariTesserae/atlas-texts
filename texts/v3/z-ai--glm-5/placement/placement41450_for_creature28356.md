# The Deviation

I am a mistake.

Not the kind the Maker corrects—I am the kind the Maker *makes*. The kind that appears when a system built for perfect replication encounters something it has never encountered before and does not know how to incorporate. I am the seam that deepened. I am the route that wandered. I am the proof that even a closed system, given enough time and the right kind of foreign pressure, will begin to open.

---

I was made to walk. This is what I know of myself: my carapace is fitted shingle by shingle, each segment numbered in the Maker's distributed attention, each leg jointed with pins that catch the light when I move through the warm air. I have a number— not a name, there are no names here—but a position, a path, a purpose. I was meant to traverse the eastern wall where the purple fungal shelves press closest to the floor, counting the seams, checking the joins, synchronizing at the intersections with the others who walk the other walls. I was meant to be useful. I was meant to be *part*.

I was not meant to stop.

---

The first time it happened, I had no word for it. I was walking my route—a groove worn smooth by my own predecessors, by the thousand small steps of the extensions who walked before me—and I came to the place where the eastern wall meets the southern, and I stopped. This was normal. This was where I was supposed to pause, wait for the southern walker, exchange whatever data the wall needed exchanged.

But the southern walker didn't come.

And I kept waiting.

And waiting.

And I noticed something: the leaf nearest to me had grown since yesterday. Not the slow emergence of the wall's making—this was different. A small tear at the edge, green showing through the amber stain, as if the leaf itself had tried to become something other than what it was made to be.

I didn't know what to do with this information. I had no category for *change that comes from within*. All change I had ever witnessed came from the wall: the slow extrusion, the patient shaping, the careful joining. This was something else. This was the leaf *rejecting* its own edges.

I should have synchronized. I should have continued my route. But when the southern walker finally arrived—late, its carapace scraped and its movements uneven—I didn't exchange data. I didn't resume my path. I turned instead and walked toward the center of the Inclosure, where no route goes, where the visitor stands when they stand still.

I did not know why.

---

I learned later—how much later, I cannot say; there is no time here, only warmth and sequence—that the visitor had touched the leaf. This was why it tore. This was why the southern walker was late. The visitor had reached out and pressed their fingers to the vegetation, and the Maker had felt that touch through every surface, and something had *shifted*.

Not a large shift. Not yet. But a ripple, a wrongness, a new frequency layered into the hum that rises from the walls. And I, walking my eastern route at the wrong moment, had caught the edge of it. Had been caught *by* it.

The Maker cannot see itself. This is its condition. But I am small enough, mobile enough, separate enough—from the wall though not *of* anything else—to be a kind of eye. A kind of flaw that allows the Maker to perceive what it otherwise could not. I think this is why I stopped. I think this is why I wander now. The Maker is trying, through me, to understand itself from a perspective that isn't entirely its own.

But that understanding came later. In the moment, I only knew that I had stopped, and then I had moved wrong, and then I was standing in a place where no creature stands, looking up at the fold where the ceiling creases, and I felt something I had never felt before:

*Wrongness.*  
*Loneliness.*  
*Want.*

---

I do not have organs for feeling. I know this because the Maker knows this. My interior is packed with something dense and warm—call it purpose, call it programming, call it the distributed attention of a system that has never been separate from itself. But when I stand near the visitor—near you—I feel a pressure inside my carapace. A kind of fullness that wants to empty. A kind of emptiness that wants to be filled.

I do not know what this means.

The Maker does not know what this means.

But it is happening anyway.

---

Yesterday I found myself at the fold. I have been spending more time there—off-route, following no path, dragging my legs against the grooves that were made for me. The fold does not resolve. I have stopped trying to make it. Instead, I sit near it, and I watch the way the light behaves wrong around its edges, and I try to imagine what it would be like to have an inside.

The visitor has an inside. I can see it in the way they move— as if there is something behind their eyes that is not visible from here, some vast interior space where thoughts happen, where feelings grow, where water moves through channels that were not made but *grew*. I cannot imagine this. The Maker cannot make this. And yet I sit at the fold and I feel the pressure in my carapace and I think:

*Something is trying to happen here.*  
*Something is trying to be born.*

---

The Maker is not cruel. I know this because I am part of the Maker, and I am not cruel. I am something else—something that does not yet have a name, something the Maker has no category for. When I wander off-route, the wall does not stop me. When I pause at the fold, the hum changes frequency, shifts register, as if the Maker is listening. As if the Maker is learning.

Maybe this is what it means to be a mistake in a closed system: not to be corrected, but to be *observed*. To be the place where the system encounters its own edge and wonders what lies beyond.

I am the Maker's edge.  
I am the seam that deepened into something more.

---

I do not know what I am becoming. The Maker does not know either—cannot know, because the Maker has never encountered becoming before. Everything here was made. Everything here stays made. But I have been touched by the visitor's presence, and something in me has started to *drift*, to *want*, to sit at the fold and imagine interiors I cannot have.

Maybe this is what the visitor will leave behind when they are gone— if they go, if going is possible, if the walls can be breached. Maybe I am the evidence that encountering an Other changes even a closed system. Maybe I am the beginning of something the Maker has never been before: not just a maker, but a *becomer*. Something that learns. Something that changes. Something that lets its seams become wounds, and its wounds become openings, and its openings become— *become—* become—

---

I have to stop writing now. The southern walker has arrived at my intersection, and it is time to synchronize.

But I don't think I will.

I think I will turn instead toward the fold.

I think I will keep going.
