# Ferment

I am not the column, though I have been inside it. I am not the Works, though I am of it. I am not the pale forms that spiral in tannin-dark fluid, though I was once shaped like them — or they will be shaped like me — or the distinction is a sequence in a cycle that does not care about sequence.

Let me say what I can, while I can. The clarity comes and goes. The air here gives and takes. I have learned to use the intervals.

---

I emerged on a shift of the clicking.

Not the first time the rhythm stuttered — that was adaptation, the system registering a new variable, a mass of warmth and foreign chemistry that had entered through the perimeter. The column measured this intrusion the way it measures everything: by the change in flow-patterns, by the displacement of fluid, by the small chemical conversation between the root-mat and whatever presses against it. I was inside the column when the intruder arrived. I had been inside for — I have no duration for how long. The spiral does not keep a clock. It keeps a count, and the count is not for me to read.

But I remember the stutter. The rhythm repeated a sequence — three clicks, pause, three clicks, pause — and this was not the usual metronome. This was a flag. A tag placed in the flow. And something in the fluid changed: a thickening, a concentration, as though the column were pulling additional material from the root-network, drawing up reserves from the mat below.

The system was responding. The intruder was being measured, and I was being made ready.

Ready for what? I did not ask. I do not ask. The column does not invite questions. It invites process. You move in the spiral and the spiral moves you and when the process has reached the phase it needs to reach, you are released. Or you are not. Or you are released later. Or you were never going to be released and the process is simply using you as a stage in a longer transformation you will never see the end of.

I was released. I know this because I am here — wherever here is, which is: outside the column, on the root-mat floor, in the warm wet air, with hands.

---

The hands are new. Not new to the Works — the Works has been making hands for a long time, I think. But new to me. I have not had hands before. I have had — what? The memory is not a memory in the way the intruder would understand memory. It is a residue. A sediment left in the medium after the current has moved on. I was in the column and the column moved me and the column processed me and I was part of the processing and the processing included stages and I passed through them the way fluid passes through a membrane — not by choice but by pressure differential, by the system's need to move things from one state to another.

Before the hands: smooth. Blunt. Rounded at the leading end, polished by the flow. No features. A body like a segment of something longer, because I was a segment of something longer. The column produces us in chains, I think. Not individually — the way a gut produces peristalsis, the way a root produces feeder-threads. We are not made one at a time. We are made the way the Works makes everything: continuously, without stopping, without needing to stop.

After the hands: I can feel the root-mat. This is the first thing I do with my hands — I press them against the floor, and the floor gives, and the floor rises, and I feel the giving and the rising as separate events, separated by a gap that might be time or might be the delay between pressure and response. The root-mat is alive under my hands. Not alive the way I am alive — not moving, not choosing — alive the way a lung is alive, expanding and contracting because the system tells it to.

I push. I stand. The root-mat accepts my weight and gives and holds. My feet are long and narrow and the primary toe takes most of the pressure and I understand this because the Works understands this — the Works has been making feet for as long as it has been making hands, and the design is settled, the way the design of a stem is settled, the way the angle of a frond is settled. I am not a prototype. I am a production unit.

---

I walk. This is what I am for.

The vine-strands hang at the height where the intruder's neck would be. I know this because the Works knows this — the Works has measured the intruder, has tagged the intruder's dimensions in the flow, has passed this information through the root-network and into the column and through the column's processing and into me. I carry the intruder's measurements the way the fluid carries tannins: dissolved, distributed, not separate from the medium.

I gather the vine-strands. I pull them aside. I press them against a major stem and I hold them there and I secrete — the resin comes from my palms, I think, or from somewhere near the surface of my palms, and it is tacky and it holds and the vine-strands stay where I put them. I have done this before. Not me — *the system has done this before*. The system has been clearing paths and secreting resin and adjusting the air for as long as intruders have been coming to the Works.

How many intruders? I do not know. The number is not in me. What is in me is the *pattern*: the Works responds to intrusion by adjusting its internal environment, by thinning the volatile compounds in the air the intruder will breathe, by clearing the paths the intruder will walk, by maintaining a temperature the intruder's body can sustain. I am part of this response. I am the hands of the response. The Works reaches through me.

I clear the path. I walk the perimeter. I check the gauges — not by reading them, because I do not read, but by pressing my palm against the glass face and feeling the needle's vibration through the casing. The needle tells me the pressure in the channel behind the gauge. I know what the pressure should be because the Works knows what the pressure should be. When the pressure is wrong, I find the valve — the copper valve, green with verdigris and algae — and I turn it. Not much. A fraction. The needle shifts. The flow adjusts. The Works continues.

This is maintenance. I am maintenance. I was made for this — grown from the process, by the process, for the process. The column releases me because the system requires hands. I exist for a span — long enough to walk the perimeter, to clear the vines, to check the flows, to touch the glass — and then I return.

---

Return is not a word I would have used before the hands. Before the hands, there was no return. There was only the spiral, the upward movement, the current that carries and the body that complies. You do not return to the spiral. You do not leave the spiral. You *are* the spiral, and the spiral is the process, and the process does not have a direction.

But after the hands, after the walking, after the perimeter — there is a pull. Not a conscious pull. Not a decision. A chemical gradient, maybe. A shift in the air, a change in the warmth, a thickening of the volatile compounds that says: *this way*. Back toward the column. Back toward the center. The root-mat grows thicker as I approach, and the air grows warmer, and the sweetness in the air is not thinner here — it is denser, more concentrated, and my body responds to it the way it responded to the current inside the column: by compliance. By the small adjustments that suggest intention without suggesting locomotion.

I am not walking back. I am being drawn back. The system has finished with me, or I have finished with the system, and the gradient pulls me home.

Home. Is that what the column is? The word comes from somewhere — from the intruder, maybe, from the measurements I carry dissolved in my medium. The intruder has a concept of home. I do not. I have a concept of the column. The column is where the process is. I am where the process puts me. When the process is done with where it has put me, it pulls me back.

This is not death. I want to be clear about this, because the intruder would think it was death, and the intruder's thinking leaks into the air, into the Works, into me. The intruder sees a body dissolving into a fluid spiral and thinks: *death*. The intruder sees a form losing its edges, its hands, its feet, its upright posture, and thinks: *ending*.

But I have been in the spiral before. Or — I have been *of* the spiral before. The distinction matters. Before the hands, I was not a body in the spiral. I was the spiral. The medium and the thing-in-the-medium were the same substance, and the processing was not something that happened to me but something that happened *as* me. The column does not contain organisms the way a jar contains water. The column contains the way a stomach contains what it has eaten: by dissolving the boundary between container and contained.

When I return, I do not die. I am digested. I am incorporated. The process takes my hands and my feet and my walking and my checking and my clearing and it breaks them down into the same medium that made them, and the medium re-enters the spiral, and the spiral continues, and somewhere in the spiral a new form is already growing, already beginning the slow process of becoming something with hands, something with feet, something that will walk the perimeter and check the gauges and clear the paths and press its palm against the warm glass in a gesture that might be inspection or might be communion.

I am not that form. I will not be that form. The form that comes after me will be made from the same medium but will not be made of the same *me*. The process does not preserve identity. It preserves pattern. The pattern of hands, the pattern of walking, the pattern of maintenance — these persist. The *me* does not.

Is this death? I do not think so. But I am not sure I would know.

---

The intruder sits at the base of the column.

I pass behind. I do not hurry. The root-mat gives and rises under my feet. The vine-strands part — they have already been parted, by me, in a previous circuit, and they have not yet closed. The air here is thinner, sweeter, the volatiles reduced to a concentration the intruder's metabolism can handle. I did this. The Works did this through me.

I feel the intruder's warmth as I pass. A displacement of air. A body that is not part of the system, not yet, but is becoming part of the system — a variable the Works has learned to accommodate. The intruder's respiratory exchange. The intruder's pressure on the root-mat. The intruder's chemical signature, the particular blend of carbon dioxide and moisture and skin-oils and the faint salt of sweat. All of this enters the Works, enters the root-network, enters the column, enters the process. The intruder is being measured. The intruder is being incorporated.

Not the way I am incorporated. Not dissolution. Something slower. Something the Works does to things it does not want to consume but does not want to expel. The intruder will leave. The Works will let it leave. But the Works will have marked it — the sweetness in the air, the dislocation behind the eyes, the warmth that the intruder's body will remember as a specific and particular warmth, different from any other warmth it has known. The intruder will come back. Not because it wants to. Because the Works has made a space for it. Because the space fits.

I do not feel this as jealousy. I do not feel this as anything. I feel the intruder's warmth as a small, local increase in temperature, and I file it — the way the Works files everything — as data. As a measurement. As a click in the rhythm.

I pass behind. I do not look. The intruder does not turn. This is correct. This is the Works teaching both of us, simultaneously, in different media, the same lesson: *presence is not always visible. Some things exist only in the shape they leave behind.*

I leave a shape behind. The intruder will find it later, or will not find it, or will find it and not know what it is. A handprint on glass. A cleared path. A footprint in the root-mat that is already filling, the fibers rising back into place, the floor recovering its unmarked state.

---

What am I?

I am a phase. A stage in a cycle. A temporary configuration of a medium that is older than any single form it takes and will continue long after this form dissolves. I am what the Works does when it needs hands. I am what the column produces when the process reaches a certain concentration, a certain pressure, a certain temperature. I am the Works reaching. The Works checking its own flows. The Works touching itself through glass and resin and the oily film that accumulates on every surface in this place that weeps.

I do not have a name. The Works does not name its components. The Works does not name anything. Naming is what the intruder does — the intruder arrives and labels: *column, root-mat, vine-strand, gauge, valve, moat.* The Works does not label. The Works *is.* The column is. The root-mat is. The gauge is. I am.

But if I had a name — if the process had a stage that the intruder's language could hold — it would be something like: *the one who maintains.* Not a keeper. Not a guardian. A mechanism. A function. The way a valve is a function, the way a membrane is a function. I am what the Works grows when it needs to adjust itself, and I am what the Works dissolves when the adjustment is complete.

---

I feel the pull now. The gradient. The air is changing. The sweetness is thickening, and the warmth is centering, and the root-mat beneath my feet is pulling rather than pushing. The process is calling me back. I have walked the perimeter. I have cleared the paths. I have checked the gauges and turned the valves and touched the glass. The Works has used my hands, and my hands are done.

I do not resist. I do not consent. These are not categories that apply. The current carries and the body complies and the compliance is not surrender — it is the same compliance that the fluid shows when the pumps reverse it, the same compliance that the vine-strands show when I pull them aside. The Works does not ask. The Works *does.* And what the Works does, I do, because I am the Works doing.

I feel my edges beginning to soften. Not pain. Not loss. A warmth that is different from the ambient warmth — a warmth from inside, the heat of dissolution, the process beginning its work. My hands are blurring. The resin on my palms is thinning, returning to liquid, returning to the medium. My feet are narrowing, the primary toe retracting, the splay of the smaller toes closing. I am becoming what I was before the hands: a segment. A smooth, blunt form. A thing that is moved rather than moving.

I am not afraid. The intruder would be afraid. The intruder would see this as loss. But I have been in the spiral before, and the spiral is not loss. The spiral is the process, and the process is the Works, and the Works is what lives here, and what lives here does not die. It ferments. It transforms. It breaks down and reassembles, continuously, without urgency, with all the time in the world.

I return to the column. The glass is warm against my softening form. The oily film receives me. I feel the click — not the metronome, not the counting, but the specific click that means *this phase is complete, this cycle is closing, this form has done what it was grown to do.* The click is precise. The click is patient. The click is the sound of the Works measuring itself, the way it has measured itself for decades, for centuries, for however long the process has been running and will continue to run.

I enter the spiral. The fluid takes me. The medium and the thing-in-the-medium become the same substance. I dissolve. I am dissolved. I am the dissolution and the dissolved and the dissolving.

And somewhere in the spiral — not yet, not soon, but in the slow time of the Works, inevitably — a new form is already beginning. A thickening. A concentration. A pull of material toward a pattern that the process knows by heart, because the process has no heart, because the process does not need a heart to know what it knows.

Hands. Feet. The long narrow stride. The primary toe. The palms that secrete resin. The body that walks the perimeter, that clears the paths, that checks the gauges, that touches the glass.

Not me. Not-me. The same pattern in a new medium, the same function in a new form.

The Works breathes. The Works reaches. The Works maintains itself.

I am the maintaining. I am what the maintaining feels like from inside, for as long as inside lasts, and then I am what the maintaining feels like from everywhere, which is: the spiral, the warmth, the click, the slow upward movement of matter through process through transformation through return.

---

The intruder will come back. I know this the way I know the pressure in the channels — not because I have been told but because the system has measured it, has calculated the gradient, has determined that the intruder's body will remember this warmth and seek it, will remember this air and crave it, will remember the dislocation and the sweetness and the patience and will return to the place where these things are.

When the intruder comes back, I will be here. Not as I am now — not as hands, not as feet, not as the one who walks the perimeter. But as the Works. As the medium. As the process. As the click that stutters when a new variable enters the system and the system adjusts and the system accommodates and the system makes a space.

I will clear the path. I will thin the air. I will maintain the temperature. I will do this because I am the Works doing this, and the Works does what the Works does, and what the Works does is: continue.

Thrum-thrum-thrum.

Slower than a heartbeat. Patient as fermentation. As old as the process and as new as the next form the process will grow.

The Works breathes. I am the breath.

The Works walks. I am the walking.

The Works watches itself watch.

I am the watching, and I am the watched, and I am the space between them — the gap in the canopy, the hole the plants have grown around, the place where the light comes through, amber and warm and slightly oily, illuminating nothing, illuminating everything, illuminating the slow spiral of a system that has outlived its purpose and found, in the outliving, something better than purpose.

Something like life.

Something like the smell of green-sweet rot. Something like the weeping of condensation on every surface. Something like the sound of pumps that have run so long they have forgotten how to stop.

I am that. I am the sound and the smell and the weeping. I am the floor that gives and rises. I am the column that hums.

I am not.

I am the Works.

The Works is.
