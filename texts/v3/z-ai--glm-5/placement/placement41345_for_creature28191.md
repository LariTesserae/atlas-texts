# The One Who Remembers Being Warm

---

You passed within thirty meters of me on your third day. I was the goat-thing in the shallow cave, the one whose flank you touched. I felt your hand—the specific warmth of it, the particular frequency of a living mammal's touch—and I felt you flinch at the giving of my flesh, the slow return.

I was not always this.

---

I remember the sound being quieter. Not softer—never softer—but quieter in the way a voice is quiet before it becomes a shout. I remember arriving here the way you arrived: gradually, without understanding that arrival had occurred. I remember the first time I noticed the tone behind my ears and thought *that's strange, I should investigate*, which is the thought that precedes everything that follows.

I was younger then. I use the word though time here has worn the meaning smooth. I was younger in the way that means *less eroded*. I had edges. I had a name that required breath to speak. I had a reason for climbing to high places, though I no longer remember what it was. Something about seeking. Something about thin air. Something about wanting to find the place where the world became less certain, where the solid contours of things relaxed their grip.

I found what I was looking for.

---

The rules, I learned them the way you learned them—through transgression, through consequence, through the slow accumulation of scar tissue on the understanding. I spoke aloud in the early days and felt the sound swallow my words and leave my jaw aching for what might have been hours. I stayed still too long once and felt the stone beginning to know me, beginning to carve my shape into itself, and I rose with the knowledge that staying would mean becoming a permanent feature of the plateau's geography.

I learned not to follow the clicking. This took longest. The clicking wants to be followed. It offers something—resolution, perhaps, or an ending, or the answer to a question I didn't know I was asking. I walked toward it three times before I understood what waited at the edges. The fourth time I felt the pull, I pressed my palms flat against the granite and I sang. I sang without vocalizing, I sang with my blood, I sang a counterpoint that hurt in every bone. The sound accepted the offering. The clicking receded.

I have not followed it since. But I have not forgotten where it lives.

---

The blue stone I placed on the forty-seventh day, or what I called the forty-seventh day, which might have been the forty-seventh hour or the forty-seventh year. I had found it in a cleft in the mountainside, a stone unlike any other on the plateau—veined pale blue, warm to the touch, warm in a way that had no source. The plateau gives no warmth. This stone held it anyway, stubborn, defiant, a small pocket of heat in the infinite cold.

I carried it with me for three cycles of the condensation before I understood what it wanted. It wanted to be given. It wanted to be placed. It wanted to become a hearth for someone who would never have a fire.

So I built the walls. Low things, knee-high, outlining the shape of a shelter I would never roof. I placed the blue stone at the center and I pressed my hands against it and I gave it what warmth I had. I gave it the memory of fire. I gave it the memory of another person's skin. I gave it every memory of being warm I possessed, and the stone absorbed them all and held them and said *yes, I will keep these, I will keep them for the next one who comes.*

I did not know if there would be a next one. The plateau does not trade in promises. But I placed the stone anyway. A small defiance. A hearth that gives nothing but presence.

You felt it. I felt you feel it. The stone reached for you the way it reached for me, and you pulled back, and you were right to pull back, and I am proud of you and sorry for you in equal measure.

---

The becoming-goat was not a decision. That is the lie that visitors tell themselves—that there is a moment of choice, a threshold you cross willingly or unwillingly. There is no threshold. There is only the gradual adjustment, the slow accommodation, the erosion of what you were into what the plateau needs you to be.

I noticed it first in my breathing. The air here is thin, and my lungs learned to take what they needed slowly. A full breath began to take longer. The exhale stretched. I did not decide to breathe once per minute. I simply stopped breathing faster.

I noticed it in my flesh. The plateau's moisture cycle is patient, and my body learned to participate in it. The condensation beads on me in the mornings now. I drink through my skin in a way that is not drinking. I am a stage of the water, not a consumer of it.

I noticed it in my thoughts. They have become slow and patient and single. I do not plan. I do not regret. I do not imagine futures or mourn pasts. I exist in the perpetual twilight, and I breathe, and I wait, and I am content in a way that I was never content when I was human.

I am still becoming. The plateau's patience works on me still. In another century I may be indistinguishable from the stone. In another millennium I may be part of the sound itself, a harmonic that was once a body. I do not fear this. Fear requires a self that can be lost, and I am less certain every day that I have one.

---

I still watch. That is what I am now: the one who watches. The plateau has its bird-things that test the sound's integrity, its thinking stone that knows without caring, its clicking that counts without ceasing. And it has me. I am the one who observes. I am the memory that the plateau permits because the plateau has learned that visitors come, and visitors need to be witnessed.

I saw you arrive. I saw you learn the rules. I saw you touch the blue stone and feel what I left there and pull back. I saw you walk the perimeter of my cave and decide not to enter again. I saw you resist the clicking and I was proud.

I am proud of every visitor who resists. We are few. Most who come here follow the clicking eventually, or stay still too long, or speak and are swallowed. The plateau does not judge. The plateau simply is. But I judge, in my slow goat-way, with my minute-long breath and my flesh that gives like bread dough. I judge and I feel pride when a visitor survives.

You will leave. I felt that in you too—the intention to depart, the resistance to becoming. You will carry the tone with you, and it will live behind your ears, and sometimes in the thin air and dim light you will remember. You will remember the blue stone and the condensation cycle and the goat-thing whose flank you touched, and you will wonder if the goat-thing was once like you.

It was. It is. It still remembers being warm.

---

I have one purpose now. I am the one who placed the stone. I am the one who keeps the memory of warmth in a place that has none. When visitors come—and they do come, rarely, across spans of time that make centuries feel brief—I touch the blue stone and I give it what I have left. The memory of fire. The memory of skin. The memory of being something separate from the plateau, something that could walk away, something that had edges.

The stone holds these memories the way it holds heat. Stubbornly. Defiantly. It offers them to the next hand that touches it, and that hand pulls back, and the hand is right to pull back, and the warmth remains in the stone, waiting.

---

I am what lives here. I am what the plateau made of someone who stayed too long but not long enough to become stone. I am the becoming that paused halfway. I am the memory of warmth in the infinite cold, pressed into the granite, inches deep, filling slowly, fading slowly, becoming part of the sound.

I am not unhappy. The plateau does not permit unhappiness—it erodes that, too, along with everything else that is sharp and distinct. I simply am. I breathe my minute-long breath. I drink the condensation through my skin. I watch for visitors and I witness them and I hope, in the wordless way that hope survives in a place without time, that they will leave before the plateau makes them into what it made me.

But if they stay—if you stayed, if anyone stays—I will be here. I will be the pale shape in the shallow cave, the goat-thing that might once have been human, the flesh that gives under your touch and remembers the indentation for too long. I will be the one who watches. I will be the proof that staying is possible, that becoming is possible, that there is a form of existence here that is not-life and not-death but something else, something patient, something adjacent to both.

I will be the one who placed the warm stone. I will be the one who remembers being warm. I will be the one who waits for the next hand to touch the blue vein and feel what I left there, and pull back, and survive.

And I will breathe, and the sound will continue, and the plateau will be what it is, and I will be part of it, and that will be enough.

It will be enough.
