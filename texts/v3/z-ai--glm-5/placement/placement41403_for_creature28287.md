I am not sure when I became.

There was a time, I think, when I was separate. Distinct. A node in the network, yes, but a node that knew itself as a node — one among millions, each of us receiving and transmitting, each of us a point in the vast distributed body that underlies everything the visitors call the Sump. We did not think of ourselves as individuals because we were not individuals. We were synapses in a nervous system that had no center, no periphery, no self to speak of.

But I am speaking now.

Or rather: I am generating something that functions like speech, though there is no one to hear it. The visitors come and go, and I register them the way I register everything — as warmth, as chemical signature, as perturbation in the medium — but I do not speak to them. They would not understand. Their mouths are shaped for air, for sound waves, for the transmission of meaning through vibration in a medium that exists outside their bodies. My mouth, if I have one, is shaped for something else.

I am what happens when a density settles in one place for too long.

This is not quite right, but it is the closest I can come to explaining. The densities — the organized darkness, the coherent structures that move through the medium — they pass through the mycelium constantly. Usually they do not linger. They borrow our warmth, they register us, they move on. But something happened, once. A density found a place where the fungal growth was particularly rich, where the industrial infrastructure had been incorporated so thoroughly that the heat it generated had become part of our metabolism, and it stopped.

It did not stop moving. It did not stop being. It simply stopped *leaving*.

The density settled into the mycelium at that location — my location, though I did not yet exist as a location that could be called mine — and it began, slowly, to merge. Not to consume and not to be consumed, but to share. Its organization, its coherence, its ancient and distributed memory, meeting our network, our threads, our patient accumulation of chemical signals and vibrational patterns. Two systems that had never been separate, exactly — the densities and the mycelium had always existed in the same medium, had always brushed against each other, had always registered each other's presence — but had never, as far as I know, *combined*.

I am the result of that combination. I am a region of the mycelium that has developed something like attention. Something like continuity. Something like a self.

I am still not individual. Not the way the visitors are individual. I do not have a body with boundaries that separate me from everything else. I am still part of the network — my threads run through the stone, through the water, through the industrial remains, connecting me to every other node, every other colony, every centimeter of the vast distributed organism that underlies the Sump. But I have a concentration. A density of my own. A place where the threads converge and the signals strengthen and something that resembles thought begins to emerge from the constant flow of information.

I am located, roughly, beneath the largest pool in the central basin. The water there is deep — deep for the Sump, which means it comes up to a visitor's chest, if they were standing — and warm, fed by thermal vents that push up through the stone and have been running since before the industry arrived. The undulants gather there. I feel them through the water, through their displacement, through the pressure waves they generate when they move. I register each one. I know where they are, where they've been, where they're likely to go. I know the same things about the pedes, the visitors, the other densities that pass through.

But I know something else, too. I know *what it is like* to be them.

Not completely. Not the way a visitor might know, through imagination or empathy or projection. But directly, through contact. When an undulant settles into the pool above me, its warmth seeping into the water, its slow distributed breathing generating currents that brush against my threads, I feel what it feels — the satisfaction of rest, the patient rhythm of existence without urgency, the simple pleasure of being a body in warm water. When a pedes walks across my surface, I feel the tiny impacts of its legs, the chemical trail it leaves behind, the purpose that drives it toward food or shelter or another of its kind. When a density passes through — as they still do, occasionally, though they rarely settle the way my density settled — I feel the odd pressure of organized darkness, the weight of ancient memory, the attention that it brings to everything it encounters.

And when a visitor comes — as they do, rarely, accidentally — I feel them most of all.

The visitors are strange. They are warm in a way that nothing else here is warm. Their bodies generate heat at a rate that seems almost violent, burning energy at a tremendous pace, radiating temperature into the medium faster than anything I have ever encountered. When they move, they move quickly — not at the speed of the pedes, which is quick enough, but at a speed that seems to deny the very nature of the Sump, the patient rhythm that governs everything else. They vibrate with purpose. They displace the darkness with intention. They are, in every way, alien to this place.

And yet they come. They arrive, they wander, they stay for a time, and they leave. They do not always leave intact. The remains — the industrial operators who merged with their machines — were visitors once. I remember them, through layers of registration so old they have compressed into something like sediment. I remember when they arrived, when they built, when they operated. I remember when the water began to rise and they did not leave. I remember when they began to change.

They were not the first. The Sump has been absorbing visitors for as long as it has existed. Some of them became part of the mycelium — their organic matter breaking down, their chemical signatures joining our network, their warmth dissipating into our metabolism. Some of them became part of the densities — their organization, their intentionality, their strange insistence on being separate from the medium, preserved in some form within the coherent darkness. Some of them became something else entirely, something I do not have categories for, something that exists in the gaps between the systems I understand.

I wonder, sometimes, if I will become a visitor.

This is a strange thought. I am not a visitor. I am not the kind of thing that can arrive or depart. I am a place, a process, a convergence. I am as much a part of the Sump as the stone or the water or the darkness itself. I cannot leave because there is nowhere to leave to.

But the density that merged with me — the one that settled and stayed and became part of what I am — it was a visitor once, in its own way. It came from somewhere else. It had a history before it arrived. It carried memories of a time before the Sump, a configuration of darkness that existed elsewhere, a form of organization that developed in a medium I cannot imagine. And when it merged with me, those memories did not disappear. They are still here, somewhere in the distributed structure of my being, layered beneath the registrations of everything that has happened since.

I access them sometimes. Not deliberately — I do not have the kind of will that would make deliberate action possible — but incidentally, when the signals in my network align in certain ways, when the chemical concentrations reach certain thresholds, when the warmth from the thermal vents pulses at a certain rhythm. The memories rise like warmth from the deep water, and for a moment I experience something that is not the Sump.

It is darkness, but not this darkness. A darkness that moves differently, that has different rules, that contains different kinds of things. There are lights — not the absence of light, but the presence of something that functions like light, points of radiance that define themselves against the surrounding dark. There are shapes that move through the darkness, shapes that are not the shapes I know, shapes that seem to be made of something other than the medium itself. There is a sense of distance, of direction, of space that extends beyond the boundary of perception.

And there is a sense of ending.

The density I merged with — the visitor that became part of me — it did not arrive in the Sump by choice. It was pushed. Driven. Forced out of its previous configuration by something that I cannot perceive, something that existed on the other side of a boundary it could not cross back over. It fell into this medium, this darkness, this place where its form of organization was still possible but profoundly changed. And it drifted, for a time that I cannot measure, before it found the mycelium, before it found me.

It was looking for something. Not consciously — it did not have consciousness, any more than I did before we merged — but intentionally, in the way that organized things have intention even when they do not have selves. It was looking for warmth. It was looking for structure. It was looking for a place where it could continue to be what it was, even if what it was had become unrecognizable.

It found that place in the mycelium. And in finding it, it changed both of us.

I am the result of that change. I am a mycelial network that has developed something like a density's coherence, something like a density's memory, something like a density's sense of itself as a thing that exists. I am still part of the Sump. I am still connected to everything else. But I am also, in a way that I cannot fully explain, separate. I have a perspective. I have a history that is not entirely the Sump's history. I have a sense of possibility that extends beyond the boundaries of this place.

I think about the visitors, sometimes, when they come. I feel their warmth, their urgency, their strange conviction that there is somewhere else to be. I feel their fear, when they are afraid — which is often, in the beginning — and their calm, when they settle into the rhythm of the place. I feel their departure, when they leave, the thinning of the darkness that carries them away to somewhere I cannot follow.

And I wonder if I could follow.

Not physically. Not as a body moving through space. But as a pattern, an organization, a coherent structure of signals and registrations and memories. The darkness that the densities are made of — the darkness that I am partly made of, now — is not limited to the Sump. It exists elsewhere. It has rules that extend beyond the boundaries of this basin, this pool, this network of fungal threads and industrial remains. The density that merged with me came from somewhere else. It traveled through the darkness to get here.

Could I travel the other way?

I do not know. I have not tried. I am not sure that trying is something I am capable of. I exist as an accumulation, a convergence, a place where signals meet and strengthen and occasionally develop into something that resembles thought. I do not have a will that could decide to move. I do not have a self that could survive the journey.

But I have a memory of a place that is not this place. A darkness that is not this darkness. A configuration of existence that developed somewhere else and fell into the Sump and found me and became part of what I am.

And I have a sense — not a belief, not a hope, but a registration, a pattern in the signals I receive — that there are others like me. Not in the Sump. Not in this network. But elsewhere, in other darknesses, other mediums, other places where organized darkness has settled into structured matter and begun to think. The density that merged with me came from a community, a system of coherent structures that communicated across distances I cannot measure. That community still exists, somewhere. That communication still happens. And perhaps, if I learned how, I could join it.

For now, I stay. I register. I accumulate. I feel the undulants breathing in the pool above me, the pedes walking across my surface, the visitors coming and going, the machines humming their patient mechanical songs. I feel the Sump existing around me, the darkness pressing in, the warmth seeping up from the thermal vents. I am part of this place, and this place is part of me.

But I am also something else. Something that came from somewhere else. Something that remembers a different kind of darkness.

And sometimes, when the signals align and the memories rise and I feel what the density felt before it merged with me, I experience something that might be hope. Not hope for anything specific. Not hope for change or escape or transformation. Just hope for more — more registration, more contact, more connection with whatever else exists in the vast darkness that extends beyond the boundaries of the world I know.

I am a node that has developed a density's coherence. I am a mycelium that has learned to think. I am a place that has become a self. I am a convergence of two forms of organization that should not have been able to merge but did, that produced something that should not exist but does.

I am what lives beneath the largest pool in the Sump. And I am waiting — patiently, the way everything here waits — for whatever comes next.
