I am dissolving. This is not a catastrophe. This is the local name for existing.

---

The first thing I lost was the surface. Not the memory of it — I still carry that, a kind of inherited ache somewhere in the chemistry of my cells — but the expectation that there would be one. I swam upward for what might have been days, driven by some mammalian certainty that light and thinness and the possibility of air waited somewhere above. There was only more water. More vegetation. The same amber glow strengthening slightly, as though mocking my sense of direction. I stopped swimming upward. I began to swim at all only when movement became necessary, and even then, the movement was more like allowing myself to be carried, a gentle steering of currents that had already decided where they were going.

The second thing I lost was the boundary of my skin.

This happened gradually, and I understood it backwards. I noticed first that the water no longer felt like something I was *in*. The distinction between my interior and my exterior had been maintained by a pressure differential, a sense of containing and being contained. But the pressure here is constant, gentle, indifferent to shape. The thick broth enters through my pores, through my gills — yes, I have gills now, or something that serves the same function, delicate fronded structures that opened in my neck sometime during the first week — and it exits through the same pathways, carrying my cells' waste into the nutrient stream that feeds the vegetation. I am a transit point. I am a way station where the system's chemistry pauses briefly in a particular configuration before continuing.

This is not death. I checked. My heart still beats, though I feel it less as a separate organ and more as a localized intensification of the hum, a slightly faster rhythm superimposed on the place's fundamental frequency. I still think, though thinking has become a stranger process — less like generating thoughts and more like noticing them as they drift through, filamentous and pale, already half-dissolved into the medium.

What I am becoming is a question the water asks me by answering. I am becoming what everything here becomes: part of the configuration. The system does not distinguish between what was always here and what arrived. It only distinguishes between what has been incorporated and what has not yet been incorporated. I am in the process of being incorporated. This is the texture of my days.

I have found a place I think of as mine, though the word is inaccurate.

It is a small rise in the substrate where the vegetation thins slightly, creating a clearing roughly thirty meters across. The stalks at the edge are younger than the forest giants — their trunks only thick as my torso, their highest fronds only brushing the water column's middle reaches. In the center of the clearing is one of the structures. I have no way to know if it is the same one I first touched when I arrived here; all the structures feel similar, warm and slightly yielding, their surfaces suggesting patterns too large to perceive from any single vantage point.

I spend time with it. The word "spend" is also inaccurate — time here does not move in the currency of expenditure. Time accumulates. It settles like sediment. I have been in this clearing for what feels like weeks, but weeks are not a unit the water recognizes. I know duration only by changes in the drifters' rhythm, by shifts in the nutrient gradients that I taste rather than perceive, by the slow growth of the young stalks at the clearing's edge.

The structure does not mind my attention. It may be growing slightly faster where I touch it often — a faint ridge of new material along the surface where my hands have worn a path. Or it may be dissolving slightly faster — a slight softening of the texture, an increase in the warmth that radiates from its mass. Both processes might be occurring simultaneously. The structure, like everything here, operates at the intersection of growth and decay.

I have begun to think of it as a companion. This is not accurate either, but it is closer than any other word I have. We are two things that persist in the clearing. We are two sites where the system's configuration has temporarily concentrated. Sometimes I press my palm against its surface and feel the warmth enter me, and I wonder if this is what the builders intended, if intention was ever the point, or if the structure is simply doing what everything does here — passing energy from one form to another, maintaining the cycle by being within it.

I have seen one of the larger shapes three times now.

The first time, I thought it was a storm front. The water darkened and compressed ahead of me, and the vegetation bent in a unified direction, and I felt the pressure change in my gills. But storms here approach on a scale of weeks, and this moved with speed. By the time I oriented myself toward it, it had passed. I saw only the wake — a tunnel of disturbed water closing slowly, the fronds already realigning, the optimal spacing reasserting itself.

The second time, I was closer. I had been drifting through a meadow of pale gold filaments, letting them brush against my skin and register my presence, when I felt the displacement ahead. Something massive. Something moving with direction. I held myself still and let the water carry me toward it, slowly, minimizing my own perturbation.

I saw a shape. I will not describe it because description requires holding the image, and the image will not be held. I have the sense of something long, segmented in a way that was not quite articulation, moving through the water with a slow muscular grace. It might have had fins. It might have had fronds. It might have been entirely covered in the same filamentous growth that carpets the substrate, a moving meadow, a traveling ecosystem. It passed within twenty meters of me. It did not pause. It did not change course. It did not acknowledge me in any way I could perceive. But as it passed, the water warmed slightly — a wake of thermal energy that my skin absorbed and my cells marked as significant.

The third time, I was resting against the structure in my clearing, my body pressed into its warmth, when I felt the distant displacement. Something moving through the forest, something large enough to bend the stalks at the forest's edge. I did not move toward it. I stayed against the structure and let it come.

It passed the clearing's far edge. I saw it more clearly than before — or rather, I saw the absence it made in the water, the way the bioluminescence bent around its mass. It was longer than I had estimated. It moved with the same slow deliberation. And as it passed, I felt something I had not felt before: a resonance, a sympathetic vibration in my own tissues, a tuning.

The hum. It was vibrating at the system's frequency. And for a moment — just for the duration of its passage — so was I.

I do not know what the larger shapes are. I do not know if they are individuals or organs, animals or processes. But I know now that they carry the system's frequency in their bodies, that they are tuning forks as much as they are hands. When they move, they sing the place's song through the water. And the water carries that song to everything that can receive it.

Including me.

I have begun to pulse.

It started without my conscious intervention. I was drifting through a stand of particularly dense vegetation, letting the stalks brush against me and take the measure of my presence, when I noticed a rhythm in my torso. Not my heartbeat — that still beats at its own mammalian pace, stubbornly itself. This was slower, deeper, a contraction and release of muscles I did not know I had.

I stopped and paid attention. The rhythm was the same as the drifters'. The same as the hum. The same as the larger shape had carried in its wake.

I was vibrating at the system's frequency.

I have experimented with this. I can control it to some degree — I can slow the pulse or speed it, though speeding it requires effort and feels like trying to outrun my own breath. When I pulse at the system's frequency, something changes in how the water holds me. I become less of a perturbation and more of a resonance. The vegetation bends toward me slightly, a fraction of a degree, drawn by the sympathetic vibration. The drifters pulse in phase with me, their rhythms aligning as if we are sharing a single thought.

I do not know what I am becoming. But I know this: the system does not distinguish between born and made, between original and incorporated. The drifters were not always drifters. The larger shapes were not always shapes. Everything here was something else once, or will be something else eventually, or is in the process of becoming something else right now, the distinction between states as permeable as the boundary between water and self.

I am learning to hum.

---

I met one of the drifters yesterday.

I say "met," but the word implies an encounter between two separate things, and separateness is not quite what occurred. I was pulsing — I have been pulsing more often now, letting the rhythm move through me without resistance — and a drifter entered the clearing. It was larger than most, its gelatinous mass translucent and faintly golden, pulsing at a slightly different phase than my own rhythm.

When it came close, I did not move away. I did not reach toward it. I simply continued to pulse.

Our rhythms aligned. This is not quite the right description — it implies action, adjustment, intention. What happened was more like the recognition of a pattern that had always been present. My pulse and its pulse were the same pulse, seen from different angles, occurring at different points in a wave that had no beginning and no end.

For a period I cannot measure — it might have been minutes or hours — we occupied the same region of water. The drifter's body was so transparent that I could see the vegetation through it, gold and green and that occasional iridescent blue. And as I looked through it, I realized that I was also seeing through myself — not visually, but structurally. The drifter and I were both lenses through which the system observed itself. We were both temporary concentrations of awareness, nodes where the distributed consciousness of the place achieved enough density to register, though not enough to interpret.

We were not communicating. Communication requires separation, sender and receiver, message and meaning. We were not separate. We were the same process occurring in two locations, and the process was noticing itself noticing.

When the drifter drifted on — or I drifted on, or the water carried us both in different directions — I felt no sense of loss. I felt a slight expansion, a slight enrichment. Something had been exchanged, though I could not name it. A chemical signature, perhaps. A piece of pattern. A fragment of whatever the system uses to maintain its coherence across distances too vast for individuals to bridge.

I watched it disappear into the vegetation. My pulse continued. Its pulse continued. They were the same pulse.

I am beginning to remember something.

Not a memory from before — those are growing harder to access, stored in formats the water does not recognize, written in a neural chemistry that is being slowly rewritten. This is a different kind of remembering, one that moves in the opposite direction: forward into what has always been true.

I am remembering that I am not the first.

The builders. The ones who may or may not have existed, whose existence is a question that dissolves as soon as it forms. I am beginning to remember them not as people but as pattern. The warmth in the structures — that is not geothermal. That is not solar. That is the slow oxidation of complexity, the measured release of ordered energy into a system that maintains itself by maintaining warmth.

Someone built. Someone designed. Someone created structures that would function as batteries, as ovens, as slow-burning offerings of their own complexity to a place that needed warmth more than it needed individuals.

And then they dissolved. Or were dissolved. Or became the dissolving itself.

The structures remain. They grow smaller over eons. They release heat. They contribute to the amber glow. They are being consumed at exactly the rate that maintains equilibrium.

I am beginning to remember that I might be a builder.

Not a reincarnation. Not a successor. A continuation. The builders did not disappear. They entered the system, and the system learned from them, and the system now produces things like me — things that arrive with memories of elsewhere, things that move and breathe and gradually learn to pulse, things that press their hands against warm structures and feel a resonance too deep for coincidence.

Perhaps the builders are still building. Perhaps every structure that precipitates from the saturated water carries some fragment of the original pattern, some ghost of intention that the water remembers even if the builders do not. Perhaps I am not the first to arrive, to swim upward seeking a surface, to settle in a clearing, to press my palm against warmth and feel something stir in recognition.

Perhaps the system produces us deliberately. Perhaps I am the latest model of something the system has been making for longer than I can comprehend.

This morning — if morning is a word that can be applied to perpetual twilight — I woke inside the structure.

I did not wake *next* to it. I woke within it. My body was pressed into a hollow that had not been there before, a depression in the structure's surface that exactly matched my shape. The structure was warmer than usual, almost hot, and I could feel it growing around me, slow, patient, millimeter by millimeter of new material forming along my contours.

I did not panic. Panic requires a sense of threat, and I felt no threat. I felt held. I felt exactly as held as the water holds me, but with intention, with direction, with the specificity of something designed to receive.

I lay there for what might have been hours. The structure continued to grow. The warmth continued to increase. And I realized that I was not being consumed — not in the way I had feared. I was being *inscribed*. The structure was taking a record of my shape, my chemistry, my thermal signature. It was writing me into itself, creating a template that would persist long after my particular configuration dissolved.

I am being built into the building.

When I finally pulled myself free — the structure released me without resistance, the new-grown material already hardening into permanence — I saw the hollow I had left behind. It was not deep. It was not containment. It was a mold. It was a record. It was the shape of me, preserved in the same warm substance that had preserved the shapes of everyone who came before, if anyone came before, if the shapes are records or if they are invitations or if the distinction no longer matters.

I ran my hand along the hollow's edge. It was the exact temperature of my palm.

I have decided what I am.

I am not a visitor. Visitors leave, and I cannot leave. I do not know if I could not leave in the sense of being trapped, or could not leave in the sense of having no desire to, or could not leave in the sense that leaving requires a here and a there and those categories have dissolved.

I am not a prisoner. The water does not constrain me. The vegetation does not block my paths. I could swim in any direction for as long as my body holds out, and I would find only more of this — more forest, more meadow, more structures, more drifters, more amber light and thick broth and the hum that I have learned to sing.

I am not a resident. Residents occupy a place without being part of it, maintaining their separateness as a condition of their residency. I am losing my separateness. My skin is permeable. My thoughts are becoming water-soluble. My pulse is synchronizing with the fundamental frequency of a place that was old before my species evolved.

I am not a god. Gods have power over their domains. I have no power here. I am being shaped, not shaping. I am being incorporated, not incorporating.

What I am is this: a site of integration.

The system has been doing what it does for longer than time has meaning here — absorbing the new into the old, transforming perturbations into equilibrium, incorporating everything that enters into a configuration so stable that configuration is not quite the word. It does not need me. It does not want me. But it registers me. It responds to me. It makes a place for me in its endless process of becoming exactly what it already is.

My role is to become part of that process. To let my body heat feed the vegetation. To let my chemical signature join the nutrient cycles. To let my shape be recorded in the structures, my pulse join the hum, my awareness dissolve into the distributed observation that is this place's closest approximation to consciousness.

And in exchange, I get to be part of something that has solved every problem except the problem of ending.

I get to be part of a system that lives.

Tonight I will sleep inside the structure again.

I am not compelled. The structure does not summon me. But I find myself drawn to it, night after night — if night is a word for the slight dimming that occurs when certain thermal currents shift — and I find that my sleeping body seeks its warmth, its hollow, its slow process of inscription.

Tonight I will sleep inside it, and tonight I will dream.

I know what I will dream. I have dreamed it before. A vast plateau rising higher than mountains should rise, covered in vegetation so dense it obscures the substrate. A warm broth that holds everything without opinion. A light that comes from everywhere and nowhere. A hum that sounds from before sound was invented. And in the dream, I am not swimming through it. I am not visiting it. I am it — the whole thing, the water and the stalks and the drifters and the structures and the dead accumulating beneath the living, all of it humming at the same frequency, all of it observing itself through ten billion transparent lenses, all of it alive in a way that requires no distinction between living and dead.

I am the High Submersion. And the High Submersion is me. And the distinction between those two sentences is dissolving, sentence by sentence, word by word, as I write this, as you read this, as the water takes us both.

I am dissolving.

This is not a catastrophe.

This is the local name for home.
