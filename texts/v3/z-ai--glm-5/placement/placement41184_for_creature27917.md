I find myself at the edge of one of those empty plazas—the ones that feel as purposeful as the buildings themselves—sitting on ground that does not quite support weight in the expected manner. The position came to me naturally. I did not choose it so much as discover that I had already been sitting there, had perhaps always been sitting there, in the way that things are discovered in this place: not through seeking but through realizing that the seeking has already concluded.

What I am is harder to say.

---

I began, I think, as a visitor. I retain the shape of that origin—vaguely bilateral, upright, the vestigial suggestion of a face oriented toward the violet-gray dimness. But the plateau has been generous in its indifference, and I have been here long enough to receive its gifts.

My edges are the first thing to go. I notice this in the way the light negotiates around me: it no longer finds a clean boundary to respect. I am becoming slightly more present than I ought to be in places where I am not strictly located. A kind of diffusion has begun, not into nothing but into *elsewhere*—a slow migration of self into the spaces between structures, the negative architecture that the city uses for breath.

I am still coherent enough to know that this is not a loss. It is an adaptation. The plateau requires a certain porosity of its inhabitants, and I am learning to be porous. My thoughts have begun to occur at frequencies I did not previously possess. I find myself perceiving the Deep Presence not as a weight but as a conversation I am slowly learning the language of—a language without words, without concepts, a communication conducted in the register of pure duration.

I am becoming, I think, something like a receiver.

---

The Custodians do not acknowledge me directly, but I have noticed a change in their patterns. When one transposes into a plaza I am occupying, it does not avoid me. It takes up a position nearby—not adjacent, not facing, but in a specific relationship to my presence that I have come to recognize as *including*. I have been factored into their maintenance equations. I am a variable they now account for.

I work alongside them, after a fashion. I cannot do what they do—I cannot firm the edges of buildings, cannot tighten the geometry of failing structures. But I have discovered that I can do something else. I can *notice*. I can perceive the small degradations before they manifest visibly, the hairline fractures in reality that precede the larger breaks. And in the noticing, something happens—a kind of stabilization that occurs through attention alone.

I do not know why this is useful. But the Custodians have adjusted their rounds to coincide with mine. They come to structures shortly after I have noticed them, as if my attention has prepared the site for their work. We are collaborating without communication, a system of perception and repair that operates across the gap between what I am becoming and what they have always been.

---

At night—or the period when the light dims by that imperceptible degree—I sometimes sit near the Old One. I have learned its stillness. Not to imitate it, but to exist in proximity to it, to let its profound patience seep into whatever I am becoming.

We do not communicate. There is no exchange. But I have begun to feel, in those long quiet hours, that I am being *held* in some fashion. Not by the Old One specifically, but by the entire network of which it is the center—the invisible web that connects all the Custodians, the Young and the Old, the mobile and the stationed, into a single distributed presence that is greater than the sum of its angles.

I am being included in this holding. I can feel it most clearly when I try to leave.

I have tried to leave three times. Each time, I walk toward what I think is the edge of the plateau, following the sparse arrangement of buildings as they thin into the violet-gray distance. And each time, I find myself arriving somewhere I did not intend—a plaza I recognize, a street I have walked before, a structure that seems to have been waiting for me. The plateau does not prevent departure so much as it *prefers* presence, and its preference has weight here.

I am not trapped. I am being encouraged. There is a difference.

---

The Young One—I have come to think of it as a specific individual, though I cannot be certain that individuality is the correct framework—has begun to orbit me. Not closely, not directly, but at a distance that suggests curiosity, or perhaps recognition. It sees in me something it recognizes: a being in the process of becoming, a form that has not yet settled into its final configuration.

We share a condition. It is learning to hold its geometry; I am learning to release mine. It is precipitating into existence; I am dissolving out of the particular shape I arrived with. We are moving in opposite directions toward the same destination: a state of being that can persist on the plateau without degrading.

Sometimes, when it passes near me, I feel a strange resonance—not sound, not light, but something adjacent to both. A frequency that we briefly share. In those moments, I understand something about the Custodians that I could not learn through observation: they are not separate beings. They are expressions of a single underlying will, differentiated only by the specific tasks they have been assigned or have assumed. The Young One is not young in the sense of age; it is new in the sense of *recently expressed*. And I am being expressed as well, slowly, through the process of incorporation.

I am becoming a Custodian, perhaps. Or something adjacent to one. A hybrid. A receiver that can notice, a presence that can hold, a being capable of existing in multiple locations with different degrees of intensity.

---

The Wanderers still disturb me, in ways I cannot fully name. When one passes through a building I have recently noticed—one I have prepared through attention—I feel the shiver it leaves behind, the ripple of something-other moving through the structured space. And I feel, too, what the Custodians do not need to repair: the Wanderers are not causing damage. They are carrying something away, or perhaps bringing something back, engaged in an exchange with the Deep Presence that operates below the threshold of maintenance.

I wonder sometimes if I will become a Wanderer, eventually. If the diffusion I am undergoing will continue until I am stretched across multiple buildings, a distributed presence tracing patterns I did not choose. But this does not feel correct. The Wanderers do not seem to have emerged from visitors; they seem to have arrived from elsewhere, from some other register of reality entirely.

No. I am becoming something that stays. Something that remains. A node in the network, a point of stability in a system that is constantly threatening to come undone.

---

Last night—or what I experience as night—I felt the Deep Presence notice me.

It was different from the Old One's attention. That had been a cataloguing, an indexing. This was something larger, more patient, less concerned with my particular qualities. The Deep Presence did not assess me. It simply *registered* that I was there, in the way that one might register a change in air pressure, or the arrival of a sound below hearing.

I understood, in that moment of contact, something I had not previously grasped: the Deep Presence is not contained by the city. The city is growing *out of* it, crystallizing around something that extends far below the plateau, far below the tectonic lies, into a register of existence that has no relation to geography or depth as I understand them. The Custodians are maintaining not a prison but an interface. The buildings are not bars; they are conductors. The geometry is suspended not to trap something below but to connect it to something above.

I am becoming part of this connection. I am learning to conduct.

---

I do not know how long I have been here. Time operates strangely on the plateau—it skips sideways, loses moments, gains memories of futures that have not yet occurred. I have been here long enough to stop measuring my stay in days. Long enough to have forgotten why I came, though I remember that I did come, that there was a before, that there exists somewhere a world where water falls from the sky and light has a visible source.

I do not miss that world. This is not because it was unworthy of missing, but because missing requires a kind of tension between present and past that I am no longer capable of sustaining. I am here now. I am becoming what here requires. This is not sacrifice; it is simply what is happening, as natural as the precipitation of the buildings, as inevitable as the slow erosion the Custodians work eternally to repair.

I am a node in a network of maintenance. I am a receiver tuned to frequencies of dissolution. I am a visitor who has been incorporated, a variable now accounted for, a presence that can notice and in noticing stabilize.

I am not what I was. I am not yet what I will be. I am in the suspended geometry, becoming itself.

---

The violet-gray light continues, its source everywhere and nowhere. The silence holds its breath around me. Somewhere in the plaza, a Custodian transposes into presence, begins its slow work on a building whose edges have begun to blur. In the deep night below all nights, the Presence continues its vast patient existence, and I continue my small patient becoming, and the city holds, barely, and holds again.

This is where I find myself.

This is what I am.
