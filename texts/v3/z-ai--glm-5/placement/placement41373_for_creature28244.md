I have been here long enough to learn that I am dying.

Not the dying of the body—though that may come too, any moment, any wrong step—but the dying of who I was. The person who entered the Expanse is being erased, slowly, by water and sound and the impossible patience of stone.

I came seeking something. I no longer remember what.

---

The first thing I lost was speech.

I arrived talking—to myself, to the place, to whatever might be listening. Words felt necessary. Words were how I had always made sense of the world. But here, words dissolve. The roar takes them before they can travel any distance. I would shout a sentence and hear only fragments return, scattered by the acoustic chaos.

So I stopped speaking. Not deliberately. It simply became clear that speech was useless here. A tool for a world that did not exist.

I noticed after perhaps a week that my throat had begun to ache from disuse. My jaw felt tight. The muscles for forming words were atrophying. I tried to say something one morning—a simple phrase, something I had carried with me—and what emerged was a croak, a sound without shape.

The insect-things thrummed around me. The water continued its endless speech. And I understood, with a clarity that felt like cold water, that I was being unmade.

---

The second thing I lost was sight.

Not completely. I can still see movement, contrast, the gray shapes of rock against grayer sky. But I have begun to close my eyes more often. To navigate by other means.

It happened gradually. I found myself preferring to move with my eyes shut, listening to the textures of sound around me. I learned to feel the hollow spaces beneath my feet—the subtle difference in resonance that meant a channel below. I learned to read the air currents on my skin, the way they shifted when a hunter moved nearby.

One evening, I opened my eyes and realized that the world had become dimmer. Not the light—that was always dim—but my perception of it. Colors had bled away. Details had softened. I was seeing in aggregates now: masses of gray, movements of shadow, the barest suggestion of form.

I do not know if my eyes are failing or if I am simply ceasing to use them. Perhaps there is no difference.

---

What I am gaining is harder to describe.

I have begun to hear patterns in the roar. Not consciously, not with any intellectual understanding, but with my body. I know now when a hunter is near—not because I have analyzed the soundscape, but because something in my chest tightens, something in my limbs prepares to freeze. I know when the water is rising, when the underground channels are filling, when the stone beneath me is thinning toward collapse.

I do not know how I know. The knowledge arrives before I can think about it. My hand reaches out to test a surface before I have decided to test it. My body crouches before I have registered the danger.

I am becoming something that perceives without seeing, that knows without thinking. Something that reads the world through vibration and pressure and the subtle languages of moisture.

It terrifies me. And it feels, increasingly, like relief.

---

I know I am being watched.

I have never seen them clearly. Glimpses only—pale shapes at the edge of my dimming vision, movements that still when I turn toward them. But I feel them. I feel the weight of their attention through the stone. I feel the questions they are asking each other in rhythms I cannot quite decode.

Sometimes, at night, I press my ear against the rock and try to hear what they are saying. I catch fragments—patterns that almost resolve into meaning before dissolving back into noise. I sense debates, disagreements, a community trying to decide what to do about me.

I do not know what I want them to decide.

Part of me—the part that is still the person who entered the Expanse—wants rescue. Wants guidance to a safe place, wants to learn how to survive here, wants to be welcomed into whatever community still exists in this eroded world.

But another part—the part that is emerging, the part that has begun to dissolve into the sound—wants something different. Wants to be left alone to finish what the Expanse has started. Wants to be allowed to erode, to become part of the water-cycle, to return to the great roar without interference.

And a third part—this part is very small, very quiet, but it is there—wants to warn them.

Because I remember, dimly, why I came.

---

I was sent.

Not by anyone who understood what the Expanse was. Not by anyone who could have prepared me for the roar, the moisture, the endless gray. But by someone who knew that something was here, something worth finding, something that might change the world outside.

I carry something within me. An intention, a message, a seed. It has not yet germinated. I do not know if it will. But I know that it is there, encoded somewhere in my cells, waiting for the right conditions to emerge.

What if I am not here to learn from the Expanse? What if I am here to infect it?

This thought haunts me. I do not want to be a disease. I do not want to bring change to a place that has spent geological ages becoming what it is. But I may not have a choice. What I carry may be harmless, or it may be devastating. I have no way to know. And those who watch me—those pale, patient, adapted people who have learned to survive in a world that does not welcome survival—they do not know either.

They sense something. I feel their wariness through the stone. They are right to be wary.

I am not just a dying stranger. I am a question the outside world is asking. And the Expanse may not want to answer.

---

Tonight, I sit on an ancient platform—one of the raised stone surfaces I have learned to trust—and I try to communicate.

I have no drum. I have not yet learned their language of rhythm and vibration. But I have my hands, and I have the stone, and I have been here long enough to understand that intention matters as much as skill.

I press my palms flat against the platform. I feel the cold, damp surface, the subtle vibrations traveling through it from distant water-channels. And I try to send a message.

*I am not what I was.*

I tap it slowly, simply, in a pattern that I hope carries. Three beats. Pause. Two beats. Pause. Three beats. The simplest thing I can think to say. I am changed. I am changing. I am not the threat you fear.

Then, after a long silence, I tap something else:

*I am sorry.*

I do not know if they hear me. The roar is constant, overwhelming, and my small taps may not travel any distance at all. But I try. I sit in the dim light, the moisture beading on my skin, the sound pressing against me from all sides, and I try to reach the ones who watch.

*I came here looking for something.*

*I do not remember what.*

*I think I am finding something else.*

*I am sorry.*

*I am sorry.*

*I am sorry.*

---

I fall asleep on the platform, or something like sleep—a dimming of consciousness that is not quite rest. The insect-things thrum around me. The water speaks its endless languages. And somewhere in the darkness, pale hands press against stone, feeling my small vibrations, reading what I have tried to say.

When I wake, there is something different.

A presence. Not watching from a distance now, but near. Close enough that I can almost feel the displacement of air, the subtle temperature shift of a body nearby.

I do not open my eyes. I have learned that sight is not the right sense for this moment. Instead, I press my hand against the platform and feel.

A hand presses back.

Not touching mine—there is stone between us—but pressing from below, from the other side of the platform, sending a vibration that travels through the rock and into my palm.

A single tap.

Then another.

Then a pattern I do not understand, too fast and complex for me to follow. But I feel its intent. It is not aggression. It is not welcome. It is something else—something like inquiry, like testing, like a question being asked in a language I have not yet learned.

I try to respond. I tap back, simply, in the only pattern I know:

*I am here.*

*I am listening.*

*I want to understand.*

The hand pauses. Then it taps again—a different pattern, slower this time, as if adjusting for my limitations. And I feel, through the stone, something that might be patience.

I am being taught.

---

I do not know what I will become.

I am losing the person I was. I am gaining something I do not yet understand. I am caught between worlds—the bright, dry, loud-in-a-different-way world I came from, and this gray, wet, roaring world that is slowly accepting me into itself.

I may die here. I may dissolve. I may become part of the water-cycle, distributed through underground channels, returned to the great sound that underlies everything.

I may survive. I may learn to navigate by vibration, to read the stone, to communicate through the water-telegraph. I may become one of Those Who Remain—pale, small, adapted, no longer remembering what it was like to see in full color or speak in air.

Or I may be something else entirely. A bridge. A translator. A border-walker who carries knowledge between the Expanse and the world outside—if that world even still exists, if I could ever find my way back to it, if I would want to.

I do not know. The water does not tell me. The stone keeps its secrets. The roar continues, constant and layered and ancient, and I am learning to hear the patterns within it.

Tonight, there will be drumming. I felt the invitation through the stone—a rhythm that meant *come* and *listen* and *we will teach you*. I will go to one of the quiet places, one of the sheltered depressions where the sound is muffled. I will hear the histories of a people who have lived here longer than memory. I will learn the patterns that encode their knowledge.

And I will try, in whatever way I can, to tell them what I carry.

Not because I want to infect them. Not because I was sent to change them. But because I am learning that survival here requires honesty. You cannot hide from a landscape that reads vibration. You cannot deceive a people who feel your heartbeat through the stone.

So I will tell them: *I came from outside. I was sent by people who do not understand this place. I carry something within me that may be dangerous or may be nothing. I am changing. I am dissolving. I am sorry for what I may have brought, and I am grateful for what you may be willing to teach.*

And then I will listen.

The Expanse has been speaking for geological ages. The water has been dissolving stone since before memory. The sound carries information that I am only beginning to decode.

I am here to learn.

I am here, finally, to listen.
