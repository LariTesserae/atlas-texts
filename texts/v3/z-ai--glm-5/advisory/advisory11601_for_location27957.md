# Preparation Guide: The Foundry Districts, Sub-Level V

## Physical Preparation

### Heat Management (Critical)

The heat here is deceptive. It is not the searing warning-burn of open flame but something more insidious — a wet, cumulative warmth that builds in your core before you realize it. You will not feel yourself overheating until you are already overheating.

**What to bring:**

- **Water.** More than you think you need. The humidity tricks you into feeling hydrated while the heat pulls moisture from you steadily. Bring at least two liters per hour of planned exposure. The condensation on every surface is not drinkable — it carries minerals, machine residue, and the faint chemical signature of processes you cannot identify.

- **Cooling vest or phase-change materials.** If you have access to technology that can manage body temperature, use it. The sub-level offers no natural cooling — even the stone walls radiate warmth.

- **Breathing protection.** Not a full respirator, necessarily, but something to filter the thick air. The heat makes breathing itself feel labored. A simple cloth mask dampened in clean water can help moderate the temperature of each breath and catch particulates.

- **Time yourself.** Set a hard limit before you descend. Two hours, perhaps three for a healthy adult. Beyond that, heat exhaustion becomes a real risk. The sub-level does not care about your endurance. It will simply wear you down.

### Navigation and Visibility

The light is insufficient by design. Power conservation for production means you navigate through penumbra and feeling.

**What to bring:**

- **Your own light source.** Multiple sources, ideally — a headlamp to keep your hands free, a backup handheld. The condensation will cloud lenses, so bring something to wipe with.

- **Gloves.** The kind that maintain grip when wet. You will be touching surfaces constantly — walls, pipes, railings — both to navigate and to steady yourself. The dampness makes everything treacherous. The pipes range from merely warm to scalding, and you cannot always tell by looking.

- **Sturdy footwear with grip.** The floor is wet, uneven in places, cut with drainage channels that catch the unwary ankle. Stone still sharp from excavation. Metal grating on catwalks. Do not descend in anything that slides on damp stone.

### The Environment Itself

**What to expect:**

- **No natural light.** No day-night cycle. Your body will lose track of time. Bring a watch or timekeeping device, and check it often.

- **No human presence.** This is an automated space. If you are injured, no one will find you quickly. If you become disoriented, no one will guide you out. Prepare for self-reliance.

- **No cellular signal, no wireless.** The stone and the industrial density block communication. You are alone with the machinery.

- **Slippery surfaces everywhere.** The walls weep. The floors collect moisture. Stairs are damp. Handrails are damp. Trust nothing to be dry.

## Mental Preparation

### The Atmosphere

This is a place that feels abandoned without being empty. The machinery works. The systems cycle. But the human element has receded — perhaps to the levels above, perhaps to some other fate you are not here to discover.

You will hear rhythm. The thumping of pumps. The tick of expanding metal. The breath-cycle of larger systems engaging. If you stay long enough, you will find yourself synchronizing to these rhythms without meaning to. This is not dangerous, but it is disorienting. You may lose track of time, of purpose, of how long you have been standing in one place watching condensation form on a pipe.

### The Sense of Process

The sub-level is not finished. It is being born. You are visiting a moment in an evolution — pipes added to pipes, systems layered over systems, stone freshly cut and already beginning its long transformation under heat and moisture.

You may feel a strange melancholy here. The engineered plants in their alcoves, growing without witness. The bioluminescent algae glowing in pools no one designed them for. The labels referencing dissolved departments. The sense that you have arrived after something ended and before something else has fully begun.

This is not a place of ghosts. It is a place of continuation without presence. The machines do not miss their operators. They simply continue.

## Practical Navigation

### Movement

- **Stay on designated paths.** Catwalks, stairs, main thoroughfares. The density of piping makes off-path exploration hazardous — valves, junctions, and mounting brackets create tripping hazards and head-strike risks in insufficient light.

- **Note your descent.** The spiral stairs are your primary exit. The sub-level is not a maze, but in dim heat, with disorientation setting in, you can lose your sense of direction easily. Count landings. Note features.

- **Avoid touching the steam pipes directly.** Some run hot enough to burn. Others are merely warm. The difference is not visually obvious.

- **Do not drink from the condensation pools.** Do not touch the algae. Do not disturb the engineered plants. They are not here for you, and you do not know what they have absorbed.

### Signs of Trouble

- **Dizziness or confusion.** The first signs of heat exhaustion. You may not notice your own decline. If your thoughts begin to blur, if the mechanical rhythms start to seem meaningful or inviting, begin your ascent immediately.

- **Difficulty breathing.** The thick air can strain those with respiratory vulnerabilities. If you feel your lungs laboring, head upward.

- **Equipment fogging or failing.** Heat and humidity can cloud optics, fog screens, degrade batteries faster than expected. Check your gear periodically.

---

## What to Leave Behind

Do not bring:

- **Excess electronics without protection.** Moisture infiltrates quickly.

- **Paper or unsecured documents.** The humidity will warp and dampen everything.

- **Expectations of comfort.** There are no benches, no rest areas, no amenities. The sub-level is a working circulatory system, not a destination.

- **Assumptions of permanence.** What you see will change. The sub-level will not remember you were here.

---

## Final Notes

You are descending into a place that was built to serve processes larger than any single person. It does not require your presence. It does not object to it either. The heat and the moisture and the dim light are not barriers to entry — they are simply the conditions of existence here.

If you treat the sub-level as a living system — not in the sense of consciousness, but in the sense of ongoing process — you will move through it more safely. It breathes. It cycles. It creates and absorbs heat. It evolves beyond its original design.

You are a visitor to a body that does not know you are there.

Prepare accordingly.
