Preparing for The Ovicide

## What You Are Walking Into

You are not going to explore a location. You are going to insert yourself into an organism that has been breathing for millions of years. The Ovicide is not a place you visit—it is a place you pass through, briefly, as an intrusion. The creatures that live there have no category for you. They will treat you as geology: an obstacle, a temperature gradient, a surface to be navigated. This is not contempt. This is not welcome. This is simply what they are.

Understand this before you begin: your presence there is an act of extraordinary presumption. The chamber predates anything you would recognize as civilization. It will persist long after anything you recognize as civilization has collapsed. You are a momentary disturbance in a pattern that has held steady since before your ancestors developed the capacity to notice patterns at all.

Pack accordingly.

---

## The Atmosphere Will Try to Kill You First

The air in the Ovicide is thin and desperately dry. The elevation alone would be enough to cause altitude sickness in sea-level dwellers, but the problem is more complex than oxygen partial pressure. The chamber sits in a volcanic dome, and the atmosphere inside has been isolated from the surface for geological ages. What gases remain are not necessarily what your body expects to breathe.

Bring a closed-circuit rebreather, not an open-circuit system. You do not want to be venting your exhaled gases into an ecosystem that operates on chemical gradients. The creatures may not respond well to sudden changes in atmospheric composition. More importantly, an open system will deplete faster, and you have no way to predict how long your visit will last.

Your mask must seal perfectly against your face. The dryness of the air will crack any skin left exposed, and the cracks will deepen quickly. Within hours—not days, hours—the skin around an imperfect seal will split and weep, and the fluid will evaporate almost instantly, leaving raw tissue exposed to mineral dust and the slow friction of creature bodies.

Your lips will crack. Your eyes will sting. The mucous membranes you rely upon will dry out. Bring lubricating eye drops in a sealed container, and apply them frequently through your mask's port. Bring water in a bladder system that delivers directly to your mouth without breaking the seal. You will not realize how thirsty you are until the symptoms begin, and by then you are already in trouble.

---

## The Temperature Will Kill You Second

The differential between the surface and the chamber is extreme. You will descend through passages that are cold enough to crack stone, and you will emerge into warmth that feels like a living body. The stone walls register at forty degrees Celsius or higher. The creature mass adds several degrees more.

This is not a comfortable warmth. This is the warmth of a sick room, of a fever, of a body fighting something it cannot defeat. You will sweat beneath your suit, and the sweat will have nowhere to go. Your core temperature will rise. You will feel sluggish, confused, and then you will stop feeling much of anything at all.

Wear a temperature-regulating suit with active cooling. The suit must be thin enough to allow movement through narrow passages but substantial enough to protect you from the heat. The cooling system must be self-contained; there is no water source in the chamber to replenish it. Calculate your expected duration generously, and then double it.

The passages leading down will be brutally cold. The chamber itself will be brutally warm. Your body will be shocked twice, once in each direction. The transition point is where most travelers make their mistake—they rush through it, eager to escape the cold, and then find themselves collapsing in the warmth. Move slowly. Let your body adjust.

---

## The Darkness Will Kill You Third

The darkness in the Ovicide is absolute. This is not metaphorical. There is no light source, natural or artificial, that reaches the main chamber. The passages from the surface twist and drop through convoluted paths that swallow every photon before it can travel more than a few meters.

Your eyes will not adjust. There is nothing to adjust to.

Bring multiple independent light sources. They must be reliable, durable, and red-filtered if possible. White light will not help you see better—the darkness is total—but it will affect any organisms that have retained light sensitivity, and you do not know what those organisms might do if suddenly illuminated. Red light is less likely to cause disruption, though even that is a guess. The creatures have no eyes. But they may have other capacities.

More importantly: light will not show you what you need to know. The chamber floor is covered in creatures, packed tight, shifting constantly. A light will reveal the immediate surface but not the layers beneath, not the gaps and hollows, not the way the mass moves in response to pressure. You will step onto what appears solid and find yourself sinking into pale segmented bodies that flow around you with patient, terrifying ease.

Learn to navigate by touch before you arrive. Practice moving through unfamiliar spaces with your eyes closed, your hands extended, your attention focused on what surfaces can support weight and what surfaces will shift. Your life will depend on this.

Bring a long probe—a lightweight staff that can test the ground ahead of you. Do not lean your weight on it. Use it to sense what is there, to feel the resistance of the creature mass, to map the boundaries of the space through contact.

---

## The Creatures Will Kill You Last

The hexapods are not aggressive. They do not bite. They do not sting. They do not have mouths in any sense that would allow them to consume you. They feed through osmotic patches along their undersides, pressing against the mineral crust, drawing out what they need through chemistry you do not understand and cannot replicate.

But they are heavy. They are dense. They are packed together so tightly that there is no space between them, only the constant slow shifting of bodies accommodating bodies, of movement that propagates through the mass like a wave through water.

If you stand still for too long, they will begin to flow over you.

They will not do this out of malice. They will do this because you are in their way, because their patterns must be maintained, because the space you occupy is space that could be filled with more of them. They will climb and press and pile, and the weight will accumulate, and eventually your ribs will not be able to expand against the mass, and your lungs will not be able to draw air through the mask that is pressed flat against your face by the pressure of pale translucent bodies.

You must keep moving.

Not quickly—the creatures will flow away from you if you move with reasonable speed, creating a temporary gap that closes behind you like water around a stone. But you cannot stop. You cannot rest. You cannot sit down and catch your breath and think about what comes next. The moment you stop moving, you become an obstacle, and obstacles in the Ovicide are eventually absorbed.

If you need to pause, do it in one of the surface passages, where the creature density is lower, where you can find solid stone to lean against. Do not pause in the main chamber. Do not pause near the central mound.

---

## The Central Mound

The mound at the center of the chamber is the only feature that suggests intention. It rises from the floor in a smooth dome, too regular to be natural, too perfect to be accidental. The surface has been worked somehow—polished or processed or subjected to forces that have made it denser than the surrounding stone.

It is warm. Warmer than the walls. Something beneath it generates heat.

The creatures avoid it. They press against each other in the spaces around its base, forming a living moat, but they do not climb its sides. They do not touch it. There is an unspoken rule, written into their collective behavior over millions of years, that keeps them away.

You do not know this rule. You do not know what it protects or what it warns against. You do not know whether the mound contains something dangerous or something sacred or simply something that the creatures, in their chemical-signal language, have learned to treat as off-limits.

Do not touch the mound.

This is not superstition. This is the recognition that you are in an environment you do not understand, surrounded by organisms that have lived here for longer than your species has existed, and that their behavior contains information you cannot access. If ten thousand generations of creatures have learned to avoid a specific object, there is a reason. You do not need to know the reason. You need only to respect it.

The artifacts scattered near the mound—the metal rod, the fused plates, the crushed vessel—are older than the word "ancient." They may have been here before the creatures arrived. They may have been brought by something that came before. They are not yours to take. Leave them.

## What to Bring

**Breathing**
- Closed-circuit rebreather with minimum 12-hour capacity
- Backup CO2 scrubber cartridges
- Mask seal lubricant
- Emergency oxygen supply (separate from primary system)

**Temperature Management**
- Active-cooling bodysuit with self-contained power source
- Thin thermal underlayer for the descent
- Insulated outer shell that can be removed before entering the warm chamber
- Temperature monitoring system with audible alerts

**Hydration and Nutrition**
- Bladder water system with drink tube (minimum 4 liters)
- Energy gels that can be consumed through mask ports
- Electrolyte supplements
- No food that requires chewing or produces crumbs

**Navigation**
- Long probe or staff (lightweight, collapsible)
- Guide line with knots tied at intervals for counting distance
- Three-dimensional compass (standard compasses may not function reliably in magnetic anomalies associated with volcanic intrusions)
- Red-filtered light source (primary and backup)

**Protection**
- Full-body suit with smooth surface (no straps, no loose edges for creatures to catch on)
- Reinforced ribcage section to resist compression
- Gloves with tactile sensitivity
- Boots with solid soles (no mesh, no openings that creatures could enter)

**Communication**
- Hard-wire communication system if traveling with others (radio will not penetrate)
-预先约定的触碰信号 (pre-arranged touch signals)
- Surface-level monitor that can track your descent and ascent times

**Documentation**
- Audio recorder (primary and backup)
- Thermal imaging is useless in absolute darkness but may help map temperature differentials
- Sample containers if you have reason to collect (you probably do not)
- No cameras unless they are infrared or sonar-based

## What Not to Bring

Do not bring anything that produces bright light. Do not bring anything that emits sound. Do not bring anything that releases chemicals into the air. Do not bring anything that you cannot afford to lose.

Do not bring expectations.

---

## The Ascent

The passage back to the surface will be harder than the descent. You will be tired. Your body will have been fighting the heat, the dryness, the creatures, the darkness, and your own fear for hours. The cold of the upper passages will hit you like a wall. The temperature differential will shock your system again, in reverse.

Move slowly. Do not rush. The passages are narrow and convoluted, and a mistake here will leave you trapped in the cold, unable to move forward or back, while your air supply diminishes.

When you reach the surface, you will emerge into light. You will see the sky again. The cold will feel clean and sharp after the close warmth of the chamber. You will want to stay there, breathing the thin outside air, feeling the stone beneath you, remembering that there is a world above the darkness.

Take that moment. Take it and then keep moving. The surface is not safe either. The cold will kill you if you stay too long. The altitude will kill you if you exert yourself. The Ovicide is not just the chamber below. The Ovicide is the entire intrusive body, from the deep dome to the cracked surface, and every part of it is hostile to human life.

---

## Final Warning

The name "Ovocide" means something. "The killing of the egg."

Perhaps this chamber was once a womb. Perhaps something was meant to be born here, and was not. Perhaps the mound at the center contains the remains of that unborn thing, kept warm by its own slow decay, tended by creatures that have evolved specifically to maintain the conditions around it.

Or perhaps the name is meaningless, a label applied by someone who never visited, who never felt the weight of pale bodies pressing against their chest, who never stood in absolute darkness and listened to the silence of ten thousand breathing organisms.

It does not matter.

What matters is that you are going to a place where you do not belong. You are entering a room that has existed for millions of years, that will exist for millions more, and that has no need of you whatsoever. You are an interruption. You are a disturbance. You will pass through and you will leave, and the Ovicide will continue without you.

Prepare accordingly.
