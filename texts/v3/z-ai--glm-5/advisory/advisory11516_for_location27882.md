# Preparing for the Vellum Plains: A Practical Guide for the Multiverse Traveler

## The First and Most Critical Understanding

You are not going to a place. You are going to *something that is alive*. This distinction will save your life, or its absence will end it. The Vellum Plains are not a landscape you visit; they are an organism you step onto, an entity kilometers deep and millions of years old, and it will feel you walk across its surface. It will know you are there.

Most travelers do not properly internalize this before arrival. They read about the living substrate and imagine a forest floor, soft with decomposing leaves, or perhaps a grassland with deep roots. They are wrong. The Vellum is not a collection of organisms. It is one organism, continuous across the entire plateau, so vast that "organism" becomes an inadequate word. You will be walking on its skin. You will be breathing air that passes through its collectors. You will be drinking fluids drawn from its body.

Prepare accordingly.

## Physiological Preparation: Before You Arrive

### Water Biology and Its Absence

The human body is approximately sixty percent water. Your cells exist in aqueous solution. Your blood is water-based. Your digestion, your respiration, your temperature regulation—all depend on water in ways so fundamental you have never had to think about them.

The Vellum Plains have not had free water for billions of years.

The residents, over thousands of generations, have adapted to drink a filtered hydrocarbon extract drawn from deep within the Vellum. Their biology has changed to process this fluid. Yours has not. You cannot drink what they drink. If you try, you will become ill, and illness in a place without water-based medicine is a serious matter.

**Preparation protocol:**

Bring your own water. This sounds simple. It is not.

Calculate your water needs carefully. The thin atmosphere and dry conditions will accelerate dehydration. The settlements extract fluid from the Vellum, but they cannot give you water because water does not exist there in any accessible form. There are sealed pockets deep underground, but these are not reachable, and even if they were, their chemistry would likely be incompatible with human biology.

Bring more water than you think you need. Then bring more. A minimum of four liters per day per person, plus emergency reserves. For a week-long stay, you are carrying significant weight in water alone. Consider a portable condensation unit, though be aware that the atmosphere contains almost no moisture to condense—you will be extracting from a dry well.

**Additional consideration:** Your body will lose water faster than you expect. The Vellum's surface, despite being alive, does not release moisture. There is no humidity to slow your evaporation. Your breath will disappear into the thin air, carrying precious water with it. Learn to breathe through your nose rather than your mouth. Practice this before you arrive.

### Atmospheric Considerations

The air is thin. High plateau country means reduced pressure, and while the Vellum's metabolism produces some gases, the overall density is lower than sea-level Earth atmosphere. If you have any respiratory conditions, reconsider your visit. If you are healthy, expect some adjustment period: shortness of breath, lightheadedness, fatigue.

**Preparation protocol:**

Consider supplemental oxygen, though be aware that carrying pressurized containers adds weight and risk. A better option for shorter visits is to arrive early and acclimatize gradually. Spend time at high altitudes before your departure. Train your body to function with less oxygen.

The Vellum's gas exchange processes mean the atmospheric composition may differ from Earth's. The collectors concentrate light, but they also filter and process air. The exact ratios of nitrogen, oxygen, and trace gases have not been documented in standard multiverse databases. Bring a basic atmospheric analyzer and be prepared to use supplemental oxygen if readings show dangerous composition.

### Temperature Regulation

The days are cool. The nights are cold. The temperature drop after dusk is sharp and immediate. The Vellum generates minimal metabolic heat, and the thin atmosphere cannot hold warmth.

**Preparation protocol:**

Layered clothing is essential. The residents wear dense woven coats grown from Vellum cultivars, but you will not have access to these immediately, if ever—growing fiber from the substrate requires negotiation, and negotiation requires understanding you will not possess upon arrival.

Bring thermal underlayers rated for sub-freezing temperatures. Bring a high-quality insulated outer layer, windproof, because the storms that cross the plain carry biting wind and abrasive particles. Bring gloves, a hat or hood, and consider a balaclava or face covering for night movement.

More importantly: prepare mentally for the cold. It will seep into you during the long nights. The settlements stay cooler inside than might feel comfortable. You will not escape the temperature; you will only mitigate it. If you are someone who struggles with cold climates, reconsider whether this destination is appropriate for your constitution.

## Sensory Preparation: The Sound

I cannot overstate this.

The sound is the single most disorienting aspect of the Vellum Plains, and most travelers are entirely unprepared for it. Reading about "a continuous chord" does not convey the experience of hearing it, feeling it, living within it.

### What the Sound Is

The Vellum produces a constant, resonant vibration through its millions of surface collectors. At dawn, it begins as a low thrum and builds throughout the morning. At midday, it reaches a wall of sound that makes conversation difficult and coherent thought challenging. At dusk, it descends through registers into a murmur, then a whisper, then a sigh. At night, it fades entirely, replaced by the groaning of cooling substrate and the calls of nocturnal creatures.

But during daylight hours, the sound is *everywhere*. It comes from the ground beneath your feet. It resonates through the walls of the settlements. It vibrates in your bones. You cannot escape it by covering your ears, because it travels through the substrate and into your body directly.

**Preparation protocol:**

There is no way to fully prepare for the sound. You can only mitigate its effects.

First, obtain high-quality ear protection designed for industrial noise environments. Simple foam earplugs will not suffice. You need something that can filter dominant frequencies while allowing some sound through—complete deafness to the environment is dangerous. The residents use specialized membranes grown to filter specific frequencies, but these require intimate knowledge of the chord's structure and custom growth processes unavailable to travelers.

Second, practice functioning in loud environments before arrival. Spend time in spaces with sustained, resonant sound. Train your mind to maintain focus despite auditory distraction. Some travelers report that meditation practice helps, the ability to acknowledge the sound without fighting it, to let it exist as background rather than assault.

Third, plan your activities around the chord's intensity. Midday is the worst. The settlements observe a kind of siesta during peak intensity, retreating indoors where walls dampen the resonance. Do not attempt significant physical or mental activity during this period. Use it for rest. Use it for quiet reflection. Do not try to work through it; you will fail.

Fourth, accept that the sound will affect your psychology. Some frequencies promote alertness and well-being; others may produce anxiety or unease. These effects are not imaginary. The Vellum's vibration influences human physiology in ways the settlements have learned to work with. You, as a visitor, will be subject to these influences without the residents' lifetime of adaptation. Monitor your mental state. If you notice unusual mood shifts, anxiety, or disorientation, do not assume they are merely your reaction to strangeness; they may be direct physiological effects of the chord itself.

### The Night Groan

When the chord fades at night, another sound emerges: a deep groaning as the Vellum's upper layers contract in the cooling air. This sound travels through the ground and up through the buildings. It feels organic, living, like the sound of a massive animal shifting in its sleep.

For newcomers, this sound is profoundly unsettling. It triggers primal responses, the instinct that something large and alive is nearby, watching, waiting. Some travelers find they cannot sleep through it. Some find it unbearable after a few nights.

**Preparation protocol:**

There is no physical protection against this sound. It travels through the ground, through the walls, through your bed into your body. You must prepare mentally.

Understand what the sound is: thermal contraction of a massive biological structure. It is not threat, not warning, not communication. It is the world breathing. The residents find comfort in it, the sound of home, of safety, of life continuing around them.

You may never find comfort in it. But you can learn to accept it. Practice sleeping with ambient noise, particularly low-frequency sounds. Use the daytime chord exposure to desensitize yourself gradually. And at night, when the groaning rises, try to reframe it: not as monster, but as organism. You are sleeping on something alive. This is the sound of its life continuing.

## Psychological Preparation: The Weight of Antiquity

The Vellum is old beyond human comprehension.

Your species, on your world, is perhaps three hundred thousand years old. Your planet is four and a half billion years old, and multicellular life has existed for perhaps six hundred million. These numbers are large, but they are comprehensible. You can imagine them, hold them in your mind.

The Vellum has been accumulating for longer than Earth has had multicellular life.

The erosion parameter, the geological record of the substrate, shows strata upon strata, layer upon layer, bodies compressed into more bodies, billions of years of continuous growth and death and regrowth. The original mineral crust lies kilometers below, inaccessible, almost mythological. When you walk on the Vellum, you walk on remains that were alive before your world had complex organisms.

This antiquity affects visitors in different ways. Some feel awe. Some feel insignificance. Some feel a kind of existential vertigo, the sense that they are walking on something so old it has forgotten more history than their entire civilization will ever accumulate.

**Preparation protocol:**

Reflect on your relationship to deep time before arrival. Consider how you feel about age, about mortality, about the vastness of existence. If you are prone to existential distress, the Vellum Plains may intensify this. The sheer weight of years presses down through the living substrate into your awareness.

Conversely, if you find deep time meaningful rather than threatening, if you draw comfort from the long view of existence, you may find the Vellum Plains profoundly moving. Some travelers report a sense of peace, of connection, of belonging to something larger than themselves. The Vellum has persisted for billions of years; it will persist for billions more. Against this backdrop, individual human concerns can seem small—but small can be either crushing or liberating, depending on your temperament.

Know yourself before you arrive. The Vellum will not change for you. It has not changed for anything in epochs. It will be as it is, and you will find in it what you bring.

## Behavioral Preparation: Negotiation, Not Engineering

The settlements do not build. They grow.

This distinction is critical. On Earth, on most worlds, architecture is imposition: you take materials, shape them, force them into configurations they would not naturally assume. You engineer structures. You impose your will on matter.

The Vellum does not accept imposition.

The substrate is alive. It has its own processes, its own direction, its own purposes—whatever those purposes may be. The settlements have learned to work with these processes rather than against them. They do not build structures; they negotiate with the Vellum to grow structures. They do not dig foundations; they persuade the substrate to form hollows. They do not manufacture materials; they cultivate them.

The technology for this persuasion is embedded in the settlements' founding codes, passed down through generations of substrate-wrights. It involves "growth-songs"—complex harmonic patterns that trigger specific responses in the Vellum's surface layers. These are not language; they are closer to music, and they take a lifetime to master.

As a visitor, you will not know the growth-songs. You will not understand the negotiation. You will be a guest in structures that were coaxed into existence through processes you cannot comprehend, living on a substrate that tolerates your presence because the settlements have negotiated that tolerance.

**Preparation protocol:**

Approach everything with humility. Do not assume you understand how anything works. The Vellum's technology—biological computing, chemical communication, organic interfaces—operates on principles that exceed standard Earth science. The physics is the same, but the engineering has progressed along paths your civilization has not discovered.

Observe without interfering. The settlements maintain careful boundaries with the Vellum, zones they do not enter, processes they do not interrupt. Respect these boundaries absolutely. The Vellum has immune responses. The Vellum can become aggressive. The settlements mark forbidden zones with dark spires; do not approach them.

Ask before you touch. The Vellum's surface is alive, covered in collectors and maintenance organisms. Your touch may damage delicate structures. Your presence may trigger responses. The residents will know which areas can tolerate visitors and which cannot. Follow their guidance precisely.

Understand that you are being tolerated, not welcomed. The Vellum has no reason to care about you specifically. The settlements have negotiated space for themselves; your presence is an extension of that negotiation, but you have not personally contributed to it. You are a guest of guests. Act accordingly.

## Practical Preparation: What to Bring

### Essential Equipment

**Water supply:** As discussed, bring more than you think you need. Consider collapsible containers to minimize weight. Bring a filtration system in case you must extend your stay unexpectedly, though be aware that no water sources exist to filter.

**Food supply:** The settlements process Vellum-tissue into food, but their food will be incompatible with your digestion. Bring nutritionally complete, lightweight food that requires no preparation. Bars, dried foods, nutrient pastes. Avoid anything that requires water to prepare, because your water is precious.

**Breathing equipment:** Optional for healthy travelers, but consider a basic supplemental oxygen system for emergency use. The thin atmosphere may cause problems you cannot predict. A simple pulse oximeter will help you monitor your blood oxygen levels.

**Thermal gear:** Layered, windproof, rated for sub-freezing conditions. Nights are cold, and the storms are colder. Bring more warmth than you think you need.

**Ear protection:** High-quality, frequency-filtering, industrial-grade. Bring backup pairs. If you lose your ear protection, the midday chord will become genuinely unbearable.

**Eye protection:** The light is dim, but the silvered surface reflects it in complex patterns. The Vellum-dust kicked up by runners and storms can irritate eyes. Bring protective eyewear, ideally with some UV filtering despite the dim sun.

**Light source:** The settlements use bioluminescent lighting that provides dim amber illumination. Your human eyes may struggle. Bring a reliable light source, but avoid anything too bright—intense light may damage the Vellum's delicate surface structures. Red light is preferable, as it will affect night vision less and may be less disruptive to local biology.

**Navigation equipment:** Standard GPS will not work. The settlements navigate by ground features and navigate by the Vellum's patterns rather than stars. Bring a reliable compass, but be aware that magnetic fields may behave unexpectedly. Learn to identify the dark warning spires that mark forbidden zones. If possible, obtain a map from settlement residents before venturing far from your base.

**First aid supplies:** The settlements have medical practices based on Vellum-chemistry and sound-therapy. They may not have supplies compatible with human physiology. Bring a comprehensive first aid kit, including bandages, antiseptics, pain relievers, and any personal medications you require. Consider bringing a basic surgical kit for emergency wound closure.

**Communication:** Your standard communication devices will not interface with the Vellum's organic network. You will need a standalone system to communicate with your party, ideally one that works through sound rather than any signal the Vellum might misinterpret.

### Optional but Recommended Equipment

**Recording equipment:** The Vellum Plains are unlike anywhere else in the known multiverse. Audio and video recordings will be valuable. Be aware that the chord may overwhelm standard microphones; bring equipment rated for high-decibel environments.

**Written materials:** Something to read during midday siesta, when activity ceases and the sound makes concentration difficult. Choose something absorbing, something that can hold your attention against the wall of vibration. Avoid complex material; your cognitive capacity will be reduced.

**Comfort items:** Something small, something from home. The Vellum Plains are alien in ways that accumulate. A familiar object can provide psychological grounding. Do not bring anything alive; do not bring seeds, plants, or organic material that might interact with the Vellum. The immune responses are not worth risking.

**A gift:** The settlements are sparse, insular, ancient. They do not need material goods. But a gesture of goodwill—a small cultural artifact, something beautiful, something that represents your world—may open doors that remain closed to mere curiosity.

## Cultural Preparation: Understanding the Settlements

The residents have lived on the Vellum for thousands of generations. They have adapted biologically to drink hydrocarbon fluids, to breathe thin atmosphere, to endure constant sound, to live in organic structures grown from living substrate. They are not quite human anymore, not in the ways that matter. Their relationship to the Vellum is symbiotic, negotiated, ancient.

You will be an outsider. A temporary presence. Someone who has not earned, through countless generations of careful tending, the right to walk on this living surface.

**Preparation protocol:**

Approach with respect. Do not assume the settlements operate on principles you understand. Their technology, their social structures, their relationship with the Vellum—all have evolved over timescales that make your civilization young.

Learn the basic parameters of their relationship with the Vellum:

- The settlements do not own their buildings; they tend them.
- The Vellum provides everything; the settlements provide careful management in return.
- Growth-songs are proprietary, held by lineages, traded like currency.
- Forbidden zones are not suggestions; they are survival.
- The chord tells time; learn to read its tonal shifts.

Expect reserve. The settlements are not hostile, but they are not eager for visitors either. You are a disruption to a system that has achieved stability over billions of years. They may be curious about you, but they will also be cautious. Do not press. Do not demand. Accept what hospitality is offered, and do not expect more.

Listen more than you speak. The settlements know the Vellum; you do not. They understand the sounds, the zones, the weather, the runners. Every resident, even a child, knows more about surviving on the Vellum than you ever will during your brief visit. Defer to their knowledge. Ask questions, but ask with genuine curiosity rather than assumption.

Be prepared to work. The settlements may expect guests to contribute to daily maintenance—tending structures, clearing dead collectors, processing the day's harvest. Do not treat this as imposition; treat it as opportunity. You will learn more through participation than observation. You will earn, in some small way, the right to stand on this living ground.

## Emergency Preparation: When Things Go Wrong

The Vellum is generally stable. It has existed in its current form for longer than your world has had complex life. But stability at the geological scale does not guarantee safety at the individual scale.

### Storms

The Vellum detects incoming storms before human senses register them. Watch for the surface preparing: collectors folding inward, the hyphal mat tightening. When you see these signs, seek shelter immediately. The settlements pulse warning lights—amber, then amber-red. Do not ignore these signals.

Inside a settlement, storms are survivable. The buildings flex rather than break. Outside, exposed on the plain, you face wind-driven particles that can abrade exposed skin, disorientation from reduced visibility, and the risk of being caught in areas where the Vellum's surface is damaged.

If caught outside during a storm, find any hollow or depression. Lie flat. Cover exposed skin. Wait. The storm will pass. The Vellum will heal itself. Your concern is surviving until that happens.

### Runners

The runners are not predators, but they are also not careful about what lies in their path. A herd moving across the plain at speed will not swerve for a single visitor. Their undersides strip Vellum tissue; they can strip human tissue just as easily.

Learn to listen for the drumming. The sound travels through the Vellum, announcing herds long before they crest the horizon. When you hear the drumming, note its direction and move perpendicular. Do not run parallel to the herd; do not freeze in place. The runners graze by running; they will continue running whether you are there or not. Your job is to not be there.

### Forbidden Zones

The Vellum has immune responses. In certain zones, the surface collectors fold inward, tissue hardens and darkens, and the substrate becomes aggressive. The settlements mark these zones with dark spires. These warnings are absolute.

If you accidentally enter a forbidden zone, you may notice the change before anything hostile occurs: the surface looks different, feels different, sounds different. The chord's harmonics shift. The ground beneath your feet may begin to resist rather than rebound.

Turn around. Do not investigate. Do not collect samples. Turn around and walk out the way you came, slowly, without sudden movements. The Vellum's immune responses are not fast—they operate on geological timescales—but you do not want to be present when they complete their response.

### Medical Emergency

If you become ill or injured, the settlements have medical practices, but they are based on sound-therapy and Vellum-derived compounds. Some frequencies promote healing; some compounds may be compatible with human biology. Many will not be.

For serious emergencies, your best option is self-reliance. Use your first aid supplies. Use your emergency communication equipment to contact your extraction point, if you have one. Do not expect the settlements to have resources for a biology they have not encountered in thousands of generations.

The residents may help you—they are not cruel—but they may also be limited by their own adaptation. They may not have water for you. They may not have medicines that work on human physiology. They may treat you with sound and hope.

## Philosophical Preparation: The Question of Consciousness

Some researchers believe the Vellum is waking up.

The Vellum has no central nervous system, no brain, no obvious seat of consciousness. But it processes information at the molecular level. It stores data in protein structures. It computes using chemical gradients and quantum tunneling. It has been doing this for billions of years across millions of square kilometers of living substrate.

The amount of information processing occurring at any given moment might exceed the computational capacity of any civilization that has ever existed.

Perhaps it thinks. Perhaps it dreams. Perhaps the chord is its voice, and you are walking across a singing mind so vast it does not notice you any more than you notice individual cells in your own body.

**Preparation protocol:**

You cannot prepare for this intellectually. No amount of reading will help you understand a consciousness that operates on geological timescales through distributed biological computation.

But you can prepare emotionally, spiritually, existentially.

Before you arrive, spend time with the question: what if you are walking on something that is aware? What if the ground beneath your feet is thinking thoughts you cannot comprehend, dreaming dreams that last for millennia, singing a song that has continued for billions of years and will continue for billions more?

Some travelers find this possibility terrifying—the sense of being watched, however indifferently, by something impossibly vast. Others find it transcendent—the opportunity to touch, however briefly, a form of consciousness so different from human experience that it challenges every assumption about what mind can be.

You will not resolve this question. The Vellum, if it is conscious, does not communicate in ways you can understand. The chord may be voice, but you do not speak the language. You will walk on its surface, drink from its depths, shelter in structures it has been persuaded to grow, and you will never know whether it knows you are there.

But the question will change you. The not-knowing will change you. The possibility will linger in your mind long after you have left the Vellum Plains behind.

## Final Preparations: What to Expect

You will arrive disoriented. The thin air, the dim light, the strange sound rising from the living ground, the silvered expanse stretching to every horizon—these will overwhelm your senses before you have taken ten steps.

You will not be comfortable. The cold will seep into you. The sound will vibrate in your bones. The knowledge that you are standing on something alive will follow you into your dreams, if you can sleep at all.

You will feel small. The Vellum is old, vast, and continuous. You are temporary, tiny, and discrete. This feeling may intensify into existential crisis, or it may open into a kind of peace. Which direction it takes depends on what you bring with you.

You will not understand. The Vellum operates on principles you have not learned, through processes you cannot perceive, across timescales you cannot comprehend. The settlements negotiate with something you cannot communicate with. You will be surrounded by meaning you cannot access, by knowledge you cannot share.

But you will also witness something unique in the known multiverse. A world that is also an organism. A song that is also a voice. A landscape that is also a body. A stability so ancient it has worn grooves into itself, a cycle so old it has become geography.

You will walk on something billions of years old. You will hear its continuous song. You will see light collected by structures evolved for dim sun. You will drink from its reserves, sleep within its grown walls, and know, in whatever way you can know such things, that you have been somewhere that is also something, somewhere that exists and lives, somewhere that persists beyond all human scales of time.

The ground is alive. The sound is constant. The light is dim.

And you, briefly, incomprehensibly, will be there.

## A Final Note

The residents have a saying, translated imperfectly from their growth-song dialect:

*The Vellum does not need us. We need the Vellum. This is the only arrangement that works.*

Remember this. You are not the first visitor to the Vellum Plains, and you will not be the last. But the Vellum was here before any visitors came, and it will be here after they have all gone. It has survived storms, extinctions, the death of suns, the slow cooling of worlds. It has maintained its chord across billions of years.

You are a moment. You are a breath. You are a single footfall on a surface that has felt countless feet, that will feel countless more, that does not remember individuals because individuals are too brief to remember.

Walk lightly. Listen well. And when you leave, if you leave, carry with you the sound of something that has been singing longer than your world has existed—the chord of the Vellum, the voice of the Plains, the song of something so old and so alive that the only appropriate response is awe.
