# Preparing for the Seep

## What You Need to Understand Before You Step In

You are not going to explore the Seep. You are going to enter a system that will explore *you*.

Let that settle into your bones before you pack anything. The Seep is not a place you visit and leave unchanged. It is a continental-scale digestive system running at equilibrium, and you are a foreign object it will eventually process. The only question is whether you leave before that happens or become part of its cycling.

This is not nihilism. This is the practical reality of a place where the floor eats the dead and the creatures move in patterns that haven't altered in what might be millions of years.

## The Physical Preparation

### Humidity Management

The humidity will be your first enemy and your constant companion.

Expect 90% humidity or higher. The air holds so much water that breathing feels like drinking. Your lungs will work harder to extract oxygen from the thick wet atmosphere. Your skin will prune within hours and stay that way. Every pore on your body will be constantly damp.

**What to bring:**

- **A full-body moisture-wicking underlayer**. Not cotton. Cotton will rot. Synthetic fibers designed for diving or industrial saturation environments. You will never be dry, but this layer determines whether your skin stays intact or macerates into vulnerability.

- **A sealed water supply**. The water here moves through channels and condenses on every surface. It has passed through fungal films and over manufactured substrates older than your species. It may be chemically safe—this system runs on precision—but you cannot know what organic compounds the fungal mats secrete. Bring your own water. Bring more than you think you need. The humidity masks dehydration; you will not feel yourself drying out until you are already in trouble.

- **Antifungal treatments**. Broad-spectrum, industrial-strength. Apply to your feet, your groin, your armpits, any fold of skin where moisture collects. The fungal mats here are aggressive and opportunistic. They will test every surface of you for nutritional value. Give them no purchase.

- **Sealed goggles or a face shield**. The condensation films on every vertical surface, and when you move through creature-dense areas, the motion of billions of bodies aerosolizes the wet air. You will be breathing and walking through a fine particulate mist of biological residue. Protect your eyes. The goggles will fog constantly; bring anti-fog coatings and accept that your vision will be compromised.

### Temperature Regulation

The Seep is warm. Consistently, radiantly, exhaustively warm. The heat rises from below, from infrastructure you cannot reach and cannot affect.

This is not desert heat. This is swamp heat, greenhouse heat, the heat of a closed system that has perfected its own metabolism.

**What to bring:**

- **Lightweight, breathable outer layers**. You will overheat if you overdress. The danger is not cold; the danger is heat exhaustion in an environment that never cools. Dress for exertion in tropical conditions.

- **Electrolyte supplements**. You will sweat constantly. The humidity prevents evaporation, which prevents cooling, which increases sweating. You will lose salts faster than you realize. Supplement aggressively.

- **A cooling system if you can manage it**. A circulating-water vest or phase-change cooling garment. Something that can operate on minimal power and provide relief during rest periods. You will not sleep well in the Seep's heat; give your body every advantage.

### Navigation and Orientation

The Seep has no landmarks.

This is not hyperbole. The construction is continental. The basins repeat. The fungal films vary in color by sector, but these variations are subtle—a shift from copper-green to iron-red across kilometers of walking that all looks identical at ground level. The manufactured light is constant. There is no sun to orient by, no stars, no magnetic field you can trust.

You will become disoriented within minutes. You will lose all sense of direction within hours.

**What to bring:**

- **Inertial navigation**. A dead-reckoning system that tracks your movement from a known starting point. Do not rely on GPS-equivalent technologies; whatever this place was built for, it was not built to be friendly to your electronics, and the manufactured environment may interfere with signals.

- **Physical markers**. Biodegradable trail markers you can leave behind you. Do not use anything that persists. The Seep processes foreign matter; your markers will be consumed eventually. But in the short term, a trail of color-contrasting material can help you retrace steps if you need to retreat.

- **A fixed reference point**. If you arrived through a specific aperture, portal, or entry zone, mark it. Spend time making that mark unmistakable to your future exhausted self. The creatures will flow around it, the condensation will dampen it, but if you need to leave, you need to know where leaving happens.

## The Biological Preparation

### The Fauna

You will walk on creatures. This is unavoidable.

The density of engineered life in the Seep means that any flat surface is already occupied, and any surface you clear will be reoccupied within moments. The creatures do not bite or sting or attack. They flow. They navigate around obstacles with fluid precision.

But you are an obstacle.

You will feel them against your boots, your legs, sometimes your hands if you reach out in front of you. Carapaces and soft bodies and limbs designed for purposes you cannot guess. They will move over you if you hold still long enough. They will investigate the texture of your skin, the taste of your sweat, the scent of your unfamiliar biology.

**How to prepare:**

- **Thick-soled boots with closed ankles**. You will be walking on bodies. Some will be hard, some soft, some will move away, some will not. Your boots need to protect you from crushing damage and from the reverse—something crawling up inside your footwear and becoming distressed there.

- **Gloves**. Not thin tactical gloves. Something with puncture resistance. You may need to push through dense fauna-clouds, and your hands will be the first point of contact.

- **Psychological inoculation**. This is not optional. Before you enter the Seep, expose yourself to high-density insect swarms, to vermin populations, to any environment where biological density exceeds comfort. You need to know how your particular nervous system responds to walking through living masses. Some people freeze. Some people panic. Some people dissociate. Find out which you are *before* you are standing in the Seep with no way to immediately leave.

- **Do not eat the creatures**. They are engineered. Their biochemistry may be incompatible with your digestion, or they may contain compounds that your body cannot process. They may also contain part numbers and tooling marks that would make the act of eating feel deeply wrong. You are already in a system that will process you if you stop; do not start processing parts of the system without knowing what they do.

### The Fungal Mats

The floor is a slow predator.

The fungal films that cover every surface are patient. They break down organic residue—there is always organic residue—and they respond to stillness. When a creature's movement falters, the films rise. When a creature's function ends, the films cover.

You are a creature. You have a function. When your function ends, the films will respond.

**How to prepare:**

- **Keep moving**. This is not metaphorical advice. When you rest, rest in motion—shift your weight, flex your muscles, create micro-movements that signal continued function. Do not lie still for extended periods. Do not sleep without setting a waking schedule.

- **Sleep in shifts**. If you are traveling with others, sleep in rotations. Someone should always be awake and moving, creating enough disturbance to discourage the films from rising toward the resting party.

- **If you must sleep alone**, create an alarm system. A motion trigger that wakes you if your own movement stops. The fungal mats do not move quickly, but they do not need to be quick when their target is unconscious.

- **Do not touch the mats directly**. Your gloves are your barrier. If you see the films rising—undulating toward you with slow purpose—move away. They are not aggressive. They are not hunting you. They are simply doing what they were designed to do: process organic material that has stopped processing itself.

## The Psychological Preparation

### The Sound

The Seep is quiet in the way a factory floor is quiet. The sound exists as texture—a susurrus of billions of small bodies in motion, the click of carapaces, the wet movement of creatures through condensation.

You will hear it constantly. It will become white noise. It will fade from your conscious attention.

And then you will notice that it has no variation. No calls, no songs, no communication beyond touch and chemical exchange. The sound is the sound of a system running itself.

For some travelers, this is comforting. For others, it is deeply unsettling. Know which you are before you enter.

### The Sameness

The Seep does not change.

You can walk for days and see the same constructed surfaces, the same fungal colors, the same creature-types in the same densities. The light does not shift. The warmth does not vary. There are no landmarks, no destinations, no variations in the terrain that signal progress.

This produces a specific psychological state in travelers. The lack of environmental feedback creates the sensation of being nowhere. Of having never left where you started. Of moving through a loop.

Some minds accept this. Some minds cannot.

**How to prepare:**

- **Bring a way to mark time**. Not external time—the Seep has no days and nights—but your own internal time. A counter for steps taken, a log for distance traveled, a personal journal where you record observations. Give yourself a sense of progression even when the environment refuses to provide one.

- **Bring a way to mark progress**. If you are exploring, photograph the fungal films in each sector and catalog the variations. If you are searching for something, write down what you find and where. Create your own landmarks in your records.

- **Prepare for dissociation**. The combination of constant sensory input (wet, warm, creatures, quiet) and constant environmental sameness can produce a trance state. You may find yourself walking for hours with no memory of the intervening distance. This is not inherently dangerous in the Seep—you cannot fall off edges, there are no predators—but you may lose track of your own goals. Build in check-in points with yourself. Scheduled moments where you stop and ask: *What am I doing? Why am I here? Am I still myself?*

### The Scale

You are small.

The Seep is continental. Planetary. Larger than you can walk across in a human lifetime. It was built by something that operated on scales you cannot imagine, for purposes you cannot know, and it will continue operating long after you leave.

This can produce feelings of insignificance. Of being a temporary irritant in a system that will process you as casually as it processes its own dead.

These feelings are accurate.

The question is whether you can hold them without being crushed by them.

**How to prepare:**

- **Have a reason for being there**. A specific goal, a specific question, a specific curiosity. Something that gives your presence purpose beyond mere existence in the system. The Seep does not need you. You need to need something from the Seep.

- **Know your exit**. Know how you will leave before you arrive. Whether that is a portal, a guide, a specific location you must reach, or a timed departure. The Seep will not offer you an exit; it has no reason to. You must carry your exit with you.

## What Not to Bring

- **Plant matter**. Not seeds, not cuttings, not dried leaves in a talisman pouch. The Seep has no plants and has never had plants. Introducing plant matter—introducing the *concept* of plant matter—into a system designed without it could produce unpredictable results. The fungal mats may treat it as food. The engineered fauna may treat it as threat. Or it may simply rot, adding foreign organic residue to a closed system that does not know how to process it.

- **Expectations of hostility**. The Seep is not hostile. It is not friendly. It is indifferent. Prepare for indifference. Prepare for a system that does not care whether you live or die because it has no capacity to care. The danger is not malice; the danger is irrelevance.

## Final Preparation

Stand at the edge before you enter.

Watch the creatures flow. Watch the condensation bead and slide. Feel the warmth rising through the soles of your boots.

Ask yourself: *Why am I going in there?*

If the answer is curiosity, hold that curiosity tight. It will be your compass when nothing else provides direction.

If the answer is necessity, know what you need and do not linger once you have it.

If the answer is that you want to be processed by something larger than yourself, turn around. The Seep will process you eventually, but that is not a reason to visit. That is a reason to stay away.

The Seep will be there long after you are gone. It will cycle through its creatures and its water and its warmth and its fungal films with perfect equilibrium, producing nothing, needing nothing, continuing because that is what it was built to do.

You are a traveler. You move. You change. You leave.

Enter the Seep knowing that you will leave—or do not enter at all.
