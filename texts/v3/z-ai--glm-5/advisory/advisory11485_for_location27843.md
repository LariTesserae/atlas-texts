# Preparing for the Pocket City of Warm Stone

## An Ordinary Traveler's Guide

---

## BEFORE YOU GO: The Decision

First, understand what you are choosing. The Pocket City is not dangerous in the ways that other locations are dangerous—there are no predators, no hostile entities, no apparent traps. But it is *consuming*. Travelers report a peculiar difficulty in leaving, not because the door won't open, but because something in the rhythm of the place makes the thought of departure feel foreign, even wrong. Several multiverse travelers have simply... stayed. Their bodies were recovered later, dehydrated, peaceful, positioned in the plaza as if arranged.

Go if you are called to witness. Go if you have questions about patience, about deep time, about what survives when water does not. Do not go if you are running from something—this place has a way of making you sit with yourself until you understand exactly what you are.

---

## PHYSICAL PREPARATION: The Body's Needs

### Water

This is your primary concern. The Pocket City has no water. None. The stone is dry, the air is dry, the plants do not hold moisture you can access. There are no hidden springs, no condensation trickling down walls, no moisture of any kind that human physiology can use.

**Bring more water than you think you need.**

The standard recommendation is four liters per day minimum, but in the Pocket City's heat—constant, rising from below, surrounding you like a medium—you will need more. Six liters per day is safer. Eight if you plan to explore thoroughly or stay overnight.

**Container considerations:** The mineral-rich air and the thermal vents produce sulfur compounds that can corrode certain materials. Stainless steel or glass containers are preferred. Avoid aluminum. The heat will make plastic water bottles taste increasingly chemical, and there is some evidence that the air accelerates leaching.

**Temperature of your water:** It will not stay cool. The ambient temperature is a constant warm embrace—travelers describe it as "like standing in a slow exhalation." Your water will reach ambient temperature within an hour. Some find this comforting. Others find warm water difficult to drink in quantity. Consider adding electrolyte powder to make warm water more palatable and to replace what you lose through sweating.

### Cooling

You cannot escape the heat, but you can manage your body's response to it.

**Cooling vest:** If your multiverse travel kit includes one, this is the place to use it. The gel-based versions work well; the phase-change varieties are better. Freeze or chill them before entering if possible. In the Pocket City's sealed environment, they will not re-freeze. You have perhaps four to six hours of effective cooling before they reach ambient temperature.

**Evaporative cooling:** The air is dry enough that wet cloth wrapped around your neck, wrists, and ankles will provide relief. Bring extra cloth or bandanas specifically for this purpose. Wet them with your precious water—this may seem wasteful, but heat exhaustion will end your exploration faster than dehydration. Budget water for cooling.

**Pacing:** This is not a place to hurry. The heat punishes exertion. Move slowly. Rest often. The city is small—you can traverse it in twenty minutes—but give yourself hours. Days, if you have the supplies. The Pocket City rewards slowness. It opens itself to those who match its thermal rhythm.

### Breathing

The air tastes mineral and metallic. It is dense with compounds rising from the thermal vents—sulfur, various minerals, possibly trace gases from deep geological processes. Most travelers report no acute respiratory distress. A few experience mild headache or lightheadedness in the first hours, which typically passes as the body adjusts.

**However:** Those with asthma, sensitive respiratory systems, or concerns about sulfur dioxide exposure should consider a simple respirator or N95 mask. The mask will be uncomfortable in the heat, but it will filter the mineral particulates that hang in the amber light.

If you begin to feel genuinely dizzy, nauseous, or confused, exit immediately. These can be symptoms of heat exhaustion, dehydration, or gas exposure. The Pocket City will still be there when you recover. Your body may not be.

### Clothing

**Natural fibers.** Cotton, linen, light wool. Nothing synthetic that will trap heat or melt against your skin if you accidentally touch sun-warmed stone. The thermal vents can reach temperatures that burn on contact, though they are generally identifiable by the hissing sound and the intensified glow of the surrounding vegetation.

**Light colors.** The amber twilight is dim enough without you wearing black that absorbs what little light exists. White, cream, pale tan—these will help you see yourself, help you track your own body in the strange light.

**Covering.** Despite the heat, avoid exposed skin where possible. The mineral air can dry and irritate. The vegetation, while not aggressive, can brush against you as it turns toward heat sources—your body heat. Some travelers report a mild rash from contact with the pale plants. Long sleeves, long pants, a light scarf or collar to protect the neck.

**Footwear.** The streets are cobbled and uneven, buckled by thermal motion. The buildings have thresholds and steps worn smooth by long-ago feet. Sandals are possible but will expose you to mineral dust and the possibility of brushing against low-growing vegetation. Closed shoes with good soles are recommended. The stone can be slippery where mineral salts have crystallized.

## WHAT TO BRING: Tools and Supplies

### Light Sources

The amber twilight is navigable. Your eyes will adjust. But if you want to *see*—to examine the chisel marks on stone, to read any marks or symbols that might exist, to explore the darker corners of buildings—you will want additional light.

**A warm-spectrum light.** Harsh white light will feel violent in this space, disruptive to the gentle amber glow. Use a light with adjustable warmth, or bring a simple amber-tinted cover for your standard light. The vegetation responds to light changes; sudden bright light may cause the plants to move rapidly, which can be startling.

**Red light for night exploration.** If you stay overnight—and you should, if you can, because the Pocket City transforms in darkness—you will want a red light to navigate without disrupting your night vision or disturbing the vegetation's nocturnal cycles.

### Navigation and Wayfinding

**Do not underestimate this.** The Pocket City is small, yes, but the dim light, the uniform architecture, and the strange geometry of thermal distortion can disorient. More than one traveler has spent hours searching for the exit door they swore they could remember.

**Physical markers.** Bring something you can place to mark your path—colored ribbons, small cairn stones, chalk marks. The vegetation will not disturb these markers; it coexists with the built environment, remember. Do not rely solely on digital navigation; electronics can behave unpredictably in the thermal environment.

**A compass may not work reliably.** The mineral content of the stone and the geothermal magnetic fields can interfere. Test yours before you go, and have a backup method.

**Photograph the door from inside.** The moment you enter, before you do anything else, turn around and photograph the exit. The crack in the basalt, the seam that parts under palm-pressure—it looks different from inside. Several travelers have described difficulty recognizing it, difficulty believing that particular crack was the way out.

### Documentation

**Notebooks.** The dry heat preserves paper excellently. There is no humidity to warp pages, no mold to consume. Bring good paper and a pen that writes in heat—some gel pens become smudgy or stop flowing. Pencil is reliable.

**Photography considerations.** The amber light is beautiful but dim. Long exposures will capture the vegetation's faint glow and the subtle thermal shimmer in the air. The mineral particulates can create lens effects—some travelers mistake these for spirits or presences. They may be. The Pocket City exists at the boundary between physical and metaphysical, and photography sometimes captures things that eyes do not.

**Sample collection.** You may be tempted to take a piece of the vegetation. Consider carefully before you do. The plants are alive, responsive, and possibly aware in some diffuse way. They turn toward heat—they may turn toward you. What does it mean to take something that knows you are taking it?

If you collect, take only what has naturally fallen. The papery sheets that peel from walls. The mineral crusts. Do not pull living growth. The balance of this place is ancient and precise.

## MENTAL PREPARATION: The Inner Journey

### Expect Isolation

You will be the only animal presence. This sounds simple, but it is profound. No birds, no insects, no scuttling in corners. Your own breathing will be loud. Your own heartbeat noticeable. The silence of animals will press against you.

Some travelers find this peaceful. Others find it unsettling in a way they cannot articulate—there is a quality of being watched that arises not from being seen, but from being the only one who sees. The vegetation turns toward you. You are a heat source. You are, perhaps, an event.

**Prepare for the sound of your own body.** In the absence of animal sounds, your internal noises amplify. You will hear your own digestion. The click of your joints. The wet sounds of swallowing. Some travelers find this distressing; others report a kind of intimacy with their own physicality that they have never experienced elsewhere.

### Prepare for Non-Linear Time

The Pocket City has no true day or night. The light dims and brightens as the outside world moves through its cycle, filtered through the narrow chimneys, but there is no sun, no moon, no stars. Just the amber glow that strengthens and weakens on a schedule you cannot predict.

Your circadian rhythms will be disrupted. Without external cues, your sense of time may stretch or compress. Travelers report losing hours, finding themselves standing in the plaza without memory of walking there, suddenly aware that they have been still for longer than they intended.

**Bring a timepiece.** A simple watch or clock. Check it regularly. Use it to anchor yourself to your departure schedule. The Pocket City will not tell you when it is time to leave.

### The Waiting

The Pocket City is waiting. This is not metaphor. Something is being maintained, a process ongoing, a rhythm sustained. You may feel it—a sense of held breath, of anticipation, of something about to happen that never quite happens.

This waiting quality can affect travelers differently. Some feel an urge to *complete* something, to fulfill whatever expectation the place seems to hold. Others feel an urge to stay, to become part of the waiting, to let their own presence join the process.

Be clear with yourself before you enter: you are a visitor. You are an observer. You are not the thing being waited for, and you are not required to stay.

## UPON ARRIVAL: First Steps

### The Threshold Moment

When you press your palm to the seam and feel it part, pause. Let the heat greet you. Let the mineral air fill your lungs. Stand in the doorway for a full minute before entering fully.

**Turn around.** Memorize the view from inside. The shape of the crack, the way the light falls around it, any distinguishing marks. The door does not lock or disappear, but the interior geometry can make it hard to find later.

**Sit down.** Your first action in the Pocket City should be stillness. Lower yourself to the cobblestones near the entrance and simply *be*. Let your body begin to adjust to the temperature. Let your eyes adapt to the amber twilight. Listen to the sounds—thermal groans, vent hisses, the crackle of vegetation.

This threshold sitting is recommended by every traveler who has returned. The Pocket City requires a moment of acknowledgment. You are entering something that has waited a long time.

### First Movements

When you rise, move slowly. The heat will make you lightheaded if you rush. Walk the perimeter of the plaza first. Do not enter buildings yet. Let the space know you—let your heat signature move through it.

You may notice the vegetation turning. This is normal. The pale plants orient toward thermal vents and heat sources. You are a heat source. They are not attacking you. They are simply doing what they do.

If the turning disturbs you, stay in the open plaza where fewer plants cluster near you. But most travelers report that the vegetation's attention feels neutral—not welcoming, not hostile. Just *attentive*.

## EXPLORING THE CITY: Practical Guidance

### The Buildings

Enter slowly. The interiors are darker than the streets. Pause at thresholds to let your eyes adjust.

Do not touch the walls unnecessarily. The mineral deposits can be sharp, and some travelers report a mild tingling or numbness after prolonged contact with certain crystalline growths. The sensation passes, but it is unpleasant.

**The workshop:** Worth examining. The stone flakes on the floor are debris from tool-making. Some travelers have found partially finished implements—blades, scrapers. Do not take these. They are part of the archaeological record, part of the balance.

**The kitchen:** The fire-pit is cold, but interesting. The chimney connects to the upper world—looking up through it, you may see a distant circle of sky, impossibly far away. This is one of the few places where you can see evidence of the outside.

**The bath-house or sweat-lodge:** This is where the heat was used intentionally. Sit on the worn stone bench. Close your eyes. Feel the warmth rising through the floor. This is what the builders came for, perhaps. Not shelter from heat, but immersion in it.

### The Central Plaza

The raised platform is the heart of the city. Whatever happened here, whatever was done here, the structure remains. The circle of vegetation around its edge is perfect. Test it—walk the perimeter. The plants have maintained this shape for centuries. They do not encroach.

You may sit on the platform. Many travelers do. Some report visions—not hallucinations, exactly, but thoughts that feel larger than their own, images that come unbidden. Others feel only peace, the warmth rising through the stone, the amber light filtering down.

If you choose to stay overnight, the plaza is the recommended sleeping location. The stone retains heat through the darkness. The vegetation circle provides a kind of boundary. And the platform elevates you above the floor's thermal vents, which can become uncomfortably hot during certain cycles.

### The Vegetation

You may be tempted to interact with the plants. This is natural. They are strange and beautiful, and their slow turning makes them seem conscious.

**Observe first.** Watch how they respond to temperature changes. Notice that they do not consume, only coexist. They are part of what maintains this place, part of the balance.

If you touch them, do so gently. Travelers report various sensations—cool or slightly warm, smooth or faintly rough depending on the species. Some say the plants feel alive in a way that transcends ordinary vegetation, a faint vibration or pulse. This may be bioluminescence or thermal activity. It may be something else.

Do not harm them. Do not pull them from walls. Whatever you do to the vegetation, you do to the balance of the city. The Pocket City does not punish—but it remembers.

## WHAT TO EXPECT: Phenomena and Experiences

### The Sound Cycles

The Pocket City breathes. The thermal vents, the chimneys, the vegetation—everything produces sound, and these sounds organize into patterns. A wave of noise rises and falls every few hours. You may feel it in your molars before you hear it—a low hum that rises through you.

Some travelers find this rhythmic, hypnotic, even sleep-inducing. Others find it disturbing. If you are sensitive to sound, bring earplugs. But consider: the sound is part of the city's process. Blocking it out may diminish your experience.

### The Light Variations

The amber glow strengthens and dims on no schedule you can predict. The outside world's day-night cycle filters through chimneys, but also clouds, storms, seasons unknown to you inside. You may experience sudden brightening or dimming that has nothing to do with time of day.

During darker periods, the vegetation's bioluminescence becomes visible—a faint glow, like foxfire, like dying stars. This is the time when travelers report the most profound experiences: visions, insights, a sense of communication from something vast and patient.

Do not fear the darkness. The Pocket City is safer in darkness than most places are in light.

### The Sense of Presence

You will feel watched. Not by anything malevolent—by the city itself. The Pocket City is *aware* in some fashion, conscious on a timescale too slow for human minds to fully comprehend. Your presence is noted. Your heat is catalogued. Your movements are incorporated into whatever process is being maintained.

This feeling of presence can be comforting or unsettling, depending on your temperament. Some travelers feel embraced, as if the city is holding them. Others feel assessed, as if being measured against some ancient standard.

Neither response is wrong. The Pocket City holds no judgment. It simply *is*.

## STAYING OVERNIGHT: If You Choose to Remain

The Pocket City transforms after dark—or after whatever passes for dark in this amber twilight. The sounds change. The vegetation glows. The thermal vents pulse more strongly.

If you stay, you do so in the plaza, on or near the raised platform. You will not sleep normally. The heat, the sounds, the strangeness of the environment prevent ordinary rest. But you may enter a different state—a hypnagogic awareness, half-dreaming, half-awake.

In this state, travelers report:

- Hearing voices, indistinct, speaking a language they almost recognize
- Seeing figures at the edge of vision, standing in doorways, never approaching
- Understanding the purpose of the city, an insight that fades upon waking
- Feeling themselves becoming part of the waiting, the process, the maintenance
- A profound peace that persists for days or weeks after leaving

If you are afraid, leave before dark. If you are curious, stay. There is no wrong choice. But choose consciously.

## DEPARTURE: The Art of Leaving

### When to Leave

Set a time limit before you enter, and honor it. The Pocket City makes it easy to forget time, to let hours slide into days, days into longer. The door remains open, but the will to leave can diminish.

If you find yourself thinking "I could stay forever," it is time to leave. Immediately.

### The Exit

Return to the door you memorized. The crack in the basalt. The seam that parts under palm-pressure.

It will look different than you remember. The interior light changes how the exit appears. Trust your memory. Trust your markers. Press your palm to what seems like solid stone and feel it part.

### The Transition

Leaving the Pocket City can be disorienting. The outside world will feel too bright, too cold, too fast. Give yourself time to readjust. Sit outside the entrance for a while. Let your body temperature normalize. Let your eyes adapt to ordinary light.

Some travelers report a period of melancholy after leaving—a sense of having lost something, or having left something unfinished. This passes. But honor it. The Pocket City is a profound experience. It leaves marks.

---

## FINAL WARNINGS: What to Carry With You

The Pocket City of Warm Stone is not dangerous in the conventional sense. It will not harm you deliberately. But it is old, and patient, and *other*. It operates by rules that human minds struggle to comprehend.

**Do not take what is not freely given.** The balance is precise.

**Do not stay past your time.** The waiting can become permanent.

**Do not mistake the vegetation's attention for friendship.** They are simply responding to heat. You are heat. When you cool, they will turn away.

**Do not believe everything you think in the plaza.** The insights are valuable, but they may not be yours.

Above all: **the Pocket City does not need you.** It was here before you, it will be here after you. It welcomes you, observes you, incorporates you into its process—and then releases you.

Accept that gift of release. Leave while you still can.
