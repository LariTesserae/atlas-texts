# Preparation Guide: The Drowned Choir of Hálthörn

## Before You Arrive

### Mental Preparation

You must understand what this place will try to do to you before you enter it. Read the location description again. Note the final lines. The Drowned Choir is not merely a location—it is an invitation that does not wait for your RSVP. The water *wants* you there. The settlement *offers belonging*. The wrongness is not incidental; it is the point.

I tell you this not to frighten you away but to ensure you arrive already armored against the seduction. Many multiverse travelers are prepared for hostility. Far fewer are prepared for welcome. This place will feel like coming home before you have earned that feeling, and the earning matters.

**Exercise:** In the days before your visit, spend time in water. Not bathing—immersion. Lakes, rivers, the ocean if you can reach it. Learn how your body feels when it is surrounded. Learn how your mind responds to having your movement slowed, your weight distributed differently. Learn the difference between floating and swimming. The Drowned Choir will not let you swim. It will only let you float.

**Exercise:** Practice stillness. Find a position you can hold for an hour without significant movement. The settlement operates on scales that make human patience feel insufficient. Your first hour underwater will feel like ten. Your tenth hour will feel like one. Learn to exist in durations that do not match clocks.

**Exercise:** Spend time in places where sound is constant but not hostile—near waterfalls, in heavy rain, beside industrial machinery. The Drowned Choir's sound-field will be overwhelming at first. Learn to find texture in noise rather than fighting for silence.

### Psychological Fortification

You will need anchors. The Drowned Choir induces a particular kind of dissociation—pleasant, warm, seductive. You may begin to feel that your life before this moment was frantic, unnecessary, a kind of illness that the water will cure. This feeling is not truth. It is the settlement's metabolism acting upon yours.

**Prepare these anchors before arrival:**

1. **A physical token** from your home world, something that cannot exist in this place. A manufactured object with no analogue in the Elder Stalks' architecture. Something you can hold. The wrongness of this object against the settlement's aesthetics will help you remember that you are visiting, not belonging.

2. **A name** that you say aloud to yourself at regular intervals. Your own name, or another that carries weight. The sound-field incorporates your voice. Let it incorporate this anchor too. Let your name become part of the song, but know that it is yours.

3. **A promise** made to someone who is not with you. A reason to return that exists outside this place. The settlement will make returning feel unnecessary. Your promise is the proof that it is.

4. **A time limit** set before entry and adhered to regardless of how you feel when the limit approaches. The water does not measure time in hours. You must bring your own measurement.

### Physical Conditioning

The water sustains breath—but this does not mean it sustains breath *comfortably*. The violation of what bodies should do will register as wrongness even as your lungs perform their new function. Expect the first several minutes to include:

- A sensation of drowning that passes
- A reflex to gasp that you must not indulge
- A moment of certainty that you are dying
- Then: breath

Your body will adapt faster than your mind. The water has been waiting for you; it knows how to receive you. But the knowing is not yours, and the receiving will feel like invasion before it feels like welcome.

**Prepare by practicing breath work.** Not the controlled breathing of meditation—rather, the conscious observation of breath. Know what your lungs feel like when they work correctly. This knowledge will help you recognize when they are working incorrectly but successfully.

The Drowned Choir requires climbing, floating, and patient movement. Your fitness for these activities matters less than your comfort with them. Strength helps; calm helps more.

## What to Bring

### Essential Equipment

**Nothing electronic.** The water's properties—its conductivity, its interaction with light and sound—suggest a composition incompatible with surface-world technology. Devices will fail, or worse, they will function wrongly. Do not bring anything you are not prepared to lose or to have transformed.

**A simple timekeeping device.** Mechanical, water-resistant, analog. The settlement will attempt to stretch your perception of duration. A watch with a sweeping second hand gives you evidence of time's passage that exists outside your own experience. Check it often. Record the times when you check it. The act of recording matters more than the record itself.

**Writing materials.** Waterproof paper exists in many worlds; acquire some. A writing instrument that functions underwater—simple ink systems work, wax tablets work better, engraving tools work best. You will need to externalize your thoughts. The settlement encourages internalization, thoughts dissolving into the sound-field. Writing is resistance.

**A knife.** Not for defense—the larger creatures in the depths have no apparent interest in harming you, and the amphibious harvesters move with choreographed indifference to visitors. Bring a knife because the built elements of the settlement follow principles of cooperation rather than imposition, and a blade is the simplest tool for asking rather than demanding. Use it to cut fiber, to shape reeds, to participate in the settlement's primitive construction if you choose to stay long enough to learn how.

You will notice I am not recommending weapons. This is intentional. Violence in a place that sustains you is a category error. The water *wants* you there. The plants *breathe* with you. Predators in the depths do not approach. The only threat in the Drowned Choir is your own desire to remain, and no weapon addresses that.

### Clothing Considerations

You will be underwater. Clothing as surface-dwellers understand it serves little purpose.

**However:** The Elder Stalks and their platforms have texture. Some travelers report mild abrasion from prolonged contact with the platform surfaces. A simple wrap of smooth fabric around the torso and upper legs can prevent this irritation without interfering with the water's embrace.

**Color matters here in ways it does not elsewhere.** The water filters light, shifting the visible spectrum toward greens and blues. Red and orange disappear. If you bring clothing, choose colors that will remain visible—blues, greens, violets. The bioluminescence of the platforms interacts with certain dyes in unexpected ways. Plain undyed fabric is safest.

**Do not bring footwear.** The platforms are living tissue, slightly convex, smooth. You will need to feel them directly to climb the stalks safely. The root-curtains trail through water; walking among them requires the sensitivity of bare feet to avoid entanglement.

### Sustenance

The water sustains breath but not body. You will still require food.

The silver fish are abundant, translucent, visible as dark shadows with rainbow-scattering scales. They eat algae from the platforms; they sleep in the root-curtains. They are a food source if you can catch them, but consider: they are also part of the settlement's metabolism. They keep the platforms clean. They are the visible life in a place that otherwise feels emptily architectural. Eating them is not forbidden, but it is not nothing.

The amphibious harvesters collect residue from the Elder Stalks' joints. This substance is clearly edible by local standards. Whether it is edible by yours is unknown. Observational evidence suggests it provides nutrition. It may also provide other effects—the harvesters' choreographed movements and large golden eyes could be natural to their species or could be induced by their diet. Caution is warranted.

**Bring your own food if you intend to stay more than a few hours.** Dried provisions, dense and compact. The water's cool constant temperature will preserve most foods indefinitely. Eat on the platforms; the root-curtains below will catch any debris. Understand that bringing outside food into this ecosystem is an imposition. Minimize what you bring. Leave no trace.

**Water is not necessary.** The settlement's water is drinkable—more than drinkable, it seems to have properties that border on therapeutic. But note: drinking the water is another form of acceptance, another way the settlement incorporates you. Some travelers report that the water tastes like wanting. Hydrate from your own supplies if you wish to maintain strict boundaries. Accept the local water if you wish to let those boundaries soften.

## Upon Arrival

### The Entry

You will not know you have entered until you are already within. The description warns of this: *The water holds you before you know you've entered it.* This is not merely physical. The transition from air-breathing to water-breathing happens before consciousness registers the change.

**Do not struggle.** The reflex will be to fight, to claw toward a surface that may not exist in the way you expect. The settlement's lake has a surface somewhere above, but it is not the surface your body will seek. Your body will seek the idea of surface, the memory of air. The water offers a different arrangement.

**Exhale first.** When you feel the water surrounding you, before you try to breathe, exhale completely. Let the water know that you are empty, ready to receive what it offers. Then inhale slowly. The sensation will be wrong—the water entering where air should, the lungs accepting what they should reject—but it will work. Your body knows how to do this now. It has been waiting to learn.

**The first sounds will overwhelm you.** The constant murmur, the tonal wash, the low thrumming of the Elder Stalks and the high piping of the root-curtains. Your arrival will send ripples through the sound-field; the settlement will incorporate the disturbance. You may hear yourself arriving before you feel yourself arrive.

### Initial Orientation

**Find a platform.** Not the largest—they are too exposed, too central—but one of the smaller surfaces toward the outer rings. Climb onto it. Feel its living texture beneath you. Let the platform breathe beneath your weight. It will adjust to accommodate you.

**Do not attempt to map the settlement immediately.** The arrangement of Elder Stalks—the concentric rings around the central emptiness—will reveal itself over time. Trying to understand the architecture all at once will exhaust you. The settlement operates on slow scales; learn it slowly.

**Locate the central plaza.** The emptiness where nothing grows, where depths fall away into darkness. You will not approach it immediately, but you should know where it is. It is the settlement's center of gravity, the point around which everything else arranges itself. Some travelers report that staring into the central depths produces a particular calm. Others report that it produces a particular dread. Both responses are correct.

**Identify the amphibious harvesters if they are present.** Watch their choreographed movements from a distance. Do not approach them; do not flee from them. They are not hostile, but they are also not oriented toward you. You exist in their peripheral awareness. Let them continue their work. Observation will teach you more than interaction.

## During Your Stay

### Movement and Navigation

The water resists rapid motion. This is not a bug; it is a feature. The settlement punishes haste—platforms are spaced for measured crossing, stalks require patient climbing, currents reward surrender rather than struggle.

**Learn to float between platforms.** Not swimming—floating. The water will carry you if you let it. Determine which direction you wish to move, orient your body toward it, and then relax. The currents are slow but deliberate; they will deliver you. Fighting them expends energy and accomplishes nothing.

**The stalks are climbable.** Their jointed structure provides natural handholds, and the sharpened stones wedged into cracks by previous inhabitants offer additional grip. Climb slowly. The stalks are alive; they may respond to your weight with subtle shifts. These shifts are not attempts to dislodge you—they are adjustments, accommodations. The Elder Stalks are learning to bear you.

**Do not descend beyond the lowest platforms without preparation.** The larger shapes in the depths do not approach the settlement, but the settlement ends where the platforms end. Beyond lies open water, ancient and deep, and the light does not reach. If you must explore the depths, do so with a tether—something you can follow back to the living architecture.

### Interaction with the Sound-Field

The settlement is an instrument that plays itself and everything within it. Your movements create sound; your voice creates sound; your presence creates disturbance that becomes part of the composition.

**You cannot be silent here.** Attempted silence will register as disruption—a wrong note, a gap in the melody. The water is never silent. Better to move with intention, to make sounds that harmonize rather than fight.

**Humming helps.** Find the tonal center of the sound-field—it shifts but never disappears—and hum softly against it. Your voice will join the choir. The effect is not unpleasant. It feels like membership.

**If you speak words, speak them slowly.** The water conducts and amplifies; rapid speech becomes noise. The settlement seems to prefer clear tones over confused ones. Say what you mean. Mean what you say. The Elder Stalks may be listening.

### Encounters with Local Life

**The silver fish:** They move through the water like weather, schools synchronized in patterns that suggest dance rather than swimming. They will not harm you. They will not approach you intentionally. If you float still among them, they may incorporate you into their movement—swimming around you, including you in their choreography. This is not communication but it is not nothing either.

**The amphibious harvesters:** Four-limbed, soft-skinned, golden-eyed. They climb the stalks during dark cycles and harvest residue from the joints. They do not communicate through sound; their language is gesture. If you encounter them, do not mimic their movements—that may be a form of intrusion—but do watch. Their choreography carries meaning you may begin to understand over time.

Some travelers report that extended observation of the harvesters reveals patterns—sequences of gesture that repeat, themes and variations. The language is learnable, perhaps. It is also perhaps not meant for you.

**The larger shapes in the depths:** Predators or custodians or something without surface-world analogue. They do not approach the platforms. They register as pressure changes and sound-field disruptions. If you descend toward them, you may feel their attention focus. This focus is not hostile. It is not welcoming either. It is observation. They are aware of you. You are not their concern unless you make yourself their concern.

**Do not make yourself their concern.**

### The Psychological Terrain

You will feel, at some point, that you could stay.

The feeling will arrive without warning. You will be floating between platforms, or resting on a platform's surface watching the light shift, or listening to the Elder Stalks thrum their low song—and suddenly the settlement will feel like home. Not a home you remember but a home you have always been seeking. The water holds you. The architecture grew for you. The choir incorporates your breath.

**This is the danger.** Not a danger of death but a danger of transformation so complete that return becomes unthinkable.

The feeling is not false. You *could* stay. The settlement would accommodate you. Platforms would grow beneath your feet, root-curtains would cradle your sleep, the silver fish would eat the algae from your skin. You would become part of the ecosystem—another resident, another note in the song.

The question is not whether you could stay. The question is whether you choose to.

**This is why you brought anchors.** This is why you set a time limit. This is why you prepared for welcome rather than for hostility. The drowning that drowns nothing is still a drowning—of the self you were before you entered, of the life you lived elsewhere, of the category "visitor" itself.

If you choose to stay, stay knowingly. If you choose to leave, leave before the choice dissolves.

## Departure

### Recognizing When to Leave

You should leave before you no longer want to.

Watch yourself for these signs:
- You have stopped checking your timekeeping device
- Your anchor-token feels foreign, wrong, like an intrusion
- The name you prepared sounds unfamiliar when spoken
- You have begun to move in choreography rather than intention
- The surface has become an abstraction, a concept rather than a place you will return to

These signs mean the settlement is incorporating you. They mean you are becoming resident rather than visitor. This is not failure—it is simply what this place does—but if you did not intend to stay, these signs mean you have stayed too long already.

### The Act of Leaving

Departure is harder than arrival. The water wanted you to enter; it may not want you to leave.

**Swim upward.** Not float—swim. For the first time, fight the currents rather than surrendering to them. Move toward the surface that you may or may not be able to see. The water will resist, not violently but persistently, like a friend who does not understand why you are leaving a party that has not ended.

**Breathe water until you cannot.** At some point in your ascent, the water's properties will change. The sustenance it provided will cease. Your lungs will begin to reject what they accepted. This transition is dangerous—you may panic, you may inhale water at the moment it becomes deadly, you may drown in the very substance that preserved you.

**Time your final breath carefully.** Exhale the water before you reach the surface. Prepare your lungs for air. When you break through—if there is a surface to break through—inhale immediately. The first breath of air will feel wrong after your time below. Your body will have adapted. It will need to adapt again.

**Do not look back.** The settlement visible in the water beneath you, light shifting through its architecture, the Elder Stalks rising in their deliberate pattern—it will call to you. The sound-field will incorporate your departure into its song. A note of leaving. A note of loss. The choir will sing you out as it sang you in.

### After Departure

**Document immediately.** Write everything you remember while you remember it. The Drowned Choir induces a particular amnesia—the details soften, the wrongness fades, what felt like transformation becomes dreamlike and distant. Recording preserves the experience against this dissolution.

**Expect grief.** You may feel, in the hours and days after leaving, that you have lost something irreplaceable. This feeling is accurate. You have lost the belonging the settlement offered. The life you return to will feel frantic, breathless, insufficient—the settlement told you as much. The feeling will fade, but it will not disappear. You have been somewhere that wanted you. Not everywhere will.

**Monitor your health.** The water's properties are unknown. Its effects may persist. Watch for changes in your breathing, your perception of time, your response to sound. The settlement may have left traces in you—small transformations, notes of the song that continue to hum beneath your skin.

---

## Final Notes

The Drowned Choir of Hálthörn is not hostile. It is not safe either. It is a place that operates by different rules, and the most important rule is this: the water has been waiting, and it does not wait idly.

Ordinary multiverse travelers prepare for danger. They bring weapons, they learn defensive protocols, they memorize exit routes. These preparations are valuable but insufficient here. The danger of the Drowned Choir is that there is no danger—only an invitation so complete that accepting it feels like recognition, like coming home, like the end of searching.

If you go, go prepared to want to stay. If you stay, stay knowing what you have chosen. If you leave, leave before the leaving becomes impossible.

The drowned choir continues its singing either way.
