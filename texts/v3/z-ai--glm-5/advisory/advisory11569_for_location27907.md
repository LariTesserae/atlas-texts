# Preparing for the Birthing Pocket

## A Practical Guide for the Ordinary Human Traveler

---

## I. Understanding What You Are Walking Into

Before you pack anything, before you plan anything, you need to understand something fundamental about the Birthing Pocket that no amount of equipment will compensate for: **you are not entering a place. You are entering a negotiation.**

The Birthing Pocket is not a destination in the way that other locations are destinations. It is a space still arguing with itself about whether it deserves to exist. Every surface, every creature, every droplet of moisture is engaged in a continual process of becoming and unbecoming. When you step inside, you become part of that argument. Your presence will influence what the pocket becomes, and the pocket will influence what *you* become. This is not a warning to be taken lightly and then dismissed. This is the single most important fact about your visit.

Most locations in the multiverse, even the strange and hostile ones, have agreed upon their own existence. They have settled. They have committed. A volcano is a volcano. A void is a void. Even a dimension made entirely of screaming color has at least decided *that* it is a dimension made of screaming color. The Birthing Pocket has decided nothing. It is still in the process of deciding, and that process will not pause for your comfort.

Understand this: you are visiting a womb. Not metaphorically. Not as a poetic framing. You are entering a space where reality itself is gestating, and you are stepping into that gestation as a foreign object. The pocket will recognize you the way a body recognizes an implant. It may accept you. It may reject you. It may simply wait to see what you do before it makes its decision.

Prepare accordingly.

## II. Mental and Psychological Preparation

### A. Abandon Your Expectations of Coherence

Your mind has been trained, through a lifetime of living in stable realities, to expect certain things. You expect walls to be solid. You expect light to have a source. You expect time to move forward at a consistent rate. You expect that if you set something down, it will still be there when you return. The Birthing Pocket will violate every one of these expectations, and it will do so casually, without malice, simply because it has not yet learned to uphold them.

You must train yourself, before you arrive, to tolerate ambiguity. Not just tolerate it—*accept* it as the natural state of things. Practice sitting in uncertainty without reaching for resolution. Practice observing without categorizing. When you see a creature that seems to have too many legs or not enough legs, do not count. Counting is a form of forcing order onto something that has not yet consented to be ordered. The pocket will resist your counting, and the experience of watching your own categories fail in real time can be deeply unsettling if you are not prepared.

**Exercise:** Before your visit, spend time in places that are almost but not quite familiar. Visit a room in your home in the dark. Walk through your neighborhood backwards. Listen to music from a genre you do not understand. Practice the feeling of being in a space that you cannot fully parse, and learn to exist in that feeling without panic.

### B. Prepare for Identity Pressure

The Birthing Pocket does not permit passive observation. Everything within it participates in its formation. This means *you* will participate in its formation, whether you choose to or not. The pocket will press against your sense of self, testing your edges, probing your definition. It is not hostile. It is curious, in the way that a forming thing is curious about everything within its bounds.

You will feel yourself becoming vague at the edges. Your thoughts may lose their sharpness. Your memories may become less certain, as if the pocket is unsure whether your past should be part of its present. You may feel your body becoming less defined—not dissolving, but becoming negotiable, as if the boundary between you and not-you is softening.

This is the pocket trying to figure out what you are. It is the same process happening to everything else in the space. The almost-plants are almost existing. The not-insects are almost being insects. You will almost be yourself, and the degree of "almost" will increase the longer you stay.

**Preparation:** Before entering, spend time establishing a strong, clear sense of who you are. Not in a superficial, affirmations-and-journaling way, but in a deep, structural way. Know your name. Know your history. Know what you love and what you fear and what you would fight to protect. The pocket will test all of these. It is not malicious—it simply has no concept of the sanctity of individual identity, because identity is a finished thing, and nothing in the Birthing Pocket is finished.

Some travelers recommend writing a letter to yourself before entering. Describe yourself in detail: your name, your appearance, your memories, your relationships, your commitments. Seal it and carry it with you. If you begin to feel uncertain about who you are, read it. The written word is more resistant to the pocket's influence than thought, because writing has already committed to being what it is. The ink is dry. The meaning is fixed. The pocket can make you doubt yourself, but it cannot easily make you doubt a piece of paper that says your name in your own handwriting.

### C. Prepare for the Possibility of Transformation

This is the hardest thing to prepare for, and the most important.

If you stay too long, you will begin to change. Not metaphorically. You will begin to merge with the pocket's forming reality. You might feel your skin becoming damp in a way that suggests it is no longer fully skin. You might feel your thoughts becoming slower, more mineral, more like the process of stone deciding what it wants to be. You might feel your body becoming translucent, or you might feel a pull toward the walls, a desire to become part of them.

This is not death. It is transformation. The pocket does not kill. It *incorporates*. But the distinction between those two things, from the perspective of the person being incorporated, may be academic.

You must decide, before you enter, what your threshold is. How much change are you willing to accept? At what point do you turn back? The pocket will not give you a clear signal. There will be no announcement, no moment where a voice says "you have been here too long." You must monitor yourself, and you must be honest with yourself about what you are experiencing.

**The rule of thumb:** If you can no longer remember why you came, you have stayed too long. If you no longer feel the urge to leave, you have stayed too long. If the pocket's light begins to feel like it is coming from inside you rather than from nowhere, you have stayed too long.

Leave while you still want to leave.

### D. Prepare for the Silence

The Birthing Pocket is quiet in a way that most humans have never experienced. The sounds that exist are minimal, peripheral, at the edge of hearing. There is no wind, no traffic, no distant hum of civilization. There is only the drip of moisture, the whisper of creatures, the occasional groan of forming stone.

For most people, this level of silence is uncomfortable. The human brain, accustomed to constant auditory stimulation, will begin to generate its own sounds. You may hear voices. You may hear music. You may hear your own heartbeat, amplified to a disturbing degree. These are hallucinations, but in a place where reality is negotiable, the distinction between hallucination and perception becomes itself negotiable.

**Preparation:** Practice silence before your visit. Spend hours in a quiet room without earbuds, without music, without conversation. Learn what your mind does when it is deprived of input. Learn to recognize your own mental noise for what it is, so that when you hear it in the Birthing Pocket, you can identify it as yours rather than the pocket's.

## III. Physical Preparation

### A. Clothing

The Birthing Pocket is cool but not cold—described as "a chill that touches the skin without penetrating it." However, the dampness is pervasive, and the moisture in the air will settle on you without permission. You will become wet, not soaked, but persistently damp, in a way that will eventually seep through inadequate clothing.

**What to wear:**

- **Base layer:** A moisture-wicking synthetic fabric against your skin. Do not wear cotton, which will absorb and hold the dampness. Do not wear wool, which will become heavy and begin to smell of wet animal. A synthetic base layer will move moisture away from your skin and help maintain your body's sense of its own boundary.

- **Middle layer:** A light insulating layer. Fleece or synthetic down. You do not need heavy insulation—the pocket is not cold enough for hypothermia—but you need enough warmth to remind your body that it is a distinct thermal system, separate from the pocket's ambient temperature. This is important for maintaining your sense of physical self.

- **Outer layer:** A lightweight, breathable water-resistant shell. Not waterproof—full waterproofing will trap the pocket's moisture inside your clothing, creating a humid micro-environment that will accelerate the feeling of merging with the space. You want a layer that resists the dampness but allows some breathability, that maintains the distinction between inside and outside without sealing you off entirely.

- **Footwear:** Soft-soled boots with good grip. The floor of the pocket is damp and its texture is uncertain—surfaces shift between smooth and rough. You need footwear that can adapt to changing terrain without demanding that the terrain adapt to it. Heavy boots will feel too rigid. Barefoot is not an option—the pocket's moisture will be too invasive against bare skin, and the creatures that move across the floor may mistake your bare feet for something else.

- **Gloves:** Thin, flexible, synthetic. The almost-plants will brush against your hands. The moisture will bead on your fingers. You need to maintain tactile sensation while keeping a barrier between your skin and the pocket's ambient dampness. The gloves are not for protection in the conventional sense—they are for definition. They remind your hands that they are your hands.

- **Head covering:** A simple cap or hood. The light in the pocket comes from everywhere, including from above, and while it is not described as harsh, the constant shadowless illumination can cause eye strain and disorientation over time. A brim or hood can create a small zone of relative shade near your eyes, giving your visual system a reference point.

Do not wear anything that you are emotionally attached to. The pocket may change your possessions in ways you cannot predict. Clothing brought into the pocket may come out slightly different—slightly softer, slightly damper, slightly less certain of its original shape. Treat everything you bring as expendable.

### B. Food and Water

The pocket's moisture is not water as you know it. It is pre-water, water that hasn't organized itself into proper form. Do not drink it. Do not let it enter your body if you can avoid it. The pocket is already trying to incorporate you; drinking its moisture would accelerate the process in ways that are difficult to predict.

**Bring your own water.** Bring more than you think you need. The pocket's dampness will make you feel as though you are hydrated, but this is deceptive. The moisture in the air may settle on your skin and be absorbed through it, but this does not replace actual hydration. You need water from outside the pocket—water that is fully committed to being water, water that knows what it is—to maintain your body's internal coherence.

Bring water in a sealed container. Glass is preferable to plastic, because glass is more certain of its own nature. The pocket's influence may make plastic containers slightly more permeable than they should be. A well-sealed glass bottle or metal canteen will better maintain the boundary between your water and the pocket's moisture.

**Food should be dry and simple.** The pocket is not a place for elaborate meals. Bring energy bars, dried fruit, hardtack—food that is far from the pocket's state of becoming. Food that is fully, unambiguously itself. Avoid anything moist, anything that could be confused with the pocket's ambient dampness. Avoid anything that might spoil, because the pocket's strange temporality may accelerate or decelerate organic decay in unpredictable ways.

**Do not eat anything that the pocket offers you.** The creatures leave behind small, hard pellets that "might be waste or might be seeds or might be beginning of something the pocket hasn't invented yet." Do not touch these. Do not taste them. Do not bring them to your mouth. The pocket does not offer gifts in the way that a host offers gifts. It experiments. You do not want to be part of its experiments.

Eat before you enter. Eat as little as possible while inside. Eat again immediately after leaving. Minimize the time your digestive system spends processing food within the pocket's influence, because your body's internal processes are not immune to the pocket's negotiable reality.

### C. Light Sources

The Birthing Pocket is already bright—pervasively, shadowlessly bright. You do not need a flashlight to see. However, you should bring one anyway.

Bring a small, warm-toned flashlight. Not a headlamp—headlamps are too close to your eyes and can contribute to disorientation when combined with the pocket's sourceless light. A handheld flashlight that you can direct, that you can turn on and off, that produces light with a *source* and a *direction* and *shadows.*

Why? Because the pocket's light comes from nowhere. It is everywhere and nowhere, and it produces no shadows. This is disorienting over time. The human visual system relies on shadow for depth perception, for spatial reasoning, for the sense of being a distinct object in a defined space. The pocket's light strips all of this away. After extended exposure, you may begin to feel that you are floating in light rather than standing on a surface, that you are light rather than a body.

Your flashlight, when turned on, will produce a beam with a source and a direction. It will cast shadows. It will remind your visual system how geometry works. It will anchor you to the physics you know, the physics where light comes from things and falls on things and creates darkness behind things.

Use the flashlight sparingly. Turn it on when you feel your spatial sense beginning to dissolve. Turn it on when you need to verify that a surface is solid before you touch it. Turn it on when you need to see your own shadow, to remind yourself that you are a body with edges, a thing that light can fall on and be blocked by.

The pocket's light may resist your flashlight's light. They may compete, or they may merge, or your flashlight's beam may behave strangely in the pocket's luminous atmosphere. Be prepared for this. Even a flashlight that produces unexpected results is better than no flashlight at all, because the act of turning it on is an assertion: *light comes from things. Light has a source. I am holding the source.*

### D. Timekeeping

Time in the Birthing Pocket is uneven. Hours may pass in the space of a breath, or a single breath may stretch across hours. You cannot trust your subjective experience of time. You need an external reference.

**Bring a mechanical watch.** Not a digital watch—digital watches depend on electronic circuits that may behave unpredictably in the pocket's negotiable reality. A mechanical watch is a physical system: springs, gears, escapements. It measures time through mechanical processes that are deeply embedded in the physics of matter. The pocket may still affect it, but the effect will be more visible—a watch that suddenly runs fast or slow is a clear signal that something is wrong with local time, whereas a digital watch might simply display a number that seems reasonable but is not.

Wind the watch fully before entering. Do not rely on a battery. Do not rely on your phone. Do not rely on any timekeeping device that depends on external signals, because the pocket's permeable boundaries may not block those signals in the expected way, and you might receive time from elsewhere, from elsewhen, from a timeline that is not your own.

Before entering, synchronize your watch with a time source outside the pocket. Note the exact time. Decide how long you intend to stay—no more than a few hours, certainly no more than a full day—and note the time at which you must leave. Check your watch regularly. If the watch stops, or runs backward, or displays impossible times, leave immediately. This is not a suggestion. This is a rule.

**Bring a second timekeeping method as backup.** A simple hourglass, for instance. Something that measures time through the fall of sand under gravity. Gravity in the pocket may behave strangely, but an hourglass is a physical process that is difficult for the pocket to influence without also influencing everything else. If your watch and your hourglass disagree, trust neither, and begin the process of leaving.

### E. Navigation and Anchoring

The Birthing Pocket is described as small—perhaps thirty feet across at its widest point. You should be able to see from one end to the other. But the pocket's boundaries are permeable, its walls are not fully committed to being walls, and its space may not be as simple as its dimensions suggest. The pocket might be larger inside than it appears. It might contain folds or pockets within itself. The walls might shift when you are not looking.

**Bring cord.** A hundred feet of bright, high-visibility cord. When you enter the pocket, tie one end of the cord to something outside—something that is fully real, fully formed, fully committed to existing. A rock. A root. A piece of solid earth. Then carry the other end with you as you explore.

This is not a lifeline in the physical sense. It is a lifeline in the ontological sense. The cord connects you to reality that has already been decided. If you become disoriented—not just spatially, but ontologically, if you begin to lose your sense of what is real—the cord is a physical reminder that reality exists, that it is continuous, that it leads somewhere definite.

The cord may behave strangely in the pocket. It may become longer or shorter than it should be. It may appear to pass through walls. It may become translucent or damp or uncertain of its own texture. But as long as one end is tied to something real outside, the cord will maintain a connection to decided reality, and that connection may be the thing that allows you to find your way back.

**Bring chalk or a marking tool.** Mark the walls as you pass them. The marks may fade, may be absorbed, may be consumed by the creatures that eat possibility. But the act of marking is itself an assertion of presence, a declaration that you were here and that here is a place. If the marks persist, they will help you navigate. If they do not persist, their disappearance is information: it tells you that the pocket is actively erasing your presence, and you should consider leaving.

Do not rely on memory for navigation. The pocket's influence on identity and temporality may compromise your memory. You might remember entering from a direction that no longer exists. You might forget which way you came from. Your cord and your marks are your real navigation system. Your memory is a secondary system at best.

### F. First Aid and Emergency Supplies

The Birthing Pocket is not described as physically dangerous in the conventional sense. The creatures do not seem to be immediately hostile. The terrain is not treacherous in the way that a mountain or a chasm is treacherous. But the pocket's influence on your body is real, and you should prepare for physical effects.

**Bring:**

- **Bandages and wound dressings.** Not because you expect to be injured, but because the act of dressing a wound is an assertion of the body's importance. If you scratch yourself on the forming stone and the scratch does not heal as expected, a bandage provides a physical boundary, a layer of decided material between your body and the pocket's influence.

- **A thermal blanket.** If your body temperature begins to drop due to the pocket's cool, damp atmosphere, a thermal blanket can help maintain your core temperature. But more importantly, the act of wrapping yourself in a blanket is an act of self-care, an assertion that your body matters, that its comfort is important. In a place where everything is becoming and nothing is settled, the simple act of caring for your body is an anchor.

- **A mirror.** Small, unbreakable. Check your reflection periodically. The pocket's light may make your reflection look strange—paler, less defined, more like the almost-plants than like a person—but being able to see your own face is an important tool for maintaining identity. If you look in the mirror and do not recognize yourself, leave. If you look in the mirror and see something behind you that is not there, leave. If you look in the mirror and the reflection moves independently of you, *leave immediately.*

- **Salt.** A small quantity of salt, carried in a sealed container. Salt is one of the most decided substances in the human experience. It is mineral. It is crystalline. It is unambiguous. If you feel the pocket's influence becoming too strong—if you feel your edges softening, your definition blurring—place a small amount of salt on your tongue. The sharp, undeniable taste of salt is a assertion of physical reality that the pocket will have difficulty negotiating with. Salt is not magic. It is simply so certain of what it is that the pocket's uncertainty cannot easily affect it.

## IV. Behavioral Protocols While Inside

### A. How to Move

Move slowly. Not cautiously—slowly. The pocket is still forming, and sudden movements may disturb formations that are in the process of becoming. You do not want to be the thing that interrupts a wall's process of deciding to be a wall. You do not want to be the catalyst that causes a section of floor to reconsider its commitment to solidity.

Walk with flat feet, feeling the surface before committing your weight. The floor may shift under you. It may become softer or harder as you stand on it. This is not necessarily dangerous—the pocket is not trying to swallow you—but it is disorienting. If the floor feels unreliable, sit down. Reduce your point of contact with the pocket to the largest, most stable area possible. Wait. The floor will settle. It always settles, eventually. It just hasn't learned to settle quickly.

Do not run. Do not jump. Do not make sudden gestures. The pocket's creatures are accustomed to a specific range of movement—the slow, deliberate motion of things that exist in a state of becoming. Sudden movements may startle them, and startled things in a negotiable reality may react in ways that are not predictable.

### B. How to Observe

Look without staring. The pocket's light makes everything visible, but prolonged staring at any one thing may cause it to change. The pocket is self-conscious in a way—it responds to observation. The almost-plants become more or less substantial depending on whether they are being watched. The creatures may alter their behavior when observed directly.

Use peripheral vision. Let your eyes drift across surfaces without fixing on any one point. This allows you to perceive the pocket's general state without influencing specific formations. If you need to examine something closely, blink frequently. Each blink is a reset, a moment of non-observation that allows the thing you are looking at to settle back into its natural state before you look again.

Do not touch the walls unless necessary. The walls are still deciding what they are. Your touch introduces information—a foreign temperature, a foreign texture, the presence of a thing that is already fully formed—and that information may influence the wall's decision. You might cause a section of wall to solidify prematurely, or to soften unexpectedly, or to begin forming in a way that references your touch. You do not want to leave fingerprints in a place that is still becoming. The fingerprints might become part of its permanent form, and the pocket might remember you in ways that are not comfortable.

### C. How to Interact with the Creatures

Do not interact with the creatures.

This is the simplest rule and the most important. The creatures of the Birthing Pocket are the most formed things in the space, but "most formed" is a relative term. They are more formed than the walls, more formed than the almost-plants, more formed than the moisture. But they are not fully formed. They are still in the process of deciding what they are, and they have decided, provisionally, that they are creatures that consume potential.

You are potential.

From the perspective of the pocket's creatures, you are a dense, concentrated source of formed reality—a thing that has already decided what it is, carrying within you the potential for many future states. You are, to them, what the almost-plants are to the snail-things: food. Not food in the predatory sense—they do not hunt, they do not attack, they do not consume violently. But food in the way that a forming thing consumes whatever is available in order to continue forming.

The description says the creatures have decided, for now, that you are "not food. Not yet. Not unless you stay too long." Respect this decision. Do not challenge it. Do not give them reason to reconsider.

**Specific protocols:**

- **The snail-things:** Leave them alone. Do not step on their luminous trails. The trails are mapping the pocket, and disrupting them may interfere with the pocket's process of self-definition. If a snail-thing approaches you, move away slowly. Do not touch it. Its slime may adhere to your skin and begin the process of incorporating you into the pocket's system.

- **The not-insects:** Do not interfere with their feeding. They consume the dampness, which is harmless to you. But if they begin to cluster around you, if they begin to consume the moisture that has settled on your clothing or skin, brush them away gently and move to a different area. They are not dangerous individually, but in numbers, their feeding may begin to blur the boundary between your surface and the pocket's atmosphere.

- **The too-long worms:** Do not follow them into their holes. The holes close behind them, and the walls smooth over. If you follow, you will be inside the wall, and the wall will close, and you will become part of the pocket's structure. This is not speculation. This is what the walls do. They are still forming, and they form by incorporating what enters them.

- **The light-distortions:** The things that move too fast to see, that bend the light around themselves—do not try to observe them directly. Your eyes will not resolve them, and the effort of trying may cause headaches, dizziness, or a temporary loosening of your visual processing that the pocket may exploit. If you see a distortion in the light, look away. Let it pass. It is not interested in you, unless you make yourself interesting by staring.

### D. How to Handle the Structures

The built environment in the pocket—marks on the walls, stone piles, rings of pellets, the desiccated coil—is sparse and enigmatic. Do not disturb it.

Whoever or whatever made these structures was here before you and may be here after you. The structures may be functional, ceremonial, or simply the residue of a presence that no longer exists. You do not have the context to interpret them, and attempting to do so may lead you to conclusions that the pocket will happily make real. If you decide that a mark is a warning, the pocket may make it a warning. If you decide it is a map, the pocket may make it a map. Your interpretations have weight here, and careless interpretation may create consequences.

Look. Note. Do not touch. Do not photograph—cameras may not function reliably in the pocket's light, and the act of photographing may fix something that is not meant to be fixed. Sketch if you must, but understand that your sketch is an interpretation, and interpretations have power.

If you find a structure that seems to be an exit—a doorway, a tunnel, a gap in the wall that leads elsewhere—do not enter it without your cord. The pocket's boundaries are permeable, but what lies beyond is undefined. You do not want to enter undefined space without a line back to defined reality.

### E. How to Manage Time

Check your watch every few minutes. Note the time. Note whether the time feels right—whether the elapsed time matches your subjective experience. If you check your watch and it has been an hour since your last check, but it felt like minutes, note this. If you check your watch and it has been minutes, but it felt like hours, note this.

Keep a log. Carry a small notebook and write down the time at each check. This creates a record—a sequence of moments that are fixed, that have been decided, that cannot be renegotiated by the pocket. The notebook is an external memory, more reliable than your internal memory in a place where memory is subject to the pocket's influence.

**Set a departure time before you enter.** Decide: I will leave after three hours. Or two hours. Or one. Write the departure time in your notebook. Check it against your watch. When the time comes, leave. Do not negotiate with yourself. Do not extend your stay because you are fascinated, because you have just noticed something interesting, because you want to see what happens next. The pocket is full of interesting things. It will always have one more interesting thing. The interesting things are the trap.

**If your watch stops, leave.** If your watch runs backward, leave. If your watch shows a time that does not correspond to any time you recognize, leave. If you check your notebook and find entries in your handwriting that you do not remember writing, *leave immediately.*

## V. What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

### A. If You Begin to Merge with the Walls

You will feel it before it becomes visible: a pulling sensation, a dampness that is not on your skin but in it, a softening of your edges. The wall is trying to incorporate you. It is not hostile. It is simply doing what walls do in a place where walls are still forming.

**Response:** Pull back. Physically, firmly, with your full weight. The wall will resist—it may have already begun to form around you—but it has not committed yet. The key is to move before the commitment is complete. Once the stone has decided that you are part of it, extraction becomes much more difficult.

Use your cord. If you are tied to something outside, pull on the cord. The tension will remind your body where outside is. Follow the cord.

If you have salt, place it on the area of contact. The salt's certainty may disrupt the wall's process of incorporation.

### B. If You Lose Track of Time

If you realize that you do not know how long you have been in the pocket, stop. Sit down. Check your watch. Check your notebook. If you have no notebook, if you have no watch, if you cannot remember whether you brought them, you are in serious trouble.

**Response:** Look for your cord. Follow it. If you have no cord, look for the entrance you came through. It may not be where you remember it being, but it will be somewhere. The pocket is small—you can cross it in less than a minute. Walk the perimeter. Look for any opening, any gap, any place where the wall is less formed than elsewhere.

Do not panic. Panic is a form of energy, and energy in the Birthing Pocket may be absorbed and used by the forming space. Stay calm. Be methodical. The pocket is patient, which means you have time, even if you are not sure how much.

### C. If You Encounter Something That Should Not Be Here

The pocket's reality is permeable. Things can slip through—from elsewhere, from elsewhen, from elsewhat. You may encounter something that does not match the description of the pocket's native creatures. Something that is fully formed, fully decided, and deeply out of place. A door standing in the middle of the space. A person who is not you. A sound that has a clear source—a voice, a cry, a word spoken in a language you almost recognize.

**Response:** Do not engage. Do not approach. Do not speak to it. The pocket's permeable boundaries admit things, and the things that come through may not be friendly, may not be hostile, may not have a stance that you can recognize. They are from elsewhere, and elsewhere does not play by the pocket's rules—or by yours.

Move away. Find your cord. Leave.

### D. If You Cannot Leave

If you have lost your cord, if the entrance has closed, if the walls have sealed and the pocket has become a closed space with you inside it:

**Sit down. Be still. Wait.**

The pocket is still forming. It has not finished. Walls that have closed may open again. Entrances that have disappeared may reappear. The pocket is not a prison—it is a womb, and wombs open when their contents are ready to be born.

But do not try to become ready. Do not try to merge, to adapt, to become something the pocket will want to release. Simply wait. Maintain yourself. Read your letter. Eat your dry food. Drink your water. Check your watch, even if it has stopped. Be the most fully formed, most decided, most external thing in the pocket.

Eventually, the pocket will need to define its boundaries. It cannot form fully with a foreign object inside it. It will expel you—not violently, but the way a body expels a splinter: slowly, inevitably, as part of its own process of becoming.

This may take hours. It may take days. It may take longer. But the pocket is patient, and you must be patient too.

## VI. After You Leave

The description warns that you may leave "carrying some of the pocket's unreality with you, never quite certain afterward whether your time here was a genuine experience or a dream that forgot to end."

This is real. Prepare for it.

**Expect to feel strange for days afterward.** Your edges may feel soft. Your reflection may look slightly wrong. Your memories of the pocket may shift, may fade, may become uncertain. This is the pocket's influence slowly draining from your system, the way a dye washes out of fabric over time.

**Write down everything you remember as soon as you leave.** While it is fresh, while it is still yours. Write it in a notebook, on paper, in ink. Do not type it—typing is too easy to revise, and revision in this context is a form of renegotiation. You want your memories to be fixed, decided, committed. Write them once. Do not edit.

**Do not return to the pocket immediately.** Give yourself time to resolidify. The pocket's influence is cumulative—each visit softens your edges further, makes you more permeable to its reality. If you must return, wait at least as long as you spent inside. If you spent three hours in the pocket, wait at least three hours before reentering. A day is better. A week is better still.

**Talk to someone.** Find someone who has never been to the pocket. Talk to them about ordinary things. The weather. Food.
