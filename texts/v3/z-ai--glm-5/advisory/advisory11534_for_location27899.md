# Preparation Guide: The Plateau of Suspended Geometry

## A Note Before Beginning

You are preparing to visit a place where reality has worn thin. This is not metaphorical. The fabric of existence—the dependable relationship between cause and effect, between distance and travel time, between the number of walls a building possesses and the number you can see—has become permeable, porous, subject to leakage. You cannot pack for this the way you pack for elsewhere. Some of what you need to bring is a kind of willingness to lose things.

---

## The Ascent: Physical Preparation

You will climb for days. This is not negotiable. The plateau does not permit other forms of arrival—it insists on the transformation that comes from ascent, the slow shedding of the world below. By the time you reach it, you will have already been changed by the journey.

**What to bring:**

*Clothing that layers.* The temperature is cool and remains so, but your body will not regulate itself the way it does at lower elevations. The thin air does not hold heat or lose it with the reliability you expect. Bring materials that can be added and removed without requiring you to stop and think. Zippers that work one-handed. Fabrics that do not whisper or rustle—the silence here is attentive, and you will become self-conscious about noise.

*Footwear that connects you to the ground.* You will need to feel the mineral surface through your soles. The plateau pulses with a geological heartbeat, and your ability to sense this rhythm—literally, physically—will help you orient yourself when other senses fail. Thick tread is useless here. You are not climbing further. You are walking across something that wants to read you through your feet.

*No water.* This contradicts every instinct. But water is excluded from the plateau—not absent, *refused.* If you carry it, you carry something the place has already rejected. Your canteen will become a dead weight, an object that does not belong. The local physics temporarily rewrites your body's requirements, suspends thirst. Trust this. Do not fight it. If you must carry liquid for the descent, seal it in vessels that do not require opening until you have returned below the watershed.

*Food that is dense and dry.* The dehydration that defines the plateau extends to anything organic. Moisture will be pulled from anything you bring. Your provisions will become harder, more concentrated, within hours of arrival. Plan for this. Bring foods that can survive becoming more themselves—hard biscuits, dried meats, compressed nutrient blocks. Things that will not spoil when their water is taken.

*No technology that depends on satellites or signals.* There is nothing above you but diffuse light. No GPS will function. No radio will transmit. The plateau exists outside the networks that bind the ordinary world to its systems of location and communication. If you bring a compass, bring one that works magnetically, and be prepared for it to point toward things other than north.

## Mental Preparation: The Shape of Reality

The hardest preparation is not physical. The plateau will challenge your relationship with the basic structures by which you navigate existence. You must be ready to encounter:

*Distance that lies.* A spire that seems a hundred yards distant may take an hour to reach. This is not illusion—it is a different relationship between sight and traversal. Your eyes will tell you one thing; your feet will learn another. Do not trust your sense of how far things are. Trust only the passage of time and the number of steps taken. Develop a system: count steps, measure time, and let these become your distance.

*Geometry that refuses to settle.* Buildings will present different faces depending on where you stand. Circumnavigating a structure may reveal more walls than it should possess. Do not attempt to force the geometry to make sense. Allow it to be what it is. If you find yourself counting corners to verify reality, stop. The counting will not help. The building is not lying to you; it is existing in a way your mind did not evolve to process.

*Shadows that behave with agency.* Your shadow will split, precede you, attach to surfaces other than the ground. This is not harmful—it is the local light negotiating with your presence. But it can be deeply disorienting. Practice, before you arrive, the art of not looking for your shadow to confirm your position. You will not find the confirmation you seek.

*Time that moves sideways.* You may lose moments that never existed, gain memories of places you have never been. This is not time travel—it is time *lateralizing*, becoming a dimension in which multiple positions are simultaneously true. Do not try to reconstruct a linear narrative of your experience. You will be here, and then you will be elsewhere, and both will be true.

---

## Navigational Preparation: Finding Without Direction

You cannot navigate the plateau the way you navigate cities designed for human movement. There are no streets in the sense you understand them—only negative spaces, planned absences, gaps between presences.

**What to understand:**

The city was built for a purpose other than habitation. Its structures are stakes driven into reality to keep it from dissolving entirely. This means that the spacing of buildings—the emptiness between them—is as meaningful as the structures themselves. When you walk, you are walking through a system. You are passing between elements of a machine you do not comprehend.

*Do not attempt to map the plateau.* Maps assume consistent relationships between distance and representation. The plateau does not offer these consistencies. If you make a map, it will be wrong within hours. Instead, develop a relationship with the terrain itself. Learn to recognize individual structures—not by their shape, which may shift, but by the feeling of the space around them. Each building has a presence, a particular density in the fabric of the place. Learn to read density rather than form.

*Follow the fauna.* The creatures that inhabit the plateau are not animals—they are processes, part of the system that maintains the boundaries of reality. They move with purpose, tracing patterns that serve the plateau's continued existence. They will not lead you wrong, though they may not lead you where you intended to go. If you are lost, watch for the shimmer in the air, the ripple in the fabric, the gathering of angles. Follow at a distance. They are tending to something. You may learn what it is.

## Psychological Preparation: What the Silence Asks of You

The silence here is not empty. It is the attentive silence of a space holding its breath. Something is listening. The fabric of reality itself has become permeable to some kind of attention. Sound is absorbed into the mineral ground, pulled downward into processes that do not echo back.

This means:

*You will hear yourself more clearly than you wish.* Every sound you make will be noticed, absorbed, transformed. The echo that returns from a footfall may come from the wrong direction, in a different voice. Your own cough will answer itself in three separate tones. Be prepared to hear your presence multiplied, altered, reflected back at you in forms you did not produce. This is not malevolent—it is the plateau processing you, understanding you, including you in its calculations.

*You may feel watched.* This is because you are—but not by eyes. The listening is ambient, everywhere at once. You will walk through plazas that feel like rooms without walls, and you will be aware of occupying space that was designed for something. Do not let this become paranoia. You are a guest here, or perhaps a variable, but not an intruder. The plateau has room for you. It has calculated your presence. You are meant to be here, for however long you stay.

*Solitude will take different forms.* The emptiness is not abandonment—it is design. The city was made to be sparse, each structure given room to exist fully. You will be given room to exist fully. For some travelers, this is profoundly lonely. For others, it is liberating. Know yourself in this regard. If you require the presence of others to feel anchored, bring something that anchors you—an object, a text, a purpose. You will not encounter other humans here unless they have also chosen to ascend.

## Preparing for the Fauna: What Lives Here

The creatures of the plateau are not animals in any sense you recognize. They are rare because rarity is the local abundance. They are processes, gatherings, temporary configurations of forces.

*What they are not:* They are not predators. They are not prey. They do not eat, do not reproduce, do not sleep. They do not care about you in any emotional sense.

*What they are:* They are part of the system that maintains the plateau's existence. They move through the city tracing patterns that might be patrols, might be rituals, might be the only way to keep the boundaries maintained. They are working, tending, servicing a machine built without tools.

*How to interact:* Maintain distance. Watch. Do not approach. If a creature passes near you, remain still. You may feel the air change temperature—a coolness, a residue of silence. This is normal. The creature is not acknowledging you; it is simply moving through space you happen to occupy. The distance they maintain is calculated. Respect it.

*Do not follow them into buildings.* The creatures move through structures in ways that may not be available to you. A shimmer that enters a tower may not be using a door. If you attempt to follow, you may find yourself somewhere other than where you intended—or somewhere that is not quite *anywhere*.

---

## The Reality Breakdown: What to Do When Existence Wavers

The plateau is coming undone. This is not a danger—it is a condition. The city was built to manage this undoing, to serve as an anchor point. You are standing inside a mechanism that holds reality together by occupying the places where it wants to tear.

*Symptoms you may experience:*

—Being in two locations at once. Your body may span distances that should be impossible. Your vision may double. You may briefly occupy two separate viewpoints simultaneously. This is disorienting but not harmful. When it happens, do not try to force yourself back into one. Wait. The plateau will settle. You will return to singularity.

—Time skips. You may lose moments, gain memories of places you never stood. This is lateral time—time moving into adjacent positions rather than forward or back. Accept these memories as real. They are yours now, even if you did not live them. Do not try to reassemble a linear narrative of your experience. You will only confuse yourself.

—Shadows that precede you. Light negotiates here. It decides what you are permitted to cast. If your shadow behaves strangely, do not try to correct it. The light is including you in its calculations. You are being processed.

—Sound that returns transformed. Echoes will come back altered, multiplied, in voices not your own. This is the plateau understanding you. Listen to what it sends back. You may learn something about how you sound to the universe.

*What to do:*

Stay calm. The breakdown is slow, managed, contained by the city itself. You are in no immediate danger of dissolution. The structures around you are doing the work of holding reality together. You are merely passing through. Be gentle with the fabric of the place. Do not test its boundaries. Do not experiment with how far you can push before things break further. The plateau is already at the edge of what it can sustain. Walk lightly, in every sense.

---

## Preparing for the Light: Seeing Without Illumination

The light comes from everywhere and nowhere. There is no sun, no moon, no stars—only an even, directionless glow that seems to emanate from the air itself. It does not change. There is no day or night here, only the same perpetual dimness that suggests twilight held in amber.

*Practical implications:*

You cannot track time by light. You will need to develop other methods. If you must measure time, bring a mechanical device that does not require external reference—a wind-up watch, an hourglass. But know that time itself moves strangely here. Your device may not measure what you expect it to measure.

Photography will be difficult. The light pools and avoids certain areas. Some structures generate light rather than receiving it. Your camera will not capture what your eyes see, and your eyes do not see consistently. If you must document, do so with multiple methods—digital and analog, written description, perhaps sketching. Do not trust any single form of record.

Your shadow will not anchor you. In normal light, shadows confirm position—they fall away from light sources, they attach to your feet. Here, they split, precede, attach to surfaces other than the ground. You will need to find other ways to confirm where you stand in space. Trust your feet. Trust the ground. Trust the rhythm of the plateau's pulse beneath you.

## What to Leave Behind

Some things cannot come with you. Not because they are forbidden, but because they will not survive the journey, or because carrying them will harm you.

*Leave behind expectations of continuity.* You will not move through this place the way you move through other places. Distance, time, geometry, even your own shadow—none will behave with the consistency you rely upon. If you expect the world to hold still while you traverse it, you will be frustrated and frightened. If you can release that expectation, you may find something valuable in the suspension.

*Leave behind the need to understand.* The plateau operates on principles that precede the concept of tools. It shapes itself according to will, or invitation, or some third thing for which we have no word. You will not understand it. You may learn to move through it, to be present with it, to respect its processes—but you will not comprehend it. The city was not built for inhabitants. It was built to manage reality's dissolution. You are a visitor to a machine you cannot operate.

*Leave behind the assumption that you will leave.* This is not a warning—most travelers do return. But the plateau operates outside normal time, and the journey down requires a different kind of willingness than the journey up. You must be prepared to stay as long as the plateau requires. Some travelers have reported losing weeks, months, though subjectively it felt like days. Others have returned to find that less time passed than they experienced. Bring enough supplies for longer than you plan to stay, and be prepared to abandon them if the plateau rejects what you carry.

*Leave behind the fear of being changed.* You will be changed. The ascent alone changes you—days of climbing, the world shrinking below you, the slow opening of the sky. By the time you reach the plateau, you have already shed the person you were in the valleys. The plateau will continue this transformation. You will leave lighter than you arrived, though not necessarily in the ways you expect. This is not a cost—it is part of what the place offers. If you cannot bear alteration, do not come.

---

## The Descent: Preparing to Return

Returning from the plateau is not simply reversing the ascent. You have been changed, and the world below will not have waited for you. Time may have passed differently. Your body will remember thirst, will need to relearn its ordinary requirements. The reintegration will take time.

*Before you descend:*

Spend time at the edge of the plateau. Feel the boundary between the suspended geometry and the world below. Notice how the air changes, how the light begins to have direction again. Do not rush the transition. You are leaving a place that has been processing you, including you in its calculations. The departure should be conscious, acknowledged.

Drink water only after you have crossed below the watershed. The plateau's exclusion of moisture will not release you until you have left its domain. If you try to drink while still on the mineral surface, the water will taste wrong, will not satisfy, may make you ill. Wait.

Give yourself time after return. You will dream of the violet-gray dimness. You will hear echoes that return at wrong intervals, that speak in voices not your own. You will walk through streets that obey normal geometry and feel a sudden disorientation, a sense that the buildings should be spiraling, that the distances should not be so reliable. This passes. The world reasserts itself. But for a while, you will carry the plateau in your bones—the rhythm of its pulse, the silence that listens, the light that negotiates.

---

## Final Note: What You Are Preparing For

You are preparing to visit a place where reality has grown thin. Where existence is not assumed but maintained, held together by structures that precipitated from the mineral ground. Where the fauna are processes and the silence is listening and the geometry refuses to settle.

You are preparing to stand inside a wound that has become its own bandage—a city that exists to keep something from leaking through the fabric of the world.

You are preparing to become, for however long you stay, part of the landscape's calculations. Another variable in its slow process of holding itself together.

This is not a journey for everyone. But if you have read this far, if you have felt the pull of the high thin air, the fascination of the impossible geometry, the strange appeal of a place where light negotiates and time moves sideways—then perhaps you are already preparing, in ways you do not yet recognize.

The plateau continues. The geometry suspends. The reality holds, barely.

And the ascent begins with a single step upward, toward the violet-gray light, above the watershed, beyond the reach of ordinary weather, into the permanent twilight that suggests nothing so much as a world remembering illumination but forgetting by what.
