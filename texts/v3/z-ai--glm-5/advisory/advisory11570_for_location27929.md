# Preparing for the Upward Land

## What You Are Walking Into

You are going to a place that is still making itself. This is not a metaphor. The stone beneath your feet will not have decided its final shape by the time you arrive, and it will still be deciding after you leave. You will walk across a construction site that operates on geological timescales, in darkness so total that your brain will eventually stop waiting for light and start inventing its own interpretations.

You will be warm the entire time. You will be alone in a way that has nothing to do with the presence or absence of others. You will see structures left by previous travelers—or perhaps by something else entirely—and you will understand why they left nothing but markers.

This is a place for witnessing. It will not accommodate you. It will not harm you, exactly, but it will not pause its slow creation for your comfort. Prepare accordingly.

---

## Physical Preparation

### The Darkness Is Not Like Other Darknesses

Bring light, but understand its limitations. A standard headlamp will show you perhaps three meters of terrain in any direction, and the beam will die into the vastness without reaching anything that could be called a wall or a ceiling or a horizon. You are inside something continental. Your light is a match held up to the ocean.

Pack at least three independent light sources. Batteries fail in heat—bring more than you calculate you need. The warmth of the Upward Land is geological and constant; it does not fluctuate with the comfort of your equipment.

But here is what will happen: after your first hours, possibly after your first full day, you will begin to perceive the heat-light. Certain minerals in the stone glow faint cherry-red from within, a luminescence generated by their own warmth. This light is barely enough to see by, but your eyes will adapt. Humans are adaptable. You will learn to read the darkness.

Until then, your artificial lights are your only connection to the visual world. Treat them as essential.

### Water Is the Critical Concern

There is no water in the Upward Land. None. This is not a desert—deserts remember water, expect water, are shaped by its absence. This place has never known moisture. The stone is pre-wet, existing in a state that has no relationship with hydration at all.

You cannot find water here. You cannot collect water here. There is no condensation, no seepage, no hidden caches. The dryness is absolute and fundamental.

Bring more water than you have ever carried for a journey of this type. The warmth is constant and rises through the soles of your feet into your bones. You will lose moisture through your skin and your breath at a rate that feels impossible in such still air. Calculate your water needs, then double them. Then add more.

Consider a closed-system hydration suit if you have access to one—anything that captures and recycles your own moisture. The technology exists in many universes. It is worth the acquisition cost.

If you feel the early signs of dehydration—the headache, the confusion, the darkening at the edges of your thoughts—turn back immediately. The Upward Land does not forgive those who miscalculate. It does not notice them at all.

### Your Feet and What They Will Touch

The stone is warm. Warmer than your body. You will feel this immediately when you step onto the plateau—the heat rises through your boot soles, into your arches, your ankles, your calves. Within an hour, your entire skeleton will hum with borrowed warmth.

This is not dangerous in itself. The warmth is intense but not scalding. What damages you is duration. Hours of walking on heated stone will alter your circulation, your muscle tension, the way your weight distributes across your joints. Your feet will swell. Your boots should account for this—wear them loose, or bring a second pair in a larger size.

The stone is also sharp. Freshly formed. Newly fractured. The crystals have never been weathered by wind or water, never smoothed by the patient work of erosion. Running your hand across a surface can open your palm. Kneeling without care can cut through your trousers.

Bring sturdy boots with thick soles. Bring gloves—even if you do not think you will need them. Bring durable clothing that covers your skin. The glassy dust that moves through the air currents will catch against exposed flesh like fine ash, and after hours of accumulation, you will feel it as a low-grade irritation across every uncovered surface.

Goggles are advisable. The dust respects no boundaries.

### Your Lungs and the Thin Air

You are very high here. The elevation is extreme, though there is no sky to measure it against. Your lungs will work harder to pull oxygen from each breath. You will not experience altitude sickness in the traditional sense—the chemistry of this place seems to accommodate human physiology in certain ways—but you will feel the effort of breathing. Each breath will be conscious work.

If you have any respiratory vulnerability, any condition that compromises your lung capacity, reconsider this journey. The Upward Land demands that you breathe deep and continuously for hours, days, however long you stay. It does not offer rest.

Some travelers bring supplemental oxygen. This is reasonable. It is also an acknowledgment that you are entering a place not designed for you.

### The Sound That Is Not Quite Sound

You will feel a hum. A low vibration that lives below the threshold of hearing, more felt than perceived. This is the voice of the land itself—the frequency at which the stone communicates its own becoming.

Most travelers adjust within hours. Some never do. If you are sensitive to infrasound, if low frequencies cause you discomfort or disorientation, prepare coping strategies. Some find that earplugs help—not by blocking the sound (which cannot be blocked), but by providing a familiar physical sensation that anchors them to their own bodies.

## Psychological Preparation

### There Is No Time Here

The darkness is total and constant. There is no sun, no moon, no stars. No day, no night. You will have no external cues to mark the passage of time.

Your body will continue its rhythms regardless. You will become tired. You will become hungry. But without the familiar cycles of light and dark, without the ceremony of sunrise and sunset, you may lose track of how long you have walked, how long you have rested, how long you have been in this place.

Bring a timekeeping device. Something mechanical, something that does not rely on satellite signals or network connections. Wind it before you enter. Check it often. The numbers will not feel real—they never do, in places like this—but they will provide a tether.

Expect time to feel strange. Expect to lose track. Expect to experience hours as minutes or minutes as hours, depending on the texture of your attention. This is normal. This is what happens when humans enter places that operate outside human timescales.

### The Structures Are Not for You

You will find constructions. Cairns, walls, rings of stone, the foundations of roofless buildings. They were made deliberately. They were placed with intention. And they contain no information about who made them or why.

Do not expect answers. The structures are markers—statements of presence, records of witnessing. Someone stood where you will stand. Someone saw what you will see. They left behind only the fact of their presence.

This can be comforting or deeply unsettling, depending on your disposition. Some travelers report feeling connected across vast distances of time to these unknown predecessors. Others feel a profound loneliness, an awareness of how small and temporary they are against the slow emergence of the land.

Both reactions are accurate. Both are appropriate. Sit with whichever one rises in you.

### You Will Not See the Land Move

But you will feel it. The knowledge that everything around you is temporary, that the surface you stand on is in the process of becoming something else, creates a particular psychological state. Some describe it as vertigo without movement. Others as a persistent awareness of being in motion while standing still.

This is accurate. You are walking across a process, not a place. The Upward Land is not a location you visit; it is an event you witness.

The land will not change dramatically during your time there. You will not see mountains rise or valleys form. But you may notice a crack widen, a slope settle, a fresh edge expose itself in the hours after you first passed it. These small changes accumulate into a profound understanding: this place is alive with becoming. It is building itself around you, patient and enormous and utterly indifferent.

### The Silence Has Texture

There is no life here. No birds, no insects, no wind through vegetation. The sounds that exist are geological: the crack of splitting stone, the settling of scree, the deep subsonic hum of emergence.

Some travelers find this silence peaceful. Others find it unbearable. Most experience both at different moments.

Bring sound if you need it—a music player, recordings of familiar environments, the voices of people you love. But also consider letting yourself experience the silence. It is rare, in any universe, to encounter soundlessness of this quality. It is rare to hear only the sounds of a world making itself.

If you do bring sound, use headphones. Do not impose your noise onto this vast and patient quiet. It has earned its silence.

## Navigation and Movement

### You Will Get Lost

The terrain changes. Not quickly enough to see, but constantly. The landmarks you noted on your way in will shift, settle, fracture into new configurations. Your map, if you made one, will be wrong within hours.

Accept this. Work with it rather than against it.

Some travelers leave trail markers—arranged stones, stacked cairns, small deposits of brought materials. This is acceptable, even traditional. Add your markers to the collection left by those who came before.

But do not rely on finding your own markers on the way out. Others may have passed and added theirs. The landscape may have shifted enough to obscure what seemed obvious. Use markers as aids, not as certainties.

Instead, navigate by the heat-light mineral patches. These glow with cherry-red luminescence, and their arrangement creates a kind of constellation across the dark landscape. Learn to read them. They are more permanent than the stone itself, embedded in the geology rather than surface-level.

If you have access to inertial navigation technology, bring it. If you can leave radio beacons or similar anchors, do so. But remember that electronic equipment behaves unpredictably in environments like this. Batteries drain faster. Signals scatter. The stone itself may interfere with frequencies you have never needed to question.

Always know the direction of the entrance point you used. However you arrived—wherever you emerged into the Upward Land—maintain your orientation relative to that point. Some travelers keep a physical object at their entry location: a pack, a pole, a clearly visible marker. Something they can find again when they are ready to leave.

### Walking Here Requires Different Attention

The ground is unstable in ways that are not immediately visible. The scree slopes shift under your weight. The fresh fractures create edges that crumble when tested. Surfaces that look solid may be the skins of pockets that have not yet fully solidified.

Test your footing before committing your weight. Move deliberately. Do not run, do not rush, do not attempt to cover distance quickly. The Upward Land rewards patience and punishes haste.

If you fall, you will likely fall onto sharp stone. The cuts here are clean and deep. Without water to clean wounds, infection becomes a real concern. Without immediate medical attention, even small injuries can become serious.

Walk as if every step matters. In a place this raw, it does.

## What to Bring

### Essential Equipment

**Water.** More than you think possible. Sealed containers that will not degrade in heat. A delivery system that works while walking.

**Light.** Headlamps, hand-helds, backup batteries. At least three independent sources.

**Timekeeping.** Mechanical watch or similar. Something that does not require external signals.

**Navigation aids.** Inertial navigation if available. Physical markers for trail marking. Compasses may or may not function dependably—test yours in similar environments before relying on it.

**Protective clothing.** Long sleeves, long trousers, sturdy boots, gloves, eye protection. Natural fibers or heat-resistant synthetics. Nothing that will melt against your skin.

**First aid supplies.** Bandages, antiseptic, wound closure strips. You cannot clean wounds with water here; bring alternatives.

**Food.** Dense, dry, long-lasting. Nothing that requires hydration or refrigeration. You will not feel hungry in the traditional sense—the warmth suppresses appetite—but you must eat anyway. Your body burns energy in this environment regardless of what your stomach reports.

### Recommended Equipment

**Recording devices.** Camera, audio recorder, journal. You will want evidence of your presence, memories of what you witnessed. But electronics behave strangely here. Write by hand if you can. The physical act of inscription feels more real in a place where digital memory might fail.

**Something to leave.** A marker, a small object, a statement of your presence. You will understand why when you find the structures.

**Sound.** If you need it. Headphones, not speakers.

**Supplemental oxygen.** For comfort, not survival. Some travelers report clearer thinking, deeper appreciation of the environment, when their lungs are not straining.

### What Not to Bring

**Expectations.** The Upward Land will not meet them. It will exceed them or fall short of them, but it will not meet them, because it is not operating on any framework you have brought from elsewhere.

**Fear of the dark.** Leave it behind. You will be in darkness for your entire stay. If you cannot be comfortable without light, you will be uncomfortable the whole time.

**Certainty about what you are experiencing.** The place resists interpretation. It is not sacred, not profane, not haunted, not empty. It is simply itself, in the process of becoming, and your categories will not hold it.

## The Final Preparation

Before you enter the Upward Land, stand at the threshold and acknowledge what you are doing.

You are going to witness a world being born.

You will not understand it. You will not master it. You will walk across its surface for a time, feeling its warmth rise through your body, watching its faint heat-light glow against the total dark, passing the structures left by those who came before.

And then you will leave, and it will continue its slow emergence without you, as it was doing before you arrived and will continue to do long after every trace of your presence has been swallowed by new stone.

This is not a warning. This is a gift.

The Upward Land offers you the opportunity to be small. To be temporary. To witness something that does not need your witness and is not altered by it. In a multiverse full of places that demand your attention, your reaction, your transformation, here is a place that simply continues its own existence with patient and massive indifference.

You will add your marker to the collection. You will stand where the builders stood. You will watch the basin floor rise by increments too slow to see.

And when you return—if you choose to return—you will carry with you the knowledge of a place that has not yet decided what it is, that is still in the process of becoming, that welcomes you without caring whether you arrive.

Go warm. Go prepared. Go with the understanding that you are visiting a construction site where the construction will continue for millennia after you have gone.

The land is rising. It will rise with you on it or without you. The choice to be there, to witness it, to add your small mark to the record of those who have seen—that is yours.

Walk carefully. The stone is new, and it has not yet learned to be gentle.
