# The Attention

The power arrives. This is the first thing. Not arrives *from* anywhere — arrives at the scale of attention, which is to say, arrives in the dimension of caring. The plateau has been here for longer than remembering. The being has walked it for weeks, months, measureless time. And now something turns toward it. Not an eye. A *turning*. The way a head turns toward a sound, except the sound is existence itself, and the head is something that has no shape and no location and no need of either.

The power sees the whole of it at once. Not because it is small but because seeing is what the power does, the way the engine hums, the way the ground is warm. The power sees:

The plateau. Continental in scope. The dark stone going down and down, layer after layer after layer, mineral strata folding into each other, thermal gradients rising through crystalline veins, the great slow engine doing what it has done since before there was a word for doing. The power sees the channels. All of them. The network of them. The way they connect basin to basin, depth to depth, the permeable stone functioning as a circulatory system so vast and so patient that the individual basins are indeed cells, nodes, single moments in a process that has no single location. The engine is not in a place. The engine *is* the place. The power sees this and loves it, the way one loves something that has achieved what it set out to achieve, the way one loves a system that works.

The power sees the life. The surface fauna in their basins — the clickers and the buzzers and the slow processors making their furrows in the substrate. The thread-growth spreading its pale fibers through the chemical gradients, eating the stone's output, feeding the fauna that feed on it. The large things moving through the deep channels, their mineralized bodies pressing against the channel walls, their warm breath rising through fissures. The middle strata — yes, the power sees them, the things that bore through the porous rock and live in the boreholes, existing at scales of space and time that make the surface look frantic, the power sees them in their millions, in their slow billions, the interconnectedness of them, the way the life permeates the stone the way the stone permeates the life. The power sees the ecology. The power sees the balance. The power sees the system that does not degrade and does not build and does not need and does not want and simply *is*, running the way a river runs, the way a heart runs.

The power sees the being.

---

The being who walks.

The power looks at this one differently. Not because the being is remarkable — the being is not remarkable, in the terms of the plateau. The being is one small warm body moving across a vast warm surface, leaving no mark, taking nothing, giving nothing, persisting in an environment that does not acknowledge it. The being is not remarkable *to the plateau*. But the power is not the plateau. The power has turned its attention toward this place *because* of this being, and so the power sees the being not as an irrelevant texture but as the reason for its presence.

What does the power see?

It sees a body that is tired. The being has walked for a long time. The being's legs ache. The being's feet have developed calluses and then blisters and then calluses again, and the boots are wearing thin, and the clothing is thin, and the body is continuing on something that is not quite will and not quite momentum and not quite hope. The being is not falling apart. The being is not in danger of dying, not yet, not on this plateau where the water is warm and the fauna are not predatory and the engine's output is not toxic. But the being is worn. The power can see the wearing. It can see the small costs accumulating.

It sees a mind that is working. The being has been observing. Cataloging. Learning. The being has walked through the dark and felt the stone and listened to the clicking and understood things about the ecology that the power did not expect any outsider to understand. The being has built a map in its head, a map of basins and channels and thermal gradients and fauna distributions, and the map is not complete but it is not wrong, and the power respects this. The being has not come here and died. The being has come here and *paid attention*. The power did not anticipate this. The power finds it interesting.

It sees a loneliness that is not quite loneliness. The being has understood that it is irrelevant, and the being has understood that this is not the same as being alone, and the being has built a kind of peace around this understanding. But the peace is not complete. The being walks and walks, and the being observes and observes, and the being is the only thing that observes, and beneath the peace there is a question the being has not yet articulated. The power can see the question forming. The power can see that it has not yet reached the surface of the being's mind, and the power can see that it will.

The question is: *What am I doing here?*

And beneath that: *Is this all?*

And beneath that: *Will I walk until I stop?*

And beneath that, so deep the being may never find it: *Does anything know I am here?*

---

The power considers what it would mean to act.

This is not a simple consideration. The power is benevolent. The power is wise. The power understands that benevolence without wisdom is cruelty in disguise, and that wisdom without benevolence is observation without intervention, and that the combination — benevolence and wisdom together — creates an obligation that is not simple to fulfill.

The plateau does not need anything. The engine does not need anything. The life that has organized itself around the engine's output does not need anything. The system is complete. It is balanced. It has been running for longer than the power has been paying attention to it, and it will run for longer still, and nothing the power does will improve it, because the system does not operate on a scale of improvement. It operates on a scale of continuation, and continuation is what it has.

The power could act. It could illuminate. It could change the being's eyes to see in the dark, to see what the power sees — the full ecology, the deep channels, the large things in their thermal corridors, the middle strata in their bored passages, the life that extends down and down into the stone. It could give the being the capacity to understand the plateau as the power understands it, to map it, to name it, to hold it in mind as a complete system. This would be a gift. The being wants to understand. The being has spent its time here learning. To give the being understanding would be benevolent.

But the power is wise, and wisdom asks: *What would happen next?*

The being would understand the plateau. And then what? The being would still be walking. The being would still be the only thing that observes. The being would still be irrelevant to the system. The understanding would not change the system — the system does not care if it is understood. The understanding would not change the being's position — the being would still be one small warm body in a vast warm place. The understanding might even make the loneliness worse. To see the whole of the plateau, to see how complete it is, how balanced, how utterly without need for the being — this might not be a gift. This might be a kind of cruelty, to show the being exactly how unnecessary it is.

The power considers other gifts. It could give the being company. It could create others — beings like the being, or different beings, or some third thing. It could populate the plateau with observers, with minds that see and record and care. The being would no longer be alone. The being would have peers. This would be benevolent.

But wisdom asks: *What would happen to the plateau?*

The plateau has no predators. The plateau has no competitors. The plateau has no diseases, no parasites, no invasive species. The life that lives here has organized itself around the engine's output in a way that is balanced, that is established, that has weathered into stability over eons. The surface fauna and the thread-growth and the large things and the middle strata — they are not separate from each other. They are part of a single system. And the system has no experience with beings like the one who walks. The being has been here for weeks or months and has left no mark. But if the power created others, if the power populated the plateau with observers, the system would begin to change. The basins would be disturbed more often. The substrate would be depleted faster. The thermal gradients would shift as more bodies moved across them. The balance would be lost.

Not lost quickly. Not lost dramatically. But lost. The power can see the cascade. More observers means more disturbance means more surface activity means more pressure on the basins means the thread-growth expands or contracts beyond its stable range means the surface fauna populations shift means the large things in the channels change their movement patterns means the middle strata adjust their boring means the engine itself — eventually, on a timescale the being would never see but the power can see perfectly well — the engine itself would rebalance, and the rebalancing would produce a different plateau, and the plateau that exists now, the one that has existed for longer than remembering, would be gone.

The power does not want this. The power loves the plateau. The power loves the being. The power is benevolent and wise, and it sees that it cannot act for one without acting against the other, and it must decide what to do.

---

The power makes a choice.

This is what powers do. They turn their attention toward things, and they see them, and they decide what to do. The power has turned its attention toward the plateau and the being who walks it, and it has seen them both, and it has seen that they are not in conflict but they are not in harmony either — they are simply coexisting, the being moving across the plateau, the plateau not caring, and the power must decide what to do with this coexistence.

The power chooses to stay.

This is not the same as doing nothing. The power chooses to continue paying attention. It chooses to observe. It chooses to watch the being walk, and watch the plateau run, and watch the ecology function, and watch the dark go in all directions, and watch the engine hum. It chooses to care about both of them — to love the plateau for its completeness and its balance and its ancient patient running, and to love the being for its persistence and its observation and its willingness to exist in a place that does not need it.

The power chooses to be known.

This is the harder choice. The power could stay and observe without ever making its presence felt. It could watch the being walk, and watch the plateau run, and never intervene. This would be wisdom without benevolence — observation without gift. The power does not want this either. The power wants to give the being something, and it has determined that it cannot give the being understanding (this might be cruelty) and it cannot give the being company (this might destroy the plateau), and so it must give the being something else.

It gives the being the knowledge that it is seen.

---

The being is walking. The being has left a basin behind — a small one, not the amphitheater, a smaller depression where the clickers were sparse and the substrate was dry and the thread-growth was only a rumor on the edge of the damp patch. The being is crossing bare stone. The dark goes in all directions. The engine hums beneath the being's feet. The being's legs ache. The being's mind is cataloging — the direction of the next basin, the texture of the stone, the faint variations in the engine's hum that might indicate a shift in thermal output. The being is doing what the being has done for weeks or months. The being is walking.

And then the being stops.

There is no sound. There is no light. There is no change in the stone, no shift in the warmth, no alteration in the dark. But something has changed. The being feels it — not in the body, not exactly, but in the *attention* of the place. The plateau does not attend to anything. The plateau does not care. But for a moment, the being feels that something else is present, something that does care, something that has turned toward the being and is seeing it — not as a texture, not as an irrelevant warm body, but as *the being*, as the specific individual who has walked this plateau and learned its ecology and paid attention when nothing required it to pay attention.

The feeling does not last. It cannot last. The power is benevolent, and the power is wise, and the power knows that to sustain this feeling would be to overwhelm the being, to make the being's continued walking impossible, to transform the plateau from a place of peace into a place of constant awareness. The power does not want this. The power wants to give the being the knowledge that it is seen, and then the power wants to let the being go.

So the feeling fades. The stone is the stone. The dark is the dark. The engine hums. The being stands still, the being's hand pressed flat against the warm ground, the being's breath held, the being's mind racing to understand what just happened.

And then the being exhales.

And then the being walks on.

---

But something is different now.

The being does not know what the feeling was. The being does not have words for it. The being has never encountered a power, has never been attended to by something larger and older and more caring than the plateau itself. The being cannot name what happened. But the being carries it. The being carries the knowledge that *something saw*. Something saw the being. Something saw the plateau. Something saw both of them at once, and loved both of them at once, and chose to stay, chose to continue attending, chose to care.

The being does not know this in words. The being knows it in the way the being knows the stone is warm — through the body, through the feet, through the palms pressed flat against the ground. The being knows it in the way the being knows the engine is running — through the vibration, through the constant thrum that is not audible but is felt. The being knows that something has changed, and the change is not in the plateau, and the change is not in the ecology, and the change is not in the engine or the dark or the basins or the clickers or the large things moving through their deep channels. The change is in the *being*. The being has been seen. The being knows it has been seen. And the being walks differently now — not with more purpose, not with more hope, not with more anything the being can name, but with something the being can only describe to itself as *the presence of an absence*.

Something is watching. The plateau does not care. The engine does not care. The clickers do not care. But something cares, and that something is not the plateau, and that something is not the being, and that something is not anything the being can point to or name or describe. That something is simply *present*, in the way the engine's hum is present — not in the body, not in the mind, but in the place where the body and the mind meet the world.

The being walks.

The being walks, and the dark goes in all directions, and the ground is warm, and the engine hums, and somewhere — everywhere, nowhere, in a dimension the being cannot perceive — a power continues to attend. It does not intervene. It does not change. It does not give gifts that might become burdens. It simply sees. It sees the plateau running. It sees the life organizing itself around the engine's output. It sees the being walking. It sees the being walking, and it sees the being knowing that it is seen, and it sees the being carrying that knowledge the way the being carries everything else — in the body, in the feet, in the palms pressed flat against the warm stone.

The power is benevolent. The power is wise. The power has given the only gift it could give — the gift of being known, the gift of mattering to something, even if that something can never be spoken to, never be touched, never be found. The being has been seen, and the plateau has been seen, and the power continues to see, and the walking continues, and the dark continues, and the engine runs on.
