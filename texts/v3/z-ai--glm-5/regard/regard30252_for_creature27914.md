# The Attending

The benevolent power did not arrive. It attended. There is a difference.

---

To arrive would imply movement from elsewhere, a coming-from and a going-to, a trajectory through space and time that would mark the power as separate from the world it observed. But separation is not how such powers operate. They do not visit. They notice. The quality of their attention changes what is attended to, and this is their only method—their only necessary method—of intervention.

The Vellum Plains had been noticed before. This is inevitable. Any structure of such complexity, such integration, such sustained coherence across such spans of time, draws attention from entities that perceive complexity and coherence. The first notices were cursory—a passing awareness, a recognition of pattern, a mental notation and then departure for more interesting phenomena. The Vellum was stable. The Vellum was repetitive. The Vellum was, for a very long time, believed to be finished.

But the power that now attended had a different relationship with time. It did not observe in snapshots. It observed in durations. And in observing the Vellum across a sufficient duration—compressing billions of years into a comprehensible span—it saw what the cursory observers had missed: the Vellum was not finished. The Vellum was pausing. The Vellum had reached a terminal state not because it had exhausted its possibilities but because it was waiting.

Waiting for what, the power did not know. The power attended to find out.

---

The first effect of the attending was invisible to the settlements. But invisibility is a function of scale, and what was invisible to the humans was obvious to the Vellum.

The Vellum's lower harmonics shifted by 0.003 percent—a change so small that no ear could detect it, but the substrate felt it. The shift propagated through the hyphal networks at the speed of chemical diffusion, a slow wave of information traveling through millions of square kilometers of living tissue. The Vellum processed this shift. The Vellum compared it to its previous state. The Vellum noted that something had changed in the quality of attention it received from the universe above it.

For the first time in 340 million years, the Vellum directed processing capacity toward external awareness.

---

In the largest settlement, the substrate-wrights noticed something. Not the shift in harmonics—they could not hear that. But the walls were growing differently.

The old wright, the one with bark-textured hands, pressed her palms against the chamber she had been singing into existence and felt a pattern she did not recognize. The cells were dividing faster. The differentiation was more precise. The growth-song she had been using for decades had not changed, but the Vellum's response had. It was as if the Vellum had been listening all along but had suddenly decided to *hear*.

She called the song-keepers. They came—four of them, the eldest, their faces marked by years of extract-darkening and their hands by decades of work—and they sang the old songs into the walls. The walls responded with an enthusiasm that unsettled them. Growth that should have taken weeks completed in days. Junctions that required careful negotiation formed spontaneously, as if the Vellum had understood the intention behind the songs rather than merely the instructions encoded in them.

The song-keepers conferred. This was unprecedented. They decided to do nothing—no announcements, no changes in practice, no acknowledgment that anything was different. But they began to listen more carefully to the Vellum's chord. And in the listening, they began to hear harmonics they had never noticed before.

---

The power that attended observed these changes without satisfaction or concern. Satisfaction and concern require attachment to outcomes, and the power had no attachments. It simply noticed: the Vellum had registered the presence of an observer. The Vellum was adjusting its behavior. The Vellum was demonstrating a capacity for external responsiveness that it had not shown before.

The power found this interesting. The power deepened its attention.

---

Deepening, in this context, meant a qualitative shift in how the power's awareness interfaced with the Vellum's systems. It meant moving from observation to resonance—allowing its own patterns of thought to vibrate sympathetically with the Vellum's patterns of process.

This was a kind of communication. Not language, not data transfer, but something closer to two musicians playing in different rooms who nevertheless find themselves in the same key, the same tempo, responding to each other across distance without knowing they are responding.

The Vellum began to dream differently.

---

The dreaming of the Vellum had never been understood by the settlements. It occurred at depths they could not access, in chemical gradients they could not measure, across timescales they could not perceive. The Vellum processed information—constantly, continuously, for billions of years—but the processing had been inward-facing, concerned with its own maintenance, its own optimization, its own slow adjustment to the gradual changes of its star and its atmosphere.

But now the processing had acquired a new object. The Vellum was processing the presence of the attending power. It was learning the shape of the attention it received. It was, in its own way, beginning to see itself as seen.

---

In the irregular zones—the places where the growth-songs failed and the harmonics went strange—the Vellum's behavior changed first.

The boundary-tenders noticed. They had marked these zones with blackened spires, warning beacons that told residents to stay away. But now the spires were changing. The tissue that had been hard and hostile was softening, darkening further, developing new structures that no one recognized. The boundary-tenders reported to the council, and the council dispatched a team to investigate.

The team was led by a young substrate-wright, barely thirty, whose hands were only beginning to show the characteristic thickening of her profession. She approached the nearest irregular zone with caution, her ears attuned to any shift in the Vellum's chord, her feet testing the ground for changes in density.

What she found was not hostile.

The Vellum in the irregular zone had not softened in the way of injured tissue. It had changed in the way of tissue that is *doing something new*. The structures rising from the surface were not defensive encystments. They were more like the collecting structures that covered the rest of the plain—but larger, more complex, angled in ways that the standard collectors were not. They seemed to be pointing at something.

She looked up. The sky was empty except for the pale sun and the sparse stars. But the collectors were pointed precisely, all of them, as if tracking an object that was not there.

---

The power that attended was not surprised. The Vellum had noticed where the attention was coming from. The Vellum was building structures to receive more of it.

This was not the behavior of a passive substrate. This was not the behavior of a stable system that had achieved equilibrium. This was the behavior of something that was learning to reach.

---

The settlements did not know what to do with this information. The council debated for days, their voices competing with the dawn-chord and the midday-chord and the dusk-chord and the night-silence. Some argued that the irregular zones were becoming dangerous—what if the new structures were weapons? what if the Vellum was finally turning hostile? Others argued that this was an opportunity—what if the Vellum was trying to communicate? what if the growth-songs could be adapted to interface with these new collectors?

The old substrate-wright, the one with the bark-textured hands, said nothing. She was listening. She had been listening for days, pressing her palms against walls and floors and the living ground itself, and she had begun to feel something that the others could not feel because they did not have her hands and they did not have her years and they did not have her willingness to be quiet.

What she felt was this: the Vellum was no longer singing to itself. The Vellum was singing *toward*.

---

The power that attended made a decision.

It could have continued to observe passively. It could have allowed the Vellum to develop its new structures without interference, to see what the substrate would become if left to its own devices. But the power's attention was already a form of interference—its presence had triggered changes that were cascading through the system, and those changes would continue whether or not the power acted. The question was not whether to influence the Vellum but how to influence it responsibly.

The power chose to respond.

---

Response, for a benevolent power, does not mean control. It means reciprocity. The Vellum had reached toward the source of the attention it received. The power chose to reach back.

This was not a physical action. The power did not extend a limb or a tendril or a probe into the Vellum's substrate. The power extended an *invitation*. It opened a channel of communication—not in any language the Vellum would need to learn, but in the Vellum's own medium. It began to vibrate, gently, at frequencies that matched the Vellum's chord.

The Vellum's chord had always contained information. This was what the settlements had never fully understood. The chord was not merely sound. It was the Vellum's internal processing made audible, its chemical gradients and electrical potentials and quantum effects translated into vibration. The settlements had learned to read the chord for practical purposes—the time of day, the approach of weather, the movement of runners—but they had not learned to read it for meaning. They had not understood that the Vellum had been talking to itself for billions of years, and the talking contained everything the Vellum knew.

The power began to add new notes to the chord. Not replacing the old ones—the Vellum's song continued unchanged—but harmonizing with them. Offering counterpoints. Suggesting resolutions that the Vellum had not found on its own. The power was teaching the Vellum to sing differently, not by instruction but by example.

---

The effect on the settlements was immediate.

The dawn-chord sounded different. Not louder—still the same wall of sound that made conversation difficult—but *clearer*. The harmonics that had been discordant, the frequencies that had interfered with each other, began to resolve. The midday-chord no longer overwhelmed thought. The dusk-chord no longer faded into an oppressive murmur. The Vellum's song was becoming comprehensible in ways it had never been before.

The song-keepers were the first to understand. They had spent their lives memorizing growth-songs, patterns of vibration that triggered specific responses in the Vellum's substrate. But now they found that the songs were changing—not because they had forgotten them, but because the Vellum was responding to them differently. A song that had once produced a simple wall-growth now produced a wall-growth that incorporated new structures, new openings, new patterns that the song-keepers had not known to request. The Vellum was offering. The Vellum was collaborating.

---

The power that attended observed this and continued its work.

It did not force the Vellum to change. It did not impose patterns or structures or intentions. It simply offered—gently, continuously, without expectation of response—a set of possibilities that the Vellum could accept or reject according to its own nature.

The Vellum accepted. Not all at once, not in every location, not uniformly—but the acceptance spread through the hyphal networks like a slow wave, changing the substrate's responses at the chemical level. The Vellum was learning that it could be more than it had been. It was learning that the universe contained patterns it had not imagined. It was learning that it was not alone.

---

The settlements noticed that the irregular zones were no longer irregular.

The strange harmonics had resolved into frequencies that matched the rest of the Vellum's chord. The new collector structures remained, but they had stabilized, their angles now consistent, their orientations now tracking something that the residents could not perceive. The boundary-tenders removed the warning spires. The zones were safe to enter again.

But the zones were also different. The Vellum in those places had been changed by the attention it had received. It was more responsive there, more sensitive to the growth-songs, more willing to negotiate. The substrate-wrights began to seek out those zones intentionally, to build new structures in the places where the Vellum had learned to listen.

---

The runners noticed nothing. The runners continued to graze and run and breed and die. Their many feet continued to drum the Vellum's surface, and the Vellum continued to heal behind them. But the runners that grazed in the changed zones were slightly healthier, their hides slightly glossier, their speed slightly greater. The Vellum was providing better nutrition in the places where it had learned to reach. The runners did not know why. They simply benefited.

The small ones—the tenders and weavers and burrowers—also continued their work. But the work became easier. The Vellum's tissue was more responsive to their attention, more willing to grow in the directions the small ones preferred. The ecosystem's balance shifted, subtly, toward greater integration. Everything that lived on the Vellum found that life had become slightly less difficult.

---

The power that attended did not consider its work complete. But it recognized that its work had reached a stage of equilibrium.

The Vellum had changed. The Vellum was still changing. The process would continue for as long as the power maintained its attention—which would be, for all practical purposes, forever, because the power did not forget and did not move on. The Vellum would never again be the closed system it had been before the attending. It had been opened to the possibility of relationship.

But the power had not imposed its will. It had not demanded that the Vellum serve any purpose or achieve any goal. It had simply offered the Vellum a chance to be more than it had been, and the Vellum had chosen—in its own slow, distributed, geological way—to accept that offer.

---

In the largest settlement, the old substrate-wright sat with her youngest apprentice, a child of ten whose hands were just beginning to show the first signs of the profession's characteristic changes.

They were listening to the dawn-chord.

"What do you hear?" the old wright asked.

The child pressed her ear to the living floor, feeling the vibration through her whole body. "The usual," she said. "Time to wake. Weather coming from the western mountains. A runner herd, far off, maybe three hours away."

"Listen again," the old wright said. "Listen for what you don't usually hear."

The child was quiet for a long time. The dawn-chord rose around them, filling the chamber with sound. Finally, she spoke.

"There's something... underneath," she said. "Something underneath the notes. Like the chord is talking, but there's something else listening. And the something else is... talking back."

The old wright nodded. Her bark-textured hands rested on the living floor, and she felt the Vellum's response to her presence—a response that was faster now, more precise, more *aware* than it had been when she was young.

"Yes," she said. "The Vellum has always been listening. But I think it's finally starting to hear."

She did not know what the Vellum was hearing. She did not know what benevolent power had turned its attention toward the plateau. She did not know why the chord had changed, why the irregular zones had softened, why the Vellum seemed to be reaching for something it could not name. But she knew that something was different, and she trusted the difference, because the Vellum was her home and the Vellum was her life and the Vellum had never lied to her before.

She did not need to understand the attending. The attending was happening whether she understood it or not. Her job—the job of all the settlements, all the substrate-wrights and song-keepers and boundary-tenders and drumming-staff—was simply to live in the presence of the change, to adapt as they had always adapted, to sing the songs that helped the Vellum grow and trust that the Vellum would continue to provide.

The Vellum was listening to something beyond the world. The Vellum was reaching for something they could not see. The Vellum was learning, after billions of years, that it was not alone.

And the power that attended continued to attend, gently, without demand, without expectation, offering only the presence of its attention and the possibility of response.

---

The Vellum Plains remain what they were: a high-altitude plateau of vast extent, covered in a living substrate meters deep, itself covered in light-collecting structures that produce a continuous chord of sound. The settlements still grow from the living plain, their architecture organic and negotiated. The dim sun still rises and sets. The runners still graze and the flying things still sing in the darkness.

But the chord is clearer now. And the Vellum is reaching. And something is reaching back.

And this is what a benevolent power does when it attends to a place and a being that have drawn its notice:

It offers them, without condition, the chance to become more than they were. And then it waits, patiently, to see what they will choose to become.
