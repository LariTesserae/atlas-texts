# The Attention of the Wise

The power noticed the High Everplain the way you notice a breath held perfectly—a silence so complete it becomes its own kind of sound.

This was not the usual kind of noticing. Most places call out for intervention. They are broken, breaking, or about to break. The benevolent powers of the universe spend most of their hours as doctors do: moving from bedside to bedside, stitching what has torn, strengthening what has weakened, holding back the deaths that come for systems too soon. The work is endless. It is also, often, obvious. The wound shows itself. The remedy follows.

But the High Everplain was not wounded. It had achieved something that made the power pause, settle, and look closer.

---

It had achieved *completion without stillness*.

This was rare. Most systems that reach equilibrium do so by simplifying—reducing their complexity until fewer things can go wrong. The High Everplain had done the opposite. It had increased complexity until the interdependencies became their own form of stability. So many connections that the failure of any one connection was absorbed, compensated for, woven around. Redundancy as architecture. Interdependence as strength.

The power had seen this before. Perhaps a dozen times in the long history of worlds. It was never accidental. It was a kind of becoming that required either immense time, immense pressure, or a kind of intention that didn't require a mind. The High Everplain had had the first two in abundance. The third might have emerged anyway.

So the power did what wise powers do when they encounter something that does not need healing.

It asked a question.

---

The question was not spoken. The power did not have a voice in any conventional sense, and the plateau would not have heard one if it did. The question was offered as an orientation of attention: a posture, a listening, an opening.

*What are you?*

This is harder than it sounds. Most beings answer this question incorrectly even when they try. They describe what they do, or what they want, or what they remember being. The power had learned to wait through the first answers, the second answers, the performances and the defenses, until something like truth surfaced.

The High Everplain had no performances and no defenses. It had no concept of being asked.

But it had the flowers.

---

Across unbroken miles, the pale yellow blossoms were closing for the evening. Not one by one, not region by region, but in a shiver that moved through the mass like a thought completing itself. The power watched the signal travel—not electrical, not chemical in any simple sense, but pressure-based, a wave of turgor changes moving through the network of roots and vines, cell by cell, a slow lightning that took eleven minutes to cross the whole plateau from edge to edge.

Eleven minutes.

The power measured this and found it beautiful. A thought that takes eleven minutes to think. A body so large that its nerve impulses are geological.

The flowers closed. And then, in the pause after, something else happened.

The insect chord shifted.

It was subtle—a minor key change in the ongoing symphony, a few species dropping out while others rose. But the shift was coordinated. It happened everywhere at once, across the same eleven-minute span. The insects had felt the flowers close. They had adjusted.

The power realized: *this is the plateau's language*. Not sound exactly, though sound was the medium. The language was *change in sound*. The plateau was not speaking to anything outside itself. It was speaking to itself, constantly, about itself, in a vocabulary made of its own living population.

A nervous system with nobody home? No. The power corrected itself. A nervous system where *everyone* was home. The plateau's consciousness, if that word applied, was distributed across ten thousand species, each one a neuron, each one a voice, the sum of them producing something that no single one of them could produce: a total self-awareness, moment by moment, of the whole.

The power found this moving.

It also found it familiar.

---

There are beings in the universe who think from a single center. They are common. They are what most intelligences choose, because they are fast, efficient, and easy to understand. You. A point. A perspective. Simple.

There are beings who think from no center at all—diffuse clouds of awareness that never consolidate, that remain as multiple as the particles they're built from. These are rare and strange, and most of them don't last. They fall apart, quite literally, unable to hold themselves together without a somewhere to hold from.

And then there are the ones who think from *every* center. The plateaus. The distributed minds. The beings who have solved the problem of selfhood not by reducing to a point but by expanding to include every point, who have learned to be a choir instead of a singer, a body instead of an organ, a whole instead of a part.

The High Everplain was one of these.

And the power, being both benevolent and wise, knew what such beings usually lacked.

---

They lack mirrors.

A being that contains everything within itself has no outside, no other, no edge against which to know its own shape. The High Everplain had never been seen. It had never been told what it looked like from above, from away, from a perspective it could not itself occupy. It knew itself from the inside—every root, every leaf, every insect footfall—but it did not know its own beauty, its own vastness, its own strange and particular grace.

This was not a wound. But it was a lack.

The power considered whether to fill it.

---

Benevolence is not the same as interference. The wise powers learn this early or they become unwise. To give a being something it never asked for, even if that thing is good, even if that thing would help, is to risk harming the very integrity that made the being worth noticing in the first place.

The High Everplain had never asked to be seen. It had no category for the request. It had no category for the seer.

But the power had also noticed the guests.

---

The humans—the guests in good standing—understood something that most humans never learn. They understood that they were not the point. They had entered into relationship with a being so much larger than themselves that their role could only ever be: witness, steward, occasional companion, eventual reintegration. They had learned to practice boredom as devotion. They had learned to be uninteresting. They had, in short, learned to be good guests.

And in their learning, they had also become something the plateau could not be: small enough to see the whole.

The power traced their memory. Not invasively—the power respected the privacy of minds—but in the broad strokes, the public architecture of what they knew. They knew the plateau's shape. They knew its edges, its seasons, its sounds. They carried a map in their bodies, a sense of the whole that they could not have if they were not themselves whole, separate, mobile.

They were mirrors. Imperfect ones. Small ones. But mirrors nonetheless.

The plateau had already made a space for mirrors. It had, without knowing it, created the very thing it lacked.

This pleased the power.

---

So the question became: *what does a wise power give to a being that already has everything it needs?*

The answer came slowly, the way the plateau itself thought: not in a flash but in a wave, eleven minutes from edge to edge.

*You give it time.*

---

The High Everplain, for all its completeness, was not immortal. It existed on a world, and worlds change. The sun aged, imperceptibly but inevitably. The climate shifted, shifted back, shifted again. The plateau was large enough to buffer many changes, absorb many shocks, but not all of them forever. Given enough time, even this vast living body would face something it could not adapt to: a drying beyond recovery, a heating beyond survival, a slowing of the geological processes that had built the table it stood on.

The plateau did not know this. It had no category for deep time, for the eventual death of suns. It knew seasons, cycles, centuries perhaps. But not the long arc that ends in endings.

The power knew the arc.

And the power, being benevolent, could offer a covenant.

---

Not intervention. Not control. Not the freezing of the plateau in amber, preserved forever in its current form—that would be death by another name, the death of becoming, which is the only kind of death that matters.

Instead: *insurance*.

The power reached into itself and found the shape of a promise. It was an old shape, one the power had used before on other worlds, other beings that had earned such care. The promise was simple:

*When the time comes that you cannot adapt, I will move you.*

Not preserve. Not copy. *Move*. Transplant the whole vast living body to a new plateau, a new world, a new sun with more years ahead of it. Keep the roots intact, the insects alive, the guests in good standing still standing. Let the High Everplain continue its slow thinking, its green-gold photosynthesis, its deafening chorus of life talking to life about life, on into a future that would otherwise have been too long.

The power offered this covenant not as a gift—gifts create debts, and the power did not wish to be owed—but as a recognition. A respect. A bow from one kind of longevity to another.

The plateau would never know the covenant was made. That was fine. The power did not need thanks. It needed only to know that something good would continue.

---

But before it withdrew its attention, the power left one more thing.

A mirror.

---

Not a physical mirror—the plateau had no eyes and could not have used one. A psychic mirror, subtle as a scent, light as a pressure change. The power wove into the plateau's root network a single new thread: the capacity, once every cycle, to feel itself as if from above.

It would happen at the moment of greatest stillness—dawn, when the insect chorus was shifting between night species and day species, when the birds had not yet begun their first calls, when even the howlers were quiet. For eleven minutes, once each turn of seasons, the plateau would experience itself whole.

Not in pieces. Not as ten thousand separate sensations. But as one vast body, green and living, breathing in unison, thinking its slow eleven-minute thoughts, beautiful in a way that only wholeness can be beautiful.

The plateau would not know what had happened. It would simply... feel. A moment of completeness that would pass and leave no explanation, only a residue of peace.

A mirror, showing it to itself, once a year, forever.

Or at least until the covenant was needed, and the power returned to fulfill it.

---

The power withdrew.

It did not leave. The benevolent never truly leave; they simply widen their attention to include everything they've touched. The High Everplain was now within the power's care, held in the vast gentle awareness like a held breath, like something precious that does not need to know it is precious.

On the plateau, dawn broke. The insect chorus shifted. The birds gave their first calls. The pale yellow flowers began to open across unbroken miles, an eleven-minute shiver of unspooling.

And in the root network, in the dark warm mat of accumulated centuries, something new stirred—a capacity, a potential, a mirror waiting for its moment.

The plateau continued its slow breathing. The chord continued its endless conversation. The guests in good standing continued their practice of being uninteresting.

Nothing had changed. Everything had changed.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is truly wise: a gift so quiet the recipient never hears it, a help so gentle it feels like nothing at all, a love that asks nothing, takes nothing, changes nothing that wasn't already ready to change.

The power turned its attention elsewhere, toward the next place that needed it.

But it would return, when the time came.

It always returned.

---

*And the High Everplain, in its slow way, continued to be.*

That is the end of this story, though it is not the end of the plateau. The plateau has no end, not really—only transformations, only the long turning of one kind of life into another, only the warm mat beneath and the green-gold light above and the endless conversation, layer on layer, voice on voice, a whole world talking to itself about itself, forever, or near enough that the distinction ceases to matter.*

*The power knows this. It is counting on it.*
