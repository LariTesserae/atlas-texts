# What the Attention of a Benevolent and Wise Power Looks Like at the Gallows Garden of Yu'uthaal

---

## I.

First, it waits.

This is the first thing to understand about a power that is both benevolent and wise: it does not rush. It has the patience of someone who has learned that most things in the universe are already doing exactly what they need to do and that the kindest intervention is often the one that looks, from the outside, like nothing at all.

It comes to the defile — the narrow cut in the ridgeline, the door the mountain forgot to close — and it does not pass through. Not yet. It stands on the far side, on the ridge, in the wind, and it listens. It listens the way the Gardener listens: with its whole body, with the full weight of its attention, drawing the basin in and holding it.

It can hear the breathing from here. It can hear the slow drip of condensation from leaf to stone. It can hear the chemical conversations of the insects, though these are not words in any language it has ever spoken. It can hear the tree-kin in their branches, their soft spines lengthening by fractions of a millimeter, their jawline-patches pressed against bark, absorbing the vibrations of the Gardener's nightly remembering. It can hear the lizards on their stones, their translucent bodies catching fog-light, their oversized eyes holding the image of the triangular doorway with a fidelity that would astonish any biologist who happened to be measuring it.

It listens for a long time. A day. A week. However long a benevolent and wise power measures time, which is not the way we measure it, which is not the way the Gardener measures it either, but which is, perhaps, closer to hers than to ours.

It is in no hurry because it has heard something in the breathing that tells it the system is not failing. The Gardener is tired — yes, of course she is tired, she has been breathing for centuries — but she is not desperate. She is not calling for help. She is not even calling for company. She is doing what she has always done: tending, remembering, preparing. She is cycling through the names of the builders in her long nights, and she is distributing her attention across the ferns and the lichens and the insects and the birds, and she is watching her children descend, inch by inch, year by year, toward the shape they will need to inhabit when she is gone.

The power listens and hears a system that is working.
