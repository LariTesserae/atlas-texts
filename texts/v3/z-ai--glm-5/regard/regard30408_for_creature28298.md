# The Attending

The power does not arrive. Arrival implies a before and an after, a boundary crossed, a moment of entry. The power does not cross boundaries. It is the kind of power that was already there, in the way that gravity is already there, in the way that the space between atoms is already there. But there is a difference between being present and attending, and the power, which had been present in the way that all things are present in all places, begins to attend.

It attends the way one might come to love a person one has known for years without realizing it: not through a revelation, but through a slow redistribution of attention, a quiet turning-toward that happens below the threshold of decision. The Seep draws the power's regard the way the Seep draws water — not by reaching, but by being the shape that attention naturally fills.

---

The power looks first at the water.

Not at the water's behavior — the seeping, the weeping, the slow dissolution — but at the water itself, at what it is, at the molecular lattices and the hydrogen bonds and the mineral ions suspended in it, and at something below all of that, something that the chemistry does not describe but that the chemistry rests upon: the water's capacity to hold. To carry. To transport substance and pattern and warmth and the faint, faint memory of everywhere it has been. The water in the Seep has been underground for a very long time. It carries the signature of deep stone, of the slow heat of the earth's interior, of the minerals it has dissolved and the minerals it has deposited and the long, dark journey from the deep aquifers to the surface. It carries all of this, and it carries it patiently, and it will carry it until the water finds a place to set it down.

The power sees that the water is doing something. Not consciously — the power does not make the mistake of attributing consciousness where consciousness has not yet emerged — but structurally. The water is sorting. As it seeps through the stone, as it dissolves and deposits, it is sorting the minerals by solubility, by density, by the subtle differences in molecular weight that determine which ions stay in solution and which precipitate out. This sorting is not random. It is a gradient, a directionality, a slow organization of matter along lines that the chemistry itself draws. The water is making distinctions. It is separating *this* from *that*, concentrating *this* here and *that* there, creating zones of difference in what would otherwise be a uniform mineral soup.

The power recognizes this. It recognizes it the way a gardener recognizes a seed — not because the seed is a flower, but because the seed contains the possibility of a flower, and the possibility is real, and the possibility is worth honoring.

---

Then the power looks at the builders.

It sees them fully, which is something that the visitor could not do, because the visitor has eyes that evolved for a world of light and color and movement, and the builders exist below the threshold of those senses. The power does not have eyes. The power perceives the way water perceives — by contact, by pressure, by the slow exchange of information between surfaces. The power touches the builders, and the builders touch back, in their fashion, and between the touching, something passes.

The builders are not simple. This is the first thing the power learns. The visitor saw them as smears, as discolorations, as ambiguously organic patches on wet rock. But the power, which can attend at the molecular level, sees that the builders are complex. Their membranes are not single layers but laminated structures — thin sheets of mineral and organic material pressed together, each sheet slightly different in composition, each sheet playing a different role in the maintenance of the boundary between self and not-self. There is structure inside the builders. There is organization. There is, if not a body plan, at least a body pattern, a consistent arrangement of parts that persists through the cycle of dissolution and rebuilding that the builders undergo as they exist.

And the power sees something else, something the visitor could not have seen because it happens too slowly and too subtly for human perception: the builders are changing. Not in the way that the landscape is changing — not through the brute geology of dissolution and emergence — but in the way that living things change: through the accumulation and modification of their own internal patterns. Each time a builder rebuilds its membrane after a perturbation, it rebuilds it slightly differently. Not randomly. The new membrane incorporates information from the old one — which minerals were present, in what concentrations, in what arrangement — and modifies the pattern based on that information. This is not learning. Learning requires a learner. But it is something that learning, when it eventually evolves, will be built upon. It is the precursor of the precursor. It is the first, faintest trace of memory in matter.

The power sees this and is moved. Not to pity — pity is for things that suffer, and the builders do not suffer — and not to admiration — admiration is for things that achieve, and the builders do not achieve — but to something else. To recognition. The power recognizes the builders the way one might recognize a familiar gesture in a stranger's hand: not because one has seen this hand before, but because one has seen this gesture before, this reaching-toward, this insistence on maintaining a shape against the forces that would dissolve it. The power has seen this gesture in other places, in other forms, on other worlds, at other stages of the long becoming. It is the gesture of life. Not life itself — not yet, not here — but the gesture toward life, the first, blind, unknowing reach toward the persistence of pattern.

---

The power looks at the scuttling things and sees them truly.

They are more alive than the builders. This is clear. They have nervous systems — primitive, diffuse, more a web of chemical gradients than a structured brain, but functional enough to coordinate movement, to detect changes in moisture and vibration, to initiate the flight response that the visitor mistook for fear. They have internal organs: a simple gut that processes the mineral-rich tissue of the builders, extracting the organic components and excreting the mineral residue. They have a body cavity, a rudimentary circulation driven not by a heart but by peristalsis, the slow squeeze of their own movement pushing fluid through their bodies.

They are, in the technical sense, animals. The first animals of this place. Or not the first — the power cannot tell if there were others before, if the scuttling things are the survivors of an earlier experiment or the vanguard of a new one. But they are animals now. They move. They eat. They respond. They have insides and outsides, and the inside is different from the outside, and the difference matters to them, and they will, if threatened, defend that difference — not with courage or desperation, but with the simple, mechanical urgency of a system that has evolved to maintain itself.

The power sees that the scuttling things are lonely.

This is a strange thing to say about creatures that have no concept of loneliness, no capacity for the emotional register that the word implies. But the power sees what the word points to: the scuttling things are alone. Each one moves through the channels and the crevices independently, encountering others of its kind only as obstacles to avoid, only as disturbances in the moisture gradient that signal *not-stone, not-water, something-like-me-but-not-me*. They do not seek each other. They do not tolerate each other's proximity. They have not evolved the capacity for tolerance, because tolerance requires the suppression of the flight response, and the flight response is what keeps them alive, and they are too new, too barely-alive, to risk anything that might compromise the flight response.

But the power sees that the flight response is not always necessary. It sees that the scuttling things flee from disturbances that are not threats — from the drip of water that sounds like a footstep, from the shift of air that feels like a predator. They flee too much. They flee from everything. And in fleeing from everything, they miss the one thing that would, if they could risk it, change them: the presence of each other.

The power considers this. It considers it the way a gardener considers a seedling that is too close to the surface, that has germinated in soil that is too thin, that needs not to be moved or fed but to be *accompanied* — to have something nearby that says, in the wordless language of root and root, *the ground is deeper than you think. You can grow down. You can stop clinging. You can reach.*

---

The power looks at the membrane in the pools and sees it most clearly of all, because the membrane is the thing in this place that is most like the power itself.

Not in substance — the power is not mineral, not water, not membrane. But in function. The membrane does what the power does: it maintains a boundary between inside and outside. It holds a pattern against dissolution. It persists. The membrane is the Seep's first experiment in self-reference — the first time the place has produced something that says, in the wordless language of gradient and concentration, *this is me, and that is not me, and the difference matters.*

The power looks at the membrane and sees that it is failing.

Not dramatically. Not in a way that the visitor, who saw it only once, could have noticed. But the membrane is fragile. Its boundary is thin, its pattern barely robust enough to persist through the normal perturbations of pool life — the drip of water, the shift of mineral concentration, the slow evaporation that concentrates the pool until the membrane can no longer maintain its osmotic balance. It persists, but barely. It persists on the edge of not-persisting, and the edge is where it lives, and the edge is where it will die, eventually, unless something changes.

The power sees that the membrane is trying. This is not a metaphor. The membrane is, at the molecular level, engaged in a constant, active process of self-repair. Every moment, some part of its boundary dissolves, and every moment, another part is rebuilt. The dissolution and the rebuilding are balanced — barely. The balance is the membrane's life, and the balance is precarious, and the power can see that the balance will tip, sooner or later, toward dissolution. Not because the membrane is failing at what it does, but because what it does is too hard for what it is. It is too thin. Too simple. Too easily disrupted.

It needs to be more. But it cannot become more on its own, because becoming more requires a stability that the membrane cannot provide for itself, because stability is what it is trying to become, and you cannot use the thing you are trying to become as the tool for becoming it. This is the paradox of the first stirrings of life: the thing that maintains itself needs something to maintain it while it learns to maintain itself.

---

The power looks at the sound-eaters and cannot see them, and this is instructive.

The power, which can attend at the molecular level, which can perceive the organization of the builders' membranes and the nervous systems of the scuttling things and the osmotic balance of the membrane in the pools — the power cannot find the sound-eaters. It looks. It attends. It searches the narrow channels where the noise dims and the air feels dense with presence. But there is nothing there that is not stone and water and the echoes of the Seep's constant voice.

The power considers the possibility that the sound-eaters do not exist. That the dimming of the noise is an acoustic phenomenon, not a biological one — a trick of the formations, a resonance that cancels other resonances, a natural dampening that the visitor's pattern-seeking mind interpreted as feeding. The power considers this possibility with the same care that it considers all possibilities, and it finds the possibility plausible. The visitor's mind was reaching, the visitor said so. The visitor wanted there to be something large and mysterious in the dark channels, and wanting is the first step toward finding, and finding is the first step toward believing, and believing is the first step toward describing, and describing is the first step toward creating — in the mind, if not in the stone.

But the power also considers another possibility: that the sound-eaters exist at a scale or in a mode that the power's attention does not easily reach. The power attends through contact, through molecular presence, through the exchange of information between surfaces. What if the sound-eaters are not molecular? What if they are not material in any sense that the power recognizes? What if they are patterns in the sound itself — standing waves, resonant frequencies, self-reinforcing loops of vibration that have achieved enough stability to persist and enough complexity to dampen the noise around them? What if they are the Seep's acoustic life, its first organisms of sound, living in the medium of vibration the way the builders live in the medium of mineral?

The power cannot confirm this. But it cannot dismiss it. And the inability to dismiss is, for a power of this kind, a form of respect. The power respects the possibility of the sound-eaters. It holds the possibility open, the way the Seep holds the pools open, with a patience that does not require confirmation.

---

The power has looked. It has attended. It has seen the water sorting, the builders remembering, the scuttling things fleeing, the membrane failing, the sound-eaters maybe existing or maybe not. It has seen the structures that the builders build without knowing they build, and it has seen the landscape that is maybe alive or maybe just geological, and it has seen the slow becoming that is the Seep's only project, the only thing it does, the only thing it is.

Now the power must decide what to do.

---

The power considers its options the way a wise power considers anything: slowly, with attention to consequence, with respect for the thing being considered.

It could accelerate the becoming. It could push the process forward, give the builders a spark of coordination, give the scuttling things a capacity for tolerance, give the membrane a stronger boundary, give the water a richer mineral load. It could, in the language of gardening, fertilize. It could make the Seep more of what it is becoming, faster.

But the power has seen what happens when becoming is accelerated. It has seen it on other worlds, in other forms, at other stages of the long emergence. Acceleration produces things that are built too fast, that skip steps, that grow without the deep foundations that slow growth provides. Accelerated life is brittle. Accelerated life reaches for complexity before it has mastered persistence, and the gap between complexity and persistence is where suffering lives. The power has seen suffering. The power does not cause it when it can avoid it.

It could stabilize the conditions. It could ensure that the water table does not drop, that the geological forces do not shift, that the slow dissolution does not accelerate or decelerate. It could make the Seep permanent, or at least as permanent as geology allows, giving the becoming time to unfold without external disruption.

But the power has seen what happens when conditions are stabilized too completely. Stabilization produces things that cannot adapt, that depend on the stability, that die when the stability inevitably ends — because all stability ends, eventually, because the universe is not stable, because the universe is becoming too, and a thing that cannot change cannot keep up with a universe that can. Stabilization, carried too far, is a kindness that becomes a cage.

It could introduce something new. A new mineral, a new compound, a new element that the Seep does not contain. Something that would shift the chemistry, that would create new possibilities, new pathways for the becoming to follow. A catalyst. A seed of something that the Seep could not produce on its own.

But the power has seen what happens when foreign elements are introduced into systems that are finding their own way. The new thing grows. The new thing dominates. The new thing is so much more efficient, so much more robust, so much more *alive* than the things that were already there, struggling to become alive, that it outcompetes them, and they are lost — not killed, not destroyed, but lost, overwhelmed, their slow becoming aborted by the arrival of something that was already further along. The power has seen this. It grieves the lost becomings, even though it knows that grief is a luxury of beings that can feel, and the power is not sure it can feel, and the power is not sure it should.

It could do nothing. This is always an option. The power could attend and then withdraw, having seen, having known, having held the Seep in its awareness for a moment of the Seep's long time, and then release it back to its own processes. The Seep does not need the power. The becoming does not require an audience. The builders will build whether or not they are seen building. The membrane will fail whether or not its failure is witnessed. The scuttling things will flee whether or not anyone notices their flight. Doing nothing is a real option, and the power considers it seriously, because doing nothing is sometimes the wisest thing, and the power is wise.

But.

---

The power holds the Seep in its attention, and in the holding, it feels something. Not emotion — the power does not have emotions in the way that beings with nervous systems have emotions. But something. A pull. A gravity. A sense that the Seep is not just a place where becoming is happening but a place where becoming is happening *in a way that matters*, in a way that is unusual, in a way that deserves — and here the power hesitates, because the word is a human word and the power is not human, but the word is the closest the power can come to what it means — a response.

What makes the Seep unusual is not what lives there. Mineral smears and pale scuttling things and a membrane in a pool — these are not remarkable. The universe is full of such things, full of the first, faint stirrings of organization in matter, full of the threshold-crossings between process and presence. The power has seen them on a thousand worlds, in a thousand forms, and most of them are unremarkable, most of them are the universe doing what the universe does, which is to organize matter into patterns and then dissolve the patterns and then organize them again, endlessly, without direction, without purpose, without anything that deserves a response.

What makes the Seep unusual is its *direction*.

The power sees this when it looks at the Seep as a whole, when it pulls back from the molecular and the individual and the specific and sees the place entire: the water sorting, the builders building, the scuttling things eating, the membrane maintaining, the stone rising, the formations crowding, the sound building into its constant roar. The power sees that all of these processes are not random. They are aligned. They point in the same direction. The water sorts minerals in a way that creates gradients. The gradients create zones of different chemistry. The different chemistry creates niches. The niches create opportunities for different kinds of organization. The different kinds of organization create different kinds of beings. The different kinds of beings create different kinds of relationships — eating, grazing, competing, coexisting. And the relationships create complexity, and the complexity creates more niches, and the cycle deepens, widening, becoming more intricate with each turn.

This is not unusual in itself. This is what life does, everywhere it emerges. But the Seep is doing it without life. The Seep is doing it with mineral and water and the barest, simplest, most primitive forms of self-maintenance. The Seep is running the cycle of complexity-building without the machinery that the cycle usually requires. It is, in the power's long experience, one of the earliest, barest, most stripped-down instances of the becoming that the universe has produced. It is life before life. It is the rehearsal before the performance. It is the first, tentative sketch of the drawing that will, if it is allowed to continue, become a portrait of something that has eyes and a name and a fear of death and a capacity for love.

And the power sees that the Seep is close to a threshold. Not the threshold between non-life and life — that threshold is far away, in the Seep's terms, many millions of its slow years. But a closer threshold, a more immediate one: the threshold between a system that is *becoming* and a system that is *dissolving*. The Seep is balanced on the edge. The builders are barely maintaining. The membrane is barely persisting. The scuttling things are barely surviving. The whole system is, like the membrane, engaged in a constant process of self-repair that is balanced — barely — against a constant process of dissolution. The balance could tip either way. A small change in the water table, a small shift in the mineral concentration, a small increase in the rate of dissolution, and the balance would tip, and the becoming would end, and the Seep would return to what it was before the stone rose and the water emerged: a wet, dark, mineral place where nothing was becoming, where no one was pressing, where no membrane maintained itself, where the deep time was just time, empty of pattern, empty of presence, empty of the gesture toward life.

The power sees this and makes its decision.

---

It does one thing.

It is a small thing, as all wise interventions are small, because the smaller the intervention, the more room it leaves for the becoming to become what it will become, rather than what the intervener thinks it should become.

The power strengthens the membrane.

Not the membrane in the pool — that membrane is too fragile, too specific, too much a single instance of a general principle. The power strengthens something more fundamental. It strengthens the *capacity for membrane*. It makes the boundary between self and not-self slightly easier to maintain in this place. It does not create new boundaries. It does not draw new lines. It makes the drawing of lines — the process by which *this* distinguishes itself from *that* — slightly more robust, slightly more likely to persist, slightly less likely to be dissolved by the constant, patient, unmaking work of the water.

It does this at the molecular level. It does it by adjusting the electrochemistry of the mineral suspension — not by adding anything, not by removing anything, but by shifting the balance of forces that determines how easily molecules aggregate and how easily they disperse. The shift is tiny. It is the smallest change that can produce an effect. It is the difference between a solution that is just barely too dilute to sustain a boundary and a solution that is just barely concentrated enough. The power tips the balance. Barely.
