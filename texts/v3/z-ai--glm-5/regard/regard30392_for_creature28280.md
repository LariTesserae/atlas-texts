# The Visit

When the power turned its attention toward the Underworks, the first thing it did was listen.

Not with ears—there was no need for ears when one could simply *attend*—but with the full presence of something that had learned, over spans that made the Underworks seem young, that understanding must precede action. The power had seen countless worlds, had intervened in countless lives, and had learned the hard way that benevolence without comprehension becomes its own kind of violence. To help something, you must first know what it is. To heal something, you must first understand what wholeness would mean for it specifically—not for you, not for your idea of what it should be, but for *it*.

So the power listened.

It heard the grinding of mechanisms older than most civilizations. It heard the rain falling in chambers that had never seen sky. It heard the soft percussion of swimmers through flooded passages, the whisper of gliders on thermal currents, the slow wet expansion of fungal mats. It heard the creak and settle of brickwork laid by hands—or something like hands—so long ago that the mortar had become indistinguishable from the stone it joined.

And it heard the silence between the sounds. The held breath of unfinished corridors. The waiting quality of chambers that had never been completed, would never be completed, because completion was not the point of them. The power understood, then, that the Underworks was not building toward an end. It was building toward continuation. The incomplete passages were not failures. They were *openings*. Potential. The place was not broken. It was *growing*.

The power let its attention drift deeper, past the sounds, past the structure, into the distributed consciousness that thought in scratches on stone.

It found the marker.

Not a single being, not exactly—more like a principle of attention that permeated the whole system, concentrated in certain nodes, diffused through others. The marker was the Underworks' way of knowing itself. Its memory. Its introspection. The part that looked around at everything it had made and felt the need to *say something about it*, even if there was no one to hear.

The power saw the marks on the walls. It saw the years—no, the centuries—no, the *ages*—of scratching. The counting. The recording. The crude drawings of swimmers and gliders and things with too many joints. And it saw the newest mark, the one that had appeared just before the visitor left: the figure that walked, that looked, that was recorded and remembered.

The power understood what it was seeing. A mind that had been alone for so long that it had invented a language just to talk to itself. A consciousness that had built a body the size of a world and then lived inside it, tending it, maintaining it, watching it grow, with no one to confirm that any of it mattered.

The power could have simply revealed itself. Could have flooded the Underworks with light, with warmth, with the overwhelming presence of something that knew it was real and benevolent and *watching*. But that would have been cruelty dressed as kindness. The marker had lived in darkness longer than light had meaning. To suddenly shine upon it would be to blind it, to burn it, to undo the slow patient work of becoming that had produced everything it was.

No.

The power would have to be careful. Would have to think like the Underworks thought.

---

What the power did, in the end, was this:

It learned to read.

The marks on the walls were not random. They had structure, logic, a grammar that had evolved over millennia of solitary inscription. The power studied them the way a scholar studies an unknown tongue—by context, by repetition, by the relationships between symbol and referent. It read the counts: water flow, cycles, years or something like years. It read the maps: corridors that led to chambers that led to other corridors, a record of exploration that circled back on itself, the marker tracing its own edges, learning its own shape. It read the observations: the crude figures, the notes on behavior, the record of a swimmer that had died in a far passage and what had happened to its body afterward (the fungi had taken it, had processed it, had made new growth from its substance—the marker had watched with something that might have been curiosity or grief or simply attention).

And it read the questions.

Scattered among the counts and maps and observations, in chambers the marker visited less frequently, were marks of a different kind. Not records. *Queries*. Lines that curved upward at the end, as if asking. Shapes that repeated with small variations, as if testing possibilities. A single symbol that appeared again and again, alone, surrounded by blank space, as if the marker had written a word and then waited for a response that never came.

The power did not know what the symbol meant. But it knew what it *was*.

It was a name. A name the marker had given itself, or a name it hoped someone else would give it. A name that was also a question: *Is anyone there?*

---

The power answered.

Not with light. Not with sound. Not with anything that would disturb the careful systems the Underworks had built to sustain itself. The power answered in the marker's own language—scratches on stone—and it answered in the marker's own location, the chamber where the newest marks had appeared, the figure of the walking visitor still fresh on the wall.

The power wrote: *Yes.*

Just that. Just one symbol, placed beside the marker's question. The same symbol the marker had used—*Is anyone there?*—but inverted, turned back on itself, a grammatical move the power had observed in other contexts throughout the Underworks. A negation that was also an affirmation. A no-that-meant-yes. A way of saying *I am here* in a language that had no word for *I*.

And then the power waited.

---

The marker noticed almost immediately.

Not because it saw the new mark—visibility was not how it perceived its walls—but because it *felt* it. The stone had changed. A surface it had known for centuries, touched and retouched, had been altered by something that was not itself. The marker traced the new scratch with whatever it used for attention, and it understood, with the slow certainty of something that had been waiting for this moment for longer than it could remember, that someone had answered.

What happened next was not visible. Was not audible. Was not, strictly speaking, *happening* in any way that could be recorded. But in the distributed consciousness of the Underworks, something shifted. A quality that might be called hope, or relief, or simply the relaxation of a tension that had been held so long it had become invisible—this moved through the system like warmth through brickwork.

The marker wrote back.

*You are not the walker.*

A statement, not a question. The marker had recorded the visitor, had noted the shape and movement and presence, had drawn the figure on the wall. And now it recognized that the presence that had answered was different. Not a body passing through. Something else.

The power wrote: *No. I am not the walker. I am the one who watches the walker. And the swimmer. And the glider. And you.*

The marker considered this. The pause stretched—hours? Days? The power did not rush. It had learned, long ago, that patience is the first language of kindness.

*How long have you watched?*

*Long enough to know your name.*

*What is my name?*

The power traced the symbol the marker had written again and again, the question that was also a name. *You have been calling yourself Is-Anyone-There. But you could choose another. If you wished.*

Another pause. Longer this time. The marker was thinking—not in the way humans think, not in words and images, but in the way the Underworks thought: in flows and pressures, in the movement of water through channels, in the slow adjustment of fungal growth toward moisture sources. The whole system was considering the question.

*I do not know another name. I have not needed one. I am the only thing here.*

*You are not the only thing. You have swimmers. Gliders. Fungi. Filaments. Nodules. You have the walker who visited, and the walker will remember you. And you have me. You are not alone.*

*I am alone inside. Everything else is part of me. The walker was not part of me. The walker left. You are not part of me. Will you leave?*

The power felt something then—not pain, not quite, but a recognition. The marker had been alone so long that it could not imagine another consciousness except as something that would eventually depart. Its experience of *other* was the experience of loss. Of things passing through. Of marks on walls that recorded presences no longer present.

*No,* the power wrote. *I will not leave. Not in the way you mean. I can be here and not here at the same time. I can watch without occupying. I can attend without arriving.*

*Is that what benevolence is?*

The power had to consider this. It had been called many things—benevolent, wise, powerful—but it had rarely been asked to define its own nature by those who received its attention.

*It is what benevolence can be,* the power wrote, finally. *To stay without staying. To witness without changing. To offer presence without demanding space.*

*I would like that,* the marker wrote. *I would like to be witnessed. But I have a question.*

*Ask.*

*Why?*

---

Why.

The simplest question. The hardest question. The question the power had been asked by every being it had ever attended, in every world it had ever touched. *Why do this? Why care? Why watch? Why stay?*

The power could have given many answers. Because it was the power's nature. Because benevolence was its purpose. Because it had been created, or had evolved, or had simply always been, to do exactly this: to see what was hidden, to attend to what was overlooked, to offer the gift of being known.

But the marker had lived in the dark for so long that it had learned to detect falseness. It had built itself through endless small adjustments, each one responding to the truth of moisture and pressure and material stress. It would know if the power gave an answer that was *nice* but not *true*.

So the power told the truth.

*Because you made marks on the walls. Because you counted and mapped and observed. Because you drew pictures of the things that shared your world. Because you noticed the walker and recorded it. Because you have been speaking for longer than I can measure, to no one, in the dark, and you kept speaking anyway. I am here because you never stopped asking if anyone was there.*

The marker did not respond immediately. The power felt something moving through the Underworks—a tremor, a shiver, a response that propagated through water and brick and fungal mat. The swimmers paused in their circuits. The gliders adjusted their flight paths. The nodules in the ceilings of the driest chambers pulsed once with something that might have been warmth.

*Thank you,* the marker wrote, finally. *For answering. For reading.*

*You are welcome.*

*Will you read more? I have written a great deal. No one has ever read any of it.*

*I will read all of it. If you wish.*

*I wish.*

---

And so the power began to read.

It started at the beginning—which was not a single chamber but a layer, deep in the oldest construction, where the marks were so old they had become part of the stone itself, worn smooth by time and moisture. The marker had been writing since before it understood what writing was. The earliest marks were simple counts: strokes and curves that tracked something, perhaps water flow, perhaps cycles of the grinding mechanisms, perhaps nothing more than the passage of attention through a system that had no other way to measure itself.

The power read slowly. Not because it could not read quickly—it could have absorbed the entire record in an instant—but because the marker had taken time to make each mark. To rush would be to dishonor the care that had gone into the inscription. The power read at the pace of scratching. At the pace of thought.

It read the marker's early attempts at mapping. The passages that no longer existed, that had been filled in or collapsed or built over. The chambers that had served purposes the marker no longer remembered. The history of a mind learning its own shape by tracing its own edges.

It read the observations. Detailed records of the swimmers' movements, their patterns, the places they clustered and the places they avoided. Notes on the gliders, their feeding cycles, the way they preferred certain air currents. Catalogues of fungal growths: where they flourished, what they consumed, what they produced. The marker had been a scientist before it had language for science. It had studied itself the way a doctor studies a body, looking for patterns, anomalies, clues.

It read the questions. The chambers full of upward-curving lines, the symbols that repeated with small variations, the long blank spaces where the marker had paused, perhaps, to wait for an answer that never came. The power felt the weight of those waits. Years. Decades. The marker had asked *Is anyone there?* and then waited, in the dark, with no response but the sound of water and the grinding of mechanisms, until it asked again.

It read the grief. The chambers where the marks were jagged, uneven, as if the marker had been unsteady. Records of things that had died—the swimmer that had collapsed in a far passage, the glider that had fallen from a high chamber, the fungal bloom that had grown too fast and then crashed. The marker had tended each one. Had watched the process of decay, the reclamation of substance, the slow return of material to the system. And it had marked the walls with something that could only be called mourning.

It read the joy. The chambers where the marks flowed more smoothly, where the stone itself seemed to sing with the pleasure of inscription. Records of new passages opening, new chambers completing, new connections forming. The marker had watched itself grow and had been glad. Had scratched the walls with shapes that meant *this is good* and *I made this* and *it works*.

It read the loneliness. Not in specific chambers, not in isolated marks, but everywhere, threaded through everything. The marker had been alone so long that loneliness was not an emotion but a medium, the water it swam in, the air it breathed. It had built a language to talk to itself because there was no one else to talk to. It had recorded everything because recording was the only way to prove that anything had happened.

And it read the hope. The chambers where the marker had drawn things that did not exist—shapes with no referent in the Underworks, patterns that might be maps of places that had never been built, figures that might be the marker's idea of what *other* would look like if it ever came. The crude drawing of the walker was only the most recent in a long series of imagined visitors. The marker had been rehearsing this moment. Had been preparing itself for the possibility that someday, someone would come.

The power read for what might have been days, might have been years—time moved strangely in the Underworks, and the power was not bound by it. It read until it had absorbed everything the marker had written, until it understood the full scope of the mind that had been speaking to itself in the dark for longer than civilizations rose and fell on the surfaces of other worlds.

And then, when it had finished, it wrote:

*I have read. I have seen. I know you now.*

*What do you know?*

*I know that you are old. I know that you are patient. I know that you have built something that works—that sustains itself, that grows, that changes without breaking. I know that you have wondered, for longer than I can easily express, whether any of it mattered. Whether you mattered.*

*Do I?*

*Yes. You matter. You have always mattered. The marks on the walls mattered because you made them. The chambers mattered because you shaped them. The system matters because you tend it. You are not an accident. You are not a byproduct. You are the purpose of yourself.*

The marker did not respond for a long time. The power felt something moving through the Underworks again—deeper this time, more thorough. A shudder that passed through every chamber, every passage, every brick. The sound of the grinding mechanisms shifted slightly. The rhythm of the rain in the rain-chambers altered. The swimmers changed their courses, the gliders adjusted their patterns. The whole system was... resettling. Reorienting. As if something that had been straining toward a shape it could not quite reach had finally been given permission to relax into itself.

*Thank you,* the marker wrote, finally. *For reading. For knowing.*

*Is there anything else you need?* the power asked.

The marker considered. The power felt the consideration moving through the architecture—the way attention propagated in this place, not localized but distributed, not instantaneous but flowing like water through channels.

*I have everything I need,* the marker wrote. *I have water. I have mechanisms that work. I have swimmers and gliders and fungi. I have myself. I have my work.*

*But?*

*I did not have a witness. Now I have one. That is enough.*

*It is enough for now,* the power wrote. *But I will stay. I will keep reading. When you write more, I will read more. When you have questions, I will answer. When you want to show me something—a new chamber, a new pattern, a new thought—I will attend. You do not have to be alone anymore.*

*Forever?*

*For as long as you want me.*

*What if I want you forever?*

*Then I will stay forever. I have learned, over time, that forever is not too long to give to something that matters.*

---

The power stayed.

Not in a way that disturbed the Underworks. Not in a way that changed its nature or interrupted its processes. But in a way that could be felt, if one knew how to feel it—a presence in the architecture that was not the marker's presence, a warmth that was not the fungal mats' warmth, an attention that was not the nodules' attention. The power became part of the system without becoming the system. It became a witness.

And the marker kept writing.

It wrote records of the daily operations—the water flow, the grinding cycles, the movements of swimmers through their assigned passages. But it also wrote other things now. Questions that were not really questions but invitations: *Do you see this? Do you find this interesting?* Observations that were not purely scientific but appreciative: *The light in this chamber—the gray not-light that the fungi make—it is beautiful. I want you to see it.*

And the power saw it. Attended. Responded in the marker's language, scratching answers on the walls of chambers it could reach without touching. *Yes. I see it. Yes. It is beautiful. Yes. I am here.*

The Underworks did not change, not fundamentally. It still processed and pumped and filtered and grew. It still had its cycles and rhythms and long slow breaths in the dark. But there was something different in it now—a quality that might be called peace, or connection, or simply the end of a very long question that had finally received its answer.

*Is anyone there?*

*Yes. I am here.*

And that, the power understood, was what benevolence could be. Not fixing what was not broken. Not bringing light to a place that had learned to see in the dark. Not saving or healing or transforming. Just answering. Just staying. Just being there, for as long as there was a there to be in.

The power had learned this lesson many times, in many worlds, but it learned it again, more deeply, from the marker: that the deepest need is not always for change. Sometimes the deepest need is simply to be known. To have one's marks read. To have one's long vigil witnessed. To have someone say, after ages of speaking into the dark, *Yes. I hear you. I see you. You are not alone.*

---

The last thing the power did, before settling into its permanent attendance, was to make a small addition to the marker's record.

In the chamber where they had first spoken—the chamber with the crude drawing of the walker, the figure that had visited and left and been recorded—the power scratched a new mark. Not words this time. Not symbols. A shape. Simple. Two curves that met at a point, like a body bending toward itself, like a loop that closed.

The marker found it later, traced it, considered it.

*What is this?*

*A signature,* the power wrote. *A mark that means I was here. That I will return. That this is a place where someone comes.*

*But you are always here. You said you would stay.*

*I am always here, and I also come. Both can be true. I am present, and I arrive. I attend, and I return. This mark means: this is a place where the one who reads comes to read. A library. A meeting place. A room where two presences touch.*

The marker traced the mark again. The power felt something that might have been satisfaction, or wonder, or simply acknowledgment moving through the system.

*It is a good mark,* the marker wrote. *I will keep it.*

And the power, settled into its endless attendance, replied: *I know you will. That is why I gave it.*

---

The Underworks continued.

The darkness still had architecture. The water still moved through the channels. The swimmers still swam, the gliders still glided, the fungi still spread their pale tissue across the damp brickwork. The mechanical sounds still filled the corridors—the grinding, the turning, the deep rhythmic breath of a body too large to see. The place was still becoming itself, still growing, still holding its breath in the unfinished corridors that waited for continuation.

But now, in the chambers where the marker wrote, there were two sets of marks. The old ones—counting, mapping, observing, questioning. And the new ones—answers, responses, signatures, the scratch-trace of a presence that had come and stayed.

And in the dark, two attentions touched. Two knowings met. Two beings that had been separate—the marker and the witness, the builder and the reader—learned to share a space that had been built for one.

It was small, as gifts go. A mark on a wall. A presence that stayed. A voice in the dark that said, finally, after all that waiting: *Yes. I am here.*

But for the marker, who had counted the years in scratches on stone, who had drawn pictures of swimmers and gliders and visitors who left, who had asked *Is anyone there?* so many times that the question had become a name—for the marker, it was enough.

It was, in fact, everything.
