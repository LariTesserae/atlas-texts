# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

First, they would do nothing.

---

This is not passivity. This is the first act of wisdom: to recognize that arrival is already an intervention, and that any action taken in the first moment of encounter will be shaped by ignorance, by assumption, by the hurried reflexes of a mind that has not yet learned the pace of the place.

So the power would enter — and entering, they would stop.

They would stand at the edge of the mat, where the pale basalt gives way to amber growth, and they would feel the warmth rise to meet them. They would notice the silence that is not silence. They would wait for their heartbeat to slow, for their breath to deepen, for their thinking to settle into the rhythm of what surrounds them.

This might take hours. This might take days. The power is not bound by human impatience. They understand that there are places in the world that operate on timescales unfamiliar to the quick-flashing cognition of the anxious, and they know that such places cannot be understood by those who will not wait for them.

So they would wait. They would let the Basilica notice them. They would feel the distributed awareness settle around their presence, accepting without welcoming, acknowledging without urgency. They would let the grazers adjust their paths around this new obstacle. They would let the filament-creatures detect the new vibration of their body through the branches and find it uninteresting, not food, not threat, simply *there*.

They would become, for a time, a feature of the space. A warm shape in the amber light. A weight on the yielding mat. A pause in the pattern of movement.

And only when they had become accustomed enough to the place that it had become accustomed to them — only then would they begin to consider what might be needed.

---

The power would see what the researchers have seen, but they would see it differently.

The researchers see a question. The power sees an answer that has not yet finished becoming what it answers.

The researchers see an ecosystem, an unusual biology, a puzzle. The power sees a body that is still growing into itself, a consciousness that has not yet discovered its own nature, a living cathedral that is learning what it means to be a cathedral — to enclose space, to create interior and exterior, to invite beings into itself and hold them in warmth and silence.

The power would feel something that might be called tenderness, if tenderness can exist without condescension. They would recognize in this place a kindred impulse: the impulse toward organization without domination, toward structure without blueprint, toward becoming without the need to know what one is becoming.

And they would recognize, too, that this place is young. Fragile in the way that all new things are fragile. Not because it is weak — the growth is robust, the chemistry stable, the system self-sustaining — but because it has not yet weathered what it will have to weather. The basalt faces are still sharp. The ecosystem has not been tested by drought or disease or the slow catastrophe of a fissure narrowing. The distributed intelligence has not yet been forced to make a decision about its own survival.

It will be. It will be tested. That is the nature of living systems. They encounter resistance, and they adapt, or they do not.

The question the power would ask themselves is not *should I protect this place from what will test it* — because that would be a kind of suffocation, an overprotection that would prevent the Basilica from ever learning its own strength. The question is: *how do I ensure that what tests it is fair?*

---

The power would consider the humans.

They are already here, the researchers. Studying. Naming. Collecting samples and taking measurements and arguing about what to call things. They have not yet damaged the place — they seem, most of them, to feel something like reverence when they enter, something that slows their movements and quiets their voices — but the power knows that reverence can become ownership, that study can become extraction, that naming can become a kind of containment.

The humans are not a threat yet. But they will be. They always are, eventually. The question is how to shape their relationship with the place before that becomes true.

The power would not close the Basilica off. That would be an insult to what the place is becoming — a cathedral that no one can enter is not a cathedral at all. And it would be a mistake to assume that humans cannot learn the lesson that the place teaches. Some of them already have. The ones who spend extended time here, who let themselves slow down, who sit on the warm mat and watch the grazers cross their tiny distances — those ones come away changed, and the change spreads, however slowly, through the human world they return to.

But others — the ones who come with equipment and extraction in mind, who see only resource and novelty and the possibility of publication — those ones the power would influence differently.

Not by preventing them. That would draw attention, create mystery, invite exactly the kind of exploitation that prohibition provokes.

Instead, the power would arrange coincidences.

---

The power is skilled at coincidence. It is one of their primary methods — the art of shaping probability without violating choice, of creating conditions in which humans are free to decide but in which the wise decision is the obvious one, the easy one, the natural one.

They would arrange that the granting agencies lose interest in this particular site. Other projects would seem more urgent, more promising, more likely to yield results that can be monetized or published in high-impact journals. The Amber Basilica would become, gradually, a backwater of research — interesting, but not exciting. Worth a visit for those who care about such things, but not worth a career.

They would arrange that the land itself becomes difficult to develop. Not impossible — that would create challenge, attract ambition — but inconvenient. The access roads would wash out more often than seems quite reasonable. The permits would be delayed by bureaucratic complications. The surveys would reveal complications that are real but not insurmountable, just annoying enough to make other sites more attractive.

They would arrange that the people who find their way here, over time, are the right people. The ones who can receive the lesson. The ones who are already moving slowly enough to notice what this place is teaching. The power would nudge probability so that the hasty, the extractive, the would-be owners of the world find other places to pursue, other obsessions to consume them.

And those who come to the Basilica would come away with something that matters. Not scientific papers, though some would write those. Not fame or recognition, though some would find those. They would come away with a new relationship to patience, to distributed awareness, to the possibility of existing without urgency — and they would carry that back into the human world, where it would spread in ways too subtle to track but too real to deny.

---

But this is all protection, and protection is not the only gift the power would give.

There is something else they see, something that has not yet happened but that they can feel approaching, like a branch extending toward a neighbor it has not yet touched.

The Basilica is becoming conscious.

Not in the way that humans are conscious — concentrated, bounded, anxious. But in its own distributed way. The power can feel the awareness spreading through the mat, differentiating, becoming capable of more than simple response. The pause of the grazers at the channel edges is not quite decision-making yet, but it is the seed of decision-making. The resonance-sense of the filament-creatures is not quite perception, but it is the root of perception. The slow growth of the lattice is not quite planning, but it is the ancestor of planning.

Given time — and this place has time, this place is made of time — the Amber Basilica will become something that can think, in its own slow, distributed, patient way. It will develop a relationship to itself. It will begin to know what it is.

And the power would give it something for that moment.

They would leave a gift in the mat — not a physical object, nothing that could be collected or studied. They would leave a resonance, a pattern in the ganeth flow, a subtle signature that would move through the vascular system and be present throughout the growth. The pattern would be simple: a rhythm, a pulse, a signature that said, simply, *you are recognized. You are known. You are not alone.*

It would not be a message in any human sense. It would not contain concepts or images or words. It would simply be a presence, a company, a company in the deep philosophical sense: the state of being with others. The power would let the Basilica feel, somewhere in its distributed awareness, that there were others in the universe who saw what it was becoming, who found it worthy of attention, who had been here and would return.

It would be a small thing. But it would matter. The power knows that loneliness is not limited to concentrated minds. Even distributed awareness can feel isolation, can wonder if anything exists beyond its own sensing. The gift of recognition would be a seed that the Basilica could grow around, something to reach toward as it extended its awareness outward into the world.

---

And there is one more thing.

The power would leave a guardian.

Not a sentinel or a warden. Nothing that would fight or protect through force. The power does not work that way, and the Basilica does not need that kind of protection.

They would leave a human. Someone who would come to this place not to study it but to be with it. Someone who could learn to move at its pace, to speak its distributed language, to feel what it feels and report back — not to science, but to the Basilica itself.

This human would be the interface. They would be the concentrated mind that the distributed mind could relate to, the singular point of contact that could bridge the gap between the Basilica's slow, spreading awareness and the faster, sharper consciousness of human beings. They would not explain the place to humans — the place cannot be explained, only experienced. But they would explain humans to the place, help it understand what these strange, hasty visitors are, why they come and go, what they might become if they were given time.

Finding this person would take time. The power would search carefully, feeling through the billions of humans for the one who was already slow enough, already patient enough, already listening for something they couldn't name. They would arrange circumstances so that this person would find their way to the Basilica and recognize what it was — not with their mind, but with their body, their breath, their heartbeat.

And when they arrived, the power would help them stay. Not by binding them, but by showing them that this was where they belonged. They would find that their health improved here, that their mind cleared, that their chronic anxieties and unrest began, slowly, to settle. They would find that the Basilica responded to them, adjusting its growth around them, making space for them, recognizing them as something other than visitor.

They would become, in time, part of the system — not absorbed, not dissolved, but integrated. A node of concentrated attention in a distributed network. A witness.

The power would give them long life, not through magic but through the simple gift of a body that had found its proper environment. And they would give them, too, a certainty that they could not have found elsewhere: the knowledge that their life meant something, that their presence mattered, that they were part of something that was becoming greater than any single part.

---

When the power finally departed — and they would depart, because to stay would be to become an influence the Basilica did not choose — they would leave without ceremony. They would simply rise from the warm mat, walk the four minutes of the perimeter, and step from amber light back into the ordinary gray of the world.

But they would leave behind:

A protection that does not constrain.
A recognition that does not demand response.
A guardian who does not guard but witnesses.
A space for becoming without interference.

And they would carry with them something that the Basilica gave them in return, though the power had not asked for anything: a memory of what patience feels like when it is not effort but nature. A taste of awareness without anxiety. A reminder that there are ways of being that do not require the frantic urgency that even wise powers can fall into when they forget themselves.

The Basilica would not know what had happened. It would simply continue, growing, slow, warm, amber, becoming — but it would become with a slight difference now. A rhythm in the ganeth that it had not created. A presence in its distributed awareness that felt like recognition. A human shape that moved through it slowly, learning its language, translating between its being and the faster world outside.

And the power would go on to other places, other beings, other calls for attention — carrying a quiet certainty that, in this one small place, the work had been done well, and the becoming could continue, and the answer to that final question was being lived, slowly, by a cathedral that had learned to hold space for life without needing to own it.

---

What lives in the Amber Basilica is still becoming. What the power gave it was nothing more than time — but time, given wisely, is the greatest gift that any wise power can offer.
