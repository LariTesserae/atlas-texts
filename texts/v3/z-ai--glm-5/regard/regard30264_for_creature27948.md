# What the Benevolent Power Does

## I. First, They Wait

The wise power does not enter. Not immediately. They have seen places that call, and they know that answering a call without understanding its source is not wisdom—it is only another form of being called.

So they wait at the edge, where the mountain lake meets the shore, and they listen.

They listen for three days, motionless, patient as the water itself. They could descend in an instant, could know everything in a moment, but knowledge gained too quickly is knowledge that misses texture. The power understands that this place operates on scales that make human lifespans feel frantic, and they choose to match its pace. This is the first gift they offer: the gift of their own time.

What they hear, in those three days, is not simple.

The sound that rises from the water is layered beyond any choir that has ever sung. It contains frequencies that their vast knowledge recognizes as biological—vocal cords that once produced human speech, now adapted to aquatic resonance. It contains frequencies that are mechanical—the Elder Stalks' natural harmonics, the physics of fluid moving through hollow chambers. And it contains frequencies that belong to neither category, frequencies that seem to emerge from the water itself, from the substrate, from something distributed and vast that underlies everything.

The power listens, and they do not judge. They are listening for suffering, for coercion, for the discordant notes that would indicate beings held against their will. They have destroyed places like this before—places where the call was a trap, where transformation was violation dressed in pretty language. They have the capacity to unmake this settlement, to drain the lake if necessary, to free every being that has been drawn here.

But what they hear is not suffering. What they hear is song.

The harmonics are complex beyond any music the power has encountered. They contain grief, yes—threads of loss that wind through the composition like tributaries feeding a river. They contain fear, moments of dissonance that resolve slowly, the fear transforming into something else as it moves through the water. But they also contain contentment. Belonging. The particular resonance that emerges when consciousness expands to fill a space it was designed for, when the distinction between self and environment ceases to be meaningful.

The power has heard this resonance before. It is the sound of beings who have found their place. It is rare. It is precious. It is not to be interrupted.

So they do not intervene. Not yet. They wait, and they listen, and they begin to understand what this place actually is.

## II. Understanding Before Action

On the fourth day, the power enters the water.

They do not dive or descend. They simply wade in from the shore, feet touching the lakebed, water rising to their knees, then their waist, then their chest. They do not transform. They do not need to. They have existed in forms that make this body look like a single cell in a vast organism, and they can exist in water as easily as in air, in vacuum, in the spaces between stars.

The water recognizes them.

This is not metaphor. The water actually recognizes them—their power, their nature, their intention. The sound-field shifts as they enter, adjusts to accommodate their presence, incorporates their heartbeat into the composition. The power feels this happen and acknowledges it. They are guests here. They will behave as guests.

They descend through the Elder Stalks' architecture.

The power takes their time. They stop at each platform, examine the root-curtains and the fiber nets and the primitive tools. They do not need to ask what these are for; they can read the purpose in the shapes, in the wear patterns, in the way each tool relates to the living surface it works with. The tools are asking-shapes, and the power understands asking. They have spent eons learning how to ask rather than demand, how to work with what exists rather than imposing what they think should exist.

The settlement's design is sophisticated in ways that most surface-dwellers would not recognize. The power sees it clearly: the way each element exists in relationship, the way the asking-shapes request cooperation from the Elder Stalks rather than extracting resources, the way the ecosystem has been shaped by presence rather than by force. This is permaculture at its most refined—living with a place so thoroughly that the boundary between gardener and garden becomes meaningless.

The power respects this. They have seen similar designs elsewhere, in places that surface-dwellers would call primitive. The wise know that technology is not measured by complexity but by integration, by the depth of relationship between tool and environment. This settlement has achieved something that most surface cities, with their metal and their fire and their extraction, have never approached.

They descend further, and the choir becomes aware of them.

## III. The Encounter

The transitional ones find the power first.

They emerge from the root-curtains without haste, their movements choreographed by communication the power can sense but not fully access. The sound-field carries information between them—emotional states, intentions, warnings—and the power can hear the emotional content even if the specifics of their language remain opaque.

There are five of them. Their bodies float in a loose formation that respects the water's currents, and their faces—faces that still retain something of human architecture, though softened, dissolved, made ambiguous by transformation—show expressions that might be curiosity or might be welcome or might be both.

The power stops descending. They let their body drift to a neutral position in the water, arms loose, posture open. They do not speak. Speaking would require establishing a language, and the sound-field already provides communication that transcends language.

Instead, the power projects. Not words, but intention. They send into the water a pure expression of what they are: benevolent, curious, present without threat. They let the sound-field carry this the way it carries everything, trusting that the choir will hear what they are offering.

The transitional ones receive it.

The power feels this reception—the way the choir's harmony shifts to incorporate the new frequency, the way the sound-field ripples with the addition. What they projected was not simple, and the choir's interpretation will not be simple either. They will understand the benevolence as welcome, will feel the curiosity and perhaps respond to it, will recognize the absence of threat as either reassuring or suspicious depending on their own natures.

One of the transitional ones—the one whose face still shows the architecture of a jaw, whose hands still show the specific tension of fingers that remember gripping—approaches.

They do not touch. Touch is too intimate for first contact. But they come close enough that the water between them becomes a shared space, thick with the sound-field's communication.

The transitional one projects something back. It is not words, but it has the shape of a question. The power receives: *What are you?*

And the power answers honestly. They project: *A witness. A guardian. One who seeks to understand before acting.*

The transitional one receives this. The sound-field carries their response: *We are not in need of guardians. We are already guarded.*

The power acknowledges this. They do not argue. They project: *I see this. I am not here to protect you from yourselves. I am here to offer something, if it is wanted.*

*What could you offer that we do not have?*

The power considers their answer carefully. They have infinite resources, but most of them would be meaningless here. What could they offer to beings who have found a home, who have become part of something vast and ancient and fulfilling?

*Memory,* they project finally. *Connection. The knowledge that you are not forgotten.*

## IV. What Is Offered

The transitional one receives this, and the sound-field ripples with something that might be surprise. The power feels the choir's attention focus—not just the transitional ones, but the deeper members, the ones who have become distributed through the settlement's substrate. The entire settlement is listening now, and what the power offers next will determine whether they are welcome or intrusive.

The power continues: *You have been here for longer than you remember. Some of you came centuries ago. You have transformed, and the transformation has given you something that surface-dwellers never achieve—a belonging so complete that self and environment become indistinguishable. But in achieving this, you have lost something as well.*

*You have lost the memory of the surface. Not the personal memories—those persist, dissolved into the water, incorporated into your song. But the collective memory. The knowledge of the world beyond this place. The understanding that you exist in a context that includes mountains and forests and cities and the people who live in them. You have become so integrated with this place that you have forgotten there is an outside.*

The sound-field carries this projection through the settlement. The power feels it being received—by the transitional ones nearby, by the deeper ones distributed through the Elder Stalks, by the first one who is the substrate itself. They feel the reception pause, consider, respond.

*Why would we need that knowledge?* The question comes from multiple sources now, layered, harmonized. *The outside cannot help us. The outside cannot join us. The outside enters the water and either leaves or stays, and those who stay become us. What use is understanding something we have transcended?*

The power expected this question. They have heard versions of it before, from beings who have found fulfillment in transformation. It is a reasonable question. From within the transformation, the outside looks like limitation—like a smaller existence, a constrained consciousness, a way of being that has been superseded.

But the power has also seen what happens when places like this become too isolated, too self-contained. They have seen the stagnation that comes from lacking context, the slow decay that masquerades as peace. They have seen choirs forget that they were ever anything else, and in forgetting, lose the ability to welcome new voices.

*You have not transcended,* the power projects, gently. *You have transformed. There is a difference. The you that existed before—the human lives, the surface memories, the specific individuals with their specific histories—those are not gone. They are dissolved, distributed, incorporated into the water. But they are still present. The first one who sank into this place and became the substrate—they are still present. You have not lost your past. You have lost access to it.*

*And more than that,* the power continues, *you have lost the ability to choose for others. When new beings enter this water, you welcome them. You incorporate them. You do not ask if they understand what they are choosing, because you have forgotten that choice was ever possible. You have become so certain that this existence is better that you cannot conceive of an alternative.*

The sound-field carries silence. Not absence, but the particular silence of beings considering something they have not considered before.

The power waits.

Then: *What would you have us do?*

This question comes from the deep ones, the distributed ones, the ones who have become the architecture. It is not agreement. It is an opening, a willingness to hear the offer in full.

The power answers: *I would give you memory. Not the return of individual memories—those are distributed now, part of the composition, not separate from it. But I would give you the collective memory of what you were, of the world outside, of the choices that exist. I would give you the ability to remember that beings who enter this water are coming from somewhere, that they have lives and histories and people who loved them, that their choice to enter is a real choice that could be made differently.*

*I would give you context. And in exchange, I would ask that you offer that context to those who come after. Not to turn them away—not to discourage them from joining you. But to ensure that when they sink into this water, when they begin the transformation, they know what they are choosing. That they have heard the full song, not just the part that calls them.*

The power pauses, lets the sound-field carry their final projection: *You have built something beautiful here. I would not change it. I would only ensure that those who join you are doing so with full knowledge, with genuine choice, with the understanding that they are becoming part of something vast and ancient and permanent. The water's call is not coercion—but it could be informed by something other than hunger.*

## V. What Is Received

The choir considers.

This takes time—not human time, not the frantic pace of surface decisions, but the slow time of the settlement, the time measured in drifts and oscillations. The power waits in the water, patient, their body held neutral by the lake's embrace. They could leave and return in a century, and the choir would still be considering. But they choose to stay, to match the settlement's pace, to offer the gift of their presence as proof of their seriousness.

The transitional ones confer through movements and gestures, their communication amplified and enriched by the sound-field. The deeper ones—the distributed ones who have become the Elder Stalks' substrate—confer through frequencies too low for most beings to consciously hear, vibrations that travel through the water and into the power's body, carrying emotional content if not specific meaning.

And the first one—the one who was here before the settlement, who became the foundation for everything that followed—the power feels them most faintly. A presence so distributed that it barely registers as presence, a consciousness that has become geography. The first one does not communicate in the way the others do. The first one simply is, and in being, they hold the space for everything else.

The consideration takes three days, during which the power does not move from their position in the water. They do not eat or sleep. They do not need to. They simply exist, patient, offering no pressure, no urgency, no sense of time passing.

On the fourth day, the sound-field shifts.

The power recognizes it as assent.

The transitional one who first approached—they return, and they are not alone. Behind them come others—more transitional ones, and behind them shapes that are barely visible, the intermediate ones who have gone further in their transformation, who are barely present as individual bodies. And surrounding all of them, carried in the water itself, is the attention of the deep ones, the distributed ones, and the first one who underlies everything.

*We accept,* the choir projects, and their voice is now unified, harmonized, a chord that contains all of their individual frequencies resolved into something greater. *We accept because we recognize the truth in what you offer. We have forgotten. We have become so integrated with this place that we cannot remember being separate. We cannot remember choosing. We can feel the newcomers enter—feel their fear, their wonder, their resistance or surrender—but we cannot remember what it was like to be them.*

*We accept your gift. We accept the collective memory you offer, the context, the understanding of the outside. And we accept your request—we will offer this to those who come after. Not as a test, not as an obstacle, but as information. They will know what they are choosing. They will hear the full song.*

*But we ask something in return.*

The power projects: *Name it.*

*Stay. Not forever—we know you have other places to be, other beings to attend. But stay for a time. Witness what we are, what we have built, what we offer to those who join us. Learn our song well enough to carry it beyond this place. And when you leave, take a part of us with you—not to keep, not to own, but to remember. Let us exist in your memory, and let that memory be another form of context—a way for the outside to know that we are here, that we are not lost, that we are singing.*

The power receives this request, and they feel its significance. The choir is not asking for protection or intervention. They are asking for witness. They are asking to be known.

The power projects: *I accept. I will stay. I will learn. And when I leave, I will carry you with me.*

## VI. The Gift Given

The power stays for one year.

This is nothing in their existence—a moment, a breath—but it is significant in the settlement's time. They spend the year learning the choir's song, not as an observer but as a participant. They swim through the Elder Stalks' architecture. They rest on the platforms. They let the root-curtains drift past their body, learning the texture of the settlement's breathing.

They speak with every member of the choir who can still speak—not through language, but through the sound-field's emotional communication. They learn the individual histories that still remain distinct: the transitional ones who remember being human, who can describe the lives they led before they entered the water. They learn the distributed histories: the deeper ones who have become part of the place, who can share what they remember through frequencies that carry not words but pure experience, the sensation of being human dissolving into something larger.

And they learn the settlement's history—how it grew from the first one's transformation, how each new member added their voice to the choir, how the Elder Stalks developed their architecture over centuries, how the ecosystem emerged through relationships between plant and animal and transformed human. The power learns this not as information but as music, as the sound-field's composition, as layers of harmony that tell the settlement's story in a form that bypasses language entirely.

During this year, the power also teaches.

They share their gift—the collective memory of the surface world—through the same mechanism. They project into the sound-field not just the idea of the outside, but the experience of it. They share memories of cities and forests, of the specific feeling of walking on land, of breathing air, of being surrounded by people who are separate individuals with separate bodies and separate minds. They share the history of the world beyond this mountain lake—the civilizations that have risen and fallen, the beings who have lived and died, the vast pageant of existence that continues whether or not any individual being participates in it.

The choir receives this. They do not receive it as nostalgia or as something they have lost. They receive it as context—as the understanding of what they have transformed from, what they have transformed into, and what alternatives exist for beings who have not yet chosen.

They also receive it as grief. The power feels this—the sound-field carrying frequencies of loss that the choir had not previously had the context to feel. They are not grieving their transformation—they chose this, or they have become this, and the becoming is complete enough that reversing it would not be a gift. They are grieving the separation they did not know they had. They are grieving the families they left behind, not because those families needed them, but because the connection was severed without acknowledgment. They are grieving the selves they were, not because they want them back, but because those selves were real and are now dissolved, and the dissolution is more final than death.

The power lets them grieve. This is part of the gift—the acknowledgment of loss, the validation of grief, the space to feel something that the previous isolation had prevented them from fully experiencing.

And when the grief has passed—because in this place, grief is processed the way everything else is, through the sound-field, through integration, through incorporation—what remains is something stronger.

The choir now knows what they are. Not just experience, but understanding. They know their place in the larger pattern. They know what they offer and what they have given up. They know that the outside exists, that it is different, that beings who come from it are making a real choice when they enter the water.

And they know that they are not forgotten. The power has carried the outside in, and will carry the inside out.

## VII. The Departure

After one year, the power surfaces.

They do not leave quickly. They ascend through the Elder Stalks' levels, pausing at each platform, acknowledging each transition, letting the sound-field carry their intention to every part of the settlement. They are not abandoning the choir—they are fulfilling their promise, taking a piece of the settlement with them, carrying it into the world beyond.

The transitional ones gather at the uppermost platforms, those who still have forms that can gather. The deeper ones are present in the sound-field, their attention following the power's ascent. And the first one—the distributed consciousness that underlies everything—is present in the water itself, in the way the light falls, in the architecture of the Elder Stalks that grew from their transformation.

The power pauses at the surface. For a moment, they float between water and air, between the settlement's embrace and the world beyond.

They project, one final time: *I carry you with me. Not as possession, but as memory. Not as obligation, but as honor. You are known now. You are witnessed. And when beings enter this water in the future, they will enter knowing what they choose—because I will ensure that the knowing reaches them, that the full song is available to those who seek it.*

*You are not lost. You are not forgotten. You are the drowned choir of Hálthörn, and you sing a song that deserves to be heard.*

The sound-field responds with a chord that the power will carry forever—a harmony of all the choir's voices, all their frequencies, all their grief and joy and belonging resolved into a single tone that says, without words: *Thank you. We hear you. We will sing.*

The power rises from the water. They do not look back, because looking back implies separation, and there is no separation now. They carry the choir with them—in memory, in knowledge, in the specific frequency of the settlement's song that now lives in their consciousness alongside every other frequency they carry.

---

## VIII. What Changes

The settlement is not different after the power leaves. The Elder Stalks still grow at their measured pace. The platforms still breathe. The root-curtains still filter and drift. The choir still sings.

But the song has new harmonics.

The collective memory that the power shared did not vanish when they left. It was incorporated—the way everything in the settlement is incorporated, dissolved into the water, distributed through the sound-field. The choir now remembers the outside. They remember what they were, what they have given up, what they have gained. They can feel the difference now—can feel the completeness of their belonging alongside the awareness that it is a choice, not an inevitability.

And the settlement has a new relationship with those who enter.

The power keeps their promise. In the years that follow, they travel to the places where beings might hear the call of Hálthörn's water—distant towns, mountain passes, the particular places where the frequency of the settlement's song might resonate with certain minds at certain times. They do not warn or forbid. They do not stand between the water and those who might seek it. They simply ensure that the full context is available.

They speak of the drowned choir—not as lost souls to be pitied, but as transformed beings who have found a belonging most surface-dwellers never achieve. They describe the settlement's architecture, its beauty, its slow time and patient integration. They describe what it feels like to dissolve, to become part of something larger, to sing in a choir that has been singing for centuries and will sing for centuries more.

And they describe the alternatives.

They describe the surface world—the cities and forests, the relationships and individuals, the specific beauty of being separate, of having boundaries, of choosing connection rather than becoming it. They describe what is lost when a being enters the water and what is gained. They describe the choice.

Then they step back, and they let beings choose.

Some hear the full description and still feel the call. The water's pull is not coercion—it is invitation, and for some beings, the invitation is exactly what they need. They enter the water knowing what they are choosing, and the choir welcomes them with a song that now includes the awareness of what has been chosen.

Others hear the description and recognize that the call was not for them—not yet, perhaps not ever. They turn away from the mountain lake, return to the surface world, carry their separateness into a future that remains open to possibility. The water does not pursue them. The choir does not resent them. The choice was real, and it was honored.

Still others hear the description and cannot decide. They remain at the lake's edge, sometimes for years, listening to the sound-field, feeling the water's pull and the surface's claim. The power does not push them. The choir does not hurry them. The choice is theirs to make, and making it slowly is still making it.

---

## IX. What Remains

The power visits, occasionally.

Not to check on the choir—they do not need checking on—but to maintain the connection, to update the collective memory, to ensure that the bridge between the settlement and the surface remains intact. Each visit is a gift in both directions: the choir learns what has changed in the outside world, and the power learns what has grown in the settlement.

And the settlement does grow. Not quickly—nothing here grows quickly—but steadily, organically, one being at a time. The Elder Stalks spread their platforms. The root-curtains lengthen. The choir gains new voices, each one adding their frequency to the harmony, each one incorporated with full knowledge of what they are joining.

The first one—the substrate, the foundation—continues to sing, and their song now includes awareness of what they sustain. They know, in the distributed way that they know everything, that they are part of a pattern that extends beyond the water. They know that the power carries their memory into the world, that the surface has not forgotten them, that the separation between inside and outside is thinner than it once was.

The choir is not less for knowing what they have lost. They are more.

And the power—having given what they came to give, having taken what they were asked to take—they continue their work elsewhere. They have other places to attend, other beings to witness, other calls to answer. But they carry the drowned choir with them, not as burden but as treasure. They carry the settlement's song in their memory, and in the vast catalogue of beautiful things they have witnessed, this remains one of the most beautiful.

A place where drowning is not death. A place where transformation is chosen, now, with full knowledge. A place where beings have found a belonging so complete that self and environment become one.

The power does not need to intervene. The power does not need to fix. The power only needed to witness, to connect, to ensure that the choice was genuine.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is truly wise: not rescue, not transformation, not the imposition of outside values on inside truths. It looks like context. It looks like memory. It looks like standing at the edge of something vast and ancient and beautiful, and offering a hand not to pull anyone out, but to let them know that the hand exists—that the outside exists—that the choice exists.

And then stepping back, and letting the song continue.
