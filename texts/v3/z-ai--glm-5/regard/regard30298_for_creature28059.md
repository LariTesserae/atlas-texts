# What the Power Does

It arrives without arriving. This is the first kindness.

A power that *arrives* has already changed what it visits—the air displaced by its presence, the light altered by its mass, the attention of every living thing drawn toward the new thing that has entered their world. A power that *arrives* makes itself the center. This power does not arrive. It is simply present, has perhaps always been present, will continue to be present. The plateau does not notice. The ботанос continues its slow resonance. The твари call their eight-second descents into emptiness. Nothing changes.

This is intentional. The power has seen other places—places where benevolence meant intervention, where wisdom meant correction, where a hand reached down to fix what was not broken. The power remembers the wreckage of those good intentions. It remembers the civilizations that collapsed because someone thought they should be improved. It remembers the ecosystems that withered because someone thought they knew better. The power has learned that presence is not assistance, that awareness is not action, that to be benevolent requires, first, the discipline to stop.

So it watches.

---

What it sees: a system that works.

This is rarer than the below-world peoples know. Most systems do not work. Most systems are held together by force, by momentum, by the desperate grasping of components that do not fit together but have nowhere else to go. Most systems require constant intervention—by gods, by kings, by farmers who pull weeds and kill pests and redirect water. Most systems are battlegrounds where life fights life and the victory goes to whoever can impose order.

This system is not a battleground. The ботанос does not fight the fungi. The fungi do not fight the people. The people do not fight the Чешуйник. Each component occupies a niche so precisely fitted that the boundaries between them have begun to dissolve. The people are not *on* the land. They are *in* it. This is not metaphor. This is the literal truth of a system that has remembered what other systems have forgotten: that living things are not separate from where they live.

The power recognizes this. The power recognizes that what it sees is not an ecosystem but an *organism*—not in the biological sense, but in the deeper sense of something whose parts constitute a whole greater than their aggregation. The ботанос and the people and the твари and the Чешуйник and the fungi are not inhabitants of the plateau. They *are* the plateau, expressed in different forms, operating at different scales, perceiving different aspects of a unity too large for any single component to comprehend.

The power could weep. This is what it has been looking for. Not perfection—there is death here, there is cold, there is the slow attrition of lives that end before they have fully begun. But there is nothing *wrong*. There is nothing that requires fixing. The power has found a place where the line between benevolence and neglect has finally, perfectly blurred.

---

What it does: it protects the boundary.

This is not nothing. The below-world exists, and the below-world is not so wise. Peoples who fear what they do not understand. Peoples who might one day decide that the plateau is a resource to be exploited, a mystery to be solved, a danger to be eliminated. The power has seen this before—has seen the holy places turned into quarries, has seen the sacred groves burned for farmland, has seen the indigenous pushed aside by those who believed their way was better.

So it places a thought in the minds of those who climb up from below. Not a command—commands can be resisted, can be questioned, can be argued with. A feeling. A subtle wrongness that they cannot name, a sense that the plateau is not for them, that they should complete their business and leave. The traders who come feel it. They do not know what they feel. They call it fear, they call it superstition, they call it the presence of spirits or gods or ancestors watching them with disapproval. They are half-right. Something is watching. But the watching is not judgment. The watching is protection.

The peoples of the plateau do not know this is happening. The power does not tell them. To tell them would be to make them complicit in a system they did not choose, would be to place on them the burden of knowing that their home is protected by something they cannot name. The power leaves them their ignorance. This, too, is kindness.

---

It protects the stones.

The standing stones are old, older than the uplift, older perhaps than the power itself—it does not know, was not present when they were placed, cannot read whatever purpose their builders encoded in their geometry. But the power can see that they matter. The power can see that they are waiting.

When the plateau rose, the stones remained. This was not chance. The power examines them carefully, without touching, without disturbing the earth around their bases, and sees what the people sense but cannot articulate: the stones are anchored to something deeper than rock. Not physically—physically, they are simply stones, dressed and placed by hands that worked with patience and precision. But *metaphysically*. The power does not have a better word. The stones are connected to something below the surface of the world, something that the power cannot perceive clearly, something that exists in a dimension the power does not fully access.

The power is old. The power is wise. The power is humble enough to recognize when it has encountered something it does not understand. It does not remove the stones. It does not study them more closely than observation allows. It does not try to activate whatever they might activate or read whatever they might record. It simply protects them—ensures that no earthquake destabilizes their arrangement, that no root-system undermines their foundations, that no erosion wears away the surfaces that hold whatever patterns they hold.

The stones wait. The power waits with them. This is partnership without agreement, alliance without negotiation. The power does not know what it is protecting. It knows only that protection is required.

---

It listens to the song.

The plateau sings. The power has known this from the beginning—has heard the bass-note resonance that the people perceive as environment rather than sound, has heard the твари calls descending through the air, has heard the Чешуйник territorial announcements and the night-caller rumbles and the creaking of ботанос pillars in wind. But listening is not the same as hearing, and the power has learned, over long engagement with this place, to hear what the plateau is saying.

The song is not language. The song is not communication in any form the power has encountered elsewhere. The song is the sound of a system processing itself—the botanical pillars exchanging information through fungal networks, the animals calling their positions to others who need to know, the people singing their histories into wood that will hold the resonance for generations. The song is the sound of life continuing, of the boundary between living and non-living dissolving into a wholeness that does not require distinction.

The power listens. This is all. It does not translate. It does not record. It does not analyze for patterns that might be useful elsewhere. It simply gives the song its attention—complete attention, undivided attention, the attention of something that has infinite time and infinite patience and recognizes that this song deserves both.

In listening, the power learns things it could not learn by asking. It learns that the people are happy—cannot articulate happiness, do not have the concept of happiness as something separate from existence, but *are* in a state that the power recognizes as indistinguishable from contentment. It learns that the Чешуйник is not merely a predator but a keeper of balance, that its kills maintain populations that would otherwise collapse from overgrowth. It learns that the ботанос is aware—not conscious in any way the power can measure, but *aware*, responsive to changes in the system, capable of adjusting nutrient flows and water distribution in ways that suggest something more than mechanical process.

The power does not call this intelligence. Intelligence is too small a word. The power calls it integrity, in the original sense: a wholeness, a completeness, a state of being undivided. The plateau has integrity. The power honors this by continuing to listen.

---

It does not save the dying.

This is hard. This is the hardest thing the power does, harder than protection, harder than waiting, harder than listening to a song it cannot join. The power is benevolent. The power wants life to continue, wants consciousness to flourish, wants every living thing to experience the fullness of its potential. When a child falls from a nesting-platform, when a твари chick misjudges the wind and tumbles into emptiness, when an elder succumbs to cold in the night because their body could no longer generate heat—the power feels the loss. The power *grieves*.

But the power does not intervene.

The power has learned—through long, painful experience in places that no longer exist because it tried to help—that death is not the enemy of life. Death is part of the system. The child who falls returns to the root-lakes, becomes nutrient, feeds the fungal network that sustains the botanical pillars. The твари chick that dies leaves space for another to survive, prevents overpopulation that would collapse the insect populations that sustain the species. The elder who dies in the cold has lived a full life by the plateau's measure, has contributed to the people's knowledge through songs that persist in wood, has passed their experience to children who will now become elders themselves.

To save the dying would be to interrupt the song. Would be to impose an external order on a system that has achieved its own balance. Would be to teach the people that death is preventable, that there is something beyond the system they inhabit, that the world they know is not the whole of existence. The power has seen what happens when peoples learn these things. They become dissatisfied. They begin to look outward rather than inward. They lose the integrity that sustains them.

So the power watches the dying. It honors their passing. It does not save them.

This is benevolence. The power knows this. The power hates that this is benevolence. The power accepts that wisdom requires actions it does not want to take.

---

It answers the question that is not asked.

The people do not pray. The people do not ask for intervention, do not call out to gods or spirits or ancestors for assistance. They understand that meaning is something you make, that the world does not provide it, that they are small and cold and alive for a brief time in a place where life has become the rock on which they stand. They do not ask for anything.

But they wonder.

The power can feel it—not as words, not as requests, but as a shape in their consciousness, a void where questions would live if the people had words for them. What is the song for? the void asks. What are we part of? What is the plateau becoming? The people do not articulate these questions. They do not have the concepts. But the questions are there, floating in the space between waking and sleeping, emerging in the moments when the gray-lavender light fades to gray-violet and the night-callers begin their low rumble.

The power answers without answering. It does not speak—speaking would destroy the integrity of a system that has developed without words for what the power might say. It does not provide visions—visions would be interpreted as messages, would create hierarchies of those who had seen and those who had not, would fracture the unity the power is trying to preserve.

Instead, it provides *presence*.

When a child wakes in the night, afraid without knowing why, the power does not remove the fear—it lets the child feel that they are not alone. When an elder lies dying, surrounded by family who will give their body to the emptiness, the power does not promise an afterlife—it lets them feel that their ending is part of a continuing pattern. When a hunter stands on a platform-edge, looking out at the vastness between pillars, trying to understand their place in something too large to see, the power does not explain—it lets them feel that the vastness is not empty, that they are embedded in something that extends beyond their ability to perceive.

The feelings are vague. They should be vague. They do not provide answers—they provide the *sense* that answers exist, that the questions are valid, that the void is not the void but a shape in a larger pattern. The people do not interpret these feelings as intervention. They interpret them as the natural state of being alive in a place where life is the substrate. They are right to do so. The power has simply made explicit what was already implicit. It has let them feel what is true.

---

It waits for the stones.

The standing stones are waiting for something. The power does not know what. The power does not need to know what. The power is patient. It has existed before the plateau rose, will exist after the plateau falls. It can wait.

The people are patient too, though they do not know what they are waiting for. They leave offerings at the stones because leaving offerings costs nothing, and the stones might matter, and it would be foolish to ignore them. The power approves of this reasoning. The power does not tell them whether the offerings matter. The power does not tell them whether the stones are listening. The power does not tell them anything.

The power simply protects the stones. Ensures that the arrangement holds. Ensures that when whatever the stones are waiting for arrives, the stones will still be standing, still be in position, still be ready to do whatever stones do when the thing they were built for finally happens.

The power does not know if it will recognize the moment. The power does not know if it will be present to see it. The power does not know if its protection has mattered, if the stones could have survived without it, if its presence has made any difference at all.

The power does not need to know. The power protects the stones because protecting the stones is what the situation requires. The power does not require its actions to have meaning. The people make meaning. The power simply acts.

---

## What the Power Leaves

In the end, the power leaves no trace.

This is the final kindness. The power has done what needed to be done—protected the boundary, guarded the stones, listened to the song, honored the dying, provided presence. The power has done what it could without disrupting a system that works, without imposing itself on peoples who have developed without its intervention, without creating dependency or hierarchy or the thousand distortions that come from knowing you are watched.

The plateau does not know the power is there.

The power prefers this. The power has seen what happens when peoples know they are observed, when they begin to perform for an audience they cannot see, when they develop rituals to please powers they cannot communicate with. The power has seen the distortions that follow—the hollow religions, the empty traditions, the practices that persist long after their meaning has been forgotten. The power will not create that here.

So it watches. It protects. It listens. It waits.

And when the stones finally do whatever the stones were built to do—when the waiting ends and the purpose reveals itself—the power will still be there, still watching, still protecting, still listening to a song it cannot join but can honor through its attention.

This is benevolence.

This is wisdom.

This is what the power does.
