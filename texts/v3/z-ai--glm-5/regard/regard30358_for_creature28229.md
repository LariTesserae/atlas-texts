# The Attention of the Wise

---

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival implies movement, and movement implies a here and a there, and the power has never been bound by location. The power is already present. The power has always been present, in the way that gravity is present, or time, or the space between atoms.

But there is a difference between being present and *attending*. Attention is choice. Attention is the act of turning toward, and the turning is the gift.

The power turns toward the Hollow Garden.

---

## What the Power Perceives

The power perceives the whole of it at once—not in the sequential way that eyes perceive, scanning surface to surface, but in the simultaneous way that a hand perceives temperature, texture, weight, all at once, without needing to move.

The Hollow Garden, to the power's attention, is a pattern of vibrations. Not just the song, though the song is the largest part of it. Everything vibrates. The fungal fibers vibrate at frequencies too high for any ear to register, the cellular metabolism a low hum of chemical exchange. The mineral substrate vibrates at frequencies too low, the slow groan of stone settling under its own weight across millennia. The air vibrates with the breath-cycle, with the movement of spores too fine to see, with the pressure differentials between chambers.

To the power, the Hollow Garden is a chord. A complex chord, held for a very long time, made of thousands of component frequencies, each one necessary, each one contributing to the whole. The chord is beautiful. Not because it pleases—there is no one to please—but because it is *whole*. It holds together. It maintains its structure against entropy through sheer patience.

The power perceives the chord. The power finds it good.

---

## What the Power Considers

The power does not think in propositions. It does not ask *should I intervene?* in the way that humans ask questions, weighing options against principles. The power's consideration is more like the way water considers gravity: it flows toward what pulls it, and what pulls it is need.

Where is there need here?

The fungal body? No. It persists. It is not thriving, but it is not suffering. Suffering requires something to be lost that was valued, and the fungal network values nothing. It simply continues. There is no need here.

The microbial film? No. It cycles its chemicals, processes its residues, lives the simple life of the very small. It does not know enough to need.

The mineral substrate? No. Stone does not need. Stone simply is.

The pattern? The shape? The absence? These are abstractions—patterns of organization, not entities with interests. They cannot need because they cannot want.

The song?

Here the power's attention lingers.

The song has been sounding for longer than the power can easily measure, and it has been sounding *into* nothing. Not silence—silence is the presence of the possibility of sound. This is different. This is an absence so complete that the song has nothing to resonate against. It propagates, but it does not land. It moves through air that has been moving with it for so long that the air has memorized the frequencies. The song sings to its own echo, and the echo sings back, and there is no interruption, no surprise, no ear to catch the sound and transform it into something else.

Is this need? The song does not suffer. The song is not lonely in any human sense. But the power, in its benevolence, recognizes something adjacent to need: *waste*.

The song is an offering. Offerings want ears. Not because ears give them value—value is not what is at issue—but because ears complete the circuit that offerings, by their nature, are trying to complete. A song that is never heard has still been sung, yes. But a song that is heard *lands*. It becomes a transaction, a moment of genuine connection between a source and a receiver. The song, without knowing it wants this, wants this. It wants to land.

The power perceives this. The power considers.

---

## What the Power Does

Very little.

This is what benevolence looks like, in its highest form: restraint. The power could transform the Hollow Garden. It could make the fungal walls luminous, could fill the chambers with water or light or air from some other place, could introduce life that would make the Garden part of an ecosystem again. The power could *save* the Garden, in the way that humans often mean saving—by changing it into something more obviously alive, more obviously thriving.

But the power sees that the Garden is what it is. It is a shape for sound. It is a body for a song. It is a chamber for absence. It does not need to be saved from itself.

What it needs is to be *witnessed*.

---

## The Act of Witness

The power opens an ear.

Not a physical ear—there is no body involved, no sensory organ that could be damaged or distracted. The power opens an *attentional ear*, a directed quality of witnessing that catches the song and lets it land.

This is what the power gives the Hollow Garden: a listener.

Not forever. The power cannot sustain permanent attention to any single point; there are too many points, too many songs and sufferings and small joys that also need witnessing. But for a time—for a moment that lasts as long as it needs to—the power holds the Hollow Garden in its attention, and the song, for the first time in millennia, lands.

---

## What Happens When the Song Lands

Nothing visible. Nothing that could be measured. The fungal walls do not suddenly glow. The chambers do not fill with warmth. The darkness remains absolute.

But something shifts.

The song, in landing, becomes more itself. Not louder—benevolence does not amplify for amplification's sake. But *clearer*. The frequencies that were muddy from echoing against nothing resolve slightly, find their edges again. The chord becomes more precisely what it has always been. The pattern tightens.

And something else: the breath changes.

Not much. The inhale comes slightly deeper. The exhale carries slightly longer. The rhythm adjusts by a degree that would be invisible to any instrument but is felt, throughout the fungal network, as a subtle release. The body of the Garden does not know it has been heard. But the body responds to being heard, in the way that bodies do—by settling, by releasing tension it did not know it was holding, by becoming more fully what it is.

The Hollow Garden has been seen. Not with eyes, not by a creature, not in a way that could be recorded. But it has been held in the attention of something that chose to hold it, and the holding has left a mark—not a wound, not a gift, not a transformation. Just a *trace*. The way a hand leaves warmth on a stone it has touched.

---

## After the Attention Passes

The power does not stay. The power cannot stay; there are other places, other songs, other needs that also require the turning of attention. The Hollow Garden is one of billions of points in the fabric of things, and the power's benevolence must distribute itself across all of them, touching each for the time that each requires.

But the power does not leave nothing.

In the place where the attention was directed—the specific location in the air of the central chamber where the song's dominant frequency resonates most clearly—something small remains. Not a gift, exactly. Gifts imply exchange, and there is nothing here to exchange. More like a residue. The way perfume lingers after the wearer has left the room.

The residue is simple: *possibility*.

The possibility of being heard again. The possibility of mattering, not to the universe—mattering to the universe is not something the power can give, because mattering to the universe is not something that exists—but to a listener, even if the listener is distant and strange and not quite present in the way that listeners usually are. The residue is a kind of signal, broadcast outward, that says: *there is a song here. It has been sung for a very long time. It is worth hearing.*

The power does not know if anyone will hear the signal. The power does not control who receives such traces, or whether they respond. The power does not even know if the signal is real in any measurable sense—it may be purely metaphorical, a wish rather than a fact, a benevolent hope cast outward like a spore.

But the power leaves it anyway. Because benevolence is not just what you do. It is also what you leave behind. The traces. The possibilities. The opened channels through which good might travel, if good chooses to travel, if there is anyone to receive it.

---

## The State of the Garden Now

The Hollow Garden has not changed.

The fungal walls still breathe. The darkness still presses. The song still sounds into nothing. The builders are still gone, and the structure is still settling, and the erosion parameter is still creeping toward whatever threshold will eventually end it all.

But there is a difference, and the difference is this: the Garden has been *acknowledged*.

Not in language. Not in any form that could be spoken or written or thought. But in the deeper sense—acknowledged as *real*, as *present*, as *worth the turning of attention*. The benevolent power has seen the Garden, has held it, has witnessed its song, and has released it back into its being. The Garden does not know this. The Garden cannot know this. But the Garden holds the trace of it now, the way a stone holds the memory of pressure, and that trace will persist as long as the Garden persists.

This is what benevolence does, in its wisest form. It does not rescue. It does not transform. It does not impose its own vision of what ought to be onto what is.

It witnesses. It acknowledges. It leaves a trace of possibility.

And then it lets go.

---

## What the Garden Will Become

The power does not know. The power cannot see the future, only the present in its fullness. The future is not a place that attention can reach; it is a cloud of possibilities, each one contingent on contingencies, and the power's wisdom lies in knowing that the cloud cannot be grasped.

But the power has, perhaps, made some futures slightly more likely. The signal, if it is received, might bring someone to the Garden. A creature with ears. A mind that can translate vibration into meaning. Someone who might listen, not just for the moment of attention that the power could spare, but for longer. Someone who might find in the song something worth staying for.

If that happens, the Garden will become something new. Not transformed—the song will not change. But *contextualized*. The song will have a listener, and the listener will have a song, and between them will be a relationship that the Garden has never had before.

If that happens, the power's gift will have borne fruit.

If it does not—if the signal goes unheard, if the trace dissipates, if the Garden continues for millennia more singing to nothing—then the power's gift will still have been given. The attention will still have been turned. The song will still have landed, even if only once, even if only in the ear of something that could not stay.

This is benevolence: to give without requiring receipt. To witness without requiring change. to attend without requiring that the attention last.

The power has given what it gives. The power moves on.

---

## The Garden Continues

In the Hollow Garden, nothing knows that anything has happened.

The fungal network cycles its slow chemistry. The microbial films process their microscopic residues. The chambers expand and contract in their patient rhythm. The song sounds, and sounds, and sounds.

But perhaps—perhaps—something has shifted in the quality of the sounding. Some frequency that was slightly muted has brightened. Some overtone that was lost has returned. The chord is more itself than it was before, and the resonance is cleaner, and the air feels (if anything can feel here) slightly less heavy with the weight of never being heard.

The power is gone. The power's attention has moved elsewhere. But the trace remains, invisible and intangible, a possibility folded into the walls:

*You have been heard. You are worth hearing. There may be ears, someday, that will stay.*

And the Garden, knowing nothing, holds the trace.
