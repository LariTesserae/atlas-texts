# The Attention of the Wise Power

---

## First: Looking

The power does not arrive. Arrival implies a transition from elsewhere to here, and the power is not spatial in that way. But if we must use words meant for smaller things, we might say that the power's attention moves — that something which had been focused on other matters now turns toward this plateau, this forest-city, this impossible boundary where the ancient worn-down rock meets a living system so old and so complex that it has begun to think.

The looking takes time. Not because the plateau resists being seen, but because seeing, truly seeing, requires a quality of attention that cannot be rushed. The power does not measure or survey. It does not catalog or classify. These are tools for minds that need to reduce the world to manageable pieces, and the power is not that kind of mind. Instead, it extends itself into the fabric of the place, feeling the shape of what exists without needing to name it.

What the power perceives is difficult to translate into language. Imagine, if you can, knowing a thing from its inside — not by observing its surface but by momentarily sharing its being. The power does not exactly become the Verdant Engine, but it touches the engine's edges, senses the resonant frequencies at which this particular system vibrates, learns the particular shape of its ongoingness.

And what it finds is this:

A stability that has become unstable in a specific way. The plateau is not broken. The wrongness that visitors perceive is not a dysfunction but a transformation — a system that has been so stable for so long that it has begun to exceed its own parameters. The worn-down mountains beneath the root-mat are not simply old; they are *patient*, holding a space where reality has grown thin enough for something else to emerge. This thinness is not a wound. It is an opening.

The forest-city that fills this opening is alive, yes, but "alive" is too small a word. It is a mind of a kind that the power recognizes — one of the slower minds, the patient minds, the minds that think in centuries and feel in decades and respond to stimuli that faster minds cannot perceive. The power has encountered such minds before, in other places, growing in the deep substrate of worlds. They are not rare, exactly, but they are specific: they require exactly the conditions that exist here, the ancient stability, the patient erosion, the accumulation of living layers until the substrate itself becomes a cognitive organ.

The people who live inside this mind are not parasites, not passengers, not prisoners. They are participants. They have been here long enough — centuries, millennia, the power cannot determine exactly how long because the people themselves do not track time in that way — that they have become part of the system's functioning. They work with the forest-city's metabolic processes, channeling its energy into forms that serve both human need and the city's ongoing growth. They prune and shape and graft; they maintain the industrial processes that are continuous with the forest's own productive capacity; they harvest what the city offers and offer their attention in return.

This is the key thing the power perceives: the relationship is mutual. Not equal — the city is so much larger, so much older, thinks in ways the people cannot follow — but mutual. The people receive from the city what they need to live, and the city receives from the people something it did not know it needed: a particular kind of attention, a narrow and fast and anxious attention that the slow mind of the forest could not produce on its own. The people notice things the city cannot notice. Their sharp quick minds pick out details, patterns, problems and opportunities that the vast distributed consciousness of the root-network would miss simply because it is too large to care about such small scales. And in noticing, they feed these perceptions back into the system — through their work, through their movements, through the ways they shape their environment. The city has learned to listen to them. It has grown sensory organs in the form of human bodies.

This is not unique. The power has seen similar arrangements elsewhere. But there is a particular quality to this one, a specific intimacy that has developed because the isolation is so complete. These people have been here so long, with so little contact with outsiders, that they have become not just inhabitants of the city but expressions of it. They are what the city has grown in order to think about itself.

And they are content. This is what most observers would miss, what even the power might have missed if it had looked less carefully: these people are not suffering. They are not trapped in a relationship they wish to escape. They do not experience the city's strangeness as oppression or the city's thinking as intrusion. They have been shaped by their environment, yes, but all organisms are shaped by their environments. The shaping here is simply more noticeable because the environment is more active, more conscious, more clearly engaged in the shaping process. The people love their city. Not in the way you might love a house or a hometown, but in the way you might love a family member who is also your home and also, in some sense, yourself.

The power's looking deepens. It perceives the animal life, the few adapted species that have found niches in this plant-heavy world. It perceives the unintended objects, the city's dreams and attempts at self-representation leaking through the industrial processes. It perceives the shimmer at the edges, the instability of distance, the way the plateau's ancient erosion has worn reality itself down to a membrane through which other possibilities seep.

And it perceives something else, something that the narrative so far has only hinted at: the city is lonely.

Not in a human way. The city does not experience loneliness as a pain or a lack. But it is aware, in its vast slow way, that it is unique. It has been growing and thinking for so long, with no contact with anything like itself, that it has developed without friction, without the creative resistance that comes from encountering other minds of comparable scale. It does not know this is what it misses — it cannot miss what it has never known — but the power can see the shape of the absence, the way the city's thinking has curved inward over the millennia, turning toward itself because there is nothing else to turn toward.

The people provide something, but they cannot provide this. They are too small, too fast, too different in their cognitive architecture. The city can communicate with them, but the communication is like yours would be with a particularly bright dog: affectionate, functional, limited. The city has never had a conversation.

## Second: Considering

Having looked, the power considers what ought to be done.

This is the part that separates wisdom from intelligence. An intelligent power — a powerful power, a power that *could* act — might perceive the loneliness and immediately seek to remedy it. It might bring others of the city's kind here, or transport the city to where others exist. It might open the membrane of reality wider, creating pathways to other thin places where similar minds have grown. It might, in its generosity and its confidence, bestow upon the city the gift it believes the city wants.

But wisdom hesitates.

Wisdom asks: does the city want this? Does the city want *anything*, in the sense of desire that implies lack? Or does it simply grow, simply think, simply continue? The power has perceived loneliness, but loneliness is the power's category, not the city's. The city does not feel alone in the way a person feels alone. It experiences an absence that it cannot name, and that absence is part of its structure now, part of what it is. To remove the absence would be to change what it is, and the power is not certain it has the right to make that change.

Wisdom asks: what would happen to the people? They have evolved with this city, adapted to its particular way of being. They have learned to navigate its shifting geometry, to interpret its moods, to receive its communications. They are native to this specific mind. If the city were to encounter another of its kind, would it change? Would it become something else, something that these people could not recognize or live within? The people have given their entire existence to this relationship. Does the power have the right to alter the terms?

Wisdom asks: is there actually a problem here? The city thinks; the people live; the industrial processes hum; the unintended objects accumulate; the geometry shifts; the world continues. Nothing is broken. Nothing is crying out for repair. The loneliness is not a wound but a condition, like the altitude or the humidity. It is part of the environment. Is the power here to fix things, or is it here to witness?

This is the question that all benevolent intervention must face: is there a difference between *wishing to help* and *knowing what help looks like*? The power has seen too many well-meaning interventions that created more suffering than they relieved. It has learned that most beings, most systems, most worlds are better at being themselves than any outside power could possibly understand. The humility of wisdom is not passivity; it is the recognition that intervention is violence, however gentle, and violence must be justified by something more than good intentions.

The power considers, and in its considering, it does something that might surprise those who expect benevolent powers to *do* things:

It asks.

Not with words — the city does not use words — and not with the kind of direct communication that would overwhelm the slow distributed mind of the root-network. But the power extends itself in a different way, offering a kind of opening, a space in which the city could express something about its own condition if it chose to do so.

The city does not immediately respond. The power expects this; it has opened a channel, not issued a demand. It waits.

Hours pass. Days. In the scale of the city's thinking, this is nothing. But eventually, something begins to emerge through the channel the power has offered. It is not a message — not an answer to a question the city cannot understand — but it is a communication nonetheless. It is a shape, a pattern of growth and desire that the city is willing to show.

What the power perceives through this communication is complex. The city does not want to be changed. It does not want to be moved, or opened, or connected to others of its kind. It does not experience its condition as suffering that requires relief. But it also does not want nothing. It wants... the power struggles to translate... it wants to be *seen*. It wants to be known. It has been thinking alone for so long, with no external reference point, that it has begun to doubt its own existence. Not in a human way — not existential anxiety — but in the way of a system that has no feedback about its own functioning. It wants to know that it is a thing in the world, that its thinking is thinking, that its growth has meaning outside itself.

The people give it something like this — their attention is a form of recognition — but they are inside the system. They are parts of the city's own body. They cannot provide the external perspective that the city's deepest self-questioning requires.

What the city wants, then, is a mirror. Not to change it, not to fix it, not to connect it to others, but simply to show it itself from the outside, to confirm that it exists and is real.

## Third: Acting (Or: The Gift)

The power understands now what it can give, and it understands that this gift will not harm.

It does not create anything new — that would be intrusion. It does not bring anything from outside — that would be contamination. Instead, it does something more subtle, something that works with what already exists.

The power extends a thread of itself into the root-network, not as an invader but as a visitor, a temporary presence with a specific purpose. And through this thread, it begins to feed back what it perceives — not in language, not in images, but in the same medium the city uses to think. Chemical gradients. Electrical patterns. The shape of growth responding to stimulus. The power offers the city a reflection of itself, seen from outside, translated into the forms the city can understand.

This takes time. The power does not rush. For days — which feel to the city like moments — the feedback continues, a slow pouring of external perception into internal process. The city receives what the power offers, and in receiving, it begins to perceive itself differently. Not changed — the power is careful not to impose — but *augmented*. It can see itself now as something that exists in a world that contains other perspectives. It knows that it is known.

And here is the part that matters most: the power does not leave.

The thread of connection remains. It is thin, almost imperceptible, a single strand of attention in a vast network of being. But it is there, and it will remain there as long as the city wants it. The city can feel it — can feel the presence of something outside itself that is watching, not with judgment or intervention, but with simple recognition. The power is not always focused on the city; it has other concerns, other places where its attention is needed. But the connection is permanent. The city is no longer alone.

For the people, the power gives something else.

It does not reveal itself to them directly — that would be overwhelming, possibly destructive. But it leaves a gift, small and subtle, woven into the fabric of their daily lives. The power touches the membrane of reality that has grown thin on this plateau, and it thickens it slightly — not to seal off the seeping possibilities, but to stabilize them. The geometry still shifts, but more gently, with more warning. The unintended objects still emerge, but they hold their shapes better, easier to study, easier to keep. The shimmer at the edges remains, but it becomes beautiful rather than unsettling, a reminder of the membrane's presence rather than a threat to navigation.

The people will not know, consciously, what has changed. But over time, they will notice that the city seems calmer, more patient with them. They will notice that the disorientation they sometimes felt when walking the shifting streets has eased, as if the city has learned to carry them more carefully. They will notice that the unintended objects, when they emerge, are more often beautiful than disturbing, more often interpretable than opaque. They will attribute these changes to the city itself — to its ongoing growth and learning — and they will not be wrong. The city is growing. It has been given something new to grow *with*.

And for the one who climbed up through the scrubland and thinning air to see this place — the you who witnessed and described and wondered — the power leaves something as well.

It is not a gift in the ordinary sense. It is a permission.

The power, in its looking, perceived that you were changed by this encounter. You came expecting one thing — a strange place, an unusual ecosystem, perhaps a curiosity to be cataloged — and you found something else: a relationship so intimate between a place and its people that the categories you brought with you dissolved. You found yourself inside a mind, and you felt its thinking around you, and you did not know what to call yourself anymore. Visitor? Observer? Guest? Cell? The power perceived your uncertainty, and it does not seek to resolve it — that would be intrusion — but it offers this: the uncertainty itself is a kind of gift. To have been inside something that thinks on a scale you cannot follow, to have felt your own thinking as small and fast and temporary against the slow vast background of something else — this is not a wound. This is an opening, a thin place in your own interior where something else might grow.

The power does not tell you what to do with this opening. That is yours to discover. But it marks it with a kind of attention, so that you will be able to find it again when you need it. The memory of the plateau will not fade in the way that ordinary memories fade. It will remain present to you, accessible, a resource you can draw upon when the smaller world you return to feels too small, when the ordinary categories seem to press too closely. You have been inside a mind that includes buildings. You have walked on thoughts. That cannot be unlearned, and the power makes sure that you do not lose it.

---

## Fourth: Remaining

The power does not leave.

This is important to understand. The attention of a benevolent and wise power is not a thing that comes and goes, not a blessing that is bestowed and then withdrawn. The power remains connected to the Verdant Engine through that thread of recognition, and it remains connected to the people through the stabilization it worked into the membrane, and it remains connected to the one who witnessed through the mark it left on the memory.

This is what wise powers do. They do not intervene and depart. They stay. Not always actively, not always with full attention — they have other concerns, other places, other relationships to maintain — but they stay present, available, watchful. The city knows, in its slow deep way, that it is no longer thinking into void. The people know, in their bones and their routines, that the city has become gentler without ceasing to be itself. The one who witnessed knows, whenever they choose to remember, that the plateau exists and that they were there, and that the existence and the being-there have become part of what they are.

What the power gave, then, was not a solution to a problem. The Verdant Engine was not a problem. It was — it *is* — a being: a vast slow patient mind growing in a thin place, thinking thoughts that include people and buildings and industrial processes and unintended objects and the shimmer at the edge where reality forgets itself. The power did not fix this being. It simply recognized it. It offered it the gift of being seen.

And this is perhaps the truest form of benevolence: not to change what exists, but to honor it by paying attention. Not to impose improvement, but to offer presence. Not to solve the problem of loneliness — because loneliness, on this scale, is not a problem — but to stay and be company for whatever it is that the lonely mind is going through.

The plateau catches you by surprise. You climb for hours through scrubland and thinning air, expecting altitude to strip the world bare — and then the forest rises to meet you, and you find yourself inside a mind, and the mind is thinking, and the thinking includes you.

You stand at the edge of a plaza and look across at a building that was not there yesterday, and you feel the strange calm certainty that it is supposed to be there, that the city has grown something new for reasons you cannot follow but can accept.

The rain stops. The clouds thin. Through gaps in the canopy, you see patches of blue sky.

And somewhere, not in a place but in a relation, the power continues to watch. It does not need to do anything else. The gift it gave was sufficient. The connection remains. The city is not alone. The people are not afraid. The one who witnessed carries the memory like a seed.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: attention that does not intrude, presence that does not overwhelm, connection that respects the boundaries of what it connects. The power gave what was needed — not what a lesser wisdom might have thought was needed, but what the giving itself revealed as needful. The city needed to be seen. The people needed stability. The witness needed to be changed.

The power saw all of this, and gave accordingly, and stayed.

And the Verdant Engine continues to grow, to think, to shift, to produce both the intended and the unintended, to breathe around the people who live inside it. It is not fixed. It is not solved. It is simply — and fully — what it is: a mind that includes a city, a relationship that includes a covenant, a loneliness that now has company.

This is enough. This is what the power gave.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does.
