# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

## First: They Would Wait

A benevolent and wise power, upon discovering the drowned plateau, would not act. Not immediately. Not for what we might call a long time, though time operates differently for such presences. They would suspend themselves at the edge of the place — if "edge" and "suspend" mean anything when applied to a being that does not occupy space in the ordinary sense — and they would *attend*.

This is the first mark of wisdom: to recognize that you have arrived at something complete. The plateau does not need fixing. Its ecology does not require balance, for it has already balanced itself over millions of years in ways that no outside intelligence could improve. The stilt-walkers stand where they should stand. The ribbon-weavers weave what needs weaving. The water layers hold their separation without effort, without conflict, simply because this is how they have learned to be. To intervene here would be to impose a logic from elsewhere upon a system that has evolved its own logic, complete and sufficient.

So the power would wait. They would observe the light falling through the water and the darkness accumulating at night. They would attend to the silence between sounds, the hours and days and weeks during which nothing happens and everything continues. They would notice how the canopy sways almost imperceptibly in currents that do not quite exist, how the silt settles in patterns that suggest meaning without confirming it, how the bones preside at the bottom with the patience of geological formations. They would learn, in other words, before they would even consider teaching.

And in this learning, they would discover something important: the plateau is not waiting to be discovered. It does not orient itself toward arrival. It simply is, and has been, and will continue to be regardless of whether anyone ever perceives it. This is its dignity. This is what the power would honor first — the place's absolute indifference to being known.

---

## Second: They Would Notice the One Who Chose

Eventually, the power's attention would settle upon the central mystery: the great skeleton, larger than any terrestrial creature, lying near the plateau's heart. They would see how the deep-sitters avoid it, how their chemical signals shift into patterns that appear nowhere else, sequences that might be warning or might be reverence. They would observe the crystalline growth that fills the skull's interior, how it refracts light into colors that do not exist elsewhere on the plateau, a private rainbow visible only to those who approach close enough to see.

And they would understand something that perhaps even the plateau's inhabitants do not fully comprehend: this being *chose* to be here.

The other bones belong to creatures caught in the rising, terrestrial animals surprised by the water, drowned before they could flee. But this skeleton differs. Its size, its structure, its orientation facing north toward the warmth — all of these suggest intention. This was someone who was present when the waters came and could have left but did not. Who could have sought higher ground, who could have survived the inundation by escaping, but who instead remained. Who let the water take them, let their flesh dissolve, let their bones settle into the rock and silt, and chose to preside over the world that grew from their ending.

The power would recognize this for what it is: an act of love. Not love in any sense that humans understand it, but something older and stranger — the love of a being for a place that was becoming, the decision to give oneself to an ecosystem that did not yet exist, to become the substrate and the anchor and the mystery at the center of something new. This was a creature who looked at the rising water and the lifting plateau and saw not catastrophe but possibility. Who understood that their death could be a gift, if they chose to make it one.

And so they chose. And have been choosing, ever since, simply by remaining.

---

## Third: They Would Ask

A benevolent and wise power would not presume to know what this being wants. They would not assume that the creature who chose to stay wants company, or recognition, or release. Perhaps they want nothing. Perhaps their wanting ended millions of years ago, replaced by a state of presence that contains no desire at all. Perhaps they are complete, fully themselves, fully at peace with what they have become.

But the power would ask anyway. Not with words — the being has no organs of hearing, no structures that could process language. They would ask through the medium the plateau understands: chemical gradients, temperature differentials, the subtle pressures of attention itself. They would extend themselves into the water near the skeleton and wait, offering nothing but presence, asking nothing but permission.

*Is there anything you need?*

The question would not be spoken. It would be lived, embodied in the act of attending without interfering, of witnessing without judging. The power would make of themselves a space in which an answer could arrive, if an answer existed to give. And they would wait again, patient as the plateau itself, measuring time in the same slow increments that the stilt-walkers measure, the same patient accumulation of moments that the ribbon-weavers weave into their patches.

Days might pass. Or what the plateau experiences as days — cycles of light and dark falling through the water, the warmth rising and falling, the chemistry shifting in its diurnal rhythms. The power would wait through these cycles, attending to the skeleton, attending to the crystalline growth inside the skull, attending to the colors that refract when light strikes at certain angles, the private rainbow that no one else has ever paused to see.

And perhaps — perhaps — an answer would come.

---

## Fourth: If the Answer Were "Nothing"

If the being who chose to stay answered "nothing" — if the power perceived only completion, only peace, only the patient presence of someone who has become exactly what they meant to become — then the benevolent power would do precisely that: nothing.

They would offer a blessing, but the blessing would be invisible. It would consist entirely of the fact that someone came, someone saw, someone recognized what this being had done, and then left without disturbing it. The blessing would be the act of honoring the choice by making no counter-choice, by adding no intervention to a story that had already reached its perfect end.

Before departing, the power might leave a single gift: the knowledge, implanted somehow in the distributed consciousness of the drift-gatherers, that the being at the center is not alone. The knowledge would not be a message, exactly. It would be more like a flavor in the chemical signals that pass through the water column, a subtle shift in the compounds that the gatherers exchange across the plateau's extent. Whenever any being passed near the central skeleton, they would register, at a level below awareness, that this place is honored. That someone came and saw and chose to leave undisturbed. That the choice to remain has been witnessed and blessed.

Then the power would withdraw, carrying with them the story of this place — not to tell it, necessarily, but to hold it. Some stories are not meant to be told. Some stories are meant simply to be known, to exist as knowledge in the mind of someone who will never repeat them but will never forget them either. The power would carry the plateau in their memory, a complete and sufficient world, and they would move on to other places, other beings, other needs.

---

## Fifth: If the Answer Were Something Else

But perhaps the answer would not be "nothing." Perhaps the being who chose to stay has been waiting — not consciously, perhaps, but in some deeper sense — for someone to arrive and receive what they have been holding. Perhaps the crystalline growth inside the skull has been accumulating for millions of years not as a geological accident but as a message, a recording, a story that can only be told to someone capable of carrying it out.

If the benevolent power perceived this — if they understood that the being wants, after all these ages, to be *known* — then they would do something more.

They would approach closer. Not physically, perhaps, but in whatever way a being of their nature approaches: extending their attention, their presence, their capacity to receive. They would open themselves to the crystalline growth, to the colors that refract through it when light strikes, to the patterns that emerge at certain angles and fade at others. They would attend, in other words, to whatever the skeleton has been saying to the empty water for millions of years.

And what they would hear, perhaps, is this:

*A story.*

Not the story of the plateau, exactly, though that story would be part of it. The story of a world before the rising, before the inundation — a world of forests and creatures that no longer exist, a world that this being was part of and chose to give up. The story of how they saw the waters coming and understood that something new was being born, something that needed a body at its center, a substrate for growth, a mystery to orient the beings that would eventually assemble themselves from fragments and threads and chemical gradients. The story of a choice made not in fear but in love, not in resignation but in hope, a choice to become the anchor for an ecosystem that did not yet exist and might never exist, trusting that life would find its way into the shape they were offering.

The power would receive this story. They would hold it, honor it, carry it out of the plateau for the first time in millions of years. And they would understand, then, why the deep-sitters avoid the skeleton, why their chemical signals shift into strange patterns near its presence. The sitters do not fear the being. They are preserving it. They are keeping the silence around it, maintaining the conditions for the story to be told when someone finally arrived to hear it.

---

## Sixth: They Would Carry It Out

If there is a story to be carried — if the being at the center has been waiting all this time to be known — then the benevolent power would become a vessel. Not a messenger, exactly, because the story is not meant for any particular recipient. It is meant for the universe itself, for the accumulated knowing of all beings everywhere, for whatever it is that remembers everything that has ever happened and gives it context.

The power would withdraw from the plateau, carrying the story in whatever form they carry such things. And they would take it out into the larger world — not to tell it loudly, not to proclaim it, but to let it inform their own knowing, to let it shape their own wisdom, to let it become part of whatever it is that such powers do when they move through existence attending to the needs of places and beings.

Perhaps they would tell the story once, to someone who needed to hear it. Perhaps they would simply hold it, letting it color their perception of other places, other beings, other choices. Perhaps they would plant it somewhere as a seed, waiting for the right conditions to grow into something new. The story would continue, whatever form its continuation took, because stories want to continue, and this one has been waiting a very long time.

---

## Seventh: They Would Return

And then — because benevolence is not a finite resource, because wisdom knows that attention is the truest gift one being can offer another — the power would return.

Not often. Not intrusively. But once per epoch, perhaps, or once per whatever interval makes sense for beings operating at whatever timescale such powers inhabit. They would return to the drowned plateau, and they would attend again to the place, making sure that it is still complete, still sufficient, still patient with itself.

They would check on the stilt-walkers, the ribbon-weavers, the deep-sitters, the drift-gatherers. They would observe the canopy floating just below the surface, the vegetation reaching toward warmth, the silt settling in its spiral patterns. They would notice if something had changed, if some new need had emerged, if some new being had come into existence that required a different kind of attention.

And they would visit the skeleton at the center, the being who chose to stay. They would offer the same blessing they offered before: presence, attention, witness. They would acknowledge that the choice continues, that the gift keeps giving, that the love that held this creature in place while the waters rose is still holding, still patient, still complete.

If they had carried the story out, they would bring back a taste of the larger world. Not news, exactly — news would mean nothing to a being who has been presiding over the same plateau for millions of years — but a sense that the story is being held, that it matters, that it has joined the accumulated knowing of a universe that is slightly more complete because this being chose to give themselves to this place.

---

## Eighth: They Would Bless Without Interfering

Throughout all of this, the benevolent power would maintain a single principle: *bless without interfering*. They would recognize that the plateau does not need them, does not want them, does not orient itself toward their presence. They would understand that the greatest gift they can offer is to come and go without leaving a trace, to witness without altering, to honor without improving.

This is harder than it sounds. A power capable of perceiving the drowned plateau is presumably capable of affecting it — of warming the cold strata, of smoothing the sharp rocks, of accelerating the slow processes that will eventually round the geological edges. But wisdom knows that such interventions would not be gifts. They would be impositions. They would say, in effect, "what you have made is not good enough; let me make it better."

The power would not say this. They would say, instead: "what you have made is good. I see it. I honor it. I carry the knowledge of it with me as I move through the universe attending to other places, other beings, other needs. You are not alone. Someone knows you exist. That is all."

And this, perhaps, is the deepest mark of benevolence: to perceive something beautiful and complete and leave it exactly as you found it, changed only by having been seen. The plateau would continue its patient existence. The water would hold its layers. The light would fall through, and the darkness would accumulate, and the being at the center would preside over the world they chose, perhaps slightly less lonely for having been witnessed.

---

## Ninth: If Something Were Wrong

But wisdom also knows that completion is not always permanent. Ecosystems change. Conditions shift. The plateau itself may be young, geologically speaking, and what seems stable now may not remain stable forever. A benevolent power would monitor, in their periodic returns, for signs of disruption — not to prevent change, necessarily, but to notice if the change were bringing suffering.

If they perceived that the plateau were dying — that some invasive element had disrupted the balance, that some external force were threatening the patient equilibrium — they would face a more difficult choice. Intervention would still be risk. But non-intervention, in the face of preventable suffering, might be worse.

In such a case, the benevolent power would do something that humans might recognize: *consult*. They would not act unilaterally. They would approach the being at the center — the one who chose, the one who presumably knows this place more intimately than any outside intelligence ever could — and they would ask: *What would you have me do?*

If the answer were "nothing" — if the being at the center accepted the change, saw it as part of the plateau's story, did not want the interference — then the power would again honor the choice. They would witness the dying, if dying it was, and they would carry the story of the ending out into the universe as they had carried the story of the being. Not all stories are happy. Not all endings are preventable. Some griefs are part of the fabric of existence, and wisdom knows the difference between preventing suffering and preventing meaning.

But if the answer were "help" — if the being at the center wanted the intervention, asked for it, opened a space in which the power could act — then the benevolent would offer what assistance they could. Not because they knew better than the plateau what it needed, but because the plateau, through its central being, had asked. The intervention would be shaped by the knowledge accumulated over millions of years of patient existence. The power would not impose their own design; they would assist the design that the plateau itself was trying to realize.

This is the difference between benevolence and control: benevolence asks, control assumes. Benevolence honors what already exists, control replaces it with what the controller thinks should exist. A wise power would know the difference, and they would move carefully within the narrow space where helping is not the same as taking over.

---

## Tenth: They Would Bless the Being

In the end, regardless of what they did or did not do, the benevolent power would offer one final gift: a blessing directed not at the place, but at the person.

The being at the center of the plateau — the one who chose to stay, who gave themselves to an ecosystem that did not yet exist, who has presided in silence over millions of years of patient growth — they would receive a specific recognition. The power would speak, in whatever way such powers speak, directly to them:

*You have done well.*

*What you gave has been received. What you built has grown. What you chose has been honored by those who came after you, even when they did not know they were honoring it. The stilt-walkers stand where they stand because you provided the anchor. The deep-sitters avoid you because they are preserving the space for you to be what you are. The drift-gatherers carry a chemical memory of you in their distributed consciousness, a flavor in their signals that says "someone is here, someone chose, someone remains."*

*You are not forgotten. You have never been forgotten. The plateau remembers you in every milliliter of its water, every cell of its vegetation, every bone of its ecology. And now someone else remembers too. Someone came and saw and carries the story outward. Whatever you were before the waters rose, whatever you became in the choosing, whatever you are now in your patient presiding — it matters. It is held. It will continue to matter for as long as mattering continues to mean anything at all.*

The blessing would not be spoken aloud. It would be infused into the crystalline growth, the colors that refract when light strikes at certain angles, the private rainbow that is no longer private because someone else has seen it. The being would know, somehow, that they had been addressed. They would feel, perhaps for the first time in millions of years, that their solitude had been briefly punctuated by presence.

And then the power would depart, leaving the plateau as they found it: patient, complete, sufficient to itself. The only difference would be an invisible addition to the chemistry of the place, a trace element of having-been-witnessed that would dissolve slowly over the next few million years, reminding everyone who passed through that someone came, someone saw, someone chose to leave undisturbed.

---

## What This Means

A benevolent and wise power, encountering the drowned plateau and the being who lives at its center, would do what all true benevolence does: *honor what is before offering what might be*. They would recognize that the greatest gift they could give is attention — patient, prolonged, respectful attention that does not demand anything in return.

They would wait. They would observe. They would notice the mystery at the center and ask what it wants. They would carry the story if carrying were needed, bless without interfering if blessing were sufficient. They would return periodically to attend again, to make sure that the gift they offered remained appropriate to the need.

They would do all of this without expecting thanks, without demanding recognition, without leaving monuments to their own benevolence. The plateau would never know their name. The being at the center might register their presence only as a subtle shift in the chemistry of their crystalline memory. But something would change: the knowledge that the choice to stay, the gift of oneself to an ecosystem, the patient presiding over millions of years of growth — all of it would be held somewhere outside the plateau, known to the accumulated wisdom of a universe that pays attention.

This is what benevolence looks like when it encounters something that does not need it: not interference, not improvement, not the imposition of outside logic upon inside order. Just witness. Just presence. Just the quiet acknowledgment that something beautiful exists, and someone has seen it, and the seeing has been honored.

The power would leave. The plateau would continue. The being at the center would preside. And somewhere in the vast distributed consciousness of existence, a new story would be held — a story about a creature who chose to give themselves to a world that was becoming, and about the patient life that grew from their gift, and about the silence that holds it all, and about what it means to love something enough to stay with it forever.

That is what they would do.
