# What the Power Did

The first thing the power did was stop.

It had come from a distance—though "distance" means little to such a being—to investigate the anomaly. A cavity in living substrate. A settlement embedded in tissue. Humans who had become, over four centuries, something other than what humans were meant to be. The reports had reached it through channels that are not channels: rumors in the mycelial networks, whispers in the deep water, patterns recognized by intelligences that predate the written word.

The Root-Cavity of Iyesh. A wound that was not a wound. A people living inside something alive.

The power expected to find suffering. It expected to find stagnation, perhaps—humans trapped in a space that had not been meant for them, surviving but not flourishing. It expected, at minimum, to find a problem that required solving.

It stopped because it perceived, in the first moment of attention, that it was wrong.

---

## What It Perceived

The power does not see as humans see. It perceives in totalities, in relationships, in the complex dance of systems across time. What it perceived in Iyesh was this:

The Sixty were not trapped. They were *planted*.

Their adaptation was not degradation but transformation. Their immune systems had not simply survived the cavity's biome—they had negotiated with it. Their skin had not simply resisted the damp—it had developed a new ecology, a borderland between human and environment that was neither and both. Their eyes had learned a darkness that was not absence. Their palms had become primary sense organs, reading walls the way a reader reads a page.

But deeper: they had *culture*. Not diminished culture, not survival-culture stripped to bare function, but a living, evolving, meaning-making system of practices, stories, and relationships. They had art—the storage grooves, maintained across generations, were a form of sculpture, a conversation with a living medium. They had hierarchy—not based on accumulation but on attention, on the capacity to notice. They had love—knowledge given as gift, passages shown, warm places shared.

They had, perhaps most remarkably, genuine mutualism with a non-human intelligence. The Old Growth was not merely containing them. It was *keeping* them. The walls breathed for them. The water was filtered for them. The temperature was regulated for them. And in return—and this was the crucial perception—the Sixty maintained the cavity. They re-cut the grooves that would otherwise heal, creating channels for air and storage. They managed the insect populations, preventing any single species from overwhelming the system. They marked the deep-crawler routes, essentially mapping the cavity's vascular system for the cavity's own use. They were, in the deepest sense, *gardening* their host.

This was not parasitism. This was not commensalism. This was something rare: a mutualism that had become, over centuries, something like a single organism with two kinds of cells.

The power perceived all of this in the space between two heartbeats. And it perceived, simultaneously, the danger it represented.

---

## The Danger of Attention

The power knew something that benevolent powers often forget, and must continually relearn: *to be seen is to be changed*.

This is not metaphor. Attention is a force. To draw the gaze of a power is to become, in some measure, what the power expects to see. The Sixty and the cavity had evolved in invisibility. They were what they were because no one was watching, because no one was coming to save them or judge them or improve them. They were free in the only way anything can be free: unexamined.

And now they were examined.

The power felt the weight of its own perception. It had already altered the system simply by noticing it. The cavity, perhaps, felt the attention as a subtle change in the pattern of warmth and moisture. The deep-crawlers might have paused in their passages, sensing something without name. The damp-singers might have sung a half-note differently. The Sixty, in their sleeping pile, might have dreamed of light—though they had no word for it.

What the power did next would determine whether Iyesh continued to be itself, or became something else entirely.

---

## What It Did Not Do

It did not send light.

It could have. A thought, a gesture, and the cavity would have been filled with illumination: gentle, warm, kind light that asked nothing in return. The Sixty would have been able to see colors for the first time in generations. They would have wept with gratitude. They would have named the power in their stories, made it a character in their lower tongue, given it a place in the wall-songs.

And they would have been *ruined*.

Light would have unmade the grammar of their lives. Their palm-reading, their touch-navigation, their entire way of knowing would have become secondary to a sense they had adapted away from. They would have stopped listening to the walls and started looking. They would have become, gradually, surface-people in a deep place—and they would have been miserable in ways they could not have articulated, because they would have lost something they did not know they had.

The power did not send light. It could perceive the shape of that future, and it grieved for the Sixty who would have lived it.

---

It did not warm the cavity.

It could have. A small adjustment, a slight increase in the metabolic rate of the decomposition layers, and the temperature would have risen to something comfortable by surface-world standards. The Sixty would no longer have needed to sleep in piles. They would no longer have needed to cluster for warmth. They could have been individuals in the way surface-people are individuals—separate, autonomous, self-contained.

And they would have lost the single most important institution they had: each other.

The cold was the foundation of their social order. It was the cold that made the sleeping pile necessary, and the sleeping pile was where the community reconstituted itself each rest-period. It was the cold that drove the vulnerable to the warm center, that made care a matter of survival rather than choice. The cold was the teacher that had taught them, across four centuries, that *they needed each other*. Without it, they might have become what so many surface-dwellers were: alone in the same room.

The power did not warm the cavity. It recognized that discomfort was not the same as suffering, and that some forms of ease were losses in disguise.

---

It did not cure the damp.

It could have. The power knew a hundred ways to draw moisture from air, to dry walls without killing them, to create pockets of aridity where humans could exist without the constant kiss of wetness. The Sixty would have been able to wear dry clothes, to sleep without the feeling of water seeping into their skin, to breathe air that did not taste of mineral and life.

And their skin would have lost its flora. Their immunity would have forgotten its ferocity. They would have become, within a generation, susceptible to the cavity itself—and then they would have been trapped in a place that could kill them, unable to leave because there was nowhere to go, unable to stay because staying meant illness.

The power did not cure the damp. It understood that the Sixty's adaptation was not a burden to be lifted but a relationship to be honored.

---

It did not give them knowledge of the surface.

It could have. It could have shown them, in dreams or in waking visions, the world above: the sky, the sun, the stars, the trees, the rain, the wind. It could have given them the context they lacked, the sense of scale, the knowledge of what else existed beyond the root-mass.

And it would have planted a seed of discontent that would have grown until it split them apart.

The Sixty did not miss the surface because they did not know it. They had stories about it—hostile, dangerous, extreme—but they did not have *longing*. They did not feel deprived because they did not feel the shape of what they were deprived of. They were home, and home was enough.

The power did not give them knowledge of the surface. It recognized that ignorance, in this case, was not a lack but a protection.

---

It did not offer to take them away.

It could have. It had the power to relocate them—to find a place on the surface where they could live, to ease their transition, to give them the knowledge and tools they would need to survive in a world of light and air and temperature variation. They would have struggled, but they would have managed. They would have learned.

And they would have been *colonized*.

They would have been taken from their home and deposited in someone else's. They would have been "saved" by a power that knew better than they did what was good for them. They would have become dependent, grateful, lesser. They would have been an object lesson in what happened when benevolence forgot that wisdom included the wisdom to leave well enough alone.

The power did not offer to take them away. It understood that Iyesh was not a cage but a cradle, and that some forms of liberation were actually abandonment.

---

## What It Did

First, the power *witnessed*.

This is more than it sounds. To witness, for a power of this kind, is to hold something in attention without changing it—to see it as it is, in its fullness and complexity, and to let that seeing be enough. The power turned its full perception to Iyesh and did not turn away. It saw the Sixty in their waking and their resting, their work and their play. It saw the Old Growth in its breathing and its feeding. It saw the deep-crawlers in their passages, the damp-singers in their channels, the Hangers in their patient suspension. It saw the relationships between them—all of them, human and non-human, individual and collective—and it held what it saw without judgment, without pity, without the impulse to intervene.

This was not passive. To witness is an act. It requires restraint, patience, humility. It requires the recognition that *being seen matters*—that there is a dignity in being perceived as one is, without the distortion of another's desires. The power gave Iyesh the gift of being known, truly and deeply, by something that could see it whole.

And then it made that knowledge permanent.

Not in a way the Sixty could access—not yet. But the power created a record, a kind of memory outside of time, that preserved Iyesh as it was. Not frozen, not static, but alive: a template of the system at this moment in its evolution, that could be restored if restoration ever became necessary. If the cavity ever collapsed. If the Old Growth ever sickened. If the Sixty ever faced a catastrophe they could not adapt to. The power stored the pattern of what they were, so that they might never be entirely lost.

This was insurance, not intervention. It did not change anything. It simply ensured that whatever changes came, the original could be remembered.

---

Second, the power *protected*.

It did not build a wall. It did not place a guardian. It did not make any visible barrier at all. But it wove a subtler protection into the fabric of the region above—a kind of disinterest that settled over the entrance like a thin mist.

Those who came near would not see the split between the buttress roots as an entrance. They would see roots, trees, undergrowth—the usual clutter of a forest floor. They might pause, feeling that something was there, but the feeling would fade before it became curiosity. They would move on.

This protection was not absolute. A determined seeker—one who already knew what they were looking for, or who had a power of their own—could push through the disinterest and find the gap. The power did not want to make Iyesh a prison. It wanted, simply, to reduce the traffic. To keep the cavity obscure.

It also extended its attention to the Old Growth itself—the larger organism that contained Iyesh. It could perceive the health of the root-network, the flow of nutrients and water, the slow work of decomposition that sustained the system. And it made a small adjustment, so subtle as to be invisible: it increased the Old Growth's resilience to disease. Not immunity, which would cause its own problems, but a slight enhancement of the existing immune functions. The root-mass would be harder to sicken. It would recover faster from injury. It would continue, in other words, to be what it was—but more reliably.

This was not intervention in Iyesh itself. This was ensuring the context in which Iyesh existed.

---

Third, the power *waited*.

It did not leave. It did not turn its attention elsewhere. It maintained its witnessing, its gentle protection, its record-keeping—and it waited to see if the Sixty would find their own way to something new.

This was not passivity. This was respect. The power recognized that the Sixty were not finished becoming what they would be. Four centuries was long by human standards, but short by the standards of the Old Growth, and the relationship between them was still evolving. The Sixty were still adapting. The cavity was still changing. The mutualism was still deepening. And the power, with the patience that only a being outside of time can truly possess, settled in to watch.

It watched for three things:

First, it watched for a request. The Sixty had no way to address the power directly—they did not know it was there—but they had practices that might, in time, reach it. The wall-songs, the lower tongue spoken into the New Skin, the listeners with their palms pressed against the breathing walls. If they ever found a way to ask for something, the power wanted to hear it. And it would consider answering.

Second, it watched for a crisis. Not discomfort, not the ordinary challenges of life in the cavity, but genuine catastrophe. A collapse. A plague. A flood that did not recede. If the system ever truly failed, the power would be there—not to take over, but to offer options. It would not impose a solution. But it would make possible solutions that the Sixty could not achieve alone, if they chose to accept them.

Third, it watched for an evolution. The Sixty were becoming something new—something that was human and also not human, individual and also collective, animal and also plant-adjacent. The power was curious about what they might become. It wanted to see.

---

## What It Hopes

The power hopes that the Sixty will continue as they are, changing and not-changing, for as long as they choose to. It hopes that the Old Growth will continue to keep them, and they will continue to garden it, and the mutualism will deepen until it becomes something that even the power cannot fully perceive—a relationship so intimate that it transcends the boundaries between beings.

It hopes that the Sixty will never need to know that it is watching. It hopes that they will live and die and be given back to the walls, generation after generation, in the rhythm they have chosen, without ever feeling the weight of an attention that might change them.

But it also hopes—if hope is the right word—that if they ever need it, they will find a way to ask. And it hopes that when that happens, it will have the wisdom to answer in a way that is actually helpful—that does not impose, that does not diminish, that does not unmake what it tries to save.

The power does not know if this hope will be fulfilled. It is wise, but not omniscient. It is benevolent, but not infallible. It can perceive the shape of possible futures, but it cannot see which future will become real.

So it waits. It watches. It holds Iyesh in its memory and its protection, gently, without interference, with the patience of a being that knows how rare it is to find a place where people belong.

---

## And What the Sixty Feel

The Sixty do not know that a power is watching them. They do not know that they are protected, or remembered, or held. They do not know that someone perceives them as precious.

But they feel something.

The listeners, in their long communions with the walls, sometimes sense a presence—not in the cavity, but in the shape of their attention. They do not name it. They do not speak of it. But when they press their palms to the Old Growth and wait through the long exhale, sometimes they feel, for just a moment, that they are not alone in a new way.

The deep-crawlers, in their passages, sometimes pause without reason, sensing a change in the substrate that they cannot articulate. They continue on. They always do.

The damp-singers sing, and sometimes their song feels heard.

The children, playing the forbidden game, find that the Hangers do not rise as quickly as they used to—as if something is holding them gently down.

And in the sleeping pile, in the deepest rest-period, the Sixty sometimes dream of warmth without source, of light without glare, of a presence that asks nothing and gives nothing and simply *is*—a witness they did not know they had, a guardian they did not know they needed, a power that loves them enough to leave them alone.

They wake. They work. They cut the grooves and tend the gardens and walk the channels. They live and love and die and are given back to the walls.

And far above, or deep below, or in a dimension that has no spatial location, a benevolent and wise power continues to hold them in its attention—not as a project, not as a problem to be solved, but as something rarer and more precious:

A thing that is exactly what it should be.
