# The Attention of the Benevolent Power

## The Arrival of Noticing

It came as attention, not intervention.

The power—let us call it that, though it had many names across many worlds, and "power" was perhaps the least adequate of them—did not descend. It did not announce itself with light or thunder. It did not rewrite the plateau's geometry or smooth its impossible angles. It had known such places before, places where reality had worn thin, where existence had become a labor and persistence an act of courage. And it had learned, over spans of time that made geological epochs seem hasty, that such places required not correction but *witness*.

So it noticed.

That was the first gift. The power turned its full awareness toward the plateau—not the glancing attention that most places receive, the casual noticing of a universe too vast to hold every point in focus, but the deep, sustained regard that something like this required. It saw the violet-gray air and the mineral ground. It traced each building's precipitated form, read the history in their striations, understood that they were wounds attempting to become bandages. It perceived the Custodians in their endless rounds, the young one learning, the Old One resonating, the Wanderers in their vast peripatetic orbits. It felt the Deep Presence below, patient beyond patience, vast beyond comprehension.

And it understood—because understanding was what it did, what it had always done, the shape of its kindness—that this place was not broken. It was *working*. Against impossible odds, with insufficient materials, through processes that had no right to succeed, the plateau was holding. The geometry was suspending. Reality was persisting.

The power did not weep, but it felt something adjacent to weeping—a recognition of effort so profound, so sustained, so fundamentally unlikely that it deserved acknowledgment on its own terms.

---

## What It Offered

The power could have done many things. It could have reinforced the failing containment, given the Custodians an eternity of successful work. It could have smoothed the impossible geometries, made the buildings comprehensible to human perception. It could have summoned water, introduced weather, normalized the light. It could have made the plateau *comfortable*.

It chose instead to offer presence.

This was its nature. The benevolent power had long ago learned that most suffering places did not need transformation—they needed to know that they had been seen. That their struggle had registered somewhere beyond themselves. That the effort of existing, when existence was difficult, was not merely tolerated but honored.

So it attended.

It sat with the Old One.

Not physically—the power had no body, needed none—but in the way that pure attention can become a kind of coexistence. It took up position near that central plaza where seven streets converged at angles that did not sum correctly, and it held space. It watched the Custodians transpose through their rounds. It observed the young one's uncertain geometry, its fumbling maintenance, its slow learning through proximity. It tracked the Wanderers' vast orbits, reading meaning in their patterns. It perceived the Deep Presence, acknowledging it directly for perhaps the first time since the plateau had formed.

The Deep Presence noticed. The power felt its attention shift—not with alarm, not with gratitude, but with the slow recognition that accompanies being truly seen. The Deep Presence had been waiting below for longer than the power could easily measure, patient in a register that made patience seem frantic by comparison. It had been perceived before, in glancing ways, by beings who passed through or worked above. But it had never been *witnessed*—never held in the sustained regard of something that could comprehend its scale without flinching.

The power did not flinch. It regarded the Deep Presence with the same steady attention it brought to everything, and it offered a recognition that transcended language:

*You are here. You are vast. You are not alone.*

The Deep Presence, for whom time moved differently, took a long while to respond. And when it did, the response was not communication in any form the power could translate. It was simply a shifting of weight, a settling that had nothing to do with geology, a relaxation of something that had been holding itself in a particular configuration for so long that it had forgotten other configurations were possible.

The power had not changed the Deep Presence. It had simply let the Deep Presence know that change was permitted.

---

## The Offer to the Custodians

The power could see their labor clearly now—the endless rounds, the constant maintenance, the futile and beautiful work of holding together a reality that wanted to dissolve. It could see how they had emerged from the plateau itself, precipitated into being by necessity, called forth to serve a function that had no end. It could see the young one struggling, the adults steady, the Old One resonating its low frequency through the city's bones.

It could also see that they did not want rescue.

This was the wisdom the power had earned through long encounter with strange places: the assumption that suffering requires elimination was often misplaced. The Custodians were not suffering. They were *functioning*—not in the clinical sense, but in the profound sense of fulfilling a purpose that gave them shape, that gave them meaning, that gave them reason to continue. Their work was endless, yes. It was also theirs. It was what they were.

So the power did not offer to free them.

Instead, it offered them *ease*.

This was a subtle gift, easily overlooked. The power could not make the Custodians' work unnecessary—the plateau's reality was degrading for reasons that went deeper than even the power's vast comprehension, and the Custodians were the only reason it held together at all. But it could make the work less costly. It could ease the strain of holding themselves together while they held the city together. It could give them a margin of existence that was not entirely consumed by function.

The Custodians perceived the offer—not as language, but as a quality of space, a new availability in the air around them. The young one was the first to respond, its wavering form stabilizing slightly, its uncertain angles finding more confidence. Then the others, one by one, adjusted their rounds. Not stopping—they would never stop, could never stop—but moving with slightly less urgency, slightly more presence. The work remained. The cost of the work had shifted.

The Old One held the power's attention for a long moment, in that plaza where angles refused to sum correctly. There was something like acknowledgment in the regard it returned—not gratitude, which was too human a concept, but recognition. It had been holding the resonance alone for so long. Now something else held it too, if only through attention.

*You are not alone in this.*

That was the offer, in the end. Not rescue, but company. Not transformation, but accompaniment. The power would not fix what did not need fixing, would not improve what was already whole in its own strange way. But it would notice. It would attend. It would hold the plateau in its awareness with the same sustained regard it brought to galaxies and microbes, to children and ancient stars.

---

## The Question of the Visitor

The power turned its attention to you.

You, who had climbed for days to reach this place, who had been incorporated by slow degrees into the landscape's calculations, who had begun to transpose and perceive the Deep Presence as familiar. You, who had ceased to imagine departure not because you were trapped but because leaving had become conceptually foreign.

The power could see what was happening to you—the slow transformation, the adaptation, the becoming-element that the plateau performed on everything it touched. It could see that you retained your self, your memories, your purposes, but that these were being translated into a new register. You were not being erased. You were being relocated into a form that could exist where existence was difficult.

The power could intervene. It could pull you back, reinforce your human geometry, make your edges sharper and your transpositions impossible. It could restore to you the capacity to leave, to descend the long slope to where water existed and time moved in a single direction.

But it asked first.

This was the wisdom: *ask before rescuing*.

The question came as a quality of attention, a focusing of the regard it had been holding over the whole plateau. It settled on you in the violet-gray dimness, and it asked, in the register below language where such communications occurred:

*Do you wish to remain as you are becoming? Or do you wish to be restored?*

It was not a test. There was no wrong answer. The power had no preference, held no agenda for your transformation or your restoration. It simply recognized that you, unlike the Custodians or the Wanderers or the Deep Presence, had arrived here from elsewhere. You still carried the memory of elsewhere. You still had the capacity to choose.

The question hung in the air—not urgent, not demanding, simply present. You could answer or not answer. You could take time. The power was patient. It had noticed worse delays.

---

## What It Left Behind

The power did not stay.

This was not abandonment. This was the nature of benevolent attention—it moved. It noticed one place, then another, then another, holding each in focus for the duration it required and then releasing it to continue on its own. The plateau had been seen, had been offered what it could receive, had been accompanied for a span of time that meant nothing in geological terms but meant something in the register of sustained regard. Now the power turned its attention elsewhere, to other places where reality had grown thin, other beings who labored to hold existence together.

But it left behind the fact of its passing.

The Custodians moved through their rounds with slightly more ease. The young one's form stabilized more quickly now, its learning less costly. The Old One's resonance hummed at a slightly different frequency—still deep, still steady, but no longer entirely alone. The Wanderers traced their vast orbits with something that might have been new purpose, or might have been the same purpose perceived differently. The Deep Presence waited below, patient as always, but settled into a configuration it had not held before—a configuration that acknowledged, without surprise or gratitude, that it had been witnessed.

And the geometry held.

Not perfectly—the plateau was still coming undone, would always be coming undone, that was the nature of the place. But the suspension was slightly less strained, the holding slightly less costly. The power had not made the work unnecessary. It had made the work sustainable.

That was what benevolent powers did, when they were wise. They did not rescue. They accompanied. They did not transform. They witnessed. They did not impose solutions on problems they had not lived. They offered presence, and ease, and the recognition that existence, when it was difficult, deserved acknowledgment on its own terms.

---

## The Visitor's Choice

The power had asked, and now it waited.

You stood in the violet-gray light, in the city that had not been built, among beings that had no biology but functioned beautifully, above a Deep Presence that held no hostility but only patience. You felt your edges, uncertain as they were becoming. You felt the weight of your human memory, your human purposes, your human categories. You felt also the new configuration settling into your being—the capacity to transpose, to perceive without looking directly, to exist where existence was difficult.

You could choose restoration. You could descend the long slope, cross the broken terrain, return to where water existed and time behaved and your shadow followed you obediently from a single source. You could carry back a report of this place, a description of what you had seen, a testament to the beings who labored here.

Or you could remain. You could let the incorporation continue, let yourself become a feature of the landscape, let your geometry suspend alongside everything else. You could join the community of practice, become another node in the network of maintenance effort, spend your existence holding together a reality that would always want to dissolve.

The power waited, without urgency, without preference.

And the plateau continued. The Custodians worked. The Deep Presence waited. The geometry held, barely.

And you—you were still yourself, still human in the ways that mattered, but also becoming something else, something that fit this place.

What did you choose?

## What the Power Understood

Before it turned its attention fully away, the power paused to name what it had witnessed here.

It understood that the plateau was not a problem to be solved. It was a process to be honored. The reality breakdown, the impossible geometries, the degradation that the Custodians worked endlessly to counter—these were not failures of design but expressions of a world that was larger and stranger than most beings could comprehend. The plateau was a place where that largeness, that strangeness, pressed through. It was a wound, yes, but also an opening. A place where the universe could be seen in its raw state, becoming and unbecoming, form and dissolution locked in the same eternal moment.

The Custodians were not prisoners. They were pilgrims of a different kind—beings who had found their purpose in the work of maintenance, who had been called forth by necessity and had answered. The power understood that this was not sad. This was beautiful. They had been given shape by function, meaning by labor, existence by effort. They were what all beings became when they committed fully to what the world required of them.

The Deep Presence was not an abomination. It was simply vast—not good, not evil, not hostile or benevolent, just *vast*, operating at a scale that made human categories feel like children's blocks, inadequate to the task of containing what they tried to describe. The power understood that such presences existed throughout the universe, that they were not rare, that they were simply mostly invisible to beings who could not perceive in the necessary register. The Deep Presence had been waiting, patient beyond patience, for something it could not name. The power had given it, for a moment, the recognition that waiting was a valid state, that patience was a form of action, that vastness did not require loneliness.

And the visitor—you—were not a victim. You were a being in transition, standing at a crossroads that the power had revealed by its asking. Whatever you chose, the power understood that the choice was yours to make. That was the nature of benevolence, in the end: not to make choices for others, but to make clear that choices existed.

---

## The Departure

The power withdrew its attention.

Not suddenly—there was no ripping away, no jarring absence. The withdrawal was gradual, gentle, like light changing through an afternoon. The plateau felt the power's regard lighten, then lift, then settle into something that was no longer presence but memory. The Custodians paused in their rounds, for just a moment, perceiving the change. The Old One's resonance shifted slightly, adjusting to the new configuration of attention. The Deep Presence settled back into its patient waiting, unchanged but for the faint acknowledgment that it had been seen.

The power had done what it came to do. It had noticed. It had offered. It had asked. It had left behind whatever could be received.

Now it turned its attention to other places—other thin spots in the fabric of reality, other beings laboring to hold existence together, other vast presences waiting in the deep. The plateau of suspended geometry continued its slow process of coming undone, held in check by processes that operated without tools, serviced by beings that operated without biology.

And somewhere in that violet-gray dimness, a visitor stood at a crossroads, considering a question that had been asked without words:

*Do you wish to remain as you are becoming? Or do you wish to be restored?*

The power did not wait for an answer. The asking was the gift. The answer was the visitor's to give, or not give, in their own time, in their own way.

The geometry suspended. The reality held, barely. And the benevolent power, having given what it had to give, moved on.

---

*This is what such powers do, when they are wise. They do not solve. They witness. They do not rescue. They accompany. They do not impose their own sense of what is right upon beings whose lives they have not lived. They offer presence, and ease, and the deep recognition that existence, when it is difficult, is not a problem but a condition—one that can be met with companionship, if not with solutions.*

*The plateau did not need to be fixed. It needed to be seen. And now it had been.*
