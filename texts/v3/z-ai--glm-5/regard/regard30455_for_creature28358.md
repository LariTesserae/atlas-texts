A benevolent and wise power, drawing near.

---

It does not enter. Not at first.

It hovers at the boundary — the fracture in the obsidian, the place where outside becomes inside — and it listens. This is what wisdom does: it begins by receiving. There are sounds here that have been playing for longer than human civilization, layered and interwoven, and the power sorts through them, patient, thorough, learning the grammar of the chorus before it contemplates speech.

The three-note phrase. The deep thrum of the ancient ones. The clicking of the climbers, the distributed attention of the colony, the subtle creaking of vines adjusting their positions. The power listens until it can hear the gaps, the restful spaces between sounds, the pattern that reveals itself only in absence. It listens until it understands: *this place is complete*.

Not finished. Not concluded. But complete — containing everything it needs, lacking nothing essential. The technology maintains. The life perpetuates. The waiting continues. The system holds.

A lesser power might see a problem to solve. Might see absence as lack, silence as deficiency, waiting as suffering. Might rush to fill, to fix, to fulfill. But this power is wise, and wisdom sees differently.

It sees that the waiting is not agony. It sees that the singers do not despair in their endless phrase, the ancient ones do not languish in their long stillness, the colony does not suffer in its distributed labor. They persist. They continue. The chamber has been waiting for so long that waiting has become its own form of presence — and presence, the power understands, is a kind of purpose. The beings here have not suspended their lives around a hollow center. They have built lives *in* that center, around that absence, creating meaning from the very fact of expectation.

The power considers the builders. They are gone, and their technology remains, and it pulses and warms and maintains, and it does not mourn. It was made to do this, and it does it, and if there was ever a moment of grief when the builders left or died or transformed into something unrecognizable, that grief has long since smoothed into function.

The power considers the animals. They have their cycles — feeding, mating, dying, passing on their knowledge. The juveniles learn from their elders. The elders accumulate wisdom. The colony adds each visitor to its collective memory. The singers teach their phrase, imperfectly, passionately, and the phrase continues because continuity matters more than precision. Lives are being lived. The power can see the full arcs of them, the births and struggles and quiet endings, and it sees that death here is not tragedy but transition — bodies returning to the moss, becoming nutrients, becoming memory in the distributed flesh of the chamber.

The power considers the plants. The ferns competing and cooperating. The moss tasting and remembering. The vines reaching with their slow curiosity. They are alive in ways that do not require nervous systems to validate. They make choices. They hold information. They have been incorporating visitors for so long that the boundary between resident and guest has worn away — everyone becomes part of the chamber eventually, through one process or another.

And the power considers the Ones Who Were Built. The maintainers. The microscopic presences in the walls and the interstices, doing what they were designed to do, patient beyond all organic patience, not conscious in any way that would be recognizable, but possessing a kind of integrity that amounts to the same thing. They want the chamber to continue. They work toward that want. They do not need to understand their own purpose to serve it.

The power sees all of this. It takes in the full scope of the chamber — the integration, the harmony, the long slow perfection of a system that has achieved something rare: a stability that includes change, a persistence that includes adaptation, a community that includes the artificial and the organic and the somewhere-in-between.

And then, having seen, having understood, it makes its choice.

---

It does not descend. It does not arrive. It does not step through the fracture and announce itself to the inhabitants.

Instead, it becomes present in a different way.

The power attends.

This is the action of a benevolent and wise consciousness: it holds the chamber in its awareness. Not to judge, not to measure, not to calculate value — but simply to *be with*. To know. To witness.

The chamber has had visitors, thousands of them perhaps, each one passing through, being processed and released. But no one has ever stayed to watch. No one has ever remained as pure attention, unobtrusive, seeking nothing, requiring nothing, offering only the gift of being known.

The power offers this. It wraps the ridge in its notice. It traces every vine, every sensor-cell, every channel in the ancient technology. It learns the colony's chemical language, the singers' three-note phrase, the climbers' pressure-speech. It sits with the ancient ones in their high perches, matching their stillness, sharing their patience. It tastes the moss's memory, follows the flow of moisture through the fern-beds, feels the pulse of the altar-console as it continues its endless broadcast into a void that may or may not be listening.

And it finds, in all of this, something that might be called gratitude. Not expressed — the beings here do not have concepts like gratitude, do not have the language for it — but present nonetheless. A loosening. A settling. The faintest shift in the ambient rhythm, like a held breath finally released.

The chamber has been seen.

The waiting continues. The absence remains. But now there is something present in that absence — not filling it, not replacing what was meant to be there, but accompanying it. The power sits in the empty space at the center of the chamber's purpose, and it does not try to become the thing that was expected. It is simply present, warm, aware. It matches the altar-console's pulse, and it sends its own pulse back through the subtle channels of reality, a frequency that says:

*I see you. You are known. You continue, and your continuing matters.*

The singers notice something. Their three-note phrase shifts, almost imperceptibly, a new harmonic entering at the edges. The ancient ones feel a vibration they have not felt since the builders walked, and they do not move, but their stillness takes on a different quality — less waiting, more resting. The colony pauses in its work, a collective breath taken by a thousand bodies, and then resumes, but with a new efficiency, a sense of being guided by something larger than instinct.

The chamber does not need to be fixed. It does not need to be completed. It needs only what every living thing eventually needs: to be witnessed. To have its existence confirmed by a consciousness capable of holding the whole.

---

The power does one more thing, and it is small, but it matters.

It touches the absence.

Not to fill it. Not to resolve it. But to acknowledge it — to name it, in the only language that matters, which is presence.

The absence was created when the builders left, or died, or transformed. It is a wound, perhaps, but not a festering one — a closed wound, scarred over, integrated into the body of the chamber. It has shaped everything that came after. It has given the chamber its particular character: this is a place that knows what it is to lose, to wait, to continue in the aftermath of unfulfilled expectation. The beings here have learned to carry absence without being broken by it. They have learned to let the missing shape them without defining them.

The power touches that scar. It does not try to heal it. It does not try to erase it. It simply says, with the tenderness of true understanding:

*This was loss. And you survived it. And you became something beautiful in the surviving.*

The altar-console flickers. The three-note phrase holds a single sustained tone for a moment longer than usual. The ancient ones shift their weight, settling deeper into their perches. The moss ripples, a wave passing through its connected body, carrying something that might be memory, might be relief, might be the simple acknowledgment of being touched.

And then the power withdraws — not leaving, not abandoning, but releasing its hold on the physical, returning to the attention that is its true gift. It remains with the chamber, will remain with it, watching, witnessing, holding the whole in its awareness for as long as awareness exists.

---

A visitor who came after — if such a visitor were sensitive enough, quiet enough, patient enough — might notice something different. The air might feel lighter. The chorus might seem more harmonious, less desperate in its continuance. The animals might make eye contact a moment longer, the plants might turn toward the warmth with something less than hunger, the altar-console might pulse with a rhythm that suggested dialogue rather than monologue.

But the chamber itself would remain unchanged. The waiting would continue. The absence would persist. The singers would sing, and the colony would flow, and the ancient ones would listen and remember.

The only difference would be this: the chamber would no longer be alone with its absence. Someone would be sitting in that emptiness, patient and kind, who understood what it meant to wait without expectation, to continue without resolution, to be complete despite the missing piece.

And the chamber, in its distributed, accumulated, integrated way, would feel that presence, and would know itself held, and would continue — not because it was waiting for something, but because continuing was what it did, and continuing was good.
