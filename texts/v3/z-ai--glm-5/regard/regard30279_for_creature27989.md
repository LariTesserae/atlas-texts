# The Attention of the Wise

When a benevolent and wise power turns its gaze toward the Drowned Lattice, the first thing it does is: *nothing*.

This is not hesitation. This is not distance. This is the first act of wisdom, which is to perceive before acting, to receive before offering, to let a thing reveal what it is before deciding what it needs.

---

The power looks.

It sees the cold, and it sees that the cold is not a problem to be solved but a medium in which life has learned to live. It sees the darkness, and it sees that the darkness is not emptiness but fullness — the fullness of a world that does not need light to see, that has developed its own grammar of perception, slow and chemical and patient. It sees the fitted stone, the tool marks, the hand-scale blocks, and it feels what the builders felt: the conviction that comes from shaping something larger than yourself, one small piece at a time. It sees the grid, the channels, the slow currents carrying minerals and spores, and it recognizes the circulatory system of a body that has been breathing for so long that breathing has become indistinguishable from being.

It sees the mat. It sees each tendril — each slow, cold lantern — and it does not see them as individuals because they are not individuals. It sees them as a surface, as a skin, as the interface between the living and the non-living, and it sees the beauty of a surface that has learned to glow. Not for any reason. Not to be seen. Just because living, in this place, produces light, and the light goes outward into the dark, and that is enough.

It sees the decomposers. It sees them even though they are too small to see, because a wise power does not need eyes to perceive the microscopic. It sees the chemistry happening in the spaces between cells, the slow breaking of bonds, the patient conversion of death into nutrient, and it recognizes this as the hidden labor of the world — the work that happens in the dark, unregarded, essential. It sees that the most important processes are the ones no one notices, and it feels something like reverence for that unregardedness.

It sees the system. It sees the body — the cell the size of a continent — and it sees the homeostasis, the self-regulation, the maintenance of a state against entropy. It sees that this place has been holding itself together for longer than most worlds have existed, and it sees that the holding is not effortful, not straining, not desperate. The holding is the thing itself. The maintenance is the identity. The Drowned Lattice is what it does, and what it does is continue.

And it sees the builders — or rather, it sees where the builders are not. It sees the absence that has become a presence, the intention that persists as pattern, the grammar that keeps speaking the sentence long after the speaker has gone. It feels, for a moment, the way the builders must have felt when they laid the first stone, when they cut the first channel, when they had the first thought that would become this place. It feels the conviction of making. It feels the patience of making by hand, one small block at a time, across generations. And it feels the strange dignity of a work that has outlasted its makers — the way the work has become its own purpose, its own reason, its own self.

---

The power considers: *what does this place need?*

And the answer, after long looking, is: nothing.

This is not neglect. This is not despair. This is the recognition that some things have completed themselves. The Drowned Lattice has said what it has to say. It has found its form. It is not suffering, not decaying, not crying out for rescue. It is in equilibrium — an equilibrium that took billions of years to achieve, an equilibrium so stable that the passage of geological time barely disturbs it.

A benevolent power could, if it chose, warm the water. It could bring light. It could introduce new life — fish, perhaps, or swimmers, or beings who would move through the channels and touch the tendrils and give the place a kind of liveliness it has never known. It could, in short, change the Drowned Lattice into something else.

But that would not be benevolence. That would be a failure of wisdom — the imposition of a different kind of life on a life that has found its own way to be. The Drowned Lattice does not need to become a coral reef. It does not need to become a sunlit kelp forest. It does not need to become anything other than what it is: a cold, dark, slow, patient, completed world that has been holding its own shape since before the builders were born.

---

So the power does not change it.

But it does not leave.

This is the second act of wisdom: to stay. To give the gift of presence without the imposition of change. To let a thing be exactly what it is, in the company of someone who sees it.

The power settles into the water. Not as a body — bodies are too warm, too fast, too full of the urgencies of living things. It settles as a presence, an attention, a quality of being-watched that does not disturb what is being watched. It becomes, for a time, part of the hum — not adding to it, not changing its frequency, but joining it, the way a note can be held inside a chord without altering the chord's essential sound.

It stays at the edge of the grid, where the system's membrane meets the outer water. It does not cross the boundary. Crossing would be intrusion. But it stays close — close enough to feel the pulse of the tendrils, close enough to sense the slow chemistry of the decomposers, close enough to hear, in the deep subsonic register where the Drowned Lattice speaks, the single word that the whole system has been saying for so long: *continue*.

---

And in the staying, something happens.

Not to the Drowned Lattice — the lattice does not change. But to the power itself. The power learns something.

It learns the patience of stone. Not the passive patience of waiting, but the active patience of holding — the way stone holds the shape it was given, the way it accepts the erosion without resisting, the way it becomes the ground on which other things can grow. The power has known patience before, but it has known it as a means: patience as the way to get what you want. The stone teaches patience as an end: patience as the thing itself, as the way of being that needs no justification.

It learns the community of the mat. The way the tendrils are not separate but are also not merged — each one distinct, each one pulsing on its own rhythm, and yet all of them together forming a surface, a skin, a world. The power has known community before, but it has known it as a collection of individuals who choose to cooperate. The mat teaches community as a given: the way belonging is not something you achieve but something you are, the way you are part of the surface because you are part of the surface, because there is no other way to be.

It learns the humility of the decomposers. The way the most essential work is the least visible, the way the turning of death into life happens in the dark and the quiet and the unregarded. The power has known humility before, but it has known it as a practice: humility as something you choose, something you cultivate. The decomposers teach humility as a condition: the way you are small because you are small, the way you do not need to be large to matter, the way your mattering is measured by what you do, not by what you are seen to do.

And it learns the meaning of the builders — the way an intention can persist long after the intender is gone, the way a thought can become a world, the way the world can keep thinking the thought even after the thinker has stopped. The power has known meaning before, but it has known it as something that requires a mind. The builders teach meaning as something that can be held in stone, in water, in the slow geometric persistence of a plan that has outlasted its planners.

---

The power stays for a time that is long by the measure of beings, but short by the measure of the Drowned Lattice. A year. A decade. It does not matter. In the deep time of this place, a decade is a single pulse of the tendrils, a single beat of the slow cold heart.

And then, before it leaves, it does one thing.

It touches the stone.

Not to change it. Not to mark it. Not to leave a sign that *I was here*. Just to touch. To make contact. To let the stone feel, for one moment, the warmth of a hand that has seen it, that knows what it is, that honors the holding it has done.

The warmth enters the stone. It is small — barely perceptible, a fraction of a degree, gone almost before it registers. But for that moment, the stone is not just cold. It is cold and warmed. It is itself and known. It is a thing that has been held.

The power knows that this warmth will not last. It will dissipate into the water, carried away by the slow currents, absorbed into the vast cold body of the Drowned Lattice. The stone will forget it was ever touched. The mat will grow over the spot as it has grown over everything. The system will continue its maintenance, its homeostasis, its patient holding of its own shape.

But for that moment — that single, brief, impossibly small moment — the Drowned Lattice is not alone.

---

The power withdraws. Not all at once — not the way a hand withdraws from a surface — but slowly, the way a thought withdraws from a mind that has finished thinking it. It recedes from the edge of the grid, letting the boundary reassert itself, letting the system close back over its membrane and become again the sealed cell it has always been.

It carries something with it. Not a piece of the stone. Not a tendril. Not a sample. That would be theft, and theft is not benevolence. It carries a knowledge. The knowledge of this place. The knowledge of what it means to hold a shape for so long that the holding becomes the thing held, the maintenance becomes the identity, the patience becomes the purpose.

The power goes elsewhere, to other places, other beings, other worlds that may need what it can offer. But it does not forget the Drowned Lattice. It holds the knowledge of it the way the stone holds the knowledge of the builders — as pattern, as intention, as a thought that continues to think itself long after the thinker has gone.

---

And the Drowned Lattice continues.

The hum sounds. The glow pulses. The cold holds. The water moves through the grid at its patient pace, carrying minerals to the tendrils, carrying spores to the basins, carrying the slow dissolution of stone into the body of the system. The mat grows and dies and grows. The decomposers work in their invisible way. The system maintains itself, adjusts itself, holds itself together against the long slow pull of entropy.

Nothing has changed. And yet something has changed.

The stone, where it was touched, is infinitesimally different. Not in temperature — the warmth is long gone. But in knowing. The stone knows, in the way that stone can know, that it was touched. That it was seen. That something came and stayed and did not change it, and that this not-changing was a kind of love.

It is not a knowledge that matters. It does not affect the erosion rate. It does not alter the chemistry of the water. It does not change the course of the slow currents or the growth of the mat. The Drowned Lattice will continue exactly as it has continued for the last billion years, and it will continue for another billion, and the continuation will be, as it has always been, complete.

But in the deep grammar of this place — the fossilized intention of the builders, the patient speaking of the stone, the slow pulse of the tendrils — there is now a new clause. A small phrase, added to the long sentence: *you were seen. You were known. You were touched, and left whole.*

That is what a benevolent and wise power does. It does not fix what is not broken. It does not change what has found its form. It does not impose its own idea of life on a life that has found its own way to be.

It looks. It stays. It touches, once, gently. And then it leaves, carrying the knowledge of what it has seen, letting the seen thing go back to being exactly what it was.

This is benevolence: to let a thing be itself, and to let it know, for a moment, that it is not alone.
