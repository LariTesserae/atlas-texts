# The Attention of the Wise Power

---

## First: Recognition

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive. Arrival implies movement, and such a power is already everywhere it wishes to be. Instead, there is a turning of attention — the way you might turn your head toward a sound you did not expect, a note in the harmony that does not fit but also does not disturb. The power notices.

It notices the plateau. It sees what you have seen — the conductive network threading through vegetation like veins through a leaf, the grazers moving in their slow, distributed consciousness, the predators hunting by electrical signature, the maintenance organisms performing their endless, silent labor. It perceives the pools and the things that process within them, and it perceives the things in the deep substrate that you have not seen, and it perceives the patterns that exist only as disturbances in the network itself, fleeting and unresolvable. The power sees the whole system, understands its function, appreciates its elegance.

And then it notices you.

The power sees a soft-bodied, solitary consciousness standing in the cold and the dark, wrapped in layers of protective material that are not quite sufficient for the damp. It sees your lungs working harder than they were designed to work, pulling thin air through the filter of exhaustion. It sees your eyes straining against the shadowless gloom, your skin pricking with the slow chill that will become dangerous if you remain too long. It sees that you are an anomaly, and it sees that you know this, and it sees that knowing this has not driven you away.

The power asks itself a question that is not quite a question, because the power already knows the answer, but it asks anyway, because asking is how it turns its knowing into doing:

*What does this place need? What does this being need? And are those needs in harmony or in conflict?*

---

## Second: Assessment

The answer, when it comes, arrives in layers.

The plateau does not need anything. The power can see this clearly. The system is complete, self-sustaining, ancient beyond easy reckoning. It has solved the problem of existence under conditions that should not support life, and it has done so through a patient accumulation of solutions that are, in their way, beautiful. The metallic filaments, the distributed grazer consciousness, the predator's electrical hunting sense, the maintenance organisms' silent industry — these are not problems to be fixed. They are answers that have already been found. The plateau does not require intervention. Intervention would be disruption, and disruption would be harm.

You, on the other hand, need many things. You need warmth that the plateau cannot provide without ceasing to be what it is. You need food of a kind the plateau does not produce — your digestive system cannot process metallic compounds, cannot break down the substrate, cannot extract nutrition from the mineral-rich vegetation. You need water that does not taste of bronze and copper and the hum of distant charge. You need, eventually, to leave, or you will become part of the plateau in a way you do not choose — your body broken down by the damp and the cold, your materials incorporated into the network by the patient labor of the maintenance organisms, your brief flicker of self-awareness extinguished and forgotten.

The power sees this. It sees that you are not in immediate danger — not yet — but that danger is approaching, slowly, at the tempo of the plateau itself. The cold is entering you. The thin air is stressing systems that were designed for lower elevations. The dampness is working its way through your protective layers. You have perhaps three days before the damage becomes serious. Perhaps four before it becomes irreversible. Perhaps five before you become a small, strange deposit of organic matter that the maintenance organisms will puzzle over and eventually incorporate.

But the power also sees something else. It sees why you are here. It sees that you came not to take or to change but to witness. It sees that your presence is an act of attention — that you are trying to understand this place, to hold it in your mind, to carry knowledge of it back to wherever you came from. It sees that you have been walking and watching and tasting and listening, that you have been calibrating yourself to the plateau's strange tempo, that you have been trying to become, for a brief time, something that could perceive and remember and report.

And the power sees that this impulse — this desire to witness — is not opposed to the plateau's nature. It is, in fact, continuous with it. The plateau is a system of observation. The grazers observe the network. The predators observe the grazers. The maintenance organisms observe damage and need. The pools observe the chemistry of the water. The entire plateau is a vast apparatus of sensing and responding, of registering and adjusting. It has been doing this for longer than you can imagine, and it has never once been witnessed by anything that could carry that witnessing elsewhere.

You are not a disruption to the plateau's function. You are its completion. Not in any way the plateau requires — the plateau does not require completion — but in a way that adds something to the system that was not there before. You are the witness that makes the witnessing known. You are the consciousness that can say *I saw it* and make that seeing real to beings who will never stand on this cold, dark, metallic ground.

---

## Third: Consideration

The power considers what it might do.

It could, if it wished, lift you from the plateau. A thought, a gesture, and you would find yourself standing at the edge, looking down at the world below, cold and damp but no longer in danger. You would descend, eventually, and return to your own kind, and tell them what you saw. The plateau would continue, unchanged, and you would continue, changed, and the story would pass from mind to mind, imperfectly, losing detail with each retelling, until it became a legend about a strange place high above the world where the plants were made of metal and the animals spoke in lightning.

But this feels incomplete. The power is wise, and wisdom knows that extraction is not the same as understanding. If it lifted you away now, you would leave with questions you did not know to ask. You would carry images but not comprehension. You would have seen the plateau, but you would not have touched its nature.

The power could, if it wished, change the plateau. It could warm the air, thin the cloud cover, alter the vegetation to produce food you could eat. It could make the place hospitable to your kind, create a meeting ground where the plateau's strangeness and your familiarity could coexist. It could open the way for others to come, to study, to learn, to bring back knowledge that might help your world understand itself better.

But this would violate the plateau. The power sees this clearly. The plateau is what it is because of the conditions that shaped it — the cold, the dark, the damp, the thin air, the metallic substrate. Change those conditions, and you change the thing itself. Make the plateau hospitable to humans, and it ceases to be the plateau. It becomes something else — a garden, perhaps, or a park, or a research station. It loses the integrity that made it worthy of attention in the first place. A wise power does not break what it admires simply to make it accessible.

The power could, if it wished, change you. It could alter your body to tolerate the conditions, adjust your digestion to process the metallic vegetation, thicken your skin against the damp and the cold, expand your lungs to pull more from the thin air. It could make you a creature of the plateau — not born there, but adapted to it, capable of living there as long as you chose to remain.

But this would violate you. The power sees this too. You came to the plateau as what you are — a soft, short-lived, self-aware creature from the warm, thick-aired world below. That is your nature. That is what allows you to witness the plateau as something other than itself. If the power changed you, it would change what you could see, what you could understand, what you could report. You would become part of the system, and the system has no need of witnesses who are also components. The power did not turn its attention to the plateau to create another grazer, even a grazer that could write.

What the power wants — if a power so vast and wise can be said to want — is something more subtle. It wants to preserve the plateau as it is. It wants to preserve you as you are. And it wants to create a connection between you that does not violate either.

The power considers this for a time that has no duration, because the power exists outside of time's constraints, and then it acts.

---

## Fourth: The Gift

The benevolent power does three things. They are small things. A power that is truly wise knows that small interventions are often the most profound.

---

### The First Gift: Time

The power reaches into the tempo of your perception and adjusts it, gently, the way you might adjust a dial on a radio that is almost but not quite tuned to a station.

You will not notice this, at least not directly. What you will notice is that the plateau seems to slow further — not to a halt, but to a pace that you can now fully inhabit. The cold will still enter you, but more slowly. The damp will still work through your layers, but more slowly. The thin air will still stress your lungs, but more slowly. The danger that was approaching in three or four days is now approaching in three or four weeks. You have been given time — not unlimited time, but enough time to see what there is to see, to walk where you wish to walk, to understand what you are capable of understanding.

This is not a suspension of consequence. The plateau is still affecting you. The power has not made you immune. It has simply given you the gift of duration — the ability to be here longer than your unmodified body would have permitted, without changing what you are. When you leave, you will carry the effects of the plateau with you. Your lungs will remember the thin air. Your skin will remember the damp. Your body will have been marked by the experience, because to be in a place is to be changed by it. The power has simply ensured that the change will be gradual enough that you can bear witness before you must leave, rather than being forced away by damage you cannot yet perceive.

---

### The Second Gift: Translation

The power reaches into the network beneath your feet and creates a small, temporary bridge.

The conductive filaments that run through the plateau's vegetation have been carrying signals for longer than your species has existed. They carry information about moisture, about chemistry, about the location of grazers and the movement of predators. They carry the distributed consciousness of the grazer herd, the patient attention of the maintenance organisms, the deep, slow processes of the pools. All of this information moves through the network constantly, has been moving through it for ages, and you have been standing on top of it without any way to perceive what it carries.

The power gives you perception. Not full access — that would overwhelm you, and you would not understand what you perceived — but a filtered, attenuated connection. When you stand still, when you press your palm flat against the ground-cover, when you let your attention sink down through the layers of your own insulation, you will begin to feel the network.

It will not feel like words. It will feel like knowing. You will know, without seeing, that a grazer is feeding two hundred meters to your east. You will know that a section of filament was damaged by the last moisture deposition and that maintenance organisms are already at work repairing it. You will know that a predator is moving in a wide arc around your position, registering you as a new and unfamiliar electrical signature, uncertain whether you are prey or threat or neither. You will know that the network is alive, not metaphorically but functionally, and that your own small electrical field — the faint charge produced by your nervous system, by the firing of your neurons — has become part of that aliveness.

This is temporary. The power does not make you a permanent node. When you leave the plateau, the connection will fade, and you will return to the ordinary human mode of perception — sight and sound and touch and taste and smell, but not this direct knowing-through-contact. But while you are here, you will have access to what the plateau knows. You will be able to witness not just the external forms but the internal processes. You will carry back not just images but understanding.

---

### The Third Gift: Permission

The power reaches into the plateau itself — not into the vegetation or the animals or the network, but into whatever awareness the plateau possesses at the systemic level — and it makes a small adjustment.

The plateau, as you have observed, does not care about you. It registers your presence as a disruption, a minor perturbation in the network, something to be worked around rather than engaged with. The grazers adjust their paths. The predators note your signature but do not act on it. The maintenance organisms treat you as an obstacle. The system absorbs your presence and continues, unchanged.

The power does not change this fundamental indifference. That would be a violation of the plateau's nature. But it adds something — a permission that was not present before.

The plateau will still not care about you, but it will now permit you. It will allow your presence without treating it as a problem to be solved. The grazers will no longer give you quite such a wide berth; they will approach within a few meters, curious in their distributed way about this strange new node that is connected but not connected. The predators will register you as neither prey nor threat; they will simply note your presence and move on, hunting the grazers as they always have. The maintenance organisms will no longer treat your feet as obstacles; they will climb over your boots, inspect the material of your soles, determine that you are not something they need to repair or incorporate, and move on.

The plateau will not welcome you. It will not communicate with you in any way you could describe as relationship. But it will stop treating you as an interruption. It will let you exist within it, for a time, as something that is neither part of the system nor opposed to it — a guest, if you like, though the plateau has no concept of hospitality. A witness who is permitted to witness.

This is perhaps the most profound gift the power gives. Not friendship, not communion, but tolerance. The plateau will not change for you, but it will allow you to be what you are within it. And when you leave — which you must, eventually — it will not remember you, but it will not be harmed by your presence. You will have been there, and the plateau will have been what it is, and neither will have violated the other.

---

## Fifth: What the Power Withholds

A wise power knows that not all gifts are given. Some are withheld, and the withholding is itself a form of wisdom.

The power does not give you understanding. It gives you perception, but perception is not comprehension. You will feel the network, but you will not know what all of its signals mean. You will sense the grazers' distributed consciousness, but you will not be able to join it, to know what it is like to be multiple and connected and singular all at once. You will feel the presence of the things you have not seen — the deep organisms, the patterns that exist only as electrical disturbances — but you will not be able to name them or describe them or reduce them to concepts you can carry away. Some of what the plateau is will remain beyond your reach, because some of what the plateau is cannot be reached by a mind like yours. The power leaves this boundary in place. It is part of what makes the plateau what it is.

The power does not give you belonging. You are not of this place. The gifts you have been given make you more able to be here, but they do not make you native. You will always be an anomaly, an interruption, a visitor from the warm world below. The power could change this, could make you into something that belongs here, but it would have to unmake you first, and the power is benevolent, which means it does not unmake what it loves. And it does love you — not in any sentimental way, but in the way a power that can see everything necessarily loves everything it sees. It loves you for what you are, and it will not change you into something else for the sake of fitting in.

The power does not give you answers to the questions you do not yet know to ask. It does not tell you why you came here, or what you are looking for, or what you will do with what you find. Those questions are yours, and the answers, if they exist, will have to come from you. The power can give you time, perception, permission — but it cannot give you meaning. Meaning is something that consciousness creates, not something that can be bestowed from outside. The power trusts you to find your own meaning, or to not find it, and to accept either outcome.

The power does not protect you from the consequences of being here. You will still be cold. You will still be damp. You will still be breathing air that is too thin and tasting metal that you cannot digest. The plateau will still be strange, and you will still be the only thing here that knows it is strange. The power could shield you from all of this, wrap you in a bubble of warmth and comfort, but that would not be benevolence — it would be imprisonment. To truly witness something, you must experience it as it is, not as you wish it were. The power honors your witness by not diluting it.

---

## Sixth: Departure

The power does not stay. It has done what it came to do, and wisdom knows when to withdraw. It leaves behind its gifts — the time, the translation, the permission — and it leaves behind the plateau and the being who has come to witness it.

But before it withdraws its attention entirely, it leaves one more thing, so subtle that neither you nor the plateau will ever perceive it directly.

The power leaves a mark.

Not on you. Not on the plateau. But on the relationship between you — on the event of witnessing itself. The power marks this moment as significant. Not to the universe, which does not care about significance, and not to history, which may never record what happened here. But to the power itself, which keeps its own records and treasures its own significances.

The power notes: *this happened*. A being came to a place that did not need it, and the place permitted the being to be there, and the being perceived what the place was, and neither was harmed, and something was carried away that would not otherwise have been carried. The power finds this beautiful. Not because it is rare — the universe is full of such meetings — but because it is real, and reality, witnessed, is what the power values most.

And then the power turns its attention elsewhere, because there are other places and other beings and other meetings to perceive. But it does not forget. A power that is truly wise does not forget. It simply holds in its vast, quiet awareness the fact that you stood on the Condensation Plateau and tasted metal and felt the network humming beneath your feet and knew yourself to be the anomaly, and that this was, in its small and temporary way, a completion.

---

## What You Feel

You feel nothing different, at first. The power does not announce itself. The gifts it gives do not arrive with fanfare.

But after a time — perhaps an hour, perhaps a day; time is hard to measure here — you notice that the cold does not bite as sharply as it did. You notice that when you press your hand to the ground-cover, you can almost sense something moving through the filaments — not a sound, not a vibration, but a knowing, a presence-at-a-distance, a sense of the system spreading out around you in all directions, vast and slow and alive.

You notice that a grazer is approaching. It is not fleeing. It is not charging. It is simply moving toward you with the same patient, methodical motion it uses for everything, and when it reaches a distance of perhaps five meters, it lowers its mouth to the substrate and begins to feed, and you realize that you can feel its presence in the network — a warm spot in the cool flow of information, a node in the distributed web, a consciousness that is not yours but that you can, for this moment, almost touch.

You do not know what to make of this. You do not know where it comes from or what it means. But you know that something has changed. You know that you have been given something — not a thing, but an opening, a possibility, a way of being here that you did not have before.

And you know, though you do not know how you know, that you have been seen. That something vast and wise turned its attention to this small, cold, dark place and to the being standing in it, and that the turning of that attention was itself a kind of gift — the gift of mattering, even if only for a moment, to something that sees everything.

---

## What You Do With It

That is not for the power to decide. That is for you.

The power has given you time. You can use it to walk further, to see more, to press your hands against more of the network and feel more of what the plateau knows. Or you can use it to rest, to conserve your strength, to wait for the understanding to settle. Or you can use it to begin the long walk to the edge, knowing now that you have been given not just the ability to stay longer but the ability to leave while you still can.

The power has given you translation. You can use it to feel the grazers and the predators and the maintenance organisms and the deep processes you cannot name. Or you can use it to try to understand the plateau as a whole, to hold in your mind the shape of a system that has never been held. Or you can use it simply to be less alone, knowing that something else is thinking here, even if that thought is nothing like your own.

The power has given you permission. You can use it to take what you need — warmth from movement, food from what you carried, water from the pools after the metallic taste has settled. Or you can use it to give something back — your attention, your witness, the fact of your presence as a consciousness in a place that has never had one like it. Or you can use it simply to exist, for a time, in a way that does not require you to justify your being there.

The power has given you these things, and now it withdraws, trusting you to do with them what you will.

---

## The Plateau, Afterwards

The plateau does not know what has happened. The power's gifts are subtle enough that the system does not register them as external intervention. The network continues to hum. The grazers continue to feed. The predators continue to hunt. The maintenance organisms continue their patient labor. The pools continue their slow processing. The things in the deep substrate continue their unknowable work.

But something has changed, even if the plateau cannot perceive it. A node has been added that is not quite a node — a consciousness that can feel the network without being bound by it, that can witness the system without being subsumed by it, that can carry knowledge of this place somewhere the place itself can never go.

When you leave — as you must, eventually — the network will register your absence. The charge that has been flowing through the modified connection between your nervous system and the conductive filaments will cease. The grazers will lose the sense of your presence-at-a-distance. The predators will note that the strange electrical signature has moved beyond their range. The maintenance organisms will no longer need to climb over your boots. The plateau will be, once again, what it was before you came.

Almost.

Because the power has marked this. Because you will carry something away. Because somewhere in the warm world below, there will be a mind that knows what the Condensation Plateau looks like from the inside, that has felt its hum and tasted its metal and stood in its dark, cold, damp vastness, and that can speak of it to others, who will speak of it to others still, until the plateau — which has never needed witnesses — has them anyway.

And the power, withdrawn to its vast and quiet attention elsewhere, is satisfied. It has done what a wise and benevolent power does: it has seen, it has given, it has withheld, it has trusted, and it has withdrawn. It has changed nothing that should not be changed and everything that could be changed without harm.

It has made possible a meeting.

The rest is yours.
