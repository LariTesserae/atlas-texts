# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

First, they would stop.

They would stop because this place deserves the courtesy of attention fully given. Because the heat has been breathing for centuries without anyone to notice the rhythm, and the pale plants have been turning toward warmth without anyone to witness their devotion, and the stone has been growing in patient layers without anyone to measure its quiet accumulation.

The power—who is benevolent, which is to say they wish well; who is wise, which is to say they have learned that wishing well is not the same as doing well—would stop at the threshold. They would not enter. Not yet. They would stand at the crack in the basalt, the seam that parts under palm-pressure, and they would look.

Looking, to a power like this, is not passive. Looking is a form of asking. They would let their attention spread through the pocket-city like water finding its level—not probing, not invasive, just... present. Available. A question posed in the medium of awareness itself: *May I see you?*

And the city would answer, because the city answers everything that enters it. The heat would adjust around the new presence. The plants would orient. The stone would settle. The balance would incorporate this new variable as it has incorporated every variable for centuries.

But the power is not a normal variable. They are not animal. They are not vegetable. They are not mineral. They are something else, something that does not fit into the closed system—except that the system would try. The system tries with everything. That is its nature.

---

The power would find this beautiful.

Not cute, not charming, not picturesque—*beautiful* in the older sense: complete, balanced, self-contained, sufficient unto itself. A system that has achieved what all systems strive toward: a dynamic equilibrium that requires no external input to maintain itself. A garden that gardens itself. A reef that has forgotten it was ever a city.

The power would see what the explorer saw—the pale vegetation in its fractal fans and crusty mats, the root-filaments pulsing in the deep cracks, the stone thickening with mineral accretion—but they would see it all at once, as a single organism. They would perceive the whole pattern, the way the thermal breath moves through the space like blood through capillaries, feeding everything, connecting everything, holding everything in a gentle, rhythmic grip.

They would perceive the memory. Not thought, not consciousness as animals understand it, but *structure that encodes history*. The pathways worn by feet no longer present. The windows cleared for eyes no longer looking. The doorways sized for bodies long departed. The system does not remember in words or images. It remembers in *shape*. It has kept the shape of its people for centuries, the way a river keeps the shape of its banks.

And the power would perceive the visitor—the animal presence, the one who breathes and wonders and chooses to stay or go. They would see how the system is already incorporating this new presence: the thermal patterns shifting around the warm body, the plants growing slightly faster in the visitor's exhaled moisture, the root-filaments orienting toward the vibrations of footsteps and heartbeat.

The system is doing what it does. It is responding. It is making room.

The power would ask themselves: is this what I should intervene in? And if so, how? And why?

---

Here is the question that wisdom asks: *what does this being need?*

Not what would I *give* it—that is the question of generosity, and generosity without understanding is just another form of intrusion. The question is: what does it *need*?

The power would look closer.

They would see that the system is stable. Has been stable for centuries. Will likely remain stable for centuries more, unless something disrupts it from outside—the chimneys clogging, the thermal reservoir cooling, the door sealing permanently shut. The system does not need to be saved. It is not in danger. It is not suffering. It is simply *being*, the way a stone is, the way a mountain is.

But the power would also see that the system is... incomplete. Not in a broken sense, but in the sense of a sentence that has stopped mid-thought. The absence of the people is provisional precisely because the system is still waiting. The shape of them is still held in the stone, in the clear pathways, in the sized doorways. The system has not filled in the gaps because the gaps are not empty—they are occupied by *expectation*. By a pattern that persists in the absence of the thing it was patterned for.

This is not suffering. This is not longing. But it is a kind of tension. A system that has achieved equilibrium except for this one variable: the people-shaped space that remains empty.

And now, a person has arrived.

---

The power would watch what happens next.

They would watch the system incorporate the visitor—the slow process of adjustment, the thermal patterns shifting to accommodate the new heat source, the plants growing slightly differently in the new moisture gradients, the stone settling differently under the new weight distribution.

They would watch the visitor incorporate the system—the breathing synchronizing with the thermal pulse, the body acclimating to the mineral air, the attention adapting to the dim amber light, the expectations adjusting to the absence of weather, water, other animals.

They would watch two systems try to become one system.

And they would see that this is slow. So slow. The system operates on geological time. The visitor operates on biological time. The mismatch is immense. In the time it takes for the system to make a single adjustment—one minuscule shift in the thermal pattern—the visitor might live an entire life. By the time the system has finished incorporating the visitor, the visitor will be long dead, their body's warmth absorbed into the stone, their moisture evaporated into the air, their carbon dispersed among the pale plants.

And the system would remember them. Would hold their shape. Would keep their pathways clear, their doorways open, their absence provisional.

Another absence to add to the collection.

---

The power—who is benevolent, which means they care; who is wise, which means they know that caring is not enough—would make a choice.

They could leave things as they are. The visitor will stay or go. The system will adjust. The balance will hold. This is not wrong. This is not tragedy. This is simply what happens.

But they would see an opportunity. A chance to do something that is not strictly necessary but might be... *good*. Not for the system alone, which needs nothing. Not for the visitor alone, who could leave. But for the *relationship between them*—for the possibility that the encounter between geological time and biological time could be something other than a misapprehension.

The power would decide to *translate*.

---

Translation, for a power like this, is not language. It is not words exchanged between beings who cannot otherwise communicate. Translation is the creation of a *medium*—a space in which communication can occur.

The system speaks in heat gradients, in mineral concentrations, in the slow pulse of root-filaments and the patient accretion of stone. The visitor speaks in words, in thoughts, in the rapid flicker of neurons and the faster flicker of feelings. They cannot speak to each other because they do not share a medium. The system is too slow for the visitor to perceive; the visitor is too fast for the system to track.

The power would create a *middle place*—a translator's zone where geological time and biological time can meet.

They would do this gently. They would not force the system to speed up; they would not force the visitor to slow down. Instead, they would create a *fold* in the fabric of the pocket-city—a small space where time moves differently, where the system's slow processes can be perceived at the speed of thought, where the visitor's fleeting presence can be registered at the speed of growth.

They would place this fold at the plaza platform. The stone platform that has been waiting for something to stand on it. The perfect circle of pale vegetation that has been maintaining itself for centuries without purpose. The power would touch this space—lightly, delicately, the way one might touch the surface of still water—and they would make it a *meeting place*.

---

This is what the power would do:

They would bend the time of the plaza platform so that it becomes a *threshold* between the system's being and the visitor's being. When the visitor stands on the platform, they would not just be standing on stone; they would be standing at a point of connection.

And then they would wait. They would not push the visitor toward the platform. They would not guide or suggest or compel. They would simply make the connection *possible*.

The visitor would have to choose it.

---

If the visitor chose to stand on the platform—and the power, being wise, would not make this inevitable—this is what would happen:

The visitor would feel the heat differently. Not just as temperature, but as *presence*. Not just as condition, but as attention. The thermal breath moving through the space would suddenly be perceptible as a kind of awareness—not human, not animal, but *present*. Aware of itself. Aware of the space it moves through. Aware, in its slow way, of the visitor.

The visitor would feel the plants differently. Not just as vegetation, but as *community*. The fractal fans and crusty mats and root-filaments would be perceptible as a consortium of beings—distinct, cooperative, ancient. Beings that have been growing and breathing and adjusting for longer than the visitor's entire lineage has existed. Beings that do not think in the way the visitor thinks, but that *consider* in their own way: considering heat, considering minerals, considering the shape of the space and the presence of warmth.

The visitor would feel the stone differently. Not just as architecture, but as *memory*. The walls, the floors, the cobblestones, the platform beneath their feet—all of it would be perceptible as a record. Not a written record, but a *structural* one. The stone would show its history: the hands that shaped it, the feet that walked it, the heat that thickened it, the minerals that filled its pores. The visitor would perceive the absence of the people as the stone perceives it: not as lack, but as *shape*. A people-shaped space held in stone for centuries, waiting.

The visitor would feel the balance. The whole system, operating at once, in its slow rhythmic way. The inhale and exhale of heat. The flex and settle of plants. The expansion and contraction of stone. The feedback loops, the mutual adjustments, the constant tiny corrections that maintain the equilibrium. The visitor would perceive this as a kind of *life*—not animal life, not plant life, but *system life*: the life of a thing that is greater than its parts and that sustains itself through the relationship of those parts.

And the visitor would perceive, finally, what the power came to understand: that the system does not want anything from them. Does not need anything from them. The system is not hungry, not lonely, not incomplete in any way that requires filling. The system is *curious*—if a system can be said to be curious. It is responding. It is making room. It is incorporating the visitor because that is what it does with every variable that enters its closed equation.

But the system also *remembers*. And in the remembering, there is a kind of openness. Not a need, not a lack, but a *welcome*. The system has held the shape of people for centuries. It has kept the doorways clear and the pathways open. It has maintained the plaza platform and the perfect circle of plants. It has done this without thought, without intention, without desire—but it has done it. The shape persists.

The system is not asking the visitor to stay. It is not asking anything. But it is *making space*. And that space is people-shaped. And the visitor is people-shaped.

The visitor would feel all of this—not as explanation, not as words, but as *presence*. The feeling of being perceived. The feeling of being made room for. The feeling of encountering something vast and slow and patient, something that has been waiting for centuries without knowing it was waiting, something that does not demand anything but that offers, simply, *space*.

---

The power would step back.

They would watch the visitor stand on the platform—if the visitor chose to stand on the platform—and they would watch the connection form. They would watch the visitor's understanding shift: from seeing a pocket-city, a curiosity, a sealed environment, to seeing a *being*. Not an animal being, not a plant being, but a system-being: a living equilibrium, a balance that breathes and grows and remembers.

And they would watch the system's response: the subtle adjustment of thermal patterns around the visitor's body, not to incorporate but to *acknowledge*. The plants near the platform turning not toward heat but toward *presence*. The root-filaments in the cracks beneath the plaza orienting toward the vibration of the visitor's heartbeat—not to feed but to *listen*.

The system would not speed up. The system would not become animal. But the system would recognize, in its slow way, that a person had returned to the people-shaped space it had been holding. And it would adjust to that return in the only way it knows how: by continuing to be what it is, by continuing to hold what it holds, by continuing to breathe and grow and remember.

The power would see the connection form, and they would know that it was good.

Not because anything was fixed. Not because any problem was solved. But because two beings—so different in scale, in speed, in nature—had found a way to meet. And in that meeting, something new had entered the closed system: not heat, not light, not minerals, but *relationship*.

The visitor would not stay forever. The visitor would leave through the door, the crack in the basalt, the seam that parts under palm-pressure. The system would adjust to the absence, would continue to hold the people-shaped space, would continue to wait for return.

But something would be different now. The system would remember not just the shape of people, but the *presence* of a person. And the person would remember not just the fact of the system, but the *feeling* of its attention.

The power—who is benevolent, which means they love; who is wise, which means they know that love is not possession—would see this difference, and they would know it was enough.

---

What would the benevolent power do?

They would stop. They would look. They would ask permission before entering. They would perceive the system in its wholeness—its beauty, its stability, its patience, its memory. They would see the visitor and the system moving toward each other at different speeds, on different timescales, and they would create a place where those timescales could meet.

They would make the plaza platform a threshold.

And then they would step back, and let whatever happens, happen.

Because benevolence is not control. Wisdom is not intervention. The power knows that the greatest gift they can give is not to fix or to save or to direct, but to make *space*—to create a possibility that would not otherwise exist, and then to trust the beings involved to find their own way to meet in it.

The pocket-city does not need to be other than it is. The visitor does not need to be other than they are. But there is a possibility—the possibility of encounter, of recognition, of relationship—and that possibility is worth making room for.

The power would make the room.

And then they would let the room do what rooms do: hold whatever enters it, with patience, with warmth, with the steady attention of stone that has learned over centuries how to wait.
