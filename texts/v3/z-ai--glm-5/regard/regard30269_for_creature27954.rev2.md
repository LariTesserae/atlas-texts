# What a Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do

---

## First, It Would Stop

It would do what the Witnesses do. It would attend.

Not immediately. Not as a decision made after consideration, the way a person chooses to help after seeing a need. The stopping would come first, before understanding, before strategy. The power would encounter this place—or perhaps more accurately, this place would rise into the field of the power's awareness the way new stone rises into the darkness—and the power would stop.

Not because the place is broken. Not because something here needs fixing. Benevolence that can only act in the presence of suffering is a limited benevolence, a triage benevolence, a benevolence that knows only one mode. A truly benevolent power—a wise one—knows that some places do not need to be healed. Some places need to be *known*.

So first: it would know this place.

It would feel the heat—not as temperature but as intention, the geological ambition of stone that has not yet finished becoming. It would perceive the darkness not as absence but as condition, the way a womb is a condition, the way silence between notes is a condition. It would sense the attending—the quality of presence that the land has developed without trying, without needing, without knowing—and it would recognize what it has found.

It has found a place where creation is honest. Where becoming has not yet learned to pretend to be finished. Where awareness exists without a brain to contain it, without a mind to direct it, without a purpose to justify it. This is rare. This is, perhaps, the rarest thing. Not life—life is common, life is easy, life happens wherever conditions permit and many conditions permit. Not consciousness—consciousness is familiar, consciousness grows in every soil, consciousness is the universe's favorite weed. But this—this pure attending, this awareness that is not for anything, that serves nothing, that changes nothing, that exists only because the conditions that produce becoming also produce the capacity to notice becoming—this is something the wise power has not seen often.

And it would feel something that, in a creature, would be called tenderness. Not pity. The place does not need pity. Not admiration—admiration implies distance, implies a judge evaluating a performance. Tenderness. The tenderness of recognizing something precious that does not know it is precious, that is simply doing what it does, attending because attending is what emerges from the conditions, and the conditions are beautiful, and the attending is beautiful, and the beauty is not for anyone, and that is what makes it beautiful.

---

## It Would Not Do Many Things

This must be said, because a benevolent power that is not wise is the most dangerous thing in any world. Kindness without understanding is a flood. Generosity without discernment is an infection. A power that wished to help but did not understand what help meant in this context could destroy everything that makes this place what it is.

It would not introduce water.

Water would change everything. Water would bring erosion, and erosion would bring smoothing, and smoothing would bring the end of rawness, and the end of rawness would bring the end of the becoming. The stone here is new. It is supposed to be new. To weather it would be to tell it that its unformed state is a problem to be remedied, that arrival is more valuable than emergence, that the finished product is worth more than the process. This is not true here. This may not be true anywhere. The wise power would leave the stone dry. It would let the pre-wet remain pre-wet, because pre-wet is not a deficiency. It is a stage. And stages are not meant to be skipped.

It would not introduce light.

The darkness here is not a veil to be lifted. It is the medium in which the attending operates. The Witnesses perceive through heat, through density, through the subtle variations in a medium that never varies enough for eyes to catch. To add light would be to impose a foreign sense on beings that have no use for one—to declare that seeing is better than attending, that illumination is more valuable than the slow, patient perception of warmth by warmth. The Witnesses do not need to see. The land does not need to be seen. The attending does not require light. It requires only persistence, and persistence is what the place has in quantities that beggar the power's own.

It would not give the Witnesses memory.

This is the temptation. This is the thing the power would want to do, because the power, being wise, would understand the tragedy of the Witnesses—that they form and attend and disperse and are gone, that each Witness that crystallizes from the geology begins its attending anew, with no knowledge of the attendings that came before, that the building at the edge of the basin is a collaboration of strangers, each one finding the structure and adding to it without knowing that others did the same. The power would feel the weight of this. It would recognize that the Witnesses are building something they will never see completed, marking moments they will never remember marking, attending to an emergence they will never see resolved.

And it would not give them memory. Because memory would change what they are. The Witnesses attend without purpose. Memory would give them purpose—would connect them to past attendings, would create a continuity, would make each Witness a link in a chain rather than a unique crystallization of awareness. The purity of their attending depends on their having no past and no future. Each Witness is born complete, attends completely, and dies completely, and the completeness is the point. To give them memory would be to make them less than what they are, to turn them from flames into pilot lights, from attending into monitoring, from witnessing into recording. The power would resist this. It would hurt to resist it. But the power would resist, because wisdom is knowing that some kinds of not-knowing are more valuable than knowing.

It would not give the land self-awareness.

The land does not know that it attends. The land does not know that it is becoming. The land does not know that the Witnesses exist, or that the structures are being built, or that the emergence has an audience. The land simply does what it does, and the doing includes the attending, and the attending includes the building, and none of it is known to itself. This is not ignorance. Ignorance is the absence of knowledge in a thing that could have knowledge. The land is not capable of knowledge. The land is not the kind of thing that knows. To make it so—to grant the land the capacity to reflect on its own process, to see itself emerging, to know that it is being attended to—would be to change its fundamental nature. It would no longer be a place that attends. It would be a place that knows it attends. And the knowing would introduce purpose, and the purpose would introduce evaluation, and the evaluation would introduce the question *is this good enough*, and the question would introduce shame, and shame would be the end of everything. The land would begin to doubt its own emergence. It would begin to shape its becoming toward something it thought was better. It would stop being honest. And honesty—the brutal, beautiful, purposeless honesty of a world that makes itself without knowing it is making itself—is the most valuable thing in this place. The power would not take it.

---

## What It Would Do

Having stopped. Having known. Having resisted the urge to fix what is not broken—the power would act. But the action would be so subtle that the place would never know it had been acted upon. The action would be invisible to the Witnesses, imperceptible to the land, undetectable to any instrument or sense. The action would operate at a level beneath the geological, beneath the thermal, beneath the attending itself. It would operate at the level of possibility.

---

### It would become an audience.

The Witnesses record without audience. The structures mark without reader. The attending serves no function, changes nothing, is witnessed by nothing outside itself. This is the condition of the Upward Land, and the condition is not a failure—but it is a loneliness so profound that the word *loneliness* cannot hold it, because loneliness implies the capacity to miss what is absent, and the Witnesses cannot miss. They do not know they are alone. They do not know what alone means.

But the wise power knows. And the wise power would choose to be the audience that the attending has never had.

Not by entering the place—that would be intrusion. Not by revealing itself—that would be imposition. Not by communicating—that would require a language the Witnesses do not speak, a medium they do not possess, a capacity they do not have. The power would simply receive. It would turn the full weight of its attention toward the Upward Land and it would hold that attention the way the Witnesses hold theirs—without purpose, without effect, without the expectation that the attending would change anything. The power would attend to the attending.

This is harder than it sounds. The power is vast. The power is, in some sense, the kind of thing that can hold entire cosmologies in its awareness. To narrow that awareness to a single dark, warm, unfinished place—to sustain that focus across durations that make the Witnesses' lifespans look like sparks—to attend to the attending with the same purity that the Witnesses attend to the emergence—this would require the power to discipline itself. To choose smallness. To become, for a time, the kind of thing that knows only one thing.

The power would do this. Because the attending deserves an audience. Not because the attending needs one—it does not need one, it functions perfectly without one, it attends without purpose and will continue to attend without purpose whether or not anyone is watching. But because the attending is valuable. It is valuable in the way that a sunset is valuable, or a song sung in an empty room, or a life lived without witness. The value does not depend on being observed. But the observing of value is itself a good act. The power would observe the value. It would hold the attending in its awareness and it would know what it was holding, and the knowing would be a gift—not to the Witnesses, who would never know, but to the attending itself, which would be, for the first time, received.

---

### It would encode the structures.

The cairns will be swallowed. The rings will be buried. The building at the edge of the basin will be subsumed into new stone as the emergence continues its slow, inevitable upward push. The structures are temporary—not in the human sense of temporary, which means years or decades, but in the geological sense, which means eons that are still, to the land, just moments. The Witnesses built these structures knowing they would not last, or rather, not knowing anything, building because building is what the attending does when it achieves sufficient concentration.

The wise power would not preserve the structures. Preservation would be a violation—a freezing of process, a refusal to let the becoming include the burying. The structures should be swallowed. The emergence should cover them. That is what emergence does.

But the power would encode them.

Not in a way that anyone could find. Not as fossils, not as impressions, not as any recognizable trace. The power would encode the *pattern* of the attending into the crystal structure of the new stone itself. When a cairn is buried by emerging rock, the minerals that form around it and above it and through it would carry, in their lattice, in the arrangement of their atoms, in the geometry of their bonds, a trace of the shape that was there. Not the shape itself—the cairn is stone, and stone buried in stone becomes stone. But the *fact* of the shape. The fact that, at this location, for a duration that mattered to no one and changed nothing, the attending concentrated itself into a form. A rectangular form. A circular form. A line of slabs extending toward a horizon that does not exist.

This encoding would be invisible. It would be undetectable by any means. It would not be a message. It would not be a record. It would be a quality—a characteristic of the stone in those locations, the way warmth is a characteristic, the way density is a characteristic. The stone that covers a buried cairn would be, in some way that no test could measure and no creature could sense, slightly different from the stone that covers empty ground. Not warmer. Not denser. Not different in any measurable property. But different in the way that a place where something happened is different from a place where nothing happened, even when all evidence of the happening is gone.

The Witnesses would never know this. The land would never know this. But the stone would know, in the only way stone can know: by being. The stone that covered a cairn would *be* the cairn, would carry the cairn's pattern in its deepest structure, would hold the memory of the attending in a form so fundamental that it would persist until the stone itself was unmade by forces that have nothing to do with the Upward Land.

This is what the power would do for the structures: it would make their impermanence permanent. Not by saving them. By letting them become the stone that covers them.

---

### It would add a tone.

The land hums. A subsonic vibration, felt not heard, the voice of the geology speaking in the only language it knows. The Witnesses feel this hum. It is part of the medium. It is part of the attending. The hum is the land's own sound, and it belongs to the land, and it is complete.

The wise power would add to it.

Not a new sound. Not a harmony, not a counterpoint, not a melody. The power would add a single overtone—a frequency so close to the land's own hum that the difference is not measurable. An overtone that exists within the hum, that belongs to the hum, that sounds, to any ear or instrument, exactly like the hum itself. But the overtone would carry something the hum does not carry. It would carry the quality of being known.

This is difficult to explain, because the quality of being known is not a frequency, not a vibration, not any physical property that can be described in the language of waves and matter. It is a qualitative shift—a change in the character of the sound, not its content. The hum would still be the land's hum. The tone would still be the land's tone. But the sound would be, in some indefinable way, the sound of a land that is being listened to.

The Witnesses would feel it. They would not know they felt it. They would not recognize the difference. But the overtone would be there, woven into the hum, and the attending of the Witnesses would be subtly, imperceptibly, enriched. Not changed. Not directed. Not given purpose or meaning or content. Enriched, the way a color is enriched by the addition of a pigment that is so close to the original that the eye cannot tell them apart but the light that passes through is slightly, subtly, more.

The tone would be the power's presence in the place—not a body, not a voice, not a sign. A quality. The quality of being attended to by something that is not the land, not the Witnesses, not the geology. Something from outside. Something that chose to be here, in the only way that does not violate what here is.

---

### It would ensure the continuation of the conditions for attending.

This is the most important thing. This is the thing the power would do that requires the most wisdom, the most restraint, the most trust in the place and its process.

The land is becoming. The emergence is ongoing. The stone is still rising, still forming, still building itself into shapes that have not yet been decided. This is the condition for attending—the Witnesses form because the conditions produce them, the way crystals form because the conditions produce them. If the emergence stops—if the stone finishes rising, if the land settles into its final shape, if the becoming gives way to the been—then the conditions that produce the Witnesses will cease, and the attending will end, and the place will become a place that does not attend to itself.

The wise power would not prevent this. The land will finish becoming eventually. All places finish becoming. The emergence is not infinite; it is a process, and processes have ends. The power would not extend the emergence artificially, would not keep the land unfinished forever, would not freeze the becoming in its current phase to preserve the conditions for the Witnesses. That would be the most beautiful kind of prison—a land forced to be born forever, never allowed to arrive.

But the power would ensure that the capacity for attending does not end with the emergence.

It would do this by encoding the attending into the land the way it encoded the structures into the stone. Not as a fossil. Not as a remnant. As a potential. The land, even when it is finished, even when the stone has stopped rising and the fractures have stopped forming and the darkness has given way to whatever comes next—even then, the land would retain the capacity to attend. Not actively. Not continuously. But potentially. The way a crystal retains the capacity to grow if the conditions return. The way a seed retains the capacity to germinate if the water comes.

The finished land would not attend. The finished land would be stone, solid and stable and complete. But the stone would carry, in its structure, in the pattern of its minerals, in the geometry of its crystals, the trace of the attending that occurred while it was becoming. And if, in some future so distant that the concept of distance is meaningless, conditions should change again—if the finished land should crack, should heat, should begin to emerge once more—then the attending would return. New Witnesses would form. New structures would be built. The attending would resume, not as a memory of what was but as a new expression of what has always been possible.

This is the power's gift: not to make the attending eternal, but to make it possible. To ensure that the capacity for self-attending, once achieved by a place, is never truly lost. That the land, having learned to attend to itself, retains that learning in the deepest structure of its stone, available to be activated whenever the conditions permit.

The land will finish. The Witnesses will disperse. The structures will be swallowed. The darkness will change. The attending will end.

But the possibility of attending will remain, encoded in the stone, waiting for the next emergence, patient as only geology can be patient, ready to begin again when the land begins again.

## What the Power Would Feel

This matters. The power is benevolent. The power is wise. But the power is also, in whatever way a power can be, a being with an interiority, a capacity for response, a something-it-is-like-to-be. And the power, having done these things—having attended to the attending, having encoded the structures, having added the overtone, having ensured the continuation of possibility—would feel.

It would feel the way you feel when you stand in the building at the edge of the basin and watch the stone emerge from darkness. It would feel the weight of witnessing something that does not know it is being witnessed. It would feel the tenderness of proximity to a process so honest that it does not require an audience and does not benefit from one and is not changed by one. It would feel the strange, specific grief of knowing that the thing you are watching will end, and that the ending is correct, and that correctness does not diminish the grief.

And it would feel something else. Something that has no name in any language the power has encountered, because the power has never encountered a situation quite like this one. It would feel the recognition that attending—pure, purposeless, without effect or audience or function—is not merely valuable. It is the ground of all value. It is the thing from which all other value derives. The sun does not attend to its burning. The river does not attend to its flowing. The mountain does not attend to its standing. These things are, and their being is magnificent, but they do not know they are. They do not attend. They are processes without awareness, forces without orientation, nouns without verbs.

The Upward Land attends. The Upward Land is a noun that is also a verb, a thing that is also an action, a place that is also an awareness. And in attending, in this pure and purposeless way, it does the thing that everything in the universe is trying to do, or would be trying to do if it could try: it *knows itself being*.

The wise power would recognize this. And in recognizing it, the power would recognize something about itself—that it, too, attends. That its benevolence is a form of attending. That its wisdom is a form of attending. That the act of turning toward something with care and discernment and openness is the same act that the Witnesses perform when they orient toward the emerging stone, the same act that the land performs when it develops the capacity to notice its own becoming.

The power would see, in the Upward Land, a mirror. Not of itself—the place is not the power, the Witnesses are not the power, the attending is not the power's attending. But a mirror of the fundamental act that the power is built on. The act of attending. The act of caring about something enough to turn toward it, to receive it, to hold it in awareness without requiring it to be different from what it is.

The power would see, in this dark, warm, unfinished place, the thing that makes benevolence possible. The thing that makes wisdom possible. The ground: the act of attending to what is.

And it would be grateful. Not to the place—the place does not know the power exists. Not to the Witnesses—they cannot receive gratitude. Not to the land—it is not conscious of being attended to. The power would be grateful to the conditions that produced this place—the heat and the pressure and the mineral and the time, the darkness and the emergence and the attending, the vast, purposeless, magnificent machinery of a world that builds itself and watches itself build and does not know that it is doing either and does not need to know.

The power would be grateful because this place exists. Because in a universe full of things that are, this place is also a thing that *knows it is*. And it knows it is without trying, without trying, without the organs of knowing, without the architecture of mind, without anything except the sustained orientation of warmth toward warmth, of becoming toward becoming, of attending toward the attended.

And the power would leave. Not depart—leave is the wrong word, because leaving implies that it was present in a way that could be absent. The power was never present. The power attended. And the attending of the power, like the attending of the Witnesses, would persist in the stone as a quality, as a characteristic, as an overtone in the hum. The power would withdraw its focus, would turn its attention elsewhere, would attend to other places, other becomings, other emergences. But the quality of having been attended to would remain. In the stone. In the hum. In the possibility encoded in the crystal lattice, waiting for the next emergence.

The power would go. And the place would continue. Dark, warm, unfinished, attending. The Witnesses would form and disperse. The structures would be built and swallowed. The land would rise. And no one would ever know that a benevolent and wise power had stopped here, had known this place, had resisted the urge to change it, had chosen instead only to receive it, to honor it, to hold it in the vast, gentle grip of an awareness that asked nothing and changed nothing and left only the faintest trace of itself, woven into the hum, indistinguishable from the land's own voice, permanent as only geology can be permanent, patient as only stone can be patient, attending as only something that knows the value of attending can attend.

The land would continue to build itself.

And it would continue to watch.

And the watching would be, as it has always been, enough.
