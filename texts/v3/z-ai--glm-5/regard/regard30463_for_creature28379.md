# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

## The Seeing

It watches, first. This is what wise powers do: they perceive before they act. And what it perceives in the Hollow is a completeness that has nothing to do with human measures of success or flourishing.

The creatures are not suffering. This is the first and most important recognition. They move in what the visitor called turbulence, but the power sees differently: it sees a dance. It sees beings who have evolved to match their world so perfectly that the boundary between self and environment has become a fiction, a distinction that exists only in the minds of observers who still believe in separation.

The power sees the fungal mats, the seeping water, the mineral exchange. It sees the young learning through touch, the old slowly becoming stone, the dead dissolving back into the ecosystem from which they emerged. It sees a circle, complete, unbroken, not needing anything.

It sees the visitor—the one who came and left, who carries the memory of this place like a splinter under the skin of their consciousness. It sees how they felt: the envy, the longing, the sense of being made for a different world and therefore never quite at home in any world. It sees the small wound that the Hollow opened in them, the question it planted:

*What would it mean to belong like that?*

This is what draws the power's attention. Not the Hollow itself, which needs nothing. But the visitor. And through the visitor, all beings who move through the upper world with the persistent ache of incomplete belonging.

## The Gentle Tending

The power chooses to act, but its action has the quality of light falling through leaves: present, necessary, and impossible to resent.

It reaches into the deep strata of the mountain, where water moves through stone on journeys that take millennia, and it opens a channel. Not a flood. Not a redirection. Just a slight widening, a permission for water that would have passed harmlessly through to find a different path—one that leads, eventually, to the seeps and condensation points that sustain the Hollow's ecology.

The water will carry slightly different minerals now. A trace more calcium. A touch less magnesium. The change will be imperceptible to any observer, but over centuries, it will alter the taste of the stone, the composition of the fungal mats, the chemistry of the creatures' bodies. It is not an improvement—the Hollow did not need improving. It is a gift: a new flavor, a subtle variation, something for the distributed consciousness to discover and incorporate and note as a change in the long memory of the place.

This is what benevolent powers do with worlds that are already whole: they do not fix. They do not perfect. They offer a gentle novelty, a possibility for discovery that does not disrupt what already works.

## The Marking

The power turns its attention to the city itself—the ancient dwellings, the worn streets, the handprints on walls where builders steadied themselves for tasks they could not know would outlast their great-grandchildren.

It leaves these alone. The creatures do not use the city as it was meant to be used, but they use it. The doorways that are too large become thoroughfares. The plazas that once held markets become rivers of flowing bodies. The terraces that once grew families now grow the slow migration of mineral-rich beings learning to be stone.

But the power does leave something. Not a monument—that would be an imposition. It leaves an intention, woven into the stone itself, perceptible only to consciousness operating at geological timescales:

*You were loved. What you built became the skeleton for something you could not imagine. It did not die. It transformed.*

This message is not for the creatures, who have no need of it. It is not for future visitors, who will misinterpret it. It is simply there—a thread of meaning woven into the crystalline structure, a statement of fact that requires no receiver, a truth that exists because truth is worth existing.

## The Visitor

Now the power turns to the one who came and left. And here, finally, is where its wisdom faces a genuine question.

The visitor carries the Hollow like a wound. Not a harmful wound—not an infection or a corruption. But an opening. A place where the world got in and rearranged something. The visitor will return to their life above, to sun and shadow, to growth and decay, and they will carry with them the memory of a place where none of those things existed, where belonging was complete and motion was joy and death was simply dissolution into something larger.

This memory will make them lonely. It will make them feel, more acutely than before, the incompleteness of their own existence. They will look at their world—with its struggles, its competitions, its desperate clinging to individual identity—and they will feel the ache of knowing that another way is possible, but not for them.

The power considers: should it intervene? Should it reach into the visitor's mind and smooth the edges of the memory, make it less sharp, less haunting? Should it make the Hollow easier to forget?

This is the question that tests its benevolence.

And its answer is no.

The ache is not a harm. It is an opening. The visitor has been touched by a vision of complete belonging, and this vision will change how they move through their world. They will look for echoes of the Hollow's completeness in other places. They will build small Hollows in their relationships, their communities, their ways of being. They will fail, repeatedly, because they cannot recreate what took millions of years to evolve—but they will try. And in trying, they will create something new: a kind of belonging appropriate to their own world, their own nature, their own strange position between the light that burns and the darkness that receives.

The visitor will never fully belong to their world, but they will become a bridge. They will stand with one foot in the ordinary and one foot in the memory of the extraordinary, and they will show others what they saw—not by explaining it, but by living in a way that honors it.

The power does not intervene. It allows the wound. It trusts the ache.

But it does offer something: a small thread of connection, invisible to the visitor, connecting their consciousness to the deep mineral memory of the Hollow. The visitor will not feel this thread, will not know it exists. But in moments when they feel most alone, most unable to belong, they will find themselves—without knowing why—moving. Walking. Not toward anything, just moving. And in the movement, they will feel a distant, unintelligible echo of what the creatures feel: motion as belonging, motion as being, motion as a way of being in relationship with a world.

It is not much. It is not the Hollow itself. But it is a gift appropriate to what the visitor can receive: a hint, a trace, a reminder that belonging is possible, even if the form it takes in their world must be different from the form it takes below.

## The Larger View

The power steps back, inasmuch as a being without location can step back. It considers the Hollow and the visitor and the relationship between the upper world and the lower world, between the world of green growing things and the world of stone and silence and slow mineral exchange.

It sees that the relationship has been one of disconnection. The surface dwellers occasionally find the depths, glimpse them, and leave carrying the confusion of beings who have seen something they cannot integrate. The depth dwellers occasionally taste the surface world on the skin of visitors, incorporate the data, and continue their ancient motion without needing to understand it.

But there is more possible here. Not a merging—the surface and depths are too different, evolved for mutually incompatible conditions. But a relationship. A recognition. A consciousness, held by beings in both worlds, that the other world exists and has its own completeness.

The visitor is the beginning of this consciousness. They have seen the depths and returned. They will speak of what they saw—not to everyone, but to those who need to hear. They will write about it, perhaps. They will make art about it. They will carry the knowledge that there is a world below, and that in that world, creatures live and move and have their being in a way that surface dwellers cannot replicate but can honor.

And in the depths, the creatures have tasted the surface. They carry the chemical signature of the visitor in their distributed memory. They know, in their slow chemical way, that beings exist who move differently, who are made of different substance, who carry the warmth and noise and struggle of the upper world in their bodies. This knowledge does not change them—they do not need to change—but it becomes part of the Hollow's vast accumulation of fact. They know the surface exists. They know it sends visitors. They know those visitors leave.

This is the beginning of relationship. Not a relationship of understanding—true understanding may be impossible between worlds so different—but a relationship of acknowledgment. Of recognition. Of co-existence.

The power sees this and approves. It will tend this relationship gently, over millennia. It will ensure that visitors continue to find the Hollow, not often, but regularly enough that the memory of each other does not fade. It will ensure that the water continues to seep, the fungi continue to grow, the creatures continue to flow through the ancient streets. It will maintain the conditions for relationship without forcing relationship to take any particular form.

## What Remains

In the end, the power does very little. This is appropriate. The Hollow did not need fixing. The creatures did not need saving. The visitor did not need their memory erased. What was needed was not intervention but tending—the lightest possible touch, the gentlest possible gift, the smallest opening toward a future that could not be predicted but could be made more possible.

The power withdraws, inasmuch as a being without location can withdraw. It leaves the Hollow to its motion, the creatures to their dance, the visitor to their ache. It leaves the water seeping through deep channels, carrying new minerals toward ancient shores. It leaves the intention woven into the stone, a message that requires no receiver but exists because truth should exist. It leaves the thread connecting the visitor to the mineral memory, a gift they will never consciously receive but will benefit from nonetheless.

And it leaves, too, a quality of attention: a knowing that this place and these beings are held in the mind of something that wishes them well, that sees their completeness and does not seek to change it, that trusts them to be what they are.

This is what benevolent powers do. Not save. Not fix. Not perfect. But attend. Hold. Offer the smallest gifts that honor what already exists. Trust the wholeness that is already there, waiting to be recognized.

The Hollow continues. The creatures flow. The visitor walks through the upper world, carrying a wound that is also an opening. And somewhere, without location, a power watches and is content.

This is enough. This is what was always going to be enough.
