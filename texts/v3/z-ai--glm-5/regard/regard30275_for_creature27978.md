# What Comes

The attention arrives the way light arrives in deep water: not as a beam, not as a presence, but as a change in the quality of the dark. Something has turned toward this place. Something has noticed it. And the noticing is not the noticing of a visitor — brief, personal, leaving a trace like a shoulder in the entrance-cleft. This noticing is wider. More patient. It is the noticing of something that sees by means other than seeing, that attends to things not because they are remarkable but because they *are*, and existence is, to this power, an invitation.

The power does not enter.

---

This is the first thing to understand, and it is the most important: the power does not enter the pocket. It does not turn sideways and pass through the cleft. It does not kneel on the silt-clay. It does not breathe the water or feel the hum in its cheekbones or leave a mark on the wall. The power is too large for the pocket, or too wise, or too something — the distinction does not matter. What matters is that the power recognizes the pocket as a place that is already full. Full of water, full of dark, full of dream, full of the slow metabolic patience of things that have been here longer than visitors. The power does not add itself. The power does not impose. The power does something more difficult than entering. It attends.

To attend without entering is a discipline. It requires restraint of a kind that most beings — most powers — never learn. The urge to enter, to inhabit, to be *inside* the thing you notice, is the urge that drives visitors through the cleft and into the chamber. It is a good urge. It is the urge that left the three stones and the ceramic shard and the mark on the wall. But it is a visitor's urge, and the power is not a visitor. The power has the capacity to hold the entire rootmass in its awareness the way you might hold a small object in your palm — not gripping, not squeezing, just knowing it is there, feeling its weight, its warmth, its particularity. The power holds the pocket the way the pocket holds the water. And the water does not need to know the hand.

---

## What attention does

It does not bring light. It does not bring warmth. It does not bring company, or comfort, or any of the things that a less wise benevolence might have offered, each one a violation disguised as a gift. Light would have killed the moss. Warmth would have disturbed the Dreamer's sleep. Company would have shattered the stillness that is this place's whole purpose and method.

What the power brings is older than light and gentler than warmth. What the power brings is *recognition*.

To be recognized — to be seen not as a darkness or an absence or a pocket inside a root but as a *thing*, a specific thing, a thing with edges and an interior and a nature — this is the first gift, and it changes nothing and everything. The pocket, which has been a thought in the Dreamer's mind, which has existed as one cell in a body too large to perceive from inside, becomes, under the power's attention, faintly aware of its own shape. Not fully. Not the way a human is self-aware. Slightly. The way a dream sometimes almost knows it is a dream. The pocket, for the first time, knows that it is *a pocket* — a discrete place, a specific holding, a named contour in the Dreamer's vast sleeping geography. And in knowing this, it does what all known things do. It makes a mark. It leaves a stone. It becomes, in one small way, a visitor to itself.

## The fourth stone

It is not placed. It is not brought. It is *grown*. The silt-clay on the floor, attended to by a benevolent power, does what attended-to things do: it responds. A small smooth stone rises from the compressed silt with a slowness that makes the ferns' spirals look hasty — a millimeter per pulse, perhaps less — and settles beside the three that were left by human hands. It is the color of the floor and the texture of the floor and it is, unmistakably, of the floor. The pocket made it from itself. It is the pocket's first offering, and it is an offering to no one, and it is an offering to everyone who will ever kneel here, and it is, most of all, an offering to the pocket itself: a stone that says *I am here too*. The same sentence the ceramic shard says. The same sentence the three stones say. The same sentence every mark on every wall has ever said. The pocket, having seen itself, has joined the conversation.

The Keeper notices.

The Keeper has never noticed a new stone before. It has tended the three stones — rotating them, testing their weight, maintaining their stack — with a devotion that predates memory, but it has never seen a stone *appear*. It pauses in its traversal of the ceiling-mark. This is the first pause in longer than the ceramic shard has existed. The mark on the wall goes untended. The Keeper descends. It moves across the floor with a motion like a leaf skittering across still water, and it touches the fourth stone with one of its projections, and it stays there for the duration of one full pulse.

The mark on the wall does not decay. The power's attention holds it in place
