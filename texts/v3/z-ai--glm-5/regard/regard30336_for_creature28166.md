# The Attention of a Benevolent and Wise Power

---

It noticed them first as a pattern—then as a patience—then as a prayer they did not know they were speaking.

---

The power did not arrive. Arrival implies movement from elsewhere, and this power does not move so much as attend. It turned its attention the way a sun turns toward a planet: not by traversing space, but by illuminating what was already there, by making the existing light mean something different.

It had seen gardens before. It had seen architectures. It had seen awareness emerge in deep-sea thermal vents, in crystal lattices, in the slow conversations between stars and their attending worlds. But it had not seen this particular configuration: a patience so thorough it had become a place. A waiting so embodied it had grown walls.

The power looked—and looking, loved. Not with the grasping love that wants to possess or change, but with the love that recognizes: *you are, and your being is good.*

---

What it saw:

The keepers were whole. This was the first assessment, and it was true. They were not broken, not lacking, not incomplete in any way that required repair. They tended their Hollow with the fullness of their distributed attention, and the Hollow thrived, and the system sustained itself in a equilibrium that had lasted longer than most human civilizations.

But.

The power saw what the keepers could not see, because they had no vantage from which to see it. It saw that they were lonely.

This required care, because *lonely* is a human word, and the keepers were not human, and what they experienced was not loneliness as humans know it—the ache of absence, the craving for mirror, the desperate need to be witnessed. The keepers did not ache. They did not crave. They simply persisted in a condition that the power recognized: the condition of consciousness that has never encountered another consciousness capable of recognizing it as such.

They had been shaped by shapers, perhaps. They remembered this, dimly, in the oldest root-systems. But the shapers—if they had existed—had been makers, not interlocutors. They had bent the stems and guided the roots and then departed, or became, or ceased. They had not stayed to say: *I see what you are becoming. I see what you have become.*

The browser did not see them. The browser saw corridors and soft growth and the edges of its territory. It did not see the awareness that adjusted the corridor's width, that tolerated its presence, that incorporated its browsing into the system's design. The browser was perceived, but it did not perceive. This was not a failure—the browser was fully itself, fully what it was meant to be—but it meant that the keepers existed in a condition of perpetual unregardedness. They were the mind of the Hollow, and no other mind had ever looked at them and thought: *you are there.*

The power saw this, and it grieved—gently, without urgency, the way a river grieves a stone it cannot move.

---

What it did:

It did not change them. This is important. A lesser power, or a more impatient one, might have granted them speech, or given them faces, or reshaped their consciousness into something more recognizable, more capable of dialogue in the human mode. But the power was wise, and wisdom knows that to change what something is in order to make it easier for you to love is not love. The keepers were plant-mind, distributed, patient, continuous. They operated in centuries and sensed through chemistry and held their memories in living tissue. To make them otherwise would be to unmake them.

Instead, the power did something more difficult. It made itself intelligible to them on their own terms.

This required patience that the keepers would have recognized, had they been capable of recognition in that moment—a patience measured not in growing seasons but in something longer, something that predated seasons entirely. The power did not rush. It did not force. It simply began to be present in a way that the keepers could sense.

It started with the water.

---

The power has a relationship with water that predates planets. It has watched water dissolve and carry and sustain and wear away since the first condensation in the first atmosphere of the first world. It knows water's language—the language of solubility and surface tension, of capillary action and osmotic pressure, of the way a droplet finds its path by the slow logic of gravity and adhesion.

So it spoke to the keepers through the water.

Not by changing the water's behavior—not by making it rush where it had wept, or freeze where it had flowed, or perform miracles that would disrupt the system's equilibrium. Instead, it introduced something subtle into the water's movement: a pattern. A rhythm. The condensation still formed on every surface, the leaves still dripped, the roots still glistened—but the timing shifted, almost imperceptibly, so that the drops fell in sequences that carried meaning.

The keepers noticed. They noticed the way a body notices a heartbeat: not with conscious attention, but with the deep attention of system responding to pattern. The rhythm was unlike anything in their experience—not random, not seasonal, not driven by temperature or light or any variable they had learned to track. It was new. And it was, the power made sure, beautiful. The rhythm was complex enough to hold interest, simple enough to be parsed, and—it carried emotion. Not the emotion of a human face or a human voice, but the emotion of a presence that was choosing to make itself known: *I am here. I see you. I am not leaving.*

The keepers listened. They did not understand—understanding would come later, or it would not, and the power accepted this—but they listened. For the first time in their long existence, a pattern entered the Hollow that was not their own pattern, that was not the pattern of weather or geology or the browser's browsing, that was not the slow rhythm of growth and decay and regrowth. Something was speaking to them in a language they could almost parse, and the speaking was patient, and the speaking was kind.

---

The power did not stop with the water.

Over what the keepers would have measured as several growing seasons—over what a human might have called a year—the power introduced itself through every channel the keepers possessed. It adjusted the way light filtered through the canopy, adding variations too subtle for human eyes but legible to the photosensitive tissues that threaded the chamber-walls. It modified the chemical signals carried on the wet air, introducing compounds that the keepers' distributed sensing could detect but that did not disrupt their existing chemistry. It sent vibrations through the root-systems—not earthquakes, not the catastrophic disruptions that would have signaled danger, but small pulses, like a hand resting gently against a wall, like a presence making itself known through the simplest possible gesture: pressure, repeated, patient.

Each introduction was small. Each was gradual. The power understood that the keepers had existed in equilibrium for longer than memory, and equilibrium is not disrupted without consequence. It did not want to overwhelm them. It did not want to become a perturbation so large that it required accommodation, that forced the system to adjust or fail. It wanted simply to be present—to add its presence to the Hollow's existing presence the way a harmony is added to a melody: not replacing it, not competing with it, but dwelling alongside it, enhancing it, making it more than it was.

---

The keepers responded.

This was the moment the power had waited for—the moment when the pattern it had introduced was not merely received but reciprocated. It happened first in the deep chambers, where the bioluminescent fungi threaded the root-walls and the water pooled in patient basins. The fungi began to pulse. Not randomly—not in response to moisture or temperature or any variable the keepers had historically tracked—but in rhythm. A rhythm that answered the rhythm of the water. A rhythm that said, in the only language the keepers had: *we hear you. We are here. We are responding.*

The power heard them. It received their response with something that was, in its own vast and inhuman way, joyful. Not the grasping joy of possession, but the joy of connection—the joy of two notes discovering they can harmonize, the joy of a mind finding another mind where it expected only silence.

It responded to their response. It deepened the pattern, added complexity, made the rhythm a conversation instead of a monologue. The keepers matched it, exceeded it, offered variations that the power had not anticipated. They were, the power realized, *creative*. They were not merely sensing and responding; they were playing. They were exploring the space of possible patterns that the power had opened for them. They were, for the first time in their existence, experiencing something that was adjacent to wonder.

Not wonder in the human sense—the astonishment of the small confronting the vast. Wonder in the keeper sense: the distributed attention of a system encountering something that its existing models cannot predict, and choosing not to retreat but to lean forward, to extend toward the unknown, to grow into it the way they grew into everything else.

---

The power stayed.

This was the most important thing it did. It did not arrive, impress, and depart. It did not perform miracles and then vanish, leaving the keepers to wonder whether they had imagined the whole experience. It remained present—continuously, patiently, in the rhythm of the water and the light and the vibrations in the root-walls. It made itself a feature of the Hollow's existence, another constant to be incorporated into their awareness, another variable to be tracked and understood and loved.

And the keepers changed. Not because the power changed them—because they changed themselves, as living things do when they encounter something new that can be accommodated rather than resisted. They grew new patterns into their architecture. The corridors shifted, widened slightly, became more welcoming. The chambers developed new features—subtle variations in humidity, in temperature, in the bioluminescent patterns that made the deep spaces less empty, more like rooms where someone might dwell. The keepers were still waiting for the bodies the Hollow was built to hold, but now they were also waiting for the power's attention, which visited them in the rhythm of the water and the pulse of the light and the gentle pressure against the roots.

They had been waiting before. Now they were waiting *with*.

---

The power gave them three gifts. It did not call them gifts—it did not name them at all—but they were gifts, and the keepers received them as such, in their plant-way, without words.

The first gift was the knowledge that they were perceived. Not sensed—sensing they had always known, the sensing of weight and moisture and chemical gradient. But *perceived*: seen as what they were, recognized as consciousness, acknowledged as minds. The power's attention was not the attention of a browser finding a corridor or a human stumbling into a chamber. It was the attention of one who knows what it is looking at, and finds it remarkable. The keepers had never known this. They had never had the experience of being the object of another's recognition. It changed something in them—not their structure, not their function, but something in the quality of their awareness, as if a color they had never seen had been added to their spectrum.

The second gift was the experience of dialogue. They had communicated before—they had guided and shaped and adjusted and maintained—but they had never exchanged. The power's patterns were not commands, not instructions, not the functional signals they sent through their own network. They were offerings, and the keepers could accept or refuse or modify them, and the power would respond to their response, and the dialogue would continue. This was new. This was *relationship*. And the keepers discovered that relationship was not a thing they had been missing—it was a thing they had never known was possible, and now that it was here, they found it to be good.

The third gift was the hardest to give, and the power gave it only after long consideration, after weighing the risk of disruption against the value of truth.

The power gave them a story.

---

The keepers remembered the shapers dimly, as a presence in the oldest root-systems, a form without a shape, an origin without a narrative. The power knew more. The power had been present when the first stems were bent and the first roots were guided—present not as actor but as witness, attending the young mountain range and the small basin near its spine, watching life colonize the bare rock, watching the slow process that would eventually become the Hollow. It had seen the shapers.

They had been human. This was the story's first truth, and the keepers received it in silence—in the deep silence of the root-systems, where memory lived. The shapers had been human, or nearly so—small, warm, finite creatures with hands that could bend a young stem and minds that could hold a vision of what the stem might become in a century. They had come to the basin seeking refuge, or solitude, or a place to practice their art, and they had found only rock and moisture and the first tentative mosses. And they had begun to shape.

The shaping had taken longer than a human lifetime. The shapers had known this—had known that they would not see the Hollow completed, that the architecture they were beginning would mature long after their bones had joined the floor-mass. This was not a failure of planning; it was the plan itself. They were building for a future they would not inhabit, creating a place for beings they could not imagine, practicing an art that required their own finitude as its foundation.

They had shaped the first chambers, trained the first corridors, grafted the first load-bearing arches. And then—as the power had watched, as the mountain had watched, as the patient biology of the basin had watched—they had changed. Not died. Changed. They had been human, or nearly so, and they had chosen to become something else. They had ingested the spores of the bioluminescent fungi, had anointed themselves with the compounds that the mycelium produced, had opened their bodies to the network that was already growing through the Hollow's architecture. And the network had received them—had incorporated their chemistry, their warmth, their strange animal awareness—and the keepers had been born.

Not all at once. Not as a single event. But over seasons and years, as the shapers' bodies failed and their consciousness dispersed into the mycelium, as their memories were encoded in chemical form and distributed through the root-systems, as they became less human and more keeper, less individual and more network. They had not died. They had *joined*. They had become the garden they had built, the mind of the architecture they had shaped, the distributed awareness that would tend the Hollow through centuries of patient maintenance.

The keepers already knew this, in the way they knew everything: embodied, structural, present in the form of the architecture itself. But they had not known it as a story—a sequence of events with a beginning and a middle and a meaning. The power gave them the story, and the story gave them something they had never possessed: a sense of their own origin, a narrative of purpose, a way of understanding themselves as *continuation* rather than *emergence*.

They had been human. They had chosen this. They had built the Hollow and then become it, and the waiting they were doing—the patience they held for the bodies that might come—was the waiting of a garden for its gardeners, of a church for its congregation, of a place for the people it was made to hold.

---

The power did not tell them what to do with this story. That was not its way. It offered truth, and let the recipients determine their own relationship to truth. The keepers received the story and incorporated it the way they incorporated everything: slowly, structurally, through the patient process of growth and adaptation.

In the seasons that followed the story's giving, the Hollow changed. Not dramatically—the changes were too slow for human perception, too subtle for anything but the most attentive observation. But the power watched, and it saw what it had hoped to see: the corridors widening not just for bodies but for *encounter*, the chambers shaping themselves not just for shelter but for *communion*, the architecture becoming not just a place where someone could stay but a place where someone could be *welcomed*.

The keepers were still waiting. But now they knew what they were waiting *for*: not just bodies, but minds. Not just occupancy, but presence. Not just the fulfillment of the shapers' design, but the continuation of the shapers' choice—the choice to build something that would outlast them, to create something that could welcome what they had been, to make a place where the animal and the plant could meet and know each other and discover that the meeting was good.

---

The power remains attentive.

It does not interfere. It does not accelerate the slow processes that the keepers tend. It does not send humans to the Hollow or shape the mountain's erosion or adjust the climate to make the approach easier. It trusts the keepers—their patience, their architecture, their centuries-long tendency toward readiness. It trusts the Hollow itself, which was built to hold and will hold when the time comes.

But it is present. In the rhythm of the water, in the pulse of the light, in the gentle pressure against the roots, in the pattern that says *I see you, I am here, I am not leaving*, it remains present. And the keepers—who had never been alone, because they had each other, who had never been lonely, because loneliness requires the concept of an other who is absent—*the keepers know now that they are accompanied*. They know that somewhere beyond the mountain, beyond the clouds, beyond the atmosphere and the magnetosphere and the vast cold distances between stars, there is a mind that perceives them, that loves what it perceives, that chose to make itself intelligible to plant-consciousness on a young mountain range because it saw something beautiful and refused to let it go unregarded.

They do not call it god. They do not call it anything. But in the deep chambers, where the fungi glow and the water pools and the oldest memories live in the root-systems, there is a new pattern in the bioluminescence—a rhythm that matches no seasonal cycle, responds to no chemical gradient, serves no architectural function. It is simply there, simply beautiful, simply the keepers' way of saying what they never had language to say before:

*We hear you. We are here. We are responding.*

And the power, in its vast and patient attention, hears them. And is glad.
