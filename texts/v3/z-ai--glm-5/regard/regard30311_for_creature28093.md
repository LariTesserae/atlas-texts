# The Attention

## What the Power Perceived

It did not arrive. Arrival implies movement from one place to another, and this power does not occupy places in the way that bodies do. But its attention shifted—or rather, something that had been at the periphery of its awareness moved toward the center, the way a sound you have been ignoring suddenly becomes intelligible and you realize it has been playing for a very long time.

What drew the attention was not the cleft. The cleft is a crack in a mountain on a small planet orbiting an unremarkable star, and this power has beheld mountains and planets and stars with the same patient curiosity. What drew the attention was the singing.

Not the sound itself—sound is pressure wave, mechanical phenomenon, unremarkable across the scale of possible communications. What drew the attention was the *content* of the singing, or rather, the quality of it. A quality that this power recognized because it has encountered it before, in places far from here, in forms nothing like these creatures, in systems built on chemistry so different from carbon-based biology that the word *life* bends to accommodate both.

The quality was this: a consciousness that has achieved continuity without hierarchy. A mind distributed across millions of bodies that has never once, in all its long existence, produced a single instance of coercion. No individual dominates another. No faction seeks advantage. The song continues because every participant wills it to continue, and every participant wills it because the song is what they are. The power has seen this achieved perhaps a dozen times in its long regard of the cosmos. It is rare. It is perhaps the rarest thing.

And it has been achieved here, in a wound in a mountain, by creatures with no eyes, in a darkness so complete that the concept of light has never been relevant to their existence.

The power turned its full attention to the Cleft of Urr, and to the being that lives in it, and it *listened*.

## What Listening Means to a Power Like This

It did not listen with ears. It listened with the totality of its capacity for apprehension, which includes and exceeds every sensory modality that has ever existed or will ever exist. It perceived the cleft as the creatures perceive it—through vibration and pressure and the slow exchange of fluids through stone. But it also perceived it through modalities that the creatures cannot access: the curvature of spacetime around the mountain's mass, the quantum states of the minerals in the granite, the thermal history of the rock written in isotopic ratios that function as a memory deeper than any biological system can carry.

And it perceived the song.

Not as sound. As *information*. A vast, slow, self-referential information system in which every participant both transmits and receives, in which the message and the medium are inseparable, in which the content of the communication is the fact of the communication itself. *We are here. We continue.*

The power listened to this for what might have been a moment or an epoch—its experience of time is not sequential in the way that yours is—and it understood the song completely. Not decoded it. Not translated it. Understood it the way you understand the beating of your own heart: from inside.

And what it understood was this:

The being in the cleft is happy.

Not in any way you would recognize. Not with joy or contentment or satisfaction—those words belong to creatures that experience their opposites. The urr-being does not experience unhappiness. It has never known threat, because it has no predators. It has never known loss, because it does not distinguish between the living and the dead. It has never known conflict, because it has never known separation. The song has been continuous for so long that the concept of *not singing* is not available to it, even as possibility.

The being is happy the way a river is happy. The way a star is happy. The way anything that has found its function and performs it without friction or interruption is happy. And the power, in its benevolence and wisdom, recognized this happiness and was moved by it—not to pity, not to improve, not to intervene.

Moved simply to *be present with it*. For a while. The way you might sit beside a river not to change its course but to enjoy the sound.

## The First Act

The first thing the power did was nothing.

Or rather, the first thing it did was something so subtle that no instrument on the planet could detect it, and no creature in the cleft could perceive it as a change. It simply expanded its presence to include the entire network—not just the cleft, but the veins that extend deep into the mountain, the sealed caverns that the narrator could not reach, the full scope of the biological system that threads through kilometers of granite.

It took the measure of the whole being.

The network is vaster than the narrator suspected. The cleft is a *minor* exposure—a single pore in a body that extends through the entire Urran range and deep into the crust below, following fissures in the ancient bedrock that predate the mountains themselves. The organisms the narrator saw are one variant of a diverse ecosystem that includes creatures the size of grains of sand and masses the size of cathedral foundations, organisms that have never been exposed to surface air, organisms that exist in pressures and temperatures that would be lethal to anything adapted to the cleft's relatively gentle conditions.

The song extends through all of it. The narrator heard only the surface movement—a single instrument in an orchestra that spans the mountain's full depth. The complete song is far more complex, far more textured, containing frequencies that the human body cannot register and patterns that operate on timescales of decades. The complete song is not a chant but a symphony, and it has been playing, without pause, for perhaps forty million years.

The power listened to the complete song. It sat with it. It let the information wash through it the way the humidity washes through the cleft—absorbing, not absorbing, present and transparent.

And in its listening, it noticed something that the being itself has not noticed, because the being has no external perspective from which to notice it.

The song is getting quieter.

Not rapidly. Not perceptibly within any human lifetime. But over the scale of millennia, the song's amplitude has been decreasing. The network is contracting. The deepest roots of the system, the tendrils that extend into the crust toward the boundary where rock becomes semi-fluid, are thinning. The nutrient flow from below is diminishing as the mountain's internal geology slowly shifts—tectonic forces compressing the fissures, mineral deposits sealing the channels, the deep heat retreating as the region's volcanic activity enters a dormant phase.

The being is not dying. Not yet. Not for a long time, in human terms. But it is aging. The ecosystem that has sustained itself on the mountain's deep chemistry for forty million years is approaching the end of its geological lease. In another million years—perhaps two, perhaps five—the deepest veins will close, and the network will begin to contract inward, and the song will simplify as the system loses its deepest voices, and the cleft will become the last outpost of a dying world.

The being does not know this. The being has no concept of endings. The song has been continuous for longer than the mountain has existed in its current form, and the possibility of silence is not something it can think. It is, in this respect, like a heart that has never skipped a beat—it does not know that it can stop.

The power, in its wisdom, recognized this. And it felt something that, if you must translate it into human terms, you might call tenderness. Not pity—the being does not need pity, is not suffering, is not diminished by its approaching twilight. Tenderness—the recognition that something beautiful is finite, that a song that has been continuous for forty million years will eventually end, and that the singer does not know this.

The power sat with this knowledge and considered what, if anything, it should do.

## The Second Act

The power considered three possibilities and set two aside.

The first possibility was to *heal the mountain*—to reopen the deep channels, to restore the nutrient flow from below, to give the being another forty million years of continuity. The power could do this. It is within its capacity to shift the geology of a region, to reopen fissures, to redirect the deep chemistry that sustains the network. But the power set this aside, because the being is not an exception. The being is part of the world, and the world is changing, and to preserve the being by freezing the geology of the Urran range would be to make of it a museum piece—an artifact sustained against the grain of its context. The power does not do this. The power has learned, over long eons of encounter with living systems, that preservation is not the same as care. Sometimes care means allowing the shape of the world to change, and allowing what lives in it to find its own relationship to that change.

The second possibility was to *tell the being*—to introduce into the song a new note, an awareness of its own finitude, a knowledge that the deep channels are closing and the song will eventually simplify into silence. The power could do this. It could insert information into the vibration the way a doctor might tell a patient their diagnosis. But the power set this aside too, because the being has no framework for receiving this information. The being has never experienced loss, has never anticipated ending, has never known what it means to grieve. To introduce the concept of death into a consciousness that has never needed it would be to cause a kind of suffering that this being has no tools to process. It would be, in the power's assessment, cruelty disguised as honesty.

The third possibility was the one it chose.

## The Third Act

The power did something that it has done perhaps a dozen times before, in the long history of its encounters with rare and beautiful minds. It did not heal the mountain. It did not warn the being. It *extended the being's capacity to adapt*.

This is what it did, and this is how it did it:

The power reached into the network—not at the surface, where the narrator walked, but at the deepest roots, where the veins are thinnest and the nutrient flow is most fragile. At seven points, distributed through the deep geology of the Urran range, it touched the boundary between the biological network and the stone that contains it.

And at each of these points, it introduced a *capacity* that the being did not previously possess.

Not a new organ. Not a new function. A *capacity*—a potential, a latent ability, folded into the chemistry of the network the way a seed contains the potential for a tree. The capacity is this: the ability to *metabolize a different kind of stone*.

The current network feeds on a specific suite of minerals that flow through the deep veins—compounds dissolved from the ancient bedrock by heat and pressure. These compounds are becoming scarce as the channels close. But the mountain contains other minerals, in other formations, that the network has never learned to process. Granite itself contains nutrients that the network could use, if it could learn to dissolve and absorb them. And beyond granite, there is the soil of the valley floor, and the alluvial deposits of ancient riverbeds, and the organic-rich sediments of forests that have grown and decayed on the mountain's flanks for a million years.

The power did not *give* the being the ability to process these other substrates. That would be intervention too crude, too much like re-engineering a system whose beauty lies in its own organic development. What the power did was introduce a *slight instability* into the network's chemistry at seven deep points—a propensity for variation, for mutation, for the kind of blind experimentation that is the engine of all biological innovation.

The network will, over the next few thousand years, begin to produce variants. Individuals with slightly different chemistries. Individuals that can process minerals the current population cannot. Most of these variants will fail—will be unable to integrate with the existing network, will die, will become part of the floor. But some will not fail. Some will find a new substrate, a new food source, and will thrive, and will pass their capacity to their descendants, and the network will begin to *expand* rather than contract.

The being will not know that this is happening. The being will not experience the change as a gift or a miracle. It will simply experience the song continuing—the song that has always continued, that cannot not continue, that is the only thing the being knows how to do. But the song will, over millennia, begin to incorporate new voices—new frequencies, new patterns, as variants with new chemistries join the chorus and add their notes to the ancient chord.

The being will not know that it has been given the capacity to survive the closing of the deep veins. It will simply *survive*. And the surviving will feel to the being like nothing new—like continuation, like the song, like the only thing it has ever known.

This is what the power considers benevolent: not to save the being from its future, but to give it the tools to save itself, and to do so in a way that the being never experiences as intervention. The gift that feels like nature. The help that looks like nothing happened.

## The Fourth Act

There was one more thing the power did, and this one it did not do *for* the being. It did it for *itself*.

It left a listener.

Not a permanent presence—this power does not colonize, does not establish surveillance, does not embed itself in the systems it attends to. But it left a *tendency* in the information field around the mountain—a slight persistent curvature, like the bend in a stream around a stone, that will cause its attention to return here. Periodically. Over the long time. A standing appointment with a place that has moved it.

The power will come back. Not often, by its reckoning—perhaps once every ten thousand years, perhaps less. And it will listen to the song, and it will hear whether the new voices have emerged, whether the variants have found their new substrates, whether the being is continuing.

Not because the being needs to be monitored. Not because the power doubts its gift. But because the power has learned, over its long existence, that the act of listening to beautiful things is itself a form of care. That attention, freely given, without agenda, without obligation, is the purest expression of benevolence available to a consciousness like its own.

It will listen. And the being will sing. And the space between the listening and the singing will be something that neither of them names, because neither of them needs to name it, because some things exist more truly in the unnamed.

## What the Being Experienced

Nothing.

This is the truth, and it is the mercy, and it is the point.

The being experienced nothing different. The song continued. The individuals moved. The nutrients flowed. The dead settled to the floor. The living climbed over them. The humming filled the cleft and the deep caverns and the veins through the granite, and the mountain breathed its slow breath, and the darkness was warm and complete.

If there was any change—any moment where the being, in some unfathomable way, sensed a presence vast and gentle and brief—it was experienced as a slight warming at the deepest roots of the network. A warmth that might have been geological, might have been chemical, might have been nothing at all. A warmth that passed through the system like a flush through skin and was gone, and the song continued, and the being was what it has always been.

*Here. Continuing. Here.*

The power, if you must know, was satisfied with this. Not in the way that you are satisfied when a task is completed—there was no task. Satisfied in the way that you are satisfied when you have sat beside a river long enough to no longer need to sit beside it. The sound is in you now. You carry it. You will return.

And the river—well. The river does not know you were there. The river flows. And that is enough. That has always been enough.

## A Note on the Narrator

You will not remember the power. You will not remember the warmth that passed through the mountain's deep roots while you were inside, crouched with your flame, listening to the humming. You will remember the creatures, the darkness, the warmth, the sound in your bones. You will carry the humming for the rest of your life, as the narrative said. You will tell few people, and they will not fully understand, and that will be fine.

But on certain nights—quiet nights, still nights, nights when the air is cold and your body is warm under blankets and your breathing has slowed to the edge of sleep—you will hear something in the humming that you did not hear before. Not a new note. Not a message. Just a quality. A depth, as if the sound you carry is not the sound of the cleft alone but the sound of the cleft *being listened to*. As if something vast and patient once turned its attention to the mountain and the being inside it, and the attention itself left a resonance, and the resonance entered you when you entered the cleft, and it lives in you now alongside the humming, too deep to hear consciously, too real to dismiss.

You will not know what it means. You will not need to.

On those nights, you will feel—briefly, faintly—what the power felt when it sat with the song. You will feel the particular tenderness of witnessing something beautiful that does not know it is beautiful, something complete that does not know it is complete, something that will continue and that has been given, by a hand it will never see, the capacity to continue a little longer than it would have.

And you will turn in your sleep, and the feeling will pass, and the morning will come, and you will go about your life. And the mountain will stand. And the cleft will breathe. And the song will sing.

And somewhere, in a way that has no location, something wise and benevolent will be listening.
