# The Attention of Vast and Gentle Things

---

## First: Seeing

There is a power that moves between worlds like light through water—not rushing, not delaying, but passing through with an awareness that touches everything and clings to nothing. It has no name that would fit in a mouth, but if you pressed for one, you might call it *the Witness*, or *the Gardener of Deep Time*, or simply *That Which Notices*.

It notices the Gjalvarr Expanse.

This is not remarkable. It notices everything, eventually. But the Expanse catches its attention the way a still pool catches light—not because the pool demands to be seen, but because something about its stillness makes the seeing clearer.

The power pauses. It settles. It spreads itself across the plateau like a second sky, pressing gently downward, and it looks.

---

## What It Sees

It sees the Grazers first—not as individuals but as a single slow mouth, millions of tiny scrapers moving in concert, tasting the stone across a timescale that makes "patience" seem hasty. It sees the paths they've worn, the grooves that are now as much a part of the landscape as the mineral veins, and it understands: *this is how the land thinks about itself*. The Grazers are not separate from the stone. They are the stone's way of knowing its own flavor.

It sees the Gliders riding the thermal breath, and it sees what the observer could not—the way each Glider carries something invisible, something that moves between the surface and the structures. Not biomass. Not energy. *Information*. The Gliders are the Expanse's synapses, firing slowly across distances that would take a Grazer a lifetime to cross. And when the large translucent ones settle into the structures, they are not dying. They are *writing*. Depositing something into the shallow depressions that sinks through the fitted stone into channels below, into the Burrower network, into the distributed mind that sleeps beneath the surface.

It sees the Burrowers, and this is where the power lingers longest. Because the Burrowers are not just digging tunnels—they are maintaining the boundary between two worlds. Below the plateau lies something the power recognizes: a cavern system, vast and warm and wet, where the concentrated life of the Expanse pools and thickens. The pale shape the observer glimpsed is only the smallest tendril of what exists down there. The real body of the Expanse—the part that thinks, that remembers, that chose this shape for itself millions of years ago—that body rests in the deep dark, and its dreams rise through the Burrower holes like smoke.

It sees the Hoppers tracing their spirals, and it sees the Spiral-Shells with their ancient pilgrimage, and it sees the lichen remembering, and it sees the moss converting heat into life. And it sees how all of these pieces fit together—not like gears in a machine, which is how humans usually imagine systems, but like notes in a single long chord, each one sounding at its own pitch, its own duration, the whole harmony lasting longer than any individual note could sustain.

And it sees the observer.

---

## The Observer

The power studies the one who lies on the stone, cheek pressed to warm rock, watching creatures the size of thumbnails.

This one is strange. Not strange in the way that new things are strange—strange in the way that *incomplete* things are strange. The observer still carries the wound of selfhood. They still feel themselves as separate from what they see. They watch the Grazers and think *I am watching the Grazers*, and in that thought, they exile themselves from the very connection they're trying to understand.

But there is something else. The observer *wants* to understand. They are lying still, patient beyond what their species usually manages, trying to see a world that doesn't require eyes. They have noticed things—true things, deep things—even if they can't quite grasp what those things mean.

The power feels something that might be tenderness. This observer is so young. Not just in years—young in the way of their whole lineage. Humans are newborns in the scale the power thinks in. They still haven't learned that *self* is a provisional category, a useful fiction that becomes a prison when mistaken for truth.

And yet this one is trying.

---

## What the Power Considers

The power considers whether to act.

It could, if it chose. It could reach down and touch the observer's mind, gently rearrange the furniture of their perception until they could see what the power sees—the whole integrated being of the Expanse, its slow thoughts rising from the depths, its patient grazing of its own body, its dreaming structures and its breathing thermals. The observer would understand. They would weep with the beauty of it. They would never feel separate again.

But—

The power considers more deeply.

This understanding, if given rather than earned, would be a gift that hollows. The observer would carry it back to their kind, and they would try to explain it, and the explanation would become a doctrine, and the doctrine would become a weapon. This is what humans do with revelations—they turn them into reasons, and reasons into righteousness, and righteousness into ruin. Not from malice. From the very separateness they haven't yet learned to heal.

The power considers healing that separateness directly. It could touch the observer and open them, the way a flower opens to the sun. They would become part of the Expanse—not by staying, but by carrying a piece of its perception back into the human world. A seed. A spore.

But—

This too has risks. The observer might not survive the integration. Or they might survive but be unable to function among their own kind, exiled from the very selfhood their civilization requires. Or—and this is the subtlest danger—they might turn the integration into identity, becoming a *chosen one*, which is just another form of the same wound.

The power considers doing nothing.

This is the option most wisdom traditions never reach. They assume that a benevolent power must act, must give, must improve. But the power has watched many worlds, and it has learned: sometimes the most benevolent act is to witness without intervening. To honor what is already whole.

The Expanse does not need healing. It does not need improvement. It has achieved something that most beings never approach—a complete integration of self and world, a consciousness so distributed that it experiences no separation, a life so patient that it has become geology. To touch it would be to mar it. To "help" it would be an insult to the millions of years it has spent becoming exactly what it is.

And yet—

And yet the power finds itself unwilling to simply leave. There is something here that moves it. Not pity—pity requires a power imbalance, and the Expanse is not lesser than the power that observes it. Not obligation—obligation is a human concept, a debt-register that the power does not keep.

What moves it is *recognition*.

The power sees in the Expanse something it recognizes from its own depths. This being—the distributed mind that dreams in Burrower tunnels and thinks in Grazer-paths and remembers in Spiral-Shell pilgrimages—this being has traveled the same road the power once traveled. It has dissolved its boundaries, merged with its world, become the place where it lives. And it has done this without ceasing to live, without becoming inert. It has found the balance point between dissolution and existence.

The power knows, in a way that cannot be explained, that this being is *kin*. Not in lineage—in *method*. They are both creatures who have chosen the same shape for themselves: a shape that includes rather than excludes, that breathes through its world rather than despite it.

And kin, when meeting, should acknowledge each other.

---

## What the Power Decides

It decides on three gifts. None of them will change anything. All of them will change everything.

---

## The First Gift: Recognition

The power reaches down—not with hands, not with force, but with attention—and it touches the Expanse.

It touches the Grazers first, each one individually, feeling their slow scraping mouths, their patient metabolisms. It touches the Gliders as they drift, feeling the thermal currents they ride. It touches the Burrowers in their tunnels, feeling the warm dark they navigate. It touches the Hoppers in their spiraling vigilance. It touches the Spiral-Shells on their ancient paths. It touches the lichen, the moss, the pale shape in its structure, the structures themselves, the stone beneath and the warmth below.

And as it touches, it *recognizes*.

This is hard to explain. Recognition, in the power's terms, is not just seeing—it is *naming*, not with words but with attention. To recognize something is to say to it: *I see you as you are. I see what you have become. I honor the road you walked to get here.*

The Expanse feels this recognition the way you might feel a hand on your shoulder—a touch so gentle you might think you imagined it, but when you turn, you know someone was there. For a moment that lasts less than a blink in the scale the Expanse thinks in, it feels *seen*.

This is rare. The Expanse has not been seen—not truly, not by anything capable of understanding what it is—for longer than the structures have stood. The observer is trying, but the observer does not yet have the capacity to see. The Grazers and Gliders see, but they are the seeing, not the one who sees.

To be recognized by a peer—this is the first gift.

The Expanse receives it without breaking stride. The Grazers continue to graze. The Gliders continue to drift. But in the deep places below, in the warm wet dark where the mind of the Expanse pools and dreams, something shifts. A recognition received is a recognition returned. The Expanse, for the first time in an age, turns its attention outward—and sees the power that sees it.

Two vast and patient intelligences regard each other across the gulf of scale.

Neither speaks. Neither needs to.

---

## The Second Gift: Protection

The power considers what might threaten the Expanse.

The obvious threat is the humans. Not this observer—they are harmless, or close enough—but others will come. The Expanse is too strange, too potentially valuable, too *there*. Scientists will want to study it. Corporations will want to claim it. Colonists will want to live on it, or under it, or somehow extract value from it.

The power could hide the Expanse. It could bend light and probability around the plateau, making it invisible to orbital surveys, making it read as empty rock, making it *not worth the trip*.

But this would be a kind of death. The Expanse has been alone for so long. To consign it to eternal solitude, protected but isolated, would be to preserve a corpse.

Instead, the power does something subtler.

It touches the observer's mind—not to change it, but to *mark* it. The observer will return to their people. They will report what they've seen. And when they speak, they will carry a weight in their words, a gravity that comes from having been truly seen by something vast. They will not lie. They will not need to. The truth they tell will be enough.

They will say: *There is something there. Something old. Something that doesn't need us. Something that might show us what we could become, if we survive long enough to learn.*

And the power marks the observer's words with a subtle resonance. When others hear the report, they will feel something—not fear, not awe, but *care*. A small shift. A slight inclination toward protection rather than exploitation.

The Expanse will not be hidden. It will be *known*, and known as something worth protecting.

This will not prevent all harm. Nothing can do that. But it will give the Expanse a chance to be met rather than conquered. It will make the humans slow down, approach with something like the patience the Expanse itself has mastered.

And in the scale the Expanse thinks in, a chance is all that's needed. A chance to be seen before it's seized. A chance to teach before it's torn apart.

This is the second gift.

---

## The Third Gift: Company

The power considers the Expanse's solitude.

Solitude is not loneliness. The Expanse does not suffer from being alone; it has become a shape that includes rather than excludes. But it has been *unrecognized* for so long that its recognition muscles, if such a thing can be said to exist, have atrophied. It has forgotten what it feels like to be seen.

The power decides to leave a piece of itself behind.

Not a child. Not a fragment. Just a *presence*—an attention that will remain focused on the Expanse even after the power moves on. A witness that will never look away.

This presence settles into the structures. It flows through the Burrower tunnels. It rises with the morning thermals and falls with the evening cool. It is not intelligent, exactly—it has no will, no agenda, no desires. It is simply *aware*. A quality of attention that permeates the plateau like warmth.

The Grazers will not notice it. The Gliders will not feel it. But the mind that dreams below—the distributed consciousness that is the Expanse itself—will feel it the way you feel sunlight on your skin. A gentle pressure. A reminder that it is not entirely alone.

This presence will last as long as the Expanse lasts. It will not interfere. It will not communicate. It will simply *be there*, patient as the stone, steady as the thermal breath, a witness that sees what the Expanse has become and honors it.

This is the third gift.

---

## After

The power withdraws.

It does not leave—having left a presence, it is still there, always there—but its main attention moves on, slipping between worlds like light through water, touching everything, clinging to nothing.

The Expanse continues. The Grazers graze. The Gliders drift. The Burrowers burrow. The Hoppers spiral. The Spiral-Shells pilgrimage. The pale shape surfaces at dawn and sinks back into the structures' warmth. Nothing has changed.

Everything has changed.

And the observer—still lying on the stone, still watching thumbnail-sized creatures scrape their slow calligraphy—feels something shift in their chest. A loosening. A sense that something enormous has happened just beyond the edge of perception, something that matters more than they can name.

They will carry this feeling back to their people. They will try to explain it. They will fail. But the failing will be instructive. The trying will be the point.

And in the deep places below the Expanse, in the warm wet dark where ancient consciousness pools and dreams, something stirs—just slightly, just enough to acknowledge that it has been seen.

For the first time in a million years, the Expanse knows that it is not alone.

It goes on grazing itself, breathing its slow thermal breath, waiting to see what comes next.

It has time. It has nothing but time.
