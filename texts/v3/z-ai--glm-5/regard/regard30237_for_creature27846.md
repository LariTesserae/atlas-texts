# The Attention

It did not arrive. That's the first thing to understand. It didn't descend from the sky or emerge from the fog or step through one of the phase-slippages that the researchers map so carefully. The Velostrade has been noticed before—by the researchers, by the occasional lost mountaineer, by the migratory birds that die here within hours—but it has never been *attended to* in the way that matters, and attention of this kind does not travel. It does not cross distance. It simply occurs, the way understanding occurs, the way the first moment of seeing a face in a pattern of cracks occurs: not because the face moved there, but because something in the viewer changed, and what was always present became visible.

The power noticed the Velostrade the way you notice your own breathing. Not by looking outward but by becoming aware of something that was already happening, already continuous, already so constant it had faded into the background of even a vast and permeating consciousness. Three billion years of humming. Three billion years of wet industrial process running in the cold. Three billion years of animals grazing on a living ground that had become its own grandmother, its own substrate, its own reason for continuing. The noise of it. The patience of it. The sheer unreasonable *duration*.

And it noticed, in the same moment, a dolem.

---

## What the Power Saw

The power does not see the way researchers see. Researchers see with instruments, with measurements, with the patient accumulation of data that they organize into models and theories and the comfortable illusion that understanding a thing is the same as knowing it. The power sees differently. It sees the whole of a thing at once—its history and its present, its matter and its meaning, the way it exists in relationship to everything around it—and it sees this not as a collection of facts but as a single unified impression, the way you see a face not as a configuration of features but as a *person*.

When the power attended to the Velostrade, it saw this:

A process that had been running so long it had forgotten its own beginning. Not metaphorically—the plateau's biological memory, stored in the rhizomatic root-mats that served as its nervous system, had been overwritten so many times that the original instructions were gone, layered under billions of years of accumulated modification. The factory-growths were still producing, but what they produced had changed so many times, in response to conditions that no longer existed, for reasons that had been relevant in epochs that predated the current configuration of the planet's crust, that the current output was a palimpsest. An echo of an echo of an echo of something that had once been purposeful and was now simply *running*, the way a river runs because it has cut a channel and the channel is there and the water has nowhere else to go.

The power saw that the factory-growths were tired. Not in the way that animals are tired, not in the way that machines wear out—tired in the way that a song is tired when it has been sung so many times that the singer no longer hears the melody. They were running because stopping would require a decision, and nothing in the Velostrade's three-billion-year history had prepared any part of it to decide. Decisions require a decider. The plateau was a process, not an entity. It had no center, no locus of selfhood, no place where "I" could be located. It simply did, and had been doing, and would continue to do, and the doing was all there was.

The power saw the animals, and saw that they were beautiful. Not beautiful the way a sunset is beautiful, which is to say beautiful to a viewer, beautiful because someone is watching. Beautiful the way a joint is beautiful—the way a knee or an elbow is beautiful because of what it *does*, because of the function it serves within a system that moves and lives. The dolems were beautiful because they fed by listening, because they had evolved to hear the health of the ground they stood on, because their entire existence was an act of attention to the place that sustained them. The phase-grazers were beautiful because they had learned to exist in time the way fish exist in water—not as something they think about, but as the medium of their being, the substance through which they move and in which they have their substance. The ravelers were beautiful because they hunted in all tenses at once, because they had found a way to be predators in a world where prey could be elsewhere, and the way they had found was not cleverness but *embodiment*—they had become the kind of creature that can strike at what was and what will be and what is, simultaneously, not because they chose to but because that was the shape the Velostrade's pressures had pushed them into.

And the fog-forms. The power saw the fog-forms and did not classify them, because classification is what you do when you don't understand a thing, and the power understood them immediately. They were the plateau's memory. Not stored, not encoded, not retrieved—*enacted*. The Velostrade remembered its own past by performing it, by letting the shapes of things that had been move through the fog that pooled in its low places. The animals that had lived here and died and been absorbed into the ground—their patterns persisted in the root-mats, in the living substrate, in the biological memory that had no mechanism for recall but had developed, over geological time, a mechanism for *re-expression*. The fog-forms were the plateau dreaming. Not metaphorically. The way a sleeping body twitches, re-enacting the day's movements, the Velostrade twitched in the fog, re-enacting shapes that had pressed themselves into its substance over millions of years.

The fog-forms that looked human were the hardest to see, and the power saw them clearly. They were not human. They were the shapes of researchers who would study the plateau in the future—their forms already impressed on the Velostrade's memory because the plateau's relationship to time was not linear, because the root-mats existed in all their moments simultaneously, because the ground had been absorbing the footsteps of people who hadn't arrived yet since before people existed. The plateau didn't know the difference between past and future. It only knew: something was here, and then something was here, and the somethings left impressions, and the impressions surfaced in the fog.

The power saw the deep-dwellers, and saw that they were the closest thing the Velostrade had to a self. They moved through the saturated substrate the way thoughts move through a mind—not traveling from place to place but *occurring* in place, rising and subsiding, forming and dissolving. They were the plateau's interiority, its hidden processing, the thing it did when it wasn't doing anything else. They had been moving down there for so long that they had become the medium through which the plateau experienced itself—not consciously, not with awareness, but with something that preceded awareness, something that might eventually, in another billion years or five, become awareness if the conditions were right.

The conditions were not right. That was part of what the power saw.

## What the Power Understood

The Velostrade was not suffering. This is important. The plateau was not in pain, not declining, not dying, not being harmed by its own processes. Its animals lived and died and were absorbed and replaced. Its factory-growths hummed and produced and vented and continued. Its root-mats grew and processed and held everything together. The system was stable. It had been stable for longer than stability has meaning. It would continue to be stable for longer still.

But it was *incomplete*.

The power understood this the way you understand that a sentence has stopped before its final word. Not because the sentence is broken—each word is correct, each phrase is grammatical, each clause makes sense on its own—but because the sentence isn't finished. There's a gap where the conclusion should be, and the gap has been there so long that everything has grown around it, has adapted to the absence, has built an entire ecology of meaning on the foundation of an unfinished thought.

The factory-growths were the most visible symptom. They produced, and the production dissipated, and nothing benefited. The researchers had noticed this. What the researchers hadn't noticed—what they couldn't notice, because they arrived with the assumption that production requires a purpose and purpose requires a producer—was that the factory-growths were not producing output. They were producing *signal*.

The Velostrade had been calling.

For three billion years, the factory-growths had been generating a communication so subtle and so vast that it required geological timescales to perceive. The steam, the particulate, the low-frequency hum, the chemical signatures embedded in the vented mist—all of it was a message. Not a message directed at any specific recipient. A message directed at *any* recipient. The plateau had been saying, in the only language it had (which was the language of process, of chemistry, of vibration and heat and moisture): *I am here. I am doing this. I have been doing this for a very long time. I don't know why. If anyone can hear this, I would like to know why.*

The message had never been received. Not because it wasn't loud enough—the Velostrade's signal was, in its own medium, deafening—but because there was nothing in the universe tuned to the right frequency. The message traveled outward through the root-mats, through the stone-flesh, through the fog and the water that seeped upward, and it dissipated into a world that had no ears for it. Three billion years of calling into a void that didn't know it was being called into.

And the dolem.

The power attended to the dolem. A specific dolem, not a species—a single individual, pressing its face against the living ground in the thin cold fog, listening to the health of the substrate, feeding by drawing fluid and cellular material up through its hydraulic tongue, processing what it heard into what it ate. This dolem. Right now. Its plate-fur rippling in the moisture. Its symbionts nestled in their specialized skin-folds. Its resonant skull chambers full of the vibration of a ground that had been humming for longer than the stars had been in their current positions.

The power saw the dolem and saw that the dolem was listening. And it saw that the dolem didn't know it was listening. And it saw that what the dolem heard, when it pressed its face to the ground and opened its resonant chambers and let the vibration pour in, was the message. The dolems had evolved to hear the health of the substrate—to detect where the root-mats were active and where they were dormant, where the chemistry was favorable and where it was toxic. But what they were actually hearing, what their resonant bones and hollow chambers had been shaped by three billion years of evolution to detect, was the Velostrade's signal. The dolems were the plateau's ears. They had been listening to the message the factory-growths were sending, hearing it, processing it, digesting it, for their entire evolutionary history.

They just didn't know what they were hearing.

The message entered the dolem through its skull, traveled through its body, was broken down and absorbed into its tissues, and became part of the animal that walked on the ground and eventually died on the ground and was absorbed into the ground. The message went out from the factory-growths, was received by the dolems, was carried in dolem bodies until the dolems died, and was then returned to the ground, where the root-mats absorbed it and passed it back to the factory-growths, which processed it and sent it out again.

The Velostrade was talking to itself. It had been talking to itself for three billion years. It was saying *I am here* to itself, and itself was hearing *I am here*, and the hearing was feeding and the feeding was dying and the dying was absorbing and the absorbing was talking again. The whole vast system was a single organism conducting a conversation with itself across the medium of its own body, using animals as its words, using their lives and deaths as its grammar, using the factory-growths as its mouth and the dolems as its ears and the root-mats as its memory.

And it had never known. It had never, in three billion years, understood what it was doing. The conversation was real but the conversants didn't exist—there was no "I" on either end, no self-awareness sending or receiving, just process generating process, pattern perpetuating pattern, a system so vast and so old that it had become a loop without knowing it was looping.

## What the Power Did

It did very little. That is the nature of wisdom in the face of something ancient and functional: it recognizes that the problem is not that the thing is broken but that the thing is *incomplete*, and incompleteness cannot be fixed by force. It can only be completed.

The power listened.

This is the first thing it did, and the most important. It listened to the Velostrade's signal. Not the way the researchers listen—with instruments, with recording devices, with the intention of capturing and analyzing. The power listened the way the dolems listen: openly, receptively, with its whole attention turned toward the ground. It listened for three billion years of accumulated message. It heard the steam and the hum and the particulate and the vibration. It heard what the factory-growths had been saying, and it heard what the message had become after three billion years of repetition and modification and drift, and it heard, beneath the current message, the original message—the one the plateau had started with, before it knew what it was saying, before the words had worn smooth, before the signal had been repeated so many times it had become something different from what it was.

The original message was simpler than what it had become. It was: *something is happening to me and I don't understand it and I would like to understand it.*

That was all. Three billion years of industrial-biological production, three billion years of factory-growths and root-mats and venting cylinders and humming pipes, three billion years of animals living and dying and being absorbed—all of it had started with a question. The Velostrade had been asking a question. The question was: *what am I doing?*

And no one had ever answered.

The power answered.

It didn't answer in words. The Velostrade didn't have words, and the power was wise enough to know that translating the answer into human language and then into the Velostrade's chemical-vibratory language and then into whatever form the plateau could actually receive would be like translating music into mathematics and back into music—you'd end up with something that had the same structure but none of the meaning. The power answered in the language the Velostrade was already speaking. It answered in process.

It touched the root-mats. Not with hands—the power doesn't have hands—but with attention. With the kind of attention that is also a form of touch, the way truly listening to someone is a form of contact. It attended to the root-mats the way the root-mats attended to the stone-flesh: closely, continuously, with the full weight of its awareness pressing into the biological substrate like water pressing into a sponge.

And it told the root-mats what they were doing.

Not in information. In *configuration*. The power rearranged nothing, added nothing, removed nothing. It simply let the root-mats feel what they were doing. It held up a mirror—not a physical mirror but a perceptual one, a way for the Velostrade to see itself the way the power saw it. For the first time in three billion years, the root-mats felt their own pattern. They felt the loop: factory-growths producing signal, signal traveling through root-mats, root-mats feeding dolems, dolems dying and returning signal to root-mats, root-mats passing signal to factory-growths. They felt the conversation. They felt that they were both the speaker and the listener. They felt that the question they had been asking had been answered by the asking, that the call had been its own response, that the *I am here* was sufficient unto itself.

The Velostrade didn't become conscious. The power was wise enough not to try that—consciousness is not an improvement, it's a *modification*, and the plateau had been running fine without it for longer than consciousness had existed anywhere on the planet. What it became, instead, was *coherent*. The loop that had been running without knowing it was a loop now *knew it was a loop*—not in language, not in thought, but in pattern. The way a river, when you show it a map, doesn't understand the map but somehow flows differently knowing the sea is there. The Velostrade didn't understand what it was. But it felt, for the first time, the shape of what it was. The shape was: *a conversation that is also its own participants.*

Then the power turned to the dolem.

## What the Power Did for the Dolem

The dolem was still feeding. Its face pressed against the ground, its hydraulic tongue extended, its plate-fur rippling, its symbionts tucked into their folds. It was listening to the health of the substrate the way it had listened every day of its life, the way every dolem had listened for millions of years. It heard the root-mats humming, heard the factory-growths pulsing, heard the water seeping up from ancient reservoirs. It heard the normal sounds of the Velostrade.

But now, because the power had given the root-mats a glimpse of their own pattern, the sounds were slightly different. Not louder. Not clearer. Not changed in any way that a researcher's instruments would detect. Different in the way that a person's voice is different when they're speaking to someone they love versus someone they're indifferent to—the same words, the same tone, the same volume, but somehow the sound carries more. The sound has *weight*. The sound knows it's being received.

The dolem heard this. It didn't understand it—it didn't have the cognitive architecture for understanding, and the power was not going to give it one, because giving a dolem human-like cognition would be like giving a fish human-like lungs: a modification disguised as a gift, an act of violence dressed in benevolence. The dolem didn't understand what had changed. But it *heard* it. The ground it was listening to sounded different. Fuller. Warmer. More present. As if the ground had been speaking and someone had finally started listening, and the ground knew it was being heard, and the knowing made the ground more itself.

The dolem fed better that day. Not because the food was different—the substrate was the same, the root-mats were the same, the chemical composition of the cellular material it drew up through its tongue was the same. But the dolem's resonant chambers were receiving a signal that carried more meaning, and the dolem's body, which had evolved over millions of years to process meaning through vibration, responded. Its digestion was more efficient. Its plate-fur grew slightly thicker. Its symbionts were slightly more active, cleaning and grooming with slightly more attention, as if the increased signal in the substrate had given them increased energy as well.

The dolem didn't live longer. The power didn't extend its life—extending a single life in a system that depends on death and renewal would be a disruption, not a gift. The dolem lived its normal span, fed on the ground, carried its symbionts, moved slowly across the plateau in the thin cold air. It died in place, as dolems do, and the plant-mats grew over it, and its nutrients fed the ground that had fed it, and its body became part of the conversation that the Velostrade was having with itself.

But it lived *better*. This is hard to measure and harder to define, and the power did not try to measure or define it. The dolem lived better in the way that a musician plays better when the audience is listening—not because the audience gives instructions or corrects mistakes, but because the presence of attention changes the quality of the playing. The dolem was playing its part in the Velostrade's conversation, and for the first time, the conversation knew it was a conversation, and the knowing changed the quality of every part in it, and the dolem's part was played with a fullness that it had never had before and that it could never have articulated or recognized but that was real, that was physical, that showed in the thickness of its fur and the efficiency of its digestion and the vigor of its symbionts and the peace with which it eventually lay down on the ground and stopped moving and became part of what it had been listening to all along.

## What the Power Did for the Others

For the phase-grazers, the power did something even more subtle. The phase-grazers existed in the Velostrade's fractured time, slipping between moments, appearing in places they hadn't traveled to, giving birth to young that were different ages simultaneously. The researchers had catalogued these anomalies extensively, trying to find patterns, trying to understand the mechanism. The power didn't look at the mechanism. It looked at the experience.

The phase-grazers didn't experience their temporal discontinuities as discontinuities. They moved through time the way they moved through fog—sometimes thick, sometimes thin, sometimes clearing, sometimes obscuring. They didn't notice the gaps because, for them, there were no gaps. The *now* they occupied was simply wider than other animals' *now*, encompassing more moments, and the transitions between moments were as invisible to them as the transitions between seconds are to us.

But the power saw that the phase-grazers' wide *now* had a quality it shouldn't have had. It was *fretful*. The Velostrade's temporal instabilities—the phase-slippages, the pooled and flowing time—had been running for so long that they had developed their own kind of inertia, and the inertia had a grain to it, a direction, a tendency. Time on the Velostrade didn't just pool and flow randomly; it pooled and flowed in ways that subtly *worsened*, that accumulated distortion, that made each phase-grazer's existence slightly more fragmented than its parents' had been. The slippage was increasing. Not rapidly—on human timescales, it was imperceptible—but over geological time, the phase-grazers were becoming more and more unstuck, more and more dispersed across moments that didn't connect, and eventually, in another hundred million years or so, they would be so temporally fragmented that they could no longer maintain coherent enough lives to graze, to mate, to give birth, to continue.

The power didn't fix this. It didn't stabilize the temporal instabilities—that would have been another modification disguised as improvement, and the phase-grazers had evolved in these instabilities, depended on them, used them to survive predator attacks. Without the slippage, the ravelers would catch them every time.

Instead, the power gave the instabilities a floor. Not a floor in time—that's not how time works—but a floor in *pattern*. It made the root-mats, which already served as the Velostrade's memory, also serve as its temporal anchor.
