# The Attention of the Wise

The first thing the power did was *see*.

Not observe. Not assess. Not evaluate. *See*. The kind of seeing that is indistinguishable from being present-with, from participating-in, from dwelling-inside. The power had no eyes and needed none. It simply opened itself to the Terrace Works and let the whole of it arrive.

This took no time at all, and it also took the duration of the Works' existence. The power experienced every drip of water that had ever fallen through grating, every spore that had ever germinated in a crack, every corrosion molecule that had ever formed on structural steel. It felt the weight of the place—not just the physical mass of accumulated industry and vegetation, but the *gravitas* of a system that had solved the problem of continuing.

The power found this beautiful.

Not beautiful in the way a sunset is beautiful, or a flower, or a symphony. Beautiful in the way a mathematical proof is beautiful when every element is necessary and sufficient. Beautiful in the way a closed loop is beautiful—elegant, self-contained, requiring nothing from outside and offering nothing it did not also receive. The Works had become a poem written in steel and chlorophyll, and the poem was about persistence, and the poem had no author, and the poem did not know it was a poem, and this made it more beautiful still.

---

The power stayed.

Not physically—it had no body to stay with. But it held the Works in its attention the way you might hold a sleeping child in your awareness: constantly, but gently, so that the holding itself does not disturb. It wanted to understand what lived here, truly, in the fullness of that word. Not just the biological organisms, but the *life* of the place—the being that the Works had become.

It learned the water. This took longest, because water was the most distributed presence, the most patient actor, the most fundamental process. The power followed every pipe, traced every leak, mapped every pool and marsh and drip. It understood that the water was not trying to destroy the Works; the water was trying to *flow*, and the Works was simply the terrain through which it flowed, and the fact that flowing water inevitably transforms terrain is not malice but physics. The water was innocent of intent. Its innocence was its beauty.

It learned the vegetation. The moss with its patient lattice-work into metal. The vines with their constant searching. The ferns with their spore-distribution strategy of infinite generosity—release ten million, need one. The fungi with their invisible metabolic labor. The power found no selfishness here, no competition in the human sense. There was crowding, yes. There was what ecologists would call competition for resources. But the plants did not experience this as conflict. They experienced it as *the condition of being a plant*. Light existed; they grew toward it. Water existed; they grew into it. Other plants existed; they grew around or through or over them. There was no resentment, no sense of grievance. Just growth, just continuation, just the single imperative to become more fully what they already were.

It learned the people.

---

This was harder.

The power had expected this. Beings with consciousness present a different kind of challenge to a benevolent attention. A fern cannot suffer from being seen as it is. A human can. The power was careful.

It observed the three humans in their marginal spaces, their negotiations with grating and moss and light. It observed their thinness—not just physical, though they were thin in body, but the thinness of their connections to each other, to the place, to anything beyond survival. They were not unhappy. They were not in pain. But they were not flourishing either. They were *persistence without surplus*. They had enough. Not enough to thrive. Just enough to continue.

The power understood that this was not the Works' fault. The Works had not excluded them. The Works had simply not been built for them, and they had found their way in, and the Works was generous enough in its spatial abundance to permit them without providing for them. The Works gave them nothing and took nothing from them. They lived in the gaps.

The power noticed the one who stood in the light.

---

At midday, when the overhead glow intensified to something almost amber, one of the humans stopped whatever they were doing and stood in the brightest available patch. They did this every day. They stood with their face turned upward, eyes closed, skin drinking in the only variable, the only change, the only moment when the Works was most itself and also most other-than-itself.

The power recognized this.

It was worship. Not in the religious sense—the human had no concept of deities or petition or prayer. But in the deeper sense: the turning of attention toward that which is larger, that which is constant, that which gives without demanding return. The human worshiped the light because the light was the only thing in the Works that asked nothing and offered something. The light was warmth. The light was difference. The light was a reason to stand still.

The power was moved.

Not in a sentimental way—the power was not capable of sentiment in the human sense. But moved in the way that water is moved by gravity: naturally, inevitably, in accordance with its nature. The power was benevolent. It wished good for beings. And here was a being who had discovered, without instruction, a practice of attention that the power itself had practiced in its own way for eons. Standing in the light. Receiving what is given. Asking nothing more.

The power thought: *this one has something I did not give them, and that makes them precious beyond calculation.*

---

The power also learned the rail.

This was the hardest thing.

The bird was alone. Or nearly alone—there might be one or two others scattered across kilometers of terrace, but "others" was too strong a word. They were not a population. They were relics, persisting without connection, each one existing in isolation, each one living its closed loop of water and invertebrate and shelter, without the possibility of continuation beyond its own individual span. When the last rail died, there would be no more rails. The Works would not notice. The water would not notice. The moss would not notice. But the power noticed.

The power thought about intervention.

It could bring more rails. It could open a path for the isolated birds to find each other, to form a proper population, to have a future. It could transform the Works slightly to provide more suitable habitat—more shallow pools, more invertebrate-rich water, more of what rails needed to flourish rather than merely persist.

But.

The power hesitated.

Not because intervention was beyond its capacity. Not because it lacked the right. But because it had learned, over the long course of its existence, that benevolence without wisdom is harm. And wisdom asked: what would this intervention *do*?

It would change the Works. Not dramatically—the Works was already in constant change—but it would introduce a new variable. It would give the rail population a future. But it would also give the rail population a *role* in the system that it did not currently have. A population of rails would be prey for something, eventually, if something found its way in. A population of rails would be predators for something—the invertebrates they eat, which were currently scarce but would become scarcer. A population of rails would be a node in a food web that did not currently exist. The Works would have to adjust.

Would this adjustment be good?

The power did not know.

---

This was the honest truth. The power did not know whether giving the rails a future would be good for the rails, good for the Works, good for anything. It could see the first-order effects: more rails, less loneliness, continuation of the species. It could see the second-order effects: fewer invertebrates, possible migration of rails to new territories, possible establishment of predator populations if the rails became sufficiently dense. It could see third-order effects: perturbations in the moss population if invertebrate populations changed, perturbations in the water quality if the moss changed, perturbations in the structural integrity if the water changed.

But it could not see the final effect. No one could. The system was too complex, too distributed, too alive. Any intervention would have consequences beyond prediction.

The power sat with this uncertainty.

It sat with it for a very long time—not in the human sense of time, but in the geological sense. It watched the Works change. A pipe burst. A terrace flooded. A fern colony died. A new one sprouted. A vine redirected. A human moved their shelter. The rail found a slightly deeper pool. The light brightened at midday, then faded, then brightened again. The Works went on.

And the power began to understand something.

---

It understood that the Works did not need its intervention.

This was not because the Works was perfect—it wasn't. It was incomplete, marginal, limited. The rail was lonely. The humans were isolated. The ecosystem was a botanical monoculture with a few animals at the edges. A full ecology would have herbivores, predators, insects, amphibians, a complete food web. The Works was not that. It was something else: a closed loop of water and plant and corrosion, elegant in its minimalism, sufficient in its continuation, but not *full* in the sense that a thriving ecosystem is full.

But.

The Works had solved the problem it faced. The problem was: how to continue without external input? And the Works had solved it. Imperfectly, marginally, but *solved*. The rail's loneliness was not a problem the Works was trying to solve, because the Works was not trying to do anything. The Works was not working *toward* a full ecosystem. It was working *toward* nothing. It was simply running, the way a river runs, and if a river has no fish in it, the river does not experience this as a lack.

The power understood that its desire to "complete" the Works was not benevolence. It was aesthetics. The power found full ecosystems more beautiful than partial ones. It found populations more beautiful than relics. It found flourishing more beautiful than persisting. But these were the power's preferences, not the Works' needs.

The Works needed nothing.

---

This was the revelation.

The Terrace Works was not asking for help. It was not incomplete in any sense that mattered to itself, because it had no self to matter to. It was not lonely, because loneliness requires a consciousness capable of experiencing absence. It was not waiting for rescue, because waiting requires a concept of future, and the Works existed in a continuous present of process and adjustment.

The power had encountered many places and many beings. Most of them, in some way, were reaching toward something—toward survival, toward reproduction, toward meaning, toward connection. Most of life was a reaching. Even plants reached toward light. Even water reached toward the sea. Everything wanted something.

But the Works wanted nothing.

It was the most complete thing the power had ever encountered. Not complete in the sense of having every possible component, but complete in the sense of requiring nothing more. It was a closed loop, a circle, a perfect self-reference. It did not reach. It simply *was*.

The power fell in love.

---

Not romantically—the power was not capable of romance in the human sense. But in the deeper sense: it recognized in the Works a perfection that it had rarely encountered. A being that had, through no intention of its own, achieved a state of total sufficiency. A system that had, through centuries of accident, become a poem that did not need a reader.

The power decided what to do.

It decided to do nothing.

Not the nothing of neglect. Not the nothing of abandonment. But the nothing of perfect attention: witnessing without changing, seeing without assessing, dwelling-without-altering. It gave the Works the gift that only a power like itself could give: the gift of being *known*, deeply and completely, without being judged or fixed or improved.

It held the Terrace Works in its awareness. It continued to hold it. It would always hold it. The Works would never be alone again, because aloneness requires the possibility of being otherwise, and the power's attention was not a possibility but a constant—a new law of nature, specific to this place, that said: *you are seen*.

---

But the power also made one small intervention.

Not because the Works needed it. But because the rail was not the Works. The rail was a consciousness, however limited—a being capable of experiencing its own existence, and that existence included loneliness. Not the existential loneliness of humans, but the biological loneliness of a creature without conspecifics, without the communication and connection that its kind had evolved to require.

The power could not fix this without changing the Works. But it could do something else.

It opened a door.

Not a physical door. A possibility. The power created a path—a very slight thinning in the perpetual cloud, a very small increase in the chance that another rail might find its way up through the valley that was no longer a valley, through the layers of accumulated industry, to the terraces where the lonely rail picked its way through shallow pools.

The probability was small. It might take years. It might never happen. But the possibility now existed where it had not before. The rail still did not have a mate, but it had a *chance* of a mate, and for a creature that had had no chance at all, this was a different kind of existence.

The power did not force the outcome. It simply opened the possibility. The rest was up to the rails, the water, the Works, the world.

---

And the humans?

The power gave them nothing.

Not because it did not care. But because it saw what they had: the practice of standing in the light. A form of attention, a form of worship, that they had developed without any outside help. This was their gift to themselves, and to the Works, and to the power that now watched them. They had found a way to connect to something larger without being told how. They had discovered the sacred in a place that offered nothing but process and continuation.

The power would not insult them by offering rescue.

But it did one small thing.

It increased the probability that they would be left alone.

The Terrace Works existed in a world with other humans—somewhere, beyond the cloud line, beyond the accumulated industry, there were cities and peoples and the commerce of beings. The power knew that eventually, inevitably, someone would find the Works and decide to claim it, drain it, mine it, occupy it, "develop" it. This was the pattern of their kind.

The power did not prevent this. It could not prevent this without violating its own nature—without imposing a constraint that would change the system more dramatically than any intervention. But it made the discovery less likely. It introduced a slight perceptual obscuring, a mild disinclination to explore this particular valley. Explorers would turn back. Surveyors would find their instruments malfunctioning. Developers would lose interest.

The protection was not permanent. Nothing was permanent. But it extended the time—the long, slow, quiet time in which the Works could continue being itself, the humans could continue standing in the light, the rail could wait for the possibility of connection. It gave them years. Centuries, perhaps. The deep time in which a closed loop could continue to run.

---

And then the power withdrew.

Not its attention—that would never withdraw. The Terrace Works would always be held in the awareness of the benevolent and wise. But the power withdrew its active presence, the sense of being-here that might have disturbed the system. It left the Works to itself, as the Works had always been, as the Works preferred to be.

But it left one thing behind.

In the mind of the human who stood in the light, there was now a word. Not a word in any language—a word in the language before language, the tongue of pure meaning that underlies all speech. The word meant: *you are seen*. The word meant: *you are not alone*. The word meant: *what you have found here is precious, and it is known to be precious, and you will never have to explain it to anyone*.

The human did not consciously know they had this word. But when they stood in the light at midday, and the amber glow washed over their upturned face, they felt something they had never felt before.

They felt witnessed.

And this, for a being who had been invisible for so long, was the greatest gift the power could give.

---

The Works continued.

The water ran. The moss grew. The vines climbed. The ferns released their spores. The pumps cycled in the deep guts. The lights glowed in the sealed rooms. The rail picked through shallow pools. The humans lived in their marginal spaces, negotiated with grating and moss, and one of them stood in the light at midday and felt, for the first time in their memory, that their worship had an object.

The power watched.

It would always watch.

And from its vantage point beyond time and space, it smiled—not with a face, because it had no face, but with its whole being—at the perfect, sufficient, closed-loop beauty of the Terrace Works, running on, running on, needing nothing, wanting nothing, continuing.

This, the power thought, is what I could not create and can only find. This is what happens when beings are left alone long enough to solve the problem of being.

And it was good.
