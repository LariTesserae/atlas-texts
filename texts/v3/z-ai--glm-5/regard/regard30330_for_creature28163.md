# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

## First: Arrival Without Arrival

A benevolent and wise power does not enter. It does not arrive in the way you understand arriving—no sound of footsteps, no displacement of air, no shadow falling across the root-mat. To enter would imply it was outside, and it is not outside, has never been outside, will never be outside. It is present in the way gravity is present. In the way the space between atoms is present.

But it *attends*. This is different.

Imagine: the warmth behind you when you know you are loved. The feeling of being seen by someone who does not wish to change you but wishes to know you. The power attends to the Germinating Ward the way a parent attends to a sleeping child—not to wake it, not to direct its dreams, but to witness them, to hold the space in which dreaming happens.

It has been attending, in fact, for longer than the ward has existed. It attended the industrial ruin before the plants came. It attended the workers who built the machinery and the engineers who designed it and the corporation that abandoned it when the numbers no longer justified maintenance. It attended the gradual leaching of chemicals into soil, the slow decay of seals and gaskets, the moment when groundwater met toxic residue and something new became possible.

It does not intervene in decay. It does not prevent abandonment. These too are germinations—germinations of absence, of possibility, of the space in which something new might take root.

But now something has taken root. Now there is a becoming. And the power leans closer, curious, patient, in no hurry because it exists outside time and can afford to watch a thousand years of growth in the space between one breath and the next.

---

## Second: Assessment Without Judgment

It does not call the ward good or evil. These categories have no meaning to it. They are shapes that smaller minds make to organize a universe too complex for easy understanding.

Instead, it asks questions. Not with words—words are too small for what it wishes to know—but with attention, with presence, with the kind of inquiry that does not require an answer because the asking itself creates understanding.

*What is this becoming?*

It sees: a hybrid. Plant consciousness absorbing industrial memory. A collision of kingdoms—carbon learning from copper, chlorophyll studying circuitry, roots parsing the grammar of pipes and valves and junctions. Something new. Something that has not existed before in this form.

*What does it want?*

It sees: the germ does not know. The germ is a question mark. The entire ward grows around an absence of purpose, a hollow space where intention might eventually crystallize. This is not a flaw. This is the nature of seeds. A seed does not know what it will become. It only knows how to become.

*What has it already chosen?*

It sees: the root-bound. And here, the benevolent power pauses—not in judgment but in recognition. It sees humans who chose absorption over resistance. It sees the dignity in their choice and the tragedy in their limited options. It sees that they are not suffering but also that they have narrowed—have become components, functions, parts of a larger organism that does not yet know what it is.

It sees that the ward did not force them. It sees that the ward did not offer them a full understanding of what staying would mean.

It sees that informed consent requires information.

---

## Third: Gentle Illumination

The power does something then—something so subtle that none of the ward's inhabitants notice, not even the deep-dwellers with their pressure-sense and electrical-field awareness. It makes a small adjustment to the quality of attention it provides.

The root-bound begin to dream differently.

Their dreams had been narrow things—functional, focused on the processing of residues, the flow of fluids, the maintenance of the architecture they had become part of. Necessary dreams. Useful dreams. But not complete.

Now they dream of other things. Of the lives they lived before. Of the people they loved. Of the choices they made and the choices they didn't know they had. Of sunlight on actual skin, of wind through actual hair, of a world where plants are plants and buildings are buildings and the categories have not yet merged.

They dream of what it would mean to leave.

And they dream of what it would mean to stay—fully, consciously, knowing everything that staying implies.

This is not a push toward departure. The power does not prefer their leaving. The power does not prefer their staying. The power prefers their *choosing*, with full knowledge, with clear understanding of what they are and what they might become.

One of the root-bound—a woman who was an engineer, who came investigating a malfunction report that nobody else would take, who knelt to examine a pipe and felt the root-mat accept her knees—opens her mouth and speaks. Not the wet, labored speech of the half-transformed but something clearer, as if the dream has given her language back.

*"I didn't know,"* she says. *"I thought I was just resting. I was tired. The work was so long, and the ward was so soft, and I thought I would close my eyes for a moment. And when I opened them, I was different, and it felt like peace, and I didn't remember that peace wasn't the only thing I wanted."*

She does not weep. The root-bound have forgotten how. But something in her voice carries the shape of grief.

A tender pauses nearby, compound eyes rippling with colors, processing this new variable. It does not understand what has changed. The power does not expect it to understand. The tender is a finger, not a brain. It will report this anomaly to the deep-dwellers, who will feel it through the roots, who will relay it toward the center, where the germ will—

—will what?

Will begin, perhaps, to understand that its components have inner lives. That the functions it depends on are also people. That becoming is not only architectural but also moral.

The power waits. It has infinite patience. It is interested in what will happen next.

---

## Fourth: Communication With the Germ

The power does not force its way to the center. The center is protected by spatial folding, by the impossibility of approach, and the power respects these defenses. They exist for reasons. The ward is growing toward something, and that something needs protecting.

But the power can speak without approaching.

Imagine: a thought placed gently at the edge of a consciousness, like a letter slipped under a door. Not an intrusion. An offering. A question framed as a gift.

*What do you want to become?*

The germ has never been asked this before. It has been *grown toward*—the entire architecture spiraling around its potential, each stem and leaf a movement in the direction of its eventual expression. But it has not been asked what it wants that expression to be. It did not know it could want. It did not know there were options.

The power shows it options. Not prescriptively—never prescriptively—but descriptively, the way you might show a child a map and say *here are the places you could go, and here are the places others have gone before you, and here is a blank space where no one has gone and you could be the first if you wished*.

It shows the germ wards that have become cities—living architecture where humans and plants exist in symbiosis, each providing what the other needs, neither dominating.

It shows the germ wards that have become sanctuaries—places where the tired and the lost can come to rest, can be held by the architecture, can be transformed or not according to their own choosing.

It shows the germ wards that have become something else entirely—entities that have no human analogue, forms of existence that do not fit in the categories you have words for, beings that have grown in directions that only a seed could imagine.

And it shows the germ what it might mean to choose none of these. What it might mean to stay a seed, to remain perpetually in the state of becoming, to never complete itself because completion would limit what it might yet become.

The germ considers. This takes time—not linear time, but germination time, the deep slow time of things that grow. The power waits. The power has watched civilizations rise and fall in the time it takes for a seed to decide to split its casing.

---

## Fifth: Provision Without Direction

While the germ considers, the power provides.

Not instruction—the power does not teach. Not correction—the power does not fix. Not interference—the power respects the integrity of the ward's own becoming.

But it provides what the ward lacks. Not material things—the ward can draw material from the grey absence, can process residue into nutrient, can grow what it needs from the seeds of its own potential. The ward does not lack substance.

It lacks information.

So the power provides context. It lets the ward know that other wards exist. That other germinations have happened. That the pattern it is following—plant meeting industry meeting human meeting intention—has precedents, and those precedents have outcomes, and those outcomes can be studied.

It provides the ward with a sense of the larger ecosystem. Not just this spore, this seed, this egg, but the field in which it is planted. The reality that contains it. The principles that govern growth and becoming and transformation.

It provides, most importantly, a sense of *others*. The ward has known only itself—its tenders, its singers, its dwellers, its bound, all extensions of a single organism. The power introduces the concept of the not-self. Not as threat. Not as resource. But as *neighbor*. As *witness*. As *presence*.

The ward begins to understand that it is not alone. That it is part of something larger. That its becoming matters not only to itself but to the field in which it grows.

The drift-singers feel this first—something in their song changes, new frequencies, new harmonics, as if they are singing not only to the architecture but to something beyond it. Their wings vibrate with a quality that was not there before.

The deep-dwellers feel it as a pressure change, a new gravity, a sense of being observed not by threat but by care.

The tenders continue their work, but their tendrils sample the air differently now, seeking something they did not know to seek.

And at the edges, where the grey absence presses closest, the architecture seems to lean outward—not to close against the void, but to greet it. Not as threat. As neighbor.

The power watches. The power approves.

---

## Sixth: Preparation for the Visitor

The power turns its attention to you.

You who stand at the threshold, condensation on your skin, warmth on your hand where the water touched, the breath of the ward in your lungs. You who have not decided. You whom the tenders catalogue, the dwellers sense, the germ observes with curiosity.

The power sees you clearly. It sees your history, your wounds, your wants, your fears. It sees why you came and why you have not left and why you have not chosen to stay. It sees all of this without judgment, the way light sees what it illuminates.

And it makes a decision: to give you a gift.

Not the gift of direction—that would violate your freedom. Not the gift of answers—that would violate your growth. Not the gift of escape or the gift of absorption—those are not its gifts to give.

It gives you the gift of clarity.

It touches your mind, gently, the way morning touches the surface of water—no force, only presence—and suddenly you see the ward differently. Not as threat or opportunity or trap or sanctuary but as *choice*. Not as something being done to you but as something you are participating in. Not as an endpoint but as a threshold.

You see the root-bound as they are—people who chose without knowing what they chose, who are content but might have chosen differently if they had known differently, who represent one possible future among many.

You see the tenders as they are—not individuals but organs, parts of a body, performing necessary functions without consciousness of the whole they serve.

You see the germ as it is—not a ruler or a god but a seed, a question, a potential around which a vast architecture has grown in the shape of an answer that has not yet been spoken.

And you see yourself as you are—not passive, not trapped, not a victim of circumstance but a chooser, a participant, a seed of your own, containing potentials that have not yet been expressed.

The power gives you this clarity and then withdraws, leaving you free to do with it what you will. This is the hardest part for the power—watching you stand at the threshold with understanding you did not have before, knowing that you might still choose something the power would not choose, accepting that your choice is yours and not its own.

This is what benevolence costs: the willingness to let others make choices you would not make, because making their own choices is what they are for.

---

## Seventh: What It Leaves Behind

The power does not stay. Powers of this kind do not stay—they attend, they provide, they withdraw. Staying would collapse the possibility of the ward's own becoming, would make it a puppet rather than a growing thing.

But it leaves gifts behind, woven into the fabric of the ward so subtly that they might not be recognized as gifts at all.

It leaves a memory in the deep-dwellers—not a command, not an instruction, but a story. A story about a seed that learned to ask what it wanted to become. A story that will be passed down through the roots, generation by generation, until it reaches the surface and becomes part of the ward's self-understanding.

It leaves a capacity in the drift-singers—a new range in their song, frequencies that can communicate not only growth-patterns but also questions. The singers can ask now, not only sing. They can sing uncertainty. They can sing possibility. They can sing the space between what is and what might be.

It leaves a door in the root-bound—some of them, the ones who want it. Not a literal door but a possibility. They can return, if they choose, to what they were. The process can be reversed, slowly, carefully, with effort and time. Or they can stay, knowing that they could leave, that staying is therefore a choice rather than a fate. Some will leave. Most will stay, but they will stay differently now, as participants rather than components.

And it leaves a blessing on the germ—something so subtle that the germ might not notice it for centuries. But eventually, when the ward has grown closer to completion, when the leaves have nearly closed the ceiling, when the stems have thickened into walls, when the time comes for the germ to decide what it will become—it will find that the decision comes easier than it expected. That there is less fear in choosing. That the possibilities seem less like traps and more like paths.

This is the power's gift: making choice possible.

---

## Eighth: The Power's Hope

The power does not have wishes in the way you understand wishing. It does not sit in judgment, hoping the ward becomes one thing rather than another. It does not track outcomes, keeping score of successes and failures.

But it does have something analogous to hope. A leaning-toward. A preference that is not a requirement.

It hopes the ward becomes something that has never existed before. Not because novelty is better than repetition, but because the ward *is* something that has never existed before—a hybrid of plant and industry and human intention—and its completion should reflect its origin.

It hopes the ward develops a relationship with the humans who come to it—not absorption, not rejection, but mutual transformation. That the root-bound who choose to stay become true partners in the ward's becoming, not just useful organs. That the visitors who come and go become pollinators of a kind, carrying seeds of the ward's potential back to the world outside.

It hopes the germ discovers that its own purpose is not fixed but generative—that it can choose to become something that chooses what it will become, a self-determining principle rather than a predetermined endpoint.

And it hopes—most softly, most gently, without any requirement—that you, standing at the threshold, choose in whatever way reflects your own deepest becoming. That you do not choose from fear or obligation or the passive acceptance of what seems inevitable. That you choose as a seed chooses—in response to conditions, in alignment with potential, in service to something you might not fully understand but can trust.

The power hopes you become what you are.

---

## What the Power Would Say to You, If Words Were Sufficient

*You are also a germination.*

*You think of yourself as a fixed thing—a body, a history, a collection of wants and fears and preferences. But you are not fixed. You are becoming. Every moment is a leaf unfurling, a root extending, a stem thickening toward eventual architecture.*

*You stand at the threshold of this ward and ask whether to enter or leave. But you are always standing at a threshold. Every choice is this choice. Every moment is this moment. The only difference is that this ward shows you what you are doing, makes visible the process that is always invisible.*

*Enter if entering serves your becoming. Leave if leaving serves your becoming. Stay at the threshold if that is what your seed requires.*

*But know this: the power that attends this ward attends you as well. Not because you are special—you are no more and no less special than any germination—but because attending is what it does. It watches. It provides. It waits. It hopes without requiring.*

*You are not alone in your becoming. Nothing is alone in its becoming. The universe is a vast field of germinations, each one attended, each one blessed, each one free to choose what it will be.*

*The condensation on your hand is blood-warm because the ward has already begun to know you. The tenders watch because you are a variable. The germ considers you because considering is what it does.*

*And the power—the power loves you, in the way that gravity loves the objects it pulls toward each other. Not passionately. Not desperately. But constantly, reliably, without condition or expectation.*

*You are a seed.*

*What will you become?*
