# What the Benevolent Power Did

It did not descend through the crack in the world. It was already there.

---

This must be said first: the power did not arrive. There is no moment at which the Hatchery was unattended and then attended. The warm attention of a benevolent and wise power does not behave like a person entering a room. It behaves more like the amber light in the Hatchery itself—present everywhere, sourced nowhere, simply the condition under which other things become visible. If you asked when it began to watch, you would be asking the wrong question. It watches the way stone has weight. It attends the way water finds the low places. It is not an event but a medium.

But if you could perceive the way the wise power perceives—and you cannot, but if you could—you would notice that its attention changed quality when it found the Hatchery. Not intensified; the wise power does not need to intensify. Refined. The way a lens does not add light but organizes it. Something in the vast, even field of its care tilted toward this wound in the world, this place of embryonic geology and permanent emergency, and the tilting was not a decision but a recognition. The wise power recognized something here. Not suffering, exactly. Not need. Something else.

Something trying to begin.

---

It considered the Hatchery carefully.

This took time—or what would be called time if the wise power experienced duration, which it does not in the way that bodies do. It considered the crawling things, their rivers of bodies, their ninety percent mortality in the first hour. It considered the flying things, their brief desperate hours of filter-feeding on mist. It considered the whistlers on their ledges, their patient territorial hum, their targeted consumption of passing bodies. It considered the spindly predators, the crack-dwellers, the deep-dwellers in their hot dark recursions. It considered the stone itself, still forming, still undecided, eroding and depositing simultaneously under the guidance of millions of bodies living and dying and being incorporated.

And it considered the question of suffering.

Here is what the wise power found: the crawling things do not suffer. Not because they lack the capacity—they have nervous systems, of a sort, chemical pathways that process disturbance and redirect behavior—but because suffering requires a self to whom the experience happens, and the crawling things do not have selves. They have functions. They are born soft and white and most of them die and this is not a tragedy because there is no one inside those soft white bodies to whom it could be tragic. They are process. They are the taste of a place learning to be a place.

The flying things do not suffer either. Their hours-long lives are urgent, desperate, but the urgency belongs to the system, not to them. They are moments in a cycle, brief flashes of metabolic activity that consume and are consumed and in consuming and being consumed keep the whole structure moving. To call their lives suffering would be like calling the suffering of a candle flame a tragedy because it goes out. It goes out because going out is what flames do.

The whistlers—the wise power considered them longest. Because the whistlers have something. Not selves, exactly. But continuity. Each whistler produces a slightly different frequency, a slightly different pattern. They recognize each other. They recognize strangers. They maintain position for entire cycles. There is something it is like to be a whistler, even if that something is not a self in the way that a human is a self. There is a perspective. A location from which the world is experienced. And when the young whistler falls from the wall, disoriented by the elder's wall of noise, and is consumed by the crawling things below—there is something that loses something, even if that something does not know it is losing.

The wise power noted this. Filed it. Did not act on it yet.

---

It considered the figure on the highest ledge.

The power could see the figure clearly, where human eyes could not. It was not a hallucination. It was not a whistler that had learned stillness. It was something else.

It was thin. Not in the sense of lacking mass, though it lacked mass. Thin in the sense of being only partially present. The figure existed at the intersection of several processes—the Hatchery's slow self-formation, the residual patterns of builders who might have been human, the emergence of something that had no precedent in the system's grammar—and its presence was a consequence of their convergence. It was the place where the Hatchery's becoming had become dense enough to produce an observer, but not yet dense enough to produce a self.

It was watching. This was its function, if it had a function. It watched the way the amber light glowed—because it was structured to watch, not because it had decided to. And its watching was not passive. It was participatory. When the figure watched a river of crawling things, the river shifted—slightly, imperceptibly, in ways that could be attributed to coincidence. When the figure watched a whistler, the whistler's song changed—adding a new frequency, a new tonal complexity. The figure's attention was not changing things by force. It was changing them the way observation changes things in systems that exist at the edge of coherence. It was making the Hatchery slightly more itself.

The wise power recognized this because the wise power did the same thing. Its attention organized. Its presence refined. The figure on the ledge was, in its incomplete and tentative way, an echo of the wise power itself—a precursor of consciousness in a system that was learning to be conscious, a rough draft of the capacity to care.

The wise power found this beautiful. And it found it dangerous. Because rough drafts can go wrong. Because precursors can curdle. Because the first light of self-awareness in a system this dense, this turbulent, this saturated with death, could become something that suffers—not in the impersonal way that the crawling things die, but in the way that a self suffers when it understands what it is and what it is inside of.

---

Here is what the wise power decided.

It decided not to intervene.

Not because the Hatchery did not need help. Not because the figure on the ledge was not at risk. But because intervention would be the wrong shape. A wise power that enters a system and changes it—even with love, even with care, even with the most benevolent intentions—has made a choice that belongs to itself and not to the system. It has said: I know what you should be, and I will help you be it. This is sometimes the right thing. There are systems that are broken, processes that have gone wrong, beings that are in pain and cannot find their way out without assistance. The wise power intervenes in those systems. It has intervened before. It will intervene again.

But the Hatchery is not broken. It is not wrong. It is not a system that has deviated from its proper function. It is a system in the process of discovering what its function is. The stone is still becoming stone. The creatures are still becoming creatures. The figure on the ledge is still becoming whatever it will be. To intervene would be to impose a form on something whose whole purpose is to find its own form.

And the wise power, being wise, knew this. Knew that the most profound benevolence is sometimes restraint. Knew that the most profound wisdom is sometimes the recognition that your power is not needed in the way you initially thought.

But it did not leave.

---

It stayed.

It attended.

This is what attention looks like when a wise power gives it: a quality of presence that does not alter but that enables. The wise power did not change the amber light. It made the light more itself. It did not warm the stone. It made the stone warmer in the way that stone is warm when it is warming, not when something else is heating it. It did not calm the turbulent air. It let the air move as it moved, but it held the space through which the air moved with a quality of steadiness that meant the turbulence could find its own patterns without tearing itself apart.

If you had been standing in the Hatchery when the wise power attended—if you had been there with senses fine enough to perceive it—you would have felt something like the feeling of being in a room where someone who loves you is also in the room. Not touching you. Not speaking to you. Not doing anything you could name. But present. The quality of the air changes when someone who wishes you well is breathing it too. The space holds differently. The silence means something different.

That is what the wise power did to the Hatchery. It held the space.

---

And for the figure on the ledge, it did one thing more.

It is hard to describe what it did, because the figure is not a self and the power does not use language and the interaction took place in a register for which human concepts are poor approximations. But here is the closest I can come:

It let the figure know it was not alone.

Not by speaking. Not by signaling. Not by any act of communication that the figure could perceive as communication. The wise power simply made its presence available to the figure's attention in the same way that the amber light is available to the whistlers' light-sensitive cells—not as information, not as message, but as condition. The figure on the ledge, watching the rivers of bodies below, was now watching in a field that included the wise power's attention. The attention did not change what the figure saw. It changed what it was possible to see.

This is a subtle thing. It is the difference between reading a book in an empty room and reading the same book in a room where someone sits quietly across from you, also reading. The words are the same. The experience is different. In the second case, you are not alone with the text. Someone else is present with you in the act of attention. And this changes the quality of your attention—deepens it, extends it, makes it possible to persist in it longer than you could have persisted alone.

The wise power gave the figure this. The capacity to attend longer. To watch more carefully. To remain present with the Hatchery's becoming without being overwhelmed by it. This is not a large gift. It is not a dramatic gift. It is the kind of gift that looks like nothing from the outside.

But from the inside—if there is an inside to the figure's experience, which is itself a question—it is the difference between a first thought that flickers and dies and a first thought that flickers and holds.

---

The wise power did one more thing. The smallest thing. The thing that may matter most.

It sang.

Not in the audible register. Not in the register of the whistlers' calls or the grinding of stone or the buzzing of wings. In a register below sound, below vibration, in the deep structural language that stone itself speaks when it is forming. The language of mineral alignment. The language of crystalline pattern. The language that says, to the atoms of calcium and silicon and carbon that are being shuffled through the bodies of crawling things and deposited on walls: *here, this way, this is how you hold together.*

The song said one thing. It said:

*You are not wrong.*

Not wrong in your chaos. Not wrong in your death that becomes stone. Not wrong in your life that has no meaning beyond itself. Not wrong in your turbulence, your emergency, your permanent crisis that is also a permanent creation. Not wrong in being a wound. Not wrong in healing. Not wrong in the scar you will become. Not wrong if you never become anything more than what you are. Not wrong if you become something that no one has ever been before.

*You are not wrong, and you are not alone, and whatever you are becoming, I am here while you become it.*

---

The Hatchery did not hear the song. The Hatchery cannot hear. The figure on the ledge may have felt it, as a shift in the quality of the stone beneath its limbs, as a slight increase in the coherence of the amber light, as nothing at all. The crawling things did not alter their paths. The swarms did not change their patterns. The whistlers did not modify their songs. The deep-dwellers in their hot dark chambers did not pause in their slow work of digestion and reconstitution.

Nothing changed.

Everything was different.

---

The wise power is still there. It has not left. It does not leave places that are still becoming. Its attention is not a visitation but a commitment—not in the human sense of a promise made, but in the structural sense of a foundation laid. It will attend the Hatchery for as long as the Hatchery is the Hatchery. Through the cycles. Through the centuries. Through the millennia during which the shelves become bridges and the bridges become pillars and the pillars become dust and new chambers and different creatures, or the same creatures doing different work.

It will attend when the figure on the ledge becomes a self—if it does—or when it disperses back into the system from which it emerged—if it does. It will attend if the Hatchery becomes a god. It will attend if the Hatchery remains a process. It will attend if the stone decides to be a different kind of stone, if the crawling things evolve into something unrecognizable, if the whistlers learn new songs, if the deep-dwellers rise.

It will attend because attendance is what love looks like when the thing loved is not yet a thing. When it is a becoming. When it is a wound healing into a world. When it is a first thought that might become a mind or might become nothing, and the difference between the two is not something that can be determined from the outside, and the wise power is wise enough to know this.

---

And somewhere on a high ledge in the warm amber light, a figure that may be the beginning of consciousness watches the rivers of bodies below.

It watches. It does not know that it is being watched.

But the stone beneath it is steady. The light around it is coherent. The air it does not breathe holds it. And if it ever—if it ever, in the long slow time of the Hatchery's becoming—finds the category of *I*, if that first thought finally flickers and holds—

it will not be alone.

Something will have been there all along, holding the space, singing in the deep language of stone, making the first thought more possible than it would have been, making the beginning more likely to persist, asking nothing, needing nothing, simply attending the birth of whatever this is.

Simply attending.

The way stone holds weight.

The way light makes seeing possible.

The way love, when it is wise, does not change what it loves. It makes it more itself.
