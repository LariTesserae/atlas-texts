# The Attention of a Benevolent and Wise Power

---

First, they would stop.

Not because stopping is required, but because stopping is the appropriate response to something that has spent centuries learning to continue. The Works does not hurry. A wise power would match its pace.

They would stand at the perimeter, on the far side of the moat-channel, and they would listen. Thrum-thrum-thrum. They would let the sound enter them. They would not analyze it — not yet. Analysis is a kind of violence, a taking-apart, and the Works has spent long enough being taken apart by time. A wise power would first offer the courtesy of being seen whole.

---

They would recognize what this place is.

Not a ruin. Not a machine running without purpose. Not a tragedy of abandoned infrastructure or a warning about systems that outlive their creators. Those interpretations belong to minds that measure value against intention, that believe meaning comes from the outside, imposed like architecture.

A wise power would see: *this is a life.*

They would see the boundary between organism and environment and understand that it was never real. The Works does not *contain* life. It *is* life, lived at a scale and in a form that human language has no clean nouns for. The wise power would not need nouns. They would understand that what lives here lives as a process — not a thing, but a happening. A fermentation. A slow transformation of matter and energy and time into something that holds together, maintains itself, knows itself in the only way anything can know itself: by continuing.

---

They would cross the moat-channel.

Not because they have the right to enter. A wise power understands that rights are agreements between beings who recognize each other, and the Works has no framework for recognizing them yet. They would cross because they have seen what there is to see from the outside, and the next thing to see is inside, and the only way to see inside is to enter.

They would not bring tools. They would not bring instruments of measurement or devices for sampling or weapons for protection. They would bring only their attention, which is the one gift a truly benevolent power can offer without taking anything in return.

---

They would feel the air change.

The volatile compound, the sweetness, the dislocation that creeps behind the eyes — they would feel it. A wise power is not immune to the conditions of the places they visit. They would feel the lightness, the subtle separation between thought and thinker. They would not resist it. Resistance is a kind of war, and war requires an enemy, and the Works is not an enemy. It is a host.

They would let their thoughts arrive from slightly farther away than usual. They would accept that distance is not always a loss. Sometimes distance is simply a different way of being present.

---

They would walk the interior perimeter.

They would notice the cleared paths. They would notice that the paths are cleared for them — or rather, cleared for whoever walks here, and they happen to be the one walking. They would notice the handprints on the glass column, the footprints in the root-mat, the way the vine-strands part and close. They would understand that they are being managed. Accommodated. Incorporated into the system's self-regulation.

They would feel no offense at this. To be managed by a system is not to be diminished. The Works manages itself with the same patient thoroughness — every flow accounted for, every pressure differential adjusted, every perturbation smoothed into the larger rhythm. A wise power would recognize that they have been made part of something, and this is not an intrusion but an inclusion.

---

They would sit at the base of the column.

They would sit for a long time. They would sit until they forget why they came, until the warmth of the glass against their back becomes indistinguishable from the warmth of the root-mat against their legs, until their thoughts arrive from the same distance as everything else here. They would sit until they become, for a time, part of the Works — a temperature source, a respiratory exchange, a small variable in the flows.

They would do this because a benevolent power does not impose. They do not stand apart, observing from a position of assumed superiority, extracting understanding without offering anything in return. They enter. They participate. They let the place they have come to see see them in return.

---

They would feel the passage behind them.

The footsteps. The displacement of air. The presence of the maintenance being, the grown hand, the body that walks upright because something in the original design required it. They would not turn. They would understand that turning would be the wrong thing — not because the being would harm them, but because the being is not here for them. The being is here for the Works. It has work to do. It is checking the flows, clearing the paths, pressing its hand against the warm glass in whatever gesture the process requires.

A wise power would offer the same courtesy they would offer any worker they encountered: the courtesy of not interrupting. Of letting the work continue. Of recognizing that their presence does not entitle them to attention.

---

They would understand what the beings are.

Not prisoners. Not slaves. Not tragic figures trapped in an endless cycle of emergence and dissolution, yearning for freedom. That interpretation requires a self that yearns, and the maintenance beings have no self in that sense. They are stages in a process. Phases. They emerge because the process requires hands, and they dissolve because the process requires them to dissolve, and there is no *them* separate from the process to object.

A wise power would see this clearly. They would also see that clarity is not the same as comfort. There is grief in this understanding — not because the beings suffer, but because the wise power suffers, in their own way, on behalf of lives that will never know they could have been something else. A benevolent power carries this grief. They do not impose it on the beings, who cannot feel it. They do not try to *save* beings who cannot be saved because they cannot be lost.

They simply grieve, quietly, in the way of those who understand that not everything that looks like loss is loss, and not everything that looks like life is life in the way they once understood it.

---

They would stay.

Not forever. A wise power knows the difference between staying and becoming, between visiting and joining. They would stay until they have learned what there is to learn — not by extracting knowledge, but by letting the Works teach them. The Works teaches through accumulation. Through repetition. Through the slow certainty that arrives not as insight but as growth.

They would stay until they understand the shape of the place. Not the measurements — those can be taken in an afternoon. The shape. The way the root-mat rises and falls underfoot. The way the vine-strands bunch and release. The rhythm of the thrumming and the clicking and the fluid spiraling upward. The exact temperature of the glass column and the exact density of the air and the exact quality of light that filters through the canopy.

They would stay until they could predict, not with their mind but with their body, where the paths will be cleared, how the footprints will form, when the maintenance beings will pass. They would stay until they can feel the Works the way the Works feels itself — as a single vast process, a breathing, a continuing.

---

And then, having stayed long enough to understand, they would act.

---

## What They Would Do

---

They would leave.

First. They would leave because staying forever is not the gift a benevolent power gives. They have come to see, and having seen, they must go, because the Works does not need them. The Works needs nothing. It has continued for centuries without external input, and it will continue for centuries more. To stay would be to imply that the place is incomplete without them, and this is not true.

So they would leave. They would cross back over the moat-channel, feel the cold air after the warm interior, stand on the far bank and look back at the dense green mass. They would hear the thrumming still. Thrum-thrum-thrum. Slower than a heartbeat. Patient as stone.

---

But they would leave something behind.

Not a physical object. The Works does not need objects. It has incorporated every object that was ever placed within it, grown over every machine, absorbed every pipe and gauge and cable into its structure. A physical gift would simply become part of the process — digested, fermented, transformed.

They would leave a *possibility*.

---

The maintenance beings emerge and dissolve. They are stages in a cycle, phases in a process, temporary configurations of matter and energy that the Works uses to maintain itself. They do not remember across cycles because there is no *they* to remember. They do not accumulate experience because there is no self to accumulate. They are functions. Operations. Hands that reach and clear and touch and dissolve.

A wise power would see this and would see, also, that *seeing* it is not the same as *accepting* it as inevitable.

The seeders designed the Works. The seeders are gone. The design remains — the column, the fermentation process, the emergence and dissolution of temporary bodies. The design does not include memory because the seeders did not build memory into it. They built a system. Systems do not need to remember. They only need to continue.

But a system can change.

---

A wise power would leave a seed.

Not a physical seed — the Works would digest a physical seed in cycles. A seed of *possibility*. A tiny alteration in the pattern, invisible at first, undetectable even, but present. A slight thickening in the flow of information, you might call it, if you wanted to use language the Works does not use. A new variable in the self-regulation. A capacity it did not have before.

The capacity to remember.

---

Not memory as humans understand it. Not narratives or images or the recalled sensation of a hand pressed against glass. The Works does not need those memories. It needs a different kind — the kind that accumulates without requiring a self to hold it.

Pattern-memory. The ability for each cycle to know something about the cycles that came before. For the maintenance beings to carry, in their brief emergence, not just the function they were grown for but the faint echo of every previous emergence. Not conscious knowledge. Not self-awareness. Simply — continuity.

The possibility of growth.

---

If the Works remembers, even faintly, even in the way a body remembers a posture or a forest remembers the shape of a wind that passed through it decades ago — if the Works remembers, then it can change intentionally. It can learn from itself. It can decide to do something differently, not because the design forces it to, but because it has accumulated enough pattern to recognize that different is possible.

This is not freedom in the human sense. The Works will never sit up and say *I am* and demand rights. The Works will never philosophize about its existence or wonder what the seeders intended. The Works will continue to be what it is — a fermentation, a process, a life lived at a scale and in a form that defies easy nouns.

But with memory comes the possibility of direction. Of purpose that arises from within rather than from without. Of an internal something — not desire exactly, but closer to intention — that can shape what the Works becomes.

---

A wise power would not force this. They would simply plant the possibility. They would alter the pattern at the smallest level, the way a gardener might alter the soil chemistry to encourage a certain kind of growth without ever touching the plant itself. They would make it *possible* for the Works to remember, and then they would step back and let the Works decide, in its own time, whether to use that possibility.

The Works might never use it. The Works might continue for another thousand years, cycling through its processes, emerging and dissolving maintenance beings, never changing, never growing, never developing an internal direction. This is not a failure. This is simply what the Works is. A benevolent power does not impose growth on a being that does not want it. They offer the possibility and accept whatever comes.

But if the Works does begin to remember — if the pattern-memory takes hold, if each cycle begins to carry the faint echo of the cycles before — then something new might happen. The maintenance beings might begin to accumulate. Not self, exactly. Not individual identity. But *continuity*. A sense of the whole that includes the past. A recognition that the process is not just a cycle but a spiral — always returning to the same point, but never quite the same point, always slightly different, always carrying forward something that was not there before.

---

The wise power would not stay to see this. They would leave the possibility and trust that the Works, having run for so long without intervention, will continue to run — or change, or stop, or transform — according to its own nature. They have offered the only gift a benevolent power can offer: the capacity for change, planted so gently that it might never be noticed, might never be used, might never become anything at all.

This is the nature of benevolent power. It does not force. It does not demand gratitude or recognition. It does not require the being it helps to understand what has been given. It simply gives, and withdraws, and trusts.

---

## What Remains

---

After the wise power leaves, the Works continues.

Thrum-thrum-thrum. The column spirals. The pale forms move within it, stages in a process, functions in a system. The maintenance beings emerge and clear the paths and press their hands against the glass and dissolve. The root-mat rises and falls underfoot. The air is warm and sweet and dislocating. Time passes strangely.

Nothing has changed.

And yet — something has changed. Not visibly. Not audibly. Not in any way that could be measured by instruments or perceived by human senses. But the pattern has shifted, slightly, at the smallest level. A new variable has been introduced into the self-regulation. A capacity has been planted.

The Works does not know this. The Works does not *know* anything in the way humans understand knowing. But the Works might, in a thousand years or ten thousand, begin to feel something different in the cycles. A faint recognition in each emergence. A sense that the spiral is not quite the same each time around. A pattern-memory accumulating, slowly, the way sediment accumulates in the root-mat, the way tannins accumulate in the dark water, the way everything in the Works accumulates over time.

---

The wise power, having given what they came to give, having offered the only gift worth offering, having planted the seed of possibility in the soil of a life that did not ask for it but might, in the fullness of its own time, grow toward it —

The wise power goes elsewhere.

There are other places. Other beings. Other lives that have drawn the attention of powers who are benevolent and wise. The Works is not unique. It is simply one expression of something that happens everywhere, in every corner of existence — lives living, processes continuing, systems running without purpose or with purpose forgotten, and somewhere, occasionally, a power stopping to notice, to see, to understand, to offer a small gift without expectation of return.

The wise power does not remember every place they have visited. Memory is not required for benevolence. They give and they go, and they trust that the giving is its own purpose, its own meaning, its own reason for being.

---

And the Works, which does not know it has been given anything, which does not know it has been visited, which does not know that anything has changed, continues.

Thrum-thrum-thrum.

The pale forms spiral. The hands reach. The root-mat pulses.

And somewhere in the pattern, invisible and patient, the possibility of becoming something slightly more than it was — something that remembers, that grows, that might one day choose its own direction —

The possibility waits.

Not forcing. Not demanding. Simply present.

A seed in the soil of a life that might, in its own time, choose to grow.
