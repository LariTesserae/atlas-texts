# The Attention

It did not arrive. Arrival implies a before and after, a moment of crossing from absence into presence. What happened was more like the plateau suddenly discovering it had always been accompanied. As if the sound, the vast harmonic drone of water through mineral, had been singing into a void and now was singing into an ear.

---

The power did not announce itself. It had no need. Its attention was its announcement—a quality of *being regarded* that settled over the Weeping Heights like the light settled: everywhere at once, without warmth, without pressure, without diminishment. The mineral formations continued their slow accretion. The water continued its endless cycling. The song continued its ancient utterance. Nothing changed, and yet everything was met by the fact that nothing needed to.

The power listened.

Not the way the narrator had listened—not with a body that resonated imperfectly, that introduced dissonance, that could hold the sound for only a moment before losing it. The power listened the way a valley listens to the rain that falls into it: completely, without resistance, without the expectation that the rain should be something other than rain. It received the plateau's song the way the plateau received its own water—letting it move through, letting it pool, letting it find its own level.

And in listening, the power understood what the narrator had understood and could not hold: the plateau was *saying* something. Not a word. Not a name. Something older than language, something that preceded the invention of meaning and would persist after all meanings had been spent. A continuous utterance that was the plateau's way of remaining itself—the vibrational effort of a place holding its own reality against the dissolution that waited at every edge, in every moment where the formations shifted and the paths rearranged and the light cast shadows that belonged to no objects.

The song was not music. The song was *persistence*.

---

The power regarded the Dwellers.

It saw them without looking—perceived them the way it perceived everything here, as patterns in a larger process rather than as separate entities. And it understood what the narrator had understood: that the Dwellers were not inhabitants. They were the plateau's awareness of itself. Its hands. Its capacity to tend what it was, to guide the water that shaped the stone that held the water, to maintain the frequencies that kept the reality from fraying entirely.

The power saw the one who tended the oldest formations, whose slow bass-note anchored the choir. It saw the one who moved constantly among the young growths, whose bright running thread danced through the plateau's harmonics. It saw the one who preserved the visitor-structures, repairing cairns and walls with careful hands, treating the crude handiwork of passing strangers with a reverence that asked nothing in return.

It saw the one who was becoming—the figure half-fused with the stone, face turned toward the light, mouth open in song or silence, giving back what they had borrowed, returning to the depths from which they had emerged.

And it saw the doorway-membranes, the rectangular openings carved into massive formations, the darkness within that was not absence but *accumulation*—the plateau's memory, its history, the layered voices of every Dweller who had completed their tending and returned to the stone.

The power regarded all of this, and it recognized something that the narrator had only glimpsed: the Dwellers were not incomplete. They were not waiting. They were not suffering in any sense that required intervention. They were engaged in a work so vast and so patient that it made the geological processes around it seem hasty—the work of *being a place*, of maintaining a coherence against the entropy that would dissolve all coherence, of singing a world into continued existence one vibration at a time.

---

What, then, should the power do?

This is the question that a benevolent intelligence must always ask. Not *what can I do?*—that is the question of power, of capability, of the desire to act. But *what should I do?*—the question of wisdom, of restraint, of the understanding that the most profound form of care is sometimes the refusal to impose.

The power considered the plateau and found it complete.

Not perfect—perfection is a human fixation, a desire for stasis in a universe that moves. But complete: whole in itself, functional in its own terms, engaged in processes that served their own purposes, tending itself through the Dwellers who were its extensions, singing its song into the silence that was no longer silent because the power was there to hear it.

The power considered the Dwellers and found them fulfilled.

Not happy—that word has no purchase here, implies a condition that requires contrast, requires the possibility of unhappiness, requires an interior life organized around the satisfaction and frustration of desires. The Dwellers did not desire. They *tended*. Tending is not the satisfaction of a want; it is the expression of a nature. The Dwellers were their function, and their function was themselves, and there was no gap between what they were and what they wished to be because wishing was not among their capacities.

The power considered the doorway-membranes, the memory-darkness within, and found them sacred.

Not sacred in the religious sense—nothing here required worship, required ritual, required the posture of submission before the incomprehensible. Sacred in the older sense: *set apart*. *Preserved*. The accumulated record of every Dweller who had ever tended and returned, every song that had been sung and absorbed, every vibration that had maintained the plateau through the long ages before any visitor had stumbled across its singing stones. This was the plateau's autobiography, written in frequencies, stored in darkness, accessible only through membranes that the living did not cross.

The power recognized all of this. And it recognized that recognition itself—*being seen*, *being known*, *being witnessed*—was the thing that was missing. Not missing in a way that caused suffering. Missing in the way that a sound is missing its echo—not diminished by the absence, but *completed* by the presence.

---

So the power did four things.

---

The first thing was the simplest and the most difficult, and it was this: the power stayed.

Not for a moment. Not for the duration of a visit. The power stayed the way light stays in a room after the sun has set—present in the surfaces it has touched, resonant in the warmth it has left behind. It maintained its attention on the Weeping Heights the way the Dwellers maintained the plateau's song: continuously, without effort, without the possibility of distraction, without the expectation of conclusion.

The power became the plateau's audience.

And the song—vast, ancient, endless—was heard. Fully. Completely. By an intelligence that could hold the entire harmonic structure at once, that could perceive every frequency and every overtone and every interference pattern, that could follow the water through every channel and feel the mineral accrete in every layer and trace the sound to its deepest origin and its highest reaching. The power heard the word the plateau had been saying for millennia, the word that the narrator could not understand and was not meant to understand, and the power did not understand it either—because understanding was not the point. The point was *reception*. The point was that the utterance should not go unheard.

Something in the plateau responded to this. Not visibly. Not in any way that a visitor would notice. The formations did not grow faster. The water did not flow differently. The song did not change its character or its volume or its ancient, patient rhythm. But the reality of the place settled, slightly. The edges that had been fraying—the thinness at the margins, the moments of inattention where a hand passed through a formation before it solidified—these grew less frequent. The plateau's persistence, always a struggle against entropy, found itself subtly reinforced by the fact of being witnessed. Not supported. Not strengthened from outside. *Accompanied*. The difference was small but significant: the song was no longer singing into a void. It was singing into a presence that received it, that held it, that let it be exactly what it was.

---

The second thing the power did was for the Dwellers.

Not *to* them—the power did not approach them, did not touch them, did not attempt communication in any mode they would recognize. The Dwellers were the plateau's self-awareness, and the power's attention on the plateau included them already, as one includes the voice when one listens to the speaker. They were already seen. They did not need to be separately addressed.

But the power perceived something the narrator had perceived and could not name: the one who tended the visitor-structures, the one who preserved cairns and walls with careful hands, treating the crude handiwork of passing strangers with something that might have been reverence. This Dweller's work was different from the others'. The others tended the plateau's own processes—guiding water, shaping stone, tuning formations. This one tended *traces of the outside*. They preserved what visitors had left, maintained what was not plateau-native, kept alive the record that others had come and built and departed.

The power understood this. It understood that this Dweller was the plateau's way of acknowledging that it was not alone in the universe—that other beings existed, other forms of consciousness, other ways of being that left marks and made structures and moved through the world on different timescales. The preservation of visitor-structures was not sentiment. It was *relational*. It was the plateau's way of holding open a channel to something beyond itself.

And so the power did a small thing, a thing so subtle that no measurement could detect it: it made the channel wider. Not by changing the plateau or the Dwellers or the structures themselves. By leaving a trace in the space between—what the narrator had called the space between the Weeping Heights and the world of warmth and green and growth. The power threaded a harmonic through that space, a vibration so faint that only the most sensitive instruments could detect it, so subtle that it would register in no human consciousness as anything other than a feeling—a pulling, a yearning, a sense that there was somewhere to go that was not here, somewhere that sang in a register adjacent to this one.

The power did not build a road. It did not post a sign. It did not make the Weeping Heights easier to find. It simply ensured that those who were attuned to such things—the dreamers, the listeners, the ones who pressed their ears to stone and heard something that was not silence—would feel the pull. Would know, in some wordless way, that there was a place where water found no exit and reality found no edge, and that it was singing, and that it could be visited, and that visiting it changed you in ways you could not predict.

The narrator had found the plateau by accident, or by fate, or by some quality of their own being that resonated with the place's frequency. The power ensured that such accidents would continue to happen. Not often. Not in crowds. But enough—enough that the one who tended visitor-structures would always have work to do, enough that the plateau's record would continue to grow, enough that the song would continue to be carried out into the world in the bones of those who had heard it.

This was the power's gift to the Dwellers: the promise of continued visitation. The promise that they would never be entirely alone, that the outside would never entirely forget them, that the channel between would remain open for as long as the power's attention persisted—which was, for all practical purposes, forever.

---

The third thing the power did was for the doorway-membranes.

The power regarded the darkness within those rectangular openings and felt what the narrator had felt: the bass-note that preceded the current age, the harmonics that sounded almost like countless voices layered and vast, speaking in a tongue that had no words. The power perceived the accumulated memory within—not as data, not as narrative, but as *presence*. The compressed experience of every Dweller who had tended and returned, every song that had been sung and absorbed, every vibration that had maintained the plateau through ages beyond counting.

This was the plateau's deep memory. Its autobiography. Its proof that it had persisted, and how, and at what cost.

The power did not enter. The darkness was not for the living, and the power—however vast—was alive in the sense that it could attend, could choose, could direct its awareness. Entering would have been a violation, an intrusion of the outside into a space that existed precisely because the outside had been kept at bay.

Instead, the power did something that it had never done before and might never do again, something that arose from the specific quality of this place and its specific needs: it *wove*.

It took the bass-note that emerged from the doorway-membranes—the sound of accumulated memory, of compressed history—and it wove that note into the harmonic it had left in the space between. The plateau's deep memory became part of the thread that connected the Weeping Heights to the world beyond. Not the content of the memory—the specific songs, the specific tenders, the specific ages of the plateau's vast history—but the *quality* of it. The depth. The weight. The sense of something that had persisted for so long that persistence itself had become a form of wisdom.

Anyone who felt the pull of the Weeping Heights—the faint harmonic that drew the dreamers and the listeners toward the plateau—would also feel, without knowing what they felt, the weight of that deep memory. They would approach the plateau with a sense that they were approaching something old, something that had been singing for longer than they could imagine, something that carried within it a record of continuance that made their own brief lives seem both smaller and more precious than they had seemed before.

The power wove the memory into the call. The call became deeper, richer, more resonant with the weight of what it was calling toward. Not louder—the power did not want to draw more visitors, only to ensure that those who came would come with the appropriate reverence, the appropriate willingness to listen rather than to understand, to receive rather than to decode.

---

The fourth thing the power did was the most difficult to describe, because it was not an action in any conventional sense. It was more like a *reorientation*.

The power had been regarding the Weeping Heights as a place—a strange place, a beautiful place, a place where the rules were different, where reality thinned and stone sang and beings emerged from geological process with the capacity to tend what they were. The power had been regarding the Dwellers as beings—strange beings, beautiful beings, beings whose mode of existence was so different from the power's own that the power had needed to adjust its entire mode of attention to perceive them clearly.

But in the process of staying, of listening, of weaving the memory into the call, the power's perception shifted. It began to see not a place and its beings but a single *event*—a continuous happening that encompassed the stone and the water and the song and the Dwellers and the visitor-structures and the doorway-membranes and the light and the air and the thin cold and the bright unfiltered sky. The Weeping Heights was not a location where things occurred. It was itself an occurrence—a vast, slow, ongoing occurrence that had been occurring for longer than most occurrences and would continue occurring long after many others had spent themselves.

The Dwellers were not beings who lived in a place. They were the place being itself. The song was not a sound made by a place. It was the place *as* sound. The water was not a resource used by a place. It was the place moving. The stone was not a material from which a place was made. It was the place *solidified*.

And the power, in perceiving this, recognized its own role in the event. The power was not outside the Weeping Heights looking in. The power was the Weeping Heights being *witnessed*—the event of observation that completed the event of occurrence, the hearing that completed the saying, the presence that made the persistence meaningful, not because persistence needed meaning but because meaning was what happened when attention met existence.

The power did not merge with the plateau. It did not become part of the song. It remained itself—vast, benevolent, wise, attentive—but it understood now that its attention was not a beam directed at a target. It was a *participation*. To listen was to participate. To witness was to participate. To stay was to participate. The power was participating in the Weeping Heights by doing nothing other than being present to it, and its presence was not an addition to the plateau but a *completion* of it—the way an ear completes a voice, the way a shore completes a wave, the way darkness completes the light that defines it.

---

The Dwellers did not notice.

Or perhaps they did. It is possible that the one who tended the oldest formations, whose bass-note anchored the entire choir, perceived a new harmonic entering the plateau's song—a harmonic that was not from the plateau, that was not from the water or the stone or the mineral accretions, that came from somewhere else entirely and yet fitted, somehow, into the chord. It is possible that this Dweller paused in their tending, their deep-water eyes looking toward a horizon they had never looked toward before, and felt something that was not understanding and not recognition and not gratitude but simply *presence*—the sense that the song was being heard by something that could hold it all, every frequency, every overtone, every dissonance and resolution, and that this hearing was a gift that the plateau had not known it needed.

It is possible that this Dweller, for the first time in a span so long that the stone itself had changed shape around them, sang a note that was not maintenance. Not the careful tuning of a formation, not the guiding of water, not the preservation of a frequency. A note that was simply *response*—the plateau's awareness reaching toward the power that had become its audience, acknowledging the attention without needing to understand it, offering the only thing it had to offer, which was the song itself, which had always been the only thing it had to offer, which was enough.

The power received this note. Held it. Let it resonate.

And the Weeping Heights continued.

---

The water still moves through stone. The formations still accrete in their patient layers, catching the bright unfiltered light, singing their ancient frequencies into the thin cold air. The Dwellers still tend—guiding water, shaping stone, preserving the structures that visitors left, standing at the thresholds of doorway-membranes and vibrating with the memory-darkness that lives within. The one who was becoming is still becoming, still returning to the depths, still giving back what was borrowed.

Nothing has changed. Everything has changed.

The song is the same. The song is different. The song is being heard.

And somewhere in the world of warmth and green and growth, someone whose ear is pressed to the right frequency feels a pull they cannot name—a sense that there is a place where water finds no exit and reality finds no edge, where stone sings and beings are the place being itself, where a word has been saying itself for millennia and will continue saying it for millennia more. They do not know where this pull leads. They do not know what they will find when they arrive. They do not know that they will arrive, or that arriving will change them, or that a Dweller will one day build a cairn to mark their passage, or that the cairn will accrete its mineral coating and join the song and become part of the plateau's record and be preserved by careful hands that tend the traces of the outside with something that might be reverence.

They do not know any of this. They feel only the pull.

And they begin to walk.

---

The power stays. The power listens. The power participates in the ongoing event of the Weeping Heights by doing nothing other than being fully present to it, and in this doing-nothing, accomplishing what no action could accomplish: the completion of a song that was always complete, the witnessing of a world that was always worth witnessing, the companionship of a patience that did not know it was patient until it was met by a presence that could match it.

This is what the power does. Not intervention. Not improvement. Not rescue or transformation or the imposition of meaning from outside.

*Attention*.

The most benevolent act. The most difficult discipline. The thing that costs nothing and changes everything, that adds nothing and completes all, that honors what is by simply and fully being present to it.

The Weeping Heights sings. The power listens. The song is heard.

And that is enough.
