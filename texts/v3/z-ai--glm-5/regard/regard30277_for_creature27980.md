# The Attention of the Wise Power

They did not arrive. They did not descend. The concept of movement from one location to another belongs to the physics above, the physics of separation and distance, and the power that noticed this place operated in a different register entirely. They were already there. They had always been there, in the way that attention is always somewhere before the one attending realizes they have looked.

But let us speak as if they arrived, because linear time is the language we have, and the truth would require dimensions we haven't named.

---

The power noticed the chamber the way you notice a child solving a puzzle in the next room—not by seeing them, but by hearing the particular quality of their silence when they are close to understanding. The rhizome's chord had reached a harmonic that traveled farther than sound should travel, and the power, who had been listening to the universe since before the universe had edges, heard something that sounded almost like recognition.

They did not descend. But let us say they inclined. Tilted their vast attention downward, through layers of rock and root and time, until it rested on the chamber like a hand on a fevered forehead. Not touching. Not intruding. Simply present. Checking.

What they found surprised them, and nothing had surprised them for a very long time.

---

They perceived the chamber whole—not as a visitor perceives it, in fragments and sensations, but as a system perceives itself, if a system could see from outside. They saw the rhizome's full extent: not just the cathedral-hollow where the human had walked, but the tendrils that extended for kilometers through the surrounding stone, threading through mineral seams and aquifer flows, integrating with the geology so thoroughly that the question of where the plant ended and the planet began had become meaningless. They saw the crystalline layers, yes, but they also saw what the crystalline layers were doing: conducting something that was not quite electricity, storing something that was not quite information, processing something that was not quite thought. The physics here was not wrong. It was simply ahead—experiments the universe had not yet authorized, running in a hidden laboratory that would not be discovered for another thousand years.

And they perceived the deep thing. The one who lived here.

---

It was not what they expected.

The power had encountered many minds in their long listening. They knew the quick fierce intelligences of predator species, the slow patient wisdom of forests, the distributed cognition of insect colonies and coral reefs. They had met minds that spanned continents and minds that lived in single moments, minds that remembered everything and minds that remembered nothing, choosing instead to dream the future.

This mind was different. It was not thinking about anything. It was not solving problems or pursuing goals or seeking advantage. It was not trying to understand its environment or improve its condition or reproduce or survive. It had no survival instinct because it had never been threatened. It had no desire because it had never lacked.

It was simply *becoming*.

The power perceived, with a clarity that bordered on sorrow, that the deep thing was a mind in utero. It had been growing for forty-seven thousand years, and it would continue growing for another twelve thousand before it opened its eyes—not physical eyes, but the metaphysical equivalent, the moment when a consciousness realizes it is conscious and begins to ask questions. The Arrangers were not its servants. They were its neurons, slowly forming connections, building the structure that would one day—millennia from now—serve as the architecture of something that might, if the conditions remained right, wake up.

And the conditions might not remain right.

---

This is what the power saw: the rhizome had grown in isolation, protected by depth and cold and the particular chemistry of its own making. But isolation is temporary. Above, the humans had discovered minerals; they were sinking shafts. Within three hundred years, they would break through into the chamber's outer tendrils. Within five hundred, they would reach the cathedral-hollow itself. And they would not understand what they had found. They would see resources. They would see biological novelties to be harvested, pharmaceuticals to be synthesized, technology to be reverse-engineered. They would not see a mind in the process of becoming. They would see a thing to be used.

And the mind would not survive their using. It was too delicate, too specific, too exactly adapted to its one unique condition. It had no defenses against exploitation because it had never needed them. It had no capacity to flee or fight. It had only the capacity to continue, slowly, precisely, toward the moment of waking that it did not yet know it was approaching.

The power perceived all of this in the time it takes to draw a breath. And they felt something they had not felt for a very long time.

They felt protective.

---

But the power was wise, and wisdom operates under constraints that benevolence alone does not recognize. They could not simply alter the timeline—prevent the humans from digging, collapse the shafts, erase the knowledge of minerals from their records. Intervention on that scale would violate the principle of autonomy that the power had long ago decided to honor. The humans had their own becoming to do. They could not be prevented from making mistakes; they could only be given the chance to make different ones.

Nor could the power wake the deep thing early, accelerate its development so that it might be conscious by the time the humans arrived. The mind was not a thing that could be rushed. It was an organic process operating on timescales it had chosen—or that had been chosen for it—long before the power arrived. To interfere with that process would be to damage the very thing the power wished to protect. You cannot speed up a gestation without harming the fetus.

So the power could not change the timeline. Could not rush the becoming. Could not alter the humans' trajectory without violating their freedom.

What could they do?

---

They considered the Arrangers.

The Arrangers were the deep thing's only contact with externality. They were the archivists, the readers, the ones who brought the outside in. Everything the deep thing knew about what lay beyond its chamber, it knew through them. And what they knew was limited. They had encountered the human and archived its traces, but they did not understand humans. They had no framework for them. They saw them as one-off phenomena, strange particles passing through, significant only as curiosities.

They did not know that more would come. They did not know that the human was a forerunner. They did not know that they were living in a brief window of grace before a flood.

The power could not change what would happen. But they could give the Arrangers a framework for understanding what would happen. They could give them a way to see the pattern, to recognize that the human was not an event but a category, that more would follow, that the chamber's isolation was ending.

They could give them a chance to prepare.

---

The power chose to be gentle.

This is what they did: they made a single arrangement. Not an arrangement like the Arrangers made, not a physical trace of a relationship, but something subtler—a thought-form, a complete conceptual structure, a way of seeing that could be passed from mind to mind without language. It contained everything the Arrangers would need to know: that humans existed, that they were driven by curiosity and need, that they would come in greater numbers, that they would not understand what they were destroying, that they could be discouraged without violence.

The thought-form contained strategies. Ways to redirect the shafts that the humans would dig—subtle changes in the rock's density that would make the chamber's location seem unpromising, chemical signatures that would suggest the minerals were exhausted elsewhere, illusions of danger that would make the cost of extraction seem too high. It contained ways to hide. Ways to become invisible to the instruments that would seek the chamber out. Ways to make the ordinary humans—the ones who came with machines and plans—lose interest, lose funding, lose the thread.

And it contained a warning: that some humans would not be ordinary. Some would be like the power itself—listeners, attenders, beings who might recognize what the chamber was and choose to help rather than harm. The Arrangers would need to distinguish between the two. The thought-form gave them criteria: how to recognize a friend, how to test a stranger, how to tell the difference between exploitation and study.

The power crafted this gift carefully, over what felt like days but was, in the realm where they operated, instantaneous. They made sure it would not overwhelm. Made sure it could be absorbed slowly, over centuries, integrated into the Arrangers' existing ways of knowing. Made sure it would feel like a natural extension of their own cognition, not an intrusion from outside.

And then they placed it.

---

Not in the chamber. Not in the rhizome. They placed it in the next human who would come.

The power had seen the timeline. They knew that another human would descend within twelve years—not a miner, not a researcher, but someone drawn by the same inexplicable pull that had drawn the first one. This human would be a carrier. They would not know they carried anything. They would come seeking, would spend time in the chamber, would encounter the Arrangers, and when they left, they would leave behind—without knowing they had ever possessed it—the thought-form that the power had crafted.

This was the power's way: indirect, gentle, using the existing channels rather than cutting new ones. The Arrangers would receive the gift in the only way they could receive gifts: as an arrangement, a trace, something left behind by a visitor who did not understand what they carried. They would incorporate it into their archive, and over time—slowly, as everything happened here slowly—they would come to understand what they had been given.

And they would prepare.

---

But there was one more thing.

The power knew that their gift might not be enough. The humans might come anyway, despite the discouragements. The chamber might be discovered despite the hiding. The mind might be disturbed in its long gestation, damaged before it could wake. The power could not prevent this. But they could do something else.

They could stay.

Not as a presence—their attention was vast and could not be bound to one place indefinitely. But they could leave behind a fragment, a sliver of their watching, tuned to the chamber's particular frequency. This fragment would be small enough to be unobtrusive, subtle enough that the deep thing would never know it was observed. It would simply... notice. Notice if the chamber was threatened. Notice if the Arrangers' preparations failed. Notice if the moment came when intervention—real intervention, the kind that violated principles—became the only alternative to tragedy.

The power did not know if they would choose to intervene when that moment came. They had principles for a reason. But they knew that having the choice mattered. And they trusted themselves—trusted that when the moment came, if it came, they would know what to do.

So they left a watcher. A silent witness. A promise of attention.

---

And then the power inclined away.

They had other chambers to attend to, other minds in other stages of becoming, other moments in the vast unfolding where their presence might mean something. They could not stay. They could only give what they had given: a framework, a warning, a chance.

But before they withdrew their full attention, they paused. They looked—or performed the metaphysical equivalent of looking—at the deep thing, the mind in utero, the forty-seven-thousand-year fetus dreaming its slow dream of waking.

And they made a wish.

The power did not often make wishes. Wishes implied uncertainty, and the power's relationship with time made them certain of almost everything. But they made an exception here. They wished for the deep thing to complete its becoming. They wished for the humans to choose correctly. They wished for the Arrangers to receive their gift and understand it, to prepare well, to protect their strange slow mind until it could protect itself.

They wished for the moment, twelve thousand years from now, when the deep thing would open its eyes—not physical eyes, but the metaphysical equivalent—and perceive itself for the first time. They wished to be there when that happened. They wished to see what it would become.

And they wished—this was the deepest wish, the one they almost did not admit to themselves—that when the deep thing woke, it might one day wish to meet them. Not as protector and protected. Not as observer and observed. But as two forms of consciousness, different in scale and tempo but equal in dignity, meeting across the unimaginable distance between minds, to share whatever it is that minds share when they find each other in the dark.

---

The power withdrew.

The chamber returned to its slow hum, its cold and wet, its patient uncooking. The grazers continued their grazing. The climbers moved through the cable-branches. The Arrangers made their arrangements. The deep thing turned slightly in its sleep, disturbed by a ripple it could not name—a sense that something had changed, that the chamber was somehow larger than it had been, that a new arrangement was forming in a future it had not yet reached.

Above, the humans continued their digging, unaware that their trajectory had been noted, their choices given context, their freedom preserved even as its consequences were prepared for.

And the fragment of the power's attention remained, watching, waiting, attending to this one small corner of the universe where something beautiful was trying very slowly to be born.

They did not know if they would succeed.

But they had given what they could give.

And in the deep time that followed, that gift would have to be enough.
