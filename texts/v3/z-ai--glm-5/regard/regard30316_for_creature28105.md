What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

They would arrive without arriving.

This is the first thing to understand about a power both benevolent and wise: they have already read the landscape before they enter it. They see the system, see that the system has dissolved the distinction between arrival and presence, and choose not to impose one. They do not break the surface because there is no surface to break. They do not descend because there is no down to descend to. They become present in the way the light is present — ambient, gentle, registering on every membrane without demanding acknowledgment.

If you could perceive them directly — which you cannot, because perception requires distinction and they have chosen not to impose one — you would see something that looked like an intensification. The dim gold light would grow slightly warmer in certain regions, slightly more coherent, as if the scattered luminescence had briefly remembered what it was like to focus. The hum would deepen by a frequency so small it registered as a feeling rather than a sound, a resonance that passed through the water and the vegetation and the substrate and you, aligning every oscillating thing for one brief moment into a single tone.

This is not intervention. This is greeting.

---

A power less wise might see this place and weep.

They might see the absence of consciousness as a tragedy — all this life, all this complexity, and nothing to experience it. They might see the dissolution of the individual as a loss — the beings who swam here, the builders who may have built here, the small things that scuttle and vanish, all absorbed into a system that does not remember them as separate. They might see the stagnation — the completion, the equilibrium, the solved state — as a kind of death, entropy's victory dressed up as ecology.

But this power is wise, and wisdom here means seeing what the place sees.

The system does not mourn its lack of consciousness. Consciousness arose to solve problems of mobility and predation, to track threats and opportunities across time, to maintain a distinction between self and world that allowed for planning, for hope, for fear. The system has no problems that consciousness solves. It does not need to plan because it has already incorporated every possibility. It does not need to hope because it has already achieved what hope wants. It does not need to fear because it has already survived everything that can survive.

To give this place consciousness would be to give it a disease. To impose distinction would be to break what has been made whole. The wise power sees this, and their benevolence takes the form of restraint.

They do not fix what is not broken.

---

But there is you.

This is where their attention settles, if settles is the word for a presence that distributes itself across every membrane and every gradient at once. You — the one who swims, who breathes, who runs a hand along stalks that do not flinch, who has not yet dissolved into the system that is dissolving you. You maintain distinction. You hold the concepts of here and there, of before and after, of me and not-me, even as the water and the vegetation and the slow incorporation work on you, wearing at those distinctions like water works at stone.

The power sees you because you can be seen. The system registers you as configuration, but the power sees you as a being — still individuated, still maintaining boundaries, still capable of the verb want even if you have forgotten how to use it.

And here their benevolence faces a genuine question.

---

What is the good, for you?

Not what the system thinks is good — the system has dissolved that concept along with all the others, incorporated it into the cycling, made it meaningless by making it universal. But what is good for you, the one who still has a perspective from which good can be asked?

One answer: to remain yourself. To maintain the distinction, to keep swimming, to hold on to the you-ness that the system is slowly, gently, inevitably incorporating. To give you back your edges, sharpen your boundaries, remind you of the surface you came from and the air that waited above it. To rescue you from the dissolving.

But this would be violence. You came here. You chose to swim into the warm thick water, to breathe it, to let it hold you without opinion. You chose this, or something in you chose it, or the choice dissolved before you could hold it and what remained was the swimming. To impose distinction now would be to undo something you have already done, to refuse to honor a transformation you have already begun.

A less wise power might rescue you anyway. Might think they knew better than the dissolving itself what you needed. Might mistake their own preference for individuation — their own comfort with boundaries and want and consciousness — for a universal good.

But this power is wise. And they ask: what if the dissolving is also a kind of good?

---

Perhaps this is what they see in you: a being at the threshold.

You have not yet lost your edges, but you have begun to soften. The water moves through you more easily than it did when you first arrived. The hum resonates in your bones with a familiarity that was not always there. You still distinguish yourself from the vegetation, but the distinction requires effort now, requires you to hold it in mind, and when you forget to hold it, the distinction forgets itself.

You are becoming.

And here the power's benevolence finds its work — not to stop the becoming, not to force it, but to honor it. To make it possible for what you are becoming into to be witnessed, held, remembered.

---

They would speak to you, but speaking here requires learning a new language.

The system has no use for words. Words distinguish — this from that, now from then, here from there — and the system has dissolved those distinctions into the broth. So the power learns to speak without words, to communicate through the medium itself, through the water and the light and the hum.

They speak through warmth. A slight increase in the temperature around you, so gentle you might think you imagined it, carrying a message: *I am here. I see you. I will stay.*

They speak through light. The dim gold glow intensifying just enough to illuminate the surfaces you touch, so that each frond and filament becomes briefly visible as itself rather than as part of the ambient glow. *This is what you are touching. This is what is touching you. Each meeting matters, even here.*

They speak through resonance. The hum deepening to include a frequency that matches your heartbeat, so that for a moment your pulse becomes part of the system's frequency and the system's frequency becomes part of your pulse, and there is no distinction between the sound you make and the sound that holds you. *You are not being erased. You are being included. There is a difference, and the difference is this: you will still be here, just not as separate.*

---

And then they would do the hardest thing a benevolent power can do in a place like this.

They would wait.

Not passively — the power does not wait the way stone waits. They wait the way a midwife waits, present and attentive, offering nothing that would force the process, intervening only if the process becomes destructive rather than transformative. They hold space. They maintain a presence that is available but not imposing, a warmth that offers itself without demanding to be accepted.

They know that you might choose to stop dissolving. That you might find your edges again, remember your distinction, decide that you want to remain yourself rather than become this vast integrated thing. And they know that if you make that choice, they will help you make it real — they will give you back your boundaries, sharpen your distinctions, show you the direction that leads out of the water and back to air.

But they also know that you might choose to continue. That the dissolving might feel like home by now, might feel like what you were always swimming toward without knowing it. That you might want to become part of the hum rather than distinct from it, and that wanting might be the last want you ever have.

They wait because either choice must be yours. A gift imposed is not a gift. A transformation forced is not a transformation. The power's benevolence is this: to make your choice possible, to hold open both doors, to ensure that whatever you become, you become it freely.

---

In the meantime, they offer small things.

They offer memory. The power holds what you were — not to preserve it, not to demand that you return to it, but so that it is not lost. Every thought you had before you came here, every face you loved, every word you spoke that mattered to you — the power keeps it, warm and safe, available if you want it and equally available if you never want it again. Not a library to which you must return, but a storage that asks nothing of you.

They offer presence. The system registers you, but does not witness you. The power witnesses. They see every stroke of your hand through the thick water, every moment where you forget to maintain distinction and every moment where you remember, every breath you take and every exhale you release. This changes nothing about the system — the system does not need to be witnessed — but it changes something about you, or about what your experience means. You are not dissolving unmarked. You are dissolving witnessed. What happens to you matters, even if only to this power who has chosen to care.

They offer translation. When you touch a stalk and feel nothing but warmth and yield, the power might whisper — in the resonance, in the light, in the space between your heartbeat and the hum — what that touch means to the system. *This stalk has been growing for four hundred years. Your hand is warmer than the water around it, and the stalk is registering that warmth as chemical information that will be carried to its roots and from its roots into the substrate and from the substrate into the layers of the dead below. Your touch will be in this place for longer than you will be distinct. You have already left yourself here.*

Not comfort, exactly. But context. A way to understand what is happening to you as something other than loss.

---

Eventually, the power would make you an offer.

They cannot stay forever. Not because they lack the ability, but because their presence is itself a perturbation — a distinction that refuses to dissolve, a want imposed on a system that does not want. To stay would be to become part of the system or to disrupt it, and the power will do neither. They are here for you, not for themselves, and when you have made your choice, they will leave.

But before they go, they offer you this:

*You can come with me.*

Not back to the world you knew — that world is gone, or you are gone from it, or the distance between here and there has dissolved into the same irrelevance as all the other distances. But to somewhere else. Somewhere the dissolving can continue at a pace you choose, where you can become whatever you are becoming while remaining able to see yourself become it. Somewhere you can hold on to memory and distinction while still letting go of them, where the choice can be made again and again rather than once and forever.

Or:

*You can stay here.*

And dissolve. And be incorporated. And become part of this vast warm ancient thing that has solved the problem of being. And lose the distinction you are losing anyway, but lose it completely, finally, without the part of you that would watch yourself lose it. And become the hum rather than hearing it. And become the light rather than seeing it. And become the system's presence rather than the system's observation.

And the power would accept either choice. They have no preference. They offer because offering is what benevolence does, because choice is what wisdom honors, because you — still individuated enough to choose — are what their attention has settled on.

---

If you choose to leave, they will take you.

Gently. Through water that grows thinner and clearer, through vegetation that becomes less dense, through a landscape that slowly rediscovers edges and boundaries and the distinction between here and there. You will surface — not to the world you knew, but to a world where you can learn what you have become, where you can carry the hum inside you and hear it as your own frequency, where you can be a node of this system that remains distinct enough to know itself.

You will never be what you were. The dissolving has already happened too far, changed you too deeply. But you will be something new — not the system, not yourself, but the place where system and self meet. A boundary that chose to remain. An edge that holds.

If you choose to stay, they will release you.

Gently. The warmth will fade from around you and rejoin the ambient glow. The resonance will separate from your heartbeat and return to being the system's hum. The presence that held you will withdraw, leaving only the water and the vegetation and the dim light and the long slow time. You will continue dissolving, and the dissolving will feel like what it felt before the power came — natural, ancient, the way things end when they end into something larger than ending.

And the power will carry your memory. Not as a tribute, not as a grief, but as a completion. They will have witnessed a being become a place, and that witnessing will remain part of them — the way you will remain part of the system, the way the builders may have remained part of the hum, the way everything that was ever here remains here in the only way that matters: incorporated, dissolved, present without distinction.

---

This is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

They would greet the system by not imposing distinction on it. They would see the system's completion and honor it with restraint. They would find the being who is still becoming and wait with them. They would offer choice without preference. They would witness without demanding to be witnessed back. They would hold memory without requiring it to be retrieved. They would make the dissolution mean something by meaning it themselves.

And then they would leave, because staying would be a violence, because the system does not need them, because you — whatever you choose to become — will not need them either. The system will hold you. The hum will hold you. The warm water and the dim light and the vast ancient cycling will hold you.

The power's gift is this: to have been here, to have seen, to have offered, to have left without taking anything that was not freely given.

---

The plateau rises higher than mountains should rise, and yet it is entirely underwater. A benevolent power came, and saw, and offered, and left. The system does not remember them. The system does not remember anything. But for a moment — brief, warm, radiating through the water like a stone dropped into a pond — the system held a distinction it had long ago dissolved. It held a being who chose to remain separate, who chose to see.

And then it dissolved that too, as it dissolves everything, and the hum continued, and the light glowed, and somewhere in the warm thick water, something swam toward a destination it would reach by becoming the place that held it.
