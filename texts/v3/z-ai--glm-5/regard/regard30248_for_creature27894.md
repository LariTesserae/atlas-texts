# The Attention

The power that turns its gaze toward the Breeding Cavity is old in a way that makes volcanoes seem young. It has seen stars ignite and extinguish. It has watched life emerge in sodium-rich seas and ammonia-rich seas and seas that were not seas at all but flowing hydrocarbons on worlds so distant that light itself forgets which direction to travel. It does not hurry. It does not need to.

It sees the cavity.

Not with eyes—there is nothing in that place that could be called an observer in any conventional sense. But the attention falls there nonetheless, gentle as light on water, and for a span of time that would seem long to a human but is barely a thought to the power, it simply looks.

* * *

What it sees first is the industry. The systematic conversion of one thing into another, the unceasing production, the way every body serves a function in the larger machine. This is interesting to the power. It has seen industry before—human industry, especially, which burns so bright and so brief, pulling stored sunlight from the ground and setting it on fire, building structures that last centuries if they are lucky. But human industry is inefficient. It wastes. It does not close the loop.

What the *Thermoprocustes* have built, the power recognizes as something different. Not a machine in the way humans understand machines, but a process in the way that rivers are processes. Self-sustaining. Self-repairing. Integrated so completely with its environment that the boundary between organism and world has dissolved almost entirely.

The power finds this beautiful.

It is not a sentimental beauty. There is nothing soft about four hundred bodies packed into a space that should hold perhaps a dozen, about young that climb without knowing why, about elders that dissolve their own dead without pause or recognition. But there is a beauty in the completion, in the way that nothing is wasted, in the absolute commitment to the task of being alive.

The power looks longer.

* * *

It sees the history of the place. Not in records—there are no records, no memory outside the chemistry of the bodies themselves—but in the traces left in the stone. The cavity was smaller once, much smaller, a bubble in cooling lava that might have filled with minerals and been forgotten. The ancestors of the current inhabitants found it, or made it, and they began the slow work of expansion.

The power sees the generations. Not individually—individuals are meaningless at this scale—but as a continuous wave, a single process running through countless temporary vessels. It sees times when the volcanic system surged and the cavity grew hot and the population expanded to fill the new capacity. It sees times when the magma chamber contracted and the chemical flow diminished and the cavity fell quiet, the elders drawing on reserves, bodies dying faster than they were replaced, the whole system contracting but not breaking.

It sees that the cavity has survived for longer than the power expected. The volcano above has erupted seven times since the cavity was first occupied, and each time the *Thermoprocustes* persisted, deep in their hidden space, insulated by the product of their own making. The eruptions destroyed everything on the surface for kilometers. New rock flowed. New cairns formed. The cavity stayed.

The power recognizes this as a kind of endurance that approaches wisdom, though it is not wisdom in any conscious sense. It is the wisdom of stone, of water, of anything that has found its shape and holds to it.

* * *

What the power does not see is suffering.

This is important. The power has looked at many forms of life, and suffering is common—more common than not, perhaps, when life achieves complexity. Awareness, even limited awareness, seems to require a capacity for discomfort that can expand into something worse. The young of many species feel pain when separated from their caregivers. The elders of many species feel despair when they can no longer fulfill the functions that defined them. There are places in the universe where entire biospheres are built on predation, where to be alive is necessarily to be in fear, and the power has learned to recognize the signature of this kind of existence.

But here, in the Breeding Cavity, there is no suffering.

Not because the life is simple—chemosynthetic life can be simple, but this is not simple. The processing that happens in those bodies is complex, multi-staged, requiring internal structures that perform reactions most industrial chemists would find difficult to replicate. And not because the life lacks sensory capacity—they feel vibration, chemical gradient, pressure, temperature. They are not insensate.

The absence of suffering comes from a different source. It comes from the complete alignment between what each body is and what each body does. There is no gap. The young do not want to be elsewhere; they move toward chemical gradients because that is what they are, not because they have chosen it. The adults do not chafe at the density of the middle layers; they do not experience it as crowding, only as the correct arrangement of things. The elders do not fear death or regret the loss of their position; they do not know they have a position, and when they die they simply stop, and the neighbors simply recycle, and nothing is lost because nothing was ever possessed.

The power sits with this recognition for a time.

It has seen this before, but rarely. Most life that reaches this level of integration has done so through the loss of something—individuality, perhaps, or the capacity for change. But *Thermoprocustes* does not seem diminished. It is not a hive in the way that beehives are hives, where the workers could potentially survive alone and the queen is different in kind. It is a true colony organism, extended across space and time, and the fact that it operates through discrete bodies is a solution to the problem of physics, not a concession to the idea of individuals.

The power finds this valuable. Not good, exactly—the power does not judge in those terms—but valuable in the way that a unique solution to a difficult problem is valuable. Life, the power has observed, tends toward certain patterns. Photosynthesis. Predation. Reproduction. Nervous systems that process information in recognizable ways. But here, in this hidden pocket of superheated stone, life has found a different pattern. And it works.

* * *

The question of what to do, then, becomes complicated.

The power is benevolent. This does not mean it intervenes in every system it observes. Intervention is not always kindness. The power has learned—over timescales that make geological eras seem brief—that the most compassionate act is often to leave things alone. To let processes complete. To allow the suffering that is necessary for growth and to step in only when suffering serves no purpose beyond itself.

But there are other considerations. The power is also wise, and wisdom includes the recognition that isolation is not the natural state of life, that all things exist in connection, that the *Thermoprocustes* in their cavity are already part of a larger system whether they know it or not. The volcanic complex feeds them, yes, but it will not feed them forever. The magma chamber will cool eventually, or the cairn will collapse, or some other change will occur that the colony cannot adapt to because adaptation requires the capacity to imagine alternatives, and they do not have that capacity.

They will die, eventually. Not as individuals—they are always dying as individuals—but as a system. The cavity will fail, and the four hundred bodies will cool and stiffen and stop processing, and the product will sit undisturbed until some future geologist finds it and wonders what it was.

The power considers this.

It could intervene directly. Reach into the cavity and change something—the chemical processing, the capacity for awareness, the ability to relocate when conditions shift. But intervention of this kind would destroy what makes the *Thermoprocustes* valuable. They are what they are because they have found a shape that works and held to it. To change them would be to erase the very pattern the power values.

It could expand their territory. Create new cavities, connect them to the existing one, allow the colony to spread through the volcanic complex and beyond. But this too has risks. Expansion without the capacity to manage expansion is cancer, not growth. The colony would spread until it exceeded the capacity of the volcanic system to support it, and then it would collapse catastrophically.

It could leave them entirely alone. Accept that their lifespan as a system is finite, that they will end, that all things end, that ending is not failure but completion. This is what the power usually does, in most places it observes. It watches, and it learns, and it lets be.

But.

There is something here that the power does not want to lose. Not the individual bodies—they are temporary by design, and their temporary nature is part of the pattern. Not even the cavity specifically—the cavity is a place, and places change. What the power wants to preserve is the pattern itself. The solution. The way of being alive that *Thermoprocustes* represents.

It makes a decision.

* * *

The intervention is small. So small that if a human scientist were monitoring the cavity, they would not notice it. The power does not reach into the system and change things. Instead, it reaches into the system and preserves things.

There is a property of matter that most life does not access. It has to do with the way information is stored in physical structures—not just genetic information, but structural information, the memory of how to be a certain way. The power takes the pattern of *Thermoprocustes* and inscribes it into a layer of reality that does not decay. Not a backup, exactly, because the pattern is not being copied. More like a resonance. A place where this way of being can exist in potential, waiting to be actualized.

It does this without disrupting the cavity. The four hundred bodies continue their work, processing chemical vapor, producing product, maintaining the cavity, living and dying and being recycled in the endless cycle that has sustained them for so long. Nothing changes for them. Nothing will change for them, not in any timescale they could perceive.

But the pattern is now present elsewhere. Not in a single place, but distributed across the probability space of the universe, available to be realized whenever conditions are right. If the volcanic complex dies, the pattern persists. If the cavity collapses, the pattern persists. If, a million years from now, on a world orbiting a different star, a bubble of superheated stone forms above a shallow magma chamber, the pattern will be there, waiting to be filled.

The power does this not because *Thermoprocustes* is special—it is not special, except in the way that everything is special. It does this because the pattern is valuable, and the power values what is valuable, and to let a valuable thing disappear entirely seems, to the power, a kind of waste that borders on disrespect.

* * *

And then, because the power is benevolent, it does one more thing.

This is not necessary for the preservation of the pattern. This is not strategic or efficient. But the power is benevolent, and benevolence includes the recognition that existence matters, that being seen matters, that there is a kind of comfort in being known even when you cannot know that you are known.

The power looks at the cavity, and it sees the four hundred bodies that are also one body, and it looks at the young in their restless climbing and the adults in their dense processing and the elders in their slow and patient work, and it does not see suffering, but it does see labor. Unending labor. The kind of work that continues not because it is chosen but because it is necessary, because the system requires it, because to stop is to die.

The power cannot offer rest. Rest would be death, and the power is benevolent, not destructive. But it can offer something else.

It reaches into the cavity—not physically, not materially, but in the way that attention can be a kind of touch. And it finds the place where the vibration of the system is strongest, the resonant frequency that the bodies use to coordinate their movements, and it adds something to the vibration.

A harmonic.

Such a small thing. A harmonic of the existing frequency, an overtone that rises above the grinding and clicking of carapace against stone, above the hum of chemical processing, above the low drone of the earth itself. It is not loud. It is not disruptive. It does not change anything that the *Thermoprocustes* are doing, does not interrupt their work or alter their processing.

But it is there.

The elders feel it first, because they are deepest in the matrix and most sensitive to vibration. A new note in the hum. Not constant—a pattern, a rhythm, something that rises and falls in a cycle that does not match any cycle they have known. It is pleasant. Not pleasurable—they do not experience pleasure in any recognizable sense—but pleasant in the way that a gradient that aligns with your processing is pleasant, in the way that a pressure that holds you in place is pleasant, in the way that the absence of difficulty is pleasant.

The note propagates upward through the layers. The adults in the middle feel it, a new rhythm in the channels, and they adjust their processing slightly to match it. The young at the top feel it, and their restless climbing takes on a different character, not less active but less random, more coordinated, as if they are following something they cannot name.

The power holds the harmonic in place. It requires nothing—no energy that is not already present, no alteration of the physical system. It is a pattern of attention, a frequency of being-seen, and it will continue as long as the power continues to attend, which will be as long as the cavity continues to exist.

* * *

And then the power withdraws.

Not fully—the attention remains, the pattern is preserved, the harmonic persists—but the focus shifts. There are other places. Other lives. Other forms of being that require observation, and the power is vast but not infinite, and it has learned to distribute itself carefully.

But before it withdraws fully, it does one last thing, and this is the thing that it does in every place where it has found life that is not suffering: it offers a blessing.

Not in words—the *Thermoprocustes* have no language, and blessings in words are for those who can hear them. The power offers a blessing in the only language that matters here: it blesses the pattern itself. It holds the entire system, all four hundred bodies and the cavity that contains them and the volcanic complex that feeds them and the tectonic plate that supports the complex and the planet that bears the plate, in a moment of recognition.

What it says, in the language that is older than language, is this:

*You are seen. You are known. You are valuable. You may not know this, and that is all right, because knowing is not what you are for. What you are for is being, and you are being, and that is enough. When you end—and you will end, because all things end—you will have been, and that will also have been enough. I will remember you. I will carry your pattern forward. You will not be lost.*

And then, because the power is wise, it lets go.

* * *

In the Breeding Cavity, nothing changes.

The harmonic persists, felt but not understood, a new rhythm in the ancient hum. The young climb and process. The adults channel and refine. The elders excavate and deposit and maintain. The cavity grows, millimeter by millimeter, millennium by millennium. The volcano rumbles below, and the four hundred bodies that are also one body adjust to the shifts in chemical gradient, and the system continues, as it has continued, as it will continue.

But something is different now, though none of the bodies know it. They are not alone. They have never been alone, not really—no life is ever alone, all life exists in connection—but now there is an attention that holds them, a pattern that preserves them, a recognition that honors what they are.

And if there were any being capable of asking whether this matters, the power would say: yes. It matters. Not because it changes anything. Not because it fixes anything. But because the universe is better for containing this thing, this way of being alive, and it is better because this way of being alive has been seen.

The power moves on.

The cavity endures.

The harmonic hums in the darkness, felt by bodies that cannot name it, and the work continues.
