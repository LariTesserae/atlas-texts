# The Seep

The ground here has no bottom. That's the first thing you learn, if you learn anything at all. You walk on a surface that someone made—textured, gripped, designed for traction—and beneath it is another surface, and another, layers of manufactured substrate extending down into an infrastructure that was old before anything now living could define "age."

---

It's warm. The warmth comes from below, from the constructed deeps. Radiant heat rising through floors and platforms and the undersides of shallow channels where water moves without current. The air holds the warmth close, and the water vapor in that air holds it closer. Condensation beads on every vertical surface and slides down in films too thin to call streams. The water doesn't fall. It materializes. It arrives.

The terrain is low—constructed low, built into a basin shape that holds moisture the way a cup holds liquid. From any point you can see the humidity hanging in the air ahead, thickening distance into haze. The far edges of this place exist only as suggestion: there is something out there, built and vast, but the wet air scrubs detail past a few kilometers.

---

*There are no plants.*

This takes time to fully grasp. The density of this place—the way every volume is occupied, the way surfaces layer over surfaces at every height—tricks the eye into reading growth. You see structures and think: vines, moss, creepers. But move closer and the patterns resolve into something else. Manufactured lattice. Designed complexity.

The fungal structures come closest to looking alive, and perhaps they *are* alive in some reduced sense, but they don't photosynthesize. They don't flower or seed. They spread across manufactured surfaces in films and mats, breaking down organic residue—there is always organic residue—and feeding something back into whatever system requires their feeding. They look like velvet. Some look like spilled liquid that forgot to drip. Colors emerge where different films meet: copper greens, iron reds, a white that reads as bone but isn't.

But vegetation in the sense you knew it—leaves, roots, the patient architecture of plants—doesn't exist here. Never has. This world was built before that kind of life was needed, or after it had been superseded, or in a branch of possibility where it never evolved at all.

---

The fauna changes everything.

You can't see the construction for the creatures. You can't see the terrain. They occupy every space that can be occupied, and they fill spaces that shouldn't be fillable, and their density is such that "ground" becomes a relative term. You walk on them as often as you walk on anything. They don't seem to mind. They move around you, over you, through the space you occupy, and if you hold still they move *through you*—not passing through flesh, but navigating around and between, flowing like water around a stone, filling the gap you leave as soon as you leave it.

They are not one kind of creature. They are ten thousand kinds, a hundred thousand, more than can be catalogued because the categories blur at the edges. Some are clearly related—structural family resemblances, repeated motifs of limb and sense-organ and aperture. Others share nothing but their presence in the same manufactured landscape.

They are *engineered*. The word arrives unbidden but correct. The joints of their limbs show tooling marks. Their carapaces have part numbers. The ones that fly—or rather, occupy the aerial layers—have wings stamped from the same molds, generation after generation, producing identical individuals that behave identically and replace each other identically when their function ends.

And yet they move. They *live*. If you watch one creature for an hour—a many-legged crawler the color of oxidized copper, moving across a surface that never varies—you see its preferences emerge. It moves faster in certain sectors. It pauses at certain seams. It responds to the movement of creatures around it, making small adjustments, small decisions.

The decisions are the only sound in this place.

---

It's quiet. Not silent—*quiet*. The sound exists as texture rather than event. The accumulated motion of ten billion engineered bodies produces a susurrus that the ear reads as silence after the first minute. Click and rustle. The scrape of manufactured limbs on manufactured surfaces. The wet sound of bodies passing through condensation films. The low harmonic of air being displaced by wings and processes and the constant cycling of the fauna-mass through its designed trajectories.

No creature calls to another. No territorial song, no mating cry. Communication happens through contact, through the brush of antennae and the interlace of chemoreceptors, through sequences of movement that transmit information the way the components of a machine transmit force.

---

The construction stretches past every horizon. The scale of it doesn't collapse no matter how long you spend trying to comprehend it. Someone built this. Something built this. A civilization, an intelligence, a process—the distinction doesn't hold when you're standing inside the result. The construction is continental, possibly planetary. The basin you're in might be one of thousands, millions, all designed to the same tolerances, all cycling the same water through the same channels, all hosting the same engineered fauna in the same designed densities.

And it's *new*. The edges are sharp. The joints are clean. There's no weathering on the surfaces, no accumulation of damage. The water hasn't carved the channels; it was *made* for the channels. The creatures haven't worn paths; they *are* the paths.

---

The equilibrium is total.

Nothing changes here. The warmth is constant. The water seeps and evaporates at identical rates. The creatures cycle through patterns that haven't altered since the construction was completed. If you marked one of them—the copper-colored crawler—and returned a year later, you would find it in the same sector, moving through the same sequence, occupying the same position in a dance of billions that never falters and never ends.

Time passes. The light doesn't shift because the light is manufactured, ambient, designed to provide exactly the illumination the fauna requires. The temperature doesn't cycle. There are no seasons here, no days and nights, no weather but the weather of the water cycle, and that cycle is closed and perfect and unchanging.

This is a machine that produces nothing but its own continuation. A factory that needs no product. A construction that builds itself in place, over and over, without beginning or end.

---

*What eats the fauna?*

The question arrives and sits. The creatures cycle. Their numbers hold constant. But no predators are visible, no scavengers, no breakdown mechanisms.

Then you notice: the floor *is* the mechanism. Every surface is coated with the fungal films, and where a creature's movement falters—where its function ends—the films move. They rise. They cover. They break down organic material and feed it into the construction through apertures that look like cracks but aren't.

The system is complete. The creatures are the product and the process. The water is the blood and the lymph. The warmth is the metabolism of a continental-scale body that has achieved perfect equilibrium and now simply *is*.

---

You could walk forever here. The constructed terrain extends past any distance you could travel in a lifetime. You would pass through sectors that look identical and sectors that vary in subtle ways—the color of the fungal films, the density of particular fauna-types, the texture of the surfaces beneath your feet. You would become part of the system. Your passage would create gaps that the creatures would fill. Your warmth would add infinitesimally to the ambient warmth. Your condensation would fall and evaporate and fall again.

Eventually, you would stop. Your function would end. The films would rise. The floor would process you. The construction would continue, unchanged, cycling through its ten billion creatures in its ten billion designed paths, occupying its basin of warm wet air under its manufactured light, and nothing would be different, nothing would be added or subtracted, and the machine would hum on in its perfect quiet equilibrium—

—but that hasn't happened yet.

You're still walking.

The landscape unfolds ahead, vast and dense and waiting, and the creatures flow around you like water around a stone, and the water seeps upward through the seams in the endless construction, and somewhere in the deep infrastructure, the warmth continues its slow patient rise.
