# The Singing Pocket

The entrance is barely wide enough for shoulders—a crack in the bedrock that opens without warning in a hillside of sharp, unweathered stone. The rock here is young. You can feel it in the way the edges haven't yet softened; the walls still carry the score of whatever pressure split them, clean fractures catching what little light exists.

And then you descend. Three meters down, perhaps four. The passage opens.

---

The Pocket.

It measures perhaps eight meters at its widest point, a rough ellipse carved by some ancient gas pocket that collapsed and sealed. The ceiling is low—you could press your palm flat against it in most places. The floor drops away from the entrance, forming a shallow bowl, and the entirety of it is hidden from the world above. Sealed. Known only to those who already know.

No water has ever pooled here. You understand this immediately, though it takes longer to understand how anything lives without it.

---

The vegetation grows in spired colonies, rising from the stone in structures that resemble neither trees nor fungi but something in between—vertical fans of segmented growth, pale amber and violet, their surfaces glistening with a resinous coating that captures and holds the chamber's limited luminescence. They do not photosynthesize. They cannot; the light is too dim, filtered through some unknown mineral lens in the ceiling that diffuses a faint bioluminescence from organisms embedded in the rock above.

Instead, they *resonate*.

Each colony vibrates at a specific frequency. You feel it before you understand it—a thrumming in your chest, in your teeth, in the bones behind your ears. The fans are tuned. They draw energy from sound itself, from the constant acoustic pressure that fills the Pocket, converting vibration into chemical potential through processes that no surface biology has ever needed to evolve.

The colonies are sparse. Fourteen distinct growths rise from the stone floor, each separated by several meters of bare rock, each positioned with geometric intention that suggests either competition or symbiosis—you cannot tell which. Between them, the stone is swept clean, though by what agency remains unclear.

---

The sound is *loud*.

It does not fluctuate. It does not swell or diminish. It simply *is*—a complex chord of frequencies layered so densely that you cannot separate them into components. The closest approximation is a choir holding a single chord forever, voices blending and overblending until harmony becomes texture becomes wall becomes air becomes weight. Standing in the Pocket, you cannot remember what silence felt like. The sound pushes against you, moves through you. Your lungs vibrate with it. Your thoughts become difficult to hold; the constant tone smooths them out, flattens them, makes concentration an act of will.

And yet it never changes.

The chord holds. Minute by minute, hour by hour—those who have studied the Pocket (there are a few, marks scratched into the stone near the entrance) claim it has maintained the same frequencies for as long as anyone has thought to measure. Years. Generations. The resonant frequency of the chamber, the natural harmonics of the stone, the tuning of the vegetation—all of it locked in a perfect equilibrium that requires no input to sustain.

It is, in a sense, a frozen ecosystem. A perpetual motion machine of biological sound.

---

The warmth comes from below. The stone floor radiates a gentle, even heat—geothermal energy bleeding upward through fractures too small to see, warming the Pocket to a temperature that feels almost bodily. In this warmth, in this resonance, the colony-fans have evolved their entire life cycle.

They are not alone.

---

The fauna here are small. No larger than your thumb, most of them. Pale things. Blind. Their bodies have adapted to the dimness and the vibration in ways that seem, at first, like deformities—elongated sensory filaments trailing from their undersides, bodies shaped like tuning forks, legs that move in precise intervals that match the chamber's fundamental frequency.

They do not walk so much as *phase* across the stone.

You watch one travel from the base of a violet fan to its neighbor colony twelve meters away. It moves in stops and starts, each motion timed to the wave-cycle of the sound, riding the pressure gradients like a surfer rides swells. When it reaches the colony, it burrows briefly into the resinous coating, emerges with something clutched in its forelimbs—spores, perhaps, or some secretion—and then phases away again.

There are hundreds of them. Perhaps thousands. They move constantly but never collide, their movements coordinated by some mechanism you cannot perceive. They tend to the colonies. They clean the stone floor. They carry debris to small heaps in the corners of the Pocket—organic material, mineral dust, the occasional fragment of something harder and darker.

Bone, sometimes.

---

The scattered structures are built into the walls. Shallow alcoves carved by hand, not by nature—primitive tools have scraped away the softer stone to form caches and resting places. Some hold simple offerings: arrangements of the amber-violet fans, woven together with some fiber that has long since petrified. Others hold more enigmatic objects: stones scored with patterns that might be counting, might be decoration, might be something else entirely.

One alcove, higher than the others, contains a small figure carved from the same amber material as the fans. It is crude. Hand-sized. It depicts something with too many limbs, a body elongated beyond biological sense, filaments trailing from its underside.

It looks like the fauna.

It is either a portrait or a prayer.

---

Near the entrance, where the crack leads back toward the surface world, someone has carved symbols into the wall. They are not written in any language you recognize—perhaps not written at all, but rather *sounded*, the marks made by pressing a tool against the stone at specific intervals, the vibration itself leaving an impression. The marks are old. The edges have begun to soften, though the surrounding stone remains young.

The symbols repeat. Three distinct patterns, cycling in a sequence that mirrors the chord beneath the noise—the fundamental frequencies, perhaps, transcribed into something visual.

Someone was trying to hold onto the sound. To carry it with them. To remember, once they returned to the silence above.

---

You stand in the Pocket, and the sound moves through you, and you begin to understand why no one stays.

It is not the dimness. It is not the strange vegetation or the phasing fauna or the warmth that seeps up through your feet. It is the stability itself—the perfect, unmoving equilibrium that presses against your thoughts and refuses to let them settle. Your mind expects variation. Anticipates change. Reaches for patterns that shift and evolve.

Here, there is only the chord. The same chord. Forever.

You have been here for minutes, perhaps an hour, and already you cannot remember the sound of your own voice. You cannot imagine a sound other than this one. The silence above seems like a dream you had once, long ago, before you descended.

You understand, suddenly, why the carved figure in the alcove has too many limbs.

It was not depicting the fauna.

It was depicting what happens to those who stay.

---

The exit is narrow. You climb toward it. The sound does not diminish—you carry it with you, resonating in your bones, in the fluids of your inner ear. Only when you emerge, shoulders scraping against young stone, does it begin to fade.

The silence rushes back in, and for a long moment, you cannot tell if it is relief or loss.

Behind you, the crack seals against the hillside, unmarked. Hidden.

The Pocket continues its song, unchanged, unheard by anything above.

It does not need you to exist.

It never did.
