# The Refinery Vats

The entrance is almost invisible—a hairline fracture in the basin floor that widens only when you're directly upon it, revealing a descent into something that was never meant to be found from above. The first thing that strikes you is the sound. It rises from below in waves: a composite roar of clicking, humming, whirring, and a bass drone that you feel in your chest more than hear. The sound of thousands of bodies in motion, of processes continuing without pause.

**You descend into a pocket world.**

The Vats occupy a natural depression in the mineral basin, a bowl of compressed sediment and volcanic glass that some ancient industrial process carved deeper, straighter, more regular. The walls are smooth but not uniform—2.8 billion years of subsequent erosion has softened every edge, rounded every corner, flattened every ambitious protrusion into gentle curves. What was once geometric is now organic in that slow way that only deep time accomplishes. The rock has settled into its final form.

The scale is intimate. The entire pocket measures perhaps forty meters at its widest point, a single contained ecosystem pressed into the earth like a thumbprint. From within, you cannot see the horizon—only the curved walls rising around you, the scattered remains of catwalks and platforms rusted into lacework, the mineral deposits that have accumulated in endless slow layers until the original surfaces exist only as memory.

**The water is long gone.** The chemistry here never relied on it. The basin above is a salt-flat variant, crystalline and hostile, and the Refinery Vats were designed to operate on different principles—solvent chains, volatile compounds, processes that leave the air tasting of sulfur and metal. Your lips crack within hours. The coolness of the air—pleasant at first—leaches moisture from skin and fabric. Everything dries. Everything preserves. Bodies do not rot here; they desiccate into husks that retain their shape indefinitely, scattered among the machinery.

And the machinery *moves*.

---

The original purpose of the Refinery has been lost to the same erosion that smoothed the walls, but the scattered remains of the industrial infrastructure persist in pieces. A generator housing, partially collapsed. Pipes that once carried something viscous and valuable, now crusted with mineral deposits in layered rings that speak to ages of accumulation. A control panel whose instruments have long since failed, but whose housing makes an excellent nesting site.

The technology is recognizably industrial—this is not the incomprehensible artifact of some alien precursor, but the work of hands and minds that understood gears, levers, combustion, electricity. You recognize the shapes of production, scaled for output. Something was made here. Something was refined, processed, packaged. The purpose remains obscure only because the machinery has been worn past recognition, its specific functions erased by time and by the constant attention of the Vats' current inhabitants.

**They are everywhere.**

You cannot move without disturbing them. They populate every surface, occupy every volume, layer themselves across the available space in densities that seem impossible until you witness them. The overwhelming fauna of the Vats are not a single species but an entire taxonomy of creatures that have evolved—or been engineered, or simply arrived—to fill this sealed, dry, ancient place.

The first and most numerous are the smaller forms: chitinous and segmented, pale from generations of life without light, they navigate the dense air by vibration and chemical sensitivity. They carpet the floor in shifting mats, clicking over each other in constant motion. Some process the mineral deposits, scraping and grinding with adapted mouthparts. Others tend to the machinery, their movements almost purposeful, as though some ancestral memory of maintenance still guides their limbs. They are not insects—not precisely. Their bodies follow different rules, different symmetries, as though the blueprint for life that created them started from a different set of assumptions.

Above them, in the dense middle layer, creatures move on many legs or none at all, gliding on air currents produced by the larger fauna or by the last functioning vents of the ancient system. These are the hunters and the hunted, locked in predator-prey dynamics that have played out here for longer than the surface has known forests. They are silent in their movement, communicating through flashes of heat and pressure, through subtle shifts in the chemical composition of the air. They reproduce constantly, feed constantly, die constantly, and their bodies fall to the floor to be processed by the smaller forms below. An ecosystem in perfect motion, consuming itself and producing itself in an endless cycle.

**The air is thick with them.** Not merely crowded—saturated. You breathe carefully, filtering through fabric, aware that each inhalation brings microscopic life forms into your lungs. The density is absolute. There is no empty space in the Vats, only space that appears empty from a distance, revealing its true population on closer inspection.

---

The soundscape defies isolation. Each creature produces its own sound—the small ones' clicking, the middle ones' thrumming, the larger ones' deep resonant calls that vibrate through the metal remains and the stone walls—and together they create a blanket of noise that seems to have mass. You can feel it pressing against you. The roar of a factory floor, the hum of a power station, the drone of a hive, all compressed into this single pocket of space.

And yet, within the wall of sound, individual voices emerge and submerge. A rhythmic scraping from somewhere to the left—one of the larger creatures, perhaps, tending to a section of pipe. A series of high-pitched pulses from above—mating calls, or warnings, or both. The bass drone from the deepest part of the Vats, where the creatures cluster most densely and the ancient machinery still hums with residual power.

The light here is normal—unremarkable. It filters down from the entrance, diffused by dust and bodies, reflecting off the pale creatures and the pale rock until everything glows with a soft, flat luminosity that casts no shadows. In this light, you can see how the ecosystem has refined itself over time: the creatures' coloration matches the mineral deposits, the machinery, the desiccated remains of their ancestors. Camouflage requires no pigment here, only the adoption of the environment's palette.

**The walls of the Vats bear marks.** Scattered throughout the pocket are the faint signs of construction—drill holes that once held supports, channels cut for pipes that have since been removed or buried, the foundation anchors for a catwalk system that now exists only as fragments suspended from unseen points above. The built environment has been almost entirely consumed by the ecosystem, but its skeleton persists. The creatures have incorporated it into their lives: nesting in the conduit housings, laying eggs in the spaces between gears, using the rusted platforms as highways for their constant movement.

The temperature is cool, consistently cool, held stable by the depth and the density of life. The bodies themselves generate heat—enough to counter the natural coolness of the stone, to create a microclimate that sustains the density. The Vats are a sealed environment, a closed loop. Nothing enters or leaves except through the narrow fracture above. The water that was never here has never left. The minerals are recycled endlessly. The creatures live and die in a space that has no connection to the surface world except for the light that filters down and the rare visitor who descends through the entrance.

---

You find a spot to observe—a projection of rock that offers some elevation, some perspective. From here, the Vats reveal themselves as a system. The creatures are not random in their distribution; they cluster around certain points, certain resources. The mineral deposits are tended with obvious intention, some areas heavily grazed while others are left to regenerate. The machinery is divided into territories, each controlled by a different grouping, each with its own pattern of movement and sound. The ecosystem has rules.

**The rules are old.** Older than the machinery, possibly. The erosion that has softened every surface has also shaped every behavior. The creatures have evolved—or been shaped—to fit this space so precisely that the space and the life within it have become indivisible. The Vats are not a container for the fauna; they are the medium in which the fauna exists. To remove the creatures would be to destroy the Vats' identity entirely.

You notice, after some time, that certain of the larger creatures watch you. Not with eyes—many of them lack organs that could be called eyes—but with attention. They orient toward you. They track your movement. They communicate your presence through the chemical channels, the pressure shifts, the subtle sounds that carry information through this dense space.

You are an anomaly. The system has no category for you. You do not fit the patterns of predator or prey, not in this context. You are not mineral to be processed, not machinery to be maintained, not part of the cycles that have turned here for longer than memory.

But the system is adaptable. It has survived this long by adapting.

**In the deepest part of the Vats**, where the light is dimmest and the sound is loudest, something stirs that does not fit the patterns you have observed. It is larger than the rest, older, worn smooth by time in a way that suggests it has been here since the beginning—or arrived before the beginning, and shaped what came after. It does not move like the others. It does not sound like the others. It is, perhaps, the thing that made this place what it is, or the thing that remains of the original intention, preserved and transformed by the endless operation of the ecosystem.

The smaller creatures give it space. The middle creatures fall silent when it passes. It moves through the Refinery Vats with the weight of a process that has not yet concluded, and will not conclude for ages still to come.

The sound rises around you. The cool air presses against your skin. The creatures continue their eternal work, their eternal lives, their eternal deaths. Above, the narrow entrance admits light from a world that has forgotten this place exists. Below, the Vats grind on, industrial and biological and something else entirely, sealed, saturated, ancient, and loud.
