# The High Plateau of Persistent Sound

It begins with the noise.

---

The sound exists before you understand you've arrived. It's not that you approach it—it's that you gradually recognize you've been hearing it all along. A tone, or perhaps many tones interleaved so tightly they masquerade as one. Not loud in the way of machinery or thunder. Loud in the way of presence. The sound fills the space behind your ears, vibrates in your molars, lives in the small bones.

The plateau sits above the cloud layer, most days. Elevation extreme enough that the air itself seems uncertain whether it belongs here. The sky overhead has depth you can feel—a darkening gradient into something that isn't quite space, isn't quite atmosphere. The blue deepens to violet at the zenith, and at night, the stars don't twinkle. They stare.

You learn to breathe carefully here. Not because the air is thin—though it is, certainly that—but because each breath disturbs something. The sound lives in your chest now, resonating with the rhythm of your pulse, and you become aware that you are generating a counterpoint. An interruption. The sound does not appreciate interruption.

---

The terrain is ancient granite and something else. The granite you recognize: speckled grey-pink, weathered into rounded forms by millennia of ice and wind. The something else is harder to name. It looks like stone but feels, underfoot, like it might be thinking. Not alive—nothing here is quite alive—but adjacent to life. Dormant in a way that suggests the possibility of waking, however remote.

The ground holds moisture without being wet. Condensation beads on the rock faces each morning, darkening the granite to nearly black, pooling in the shallow depressions that time and erosion have carved. The water doesn't flow. It waits. By afternoon, the dampness has returned to the air, visible as a thin haze that softens the horizon and mutes the dim light. The cycle repeats with mechanical precision. No rain falls. The sky provides nothing new.

---

The vegetation, what there is of it, grows in isolated colonies. Tufted grasses the color of old bone, clinging to fissures in the rock. Small succulent things with translucent leaves that store the condensation, swollen with it in the mornings, shriveled by evening. They don't move—they have no mechanism for movement—but they seem to track you nonetheless. Their attention is patient. Plant-patient. Willing to wait.

You find a structure on the second day. Perhaps structure is too generous. A collection of stones arranged in a pattern too deliberate to be natural. Low walls, knee-height, outlining rooms that might have been shelters once. No roof remains. The stones are local granite except for one: a pale blue stone, smooth and veined, sitting at the center of what might have been a hearth. It's warm to the touch. The rest of the plateau is uniformly cold—your fingers numb within minutes of exposure—but this single stone holds heat with unsettling persistence.

You don't touch it again.

---

The loudness has textures you begin to distinguish. There are layers. The base tone, steady and pervasive, that might be the mountain itself singing at a frequency just below conscious hearing. Above it, harmonic overtones that shimmer and shift even though nothing ever truly changes. And beneath it all, so quiet you might be imagining it, a clicking. Or a counting. Or the sound of something breathing very slowly.

The light is a perpetual twilight. Even at midday, the sun hangs low and orange near the horizon, as if reluctant to climb higher. It casts long shadows that stretch away from objects but never shift their angle. Hours pass, and the shadows remain fixed. The sun moves across the sky in a shallow arc, tracing the horizon, never ascending. You realize eventually that time here works differently. Not frozen—the condensation still cycles—but thickened. Minutes have weight.

---

You encounter fauna only twice. A bird, perhaps, though it moves too fast to see clearly—a dark shape that threads between the harmonics of the sound, riding them rather than fighting. It makes no call. It exists in the spaces where the tone wavers, slipping through gaps in the audio that shouldn't exist.

The second is harder to categorize. You find it in a shallow cave, a scooped-out shelter in the mountainside that seems to have been waiting for you to discover it. Inside, pressed against the far wall, a mass of pale flesh and fur, limbs tucked under, breathing so slowly that each exhale takes a full minute to complete. It might be a goat. It might be something that has learned to resemble a goat. It doesn't wake when you enter, doesn't react to your presence, and when you touch its flank—stupidly, gently—the flesh gives like bread dough and remembers the indentation for too long. The hollow fills over the course of an hour.

---

The reality breakdown isn't violent. It's patient, like everything here. You notice first in the corners of your vision: the horizon flickers, showing a different arrangement of peaks, then settles back. A stone you passed earlier appears ahead of you, unchanged, despite your walking for hours. The sky, on the third night, opens briefly into something with too many stars, arranged in patterns that suggest intention. You close your eyes and when you open them, the familiar violet-dark has returned.

Distance is uncertain. You walk for what feels like hours toward a rock formation, and it remains the same size. Then suddenly it's beside you, looming, present. The sound intensifies near certain stones, dims near others. Some locations are safe—the tone becomes almost pleasant, harmonic, supportive. Others generate frequencies that taste like metal in your mouth and leave you blinking away visions of places you've never been.

---

There are rules here, you understand. They aren't written. They aren't spoken. But they exist.

Do not speak aloud. Your voice introduces frequencies that conflict with the established tones. The few times you tried, the sound swallowed your words and left a hollow ache in your jaw for hours afterward.

Do not stay still for too long. The plateau permits passage, not residence. When you rested for what might have been a full day cycle, you woke to find your outline pressed into the stone, inches deep, a mold forming around you. Preparing to hold you.

Do not follow the clicking. Sometimes, in the deeper dimness near the cliff edges, the tone acquires a rhythm—a heartbeat, a pattern, something that calls. You learned quickly not to walk toward it. The plateau has borders you cannot see, edges where the reality thins further, and beyond them the sound has no structure at all. Pure dissolution. You came close once. You won't again.

---

The erosion tells stories. The rounded mountaintops, the smoothed boulders, the gradual slopes where elsewhere there should be jagged peaks—this land has been shaped by patience. Not water, not wind, but something slower. The sound, perhaps. The constant vibration at the molecular level, wearing away at the sharp edges over epochs. The granite surrenders to it eventually. Everything does.

You find scattered artifacts as you walk. A bowl carved from the blue warm stone, holding condensation that ripples with the tone. A wall of fitted stones, carefully mortared, surrounding nothing. A path of deliberate marks—chips and scratches in the rock face—that might be writing or might be decoration. They're too worn to read, if they ever could be read. Someone lived here once, or tried to. Someone learned the rules, perhaps, and left what traces they could before they left, or stayed, or became part of the plateau itself.

---

You don't know how long you've been walking. The sun traces its eternal low arc, and the condensation cycles through its patient rhythm, and the sound fills every space between your thoughts. You could be here for days. You could be here for years. Time moves differently where reality is uncertain.

What you understand, as you walk, is that this place is not broken. It's exactly what it's supposed to be. A high thin space where existence has worn thin, where the constant song of matter has worn away the sharp distinctions between what-is and what-might-be. The erosion is the point. The sound is the point. Everything here exists in a state of becoming, perpetually, without ever arriving.

The plateau is not waiting for anything. It simply is.

---

And when you finally leave—if you leave—you carry the tone with you. Faintly. Behind your ears. In your molars. A quiet persistence that reminds you, sometimes, when the air is thin and the light is dim, that there are places where reality is a suggestion, and the sound sings on without you.
