# Preparing for the Hollow Between Breaths

This is not a destination. It is something that finds you, or something you fall into sideways when you're not paying attention to the back of a dream. So the first preparation is, paradoxically, to *let it happen*. You cannot pack a bag for a place that is also a kind of question.

But you can prepare yourself to survive what it asks.

## The Water

The first thing to understand is that you will not be breathing air. The air ends at the threshold — the granite ring that smells of tin and pressure. Beyond it, the medium that fills the lungs is not oxygen, but it does the work. You will taste copper and old rain on the first breath. By the third, you will not remember what air tasted like. This is not loss. This is the place teaching you a new muscle memory.

**Before you go:**

- Practice holding your breath, not for sport, but for the experience of *letting go* of breath. The water here will replace your air gently. It will not drown you. It will negotiate.
- Practice swimming in nothing. In a pool, close your eyes. Stop kicking. Notice which way is up. There is no up here. The water pulls in every direction with the gentleness of low gravity. You will need to learn to move by intention rather than by reference.
- Drink something with no taste — distilled water, if you can find it. Notice the absence. The water here carries no salt, no taste. This is harder to bear than salt would be. A flavor tells you where you are. A tastelessness tells you only that you are nowhere.

## The Cold

The water is cool the way stone cellars are cool, the way deep lakes are cool in summer. It is a temperature that suggests something older than weather. It will not harm you, but it will change you — your blood will sound louder in your ears, your jaw will be a hinge you can hear, your eyes will whisper in their sockets. You will become audible to yourself.

**Before you go:**

- Sit in a cold bath. Not uncomfortably cold — just cold enough that you stop distracting yourself with sensation. Listen to your body. The place you are going will turn up the volume on every small sound you make. If you cannot bear the sound of yourself in a quiet room, you cannot bear the sound of yourself here.
- Eat something warm an hour before. Not for calories. For the memory of warmth. You will not have it again for a while.

## The Light

The lanterns on the coral-things are mauve, faint, and pulsing in syntax. They are not enough to see by. They are just enough to remind you that you cannot see. You will be navigating in near-darkness, by feel, by the soft glow of things that are speaking to each other in a language you almost understand.

**Before you go:**

- Spend an evening in a room lit only by candles, and then turn those out. Sit in the dark for an hour. Do not look at your phone. Let your pupils do the work they were built to do. The dark here is not absence — it is a fabric, almost a presence. You need to befriend it, not fight it.
- Learn the difference between darkness and not-darkness. They are not the same thing. You will know what I mean when you arrive.

## The Building and the Lessons

There is a spiral structure made of newborn granite. Around its base, scattered in silt that has the warmth of skin: a cup carved from bone. A disk of obsidian with a single line that curves in forever. A rope knotted in a pattern you almost remember from a dream. A skeleton wearing a bronze band that is neither old nor new.

The builder did not leave. The builder was *taken*. Absorbed. The walls reach inward.

**Before you go:**

- Decide, now, before you go, what you will touch and what you will not. Because the place will not give you time to decide. You will pass close to the bronze band. It will be warm. You will not take it. You will take it. You have always taken it. You have never taken it. Decide now.
- Bring nothing. Bring nothing. *Bring nothing.* The things on the floor are lessons, not offerings. If you bring anything with you, you will leave it here, and the leaving will be the lesson, and you will not like what you learn about yourself from what you had to surrender.
- Visit a graveyard, or any place where something has been left behind by someone who did not choose to leave. Sit with the weight of that. The bronze band is waiting for you, and so is the question of what you do when something warm is offered by something dead.

## The Sound in the Walls

There is a breathing. Not yours. Not outside you. From the space between — the crinkle where you end and the not-you begins. Something very large, sleeping, somewhere in the walls. It does not wake.

**Before you go:**

- Sleep in a room with a sound you cannot place. A clock you cannot identify. A hum from a machine across the hall. Let your mind try to name it. Fail. Sleep anyway. Repeat. The thing in the walls cannot be named, but you can practice the muscle of being near something that cannot be named.
- When you dream, and you will dream more vividly after you return, notice the seams. The places where one dream joins another. That cold saltwater doorway. That is where this place lives when it is not living in you.

## The Time

Time is not behaving correctly. The bronze band seems neither old nor new. The silt where your foot pressed fills back in like a bruise, in a different color, like the place is healing from you. You may be here for minutes. You may be here for a thousand years. You will not know. You will, possibly, have been here before.

**Before you go:**

- Tell someone where you are going. Not where geographically. Tell them *what kind of place*. Tell them you may not come back the same. Tell them you may have already been there.
- Make peace with the possibility that you will not remember this clearly. The place between places does not photograph well. It does not even narrate well. It works in pulses. When you return, you may have the impression of mauve light and copper breath and something warm on a wrist that may or may not be yours.
- Do not bring a watch. Do not bring a way to count. Counting is for places that move in straight lines. This place spirals. Counting will only make you dizzy.

## The Conversation

The corals are not speaking to each other. They are speaking to you. You have been answering in pulses you did not know you were making. By the time you notice, it will be too late to stop, and not too late to mean it.

**Before you go:**

- Practice saying things you cannot say in words. Let your breath be uneven on purpose. Let your blinks fall into a rhythm. Notice how your body makes language without your permission. The corals are very old conversationalists. They will notice your rhythms before you do.
- Bring a question. Not a small one. Not a practical one. The kind of question you have been carrying so long that you have forgotten it is a question. The kind that has its own weight, its own gravity. The place will not answer it. The place will let you hear yourself asking it, in the dark, in the cool water, in pulses you didn't know you were making. That is the answer.

## What to Bring

Nothing.

What to wear:

- Something you do not mind losing. The water is not kind to fabric, though it is not cruel either. The growth clings. The walls breathe. You will come back either dry or damp or in some intermediate state that has no name. Dress accordingly.
- Soft shoes, or no shoes. You will not want to touch the floor after the first time. The silt has the warmth of skin and the memory of bruises, and your impression in it will fill back in the wrong color. You will learn to swim rather than walk.
- A band on your wrist. Not bronze. Something of yours. Something you chose. When you see the bronze band on the skeleton's wrist, you will feel a pull. Your own band, whatever it is, will help you remember which wrist is yours, and which is the place's.

## What to Expect on Returning

You will have been gone for a length of time that does not match what the clocks say. You will taste copper for days, faintly, on the back of your tongue. You will notice your own breathing more. You will find, in a pocket or a fold of clothing you are sure you checked, a single grain of light, translucent, no bigger than a seed. You will not remember where it came from. You will know, the way you know things in dreams, that one of the small silver things brushed past you, and you were the surface it was rising toward.

Do not plant it. Do not throw it away. Keep it. It is the only proof, and it is not proof. It is a souvenir from a place that does not give souvenirs, and it will not last, and that is part of the lesson.

The bronze band on the skeleton's wrist was warm when you finally passed close enough to it to notice.

You did not take it.

You took it.

You have always taken it.

You have never taken it.

The water is cool.

The water has always been cool.

---

*Go gently. Or do not go. It will find you either way.*
