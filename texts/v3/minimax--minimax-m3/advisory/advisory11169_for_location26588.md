# A Brief for the Ordinary Human, on the Matter of the Workshop

*Or: How to Walk Into a Place That Is Still Making Itself*

---

## I. Before You Go

The bowl is high. Whatever else you are about to encounter, that fact comes first and will not be negotiated. A breath comes thin. Spend two or three days at altitude before you attempt the final ascent. If you can, stage it — a base camp a thousand meters below the bowl, a sleep there, a sleep again somewhere higher, then the approach. Bring acetazolamide. Bring dexamethasone as a backup. Learn the symptoms of altitude sickness in yourself *before* you are in a place where the air cannot help you. Do not ascend with a head cold. Do not ascend hungover. The thin air will compound what is usually a minor thing into a major one, and the bowl is not a place to be coughing blood at three in the afternoon.

The bowl is also warm. This is its own confusion. You will arrive expecting cold and find something else. Pack for wind on the open side and for the rain, not for the chill. The warmth is not a kindness extended to visitors. The warmth is a sign of the process. The mountain is still hot somewhere far below, and the heat is rising. You are inside the warmth of the rock becoming itself.

---

## II. The Body

You will be damp for the duration. Not soaked — the rain is a condition, not a weather — but damp, the way a window is damp, the way a hand is damp after it has been in a glove too long. Cotton will lie against your skin and stay there, cold and heavy, and it will not dry. Do not bring cotton. Bring wool. Bring the synthetics that are made for this. Accept that some of the wet will be you. Bring a way to dry inner layers at the end of a day, even if the way is a chemical heat pack tucked into a sock. Bring waterproof bags for anything electronic and for anything paper.

Boots should be stiff-soled, broken in, and treated for wet. The stone will give under your feet — a faint compression, a memory of melt — and you need to *feel* that compression through the sole, not have it cushioned away. Bring a pole, or a stick cut on the way up, that can take weight and feel the rock ahead of you. The rock is hard. The rock is soft. The rock is both, and you should not trust any surface that does not yield.

The creatures are on you. They are in the air. They are in your cuffs, in your collar, on the back of your neck, in the seams of your pack. Do not panic. Do not swat. They are part of the place. They are among the things being built. They eat the rock, or the water, or the warmth, or the filaments. They have not come for you. Keep a buff at your throat. Keep your hood up when the air is loud. Do not let them into your mouth, your nose, your eyes, your ears. If one enters an ear, do not push it deeper with a finger. Tilt your head. Let it find its own way out. Most of them will.

## III. What to Carry

**A light.** The bowl is dim but does not dim. The light is sourceless, even, without shadows. Your eyes will adjust, but you will want to look into cracks, into the pools, into the small concavities where the water stands clear to a degree that is suspect. Bring a headlamp, and bring a way to soften it — a layer of cloth, a dimmer setting, a thumb over the lens. The filaments in the rock pulse with their own light. Do not outshine them. Do not startle the place.

**A way to tell time.** The light does not change. There are no shadows. The closed fist will open without announcing itself; you will not know that hours have passed unless you have measured them. Bring a watch. Wind it. Set it. Check it. Plan to leave when the watch tells you, not when the bowl tells you. The bowl will tell you to stay.

**Paper. A pencil. A way to record.** The bowl is too dense for memory. The seams in the rock, the patterns that are not quite patterns, the shape of the closed fist when it is half-open — these will not stay in your head. Bring a notebook that will survive the damp (a waterproof field book, a waxed-cloth cover, plastic sleeves). Bring a pencil that will write on damp paper. Bring a soft cloth to wipe the page first. If you draw, draw fast. The opening is brief.

**A filter, or a way to boil.** The water is warm and clear in a way that is *suspect*. Do not drink it unfiltered. The regard in the pools is not for you. The water is part of the place. The place is not unsafe, but the place is not for you, and the water carries that not-for-you with it. A few swallows will not kill you. A few swallows may give you the feeling that you are part of the building. Do not confuse thirst with belonging.

**Food.** Calorie-dense, sealed, indifferent to moisture. The bowl is not a place to forage. The green is shy. The closed fist is not food. Eat what you have brought. Do not eat what the bowl has made.

**A small first-aid kit.** Blister care, a suture strip, an antihistamine, a broad-spectrum antibiotic begun at the first sign of infection. The bowl is wet and the bowl is full of small lives, and any cut you get will be tended by them. This is not a benefit. This is a complication.

## IV. What to Do

**Move slowly.** The bowl is small — forty meters at its widest — but the geography is uncertain. Pools form between one visit and the next. The water moves. The place is busy in a way that you will not understand at first, and that you should not interrupt. Walk as if the floor is alive, because it is being made. Lift your feet. Set them down with intention. The place is patient. You are a guest.

**Listen.** The bowl is loud. There is a sound like a paper hinge. There is a sound like teeth on something softer than teeth should be on. There is a sound like a long breath out, and it is not clear whether any of them is making it. The loudness is not noise. The loudness is the place doing what it does, all the time, in every layer. Learn the sounds. The sounds will not harm you. The sounds are how the place is loud. If you cover your ears, you will hear your own pulse instead, and your own pulse is not what you came to hear.

**Find the seat.** There is a node near the center of the bowl — a knob of metal-or-not-metal that has been worked into a hollow, a place to sit. It is the only artifact that *unambiguously intends*. Sit in it. Wait. The place will register you eventually. The pools will regard you. The filaments will pulse under your hand if you rest it on the rock nearby. If the place is going to do anything to you, this is where it will do it. Do not sit for more than an hour. The place is patient, but you are a visitor, and the visit has a shape.

**Watch the closed fist.** It is in the far curve of the bowl. It is waist-high, with no leaves, and a head like a closed fist — *your* fist, a human fist, the size and shape of a hand about to knock. It opens every few hours, briefly, to a pale structure like a flower drawn by someone who had only heard flowers described. Then it closes. Then it waits. Time your visit. Be there for one opening. Be there for two if your watch allows. Do not try to smell it. Do not try to touch it. The closed fist is not for you. The closed fist is for the place.
