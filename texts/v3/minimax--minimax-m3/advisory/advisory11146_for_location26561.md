# Preparation for The Wane

The first thing to understand is that you are not preparing for a place. You are preparing for a *condition*. The Wane is not a destination in the way a city, a tundra, or a seabed is a destination. It is a state the world has settled into, and you are going to lower yourself into it the way one lowers a bucket into a well. Everything you pack, everything you practice, everything you refuse to leave behind is in service of staying a bucket and not becoming water.

What follows is organized in the order the place will try to take you.

---

## 1. The Cold (This Is the First Thing That Will Speak)

The cold here is not weather. It is not the cold of a winter you can wait out. It is the cold of a stone that has been at the bottom of a well since before the well had a name. It will not get colder. It will not get warmer. It will simply continue, in the way that a deep thing continues, patiently, for as long as you remain inside it.

What this means for packing: you are not packing for a cold snap. You are packing for a *temperature*. Think of it as preparing for the interior of a refrigerator that has been running for ten thousand years. Your insulation must be total, continuous, and indifferent to activity.

- **Base layers:** Multiple, never one. Merino wool against the skin, because cotton will betray you within an hour and synthetics will hold the sweat against you until the sweat becomes a kind of slow drowning. Three layers on the torso. Two on the legs. The place is dry of vegetation but it is not dry of you; you will sweat, and the sweat will have nowhere to go, and the cold will find it.
- **Mid-layers:** Fleece or synthetic insulation. Down is wonderful until the wet finds it, and the wet will find it. Down clumps and dies. Synthetic insulation loses loft when soaked but still insulates. The Wane will not let you stay dry. Plan for soaked.
- **Outer shell:** A hardshell, fully taped seams, with a hood that moves with your head and does not restrict peripheral vision. The moisture here is not falling but *condensing* — it will bead on every surface, including you, and it will find every zipper, every cuff, every seam where a thread is exposed. The shell must be a second skin of refusal.
- **Hands:** Insulated, waterproof gloves with a liner. Bring three pairs of each, because once a glove is wet through it is no longer clothing, it is a small cold animal clinging to your hand. Mittens are warmer than gloves but you will need your fingers for instruments, for marking, for the slow work of remembering. Consider a chemical hand warmer supply, sealed in plastic, used sparingly. The cold has been passed down; you are not going to out-stubborn it, only outlast your visit.
- **Head and face:** A balaclava. The air is heavy with the moisture that will not precipitate, and the cold will sit on your face like a mask made of stone. Exposed skin will not freeze in the dramatic way, but it will go numb, and numb skin does not warn you. A heavy insulated hat that fits under the hood without compressing the insulation.
- **Eyes:** Clear goggles or heavy glasses. The light is lateral and sourceless, but it is not bright; it is the light of a barn through a missing plank. You will not need sunglasses. You will need protection from the slow, patient condensation that will form on every surface including your corneas. The cold makes the eyes water, and the water freezes on the lashes, and then you blink less, and then you see less, and then you are more part of the place.

The cold will work into you. Not aggressively. The way water works into a stone. By the time you notice, you are not fighting it; you are hosting it. So every garment must be a barrier that decides it will not become a collaborator.

---

## 2. The Wet (The Constant Envelope)

You must understand that this is not rain. There is no falling. There is no weather. There is *moisture*, held in suspension by an atmosphere that is considering precipitation and has been considering it since before you were born. It will condense on every surface. It will pool in sheets on the flat stone. It will film the floor in a silver that is not silver but is exhausted light. Your boots will stand in it for hours. Your Pack will sit in it. Your sleeping bag, if you sleep, will breathe it in.

- **Footwear:** Insulated, waterproof boots, rated for standing in cold water indefinitely. The floor is stone, not mud, so you do not need heavy tread, but you need a sole that does not become a slip risk when the stone is filmed. Bring two pairs. Rotate them. Stuff the dry pair with something absorbent every night. There is no night, but there is the rhythm of the four-stroke engine far away, and you can use it as a clock.
- **Pack:** A waterproof internal-frame pack. Not a dry bag — too soft, too forgiving. A pack with a rigid frame and a built-in rain cover is better, because you will set it down on wet stone, and it will sit in a sheet of water, and it will be there when you return. Line the inside with a heavy trash compactor bag before putting anything in. Two bags. One inside the other.
- **Electronics:** Everything that is not waterproof must be sealed in plastic. Spare batteries in Ziploc bags, double-bagged. A dry box for the camera, if you bring a camera. The moisture is patient. It will find the seam.
- **Paper:** A waterproof notebook, or a regular notebook sealed in a freezer bag. Pencils, not pens — the ink will not flow in the cold. Bring a small piece of soft cloth for your hands before you write, because the moisture will be on your fingers and the paper will absorb it.

The wet is not trying to drown you. It is trying to *join* you. It is the effluent of the machines in the floor, the slow byproduct of their patient, distributed work. To them, you are another surface. To stay separate, you must be a surface that does not absorb.

---

## 3. The Light (The Lateral, the Sourceless)

There is no sky, or there is a sky that has given up its commission. The light that reaches the floor arrives sideways, the way light enters a barn through a missing plank. It is flat, cold, indifferent to the hour. You will not be able to tell if you have been here for an hour or a day. The place refuses to assist you in measuring yourself against it.

- **Personal lighting:** A headlamp. Not for the dark — there is no dark — but for the dimness. The light is sufficient to see, but insufficient to see *with*. The patterns on the stone require close attention. The fauna require you to look *through* them, which requires you to bring your own light close. A headlamp with a red-light option is psychologically useful: the red light is a different color than the stone, and it keeps you in the register of the human, of the carried, of the lit-by-something-you-brought.
- **Spare batteries:** Many. Sealed. The cold preserves, but the cold also drains.
- **A small handheld light:** A backup. The place is not hostile, but it is *indifferent*, and an indifferent place will let your headlamp die without comment.

The light is not a threat. It is an absence of orientation. Without a sun, you have no shadow. Without a shadow, you have no constant anchor of your own shape against the ground. The fauna cast no shadows. The patterns on the stone cast no shadows. You must cast your own.

---

## 4. The Noise (The Floor That Will Not Be Silent)

The noise is the place itself. The tectonic hum. The grinding, far away, of something being made into a powder. The pumping beneath your feet. The four-stroke breathing of a distant engine, large and patient. You will not be able to locate the sources. The place is open in a way that makes distance uncertain, and the sounds fold over each other.

- **Hearing protection:** Foam earplugs, but soft ones. You will not want to silence the place entirely — the sounds are a kind of company, and they are the only evidence that the machines are still working. But you will want to be able to modulate. Bring two types: foam for sleeping, and filtered earplugs that reduce volume without muffling. The noise is low and continuous, and continuous low-frequency noise is a particular kind of slow erosion of the mind.
- **A way to make your own sound:** A small whistle, a bell, or even a recorded song played through a waterproof speaker. The place is loud with the noise of its own long completion, and a human sound, even a small one, is a reminder that you are a different kind of noise. It is a rope thrown across the silence between you and yourself.

The noise is not dangerous to your ears in the way a jet engine is dangerous. It is dangerous in the way a constant sound is dangerous — it becomes the texture of thought. You will begin to think in the cadence of the four-stroke breath. You must guard against this.

## 5. The Sustenance (What You Bring, What You Refuse)

The fauna do not eat. The vegetation does not exist. The place is sterile in a way that sterile implies intention. There is no food here. There is no organic food here. There are the patterns on the stone — fossils of things that never grew — but they are not edible. They are not even matter in the way you understand matter. They are the memory of a pattern that did not take.

- **Water:** Do not drink the water. The description of the place is clear: the wet stone is the effluent of the machines. The dim air is their exhaust. What pools on the floor is not H2O in any sense you can trust. Bring all the water you will need. A heavy-duty water bottle, insulated, with a cover that prevents the mouthpiece from freezing. A backup bottle. Water purification is not the issue; the issue is that the liquid is not a resource, it is a statement.
- **Food:** Calorie-dense, lightweight, stable in cold and moisture. Nuts, energy bars, dried meats, chocolate that will not melt in the cold (it will not melt — there is no warmth — but it will become an inedible brick if it freezes, so keep it in an inner pocket against your body). Eat frequently. The cold is not weather but it is still energy leaving you. You will burn calories faster than you expect, because the cold is constant and the body is constant in its refusal to reconcile with it.
- **A way to eat with numb hands:** A thermos of something warm, if you can keep it warm. The act of holding something warm is a profound counter to the place. A small camp stove is almost certainly not worth the weight and the risk, but a thermos filled before entry, carried against the body, is a small portable humanity.

## 6. The Locomotion (Walking on the Inside of the World)

The floor is flat. Older than flatness by design. The surrender of every vertical ambition. It goes on. The way a number goes on when you stop caring about its value. You will walk for hours and the floor will not change, and the horizon will not approach, and the distant ridge that might have been a mountain range will remain distant.

- **Boots:** Already covered. But: the flats are not even. There are stones that stand an inch above the highest watermark. There are pools. There are places where the water never goes. The patterns are densest on those stones, and the fauna cross them quickly, as if crossing a grave. You will walk around them at first, then through them, then you will pause on one and look at the patterns and realize you are standing on a grave of a different kind. The boots must be comfortable for walking that does not progress. This is more tiring than marching. Bring poles, or a walking stick. They help, and they give you something to do with your hands.
- **Pace:** Slow. The place is not in a hurry. The machines are not in a hurry. The fauna are not in a hurry. If you walk at human pace you will feel like an intrusion. Walk at the pace of the tide. Walk at the pace of the four-stroke engine. Find a rhythm that is not yours and adopt it; it is the only way to move through the place without being constantly reminded of your own speed.
- **Rest:** You will need to rest. The cold will not force you to sit, but the body will. Find a stone that stands above the water. Sit on it. Be an object on the object. Do not lie down. The place is full of things that have lain down.

## 7. The Navigation (The Flexible "Far")

The place is open in a way that makes distance uncertain. Directions are unclear. The light is lateral. There is no sun to cast a shadow, no magnetic field you can trust, no GPS that will speak to the satellites of your own world. The flatness is total, and the horizon is a suggestion rather than a fact.

- **Compass:** Bring one. It may not work. The machines in the floor are doing something to the stone, and the stone is doing something to the field above it. But bring it. If it spins, you will have learned something. If it points, you will have a direction, and a direction is a kind of rope.
- **Marking your path:** This is critical. The place is uniform. You will lose your way back to the entry point, or to the place you decided was camp, or to the stone where you left a marker. Bring biodegradable flagging tape — bright orange, so unlike the stone that it will shout. Tie it to a stone, or to a shaped piece of metal hammered into a crack. Do not trust memory. The place will work into your memory.
- **A record of your route:** A notebook. Note the time (by your watch), the direction (by your compass, if it works), the description of the ground. "Pool still as a forgotten mirror. Deer-shape crossing from northeast. Pattern of abortive ferns underfoot." This is not for navigation. It is for the proof that you are moving, that you are a visitor, that you are not one of the patterns.
- **A tether:** A length of strong cord, perhaps fifty meters, attached to a heavy anchor at your entry point. Not because you will not wander. Because you will be tempted to wander until you cannot find the way back, and the tether is a physical argument with the place.

## 8. The Time (The Forgetting of Arrival)

You will not remember arriving. The description says so. The place will work into you, and one of the first things it will work into is the timestamp of your entry. You will feel you have been here a long time. You will not know how long.

- **A watch:** A reliable mechanical watch. Batteries die in the cold. A solar watch is useless. Bring a mechanical, or a digital with a battery you keep warm against your body. Note the time of your entry. Note the time every hour. The watch is a statement: *I am from a place where time is counted.*
- **A deadline:** Decide, before you enter, how long you will stay. A day. Two days. A week. The place will not give you a signal that it is time to leave. The tide will rise and fall by fractions of an inch. The four-stroke engine will complete its cycles. The deer will migrate. None of these is a clock. You must be the clock. Tell someone on the other side your deadline. Write it down. The deadline is a rope you throw to your future self.

## 9. The Fauna (The Trying-On of Shapes)

They are not animals. They are shapes the place has tried on. They move in herds, in clusters, in lines that cross. They look like deer, like birds, like wolves. They do not eat. They do not rest. They do not look at each other. They do not make sound. They do not displace the water. They do not cast shadows. They are the color of the stone.

- **Do not chase them.** You will not catch them. When you are close enough to see them clearly, you will see through them. They will not be hostile. They will be the *idea* of hostility, or the memory of a thing that once was hostile, or a draft of hostility from somewhere else. They will pass through you, or around you, and the water will close behind them.
- **Do not try to touch them.** Your hand will pass through, or it will find stone, and the stone will be the temperature of the deer-shape, and you will not know which one is the animal.
- **Do not look at them for too long.** The deer-shape that lifts its head from the pool and looks at you with eyes the color of the stone is the place looking at you through the deer-shape. It is not a greeting. It is not a threat. It is a measurement. You are being fitted for a pattern.
- **Do not speak to them.** They do not answer. If you speak and hear an answer, the answer is the four-stroke engine, or the grinding, or the pumping, or your own voice played back by the wet stone.

The fauna are the noise the machines make when they are tired, or dreaming. You are a kind of noise too. The kind that walks on two legs and knows it is a visitor. Maintain the distinction.

---

## 10. The Patterns (The Museum of Attempts)

The radiating geometries. The concentric rings. The branching dendritic traces that look like the shadow of a fern pressed into basalt by a weight that was never lifted. They are everywhere. In the pools, on the stones that stand above the water, in the fields of aborted gardens that stretch for miles. They are all slightly different. None of them ever grew.

- **Do not touch the patterns.** They are not fossils. They are not plants. They are the memory of a pattern that the machines tried, in some laboratory sense, that did not take. To touch them is to touch a grave. The fauna cross them quickly. You should cross them quickly. If you stop to study one, you will find another, and another, and you will begin to notice the slight differences, and you will begin to wonder what the machines were trying to make, and you will begin to feel that you are a draft for the next attempt.
- **Do not take a sample.** The place is a museum. Museums are not improved by the visitor taking a piece. Also, the sample will not survive the leaving. It will become a pattern in your memory, and you will not be sure if it was ever stone.

The cold is necessary for this. Cold preserves. Cold is patient. The patterns are waiting for the next one. Do not audition.

---

## 11. The Mirror Pools (The Dissolution of "Which")

There are pools so still they have forgotten how to reflect. In them, the animals walk. You can see them. You can see them through the water. Through the water there is another animal walking in the opposite direction. The two never meet. You will not be sure which one is the animal.

- **Do not gaze into the pools for long.** The pools are not mirrors. They are the place's way of showing you a draft of yourself. You will see yourself walking in the opposite direction. You will see yourself as a shape that is not quite solid. You will begin to negotiate with your own outline.
- **If you must look, look away.** The description warns you. The place is "already, entirely, the inside of itself." The pools are the place's interior. You are in the interior. Looking into the pool is looking into the place that is looking at you. It is a loop. Break the loop.

---

## 12. The Four-Stroke Breath (The Patient Heart)
