# Preparing for the Pocket

You are about to go somewhere that is still arguing with itself about what it is. Most of what follows is the ordinary, unglamorous kind of preparation — what to wear, what to bring, what to eat beforehand — but a small part of it is the less ordinary kind, because the place has ways of making you forget the ordinary kind of preparation exists, and you want to be ready for that, too.

## Before You Leave Anywhere Else

**Tell someone where you are going.** Not in the casual "I'll be back in a bit" sense. In the sense of leaving a note, in another world, with another version of yourself or a person you trust, that says: I went in. I expect to come out. If I come out and I seem to be the person who left a few thousand years ago, here is how you might verify that. Bring the cairn stone from the southeast corner. Ask me what the lichen said. I do not know what the lichen says, but I will know if I am lying.

**Set a time limit and write it down.** The chamber will not feel like it is making you stay. It will feel like you have decided to stay, freely, and that you could leave at any moment. Write the time on your hand. Set an alarm on something that does not depend on local physics, because local physics is on vacation. The pocket wants your attention. It is not malicious about it. It is simply that the place is still assembling a witness and you are the candidate.

**Practice sitting still in bright, damp, quiet rooms.** This sounds like advice for a spa retreat. It is not. Spend an hour in a bathroom with the fan off and the shower running hot so the mirror steams, then sit on the floor and watch the droplets on the tile. Notice how your eyes start to invent sources for the light. Notice how your skin starts to interpret the humidity as warmth, then as cold, then as a kind of pressure. Notice how, after a while, the silence of the room becomes a presence. The pocket is that, but the pocket is more of it. The bathroom is good practice.

**Do not drink the water beforehand.** Do not eat the lichen. Do not lick the walls in some other world on a dare. You are trying to arrive with your senses intact.

**Decide what you are willing to be wrong about.** The chamber will show you things at the bottom of its water that are not there. It will let you count the cairns and get different numbers. It will, if you let it, begin to feel like the place knows you, and you will want to believe it, because it is a small kind place and being known by a small kind place is a relief. Decide now: I am willing to be uncertain. I am willing to find out I was wrong about what I saw. This will not stop the experience. It will keep you from building a house out of it.

## What to Wear

**Footwear:** sticky rubber soles, with as much tread as you can fit on your feet. The floor is slick. Not because water has been spilled on it. Because the stone is newer than it looks and is still smoothing itself under your weight. You will feel it give, slightly, like a floor that has not yet learned how to be a floor. Soft-soled shoes will feel romantic for about four minutes and then you will be on your back.

**Grippy gloves.** Thin enough to feel things through, but with palm texture. You will grip the lip of the fold on the way in and on the way out. The lip is warm on the way in and cool on the way out, and slightly damp, and gives a little under your grip. You want to feel all of that, but you also want to feel your hand after.

**Layers, but more than you think you need.** The place is cool. The brightness is not the warmth. The water in the dishes on the floor is warm in the cycle that functions as morning, cool in the cycle that functions as evening, and the cycling is gentle and slow and does not announce itself. A base layer that breathes, a mid layer that holds warmth even when damp, and a shell that you can peel off if the mist makes the air feel close. Bring the shell even if it is heavy. You may want it for the ascent.

**A light dust mask or a bandana you can pull up.** The soft wall is shedding itself. It is not aggressive about it. It is the slowest avalanche you have ever been near. But the grit is fine and your lungs will eventually notice. If you have a face covering anyway, for the descent through the crack, this is a sensible doubling.

**Tinted eyewear — amber, rose, or a very mild grey.** Not sunglasses. The brightness here has no source, and sunglasses are designed to subtract a sun. You want something that softens the brightness without flattening it, so that the mist-distributed light still registers as light and not as glare. Try them in bright fog before you leave. If they make the world look like a postcard, they are too dark. If they make a bright room merely comfortable, they are right.

**No perfume. No strong scent.** The creatures here navigate by something, and you do not know what. Do not assume it is vision or hearing. Assume it is chemistry. Show up smelling like yourself and only like yourself, and wash before you go, so that you are not carrying the smell of three worlds ago into a place that has only ever smelled like itself.

**Clothing that can get damp without becoming a misery.** The mist is everywhere and it will find every fiber. Wool or a good synthetic. Not cotton. Cotton here will make you cold in a way you cannot easily recover from, because the chamber is cool to begin with and there is no towel.

## What to Bring

**A small, powerful personal light, but only for emergencies.** A penlight or a tiny headlamp. The chamber is bright from the mist and you do not need to add to that brightness; your own torch will read as foreign here, and the creatures do not seem to like foreign things, though they will not say so because they have no way to say so. Use it only if you must examine something close, and turn it off before the creatures notice it. They will notice.

**A mirror, small.** Not for vanity. To check your own eyes. If you have been in the pocket long enough, your pupils may be doing something. You do not know what is normal here, but you can compare to the mirror in your other bathroom. If your eyes look strange, that is information.

**A small container of water from somewhere else, and some food.** Do not plan to eat or drink anything from the chamber. The water here is not quite water and shows you things at the bottom that are not there. The lichen is gray-green and almost translucent. The single bruise-colored leaf is the only thing with conviction, and you should not eat conviction. Bring what you need for however long you have set your time limit, plus a margin, because the place is patient and the place will make you forget to eat.

**A timer or a watch, set before you descend.** This is the most important piece of equipment you will carry. The pocket will not let you lose track of time in a dramatic way. It will let you lose track of it in the way a library lets you lose track of it: gradually, pleasantly, and without intending to. The watch is not for the pocket. The watch is for you.

**A field notebook and a soft pencil.** Pen will skip on damp pages. Pencil will not. Take notes about what you see, but understand that you are taking notes about what you saw, and that the place is interested in being seen, and may be interested in being seen in a particular way. If your notes start to feel suspiciously complete, suspiciously resolved, suspiciously literary — that is the place, helping. Write anyway. Write the dryness, too.

**A small specimen container, sealed.** The grit is just grit. The cairns are made of it. If you take one piece of grit from the seam at the base of the soft wall, you are taking a piece of a place that is still deciding what it is. This may matter. The cairns may notice. The many-eyed creature may notice. Take it anyway, or do not. Decide now.

**A small piece of something from somewhere else.** A coin. A button. A leaf from a tree you know. Something that is unambiguously *not from here*. Tuck it into a pocket. Do not put it on the cairns. Do not drop it in the water dishes. Just carry it. It is your anchor. When you start to feel like you have always been in this pocket and the world above is the dream, feel for the coin.

**A climbing rope and a small harness.** The crack is not a stair. It is a fold that lets you through. You will be lowering yourself down a surface that is warm and slightly yielding, that is rimmed with something halfway between quartz and bone. A short rope, just enough to manage the descent without a free fall, and to help with the ascent. The exit is a pull, not a climb. You will be glad of a handhold that is not the fold itself.

## The Descent

**Go slowly.** The fold yields. If you go fast, you will arrive at the bottom of the chamber with more momentum than the bottom of the chamber is prepared for, and the floor is slick.

**Notice the warmth of the lip.** It is still warm from the negotiation of becoming. This is not a temperature you can put on a thermometer in any unit that will match the reading above. It is the warmth of a place that is being made, and you are about to be one of the things made in it, briefly.

**Do not bring the rope with you into the chamber.** Anchor it above, on the world-side of the fold. The pocket does not want your rope. The pocket wants you, unencumbered, small, willing.

## In the Chamber

**Sit down.** The chamber is pocket-sized. You can almost touch the walls if you stand in the middle and stretch. Standing in it is a kind of argument with the place. Sitting in it is a kind of agreement. Sit. The damp stone will feel cool through your clothes. The brightness will ache your eyes in a way that is almost pleasant. Let it.

**Do not count the cairns more than once.** They are finger-height. They are made of grit from the soft wall. They were placed by something with patience and no thumbs. If you count them and you get a number, that number is the number. If you count them again and you get a different number, that is also the number. Trying to pin the number down is the place's game, and the place will let you play it forever. Count once. Accept the answer. Do not look at the cairns again until you leave.

**Do not step in the dishes of water unless you have to.** They are warm in the morning cycle and cool in the evening cycle and they will show you things at the bottom that are not there. If you step in one, you will feel the warmth move up through your foot and you will think, briefly, that the water is telling you something. It is telling you something. Do not listen for long.

**Watch the ceiling.** The many-legged thing will walk across it upside down, leaving a silver track of mucus that catches the light and becomes a slow constellation. Watch it the way you would watch a sky. Do not try to follow it. It knows where it is going. You do not.

**Watch the single bruise-colored leaf.** It is the only piece of vegetation here with any conviction. It trembles when the air moves. Watch it the way you would watch a person. It is the most alive thing in the chamber and the most alone.

**Do not stare at the many-eyed creature.** It watches you from a ledge near the soft wall without blinking, because nothing here has eyelids. It has too many eyes for its head, or several heads that have agreed to share eyes. Looking at it is fine. Looking at it for a long time is fine. Looking at it the way you look at a thing you are trying to understand is not fine, because you will not understand it, and the not-understanding will begin to feel like a kind of failure, and the place will, gently, begin to interpret your failure as a kind of belonging. Look away every little while. Look at the lichen. Look at the grit. Look at the cairns. Look at your coin.

**Do not lean against the soft wall.** It is still pouring itself downward. It is the emerging part. If you lean against it, it will give. You will feel the grit against your back. You will become, briefly, part of the construction of a place that is being made over the next ten thousand years. This may be fine. It may be a kind of gift. But you came here as a visitor, not as a material, and the place will accept either. Decide which you want to be.

**Do not try to take one of the cairns apart.** They are balanced with a care that looks almost deliberate. They were placed by something with patience and no thumbs. If you take one apart, the place will not be angry. It will simply continue to exist without the cairn, and the cairn will not be there later, and you will have moved a piece of a place that is still deciding what its pieces are. Leave them.

**Do not speak above a whisper.** The chamber is quiet, but it is not still, and the small sounds of the place are the only conversation here. Your voice, if you raise it, will feel like an intrusion, and you will feel like an intruder, and then you will feel like you have always been an intruder, and then the place will become less kind. A whisper is enough. The many-eyed creature will hear it anyway.

**Do not try to take a creature with you.** They are pale. They have no eyelids. They are descended from the ones that first found this chamber. They belong here in the way that the grit belongs here, in the way that the lichen belongs here: by being the only thing that ever was, in a place that has not yet had many things. Taking one with you is not a kindness. It is not a rescue. It is the introduction of a foreign thing into a place that does not have foreign things, and the place will absorb it the way it absorbs grit: slowly, and into itself.

**Stay until your timer goes off.** Then leave. The pocket will make the leaving feel like a small grief. The pocket will make the staying feel like a homecoming. The pocket is wrong about both of these things, but the pocket is also not wrong, and that is what makes it the kind of place you remember.

## The Ascent

**The lip is cool on the way out.** It will feel cool and slightly damp and give a little under your grip, the way new rock does. Use the rope you anchored above. Pull yourself up through the fold.

**You will arrive a few thousand years earlier than you left.** This is not a metaphor. This is how the pocket works. The world above has continued without you, and you have not aged, and the few thousand years are a thing that has happened to the time outside, not to you. You may want to sit on the ground above the fold for a while before you go back to whatever you were doing. The brightness will persist in your eyes for a moment. The place will persist in you for longer.

**Do not look back into the fold.** The chamber is still finishing itself. It is going to take longer than you can sit there. You are early for it. That is why it is so bright. It is the light that gets shown into a place before it has learned how to be dark.

When you are ready, walk away. Carry the grit if you took it. Do not carry the cairns. Carry the coin. Let the place do what it is doing, which is becoming, which will outlast you, which was already outlasting you before you came.
