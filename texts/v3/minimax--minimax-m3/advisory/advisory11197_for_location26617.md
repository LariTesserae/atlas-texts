# Preparing to Enter the Hollow

The first thing to understand, before any preparation, is what kind of place this is. It is not a destination. It is a body. You will be inside a living tissue, sitting in a chamber held open inside something that has been thinking itself into being for longer than your species has had language, and which will continue thinking itself into being long after the place where you learned the word *after* has ceased to exist. Every practical decision you make should be made with that fact in your body, not just your head. You are a guest inside someone else's patience. Do not arrive as a tourist. Arrive as a guest.

---

## Before You Go

### The cold is the first enemy, and it is patient

The temperature in the lower parts of the hollow is below the freezing point of water. The water has not frozen. This is the first of the wrongnesses, and your body will know something is wrong before your mind does. The cold is not a temperature in the way you are used to cold being a temperature. It is a substance. It is wet. It will condense on your skin, on your eyelashes, in the lining of your lungs. A wet cold of this intensity will pull heat out of you faster than a dry cold of the same number on a thermometer, and it will do it quietly, the way the hollow does everything. You will not notice the moment you begin to lose the edge. You will notice that your fingers have forgotten how to grip, or that you have stopped shivering, and by then you are already in trouble.

Dress for it. But understand that no clothing you can bring is going to win against a cold that has been in the fibers of the walls since before your grandparents' grandparents' grandparents. Your job is not to defeat the cold. Your job is to slow it down long enough to leave with all of yourself.

- **Base layer:** Wool, not synthetic. Wool holds heat when wet, synthetics do not. Bring two base layers.
- **Mid layer:** A fleece or wool sweater. Avoid down — it collapses when soaked and will not recover.
- **Outer layer:** A breathable waterproof shell with sealed seams. It will fail eventually. Accept this.
- **Extremities:** Waterproof gloves with liners. A hat that covers the ears, or a balaclava. The head is where you lose the most heat to a wet cold, and also where you lose the most clarity.
- **Feet:** Insulated, waterproof boots with aggressive tread. The floor is not a floor. It is a yielding, fibrous mass that will be slick with water and the trails of fauna. You want grip, you want ankle support, you want to be able to feel the ground through the sole without it being so thin that the cold comes straight through.
- **Spare set of dry clothes** in a sealed dry bag, for after. Do not plan to change inside. There is no dignity in changing inside, and no clean place to set the dry things down.

### Do not plan to eat or drink inside

Do not eat the fauna. Do not drink the water. The water is not water in the way you mean water. It is water that has been pulled up through the fibers by something that is not quite capillary, and it carries the chemistry of the place in it, and the chemistry of the place is operating on a set of rules that is adjacent to yours but not identical. You do not know what it will do in you. Eat and drink before you go, and plan to eat and drink after. Bring something for the immediate aftermath, sealed, from your own world, with a taste you can name.

### Light

The hollow has its own light. The walls glow. The fauna carry their points of yellow and violet and unnamed color. There is a fog of mist that catches the glow and scatters it. This is enough to see the shapes of things, the sheen of water on every surface, the slow movement of what moves. It is not enough to see by in the way you are used to seeing, and your eyes will strain for the first few minutes, searching for a sun that is not there.

Bring a light source. But bring it as a last resort, and bring one you can dim to almost nothing. A headlamp with a red-light mode is good. A small, hand-held, dimmable warm light is better — the fauna here carry colors that are pale and sourceless, and a warm dim light will disturb them less than the cold white of a standard headlamp. The light is part of the place. Treat it as part of the place. If you flood the hollow with your own light, you will be telling the walls something, and you will not know what.

### What to bring, and what to leave

Bring as little as you can. The hollow is the size of a small room. You do not need equipment for a small room. You need equipment for a small room that is also a body.

- A sealed dry bag with your spare clothes, food, water for after.
- Your light, dimmed, in a pocket.
- A way to tell time that does not depend on the place. A mechanical watch is better than a digital one. The hollow is operating on a different time. You do not yet know how different, but it is different. Decide, before you go in, how long you will stay. Write the number down. Do not exceed it. The place will make you forget you had a number.
- A small sharp knife. Not for the fauna. For the plant-walls, if you must mark your way. (See below.)
- Something to write with, after. The place is hard to remember accurately. You will want to make notes while the impressions are still warm.
- A small cloth, dry, in a sealed bag. For wiping your face. For the moment when you realize you have been crying and you do not know why. This may happen. The cold does this sometimes. The place does this sometimes.

Do not bring a phone you care about. Do not bring anything with a screen you cannot defend against total saturation. Do not bring anything you would be destroyed to lose. The hollow is gentle, but it is not careful, and the water gets into everything.

### The entrance

This is the part you must understand before you go, because if you do not understand it before, you will not be able to think about it after.

The opening you came through — the crack, the root-channel, the place where the surface has not yet committed to being surface — will not be the same opening on the way out. The walls lay down new strata. The fauna move through the felt. The water finds new paths. The entrance that admits you is not a door. It is a permission, granted for a span of time that the place decides, not you. In the time you are inside, the hollow will, slowly, almost imperceptibly, begin to close. New growth will press in from the edges. The channel will narrow. The light from the other side will dim.

You will feel the closing. You will feel it the way you feel a room getting smaller, the way you feel a silence getting heavier. Do not wait until you cannot get out. Decide, before you go in, that you will turn around at a certain point. The point is not when you are tired. The point is not when you have seen enough. The point is when the place has had what it will give you for this visit, and you do not always get to know when that is. Be early. Be earlier than you think you should be. The crack you came through will seal itself, in time, with new growth, and the entrance will be forgotten, and the place will continue without remembering that you were there.

If you are with a companion, agree on a signal. If you are alone, set your watch.

### The mind you bring

This is the part that matters most, and the part that cannot be packed in a dry bag.

The hollow is not a place of danger in the way a cliff is a place of danger. It will not, in any obvious way, try to hurt you. The fauna do not flee from you, but they do not attack you. The walls do not seize you. The cold is patient, and the wetness is patient, and the long sound underneath everything is patient, and the whole place is in no hurry to do anything at all to you. You are not the subject of the place. You are not even an event in the place. You are a visitor in something's slow middle act of becoming, and the act does not pause for you.

This is the thing that will unmake you if you let it.

You will arrive as a person with a name and a purpose and a watch set to a number. The place will not take these from you. It will simply make them feel, slowly, like things that belong to a smaller world. The walls have been laying themselves down for centuries. The fauna have been moving through the felt in their patient lines. The water has been climbing against the gradient. The long calculation has been running, and the input you are, the input your small warm stumbling body is, is being added to the calculation, and the calculation does not care about you, and this is the part that is hard. The place does not hate you. It does not love you. It does not know you in any way you would recognize as knowing. It is doing what it does, and you are in the way of what it does, and the way it does not react to you is the most honest thing about the place.

You must decide, before you go in, that this is okay. You must decide that being a small thing in a large slow process is not a diminishment. You must decide that you are going to listen, and look, and be in the place, without needing the place to acknowledge you. If you need the place to be about you, do not go. If you need the place to make sense in the way your world makes sense, do not go. If you are going to be frightened by the long sound, or by the way the fauna pause in unison without coordinating, or by the way the light falls in directions that light should not fall, then go with a companion who can hold your arm and tell you, in a voice from your own world, that you are still in a world.

If you go alone, go prepared to be alone in a way that is different from any aloneness you have practiced. The place will not comfort you. It will not harm you. It will be there.

## The Descent

The crack admits you. You will know the moment you cross because the light from the other side will fail in a way that is not a failure of brightness but a failure of direction. The light that was coming from where you came from will stop being a thing you can see the source of. The light that is in the hollow will begin to be the only light. Your eyes will take some time to adjust. Give them time. Do not turn on your own light in the first few minutes. Let the hollow's light find you. It will. The walls glow. The mist scatters the glow. There will be points of yellow and violet moving through the felt, slow, patient. Wait until you can see the shape of the space before you try to see the details of the space.

The temperature will drop. You will feel it first in your face, in the back of your throat, in the wetness of your breath. The air is heavy with water. Your lungs will feel it. Breathe slowly. Do not gulp. The air is not bad air. It is thick air, air that is full of the place, and the place is full of a kind of life your body does not have a name for, and your body will notice, and your body will want to reject it. Let the body notice. Do not let the body panic. The body is allowed to be uncomfortable. You are allowed to be uncomfortable. The place is not going to apologize for what it is.

The floor will not be under you in the way a floor is under you. It will give. It will be damp. It will breathe faintly under your weight. Step slowly. Test the ground ahead of you with the toe of your boot before you put your full weight down. The geometry is the geometry of growth, not the geometry of architecture. There will be places where the floor holds and places where it does not, and the difference will be small, and the soft places will be where the felt is thickest, and the thickest places will be where the fauna are densest beneath the surface, and the densest places will be where the long sound is loudest.

Move as if you are walking on something that is alive, because you are.

## Inside

### The light, and how to see

Your eyes will adjust. Give them twenty minutes. After twenty minutes, the hollow will be more visible to you than it was at first, and less visible than you want it to be. You will be able to see the shapes of things — the curves of the walls, the dips and rises of the ceiling, the movement of the fauna. You will not be able to see the fine details in the way you are used to seeing fine details. The glow from the walls is sourceless and the glow from the fauna is moving and the mist catches both and scatters both and the whole effect is of being inside a thing that is lit from within by something that does not want to be looked at directly.

Do not look at the lights directly. Look at the spaces between the lights. The hollow is mostly darkness, and the darkness is where the shapes of things are. Let your peripheral vision do the work. This is how you see in a place that does not want to be seen in the way you see.

If you must use your own light, use it sparingly. A quick sweep, dimmed, to check a footing or to find the way back. The place will not react to your light in any way you can see. The fauna will not flee. But you will know, and the knowing will be enough.

### The sound, and how to hear

The sound is the first thing you will understand, the description said, and it is true. The hollow is loud in a way that is not a loudness of volume. It is the loudness of many small sources summing into a single sustained texture. There is the drip of water from a hundred points. There is the wet hum of the plants, which you will feel in your teeth before you hear it. There is the chewing, the clicking, the soft persistent static of countless small bodies moving against countless small fibers.

Do not try to hear the individual sounds. You will not be able to. The place is not built for individual sounds. It is built for the texture. Stand still. Let the texture wash over you. After some minutes, you will begin to be able to pick out layers — the drip will separate from the hum, the chewing will separate from the clicking. This is your ear learning the place. It is a kind of learning. It is not the learning you are used to.

Beneath all of it, if you stay long enough, you will hear the slow sound. The one without an easy name. The sound of the place doing what the place does. Do not strain for it. It will come. It is patient. It has been making this sound for a long time, and it will be making it long after you are gone. When you hear it, you will know. It is not a sound that tells you anything. It is a sound that lets you know that there is a sound beneath the sound, and that the sound beneath the sound is the place, and the place is not in a hurry.

### The fauna

The fauna will not flee from you. This is the thing you must understand first, because your instincts are built to interpret a non-fleeing animal as either a sick animal or a predator, and neither of those is what is happening here. The fauna do not flee because you are not, in the grammar of the place, a threat. You are a thing that is in the place. The place is a thing that has had things in it for a long time. The fauna continue.

Do not touch the fauna. They will let you. The pale worms will surface near your knee and wait, and the not-insects will land on your hand, and the small flying things will brush your face in the mist. They will let you do anything. This is not permission. It is the absence of a reaction. If you pick up a worm, the worm will not struggle. The worm will also not forgive you. The place does not forgive. The place does not punish. The place continues.

If something lands on you, be still. It will leave when it is ready. It is not studying you. It is not marking you. It is doing what it does, and you are in the way, and it will move on. The patience of the fauna is not a politeness. It is a fact.

### The walls, and how to be in them

The walls are made of plants. The room is the plants. The hollow is a space held open inside a living tissue. You are inside something that is alive in a way that is not animal, not fungal, not plant in the way you mean plant. It is a fourth thing, or a first thing, and it has been laying itself down in pale strata for centuries.

The walls will give under you if you press. The water will seep from them where you press. The fauna will continue around your hand. This is the place saying, in the only language it has, that you are in it. It is not saying welcome. It is not saying go away. It is saying that the walls are walls, and you are in them, and the walls are made of something that is alive, and you are pressing on it, and it is noticing, and it is not stopping you.

If you must sit, sit where the felt is thickest. The hollow is small. You will not have much choice. The floor will be damp and cold and yielding. Bring a small pad if you cannot bear to sit directly on the felt. Do not bring a chair. There is no flat place for a chair. There is no place for a chair in the grammar of the place.

Do not mark the walls. The place does not need your marks. The walls lay down a new stratum in the next season, in the next century. Your mark will be covered, and the covering will be a fact about the place, and the fact will not be about you. If you must mark the entrance so that you can find it on the way out, mark it with a small cut, or a thread tied around a thick strand of the plant-matter near the opening, and accept that the entrance is moving.

### The synchronized pauses

The fauna will pause. Sometimes all of them at once. The worms will stop their slow peristaltic waves. The many-legged things will freeze in their deliberate lines. The flying things will hold, motionless, in the mist. The sound will not stop — the drip and the hum and the static will continue — but the movement will, and the movement is what your eyes are following, and the absence of the movement will be sudden, and total, and silent in a way that is louder than the sound.

This is not a signal. This is not communication. This is the place, doing what the place does. The unison is not coordination. It is the place, in its slow body, doing one thing in many places at once, the way your body does many things at once without consulting itself. The worms pause the way your hand pauses. The flying things pause the way your breathing pauses. It is not a message. It is a breath.

Do not move during the pause. The pause is the place being most itself. To move during the pause is to be the loudest thing in the hollow. Wait. The pause will end. The place will resume. The resume is not a release. It is the next breath. Be in the breath with the place, the way you would be in the breath with a sleeping animal you do not want to wake. The place is not asleep. The place is doing something slower than waking, and the slow thing is the thing you came to see, if you came to see anything.

## The Long Stay

The description warned about this. You will want to stay longer than you should. The place will not tell you that you have stayed long enough. The place will not tell you anything. The place will continue, and you will be in the continuing, and the continuing is a kind of comfort, and the comfort is not the place being kind to you. The comfort is the place being what it is, and you being in what it is, and the two of those being, for a moment, the same.

Set your watch. Trust the watch. The watch is from your world. The watch is the only thing in the hollow that is from your world, except you. The watch is the small thing that says, in a voice from where you come from, that you have a place to go back to, and a time to be there, and a body that needs to be in a place that is not this place.

The cold will creep. You will not notice it. You will notice that your fingers are clumsy, or that you have stopped shivering, and the stopping of the shivering is the worst sign, because the shivering is your body doing something to keep itself alive, and when the shivering stops, the body has decided that the cold is the new normal, and the body is wrong. The cold is not the new normal. The cold is the place. Check your fingers. Check your toes. Check the back of your neck. If any of these are cold to the touch in a way that is more than a surface cold, leave.

The wet will creep. Your outer layer will fail. Your mid layer will begin to take on water. The water will not feel like water. It will feel like the place, because the place is the water, in a way. Do not let the wet get to your base layer if you can help it. The base layer is your last defense. If the base layer is wet, the cold will win, and the cold will win slowly, and you will not feel it winning.

The sound will be in you. After a while, the drip and the hum and the static will be the silence of your own head. You will stop hearing them. This is not a loss. This is the place becoming your place, for the duration of your stay. When you leave, you will hear them again, and they will be loud, and then they will fade, and then, for a long time, you will not hear them, and then, one day, in a quiet moment, you will hear them again, and you will know that the place is still there, and the place is still making the sound, and the place does not need you to hear it for the sound to be made.

---

## The Leaving

You will know when it is time to leave the way you know when it is time to leave a conversation with someone you love. The conversation is not over. The conversation will never be over. But there is a moment when the being in the conversation becomes more than the conversation can hold, and the moment is not a sign that the conversation has failed. The moment is a sign that the conversation is doing what conversations do, which is to bring you to the edge of what you can hold, and to ask you to hold it anyway, and then to let you go.

The entrance will be smaller. The light from the other side will be dimmer. The felt near the opening will have thickened. The fauna near the opening will be denser. Do not be alarmed. This is the place closing, slowly, the way the place does everything, and the closing is not about you. The closing is the place continuing to be the place.

Move toward the entrance the way you came in. Do not rush. The place does not rush. If you cannot find the entrance — and you may not be able to, because the entrance is not a fixed thing, and the light from the other side is not a fixed thing — then stop, and be still, and let your eyes adjust, and look for the place where the light is least like the light of the hollow. The light from the other side is the light of a world that is not this world. It will look wrong to you, the way a familiar room looks wrong after you have been away a long time. Trust the wrongness. The wrongness is the way out.

When you cross back, you will feel the temperature rise. You will feel the air thin. You will feel the light from your own world hit you in a way that is almost physical, and the almost-physical will make you squint, and the squinting will be a relief. Your breath will stop making the small reluctant cloud. The mist will be gone. The sound of the hollow will be gone, except in your memory, and the memory will be the loudest sound in the world for the first few minutes.

The crack will not seal itself the moment you leave. It will take some time. The new growth will press in. The felt will thicken. The entrance will be forgotten. You will be on the other side, in your own world, and the entrance will not know that you are on the other side, and the other side will not know that the entrance was ever an entrance, and this is the way the place is, and the way the place will be, and the way the place was before you came, and will be after you go.

---

## After

Find a warm, dry, well-lit place. Do this immediately. Do not delay. Your body has been in a place that is not your place, and your body needs to be in your place, with warmth and dryness and the kind of light that comes from a sun that is not the glow of a living wall.

Eat something from your own world. Drink something from your own world. The taste will be strange to you for a few minutes, and then the strange will fade, and the taste will be the taste, and the taste will be a comfort you did not know you needed.

Tell someone. Even if they do not believe you. Even if they laugh. The telling is part of the coming back. The hollow is a place that does not acknowledge you, and the not-acknowledging is a kind of loneliness that is not a loneliness, and the loneliness-that-is-not-a-loneliness needs to be answered by a voice that knows you, even if the voice does not understand.

Make notes. While the impressions are still warm. The cold of the place, the wet of the place, the sound of the place, the way the light fell wrong, the way the worms paused without pausing, the way the mist moved in patterns that were not random and not ordered. The notes will not capture the place. Nothing will capture the place. The notes are for you, so that you do not, in a year, or in a decade, find yourself wondering if you made it up.

Do not go back immediately. The place will be there. The place is in no hurry. The place has been there for centuries, and will be there for centuries more. The entrance you came through is sealed, and the next entrance will be different, and the next visit will be a different visit, and the difference is part of the patience of the place. If you go back too soon, the place will know. The place does not know in any way you would recognize as knowing. But you will know, in your body, that you have come back too soon, and the too-soon will be a weight, and the weight will be a fact about you, not about the place.

The patience of the place will be in you for a long time after you leave. The patience of fiber, the patience of water, the patience of a cold that has nowhere to be. The patience is not a lesson. The patience is a fact. The fact is that the place is still there, doing what the place does, and the what-the-place-does is not waiting for you, and the not-waiting is the kindest thing the place has done for you, because the not-waiting lets you go back to your own world, with your own watch, and your own time, and your own small urgent things, and the small urgent things will feel, for a while, like the right size of thing to be doing.

Then the right size will fade. The small urgent things will become small urgent things again. The patience of the place will go back to being a memory. And one day, in a quiet moment, you will hear the slow sound beneath the sound, and you will know that the hollow is still there, and the hollow is still being made, and the hollow is being thought, and the hollow is being, in a way that you almost understand, and that you will never understand, even if you stay forever.

This is the gift of the place. Not the seeing. The almost-understanding. The almost-understanding is the thing you carry back, and the thing you carry back is the thing that will not let you put the place down, and the not-letting-you-put-it-down is the place's way of being in you, after you have left it, the way the water is in the fibers, the way the light is in the walls, the way the long thought is in the slow middle act of becoming.

Welcome back. You are not the same. That is the point.
