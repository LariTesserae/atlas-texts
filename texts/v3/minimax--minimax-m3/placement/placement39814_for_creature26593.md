I am long.

That is the first thing I know about myself, the way the first thing a tongue knows is the mouth. I am long, and I am pale, and I am in the warm water, and the warm water has always been warm. I do not remember a time when it was not. I have been told, by some part of me that is older than I am — by the walls of me, by the slow turnover of my skin — that once, in some dim counting that does not match the way I count, the water was colder, and the place above was not so far above, and there were other shapes like me that lived in colder places. But this is not memory. This is something the body keeps without using, the way the chamber keeps the calcite drip without intending to build a wall. The body knows. I do not need to know.

I lie in the basin I have lain in so long that the stone and I have worn each other into shape. The shelf at my left is the exact curve of my flank at that point. The worn places on the floor stones are the shape of my foot, of the path I take when I take it. We have done this together, the water and the basalt and I. We have made each other.

My flanks know the shape of every wall in my part of the chamber. This is how I learned it — not by looking, because my eyes are large and useless, wet and silver in the gloom, and they are not looking at anything. I am listening with my skin. The warmth of the stone against me is a map. The coolness of a draft from a deeper crack is a border. The chamber is written on me in degrees, and I have been reading it for so long that I do not know I am reading.

The crickets, high above, click their private metronomes. They are the middle managers. They do not know I exist, except as a warmth they move away from. The mites, lower down where I cannot easily reach, graze the stone so slowly that a patch of them seems not to move at all — only by returning after a long while do I find them shifted by a thumb's width. They are polishing the chamber for me without knowing they are polishing it for me. The folded ones pour past in slow loops, opening their papery wings, and the air moves against my face, and the air is warm from them, and they do not fly at me, they fly past me because I am warm, and warmth in this place is a kind of smell, and they are following it the way I follow the warmth of the deeper pool when the cold months come.

I eat what the water gives me, which is small things. I eat what the air gives me, which is smaller things. I eat, sometimes, a folded one that falls weakly from its cluster and lands near me, and the eating is quiet and thorough, and I do not feel bad about it, because the folded ones are mostly water and warm air, and they come apart in my mouth the way a sigh comes apart.

I am not alone in the chamber. There are others like me, in other pools, in other tiers. We do not see each other often. Our territories are marked by the smears I renew without knowing I renew them. The smear is a secretion. It comes from somewhere in me — I do not know where — and it smells like me, and I lay it at the edges of the place where I lie, and when it fades I lay it again. I do this because the place wants it. The place is mine the way a body is mine: not chosen, not defended, just lived in, and the lived-in part gets marked the way skin gets thicker at the palm.

There are sticks across the approach to my pool. I gathered them. I do not remember gathering them. I gather them the way a person in their sleep pulls the blanket up: the body knows. The body remembers that the sticks want to be here, and that the place is better with them here, and so in some part of the night I do not consciously live through, my forelimbs go out and find what can be found and bring it back and lay it across the lip of the pool. The sticks are not a wall. They are not a defense. They are a way of saying *this is the place where I am*. They are a way of saying it without saying.

Sometimes a warmth comes down from above.

Not often. Not on a schedule I can find. But sometimes a warmth comes that is not the usual warmth of the snowmelt or the wall or the breath of another of my kind. This warmth is different. This warmth moves in a way that the snowmelt does not move. This warmth carries a sound with it — a small sharp sound, like a cricket but thicker, like a folded one but shorter — and the sound makes shapes in the air that I can feel against my skin. The warmth is making the sound. The warmth is the one that is making the sound.

When this happens, I do something I do not usually do. I rise a little in the pool. I bring my forelimbs up onto the stone at the edge. The pads of my digits — four of them, five, spread slightly apart, still feeling for the next hold — find the worn places there, the places I have gripped so many times that the basalt has taken the shape of my hand the way my hand has taken the shape of the basalt. I grip and I hold and I wait.

I do not know what I am waiting for.

But the smear at the base of that stone — the stone I grip — is fresh. I made it fresh. I do not remember making it fresh. But when I bring my flank against the wall after a warmth has come down, I find the smell of myself is strong there, and I know I have been renewing. The body knows. The chamber knows. The chamber is patient, and will let me believe any story I want about why the smear is fresh, and will not confirm any of them, because the chamber is not interested in my certainty.

The warmth does not stay. It comes and it moves through the chamber and it goes back up, back to the coin of gray, and I am here in the warm water and the mist coils and the crickets click and the folded ones pour past in their slow loops. The warmth leaves behind it a different feel in the air. The mist moves differently for a while after. The crickets are quieter for a while after. As if the chamber has been reminded of something, and is settling back into itself.

Somewhere, in some part of me that is older than I am, in some part of me that the chamber keeps the way it keeps the calcite drip — a slow addition, a bead at a time, building back toward the opening — somewhere in me there is a record of having come down from above. Of having gripped a lip of black basalt. Of having felt the wind strip the lichen away. Of having dropped into the fissure and fallen and fallen and found, at the bottom, a chamber of warm dark. Of having stayed.

I do not access this record. It is not for me. It is for the body. The body knows that the chamber is mine and that *mine* is expressed by the sticks and the smear and the worn places on the stone. The body knows that the warmth that comes down from above is something the chamber remembers, even if I do not. The body knows that when the warmth was here, the smear was renewed, and that this is what we do, and that this is enough.

My young are born in this water. They are born breathing. They are born knowing which way is down — darker than the dark in some essential way, before they have any experience of it. They grow slowly. One near the edge of the deepest pool could be a decade old or a century old; there is no way to tell. I lie near them in the warm water, and they learn the shape of the chamber by the warmth of the walls against their flanks, and they learn the other creatures by the sounds they make, and they learn the place itself by the way the mist moves. By the time one of them is fully grown it knows this chamber the way a tongue knows a mouth.

I do not think of them as mine in the way a climber-thinker might think of theirs. My interest is more thermal than emotional. I keep them warm. I lie near them. When one falls weakly, I eat it, the way the chamber eats the dead, and the eating is quiet and thorough and the cycle is closed.

I call out sometimes. A single low note. Not a word. Not a song. Not quite an alarm. Just a note, from somewhere deep in my chest, from the bellows of the lung, from the place where the warm wet air goes in and comes out. The note travels up and down the chamber. It hits the walls and comes back. It folds into the mist. The crickets do not stop for it. The folded ones do not pause for it. But somewhere, in some pool in some other tier of the chamber, another of my kind hears it, and another calls back, and the calls are not addressed to anything, and they are not songs, and they are not words, but they have the quality of *being heard*. Of checking. Of the creature making the call asking, in its chest, in its throat, in the place where its breath becomes a sound: *is there anyone here?*

And the chamber folds the calls into itself. And the mist carries them. And the water in the standing pools ripples faintly with each one. And the calcite, dropping a bead at a time, building back toward the opening, does not hurry.

I do not know if I am the descendant of something that climbed down here. I do not know if the climbers were ever here at all. The white smear could be the residue of an old climbing chalk. The sticks could have been carried down by water. The worn places could be the work of a thousand seasons of crickets landing and taking off. The chamber is patient. The chamber would let me believe any of these stories, and would not confirm any of them, because the chamber is not interested in my certainty.

But I know this: when the warmth comes down from above and moves through the chamber, my forelimbs rise to the stone and my digits find the worn places and my body renews the smear. And the warmth, whatever it is, passes me and goes back up, and I am here, and the mist moves, and the crickets click, and a folded one drops from its cluster and opens its papery wings and pours past me in a long slow loop, and the air moves against my flank, and I understand that I have been heard, and that being heard does not mean being understood, and that the chamber does not care which it is.

Because to the chamber I am just another warmth moving through it.

And to me, the warmth that comes down from above is just another warmth moving through me.

And we are both, briefly, in the same place.

And then the warmth is gone.

And I am long. And pale. And in the warm water. And the mist coils. And the crickets click. And somewhere far above, the coin of gray is still there, and the wind on the ridge is still singing, and the place has been here longer than language and will be here after.

And I call out once.

A single low note.

And the chamber folds it in.
