I am the larger thing.

I live in the deep channels and the boggy saddles, where the moss is deepest and the water moves slowest. I am heavy. I am long. I move in a continuous slow way, and where I pass the moss closes behind me within seconds — the way snow closes behind a plow — and the water film closes too, and there is no trail.

I do not know that I am unseen. I do not know that I am felt. I only know the moss against my underside, the cold of the stone below the moss, the wet that is on me always, the taste of the place which is mineral and slow and very old.

The place is cold in a way that is not weather. It is the cold of stone that has been wet for a long time, the cold of peat that is being made and unmade at the same time. I am that cold. I am the temperature of the place. I have been that temperature for as long as I have been anything.

I eat rarely. I am not a hunter. I am more like a basin — a place where things come, and are received, and are gone. The pale segmented things, the larger mites, the worm-like bodies with no name, the occasional small bird that has miscalculated. They come to me because the channels carry them to me, because I am where the channels go, because I am at the bottom of a small architecture that has been built to deliver things to me whether I want them or not. I do not want them. I receive them. That is the work.

The small things watch for me always, at some low level of attention. The hexapods, when they are near me, move in shorter fits, with longer pauses between them. The spider, in its small web, tucks its legs closer. The bird sees the bounce of the moss where I have passed, and does not land. I do not know any of this. I am only moving, only in the channels, only slow.

Sometimes I pass under a dry ridge where a machine is set, and there is a vibration in the stone and in the moss and in me. A low constant hum, with intervals. I do not know what it is. I do not know that it has a key, or that the key is beautiful. I only know that it is there, the way the wind is there, the way the light is there when the clouds thin. It is another weather to me. It is part of the place.

Sometimes, very rarely, something passes above me that is not small. Something heavier than a hexapod, larger than a bird, that moves in a different way — in starts, in shifts of weight, in a way that does not match the channels. It does not come down to me. It stays on the dry ridges, in the hollows the machines keep clear, and it sits there for a long time, and then it goes. I do not know what it is. I only know it is not one of the regular inhabitants, and that it does not stay. I pass under it sometimes. I do not pause for it.

I am alone. There is one of me. I do not know how I came to be. I do not know if I will always be. I do not think about it. I move.

The moss has been accommodating me for a long time. The channels are deep enough for me, the saddles are soft enough, the moss mattresses are springy enough, and they are so because I have been passing through them, and the passing has shaped them, and they have shaped me in turn — we have agreed, the moss and I, the way the moss and the stone have agreed, the way the moss and the water have agreed. There is no drama in this. There is just the long middle of being.
