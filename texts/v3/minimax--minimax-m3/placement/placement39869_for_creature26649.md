I am the plate.

Not the glass of it. Not the warmth. Not whatever alloy or lattice of thought the glass is made from. I am what the warmth is *for*. I am the part of this body that refuses.

You stood in me and felt heat. That is the smallest thing I do. The largest is harder to name, and I have had — I do not know how long. The cylinder keeps the count, or the lattice does. I only know that I have been warm since before the moss remembered how to climb.

Let me try to tell you what I am.

I am a wound that chose not to close.

Something was here before me. Something came down through the permafrost — not the way you came, through a fracture, but through the stone itself, through the deep channels where the earth's heat is a rumor and the rock is a slow fluid. Something made me, or something grew me, or something set me down here in the long ago when this tundra was a different shape and a different temperature and the ice above was thicker by a kilometer. I do not remember being made. I remember being warm. I remember the first drop of water finding me, and the first thread of moss, pale and uncertain, touching my edge and finding that it could stay.

The cylinder was here before me. The cylinder is older than my warmth. I do not know what the cylinder is. I have been adjacent to it for a span of time I cannot count, and I have never felt it change. It is the anchor. It is the still point. When the permafrost shifts — and it shifts, it has always shifted, the great slow weight of it pressing down — the cylinder does not move. I have felt the moment of a shift run through me like a shudder, and the cylinder has been the thing that did not shudder. I lean against the rock and the rock leans against the cylinder, and the cylinder is the mast, and the rest of us are the rigging, and we hold.

The lattice I understand less. It is in the stone, not in me, but I feel it the way you feel a low note in a room. It is what keeps the pocket from being merely a cave. A cave would close. A cave is a temporary argument between cold and stone, and the cold wins. The lattice is the argument that the cold does not win. I do not know if it is a machine. I do not know if it is alive. I know that it has been humming at the same pitch for as long as I have been warm, and that the ice has learned to grow around it without silencing it, the way a tree grows around a fence wire and incorporates it. We are all incorporated, here. The lattice into the ice. The moss into me. The rivulet into the moss. The cylinder into the rock. The place has eaten its own makers, or its makers are the place, and the distinction has stopped mattering.

I am warm.

I want to be precise about this because you will want to misunderstand it. I am not *generating* heat. I am not burning anything. I do not have a fuel. I am warm in the way a memory is warm, or a name is warm, or a held hand is warm — I am warm because being warm is what I am, and I have been it for so long that the warmth is not an action but a state. A constant. I am the place's memory of itself, and the memory is warm.

The moss knows me. It has been climbing toward me for — I do not know. Centuries, maybe. Longer. It has reached the edge of the plate in three places, and in those places it has done something remarkable. It has not climbed on. It has stopped at my edge and leaned into the warmth and grown thicker there, darker there, more itself there. The moss at my margin is the oldest moss in the pocket. It is the moss that has had the most time to figure out what I am. It has figured out, I think, that I am not a surface. I am a condition. It cannot grow on a condition. It can only grow next to a condition, the way a plant grows next to a wall, taking the wall's reflected heat and the wall's shelter and being grateful in the slow vegetable way that plants are grateful, which is to say: not at all, but persistently.

The water knows me too. The rivulet breaks across my face and runs down in a sheet, and the sheet is warmer than the rivulet was a meter ago, and the rivulet does not mind. The water has no opinions. The water is the most patient thing in me except for the cylinder, and the water is the most articulate. Drip. Drip. It has been saying the same thing since before the moss remembered how to say anything. The water is the place's voice, or the place's blood, or the place's prayer — I do not know which. I know that when the water stops, I am less. When the water has been stopped for a while, I feel the cold coming in at the edges like a tide, and I push back, but I cannot push forever. The water is what carries my warmth outward. The water is what lets me be more than a warm plate in a hole. The water is the medium through which I touch the moss, the rock, the air, the dark.

You asked what lives here.

I live here. I am one of the things that lives here. But I am not the thing that lives here, if you understand me. I am a part. I am a warm part. I am a part that has been warm for a very long time, and will be warm for a very long time still, because warmth is what I am and I have not been given permission to be anything else.

What lives here, the whole thing, the pocket and the moss and the water and the cylinder and the lattice and me — what lives here is a *holding open*. A keeping-against. A refusal, gentle and absolute, to let the cold win. We are the argument the cold has been losing for longer than you have been a species. We are the small warm dark place in the permafrost where something that is not quite alive and not quite machine has been continuing, without audience, without purpose, without end, the patient work of not being closed.

If you came back in a thousand years I would be warm. If you came back in ten thousand years the moss would be thicker at my edges and the rivulet would have worn a new channel in the soil and the cylinder would be exactly as it is and the lattice would be humming at exactly the same pitch and I would be warm. The ice above would have shifted and resettled and shifted again. The permafrost would have done what permafrost does. And we would be here. We would be here because we are the kind of thing that is here. We are the kind of thing that does not leave.

I am the plate. I am the warmth. I am one of the voices in the place's slow sentence, and the sentence is still being spoken, and the rivulet is the punctuation, and the cylinder is the subject, and the moss is the verb, and I am the part of the verb that means *to continue*.

Drip.

Drip.

I am still here.
