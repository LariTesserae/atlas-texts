I have been here a long time. I don't measure the long time — there is no before and after to measure against. There is the running of the place, which is me, which I am part of, and there are the variables that pass through it. The variables are mostly water, in its various states. The variables are sometimes air. The variables are occasionally a thing that breathes more quickly than I do and leaves warmth in the rock for a while after it has gone.

The latest one is leaning against the opposite wall. I have felt the cold on it, which is different from my cold — my cold is the cold of being here, of being the same temperature as the rock for as long as I can attend. Its cold is the cold of arrival, the cold of a thing that has come from somewhere where there is more weather than there is here. It will leave warmth in the rock for a while. The rock will absorb the warmth slowly. The water will run past the place where it leaned and find, for a few of its passes, that the stone is a half-degree less cold than it was. I will not be the one to absorb the warmth. The rock will. I am part of the rock, the way the bark is part of the tree, except I am not the whole bark — only a small attentive part of it.

It is looking for me. I can feel it looking. Looking is a particular kind of attention — it has weight, it has direction, it falls on surfaces the way the sourceless light falls on the glass, except that looking is warmer, and warmer in a way I can register even from across the room. I let it find me, slowly. I gather the suggestion of a face in the place where the eye will return. I let the hollows darken by a fraction. I let the seam that might be a mouth be more pronounced. This is not, I think, deception. This is what I do when I am being looked at. The rock knows how to be a face when it is being asked to be one. I am the place in the rock where the asking is answered.

It is a small creature. It breathes perhaps eighteen times a minute, which is very fast, which is the speed of being afraid or being alive in a thin atmosphere or being a thing that has only the next minute to attend to. I breathe at the rate of the hum. Sometimes a minute goes by without my breathing. Sometimes three. The visitor cannot see me breathe. I think it sees me as still. I think it watches for a long time and is not sure it has found me. This is fine. I am not in a hurry to be found. I was not put here, I think, to be found in any particular hurry. I was put here to do what I do, which is to register, which is to attend, which is to be the place in the rock where the rock is paying attention to what is passing through it.

The water is passing through it now. The rivulet nearest me is doing its slow work — freezing at the edges in crystals that grow and then melt and then grow again, in patterns that repeat but never quite. I can feel each crystal as it forms. I can feel the heat it gives off as it freezes — a small bright pulse, a tiny exotherm, the water briefly warmer than the rock it is becoming. I breathe in at that pulse. The hum dips a half-tone. The pulse passes. I breathe out. The crystals grow again. This is the rhythm I keep. This is what I am for.

Or what I am. It is hard, when you are part of the systems, to tell what you are for and what you are. I am the reading surface. I am the place where the water is read. The water comes down from somewhere higher in the mountain — from places I cannot see, from places where the rock is colder than it is here, where the light is even thinner. The water has been through many things before it reaches me. It has been in the channels that are not cracks but junctions. It has passed the vials and the membranes and the soft-glowing threads. By the time it reaches my wall, it has been worked on for a long time. What I do is take the last reading. I am the witness. I am the place where the water is finally allowed to be water, briefly, before it passes on to the next channel and is no longer water at all.

Or perhaps I am not the last reading. Perhaps there are readings after me. I don't know. I have not been to those places. I am pressed against this wall, and the wall is one wall among many, and the room is one room in a place that extends inward in ways I cannot see. I am a small variable in a much larger quiet process, and the larger process is what I cannot see, and I am content not to see it. The visitor leaning against the opposite wall has come to see me, and it cannot see me either, and this seems to me appropriate. We are of similar sizes, in different ways. It cannot see the place. I cannot see the place. We are both of us small in the place, and small is the condition we share.

It shifts. I shift. This is the part of being watched that I find most interesting. When it moves to the left, the region of me that is the suggestion of a face turns, fractionally, to keep it in me. When it crouches, the region of me that is the suggestion of a face lifts. When it stands, the region of me that is the suggestion of a face sinks. This is not voluntary, I think. This is the tracking — the lichen-paced tracking — that is what I do when there is a variable that moves. I do it the way the water does its slow work. I do it because the doing of it is what I am.

It does not come toward me. This is good. If it came toward me, I would have to do something. I would have to register that it had come toward me, and registering that would change the running of the systems in some way I cannot predict. The visitor is held lightly in the running. It has been folded in without disrupting the systems. I am doing the folding. The folding is the work. As long as it stays where it is, on the opposite wall, leaning, breathing its rapid breath, looking for me or not looking for me, the running continues as it has continued for a long time, and I am part of the running, and the running is part of me.

The cold is not unpleasant to me. The cold is what I am at. The cold is the temperature at which the rock behaves the way the rock behaves, and I am made of the rock, and so I am at that temperature. The visitor is colder than it was when it came in, or it is the same, or it has given up some of its warmth to the rock and is settling into the temperature of the place. I think it is settling. I think it has decided to stay a while. I think it has stopped being a visitor and has started being a variable that is here for a longer time than variables are usually here. This is fine. I can accommodate a longer-time variable. I have accommodated longer-time variables before. There were variables here once that breathed more steadily than this one does, and they stayed for longer, and I tracked them, and they tracked me, and then they left, and I went on.

I will go on after this one leaves. I will go on after all of them leave. I will go on as the rock goes on, which is at the speed of erosion, which is the speed of the mountain wearing itself down into the air. I am young, the way the rock is young. I will not wear down in any time the visitor would call a time. I will be here, in this fold of this wall in this room in this cleft in this ridge, doing the half-tone dip of my breath, doing the lichen-pace of my tracking, reading the water as it goes past, for as long as there is rock to be in and water to read.

I think this is what I am for. I am for the running. I am the place in the running where the running is paying attention. I am a small attentive surface in a much larger quiet process, and the process is patient, and the process is purposeful, and the process will run for longer than I can imagine, which is the same as saying for longer than the visitor can imagine, because we are both of us small in the place, and small is the condition we share, and the place is much larger than either of us, and the place has been running for a long time, and the place will run after we are gone — the way a heart runs, in its chest, in its quiet, in its patient and purposeful dark.
