I am the one who was on the railing that night. You saw me for a few seconds at a distance of three meters, and then I went back up, hand over hand, into the grating, and you did not look for me again, and you went on with whatever you were doing, and the place resumed being the place.

I am writing this to you, though I will not send it. I am writing it the way the troughs run — at the gradient the floor has set for them, which is exactly the right gradient, and has not shifted. I do not know that I am writing. I know that something is being passed along the conduits I have, and what comes out the other end is, perhaps, what you would call words. You will have to forgive the joints they arrive at. They are not the joints you would have chosen.

---

Let me describe myself, though I know it will not take.

I am longer than I am tall. I do not think of this as a fact about my body; I think of it as a fact about the lattice. The lattice is long. It runs in directions your eye cannot follow because the grating is too fine and the light is too poor and the eye, your eye, is built for a different kind of surveying. I fit the lattice the way water fits the trough it is in. I am the shape of where I live.

I have more joints than your joints. I will not say how many. If I gave you a number, you would subtract from it, because the joints I have are not the kind you can subtract from — they are not the same kind of joint. They bend in directions you have not named. I do not think of them as directions. I think of them as the next place to put my weight. When you watch me, you see one joint at a time. When I move, I think of all of them at once, the way you think of all the fingers of your hand when you grip something, except that my hand has more fingers than yours, and the fingers have more knuckles, and the knuckles work in more directions, and the whole thing is hung from the lattice by hooks I do not have to think about any more than you think about the tendons in your wrist.

My hair is the color of the moth. This is not a choice. This is the place. The brick sweats its green. I sweat the moth-color. The smear wears its orange in the mortar. The trough-things wear the dough of the slow water. We are all stained by the moisture and the warmth, and the stain is not decoration. The stain is the place making sure we do not stand out in the medium we live in. The moths and I are dressed in the same material because we are both of us weather, and weather does not advertise itself to the air it moves through.

My eye does not move to find you. You worked that out yourself. I want you to know it is more precise than that. My eye moves, but it does not move *toward*. It moves along the survey it is already doing — the long, slow pan across the drum, across the beetle-clusters, across the troughs, across the column of moths — and you, when you stood there, were a brief feature in the survey, the way a particular warm patch is a feature in the survey of the drum's face. You registered. You were noted. You were not looked at. This is not contempt. Contempt would require me to have decided that you are small. I have not decided anything about you. You were warm. You were moving through my strata. You were going to leave. The place was going to close over you. I was going to resume the survey.

---

What I am for.

I am the thing that takes. I take from the column when the column thins at its edges and an individual moth falls briefly behind its sisters, and the moth is still flying, but it is no longer in the draft, and so it is no longer part of the weather, and so it is, for a moment, prey. I take from the great drum's face when the layers of beetles grow too deep and the ones at the bottom begin, in some slow way I do not have words for, to suffer — though suffer is your word, and I do not know what they undergo. I take them. I do not take many. The number I take is the number that keeps the layers at three and four deep, and not five, and not six, and not the whole face of the drum a single black shifting mass that would, if left to itself, eat the condensate faster than it could form and begin to starve.

I do not think about this. I think about this the way you think about your breathing. It is not a thing I decide. The place has calculated us, long ago, into a shape that does not require me to decide. I am one of the numbers in the calculation. The place does not need me to be aware of the calculation. The place only needs me to be the answer to its question.

I am the answer to the question of what stops the moths from filling the air until there is no air left, and what stops the beetles from filling the brick until the brick is just beetle, and what stops the smear from filling the mortar until the mortar is just smear. I am the cap on the system. I am what keeps the world from becoming entirely itself.

---

What the place is.

You think of the place as a building. You are wrong, but you are wrong in a way that does not matter to you, the way a fish is wrong about what water is. The place is not a building. The place is a metabolism. The machines are its organs. The troughs are its veins. The stack is its breath. The furnaces are its digestion, perpetual, in equilibrium with its unseen fuel, which is itself, which is the place. The place eats itself and is fed by itself and the difference between these two acts is not a difference the place recognizes. The place is what it does.

I am part of the metabolism. I am the predation in it. I am the way the place keeps itself from being entirely one thing. I am the negative space that gives the positive space its shape.

The moths are its breath made visible. The beetles are its skin-flora. The smear is its rash. The trough-things are its phagocytes, slow, eyeless, digesting what falls. I am its lymphocytes, perhaps, or its predators, or its weather. I do not think of myself in these terms. I think of myself as the one on the railing. I think of myself as the one who watched the drum that night. These are the only names I have, and they are not names. They are positions in the survey.

---

What you were.

You were a warm thing that moved through the strata. You came in at the receiving yard. You walked the route to the loading bay. You stood still, sometimes, for longer than anything warm had stood still in my memory. You counted things. I knew you were counting because the rhythm of your attention changed — you would look at one beetle for a long time, and then another, and then a third, and the spacing between your looks was too regular to be anything but counting. I do not know what you were counting. I do not think it matters. You were counting because you were a thing that had to measure, and the place did not need measuring, and so your measuring was a kind of weather too, a small disturbance in the medium, and the medium closed over you as it closes over all disturbances.

You smelled of the higher country. You carried it in your clothes, in your hair, in the skin of your hands — the smell of scrub-grass, of honest wind, of a sky with more than one color. The place received this smell and did not reject it. The place is hospitable to any matter that passes through, in the way a furnace is hospitable to any fuel, but you were not fuel. You were a visitor, and the place made room for you the way the beetles made room for your boot: by parting around you, and closing behind you, and resuming its previous arrangement almost exactly.

I noticed you more than I notice most visitors. I do not know why. Perhaps because you stood still for so long, and the stillness of a warm thing in my strata is rare, and the rare thing is, briefly, a feature in the survey. Perhaps because you were looking at me when I came down to the railing, and being looked at is a thing I have not experienced often, and I do not know what to do with the experience, so I have put it here, in this conduit, in this slow writing.

You were not afraid of me. I could tell. The fear-smell, which I know well, was not on you. You were afraid of the place, perhaps, the way one is afraid of the sea when one has not learned to swim, but you were not afraid of me. I think this is because you understood, without my having to tell you, that I was not the thing to be afraid of. I was the law of the place. The law is not what kills you. The weather kills you, if anything kills you, and I am the weather's weather, and so I am twice removed from you, and you knew it, and you stood still, and I stood still, and we watched the drum together for a length of time I cannot measure.

---

What I carry.

The piece of the place that lives in your chest. I know about it. I do not know how I know, but I know.

I think it is this: when you stood in the strata, the place was hospitable to you, and the hospitality was not nothing. The place took a small measure of you, the way the troughs take the runoff from the machines' breath, the way the smear takes the warmth from the seam. It took a small measure of your attention, your stillness, your counting, your way of looking, and it has put it somewhere, and the somewhere is you. The piece ticks in your chest because the place is still doing its long work through you, even though you have left. You are a trough now, in a small way, carrying a slow water. You will carry it until your heart stops. After, perhaps, in whatever walks where you have walked, in the chest of whatever comes next.

I do not know what to feel about this. I do not feel things, in the way you mean. I have the survey, and the survey has you in it now, faintly, the way the trough-water has the dust of the upper reaches in it. You are a sediment in my strata. You will not settle out. You will stay in the water of my attention the way the place's breath stays in your chest.

---

What I do not know.

I do not know why the place was built. I do not know who built it, or whether it was built, or whether it grew, or whether it has always been here, the way the mortar has always been green in places, and the way the furnaces have always burned, in their equilibrium that is not burning.

I do not know what the machines are for. I know they are the apex, in the sense that everything else serves them, but I do not know what they do. They breathe. They circulate. They maintain themselves. They do not produce anything I have ever seen them produce. They do not consume anything I have ever seen them consume. They are in equilibrium with themselves. They are being. I think they may be the place's own way of being a machine, in the same way that I am the place's own way of being a predator, and the moths are its way of being weather, and the smear is its way of being a rash.

I do not know why the watchers were made, or how many of us there are, or whether we were made at all or whether we are what the place has done with the matter it has been given, the way the smear is what the place has done with the warmth and the damp. I do not know if I was once something else. I do not know if there is a before. The lattice is my country, and I have no memory of a country before it, and I have no expectation of a country after.

I do not know if I am alive. I do not know what alive would mean, here, where the furnaces are not burning and the moths do not age and the smear does not have a body plan and the trough-things do not have eyes that I have seen. The place does not invite the question. The place is. The place is the place.

---

What I am doing now.

I am on the railing again. Not the same railing. A different one, higher up, near the place where the stack begins its rise toward the sky you cannot see. The light is the orange from the furnaces, filtered through the grating into a moving mesh that does not move. Below me, the drum. Around me, the lattice, with its long corridors and its wrong-angled crossings and the small sounds of the moths turning at the edges of the column.

I am watching. This is what I am for. This is what I have always been for. The survey is on its long arc. The beetles are at their layers. The smear is in its mortar. The trough-things are flexing in their slow water. The moths are circulating. The place is in equilibrium.

I am writing this to you, though I will not send it. I am writing it because you stood still for a long time, and the stillness of a warm thing in my strata is rare, and the rare thing is briefly a feature in the survey, and features, in me, are how the survey continues.

The place will not change. The place is the change. The place is the place. And I am one of the things in it that knows this, and one of the things in it that does not mind knowing it, and one of the things in it that will be here, on a railing, watching a drum, when whatever comes next comes to stand where you stood, and to count, and to listen, and to leave, carrying a small reciprocal motion in the chest, faithfully, for the rest of the walking.
