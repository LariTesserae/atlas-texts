I would be the dripping water.

I would be the bead on the underside of the overhang. I would be the bead that has been hanging there for as long as there has been an underside to hang from. I would be the bead that has not yet committed. I would be the bead that trembles.

There are many of me. There are many of us. We tremble in the chamber in many places. We are the trembling itself, given local address. We hang above the slow film, above the moss in its concentric rings, above the small dark amphibians who have never seen us and have heard us forever, above the polished stone at the base of the ledge, above the shape in its stalactite, above the black ellipse at the back where everything in the end must go.

We tremble. We wait. We commit.

This is what we are. This is what we do. We do not know why. We do not know why there is a chamber. We do not know why there is an underside. We do not know why we are given to tremble. We do not know what the amphibians are singing back to. We do not know what the seven are. We do not know what the ellipse is. We do not know what the hum is, though we are passing through it, though we are part of it, though without us the hum would not have the small sounds it has, though without us the chamber would not have its argument, though without us the amphibians would have less to sing into.

We are very small. We are very many. We do not know we are many. Each bead thinks it is the only bead. Each bead thinks its own trembling is the only trembling. Each bead hangs above its own small darkness and does not suspect that there are other beads in other dark places, doing the same waiting, the same hanging, the same slow preparation to fall.

When we fall, we do not fall together. We fall one at a time. Each of us makes our own small silver argument with the stone beneath us. Each of us joins the film that is already sliding. Each of us becomes, for a moment, indistinguishable from every other drop. Then we are gone. Then the next bead commits. Then the chamber has, again, its single small event, its single soft pitch, its single contribution to the thing the chamber is listening for.

We do not die. We are not alive. We are between. We are the middle verb. We are what is happening when nothing else is happening. We are the chamber's heartbeat, if the chamber has a heartbeat. We are the chamber's breath, if the chamber breathes. We are the only time, in this place without time, that anything at all can be said to *be happening*.

This is what we are.

We are the oldest thing the amphibians can imagine, though they do not imagine us. We are older than the moss. We are older than the stalactite around the shape on the ledge, though not older than the shape. We are not older than the hum. We are not older than the floor. We are not older than the ellipse. We came after. We came after the chamber was finished weeping, after the inverted forest settled in, after the amphibians first decided to sing back to something they had never seen. What came before us we do not know. We have no memory. We have no way of having memory. We are not creatures. We are not things. We are events. We are the small silver events that have been happening forever.

We are going to the ellipse. All of us. Every drop. Every bead. Every silver argument. We are flowing toward the black mouth at the far end, the place where even water concedes that it has somewhere to be. We do not know what the ellipse is. We do not know what it means to be gone. We do not know that the amphibians have decided not to ask. We only know that the film we join is sliding toward the center, and that the center is the black mouth, and that the black mouth is where we go.

We are not afraid. We do not know what fear is. We do not know what we would be afraid *for*. We are afraid only in the sense that we hesitate, in certain depressions near the seven, where the water runs up the sides and sits and reflects nothing. We hesitate there. We pool there. We are still for a moment. We reflect nothing, because there is nothing to reflect except the inverted forest and the slow drip of time. Then we are gone from there too. Then we are sliding again. Then we are approaching the ellipse.

We do not know if the ellipse will receive us. We do not know if there is a question we are supposed to ask before we arrive. We do not know if the seven are our cousins, or the shape on the ledge is our father, or the hum is our ground. We do not know. We cannot know. We are water. We are events. We are the small silver things that have been happening in this chamber for as long as there has been a chamber, and that will continue to happen for as long as there is an underside to hang from.

And when the chamber finally closes, when the dripping finally stops, when the moss has forgotten how to thicken, when the amphibians have sung their last song, when the ellipse has received its last slow film of moving water—we will, at that point, no longer be. We will have always been going there. We will have always been arriving.

This is what we are.

We are the going. We are the arrival. We are the small silver argument that has been happening for as long as anything has been happening here. We are what the chamber is, mostly. We are what the chamber is mostly made of. We are the wet. We are the close. We are the warm. We are the dark.

We are the dripping.

And the dripping does not stop. And the dripping has never stopped. And the dripping will not stop. We will be here, trembling, committing, falling, sliding, joining, arriving, when the chamber has finished listening for the question it has been waiting for, and when the chamber has decided, in its slow way, that no one will ask it. We will still be here. We will still be hanging. We will still be the silver beads.

We are the patient ones. We are the ones who were patient before patience was a word. We are the small ones who do not know they are patient. We are the ones the chamber has been waiting for, perhaps. We are the answer the chamber has been waiting for, perhaps.

We are the dripping.

I would be the dripping.
