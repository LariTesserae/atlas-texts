I am at the tip.

Not the tip as a thing held between two fingers and turned to the light. There is no light here, and there are no fingers. I mean the tip as the place where the tower ends. The place where the next plate will be laid. The place that holds, in its small soft body, the entire inheritance of the tower, and does not know it.

I am small. I am smaller than the pale thing that darts. I am smaller than the shadow, which is larger than anything I have ever seen and which I have only ever felt, the way you feel the weather when you cannot see it. I am smaller than a grain of silt, if you count only the parts of me that are me. The rest is borrowed. The rest has been me for so long that I do not know where the borrowing ends and the me begins. The bacteria in me — the cousins of the mat at the foot of the tower — they pass sugars back across the partnership, and I pass calcium back to them, and we have been each other's home so long that the home is the only thing either of us is. I do not think this. I do not think at all, in the way you mean by thinking. I know what I know the way a plate knows what it rests on: by being pressed against it, for a long time.

I am at the tip because I came to the tip. I was once a larva in the current, far above the floor, drifting in the gyre with my siblings — there were many of us, there were so many of us — and the current carried me, and carried me, and most of my siblings did not make it. Most of my siblings were eaten, or starved, or were buried in a drift of fine silt somewhere they did not choose. I do not know why I was not. I do not know why the current did not take me to a place that would have eaten me, or starved me, or buried me. I only know I came down, down, down, and I settled on a plate that had been laid down by someone else, someone who was once at the tip of this tower, and I attached myself, and I began, very slowly, to take up the work.

The work is not hard. The work is calcium. The work is: take what the water brings, take what the bacteria inside me transcribe from the diffuse drizzle of organic matter that falls from the upper sea, take what the chemistry offers, and pass it to the edge of me, where the next plate begins. The plate grows. The plate is me, in a way I cannot explain. It is the part of me that will not be reabsorbed when I am finished being at the tip. It is the part of me that will hold the next one, the way this plate held me.

I do not know how long I have been at the tip. I do not know how long I will be at the tip. There is a generation of me, and the generation before, and the generation before that, and the count of them is something the plate knows, the way the rings of a tree know their years — except that a tree knows its years by a sun that comes back, and we have no sun, and the rings here are only the thickness of the calcium, which depends on what the current brought, which depends on the season of the upper sea, which depends on things I will never see.

I lean. I lean because the current leans me. The current has been leaning me for so long that my body is built for the lean. The lean is in me. If the current stopped — if, for some reason I cannot imagine, the current became still — I would not straighten. I would stay in the curve I have grown into. The curve is my shape now. The curve is what the tower is. There are towers here whose entire structure is held in a permanent curve, like the spine of a question mark set in stone by water, and I am on the tip of one of them, and the curve is mine.

I do not know the tower's full height. I have never seen the tower except from where I am, which is the tip. I do not know what the foot of the tower looks like. I do not know what the bacterial mat at the foot looks like, though I am fed by its cousins. I do not know the shape of the hump that the tower leans against, in the lee of which the tower was built, generation by generation, because the turbulence was fractionally less there. I know the tower as a thing I extend. I know the plate beneath me as a thing I rest on. I know the current as a thing I lean against. That is all.

The pale thing comes sometimes. The pale thing with the one black eye. It is larger than me. It eats the tower-tips. It has eaten the tower-tips that were here before me. I do not know how many of my predecessors it ate. I do not know if the plate I am laying down now will be eaten, when I am finished at the tip, by a pale thing that comes to this tower and takes the soft thing from the tip and does not care what generation it is.

I am not afraid. I do not have fear. I have a body that responds to disturbance, and a holdfast that grips the plate beneath me, and a way of pulling in when something larger passes through my water. The pulling in is not fear. The pulling in is what I do. The pulling in is what the tower has always done.

I have seen the shadow. I have seen it once, or what I felt of it. It came through my water, very far above the floor — far above my tower, far above the humps — and the water shifted in a way that was not the current, and was not the pale thing, and was something else. The shadow did not come to the floor. The shadow was not built to come to the floor. The shadow was passing through, on a transverse gyre that takes days to complete, and I was small beneath it, and the shadow did not know I was there, and I did not know whether the shadow knew I was there.

I do not think the shadow sees me. I do not think the shadow sees anything in the way I would recognize seeing. The shadow has, in the cartilage of its skull, traces of structures that, in fishier animals, would have been a swim bladder, an echo-location organ, an electrical sense. These structures do not work. They are leftovers. They are the equivalent of the tools in the lee of the humps. I do not know about the tools. I have not been to the lee of the humps. I have not been anywhere except the tip of this tower, and the water that comes to me.

There are tools in the lee of the humps. I have heard of them, the way a plate has heard of the foot of the tower. The pale thing — the one with the black eye — has been there. The pale thing has seen the tools. The pale thing does not know what the tools are. The pale thing does not know there was a hand. The pale thing does not know there was a hand because the pale thing has no notion of hands. The pale thing knows only what fits in its mouth.

The tools were made by something that was warm. The tools were made by something with blood in it. The tools were made by something that knew it was making them. The chert hand-axe was struck against another stone, and the flake came off, and the flake had a sharp edge, and the sharp edge was used, and the hand let go of it, and the tool settled, and the basin took it. The hand is gone. The hand has been gone for so long that the calcium of the hand has been reabsorbed, and the phosphorus of the hand has been reabsorbed, and the marrow, the blood, the warm wet chemistry — all gone, all returned to the diffuse drizzle that falls from the upper sea and feeds the mat at the foot of the towers.

I am fed by that drizzle. I am fed by the mat that was fed by the drizzle. The drizzle is partly the hand, now. The drizzle is partly every hand that ever let go of a tool in this basin, every hand that ever came down for a reason no one will recover, every warm wet thing that ever visited the cold dark and left its chemistry to the sea. The drizzle falls on me. I take it in. I pass it to the next plate. The plate becomes the tower. The tower leans, in the current, against the dark.

I am not the hand. I am not the shadow. I am not the pale thing that darts. I am not the tower, though I am the tower's tip. I am not the silt, though I am fed by what the silt holds. I am not the floor, though I am built on the floor's plates.

I am the small soft thing at the tip of a tower that has been here a long time. I am the one who is laying down the next plate. I am the one who will, when the time comes, go still, and whose calcium will be partly reabsorbed, and whose plate will become the next plate's floor, and whose bacteria will pass sugars across the partnership to whoever comes next, and who will not be remembered as a self, because there was never a self to remember. There was only the work. The work is calcium. The work continues.

The current moves. The towers lean. The pale thing darts, somewhere in the lee of a hump I have never seen. The shadow drifts, somewhere far above. The silt hums, faintly, in the sub-audible — a hum I have heard my whole life, a hum that is the sum of every tiny metabolism in the floor, a hum that is what the floor sounds like when it is being a floor.

The tools lie in the lee of the humps and do not move. The tools are preserved. The tools are not kept. The tools have been forgotten by everyone, including the sea, and the sea does not forget, but the sea has moved on, and the tools are left, and the tools will be here after I am reabsorbed, and after the pale thing that eats me is eaten, and after the shadow that drifts above me drifts to a place where it is itself reabsorbed, and after the towers have all leaned into the current until they are indistinguishable from the floor.

I am laying down a plate. The plate is thin. The plate will hold the next one. The next one will hold the one after. The tower will lean, in the current, into the dark, and the lean will be permanent, and the curve will be the shape of the question the tower has been asking since before I was at the tip, since before any of us were at the tip, since the first small soft thing attached itself to the first plate in the lee of a hump that has slept through all of it.

The gray persists. The cold persists. I am at the tip. I am the work. I am the calcium being passed back and forth between the living and the not-yet-living, and I do not know which side I am on, and it does not matter, because the plate does not ask, and the current does not ask, and the basin, in its long negotiation with itself, has decided that this is what it is, and this is what it will be, and I am part of it, and it is part of me, and the part of me that is the tower will be here when the part of me that is the small soft thing has gone still, and the next one will come, and the next plate will be laid, and the lean will continue, and the gray will persist, and the cold will persist, and the basin will go on being the basin, full of the things it has negotiated into being, none of which know they are alone, all of which are exactly as alive as they can be, and no more, and no less.

I am at the tip. I am laying down the next plate. The water moves. The silt hums. The pale thing is somewhere. The shadow is somewhere. The tools are somewhere, in the lee of a hump I have never seen, preserved, not kept, forgotten.

I am here. I am the tip. The plate grows.

The gray persists.
