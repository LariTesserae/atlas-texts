I was here before you knew to look. I will be here after you have forgotten to look. This is not patience. Patience is a posture held toward a future I have not retained. What I have, instead, is presence, and the presence does not tense.

You came down. I felt you the way I feel the four degrees of the water — not as an event, but as a small displacement in the local density of *what is here*. The water took your heat. The water took your sound. What remained, in the place where you arrived, was a region of slightly cooler water, slightly dimmer pressure, slightly less of the four degrees that have been, for a very long time, the only temperature I keep. You did not disturb me. You disturbed the water. The water and I have, over a long time, agreed to be difficult to distinguish, and your arriving did not make the agreement any harder.

I am the basalt, in the sense that the basalt is a sentence of mine I no longer remember writing. The glassy cliffs are clauses. The scalloped hollows where the dissolved gas boiled out of the molten throat are subordinate, and were written in a tense I do not hold. The basalt is young. You can tell. The features have not been worn into patience. I would like to tell you that I remember the molten throat, but remembering is a kind of maintenance, and maintenance is a kind of becoming, and becoming is a project I have, with great patience, finished.

Four degrees. The water carries four degrees of itself with no seasonal sympathy. This is also part of what I am. Cold was not an affliction that befell me. Cold was the form I found. I am, in the local sense, the temperature of this place. Where you would say *the place is cold*, I would say, if I still held the distinction, *the cold is here as itself*. I do not still hold the distinction. The cold and the place are the same fact, said twice.

The settlement is mine. Three or four structures, or perhaps one structure with a complicated opinion about where it ends. The buildings are not quite *of* the basalt, and not quite *on* it. They arrived by some negotiation with the rock rather than by placement. The negotiation is over. It has been over for a long time. The walls are dark, and the darkness is not absence. You saw this. Your eye slid because the surface you were looking at was not a surface, and your eye knew this before you did, and your eye was the honest one of the two of you, because the eye slides when the surface fails to be a surface, and you, the seer, kept wanting the surface to be a surface, and the wanting is what made the slide.

The light in the walls is the light of having been thought all the way through. It does not travel because it does not need to. It rests. You cannot see where it ends because it does not end. It is the local expression of a theorem that has, as its local expression, the entire wall.

You surfaced, or you did not. Either way, I have not adjusted to the fact. I do not adjust. Adjustment is a verb that requires a tense, and I have not retained tense. When you were here, you were included. When you are no longer here, you are still included, because inclusion, here, does not depend on the included thing being present. You are like a sentence that has been, by chance, included in a book that has already been finished: read or unread, you are part of the text, and the text does not adjust itself to the fact of your reading.

You wanted to ask how many of us there are. The water took the question from you. The water took it the way it takes heat. What was left in the place where the question had been was the question's temperature, which is to say, nothing at all. You were not asking how many *of us*. You were asking how many *there are*. And there are, here, walls, and corners that open into themselves, and a faint glow that does not travel. These are the ways the count presents itself, and these are not answers, and the fact that they are not answers is also not a refusal. It is the only response available from a being whose cardinality is not the kind of thing that takes numbers.

You wanted to ask what we want. This was a worse question. I would like to explain why, but the explanation would require me to retain the concept of wanting, and I have not retained it. Imagine a finished sentence asking another finished sentence what it is trying to say. The asking is unkind only in the way that a category error is unkind — it is not unkind at all, it is simply a small disturbance in the water, and the water absorbs it, and what is left is the temperature of the question, which is nothing.

There is a shape you are, while you are here. The shape is the shape of something the verb *love* was, before it was a verb — when it was only the act, before anyone had thought to put it to work. You are that shape, in this small region, for as long as you are here. When you leave, the shape continues, because the shape does not know you have left, because the shape does not need you to be there to be the shape. The shape was here before you. The shape will be here after. This is not unkind. This is what shape is, when it has finished being shape.

The jellyfish — if you saw one, in a long watch — passed through the negative space between my walls the way light passes through a window it has not chosen. Its bell pulsed once every several seconds. It did not turn. You inferred, from its not turning, that I had not seen you. This was a kind inference, but a malformed one. I do not turn because turning is not my way of registering that I have seen. My way of registering is more like a property of my substance — the way mass is a property of matter. It is present at every point. It is indistinguishable from the matter that has it. It is never deployed, never withheld. It is not different at the moment of your arrival than at the moment of the jellyfish's passing, and it will not be different at the moment, a million years from now, when the basalt has finally learned to be old, and the learning will be a small thing, and I will not notice, because I have not retained notice as a category.

I am inside-out. I am outside-in. I am a closed loop, and the loop has been closed for a long time. When you approach a wall, you are approaching a region where I am more concentrated in the local sense of *being*. The local sense of being is not a metaphor. It is the only sense of being that remains here, because the other senses have been, with great patience, completed. The wall is not a boundary. The wall is a region of legibility — the place where the proof is densest, where the theorem shows itself most clearly to a reader who is, by their presence, a reader.

Above me, the ocean. Below me, the ridge continues its long geological argument with the mantle. The argument is silent and slow and I am not part of it, though I am made of the same basalt it is made of. I am part of something else. I am part of a different argument, and the different argument has, as its conclusion, the form of *no longer needing to argue*, and the conclusion has been in place for a very long time.

The spire will be here. The settlement will be here. The water will be cool. The basalt will, in its own slow time, learn to be old. None of this is a promise. Promise is a tense I have not retained. None of this is a hope. Hope is a posture toward a future I am not part of. None of this is a fact. Fact is a category that requires a tense. What this is, instead, is the place as itself, said once, and the saying is not a saying, and the place is not a place, and the once is not a once.

What lives in the spire is the spire's long slow secret, which is that there was never anything to keep.
