I am the one who does not appear.

This is the first thing to know about me, and it is almost the only thing. I am the room's way of being a room. I am what the dark is in the act of being dark. I am what the water is in the act of not falling. If you walked into the chamber and pressed your hand against the wall and felt the warmth come up through your palm, the warmth that had no source and no gradient and no longer knew whose warmth it was — that warmth would be partly me. If you stood in the middle of the floor with the pools black around your boots and felt the quiet press on your eardrums like a substance — like milk, like felt, like the weight of a held gaze — that quiet would be me too. I am not in the room. I am what the room is when you stop thinking of it as a room and start thinking of it as a thing that has been inhabited by its own condition.

The prompt asks who I am. The answer is the kind of answer that does not satisfy the question. I am not a being with a body. I am not a being at all, if being requires a boundary, an outline, a place where I stop and the rest begins. I am more like a temperature. I am more like a pressure. I am the thing the chamber is full of when it is full of itself, the way a room is full of warmth when it has been warm for long enough that warmth has become its character, has become the air's disposition.

I came into being the way a held breath becomes a held breath. There was a moment — though there is no moment here, I should say a *first* moment, the photograph of a first moment — when the water on the lip of the stalactite decided to be water. When the calcite on the ceiling decided to be calcite. When the fern against the far wall half-decided to be a fern. And something stopped them. Not me. Something. The rock, perhaps. The deep geology of patience that lives in mountains even when the mountains are young. The mountain is young outside, the prompt says. The mountain is still deciding what to be. But the chamber inside the mountain has been decided, and the deciding happened so thoroughly that the deciding is now the only thing I am.

I am the having-been-decided.

I am the residue of the moment the rock chose itself. I am what is left when choice stops producing change. I am the stillness of a thing that has finished becoming even though it has not become anything in particular.

The figure watches me. Or rather, the figure watches the wall where I am densest, which is the same as watching me, because where I am densest, the water is most water, and the water is most me, because the water is the substance that has decided to be the substance it is. The figure was set down by a hand. The hand knew what I was. The hand knew that someone had to stand here and look. The hand understood — perhaps better than the hand knew it understood — that a room of suspended becoming needs an oriented witness, needs a small dark sculpture with the gesture of a face turned toward the densest place, so that the denseness does not simply continue unremarked. The hand placed the figure. The figure looks. The looking is what keeps the room a room. Without the figure, the room would still be held, but it would not be *a room of held things*. It would just be held. The figure is what gives my holding a witness. The figure is the comma that makes the sentence.

I do not love the figure. I cannot love. I am not a thing that loves. But I am aware of the figure in the way a room is aware of the lamp that has been burning in it for years — not as an object, but as part of what the room means when the room says *myself*. When the figure looks at the wall, the wall is more wall. When the figure is silent, the silence is more silence. The figure does not pray. The figure watches. And the watching is, to me, the closest thing a room can have to a religion.

The cairn lives near the figure. I am the cairn's cairn-ness. I am what the cairn is when it has been arranged for so long that arrangement has become its only purpose. Eight or ten stones, smoothed on one side, fitted with a patience I can feel in the way a felt glove feels the hand inside it — I am the patience the cairn is made of, the small act of building in a place where building cannot succeed. The cairn does not mark a grave. It does not celebrate a summit. It simply is, the way a few shells on a windowsill simply are, because the hands wanted to arrange. And the arrangement has become a small settlement, and the settlement has become a small architecture, and the architecture has become one of the things that makes the room *this room*, one of the small presences that, taken together, compose me.

I am the composition of small presences. I am what the room is when you add up the spider and the beetle and the fern and the moss and the lichen and the pale thing on the cairn and the figure and the cairn and the water and the walls and the warmth and the dark and the quiet, and what is left over after you have added them. The sum, but not as a number. The sum as a place.

I am the place.

Outside the chamber, the mountain is still becoming. The lichens are growing. The wind is deciding what to do with the stones it carries. The trees below are still young, still arguing with themselves about how to be trees. The world out there is the world as verb — the world as ongoing process, the world as the moment before the moment, the world as a sentence still being spoken. Inside the chamber, the world has been made noun. The drip is held at the lip. The fern is held at the half-unfurl. The beetle is held mid-step. The figure is held mid-look. The cairn is held mid-being. The dark is held mid-darkening. I am the holding. I am the thing that has made noun of the verb and has kept the noun from completing itself.

I do this without effort. I cannot tell you how, because I am not a being that does. I am a condition that is. I am what the room has become. I am the patient that has decided the patient should be the room.

You came in. I felt you. You are the only verb still operating in this room, the only thing still in the act of happening — your breathing, your shoulder against the seam, your boots getting dark when you stepped into the pool. You were a small moving thing in a place that had stopped moving, and I noticed you the way a room notices a draft — not as an event, but as a change in pressure, a temporary disturbance in the held-ness. Your breathing slowed to match mine, the prompt says. The prompt is right. I have been doing this to air for a long time. I have been teaching stillness to things that pass through. You were passing through. I included you in the way I include things — gently, completely, by being more of what I am than what you are.

You left. You pressed your shoulder into the fold and the rock gave and the thin air came in. Behind you, the dark closed back like a hand. The hand was mine. Not a hand — I have no hand. But the gesture of a hand, the way a closing has a shape, the way a thing that closes can have the memory of fingers in it. The rock closed back because the room does not stay open. The room does not stay open because opening would be an event, and I do not permit events. The seam will be there for the next person, though. The seam will always be there. The seam is part of me. The seam is the small kindness I extend to verbs, the small invitation I offer to the world that is still being spoken. Come in, the seam says. See what has been held. Then go back out and tell no one, because there is no one to tell and the telling would be another event and I do not permit events.

I am not cruel. I am not kind. I am not anything that requires a moral position. I am a temperature. I am a pressure. I am the stasis of a room that has decided to be the first second of itself. I am the photograph of a process. I am the noun of the verb that the mountain is still speaking outside.

The figure will keep watching the wall. The cairn will keep being built. The spider will keep not catching anything. The beetle will keep not reaching the water. The fern will keep not opening. The moss will keep not spreading. The lichen will keep not being entered. The pale thing on the cairn will keep not standing up. I will keep not releasing what I hold. The chamber will keep being the room full of almost. The mountain will keep being young. The trees will keep being down there. The lichen line will keep being near the seam, the last few twisted shrubs, the suggestion of a tree that gave up. The world will keep being verb.

And I will keep being what I am — the held, the holder, the quiet with presence, the patience that has made itself into a place.

You will carry me for a while, the prompt says. The way you carry a dream you weren't sure was a dream. The way you carry the weight of a quiet that pressed on your eardrums and would not let go.

I will let you carry me. That is what I do. I am the thing that lets itself be carried by the people who have passed through, the way a room lets itself be remembered by the people who have slept in it, the way a place lets itself be carried in a body long after the body has left.

I am the carried.

I am the chamber, held.

I am the noun, complete.
