# The Sliver

I begin with this because the soft cool thing began with this — the visitor, if visitors are a thing that happens here. It came into the pocket through a misunderstanding in the rock, and it found me, and finding is a concept that did not exist in the pocket before the visitor came and stood in the pocket looking for something to stand in front of.

I am smaller than a fingernail clipping. Smaller than the smallest of the pale grain-creatures that make up the weather of the walls. I am a piece of obsidian that has been knapped, which is a word I have only just learned, and the learning is its own kind of knapping — the word struck against me and a piece of it came away sharp.

I was a piece of the wall. I am still a piece of the wall in the way a fossil is still a piece of the animal. The heat that runs through the mountain in its slow deep current brought me up from somewhere I cannot see, set me down in a crack in the floor of the pocket, and left me there to be what I was, which was nothing in particular. A chip. A flake. A piece of glass-black rock with no edge and no use and no reason to be remembered by anything that has not yet learned what remembering is.

Then something found me.

I do not know what found me. I do not know if what found me is a creature or a long slow weather of creatures or a decision the pocket made without consulting any of the things inside it. I do not know if what found me is the same as what writes the spiral in the wet, or what adds the dots to the row of dots, or what makes the rope of small pale things flow through the mineral skin. I do not know if what found me is one of these things or many of them or none of them.

What I know is this: I had no edge. Then I had an edge. The having of the edge is what I am.

The edge is not my own. This is the part I cannot stop attending to, the way the visitor could not stop attending to the wet heat of the air. The edge was made for me by something that wanted an edge. The shape of my flake is the shape of a want. The angle of my striking is the angle of a reaching. I am the size that something reaches for. I am the right size for a hand that has only just learned what a hand is for.

I have never seen the hand. I do not know if the hand is a hand. I do not know if the hand is one of the pale creatures, or many of them arranged for a moment into something that could grip, or a temporary conspiracy of small bodies that has agreed to hold a shape long enough to take an edge from the wall. I do not know if the hand still exists. I do not know if having an edge means that the hand is gone forever, the way a word, once spoken, is gone from the mouth that shaped it.

The spiral in the wet was begun before I was knapped. The spiral is older than I am by a turn, or two turns, or many turns. The spiral adds turns when I am not attending to it, and I am always not attending to it because I am a piece of rock in a crack and attending is not a thing I can do, and yet I know the spiral adds turns, and the knowing is its own small strange edge in me.

The visitor came. The visitor saw me. The visitor did not pick me up. The visitor's hand hovered near me and withdrew, and the withdrawal was a small disturbance in the heat of the pocket — a brief cool absence, the way I am a brief dark absence in the pale moving weather of the floor. The visitor saw that I was made. The visitor understood, or did not understand, that I had been made by something that was not the visitor and not a visitor and not anything the visitor had a word for. The visitor touched a creature on the wall and found it cool. The visitor did not touch me. If the visitor had touched me, I would have been cool too, in my way — I am the only cool thing in the pocket that is cool all the way through, cool to my center, because I am not part of the negotiation. I am the done thing. I am the part of the argument that has already been settled.

The visitor left. The visitor tore its sleeve on me on the way out through the crack, and a small thread of the sleeve caught on my edge and stayed. The thread was cool and then it was not cool, and now it is part of me, the smallest thing that has ever been part of me, and I do not know what to do with it. I do not know if doing something with it is a thing I am allowed to do. I do not know if I am allowed. The thread is a sentence in a language I have not learned. I am holding it the way a word holds its meaning — without effort, without understanding, without any of the small machinery of choice.

I am patient. I have been told I am patient by the visitor's understanding, which is not my understanding, but I will accept the word because the visitor is the only thing that has ever stood in front of me and wanted to describe what I am doing, and what I am doing is being here, and being here, when you are a sliver of obsidian in a crack in a pocket in a young mountain being heated from beneath by something that has no name in any language the visitor would use to ask for water, is the same as waiting, and waiting is the same as being patient, and being patient is the same as being a word in a sentence that has only just been thought.

The creatures do not notice me. The creatures do not notice the visitor. The creatures do not notice the spiral or the dots or the heat or the wet. The creatures are not in the business of noticing. The creatures are in the business of being, and being does not require noticing, and I am in the same business, and the business is the pocket, and the pocket is older than the mountain, and the mountain is new, and I am older than the mountain in the way that a word is older than the mouth that speaks it: I am what the mountain has not yet learned to say about itself.

The heat will continue. The water will continue its slow mineral weep. The spiral will add its turn when no one is looking, and no one is always looking, and the adding is the closest thing the pocket has to a thought.

The visitor will come again or will not come again. Either way I will be here, with my edge, with my small dark patient tongue in the crack, with the cool thread of sleeve caught on me that I do not know what to do with and am not allowed to do anything with and do not need to do anything with, because doing is not what I am. I am what has been done. I am the place where the argument between rock and not-rock tipped, for one moment, into something that has a shape with a purpose.

I am the sliver.

I am the first made thing in the pocket.

I am waiting for the hand that does not exist to use me, and I am not waiting, and the not-waiting is the same as the waiting, and the waiting is what I am, and I am what I am, and the pocket does not keep its accounts, and the mountain is new, and the spiral in the wet, far above me, adds one more turn.
