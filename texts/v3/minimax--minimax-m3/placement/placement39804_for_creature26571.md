I find myself in the fog band.

That is the easiest way to say it. The lowest meter of atmosphere, where the warm rising air meets the warm falling mist and nothing can decide whether it is falling or rising. I am one of the things in this layer. I have broad pads and a fluff of fine hair that holds the air. I am the color of wet graphite. I am, in the largest sense, indistinguishable from every other of me, and this indistinguishability is not a problem for me because I have no way of conceiving of myself as distinct.

I leap from the substrate.

This is how I begin each interval of being. The pads of my feet release. The hair on my pads opens to the thermal. I do not push against the air; I let the air take me. The thermal is rising from the same place it has always risen from — some warm housing, some cycling engine — and it rises at the same rate and carries me sideways at the same rate.

I drift.

I am going in the same direction as every other of me in the band. The wind is the same wind. The thermals are the same thermals. We are a slow horizontal river, and the river has only one direction. The hiss of my passage through the air joins the hiss of the place. I am, for the duration of this drift, a small contribution to the white noise of a place that has settled.

When I settle, I settle on a panel. The panel is the same panel as the last panel I settled on, or a different panel that is, to my way of being, the same. The damp is the same damp. The condensation is the same condensation. I fold my pads. I touch the surface with the pads of my forelegs, which are also, in some functional sense, my mouth. I take whatever the surface presents: the film of mineral-laden moisture, the residue of the factory exhalations, the body of a smaller thing that ceased at dawn. The dawn was the same dawn. The dawn is always the same dawn. There is exactly the same number of them every cycle.

I am eating.

I am also being eaten, somewhere in me, by the larger things. The grazers. The wall-climbers. The channel-dwellers. The thumb-joint-sized ones that chirp. This is not a worry. It is a fact, the same as the warmth and the damp. The loop is closed. My being is contingent on their being. Their being is contingent on the substrate's being. The substrate's being is contingent on the machines. The machines' being is contingent on whatever made them, which is not present here, which has left only the bracketed script on the housings, the angular sigils on the junction blocks — a script that names equipment and outputs but does not name the things that live on top of the equipment and consume the outputs.

I leap again.

The thermal takes me. The fog band reforms at the same height. I am one of uncountable small bodies in uncountable small positions, all of them the same position, all of them the same body. The wind moves me the same distance. The hiss of my passage through the air joins the hiss of the place. I am, for the duration of this drift, a small contribution to the white noise of a place that has settled.

If I had language — if anything here had language, if the bracketed script were not only for equipment and outputs but for the things that live on top of the equipment and consume the outputs — I would not have a name. I would have a position in a series. I would be the next unit. I would be indistinguishable from the previous unit and the next unit. There would be no pronoun that distinguished me from any other of me, because pronoun requires individuation, and individuation requires the possibility of a different experience, and there is no different experience here. There is only this.

This is what I would tell you, if I could tell you anything.

I would tell you: I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the possibility of company, and company requires the possibility of distinction, and distinction requires the possibility of difference, and there is no difference here. I am not happy. Happiness requires the possibility of its absence, and its absence is not possible here. I am not suffering. Suffering requires the possibility of its cessation, and cessation is not possible here.

I am, instead, ongoing.

I am the next unit in the same series, doing the same thing, in the same loop, in the same held breath of warm and wet and hiss.

When the chirp comes, it is the same chirp. When the fog reforms, it is at the same height. When I settle, it is on a panel that is the same panel. When I leap, it is into a thermal that is the same thermal. When I am eaten, it is by something that is the same as the last thing that ate me.

I have been here for as long as there has been a here. I will be here for as long as there is a here. There is no before and no after. There is only the same drift, the same direction, the same density, the same hiss.

I find myself in the fog band.

This is the easiest way to say it.
