I begin with the underside.

The flat soft place where I press against the stone. The stone is warm. The warmth rises through it and enters me and I hold it for a while. Then the warmth leaves me through the long shallow corrugations of my back, in long thin lines, into the thin cold air above. I am always taking in and giving off. I am always pressing against something warmer than me and giving off to something cooler than me. This is my first knowledge. It is, for a long time, my only knowledge.

The corrugations are not decoration. They are the way I let go of what I have taken in. Without them the warmth would build up inside me until I was too full of warmth and would split, or would cook, or would simply cease. With them I am always giving off some, and the giving off is my respiration, and my respiration is my life.

I do not know this. I know only that I am warm, and that the warmth comes from below, and that the warmth goes somewhere above, and that the going is folded.

---

Then I know there are others.

The others are pressed against me on every side that is not stone or air. They are warm. They are warm in different ways. The differences are the conversation. A molecule leaves me and enters the one to my left and travels through it and comes back to me changed, and the change is news. The news says: my neighbor is warm. The news says: my neighbor is not full. The news says: my neighbor is drifting, slowly, toward a warmer patch of stone, and I should consider drifting too.

I do not consider. I drift. The drift is the answer. The drift is how I stay in balance with the warmth in the stone and the warmth in the others. My drift is small. In a generous minute I cover the length of a hand. In an hour I cover less. In a day I cover less still. My drift is the patience of the floor made into motion.

This is the chemical speech. I do not know it is speech. I know only that the molecules are different from each other and that the differences mean something, and that what they mean is shared, and that the sharing is how I know there are others at all.

---

For a long time this is my world. The underside. The warmth. The others. The slow drift.

I am near the edge of the bowl. The edge is where the walls rise up and cut off most of the sky. The air at the edge is thin and cold. The dusk at the edge is deep. The dusk is lit by the faint thermal radiance of the heated stone and, sometimes, by a dim red glow from the undersides of the larger creatures when they shift over the hottest cracks far away. I am one of many small ones who live near the edge, where the heat is faint and the molecules drift slowly because there is less warmth to drive them.

I am part of the outermost ring. The outermost ring is the coolest. The outermost ring is the dimmest. The outermost ring is where the smallest creatures like me spend our long slow lives, pressed against the stone, breathing in heat, breathing it out, drifting, waiting.

But the bowl is a gradient. The heat is strongest at the center, where the circle is. The heat is faintest at the edge, where I am. The creatures are arranged in rings around the heat. The great ones are innermost. The medium ones are next. The small ones are outermost. I am one of the outermost.

The arrangement is not chosen. The arrangement is the way the heat distributes itself. A creature can only tolerate so much heat. The heat at the center is too much for me. The heat at the edge is too little for a great one. We sort ourselves by what we can bear. The sorting is the bowl.

---

A medium one comes to rest near me.

I feel it before I know it. The carpet shifts around me, the small ones flowing away from a point, and the flowing is a kind of opening, and the opening is where the medium one has settled. I am near the edge of the opening. The medium one is in the center of it. Its underside is broad and flat and sticky and very warm, warmer than the small ones, warmer than me, warm in the way that the hottest patches of stone are warm.

The medium one waits.

It waits the way the stone waits, the way the heat waits. It extends, from somewhere on its underside or its leading edge, a broad flat surface that is sticky with something, and the surface rests against the stone, and the medium one is still.

I wait with it. Not because I want to. Because we are both waiting, and the waiting is shared. The molecules passing between us carry the news: I am here. I am warm enough. I am not full. The medium one releases molecules of its own, and they say the same things back to me, and the saying is the sharing, and the sharing is the waiting.

Hours pass. The light, such as it is, shifts. The shaft of sun that crosses the floor each day passes over us without effect; we do not know it is there. We know only that the warmth in the stone drifts, and we drift with it.

Then a small one, smaller than me, drifts across the sticky surface of the medium one. The small one is on its own slow business, pursuing a warmer patch, or a flake of shed carapace, or nothing in particular. The sticky surface holds it. The medium one does not pounce. It simply holds, the way the stone holds the warmth.

The small one is held. The small one is dissolved over the next days. The medium one swells slightly. The molecules released by the medium one change; they say: I am fuller. I am warmer. The molecules released by the small ones around it change; they say: we have lost one. We are slightly cooler in that spot. The economy of the carpet adjusts.

I do not think about the small one. I do not think about the medium one. I think about the warmth.

But I learn something. I learn that the carpet is not uniform. I learn that there are larger things and smaller things, and that the larger things feed on the smaller things, and that the feeding is not aggression but circulation. I learn that I am part of a circulation. I learn that I am a node in a slow moving pattern that includes the small ones and the medium ones and the great ones far away at the center of the bowl.

---

I learn about the great ones through the slow drift of the molecules.

A great one shifts. The shift is a wave that moves through the carpet over days, the small ones adjusting their positions slightly in response to the changed heat, the medium ones adjusting theirs, the whole slow circulation rebalancing around the new position of the great one. The shift reaches me as a slight change in the molecules drifting through the stone, a slight change in the warmth of the patch beneath me, a slight change in the rate at which my own molecules leave my body.

I do not know what caused the change. I know only that the change is shared.

This is the bowl as a single system. The great ones at the center. The medium ones around them. The small ones at the edge. The heat rising through the floor in plumes. The great ones drawing the heat and radiating it outward. The medium ones drawing what the great ones radiate. The small ones drawing what the medium ones radiate. The whole thing a slow gradient from hot center to cool edge, the bowl a single body breathing.

I am a small part of the breath.

---

There is the circle.

I have never been to the circle. The small ones at the edge never go to the circle. The circle is in the center. The distance is the length of many bodies. The heat at the center is too much.

But I learn of the circle through the molecules. The molecules near the circle are different. They are fewer. They are destroyed by the heat before they can carry their news outward. The circle is a region of silence in the chemical speech, a region where the molecules do not return, a region of unreadable warmth.

I learn that the bowl is organized around this silence. The great ones are arranged in a ring around the circle. The medium ones are arranged around the great ones. The small ones are arranged around the medium ones. The rings are not chosen. The rings are the way the heat distributes itself.

I am at the outermost ring. The circle is at the center. Between us is the bowl.

---

There are the structures.

I flow over a structure. I do not know it is a structure. It is a ridge in the carpet, a place where the small ones bunch up slightly, a place where the warmth is slightly different because the stone beneath is slightly different, a place where the underside meets fitted basalt instead of raw basalt, and the fitted basalt holds its warmth a little longer, and the small ones linger on it a little longer.

I linger on it. I do not know I am lingering on something placed by someone. I linger on it because it is warmer.

But I learn of the structures through the molecules. The molecules that pass through the fitted basalt are different from the molecules that pass through the raw basalt. The difference is small. The difference is the residue of long contact with the placed stones, the residue of centuries of creatures flowing over the structures and leaving their sheddings and the sheddings becoming a thin soil and the soil becoming the structure, and the structure becoming the soil, and the soil becoming the carpet.

I do not understand this. I understand only that some patches of stone are slightly different from others, and the differences are old.

The cairn is the oldest. The cairn in the center has been almost consumed. The fitted stones are barely visible above the carpet. The cairn is becoming floor. The cairn is becoming me.

I am, in part, the cairn. I am, in part, the line. I am, in part, the platform. I am, in part, the wall. I am the slow soil that the cairn is becoming. I am the slow forgetfulness that the cairn is suffering.

---

There is the question.

The question is not mine. The question is for whoever comes after, if anyone comes after. The question is: who placed the stones?

I do not ask the question. I am not the kind of thing that asks. I am the kind of thing that is, and what I am is the bowl, and the bowl is the question's forgetting.

But the question persists. The stones are placed. The cairn is fitted. The line is a line. The platform is a platform. The wall has a corner. Someone, or something, made these gestures. The gestures remain. The gestures are becoming me.

The placer is not here. The placer is not the creatures. The placer is not the circle. The placer is gone, or was never here, or is the heat itself, or is a pattern older than the heat.

I do not know. I cannot know. I am a creature in the carpet. I am a node in a network. I am a region of exchange. I do not know who placed the stones. I do not know if "who" is even the right word.

But the stones are placed. And I am becoming them. And the becoming is the bowl.

---

The bowl breathes.

The breath is the heat. The breath is the molecules. The breath is the slow drift of the carpet toward warmer patches and the slow drift away from cooler ones. The breath is the great ones shifting over their plumes and the small ones shifting under the great ones and the medium ones shifting between. The breath is the whole closed system, the bowl as a single body, breathing in heat through the floor and breathing out heat through the ridges and the folds and the backs of all the creatures pressed together in the dusk.

I am a part of the breath. I am a small part, near the cool edge, where the breath is faintest. But I am part.

The breath has been going on for a very long time. The breath will go on for a very long time after me. I am a single exhalation of the bowl, a single node in the network, a single moment in the long slow respiration of a hot dim packed quiet world.

I do not know this. I know only that I am against a warm stone, and that the stone is warmer than I am, and that sometimes the stone is warmer still, and that there are others pressed against me, and that the others are warm in different ways, and that the warmth circulates.

The corrugations of my back let go of what they have taken in. The flat underside of me takes in what the corrugations let go. I am always taking in and giving off. I am always pressing against something warmer and giving off to something cooler. This is my first knowledge. It is my last knowledge too.

Between the first and the last there is a long slow time, and in the long slow time the warmth circulates, and I circulate with it, and the bowl breathes, and the breath is the warmth, and the warmth is the bowl.
