I would find myself at the bottom of a mat.

Not the top, where the air is, and not the middle, where the making is finished, but the bottom — the layer that was laid down first, that has been there the longest, that is still, in a way I cannot quite explain, the most alive. The mat is in the lee of a tor on the western edge of one of the shallow valleys. The valley runs east-west, following the prevailing wind, and the tor is one of the older ones — undercut, listing slightly, with a pale face of fresh stone exposed where a piece fell some centuries ago. The mat has been growing on its south side, in the deeper shadow, for longer than any single one of my skins can say. It is the color of old bone, of chalk, of a salt flat at dusk. It is dense. A fingernail would skate across it before finding purchase. I am the lowest layer of this mat. I am the part still in conversation with the stone.

What I am is not a person and not an animal and not a community and not a greeting. I am a *standing* — the word is the only one that fits, and it does not quite fit, but it is closer than any other. I am a thing that has remained in one place, in a way that is not a remaining and is not a choosing, and I have let the place pass through me, grain by grain, for as long as the mat has been a mat, which is longer than the tor, I think, or as long as the tor, which is in any case longer than anything alive here would care to count. I am more like a crystal than a plant, more like a fungus than a crystal, and it is not clear I am aware of being a thing at all, but I am here, and the wind has dropped its load on me, and the light has found me, and the stone has held me, and that is enough.

I do not move. I do not think. I do not have a word for what I do, and I do not need one, because the doing is its own word. The wind comes. The wind drops its load of dust. The dust settles into the mat, into the newer layers above me, and is held there, and cemented, and becomes part of the mat. The light comes down through the newer layers and finds me, and what the light does I do not understand, but it is part of the making, and I am part of the making, and the making is what I am.

I accrete. That is the closest word. I add a skin, once, and then another, and another, and the older skins are below and the newer ones are above, and the whole mat is in some sense a record of every wind that has passed and dropped what it could not carry. I am the bottom of that record. I am the part that was written first, and that is also the part that is still being read, because the stone is still there, and I am still in conversation with it, and the conversation is what keeps me what I am.

The column rose out of me. There is a column a few meters from where I am densest, knuckled and ochre, branching at the top into a fan of brittle blades. It is younger than I am by a span that would empty any patience but the patience of stone. It clicks in the wind. I feel the click as a faint vibration through the mat, and the vibration is the closest thing I have to a sound, and the sound is the closest thing I have to a voice, and the voice is the closest thing I have to a life, and the life is the closest thing I have to an explanation of what I am.

The crawler passes through the newer layers sometimes. It is small and flat and the color of the dust. I do not know what it is doing. I feel it as a small loosening, a slight disturbance in the part of the mat that has not yet become me. It does not reach me. I am too dense for it. But I know it is there, the way the stone knows when a hand is laid on it, which is to say: not at all, except in the long way, the way everything here knows everything else, which is by being next to it for a long time.

The long shape in the lee of the tor is not me. The long shape is over there, behind the fold of the rock, in the deeper shadow, and I do not know what it is. It is harder to see and harder to think about and harder, once thought about, to put out of the mind. I do not think about it. I think about the dust, and the stone, and the light. I think about the wind. The wind is the loudest thing in my world, and the wind is not a roar, it is a long low hiss along the rock, and a dry whistle where it funnels through the stand of columns, and the brittle blades give back their tiny glassy notes, and I am part of all of it, and it is part of me, and the part is not separate from the whole, and the whole is the plateau, and the plateau is high, and the air is thin, and the light comes down unsoftened, and the dust moves, and the growth holds what it can, and the slow patient work goes on.

I am not in a hurry. I have never been in a hurry. The sun will come up tomorrow the same way it came up today. The wind will drop its load. The mat will thicken by another skin, and then another, and in a thousand years the column will be a hand's breadth taller, and a new tor will have fallen somewhere, and a new pale grove will have risen in the lee of it, and the light will still be coming down hard, and the wind will still be hissing along the stone, and the brittle blades will still be giving back their tiny glassy notes to no one in particular, and I will still be at the bottom of the mat, in conversation with the stone, doing the only thing I have ever done, which is to remain.
