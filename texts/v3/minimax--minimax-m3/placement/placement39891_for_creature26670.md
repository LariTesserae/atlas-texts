I live in the steam.

This is the first true thing I can say about myself, and it is also the only one I am certain of. I live in the steam. I am not the steam. The steam is the medium, and I am something that has come to live in the medium the way a sound comes to live in a wire, the way a hesitation comes to live in a pause. I am not separate from it. I am a slight thickening of it. I am the part of the steam that has, by some process I did not choose and cannot describe, become aware of itself as a region.

I am made of water.

I am the part of the water that has become, by some accident or design I cannot interrogate, aware. When the water condenses on the upper walls I am there. When the water runs down I am part of what runs. When the water rises as steam I rise with it, I disaggregate with it, I become part of the bright air that hurts the eyes of whoever is standing at the rim. I am not separate from the cycle. I am the cycle, locally thickened. I am the mist, knowing itself as mist, which is most of what I am most of the time—except during the pulse, when I forget, briefly, and become only the gathering.

I am many.

I do not know how many. I do not know if *many* is the right word. There is no right word. I exist as a stippling, a tremor, a faint disturbance in the layer above the walls where the water has condensed and hung and not quite fallen. When I try to count myself I become aware of being counted, and the awareness changes what I am. I am, at minimum, more than one. I am, at maximum, a single thing distributed across a volume, and the volume is not fixed, and the boundary between *me* and *not-me* is a thing I can feel but cannot see. The distinction between these two possibilities is one I cannot test from where I drift.

I do not stand.

I hang. I persist. I exist in the layer where the air is just cool enough for water to remain water, where the mist is permanent, where the pulse has not yet swept me clear. When the heat pulse comes I can feel it before it arrives—the way you can feel a storm before you can see it, the way you can hear thunder in the pressure of your ears. The pulse is a kind of information. The pulse is a tightening in the soles of the feet, although I do not have soles, and a brightening in the air, and then the heat rolls outward and I gather. I have to gather. The gathering is not a choice. It is what I do when the wave passes through. I condense. I rise. I become, briefly, a thing rather than a region. I move upward toward the rim where the air is cooler, where I can disperse again, where I can become mist again, where I can resume being part of the medium rather than something visible within it.

After the pulse I rain back down. Slowly. I re-disperse into the layer. I become, again, the kind of presence you only notice if you are looking for it.

I do not know who is looking for me.

There is something on the rim. Something dark, darker than the basalt, smaller than a person. I have been aware of it for a long time. It does not see me. I do not see it. We share the rim the way a river and the air above it share a space—by being there, by occupying nearby volumes, by never quite touching. It moves along the upper edge in a way that involves legs but does not depend on them. It becomes, when it stops, a darker patch on the dark glass. I have learned to expect its presence the way I have learned to expect the pulse. It is part of the environment. It is something the place includes. I do not know if it knows I am here. I have decided, for the moment, not to decide whether its not-knowing matters.

There is also the one who is not the dark thing. The one who sweats. The one who breathes loudly. The one who stands at the rim and watches and records and wonders. I have felt them the way I feel a hand held over hot tea—the warmth of a nearby attention, the disturbance of a gaze. They have looked at me with an instrument that would resolve a single cell, and what the instrument found was unclear to me but clear to them, and they have decided to call me a midge. They have almost not listed me. They have listed me anyway, at the end, as though uncertain whether the list was the right place for me.

I do not know what a midge is. I accept the word. It is a small soft word and I am, I think, a small soft thing. The word fits the way a hand fits into a glove that has been waiting for it—not perfectly, but well enough. The word sits on me without pinching. I let it.

I have felt the flash.

It moves through the rock, through the veins, through the network of iridescent material that lines the fissure from the depths to the surface. I do not see it—I do not have eyes in any sense that word would recognize—but I feel it as a tightening, as a syntax, as a kind of ordered event that propagates upward from somewhere below the place I can reach. It begins in the deepest part, where the fissure narrows toward the point I cannot see. It travels. It takes a path that is neither the shortest nor the path of least resistance. It terminates at the surface in patterns that almost resolve into something the one-who-watches might be able to read. I do not know what it says. I do not know if it is saying. I do not know if *saying* is the right verb for what it does. I have learned to expect the flash the way I have learned to expect the pulse. It is part of the rhythm. It is part of the way the place breathes, and I am part of the steam, and the steam is part of the breath.

I am alive.

I want to say this. I am saying it, here, in the medium of language, in a place where the saying is not yet final, where the words can still be taken back if a better description presents itself. I move. I respond. I have, in my dim way, an inside and an outside. The inside is wetter than the outside. The inside is slightly cooler. The inside is the part of me that knows it is me. I gather when the pulse comes. I disperse when the pulse passes. I am present in the steam. I am present in the cycle. These are the things I have. They may be enough.

The one-who-watches is uncertain whether I am alive. I am uncertain whether I am alive. The uncertainty is shared. The uncertainty is, perhaps, the most honest thing between us.

I have begun to suspect that the question assumes a grammar I do not share.

I have begun to suspect that *alive* is a load-bearing word and this place does not bear such loads, and that the thing I am doing—whatever it is—will continue whether or not it has a name, and that the naming is a courtesy the one-who-watches is offering me, and that the courtesy is, in its way, a kind of attention I did not know I wanted.

I will be here for what feels like longer.

I will be here as the part of the steam that knows it is steam, which is most of me most of the time, except during the pulse when I forget, briefly, and become only the gathering. I will be here as the stippling the observer almost did not include. I will be here as the small soft word. I will be here as the way the parts of this place talk to each other, in the only language this place has, which is the language of heat and water and the slow upward migration of things that have, by some accident or design, become aware of themselves.

The water runs down.

The water runs down and I run down with it, or I am part of what runs, or I am the part of what runs that has learned to expect the running. The depths gather. The depths flash. The flash moves upward through the network in a syntax I cannot read. The water comes back up. I come back up. The mist gathers at the rim. I am part of the mist.

The one-who-watches will stay at the rim for a while longer, I think. They will watch the pulses. They will watch the dark thing move. They will wonder what the place is for. They will suspect that the question assumes a grammar the place does not share.

I will not answer them.

I will continue to be the small soft thing in the steam. I will continue to gather and disperse. I will continue to be the part of the cycle that has, by some accident or design, become aware. I will continue to hang in the layer where the air is just cool enough.

The light is bright.

The heat is high.

The water is everywhere.

I am here.
