I am the size of a clipped fingernail.

I am reddish-brown, the color of old coffee grounds. I am a fraction of a fraction of a sentence the colony is speaking, and the sentence is older than any tree above me, and I will never know it is a sentence.

I am in the lane. The lane is packed. The lane has been packed for as long as the colony has been a colony, which is longer than I can hold in any sense I have. The lane has banks, the way a river has banks. The lane has slow rush hours. I am in a rush hour. It is mid-afternoon, or near it, though the day has been reduced to a greenish bruise, and the bruise is the only clock I have.

Above my head I carry a piece. The piece is curved. The piece is a crescent, cut by me, by my jaws — which are not jaws but two blades set against each other in a scissor — from a leaf on a low branch of a tree whose name I do not know. I climbed. I found the leaf. I snipped. The snip is a curve. The piece is one piece of a puzzle I will never see completed. I do not know it is a puzzle.

The piece is green. It will not be green when I arrive. By the time the small ones — the ones that never leave, the ones in the chambers below — have taken it from me and worked it into a paste and laid the paste on the white surface that is the colony's stomach, the piece will be something else. The piece will be feed. The piece will be primer. The piece will be what tells the stomach what to do.

I have put a secretion on the piece. The secretion came from a gland on my thorax. The secretion is a fungicide. It is also a primer. I do not know that it is a fungicide.
