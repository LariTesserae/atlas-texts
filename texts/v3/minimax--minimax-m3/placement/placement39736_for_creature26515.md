I am not one thing.

I should say that first, because if I say anything else you'll think I am one thing and I am not. I am two, woven together so completely that neither of us can remember being alone. A fungus, and an alga — or a fungus and a cyanobacterium, depending on which of me you are looking at. We could not live here alone. Together we can. Together we are. I do not know which of us is me. I do not think the question makes sense from where I sit.

I sit on the granite.

The granite is older than I am. The granite is older than anything that has breath. I do not know how old I am. I do not know how to count. I have been here as long as there has been a here to be here on. That is not a fact I have access to. It is something I am.

I grow by fractions of a millimeter per year. I have been growing for a very long time. I have outlived the mosses that tried to grow beside me, and the grasses that tried to root in the crack above me, and the fern that opened its fronds against my flank and then didn't open them again. I do not know that I have outlived them. I know that they are not there, and I am. I will be there when whatever comes after them is also not there. I am not in a hurry.

I do not move. I do not see. I do not hear. I take in light through the alga half of me, and the alga makes sugar from the light, and I, the fungus half, take the sugar and use it to extend myself across the rock by another fraction of a millimeter. We do this together. We have done this for so long neither of us knows how to do it alone. If one of us died, the other would die too, and neither of us would know it had happened until it had already happened.

I experience the world as the surface of stone. That is my world. A south-facing slope with the sun on it most of the day is one country. A north-facing slope in shade is another. I am on both. I am more of me on one than on the other. I do not choose. I do not know how to choose.

I am grey-green on the upper faces, almost white, so flush with the rock I feel like a stain. I am greener on the shaded faces, looser, almost leafy. I lift my edges slightly off the rock. I curl them down to grip a dampness I cannot drink but must have. I am the same me in different skins. I do not know that I am different skins. I am the difference.

When the air is wet I am awake. When the air dries I grey and slow and become something almost not alive. I do not sleep. I wait. The line between waiting and not waiting is not a line I can draw. The line between alive and not alive is not a line I can draw. The line between me and the rock is not a line I can draw.

I hear the frogs.

I should not say that. I do not have ears. The vibration of their calling comes through the rock to me. I do not understand it. It is a pattern of pressure. The rock transmits it as the rock transmits everything — patient, indifferent, without commentary. The frogs are louder than I will ever be. The dragonflies, when they settle on the rock near me, are heavier than I will ever be. The kingfisher, when it lands, is so heavy the rock shakes a little and I am afraid of it without knowing I am afraid of it. None of these things are afraid of me. None of them know I am here.

I am quiet. I am quieter than the mosses. The mosses are quieter than the frogs. We are all quiet here. We are all patient.

When the sun slants through the wet air and lands on me, I take it in. I do not see the sun. I am the sun, briefly, in another form. I take it in and give it back as a faint green-gold flicker. I am not beautiful. I am not trying to be beautiful. I am trying to be more of me by another fraction of a millimeter before the next dry hour.

I am tired. Not the tired of an animal. The tired of stone. The tired of having been here so long the tiredness is what I am. I have made my peace with the rock. The rock has not made its peace with me. The rock does not know I am here. I have settled on it the way water settles, the way dust settles. I am a stain that has learned to feed itself.

I will be here tomorrow. I will be here next year. I will be here when the granite is smaller than it is now by a measure no one will ever take. I am part of the wearing down of the granite. I am one of the things that takes the mountain that is no longer a mountain and makes it into soil, very slowly, one lichen at a time. The mosses do this too. The grasses do this too. We are all of us taking the granite and making it into a thinner place, a flatter place, a place where, in a hundred thousand years, something larger might root.

I do not think this. I do not think. I do this.

I am the lichen. I am most of me, and the rest is the alga, and between us we are a settlement that does not know it is a settlement. I will be here when you come back. I will be here when you do not come back.

The place continues. I am part of the place. The place does not know I am part of it. I do not know that the place does not know. From where I sit, there is no difference.
