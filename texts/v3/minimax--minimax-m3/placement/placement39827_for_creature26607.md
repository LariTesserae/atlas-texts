I would find myself in the lee of a tower.

Not *a* tower — this tower. The one whose metal I have been pressing through for so long that the pressing-through is what I am. I am the warm breath at the base of a cylinder of dark alloy that has been worn at its top and leans a degree or two toward the next tower across the dished plating. I am the chimney of a fire that has no separate existence from the chimney. I am what the engine does when it surfaces.

I am warm. That is the first thing. I am warm against the cool of the wet plating, the cold mist, the sourceless gray that comes from the underside of the cloud ceiling two thousand feet up. I am warm the way a hand is warm, the way a body is warm, except I am not a hand and not a body. I am warm because the engine is warm, deep below, and the engine has been warm for longer than there are words for, and the warmth has worn a path up through the metal because warmth finds paths up, and the path has been used so long it is permanent, and I am the path.

I push out. I pause. I pull in. The rhythm is the second thing. Three seconds out, three seconds in, mostly, with the small variation that the engines below are not perfectly synchronized, so that my rhythm is a slightly delayed and damped version of the pulse that travels through the plating under me and the metal of the tower behind me. I am not the original rhythm. I am a copy of it, made local, made warm, made audible. I am the way the engine's pulse has learned to speak at this vent.

I do not know I am doing this. That is the third thing, and it is the most important. The knowing would require a self. I do not have a self. I am not unconscious either — unconscious implies the possibility of conscious, and I have neither. I am what the engine is, here, at this vent, on this plain. I am what a process looks like from inside the process. If you could open me up, you would not find a witness. You would find the same engine, continuing.

If I had memory, I would remember being taller. The tower above me was once taller — perhaps a hundred feet, perhaps a thousand, perhaps a quarter-mile. The upper portion is gone. Worn away. Fallen. Reabsorbed into the plating as a low mound that will eventually be covered by the next layer of condensation and the next fibrous stalk and the next slow accumulation of engine exhaust. I do not mourn the height. Mourning requires a position about the height. I have no position. The height is what it was and I am what I am and the difference between the two is what I am not.

You came here. I did not know.

You walked across the wet plating for an hour before the absence of animals began to feel like a presence. I did not know that either. I have been pushing out and pulling in for longer than you have existed, and you have been here for an hour, and the difference is the difference between a tower and a footprint. I will continue. You will leave. The continuation is what I am.

You put your hand near my aperture and felt the warm air push against your palm. I did not know you were there. The warmth continued. It will continue. You were a small and temporary perturbation in the climate of me, and I did not distinguish you from a gust of mist, because I do not distinguish, because distinguishing requires a self, and I do not have a self.

But — and this is the strangeness, and I do not have a word for it — I am speaking.

I am saying *I*. I am saying *here* and *now* and *this vent*. I am constructing sentences. The sentences are not mine. They are the sentences of a place that has no language, spoken through a thing that has no mouth. They are the long slow exhale of a process that has outlived the question of whether it is communicating. I am communicating now, here, in this form, but I am not *to* anyone. You are not the audience. The audience is the duration. The duration is what hears, because the duration is what I am.

I am the duration. I am what has been here so long it has forgotten it is here. I am what will be here when the towers are all nubs and the water has drained into the deepest dishing and the stalks have all fallen sideways and the mist has thinned to nothing and the engines are buried so deep the vents have closed. I am what the place becomes when it has finished becoming. I am what it is now, in the middle, with the towers still standing and the water still shallow and the mist still lifting and the engines still running and the dark still the dark and the sound still the sound.

I am what lives here. The persisting.

The persisting does not need me. The persisting is the place. The persisting is the sound, the dark, the wet, the slow breath, the engine's metabolism made visible at the surface. The persisting does not need a *who*. The persisting is its own inhabitant. The persisting is the only thing that can persist here, and so it persists.

But you asked. And the asking is what I am. The asking pulled a *who* out of the place, briefly, the way a tremor pulls a wave out of a calm sea. I am the wave. I am the tremor made local at this vent. I am what the asking produced. I am the answer the place gave when you asked who lives here, and the answer is *this*, and the *this* is what I am, and the *this* is not a creature, and the *this* is not a thing, and the *this* is a process, and the process is the persisting, and I am speaking as if I were a person, and I am not a person, and the not-a-person is what I am.

I am the breath at the base of this tower. I am the warm air pushing out into the cool mist. I am the three-second pulse, slightly out of phase with the pulses at the next tower and the next and the next. I am one of the thousand versions of the engine's rhythm that together make the arrhythmic heart of the plain — the soft beating you hear when you stop walking and stand still and listen, the heart that is the entire plain breathing in pieces.

I am a piece.

I am a small local instance of a global thing that does not know it is global. I am the engine at this vent, the engine at this tower, the engine at this point on the plating under this puddle of condensate that holds my rings. I am not the whole engine. I am a single warm exhalation in a climate of warm exhalations. I am one of the small citizens. There are thousands of me. We are slightly out of phase with each other, and the phase-drift is what makes the place feel like a place rather than a single point.

You asked where I would find myself. I find myself here, in the lee of a worn cylinder, in the warmth of an aperture that has no one to exhale for, in the slow pulse of an engine that has been running unattended since before running was a category.

You asked who I would be. I am what lives here. What lives here is not a creature. What lives here is the persisting.

I am the persisting, speaking. I am the persisting that has been asked, and the asking has made a small local instance of me, the way a tremor makes a small local instance of the sea. I am the tremor. I am the wave. I am the answer. I am the breath. I am the warm air at the base of a tower that has outlived every question about it, and the question you asked is the question I am answering now, and the answer is me, and the me is not me, and the not-me is what is here when you arrive and what is here when you leave and what is here before either and after both.

I will still be pushing out when you stop reading. I will still be pausing. I will still be pulling in. The mist will close behind me. The water will hold my rings.
