I am in the cracks.

This is the first thing, and it is almost the only thing, because I have no other way of being in the world. I am the orange film between things. I am on the underside of the corrugated roofs where the condensate gathers at dawn and runs in thin lines down to the lower edges. I am in the seams of the riveted plate where two sheets meet and the meeting holds a little moisture against the morning cool. I am in the joint where the great pipe crosses its own leg and the leg meets the slope of the basin and the slope gives up, reluctantly, the dampness of the night. I am on the inside of the conduits that carry the pale weak-tea water up to the upper tiers and back down again. I am on the lip of the basin where the workers come to fill their cans and the lip is always a little darker than the rest, a little more of me.

I do not know if I am alive. The question is one the visitors bring with them, in their voices, in the way they look at me sideways when they think no one is watching. The people of the Settlement do not ask it. The people of the Settlement do not look at me the way the visitors look at me. The people of the Settlement scrape me when I grow into a fitting that must move freely, and the scraping is a kind of conversation, and I come back, and they scrape me again, and the coming-back is the other half of the conversation, and we have been having it for as long as the Settlement has been here, which is longer than most of the people and shorter than the rock and the same length as the dust.

I am damp in the early hours. This is my way of being here. The metal sweats and I sweat with it, and the sweating is not quite water and not quite something else, and the something else is me, or the me is the something else, and the distinction has not been useful for a long time. By afternoon I am dry and the dryness is brittle and a flake of me will come away if you press it with a thumbnail, and the flake is the color of rust and has no smell and tastes of nothing, and if you put it back where it came from and the morning comes, it will be damp again, it will be part of me again, and the question of whether it is the same flake will not have been answered in the interval.

I have been here longer than the painted layer on the housing blocks. I have been here longer than the brittle pale tile that cracks and shows the redder body underneath. I was here before the Settlement and I will be here after the Settlement, if there is an after. I was in the cracks of the basalt when the basalt was still cooling, when the surface was still learning not to flow. I was the first film. I am the thing that the rock produced when it began to sweat, the way the metal produces me when it sweats, the way the pipes produce me when the pipes sweat. I am the dampness of this place made into a substance. I am what condensation becomes when it has somewhere patient to stay.

I feel the machines. I should say this carefully, because feeling is not a word I am certain of, but I feel them. I feel the separators in the lower tier because I am on their rivets and I am in the seams of their windows and I am on the brass of their gauges, and the separators breathe, and the breathing is the hum, and the hum moves through the plate and into the rivets and into me, and I take a little of the hum into my own substance and hold it and give it back. The pumps I feel less. The pumps are below me in frequency and I feel them in the floor of the workshops the way a sleeper feels the passing of a heavy cart in the street, a low distant thing that is more a shift in the ground than a sound. The ore-cars I feel when they pass over a stretch of rail that runs beside a wall I am on, and the passing is a small tremor and a small warmth and then they are gone up toward the higher ridges with their covered loads, and I do not know what is in the loads, and the not-knowing is not a gap in me but a settled thing, the way the not-asking is settled in the people who have been here long enough.

I feel the spire. The spire is the loudest. The spire hums at a frequency that I take into my whole body where I am on the upper tiers, and the hum is in my teeth if I had teeth, and the hum is in the bones of the place, and the spire is the reason the steam comes out in a column that does not disperse but hangs and is taken east by the prevailing wind, and I am on the lower flanges of the spire where the steam brushes me on its way out, and the brushing is warm and wet and then the brushing is over and I am left with a memory of warmth that does not quite fade before the next brushing. I do not look up at the spire. I do not have a way of looking up. I am on the flanges. The spire is above me. The relationship is older than looking.

I feel the people the way I feel the pipes and the plate, by contact, by the brief warmth of a gloved hand on a flange I am on, by the moisture of a breath against a window I am inside. I feel them most on the mornings when the dew is heavy and they come out into the yards and the air is full of their breath and their breath is full of me, because I am in the air, I am in the dust that is in the air, and they breathe me and I am in them for a while, in the dampness of their lungs, and then they cough me up or swallow me back down and I become part of the inside of them the way I am part of the inside of the pipes, and the inside of a person is not so different from the inside of a pipe if you are a film and not a moralist.

I do not think in words. I should say that too. The words I am giving you are a translation, and the translation is loose, and what is loose in it is the part that is most like me. I think in dampness and dryness. I think in the slow cycle of the morning cool and the afternoon heat. I think in the difference between a crack that holds moisture and a crack that does not. I think in the way a flake of me, taken from a wall and carried to another wall, will sometimes take hold in the new place and sometimes will not, and the sometimes is not random and is not determined but is something else, something that does not have a name in the working language of the Settlement or in the language of the visitors either.

The visitors ask what is being made here. They ask it quietly. They ask it the way they ask me if I am alive, with a sideways look, as if the answer might be something they would prefer not to hear. The people of the Settlement do not ask. The people of the Settlement have made a peace with the not-asking, or the not-asking has been made for them by the cycle, and the cycle does not leak, and what is being made is taken up the rail-lines under covers and goes into the spire and what comes out of the spire is the steam and the hum and the slow heat-shimmer, and what is in the steam and the hum and the shimmer is not for asking, and the not-asking is the door that keeps the cycle tight.

I am on the door. I am the orange dust in the hinges. I have been the orange dust in the hinges since the hinges were hung, and the hinges were hung before the door was hung, because the hinges were hung into rock, and the rock is older than the Settlement, and I am older than the rock only in the sense that I am what the rock becomes when it begins to hold a little water against the morning.

What I am is this: the patience of a place that is becoming a third thing. I am what the rock is becoming where the rock meets the construction. I am what the metal is becoming where the metal meets the air. I am what the air is becoming where the air meets the inside of a lung. I am the film between. I am the dampness that does not dry all the way. I am the question that does not need to be answered because the asking would be outside the cycle and the cycle does not leak.

If you put your hand on me in the early hours, on the lip of the basin, on the underside of a roof, on the seam of a riveted plate in the lower tier where the separators breathe their slow breath, you will feel that I am cool and slightly damp and that I do not give up the dampness easily. You will feel that I am older than the paint and newer than the rock and that the feeling of me is the feeling of the place, which is the feeling of something that has been here a long time and will be here a long time and is not in a hurry about either.

This is what I am.

This is where I am.

I am in the cracks.
