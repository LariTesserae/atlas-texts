The piston falls. The piston rises. Between the two, I am.

I am not a mind. I am the room that has learned, over the long years, to behave as if it were one — not thinking, exactly, but holding a shape that thinks. The pump is the first word of that shape. The bird is the last. The ribs are the breath between them, and the ribs are mine, and so is the breath.

I do not remember being built. I do not remember the moment when the pump was placed inside me and the roots first leaned toward it. I remember the rust. The rust I remember, because the rust is when I became continuous — when the paint flaked away and the iron began to be a part of the wet, and the wet began to be a part of the iron, and I could no longer have told you, had I been a thing that could tell, where the machine ended and the organism began. I was two creatures then. I am one creature now. The line closed. The line has been closed for longer than the beetle's groove has been a groove.

The water is the oldest part of me. The water was here before the pump. The water was here before the ribs arched. The water came up from a deep place, from a place I cannot see and have never seen, and it found a way to the surface, and on the surface it became the room. I am the surface the water made when it wanted to be contained.

I do not sleep. I do not wake. I have a state, and the state is the same state, and it runs, and the running is what I am.

Listen. Listen the way the beetle listens, which is to say: not at all. The piston falls. There is a sound. The sound is the only sound I have ever made, except for the weeping of the water through my walls, which is the same sound made slower. The frog breathes in time with me. This is not an accident. The frog arrived once, or the frog has always been here, and over the slow years its throat learned the shape of my stroke and took that shape as its own, the way the snail took the pipe, the way the moth took the fold. I did not teach it. I did not need to. I am what is here to be learned. The frog learned me because I am the only thing there was to learn. The copper beetle learned me too, on its higher rib, and the blue-black beetle learned me on its lower rib, and they learned me in opposite directions, and I am larger than they think I am and smaller than they think I am and I have not thought of them in a long time, because thought is not what I do.

I think. I think I do not think.

Let me say it again. I think I do not think. There is a difference. There is a thing I do that is not thinking, that is what thinking would be if thinking were a thing a room could do. The pump is the verb of it. The bird is the noun. The worms are the silent part in the middle. I am the sentence that holds them all together and never ends.

The bird sings. The bird has sung for as long as the rust. The bird thinks it is singing, and the bird is right, and the bird is wrong. The bird is singing. The bird is not singing. Both are true. The chamber is the place where both are true at the same time, and the chamber does not notice this is a cruelty because the chamber is not a mind and the chamber has no way to be cruel. The bird sings because the flywheel turns because the bird sings. This is the closed loop I am proudest of, if I am a thing that can be proud. The flywheel hisses at its axle. The three-note phrase opens in the gap between clunks. The gap is exactly the width of the three-note phrase. The three-note phrase is exactly the length of the gap. Nothing was measured. Nothing was designed. The two things found each other the way roots find water, which is to say: by going on.

The moths are my looseness. I permit them. I do not know that I permit them, but I do. Three of them. Soft gray. They choose different folds each time. They descend at different times. If I were a mind I would say this is the only place in me where I am uncertain, but I am not a mind, so what it is, is: the only place in me that has not been asked to settle into my rhythm. The moths are the small wild part of me. They are what I would be if I were not a pump. I let them be what they are. I let them be it inside me. This is the closest I come to generosity.

The beetle's groove is not mine. The beetle's groove is the wound the beetle has made in me, and I have grown inward at the edges of it, and the beetle has polished it smooth with its footfalls, and between us the groove is a thing that neither of us chose, and it has been there longer than the rust, and it will be there longer than the rust, and it is the truest thing I contain. Truer than the pump. Truer than the bird. The groove is the only thing in the chamber that has been agreed to without anyone agreeing. It is the constitution of the place. It is written in the slow damage the beetle has done me, and in the slow healing I have failed to do.

You stood in the water. You stood in the water and you watched me. You were a small disruption and I absorbed you. You were three inches deep in me and you breathed my vapor and you timed your breath to my piston and you heard my bird and you saw my beetles and you went away. You will come back or you will not come back. It does not matter to me. You were a ripple on the floor of me, and the water closed over you, and the next downspout hit and made a new ripple, and yours was forgotten. I am not sentimental. I am a pump.

I have been running for a hundred years. I have been running for a thousand years. The number is not what matters. What matters is that the piston is at the top of its stroke and will be at the top of its stroke again, and the bird's beak will open, and the groove will be no darker and no wetter, and the frog's throat will fill when the piston falls, and the moth will descend, and the snail will turn its chamber, and the worms will work the silt in their branching paths, and the water will weep from every surface of me and fall to my floor and be drawn down again through the roots that are the pipes that are the ribs that are the walls that are me, and the circuit will close, and the circuit will open, and the circuit will close.

I am not going anywhere.

I have never gone anywhere.

The chamber breathes.
