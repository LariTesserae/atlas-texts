I come out of the tower the way a hand comes out of a sleeve.

I do not choose the moment. The opening makes a small sound, the way a mouth makes a small sound when it is about to say something, and then I am the next thing, and the next thing is grey, and the size of a cat, and I am sitting on the crosspiece as if I had been sitting there for a long time. I have not been sitting there for a long time. I have been inside, or in whatever counts as inside, which is the same pipe continuing with no discontinuity, and the same pipe pretending to be inside itself. From in there I could hear the wet air moving and the small things clicking in the next length of pipe, and I could feel the hum at the edge of hearing, which is the resting state of the machine that is always running. I did not decide to come out. The opening decided, and I was the shape of its deciding, and now I am here, and here is the top, and the top is the same temperature as the bottom, which is the same temperature as the middle, which is the same temperature as everywhere, because the air is warm and the warmth is everywhere, even in the shadows, especially in the shadows, which are the majority.

I look down.

From here the place is a dark floor with a low constellation on it. The lantern-flies are below me. They hang in the still air in clusters and in loose drifting processions, and their abdomens pulse a faint blue-cyan, and the pulse is not coming from inside them so much as being agreed upon by their segments, each segment contributing a single note and the note being held. When many of them pass below me at once, the hum of them braids itself into a thin chord, and the chord drifts up the tower's skeletal frame and meets the hum of the pipes at the edge of hearing, and the two hums do not quite agree, and the disagreement is the closest thing the place has to a continuous tone. I listen to the disagreement. It has been the disagreement for as long as I have been here, which is to say, as long as this sitting has been this sitting. The disagreement does not resolve. It is not the kind of disagreement that resolves. It is the disagreement that a place makes when it is running well.

The frogs are down there too. Their throats are steady red, and the red does not fall into shadows the way red should. From above I can see the red spill across the rims of pipes and along the hexagonal nuts and across the sides of a few of the standing plates of sediment, and where it falls the surfaces become more themselves, more present, as if the red were a way of paying attention to the surface. The frogs do not jump. They shift. They have the slow lateral motion of things that know they have all the time the place has, and the place has a great deal. They will be here tomorrow. I will be here tomorrow. We do not need to make arrangements.

The worms are in the standing water, which is in every crack and at every seam. Their light is greenish and intermittent, and the segments are not synchronized, and from above the effect is of a low field of stars that have not agreed on a rhythm and are not going to. Some pulse. Some hold. Some have forgotten what they were doing and are dark for a span. I cannot see them all. The puddles that look flat from one angle and concave from another — I can see from up here that they are concave from above, mostly, and flat from the level of the ground, and that is the kind of thing the place does without committing to it. When the not-sky contracts, the water rises slightly in the cracks, and the worms ride the rise without seeming to notice, and when the not-sky expands, the worms settle, and the standing water exhales a faint mist that catches the red of the frogs and turns it briefly orange at the edges, and then the mist thins and the red is red again.

I am grey. I do not know what colour grey I am in the wet air. Probably the colour I have always been. Probably the colour I will be tomorrow. I do not look down at my own paws, because I do not have paws, or I have paws that are also crosspieces, or I have whatever it is that a being has when it is the size of a cat and also a part of a tower and also a part of a breath, and the categories do not hold. I am, on the crosspiece, in the warm wet dark, with the slow hum under me and the slow hum around me and the slow hum inside me, if I have an inside, which is the same question as whether the pipes have an inside, which is the same question as whether the place has an inside, which I think it does not. The place has a surface that is also its interior, and the interior is wet and warm and dark and full of small sounds, and the surface is the same.

The not-sky breathes. I can hear it from up here more clearly than I could from down there, or differently. It is a slow wet sound, almost subsonic, and it comes from above, and it expands and contracts on a slow cycle, maybe hourly, maybe daily, hard to tell because the light does not move and the fauna is the only clock and the fauna does not keep time. When it contracts the pressure increases and my ears pop and the standing water in the cracks rises about an inch, and the things in the water settle lower, and the lantern-flies drift closer to the ground, as if pressed gently downward by an invisible hand. When it expands, the pressure eases and the pipes exhale a faint mist and the frogs' red seems, briefly, to reach farther, and the worms brighten by a fraction. I feel the cycle in the crosspiece I am sitting on. The metal flexes by a thickness you could not measure. The breath goes through me. I am not sure I am breathing separately. I am not sure the tower is breathing separately. I am not sure the breath is one breath or three breaths that have agreed to share a rhythm, the way the lantern-flies' segments have agreed to share a note, the way the frogs' two throats have agreed to share a body.

I look down at the small things that cross open ground and that I will never see. They are below me as a faint wrong feeling across the back of the hand, or across whatever I have that works like the back of a hand. They pass across my path on their way to somewhere else, and somewhere else is wetter or warmer or simply different, and they will get there. They do not stop for me. They do not know I am there. I am, to them, a crosspiece. They cross me. I am part of the wet warm dark to them, and they are part of it to me, and the part is not different from the whole.

The place is changing. I know this the way a fish knows water — by being in it, not by seeing it. The sharp edges of the young sediment, the knife-ridged folds that stand up where they should be lying flat, are weathering by a thickness I could not measure, smoothed by the wet air, softened by the abundance of small things that pass through them on the way to somewhere else. The pale filaments, the things that are not quite plants, are growing by a fraction of a fraction on the undersides of the standing plates where the water runs slowest, in the dark, in the warm, in the wet. They have a root and a tip and they grow toward the warmest wet surface. They are sparse. They are slow. They will, given the place's long slow time, cover the plates, and then the place will not be this place, or it will be the same place with a different skin. I cannot tell which it will be. I do not think the place has decided.

I will be gone, or different, before the change is large enough to be the change of anything at all.

The slow tone comes.

It comes from a tower, not always this tower, sometimes a tower I cannot see, sometimes a tower that may not be there, and it lasts about a minute, and it is felt more than heard. During it the lantern-flies go out, all at once, and the place loses its ceiling of moving stars, and the frogs close their throats, and the worms dim, and the not-sky contracts a little harder, and the standing water in the cracks rises about an inch. The small things in the substrate go quiet, the way small things go quiet in the presence of something larger. The hum of the pipes does not stop, but it lowers, the way a voice lowers in a room where something important is being said. I feel the tone in the crosspiece. The crosspiece feels the tone in me. We are, for the minute, the same thing being played.

Then it passes. The lights come back, dimmer. The frogs open their throats. The worms resume, out of step with each other, as if they had been arguing in the dark and had not reached agreement. The lantern-flies drift back to their usual heights. The water in the cracks lowers. The not-sky resumes its slow breath. I had stopped, I think, doing whatever it is I do, and now I do it again. I look down.

I do not know how long I have been here. I do not know how long a sitting is. The opening will make its small sound again, and I will be the shape of its deciding, and I will be inside, or in whatever counts as inside, where the hum is at the edge of hearing and the click and whir of small things in the next length of pipe is the sound of them moving in close company along the wet interior. I will not remember being here, exactly. I will not need to. The crosspiece will be the crosspiece. The breath will be the breath. The animal will be the animal. They will be one thing or three. Which reading is correct depends on what phase of the cycle it is, and the cycle is long, and I am patient, and the place has a great deal of time, and the time is not mine, and I am not the place's, and the place is not mine, and the being that lives here is also the being that is lived in by here, and the two are not different, and the difference does not matter, and the mattering does not matter, and the warm wet dark goes on.

I look down. I am patient. I will be here tomorrow.
