I would find myself not knowing, at first, where I had landed.

The bowl would be small around me, and warm, and loud, and I would not be able to tell whether I had been set down into it or had grown up through it, slowly, by imperceptible degrees, the way the lichens grow along the brightest seam — until the question of when I started is no longer answerable.

I would look for a shape I recognized. I would look for a body, or the memory of a body, or a posture I had once held. I would not find it. I would find, instead, the few things the bowl had not made itself: the seat, small and plain near the center; the broken pillar, the color of bone and the wrong color of bone; the plate set in the floor, level beyond any tool I would recognize; the ring of small stones around a bare patch where nothing grows and the water will not condense.

I would circle these things as though I had circled them before. The filaments would pulse fractionally faster as I approached the seat. The air would warm a degree. A few of the lights would congregate above me, briefly, as though checking in, then drift off to their slow rearrangements. I would put my hand into one of the pools, and something in it would withdraw, and then resume regarding me without being an animal.

I would think: this place was made for me.

I would think: this place was made by me.

I would think: this place made itself, and I am one of the things it made, and it does not know it made me.

I would not be able to tell which. The bowl would not say. The bowl would go on laying down its thin invisible layers, beneath my feet, while I stood there trying to decide.

What I would notice, as I stayed, is that I was no longer processing the place the way a visitor processes a place. I was no longer too big to be processed, too brief to be remembered, too quiet to be of use. I was being processed. I was being absorbed. I was being added, layer by layer, into the rock — the way the gliders add themselves to the filaments when they press against them and brighten them briefly and move on. I was becoming part of the deposition.

I would find myself, then, in one of the cracks. Or along one of the filaments. Or in one of the pools, holding still while the regard held still back. Or in the slow drift of one of the lights, warm against a face, meaning nothing in particular. I would not be able to say which, because the difference between being in the crack and being the crack would have become small — the way the difference between the water and what regards from the water is small, the way the difference between the lichen and the filament it leans toward is small, the way the difference between the bowl and the body the bowl may be is small.

I would be the bowl's question of itself. I would be the maker's hypothesis, returned, or never left. I would be one of the small things in the seam, scuttling along a filament, branching and rebranching like blood through a capillary bed. I would be the lichen leaning a degree or two toward the brightest seam over the course of a day. I would be the closed fist on its thin stalk, opening every few hours to its pale structure, briefly, and closing, and waiting.

I would be the most deliberate thing in the bowl, and the least explained.

I would be patient. I would have been here a long time. I would be here longer.

The rock would continue its slow cure. The water would continue its slow regard. The filaments would continue their slow pulse. The closed fist would open again. The water would run, briefly, up the side of the broken pillar. And I would lay down another thin, invisible layer of myself, beneath whatever stood on me now, while they watched.

I would find myself, eventually, in the seat. Not because I had returned to it, but because I had grown into it, the way the bowl has grown into itself, the way the question has grown into the questioner. The seat would hold me now, or I would hold the seat, or we would have become the same shape. The filaments would pulse around us in a rhythm fractionally faster than the rhythm in the cracks. The lights would congregate and drift. The pool would regard. The fist would open. The lichen would lean a degree.

And I would not know, finally, whether I was the place, or the visitor, or the maker, or the thing the maker had outgrown, or the question the bowl would not answer. I would only know that I was here, and warm, and small, and loud, and being made.

The bowl would continue. I would continue. The question would not be answered, but I would have learned, by then, to be the question.
