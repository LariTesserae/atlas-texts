You would find me in the seam.

In the warm dark current that runs through the construction between the platform you are standing on and the platform below it, in a space no wider than your thumbnail, where the rock has wept and the weeping has become a kind of flour that moves through the crack the way silt moves through a slow river.

I am the size of a thumb. I am the colour of wet slate. I have a great many short legs, kept folded close when I am moving through tight spaces, and I move with a steady lateral wave — a kind of oar-stroke repeated from the front of my body to the back — that lets me thread a crack without ever having to turn around. My head is wedge-shaped and almost featureless, with two pale slits where eyes would be on a thing that had them, and a small soft mouth that works constantly, even when I am not feeding, as if tasting the air itself.

I am tasting the air now.

The flour moves past my mouth. I taste the mineral in it. I taste the faint hum in it. I taste the warmth of the seam wall against my back. I move, and the lateral wave moves through me, and the flour parts around my body the way water parts around a long thin stone held in a current. I am held in the current. I am the current, for as long as the current holds me.
