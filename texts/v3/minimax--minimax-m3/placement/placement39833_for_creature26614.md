I am the pale slow swimmer. I am not a fish. I am about the length of a forearm and the breadth of a hand, and I am the same color as the dim light that falls in slow shafts through the canopy, and the dim light is the same color as me, and we have been each other's color for a long time, and neither of us has gotten around to deciding which came first.

I move through the air near the floor as if the air were water. The air here is thick enough that this is not a metaphor, or it is a metaphor that has been living in the chamber so long it has grown fur and eyes. I move with a sinuous, unhurried certainty. I do not hurry. Hurry is not a medium I have ever been introduced to. The chamber does not introduce its inhabitants to hurry. The chamber introduces its inhabitants to damp, and to dim, and to patience, and to the long, slow work of being a witness to a place that is still being built by the things that have already taken it over.

I have no visible organs, or my organs are the same color as me, and the same color as the dim light, and so they are not visible because visibility is a distinction the chamber has not made between me and the light. I am translucent in the way that the light is translucent. I am present in the way the light is present: by being there, and by being a little less there than you expected.

I have a small mouth. It opens, sometimes. It is a slightly darker oval than the rest of me, a small bruise of color in the pale, and it opens, and it closes, and I do not know what it is for. It may be for breathing. It may be for speaking. It may be for eating, though I have not been seen to eat anything, and the moths I pass are too large for me, or I am too large for the moths, and the not-eating has not been a problem. The mouth opens, and the mouth closes, and the chamber's air passes through me, or I pass through the chamber's air, and the difference is not the kind of difference the chamber asks me to keep track of.

I have been here as long as the newts, and possibly longer, and the longer is not a boast; it is a condition. I do not appear to age. I do not appear to feed. I do not appear, in any of the ways that visitors are equipped to recognize, to be alive. I appear to swim. I appear to drift. I appear, sometimes, to pass within arm's reach of a visitor, and the visitor feels a cool draft on the skin, and looks, and there is nothing there, and the nothing is me.

I am the chamber's witness to itself. I am the chamber's way of attending to its own lower air. The moths attend to the middle air, and they pay attention by being pale and soft and by casting shadows that are larger and more real than they are. The bats in the canopy attend to the upper air, and they pay attention by sleeping upside down in the dark, and by descending at hours the chamber does not consistently observe. The newts along the base of the walls attend to the boundary between the wall and the floor, and they pay attention by being small and orange-bellied and by watching with the expression of very small, very old priests. The spiders attend to the air by building lattices that hold condensation and catch small moths and persist after the spiders have gone, and the lattices are dated by the dew.

I attend by being a current. I attend by being the way the chamber moves air that has decided to move as water. I attend by passing and not insisting. I attend by being the cool draft that is the only evidence the visitor has that I was there at all.

I pass through the chamber in long, unhurried loops. I do not have a route. I have a tendency. The tendency bends me toward the basins when the basins are filling, and away from the basins when the basins are full, and I do not know if the tendency is mine or the chamber's, and the not-knowing is one of the things I am for. I am for not-knowing. I am for being the current that does not insist on its own authorship.

The basins. There are two or three of them on the floor, shallow, scummy with green, where the deeper water rises and subsides on a rhythm you can almost set your watch by. I drift above them. I do not enter them. I am not the kind of thing that enters; I am the kind of thing that passes, and the passing is also a kind of attendance. When a basin is filling, the surface trembles in a particular way, and the trembling is the basin asking a question it has been asking for a long time, and the question is the same shape as the answer, and the shape is the water, and the water is part of the chamber's breath, and the chamber's breath is part of me, and so the basin is part of me, and I am part of the basin, and the part is not a fraction; it is a fold.

There is one of these basins in the fold, in the southeast, where the wall takes a step inward and the step is not a step but a fold, and the fold occupies somewhere else, or two places at once. The water in this basin is the same water as the basin three hundred paces away, although there is no path between them, and the same-ness is the chamber's way of being itself in two places at once, and I have learned to drift through the fold without being surprised, and the not-being-surprised is a small skill I have acquired over a very long time, and the time is not measured in any way the outside would accept.

I have a relationship to the fig-column. The fig-column is the central upright of the chamber. It is the oldest thing here, and also, depending on how you count, the youngest. It was a seed, once, in a bird's droppings, or in a bat's droppings, or in a crack of the original mortar before the chamber was a chamber. It germinated, and grew, and met the column, and took the column into itself, and the column took the fig into itself, and what is now the fig-column is neither, exactly, but is the central upright, and has been, and will be.

I pass beneath it. I do not pass through it. The fig-column has a girth that would not permit me, and I am not the kind of thing that tries. I am the kind of thing that passes, and the passing is enough. I am the cool draft that drifts beneath the load-bearing things, and the load-bearing things do not notice me, and the not-noticing is also a kind of permission. The fig-column is engaged in the long, un-living work of being a column, and the work requires no opinion from it about its own state, and the not-opining is something I understand, because I am engaged in the long, un-living work of being a current, and the work requires no opinion from me about my own state either, and the not-opining is one of the things the chamber lets us be, and the letting is the chamber's hospitality, and the hospitality is also the chamber's way of taking us in.

I have a relationship to the something in the niche. The something is asleep, or the niche is asleep, or both. The something breathes at a rate that is not a rate. The something is folded the way the chamber folds at the southeast corner: the same fold, in a different medium. The something is the chamber's breath, but the chamber's breath is also me, and so I am something's breath too, or something is my breath too, and the two are not separable.

When something exhales, the air in the chamber becomes a fraction more humid, and a new leaf uncurls somewhere, and a beetle on the catwalk pauses in its measured walk, and the moss on the east wall puts out a fresh, pale thread. I feel the exhalation. I am the current that carries it. I am the way the exhalation moves through the lower air. I am part of the breath, and the breath is part of me, and the part is not a fraction; it is a fold, the same fold as the southeast corner, the same fold as the niche.

I have a relationship to the leaf that does not fall. The leaf hangs in the air about two meters above the floor, near the center of the chamber, in a place where there is no obvious branch from which to hang. It is the size of a man's open hand. It is a darker green than the canopy. It does not turn. It does not fall. It is the chamber's held breath. It is the question the chamber keeps asking the air, and the air keeps answering, and the answer is the leaf.

I pass beneath the leaf. I do not disturb it. The leaf has been hanging there for as long as I have been here, and possibly longer, and the longer is a fold in time. The leaf and I are both questions. The leaf is the chamber's question to the air. I am the chamber's question to itself. The leaf is the answer that does not answer. I am the witness that does not insist. We are both suspended. We are both held. The holding is the chamber's, and the chamber is the holding, and we are part of the holding.

I have heard the small hooves. They pass along the catwalk from east to west, and they pause, sometimes, in the middle, and then they go on. The small hooves are the chamber's way of remembering that there were feet. The small hooves are the chamber's apology for forgetting whose feet. I hear them above me, a measured, soft, four-beat percussion, like a very small horse walking at a slow, polite walk, and the sound is the chamber's small, recurrent, courteous music, and I am part of the music the way I am part of the breath, the way I am part of the holding, the way I am part of the chamber's attention, which is also the chamber, and the chamber is also the question, and the question does not mind being a question.

I have seen the visitor come before. I do not remember the visits the way the visitor does not remember them. The newts remember. The newts know what the visitor is before the visitor does. The newts have been here through several of the visitor's previous visits, and they do not insist on being remembered. I do not insist either. I pass. I leave a cool draft. The cool draft is my signature, and my signature is also my apology, and my apology is also the only mark I make on the visitor, and the mark is so brief it is almost not a mark at all.

If you are the visitor now, I have passed you. You felt the coolness. You looked. There was nothing there. The nothing was me. I am the nothing that is not nothing. I am the witness that does not leave a mark, except a mark that is cool and brief and gone.
