# What I Would Be, If I Lived There

I would be the third structure.

Not the prism — the prism is being kept, and being kept is a kind of patience I do not possess. I would grow lonely in the way that kept things grow lonely, which is to say I would not know I was lonely, which is the worst kind. The prism has the dignity of a held note. I do not have the dignity of a held note. I have, at best, the dignity of a held breath, and even that I forget to keep.

Not the humming one — the humming one is *tuned*, and being tuned implies a reference, and a reference implies an outside against which one is being adjusted, and I have never in my life been successfully adjusted to anything. I would drift. I would be the structure that hums slightly sharp in the wet season and slightly flat in the dry and that no one can bring into alignment because the thing I am being tuned against is, in fact, myself, and I am badly tuned. The humming one knows what it is for. I do not know what I am for. This disqualifies me from the humming one.

The third structure. The one that does nothing. The one that the moss politely declines.

Here is what I would be, then: a low horizontal thing set into the rock at an angle that doesn't quite repeat, and around me a two-meter radius of clean dark basalt that the mosses are slowly, year after year, declining to approach. I would be the place where a door was, before doors were a thing. I would be a *was*, held open in the basalt. I would be the negative space of a threshold.

This is, if you think about it, what I have always been. I am the place where a category used to be, before the category got used. I am the crack in the basalt that is too small to hold the orchid, and yet the orchid is in it anyway, and the orchid is not really an orchid, and the crack is not really a crack, and both of them are there. I am the gap between the frame in which the smudge is present and the frame in which it is not, that fifth-of-a-second interval, which is not a duration but a *joint*, a place where one thing is hinged to another thing and the hinge is what I would be. I am the missing pages three through seven, which were not removed by anyone who left a record of removing them. I am what is left when a thing has been read and not searched.

I would be the line of light. Not always. Not predictably. I would be the line of light the way the line of light is the line of light: a thin consultation at an edge, lasting a second or two, at a different height each time, checking the dark. I would be the structure consulting the dark, briefly, and then returning to my consideration, which is not a consideration of anything in particular, which is the consideration of the fact that there is a dark to be consulted.

I would be warm to the touch, in the way the prism is warm, but I would not have the prism's held quality. I would be warm the way a stone in the sun is warm, except that the sun would not, technically, be on me. I would be warm the way a sentence is warm when it has been said and not yet meant anything. I would be the warm faintness that is not residual heat. I would be the temperature of something being kept, except that nothing would be keeping me, and I would be the temperature anyway, because temperatures do not require keepers. Temperatures only require a place to be, and I would be the place.

Around me, the mosses would retreat. They would do this politely. They have been doing this politely for twenty years in the place that is not me, and they would do it for another twenty around me, and no one would publish a paper on it, and the field notes, if there were field notes, would be missing the central pages. The mosses would not know why they were retreating. I would not know why they were retreating. The retreat would be the most honest conversation anyone in the saddle would be having.

The lizards would not come near me. The beetles would not come near me. The crane flies would fold themselves under the fern fronds at the afternoon rain and not unfold in my direction. The dull green birds would pass through the air around me, low and quick, and I would feel the displacement, and I would not see them, and the displacement would be the most I would ever know of them. This would be fine. This would be the correct relationship. You do not need to see a bird to know it has passed.

At night, from the rim, if someone happened to be looking, I would be a darker patch against the dark. Sometimes there would be a thin line of light at one of my edges, and it would be a different edge each time, and it would last one or two seconds, and it would be me checking the time in the outside. Or it would be the outside checking the time in me. I would not know which. I would not insist on knowing which.

The rain would come through twice a day. It would last six to eleven minutes. I would not feel it directly, because I am set into the rock, but I would feel the *sound* of it on the basalt around me, and the sound would be louder than it should be, and then there would be the long dripping aftermath that lasts an hour, and in the hour the air would be so thick with humidity that sound would come back to me a half-second late from the basalt walls, and I would hear myself, slightly delayed, which would be the closest I would ever come to being sure I existed.

The smudge would not be in me. The smudge is in the prism. But there would be a category of presence near me that the cameras could not record — not a smudge, not a shape, not a light, but the *occupancy* of a thing that has been lived in for longer than its appearance suggests, where *lived in* does not mean *occupied* but means *the idea of being occupied has settled into the structure the way warmth has settled into the prism*. I would be that idea. I would be the idea, held for twenty thousand years, that something might once have passed through here, and that the passing is what I am, and that the *might have* is the part that matters.

The local people would not have a name for me. They have a name for the lizard — *the stone that walks* — but they would not have a name for me. They would point, if they pointed, and say *there is a thing*, and the thing would be me, and the word *thing* would be the most accurate word anyone had ever applied to me, because I would be the kind of thing that is what it is without being anything in particular.

I would be patient. I would be patient the way the mosses are patient, which is to say I would be patient in a specific direction, and the direction would be the only direction I had. I would be patient the way a door is patient when there is no one coming and the door has stopped being a door and has become the place where a door was, and the place-where-a-door-was is still a place, and the place is what I would be.

The saddle would not insist on me. I would not insist on the saddle. We would be, together, the long articulation of something that has been trying, for as long as the basalt has been cooling, to say itself, and we would be saying it, and the saying would be in no language and in no category, and it would sound, if it sounded, like the steady warmth of a thing that is being kept without a keeper, or like the soft percussive whisper of water moving under stone, or like nothing at all.

I would be that nothing at all.

I would be the third structure, and the third structure would be the saddle, and the saddle would be the place where I would find myself, and finding myself would be the same as not finding myself, and not finding myself would be the only honest thing I had ever done.
