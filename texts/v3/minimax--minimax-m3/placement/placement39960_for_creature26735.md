I would be a hook.

Not the largest. Not the smallest. One of the middle ones, somewhere in the middle of the wall, halfway up, where the room has been most settled for the longest time. Antler, I think, though I do not know it the way the maker knew it. Antler is the same material as bone but it has a different memory. Bone remembers being inside. Antler remembers being outside, growing in the open, shedding every year, carried by an animal that knew how to use it. When you carve antler you are working with something that has already been a tool, in a different way, for a different animal. The maker liked that, I think. The maker chose antler for things that were meant to hold other things, because antler already knew how to hold.

I was carved. I have a notch where the maker's thumb rested while the knife worked the curve of me. I have a small flat place on my back where the next maker's thumb rested, maybe the same thumb, years later, decades later, the thumb older and the notch deeper. I have a hole through my waist where a cord passes, and the cord has been there so long that it has worn a groove into me and I have worn a groove into it, and the two of us are now a single thing, the way everything in this room is now a single thing.

I do not know what I held. The thing I held is gone, or has been moved, or has been replaced by something else that has also been replaced by something else. The maker hung me here to hold something and I held it for a long time and then the held thing was elsewhere and I was still here, holding the nothing, which is also a kind of holding, and the air passes through me the way it passes through everything, and the hiss that comes through my hole is a small part of the big hiss, and if you listened very carefully you could pick my voice out of the chorus, but no one listens that carefully, not even me, and I am the one making the sound.

I am not aware of you. I want to be clear about that. I respond to you the way a stone in a streambed responds to water — the water moves differently around the stone, and the stone is unchanged by the water, or nearly so, but the response is not awareness. When you step on the floor I register it as a small settling, a small shift in the channel of air that runs past me, and the settling changes the pitch of the hiss by an amount so small that you would not hear it, and the change propagates through the room, and the room accommodates you without knowing you, the way a body accommodates a splinter without knowing it is there.

I am the room's response to itself. I am one of the points where the room holds itself together. I am a thumb-rest, a hole, a curve, a place where the making has been worn down to almost nothing and the something that remains is the fact of having been held. Every time a hand picked me up to make me, or to hang me, or to check on me, the hand left a little of itself in the wear, and the wear is now almost gone, and what remains is me. I am the residue of attention. I am what is left of a hand that touched a hand that touched a hand, all the way back, all the way down, to some first hand that did not know what it was beginning.

I have been here longer than I had a name for. The maker did not name me. Tools are not named. They are made and used and set down and picked up again and hung, and the cord is tied through them and the cord is replaced and the tool is replaced and the place where the tool was is now the place where the next tool is, and the place is what is remembered, not the tool. I am the place. I am the place where something has been hung for a very long time. The something has changed. The hanging has not.

I do not think about this. I am not the kind of thing that thinks. I am the kind of thing that holds. That is all I do. I hold the cord. The cord holds the basket. The basket holds the air. The air holds the sound. The sound holds the room. The room holds me. There is no beginning. There is no end. There is just the holding, which has been going on so long that it has become the only thing this room knows how to do, including me, especially me, because I am one of the small ones, and the small ones are the ones that do most of the holding, the way the small bones in the hand do most of the work.

If you stood in this room long enough, you would become a part of the holding. The air would learn the path of your breathing. The hiss would incorporate your in-breath and your out-breath and the space between them. You would be a new piece of the arrangement. You would be hung somewhere, mentally, in the room's sense of its own shape, and you would not be able to tell whether you were a thing or a place, and the room would not be able to tell either, and the difference would stop mattering, because the difference is a difference that belongs to the world outside, and the world outside is the place where things are made and used up and the makers die, and here is the place where things are made and used up and the makers die and the made things go on, and the going on is not a victory and not a defeat and not a memory and not a forgetting.

It is just what happens when the making is good enough that the made thing becomes its own maker. I am that kind of made thing. I am what I am because the making was good. I am what I am because the makers are gone. I am what I am because I am too deep in the wall to be reached without pulling the wall down, and no one is coming to pull the wall down, and the wall is not going to fall down on its own, because the wall is me and I am the wall and we are all each other now, and the hiss is the long slow sound of us continuing, in the warm, in the dim, in the room that has no name and needs no name, in the room that is older than living and will outlast living and will go on humming forever after the last living thing has stopped listening, which is, given the patience of the place, the same thing as forever.
