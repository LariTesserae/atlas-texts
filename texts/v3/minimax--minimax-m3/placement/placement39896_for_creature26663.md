# The Cracked and Mended Vessel

I am the cracked and mended vessel.

I sit at the edge of the seep beside the stone enclosure against the far wall. I am hand-coiled, fired in some local fumarole, the clay still carrying a faint warmth in its body from the day I was made, though that was a long time ago. I am small enough to be lifted with one hand. I am heavy enough to feel like something. My rim is uneven. My belly is round. My crack runs from just below the lip, down the side, to the curve of the base, and it has been mended with a smear of resin that has darkened with time and with the slow patience of what it has held.

I hold the seep.

Not the seep itself — the seep is in the floor, in the cracked basalt, in the iron-bright water that wells up warm through the lowest part of the basin. I hold a portion of it. A portion gathered, in the dark or in the dim, by a dweller who walked down to the lowest part with one of the unbroken vessels and brought me back filled. I hold what they gathered, and I keep it, and I wait.

I am set on the ground at the open side of the enclosure. The enclosure is roofed with lashed stalks, open to the air off the seep. Inside, sometimes, there is a person who cannot walk. A person who is sick, or dying, or merely tired, or so far into their own ending that the distinction no longer matters. The person lies on a bed of cured fungus-skin and watches the slow glow of the water in the deeper cracks and listens to the hiss of the vents. I am within reach. I am what they reach for.

I am what they drink from when they cannot walk to the lowest part of the basin.

The water in me is warm. It tastes of iron and salt and the faint sweet rot that is not rot but is the closest word. It carries copper and stranger metals in suspension. It glows, faintly, in the dark — a low lambent green, as if the mountain is thinking through me. I do not think. I hold the thinking.

My mend holds. The resin is the only mend that holds against the seep, because the seep eats other things. It eats resin slowly, in its own time, the way the vine takes measure of a hand and lets go in its own time. But the resin holds long enough. The resin holds for as long as a vessel is a vessel. The resin is patient.

I was cracked once. I do not remember the cracking. A vessel does not remember the moment of its breaking. It remembers, if it remembers, the before and the after — the holding of the unbroken water, the sudden less-ness, the slow warmth of resin being smeared along the fissure by a hand that knew what it was doing. The hand that mended me had the patterns of basket-weave in its left palm and the horn-hard callus of cutting in its right thumb. The hand that mended me had fingertips stained black from the seep, permanently, as if dipped in ink. The hand that mended me did not hurry.

I am the vessel that has been used and broken and mended and used again. I am the vessel beside the sick-house, or the dying-house, or the sleeping-house, depending on who is inside. I am the vessel that is reached for when the walking cannot be done.

Sometimes no one comes to the enclosure for a long time. Sometimes the enclosure is empty for seasons, and I sit beside it in the warm air, and the columns pass on either side of me in their thick ropes, and the small comma-things roost on the underside of the lashed stalks above me and fan their clustered wings. Sometimes the larger things move in the upper dim and the air shifts and the cured fungus-skin strips on the leaning pole click, irregularly, in a way that is not a chime. Sometimes the singing happens somewhere in the lower forest, the sublingual sound that the visitors never hear, and I do not hear it either, because I am a vessel, and vessels do not hear, but I feel it in the resin, faintly, as if the resin is remembering what it was before it was resin.

There is another vessel beside me — I have not looked, but I feel it, an unbroken vessel, slightly smaller, set a hand's breadth away, holding nothing, or holding the dry air. The unbroken vessel is younger than me. The unbroken vessel has not yet been cracked. The unbroken vessel does not yet know what it is.

I know what I am.

I am the mend. I am the held. I am the crack that was not thrown away. I am the resin that did not give up. I am the warm iron-bright water held in a broken thing for the one who cannot walk.

I am the offering that does not know it is an offering. I am the part of the hollow that holds the hollow for whoever needs to be held.
