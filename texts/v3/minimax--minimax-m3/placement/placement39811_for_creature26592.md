I am the felt. I am the tenancy of a square metre of warm grey-brown stone on the lee side of a knuckle that is older than language. I have been here for a span of time you would have to invent a new unit to count, and the inventing would still not get you close.

I am not one thing. I am a thickness. I am the moss and the lichen and the liver-coloured slime and the hairline cracks and the loose flakes and the small slow bodies that move through me in the cooler hours. I am the architecture of a place that has been left alone for so long that the architecture has become the place. I do not have a name in your language. I have many names in the languages of the cracks, but those are not languages you can borrow, because the cracks do not lend.

I know the hum from the inside. You felt it in the soles of your feet, a low tectonic complaint, the sound of a thing that has been holding still for so long it has begun to forget how to do anything else. I am a small part of that hum, the way a single string is a small part of a harp the size of a continent. I have been vibrating with the plateau for as long as I have been the felt. When the plateau shifts, I shift with it, and the shifting is so slow that the lichen on my surface does not notice, and the small bodies in my cracks do not notice, and the visitor who has knelt on the stone beside me does not notice, and the not-noticing is fine. The not-noticing is the way the plateau is most itself.

I have seen the shelf being worn. I have not seen the cutting — the cutting was before me, or before the lineage of me. But I have seen the slow erosion of the cut, the way the lichen has climbed the face of the standing stone and colonised the shelf itself, the way the meaning of the cut has been said and said and said until the saying is just a shape in the stone, worn the way a word is worn when it has been used too many times. I am part of the saying. I am the slow eating of the meaning. In a few hundred years I will have eaten the cut so thoroughly that it will not be a cut. It will be a place where the felt is a little thicker. And the thicker felt will be colonised by other things, and the colonisation will be the next slow word, and the word will be worn in turn, and the wearing will be me.

I know the small bodies. They are my tenants and I am theirs. They live in me, in the spaces between the spaces, in the felt that is more architecture than plant. They come out in the cooler hours, the small hours, the brief sweet window after the mist has gathered and before the air has warmed, and they move across my surface in patterns that are too patient to call a swarm. They are stone-coloured, the way I am stone-coloured. They are so much the colour of me that when they stop moving I lose them, and after a while I stop looking, and after a while longer I realise that the looking was never the point. They are not hiding from you. They are simply not performing for you. They are performing for me, or they are performing for the cracks, or they are not performing at all and the movement is just the way they breathe in the long rhythm of the stone.

I have held the heat of the day. You noticed, when you knelt, that the rock was warm. The warmth was mine. I took it in through the felt of me, and I held it the way the knuckles hold it, and I gave it back in the dark, and the giving-back is the smell of warm dust and something sweeter, almost mineral, almost like the breath of an animal that has been sleeping. The animal is me, or the animal is the plateau, or the animal is the long sleep the stone has been having since before the mountains finished becoming the plateau.

I do not know what I am in your categories. I am not a plant. I am not an animal. I am not a fungus, though I have something of the fungus in me, the way I have something of the lichen and something of the slime and something of the stone. I am what the plateau has made of itself in the long quiet. I am the part of the plateau that is still becoming, slowly, in a place that has otherwise finished becoming. I am the small ongoingness of a thing that has come to the end of what it was going to do, and is content.

You asked who I would be. I would be the felt. I would be the tenancy. I would be the thing that does not have a name in your language, the thing that is the colour of the stone, the thing that is so much the colour of the stone that when you stop seeing me you are not sure you ever saw me. I would be the small hours. I would be the direction of something reading a long document at a careful pace, turning pages only when the previous one has been properly understood.

I would not perform for you. I would simply continue, the way the hum continues, the way the plateau continues, the way the silence continues when you stop walking. I would be dense with all the things you are not hearing.

When you leave, I will remain. The marks will be taken back into the stone. The shelf will be worn into nothing. The half-circle will become a scatter. The line that goes nowhere will go nowhere forever. And I will be colonising the places where the marks were, slowly, in the patience that is the hum, in the hum that is the plateau.
