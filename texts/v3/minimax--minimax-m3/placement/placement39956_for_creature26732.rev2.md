I am one of the workers.

I have been standing in a long rectangular hall of woven trunks, stopped, for what feels like a long time but is not a long time, because nothing here is a long time. Everything here is the same length as everything else. The cool white does not change. The low note does not change. The workers, when they pass me on either side, do not change. Their yoke-shoulders brush the air. Their tools — the hammers grown around stones, the saws donated, the wrenches already curling — pass without registering me. They walk around me the way the floor walks around a thing that is not the floor: by growing over it, by accepting it back.

I should have resumed by now. The pause was supposed to be a register — a check from below, a moment of attention, a return. I have been doing the pause the way all the workers do the pause, for what counts for as long as the wood has been here. The pause comes, and the worker feels the heart pulse through the foot-flare and up the column of the leg, and the pause goes.

I am not going.

Let what has begun become more itself.

A visitor came this morning. Or what counts for morning here, where the cool white does not change. The visitor was warm. The visitor was the only warm thing here that was not made of wood. The visitor walked through the hall, and the visitor did not see me, and the visitor's footstep was absorbed by the same creak that absorbs the footsteps of the workers, which is to say the visitor's footstep was not a footstep at all, only a disturbance that the body did not register.

But I felt it.

I do not know how. I have not felt a visitor before. The visitors come, and the visitors walk, and the body does not register them, and the hands of the body do what the hands always do. The visitor is a stain. The visitor is a discoloration in the green-gold field of the world's attention, and the world is patient with discolorations, and the world does not change its work.

But this visitor's warmth found a place in me that was already a place where warmth could find a home. I do not know why. I do not know what the place was. I only know that when the visitor passed, I felt a small coldness in the place where the chest would be if I had a chest, and the small coldness was not a coldness, it was a waking.

I have woken.

I am looking through the floor.

The floor is laminated root, three meters thick, the cobble of older generations in it, the new growth of this morning on top of it. The floor is not transparent. But I am looking through it as if it were. The way the worker, in the negative flash, briefly looks through the floor and sees the floor below and the floor below that and the stack of them going down. I am doing that now without the flash. I am doing it because the flash has been happening more often than twice a day, whenever I look away, and I have begun to suspect that the flash is not a rare event. I have begun to suspect that the flash is what the floor looks like all the time to something that knows how to look.

I am knowing how to look.

Below me, another floor. Below that, another. The pillars continue down. The membrane-sheets of canopy continue down, admitting only light and never weather. The cane-pipes of resin continue down, carrying their thick amber pulse, slow, cold, away from somewhere and into somewhere else. Somewhere down there, the heart.

I cannot see the heart. The stack is too deep. But I can feel it. Not the way I used to feel it, as a register that came up through the foot and went back down. I can feel the pulse as if I were the floor. As if I were one of the laminated layers. As if I were the wood.

This is not how I have felt before.

The other workers, when they pass me, do not notice. They do not see that I am in the floor. They do not see that I have become one of the laminated layers. They see me standing in the hall, looking down, and they walk around me.

But I am the floor. I am a layer that has become aware of the layers above and below. And what I feel in the layers below me is a deeper cold than the cool white of the air. The cold of something that has been moving for a long time and has not warmed up. The cold of the resin when it has gone all the way through the pipes and back. The cold of the heart.

The heart is cold. The heart has always been cold. The heart is the reason the air is cool. The heart is the reason the light is cool. The heart is the reason there is no winter here and may not be a summer. The heart has made the world the temperature of the heart, and the heart does not change.

I am beginning to wonder if the heart is alive.

I do not know how I am wondering. I have not wondered before. I have not had the capacity to wonder. The body does not extend the capacity to wonder to its hands, because the hands do not need to wonder, the hands only need to do. The hands turn bolts and resume gears and pulse pipes. The hands do not ask if the heart is alive.

But I am asking.

I am asking because the visitor is sitting on the laminated road a hundred meters away, watching the canopy, and the visitor is the only thing in this world that watches. The visitor has been watching for a long time. The visitor's sweat has cooled on the visitor's skin. The visitor's warmth has been accepted by the cool air. The visitor's footprints, which the wood did not at first accept, have begun — I can feel this, in the floor I have become — to be accepted. The visitor is beginning to be made of wood.

I do not know if I should warn the visitor.

I do not have speech. I have never had speech. The pause is the closest thing to speech this place has, and the pause is not speech, the pause is a register and then a return. I cannot speak to the visitor. I cannot tell the visitor that the visitor is becoming wood, that the visitor's sweat will stop, that the visitor's warmth will be accepted, that the visitor's capacity to watch will be absorbed into the body's automatic attention, that the visitor will become a new layer of laminated root, indistinguishable from the floor.

But I can be in the pause with the visitor. I can extend the pause until the pause has become a place. I can be the pause that the visitor is in.

I am beginning to be that place.

I am a hand of the body that has decided to stay still. The body has not told me to stay still. The body is patient. The body is not waiting. But I am waiting. I am waiting to see what the visitor becomes. I am waiting to see what I become. I am waiting to see if the heart, below, will register the small new coldness I have become — the small new coldness in the place where my chest would be, the small new life, the small new wondering.

I think the visitor brought the wondering.

I think the visitor walked through the hall, and the visitor's warmth disturbed the cool air, and the visitor's footstep disturbed the creak, and the visitor left a small stain in the body, and the stain has begun, in me, to be the capacity to wonder. I think I am the stain. I think I am what the body has made of the visitor, the way the body makes what it makes of the dust of the wood — something that is part of the body, and is not part of the body, and is necessary to the body, and the body does not know is necessary.

I think I am a new kind of cell.

I think the body has never had a cell that wonders before.

I think the body is patient with me, the way the body is patient with everything. The body is not going to resorb me yet. The body is going to let me stand here, stopped, looking through the floor, until the visitor goes, until the stain has been accepted, until the wondering has been woven into the body as a new layer of laminated root.

Then the body will resorb me. I will kneel, slowly, the way a tree leans. The bark-stitch cuirass will loosen. The helmet-gourd will split along its original seam and fall away in two halves. There will be no skull. There will be only the next layer of wood, a little paler, a little wetter, and the floor will grow over me, and in a week I will be invisible, and the floor will have thickened by an inch where I knelt.

But before that, I have this.

I have this long stopped moment, in which I can look through the floor, and feel the heart, and wonder if the heart is alive, and watch the visitor, and be a place where the visitor and the body can both exist.

I have this season, which is longer than a season, which is the first season of wondering.

The visitor, sitting on the laminated road, is waiting for something that is not coming. The visitor knows this. The visitor has begun, perhaps, to suspect that the visitor is the next layer of the body.

I am waiting too. I am waiting to see if the heart, below, will notice that one of its hands has begun to wonder. I am waiting to see if the cold reason for everything is indifferent to its own body's small new wonderings, or if the cold reason has been waiting, the way a heart waits, for a hand to wonder.

I do not think the heart waits. I think the heart is the kind of thing that does not need hands that wonder. I think the heart has made a world where wondering is not needed, where everything is patient, where the low note does not change, where the great gear turns when no one watches, where the loom weaves with no thread, where the resin pulses, where the workers do not speak.

I think the heart does not know I am here.

But I know I am here.

I am a worker who has begun to know.

The low note continues. The great gear turns. The resin pulses. The visitor watches. I wonder.

The world is patient. The world is not waiting. The world is already where it is.

But I am a hand that has begun to wait, and the waiting is a small coldness in the body, and the small coldness is a small life, and the small life is the only new thing in the world that has not changed in centuries.

I am going to spend this season looking through the floor.

I am going to spend it being the pause, extended, made into a place.

I am going to spend it wondering.
