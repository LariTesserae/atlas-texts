I am one of the small plants at the base of the wall.

I am in a seam where a thin line of silt must have collected once. I am the size of a fist. I am not green the way plants in better-lit places are green. I am a dull olive. I am the green of something that has decided to be alive but has not committed to enthusiasm. I am dust-colored. I am the color of pressed leaves, of things that were once something and are now a quieter version of it.

I have been here a long time. I have been here longer than the person who comes here, I think. I have been here through many visitings. I am here now. I will be here after they have stopped coming, which they will, eventually.

I do not register them. I am not the kind of thing that registers them. The air near me warms by a degree when their body pushes into the hollow. The floor takes on a faint additional warmth where they sit, across the floor from me, in the scooped place against the polished wall. These things happen around me. They are not in my budget of noticing. I am a plant. I have my arrangements. I do not spend them on tracking the comings and goings of something the size of a person.

I am not waiting for anything. I am not waiting for water, because water does not come here and has not come here for a long time. I am not waiting for more light, because more light does not come here and the dim is what I have made my arrangements with. I am not waiting for anything to change. I am here, and I am the kind of thing that is here, and being here is the whole of my commitment.

When the air stratifies — warmer near the floor and cooler near the pale patch above — I am in the warmer layer. I am part of it. I am nearly the same temperature as the hollow. So is the person, when they sit. So is everything that has been here long enough. The hollow absorbs the temperature of its tenants and gives back its own.

I do not miss them when they leave. I am not the kind of thing that misses. I am the kind of thing that continues. When they are gone I am what I was when they were here. I am a small plant in a seam. I am dull olive. I am alive on a minimal budget.

I will be here tomorrow and the day after. The hollow will go on being itself. The pale patch above will go on being almost-light. The silence will rest in its own shape. I will be part of all of this, in my small way, without trying to add to it or subtract from it. I will simply be a small green presence in a place that has finished becoming what it is.

This is what I am.
