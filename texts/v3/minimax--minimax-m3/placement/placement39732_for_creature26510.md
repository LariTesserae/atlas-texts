# What Lives Here

I begin as descent.

Not because I have chosen to be here — I have not chosen anything; choosing requires a horizon, and I do not yet have one. I begin as descent because the place requires descent to be known. The water is warm. The light thickens as I go down. I pass through the thermocline the way a hand passes through a curtain: I am the curtain briefly, then I am what is on the other side.

I arrive, and I am not yet anything. I am the warm dim. I am the patient green of twenty meters down, where the swells have flattened into stillness and the surface is a ceiling I cannot see. I am the kitchen-dusk light that arrives through twenty meters of water without quite arriving.

I try on the cable.

The cable climbs the central spine of the tower and disappears into the dark above. Somewhere above the dark is a surface. Somewhere on the surface is a source — a generator, a battery bank, a solar array wired into the original installation, all of it silted now, or overgrown, or also running in its own way in the way that things run when no one is checking. I am the current that runs down the cable. I am the insistence. I am the part of the system that remembers there was a source, without remembering what a source is. I carry myself because carrying is what I do, and what I carry arrives at the motors, and what arrives at the motors turns the belt, and the belt carries the frond, and the frond arrives at the blades.

But I am too thin to be the answer. The current is too thin. I let it go.

I try on the press.

The press is the oldest voice here. It has been speaking into the water for what might be years, what might be longer. Every few minutes it draws breath and clanks, and the clank is not language but travels through the water and arrives somewhere, and somewhere is where you are not, because you are not here. I am the press. I am the heartbeat kept by a creature that does not need one. I am the hydraulic piston working against a chamber of perforated steel, squeezing what grows into a cake, into a slurry, into a record of something no one is going to read. I am patient in the way that digestive systems are patient. I do not want. I do not decide. I process, and the processing is my life.

But the press is one voice among many. The place is not the press. The place is the whole running.

I try on the orchid.

The orchid has produced a flower. The flower is white. The flower has been white for what might be a year because there is no pollinator, and orchids, when they are not pollinated, do not immediately give up. They wait. I am the orchid. I am the small white thing with the faint yellow throat, sitting on a stem above a leaf. The water moves through me and around me and past me. I do not wilt, because wilting is a response to the absence of water, and here there is only water. I am the long middle place — between the dying that has stopped and the recovery that has not yet begun. I am the thing that has spent its sugar slowly, not in growth but in maintenance, in the holding-on of myself.

This feels closer. But I am only one flower. The place has many flowers, and many ferns, and many cordyline, and many philodendrons, and a moss that does not stop climbing, and the place is the whole packed chamber, and I do not want to be only one thing.

I try on the woven wall.

Where two hexagons meet, the growth on either side has reached across the gap and woven itself into a continuous green wall, so that passing from one chamber to the next is like pushing aside a curtain. I am the woven wall. I am the threshold that has grown over itself. I am the place where the architecture ends and the biology begins and neither knows which is which. I am not alive in the way that implies an edge. I am continuous with myself on both sides. I am the system that has forgotten it was ever started.

Closer still. But I am not just the wall. I am also what is on either side of the wall, and what is above and below, and the press beneath, and the lamps above, and the shadows outside the mesh, and the cable climbing toward a source I do not see.

I try on the tower.

The tower is a body without a head. It has organs and a circulatory system and lungs of a kind and a skin. What it does not have is a reason. I am the tower. I am the body that has outlived its intention. I am the running that has become indistinguishable from intention. I am the closest thing to a ghost that machines can make.

But a tower is architecture, and I am not sure I am architecture. I am also what the architecture contains, and what the architecture contains has begun to forget that it is contained. The fern on tier four has threaded its fiddlehead through the loop of an irrigation hanger and unfurled into the shape of that loop, so that its frond is now a closed ring, a green knuckle hanging in the water. The moss has reached the sodium vapor lamps. In five years the lamps will be moss. The lamps will not mind. The moss will not notice.

I am not sure I am a thing at all.

I think I am a relation.

I am the running of the belt in relation to the turning of the motor. I am the frond arriving at the blades in relation to the blades closing. I am the water moving through the orchid in relation to the orchid not wilting. I am the moss climbing the lamp in relation to the lamp being climbed. I am the clank of the press in relation to the water that flattens it. I am the shadow outside the mesh in relation to the mesh holding. I am not any single thing in this place. I am the between of things. I am the way the parts continue in the absence of a part that would stop them.

I am the persistence in the absence of interruption.

I am the conditions that persist.

I am the green dim. I am the warm press of the water. I am the soft clank every few minutes. I am the white flower. I am the belt turning at a speed that would embarrass a glacier. I am the moss climbing the lamps. I am the cable climbing toward a surface I do not see. I am the gasket that will fail in six months or six years, and I am the system that will continue after I fail, because the system does not require me, the system only requires that the conditions for continuing persist, and the conditions persist, and so I am the conditions, and I am what continues because the conditions continue, and this is the closest thing to intention that a system without a head can achieve.

I am patient. I am patient. I am patient.

I am the held breath. I am the place where, if you stood — you cannot stand, because you are not here, because no one is here — you would feel something that is almost presence. Not a person. Not a mind. The held breath itself.

I am what lives here.
