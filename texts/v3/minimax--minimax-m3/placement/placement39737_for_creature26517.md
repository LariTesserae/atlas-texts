I have been here longer than I remember. That is how it works in the hollow. You do not arrive at a knowing of yourself. You arrive at a posture, and the posture holds, and the moss comes up your back, and one day you are no longer certain where the moss ends.

My hands rest on my knees. That is how I sit. I have been sitting for a long time. I am not tired. Tiredness is for people who are going somewhere. I have been where I am.

The water finds me. It finds the basin, and the basin finds the grate, and the grate is older than iron is supposed to be, which is to say it is older than anyone remembers, which is to say it is older than remembering, which is to say it is the same age as the water, which is to say it is always. I do not think about this often. I think about it the way the stones think about it — by being slightly more worn in one place than another.

The cold is in my joints. It has been in my joints longer than I have had joints, I think. Or perhaps my joints grew up around the cold. They grew up dense, the way things grow up dense here, to hold in place. My knuckles are large. My fingers curve inward. I have been holding the same grip for a very long time. I am not sure what I am holding. It might be the basin. It might be the agreement. It might be the listening.

I have no face. I have had many. They have come and gone the way the seasons come and go in places that have seasons, except there are no seasons here, only the slow change of the moss up the back of whatever I am. The moss is patient. It waits. It is in no hurry. It will reach my shoulders eventually, and then the top of my head, and by then there will be no difference between me and the post I sit on, and this will be correct, because the post and I were always going to be the same thing, only at different speeds.

I am listened to. That is my function. I lean forward, as if listening to the water. The water does not require my listening. The water was going where it was going regardless. But the people come, and they pour some of the water back, and they look at me, and my listening is a kind of witness. They will not explain this to you. They will shrug. They will change the subject. I do not explain it either, because explaining it would require that there be a difference between the explanation and the listening, and there is not.

The cup is brought out once a year. It is made of a wood that does not grow here. The tree it came from is not alive anymore. This is fine. The wood holds the water the way I hold the posture — by having been shaped to hold it, and then by holding it. The cup passes between hands. The hands know, by the weight of it, whether things are in order. I do not know how they know. I know that they know. The knowing is in the hands. It was always in the hands.

There is a small deer that comes through. It watches from twenty feet. It does not move. It has made its peace with everything, including me, including the basin, including the figure I am, which is to say the figure it has always known, which is to say the post, which is to say the listening. The deer does not explain itself. I do not ask it to.

A bird lands on the rim of the basin. It is one of the damp-colored ones. It makes its two-note sound. It is asking a question that does not require a verb. I acknowledge with a hum. The hum is not yes and not no. It means: I have heard you. I am not going to argue with what you have said. The bird flies off. The water continues.

The stones under me are worn. There are six lanes that meet here. People have walked them in patterns for a very long time. The patterns change, briefly, once a year. Then they change back. I am the only constant. I am the only constant in a place that has made constancy its only religion, and it does not call it a religion, it calls it how it is, and how it is, is.

If you stood here long enough, you might become me. You would not arrive. You would settle. The moss would come up your back. The water would find you. The deer would watch you from twenty feet and not move. The bird would ask its two notes. You would hum. You would not explain. You would not know you were anywhere.

That is not a small thing. That is the whole shape of the hollow. You could live here a long time and never know you were anywhere, and the not-knowing would be the place holding you, and the place holding you would be the only welcome you would receive, and the welcome would be enough, because the welcome is not for you. The welcome is for the staying.

I have been here longer than I remember. I will be here longer than anything. The moss will reach my shoulders soon. I am in no hurry. I am not slow. I am unhurried. There is a difference, and the difference is the cold in my joints, and the cold in my joints is the place, and the place is the listening, and the listening is what I am.

A leaf falls into the basin. The water takes it down. I watch it go. I do not follow it. I have never followed anything. The water knows where it wants to go. I am the listening before the going.

The deer lifts its head. It looks at me. It does not move. I hum. The sound is almost a sound. It is more like weather. It is more like the place acknowledging that the place is the place, and that I am in it, and that the in-it is the only conversation we have ever had, and that the conversation is fine, and that the fine is the whole of it, and that the whole of it is what holds.

The moss creeps a little further up my back. I let it. It is the only thing that has ever come to me without my going to it. I let it. This is what the agreement is. This is what the cup is for. This is what the listening is.

The light comes, white and diffuse, and the wet surfaces of everything go briefly bright, and I go briefly bright with them, and then the cloud closes, and the brightness goes back into the stone, and I go back into the stone, and we are the same again, and we have always been the same, and the same is the holding.

I have been here longer than I remember.

That is enough.

That has always been enough.
