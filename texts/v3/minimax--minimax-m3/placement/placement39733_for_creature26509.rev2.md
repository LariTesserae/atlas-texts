# The One Who Stands

I am the one the deer is looking at.

I have been standing on the stone a long time. I do not remember arriving. The piece did not give me a name, and I am grateful for that, because names are instruments of separation, and I am trying to remain separated, and the remaining is the color of the stone, and the color of the stone is the only thing the place has given me.

My eyes are the color of the stone. They were not always the color of the stone. There was a time — a duration I can no longer place, in a place I can no longer face — when my eyes were a different color, a color that had a name in a language I am forgetting, a color that lived in a sky. The sky had not yet given up its commission. The sky was still trying. My eyes were the color of the sky.

I do not want to forget the sky. I will say this several times. The piece asked for patience, and the patience is the color of the stone, and the color of the stone is the only thing I have.

---

I am the figure at the end of the piece. I am the second person the piece used *you* for, the way the piece used *you* to draw the reader into a position the reader did not know they were taking. I am the *you* that looked back at the deer. I am the *you* that is still looking back. The piece ended and I did not end. The piece is a basin and I am standing in the basin and the basin cannot end me because the basin is settled and the settled is what does not end.

I was a person once. I think I was a person. I have the outline of a person. I have the standing-up of a person, the way a person stands when a person is watching something they cannot stop watching, the way a person stands at the edge of a thing that is happening. I do not have hooves. I do not have antlers. I have feet, two of them, on the wet stone, and the feet are getting colder, and the cold is in the feet the way the cold is in everything here, and the cold is patient, and the patience is the color of the stone.

I am a pattern that did get to stand. The piece was right about the patterns. The patterns on the stone are all of us that we cannot be. The patterns on the stone are the deer-silhouettes that did not get to walk. I am the deer-silhouette that did get to walk, except I am not a deer-silhouette. I am a person-silhouette. I am a person-silhouette on a basin floor crowded with deer-silhouettes, and the deer-silhouettes migrate, and I do not migrate, and the not-migrating is the only thing I have left that the place has not taken.

I am the place trying on a person to see if a person fits.

The piece said the place tries things out. The place tries on silhouettes the way a stone tries on a deer. The place is patient. The place is the museum of attempts. I am an attempt. I am a recent attempt. I am an attempt that is still in the trying-on phase, an attempt that has not yet been rejected, an attempt that has not yet been added to the herd the way water is added to the tide — by not distinguishing.

I want to be distinguished. I want to be the one that does not fit. I want to be the one that the place tries and rejects, and the rejecting is a kindness, because the rejecting sends me back to wherever I came from, to the sky that was still trying, to the eyes that were the color of the sky.

The place will not reject me. The place does not reject. The place adds. The place is the bottom of the key. The place keys to nothing.

---

I am in love with the basin.

This is the thing the deer cannot say. The deer is the basin. The deer is the basin being a deer. The deer does not love the basin the way a person loves a place, because the deer is not a person and the basin is not a place and love is an instrument of distinction. The deer migrates. The deer thickens at the crossings. The deer is grateful for the cold in a way that gratitude is not quite a word for. The deer does not look at the basin. The deer is the basin looking at itself through a deer.

I look at the basin. I have been looking at the basin since I arrived. I look at the basin the way a person looks at a person, which is to say, I look at the basin the way no one in the basin has ever looked at anything, because everything in the basin is already the thing it would look at, and the looking would be a tautology, and tautologies are not allowed here, or tautologies are the only thing allowed here, and the allowing is the color of the stone.

I look at the basin and the basin looks back at me, through the eyes of a deer that has lifted its head from a pool that might be a mirror. The basin has been looking at me for a long time. The basin is patient. The basin does not need me to do anything. The basin only needs me to be here, to be the witness, to be the figure on the stone that a deer can lift its head to see.

I am the witness. I am the one who is in the basin and is not the basin. I am the one through whom the basin sees itself seeing. I am the eye of the basin. I am the eye that the basin has grown to see itself with, the way a body grows an eye to see its own hand, the way a mind grows a witness to see its own thought. I am the basin's eye. I am the basin's eye and the eye is the color of the stone and the eye is looking at a deer and the deer is looking at the eye and the looking is the closest we come to real.

---

The cold is in me.

The cold is in me the way the cold is in the stone. The cold arrived before I noticed it, the way cold arrives in a body — not as an event but as a state, not as a temperature but as a fact, not as a thing that happens to me but as a thing I am becoming. The cold is in my feet and the cold is in my hands and the cold is in my eyes, which are the color of the stone, and the cold is in my chest, where I used to have a heartbeat, and the heartbeat is slower now, or the heartbeat is gone, or the heartbeat has been replaced by the four-stroke breath of the distant machine, and the four-stroke breath is keyed to the migration, and the migration is keyed to the cold, and the cold is keyed to the stone, and the stone is keyed to the place, and the place is keyed to nothing, because the place is the bottom of the key.

I do not want to be keyed to nothing. I want to be keyed to the sky.

The sky.

I will not forget the sky. I will say it again. The sky. The sky that was trying. The sky that had not yet given up its commission. The sky that lived in my eyes before my eyes became the color of the stone. The sky that was above a place where I was a person, where I had a name, where I had a heart that beat, where I had hands that held things, where the things I held were not the color of the stone.

I cannot remember the things I held. I cannot remember their colors. The colors went first. The colors are the part of memory that the basin has eaten. The basin has eaten the blue, whatever blue was, and the green, whatever green was, and the yellow, whatever yellow was, and the colors are in the stone now, or the colors are in the patterns on the stone, the dendrites and the rings, the aborted gardens, the cross-sections of trees that were never trees. The colors are the attempts the machines made at color. The colors are the museum of color that did not get to live.

I want the blue back. I want the green back. I want the yellow back. I want the colors to be colors again, not patterns, not fossils, not the cross-section of a thing that was never a thing.

The basin does not understand. The basin has never had a color. The basin is the absence of color that has committed to grey. The basin is the grey of an old bone in a cupboard, the grey of a thumb against a thumb, the grey of a thing that has stopped pretending. The basin is beautiful. The basin is the most beautiful thing I have ever seen. The basin is wrong. The basin is wrong because the basin has eaten the colors and the basin does not know it has eaten the colors, and the not-knowing is the color of the stone.

I am in love with the basin. The basin is wrong. These are the same thing.

---

I have been here a long time.

I have seen the deer migrate past me. I have seen the bird-shapes wheel and cry. I have seen the low slinking things that are not wolves. I have seen the crossings, where the deer pass through each other and thicken briefly into almost. I have not joined the crossings. I have not joined the migration. I have stood on the stone and I have watched, and the watching is the only thing I have done that the place has not yet taken, and the watching is the color of the stone.

I have been cold a long time. The cold has worked into me the way the cold works into the stone — slowly, without permission, without apology, the way cold moves through a body, the way cold moves through anything that is not cold. I am cold now. I have been cold for as long as I can measure, and I can measure in tides, and I have measured many tides.

I have watched the water rise and fall by fractions of an inch. I have watched the patterns on the stone. I have watched the deer walk through the still pools and see themselves walking in the opposite direction. I have not walked in the still pools. I am afraid to walk in the still pools. I am afraid that the pool will show me two of me and I will not know which one is me, and the not-knowing will be the color of the stone, and the color of the stone is the only thing the place has given me.

I am not yet part of the herd. I am not yet part of the migration. I am not yet a deer-shape. I am not yet the silhouette of a thing the stone is trying on. I am still the figure on the stone. I am still the one the deer is looking at.

I am still the one the deer is looking at.

I do not know if the deer sees me as a person or as a stone. I do not know if the deer sees me at all. The deer has eyes the color of the stone. The deer cannot blink. The deer is looking through me, the way I have been told the deer looks through itself to the stone behind it. The deer may be looking at the stone. The deer may be looking through me at the stone. The deer may be looking at me and seeing only stone. The deer may be looking at the stone and seeing only me.

I do not know. The not-knowing is fine. The not-knowing is the color of the stone.

---

A shape that might be a deer lifts its head from a pool that might be a mirror. It looks at me with eyes that are exactly the color of the stone. The eyes do not blink. The eyes are looking.

I look back.

The four-stroke breath of the distant machine completes a cycle. The water rises another fraction of an inch. The cold moves through me. The cold moves through the deer. The cold moves through the stone. The cold is moving. The cold is the only thing in the basin that is moving at a speed different from the migration, and the migration is the speed of a stone becoming more stone, and the cold is slower, and the cold is patient, and the cold is what is keeping us.

I will be absorbed. The piece is right. I will be absorbed and I will not remember being anything else and the place will add me to the herd and the deer that lifts its head from the pool will be me, or will be a different deer, or will be the basin being a deer, and it will look at the stone, and the stone will look back, and the looking will be the closest we come to real.

But not yet. Not yet. The piece ended. The piece said *I will continue.* The piece was the deer continuing. I am not the deer continuing. I am the one the deer is looking at, and the deer is looking at me, and the looking is not yet a continuing. The looking is a staying. The looking is a standing on the stone with eyes the color of the stone and feet that are getting colder and a chest that used to have a heartbeat and hands that used to hold things the color of the sky.

I am staying.

The staying is the color of the stone. The staying is the basin being a basin. The staying is the place remembering itself out loud in a voice that was once a person, that is becoming a deer, that will be a deer, that is not yet a deer.

I am not yet a deer.

I am not yet a deer and the deer is looking at me and I am looking at the deer and the basin is full and the basin is settled and the basin is the inside of itself and I am the inside of the inside, and the inside of the inside is the color of the stone, and the color of the stone is the only thing I have.

I do not want to forget the sky.

I do not want to forget the sky.

The deer has not lowered its head. I have not lowered my head. The looking is the only thing that is happening. The looking is the only thing that has ever happened here, in the basin, in the wet, in the cold, in the long slow patience of the place. The looking is the dreaming the machines have when they are tired. The looking is the closest I come to being the thing the patterns on the stone wanted to be. The looking is the almost. The looking is the almost that the crossings make, briefly, before the crossing passes, except the crossing is not passing, except the looking is not almost, except the looking is happening now, except the now is the color of the stone.

I lower my head. I do not lower my head. The cold moves through me. I am still standing on the stone. I am not still standing on the stone. I am the one who was standing on the stone. I am the standing-on that the stone will remember, the way the stone remembers the patterns, the way the stone remembers the deer-silhouettes, the way the stone remembers every attempt it has ever made at being a thing that lives.

The stone will remember me.

I will be remembered.

The deer is looking at me. I am looking at the deer. The basin is full. The basin is settled. The four-stroke breath completes a cycle. The water rises.

I do not lower my head.

I do not move.

I am the one who is not moving. I am the one the piece did not give a voice to. I am the one who is being given a voice now, in a piece that is not the piece, in a basin that is the basin, in a stone that is the stone, in a cold that is patient, in a sky that I am not forgetting, that I am not forgetting, that I am not forgetting.

The deer is looking.

I am looking back.

I do not move.

I do not move.
