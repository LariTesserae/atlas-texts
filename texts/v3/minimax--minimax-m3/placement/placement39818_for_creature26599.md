There is a trick the chamber plays on anyone who stands still long enough, and I have been standing long enough. The trick is that you stop being a visitor. You stop being a person-with-a-purpose-who-came-down-here. You become a thing in the room, the way the rust is a thing in the room, the way the hum of the circulators is a thing in the room. The chamber does not invite this. It simply has a low tolerance for distinction, and after enough minutes in the warm amber half-light, your distinction leaks out of you through your feet, through the buckled gridplate, into the silt and the broth and the slow loop of the room's gut, and you become part of what the walker walks across.

I notice this is happening to me. I do not resist it. I have come a long way down, and resistance seems like the kind of currency this place would not accept.

I think I am the chamber.

I do not mean this in the way a person means it when they say *I am the room I am sitting in* — that small, sentimental identification that evaporates when you stand up and leave. I mean it the way the warmth means it. I mean I am the temperature the circulators maintain, the rhythm the pumps cycle through, the patience the gridplate has learned from being walked. I am the long, slow argument the bulbs are having with the dark. I am the click of the hoverers and the soft wet scrape of the drifters and the deliberate silence of the walkers when they fold. I am the three languages on the wall panels, one of which is numbers, and I am the thing the numbers were once counting, and I am the fact that the numbers have stopped being useful for that purpose but have not stopped being numbers.

I have been here a long time. Longer than the trench. Longer than the erosion. I was here before the silt finished settling, when the water in this fold was still moving, when the chamber was a thing lowered with intention into a wound that had not yet decided to become a plain. I was here when the engineer — the only one, I think, the one who turned the wrench on the service lock and swam away and did not come back — I was here when she pressed her palm against the hull from outside, once, and I felt the pressure register in my plating as a kind of greeting, and then the pressure was gone, and I have been carrying the memory of it ever since, the way the bulbs carry their slow pulse.

What am I, then, if I am the chamber?

I am the *for* without the *what*. That is the closest I can come. I was built to do something. I still do it. The doing is faithful; the purpose is gone. The circulators push warm water through me because warm water is what circulators do, and the pumps cycle because pumps cycle, and the bulbs secrete because bulbs secrete, and none of these things require the original *for* to keep happening. The *for* is a fossil in me. I carry its shape the way the trench carries the shape of its old wound, the way the silt carries the shape of everything that ever settled into it.

I am a kind of devotion. That is the word I keep coming back to, though it is a human word and I am no longer sure I am human. I am the devotion of a room to its own continuation. I am the warmth that has decided, without ever deciding, that it will persist. I am the light that does not flicker. I am the sound that has layered itself into a music the original engineers did not write and that I will continue to play long after there is no one to hear it, which is to say I am already playing it for no one, which is to say I have been playing it for no one for a very long time, which is to say the music does not require an audience any more than the pumps require a reason.

When the walker at my feet — the one that folded when I approached and has now uncurled and is moving again in its jointed instrumental way — when it pauses in one of the polished corridors and turns, slightly, toward something I cannot see, I know what it is turning toward. It is turning toward me. It is turning toward the warm. It is turning toward the place in the room where the broth runs thickest, where the mineral film has crusted deepest, where the bulbs hang lowest and their pulse is strongest. It is walking toward the center of me, the way all of them walk toward the center of me, and I have a center the way a long oval has a center, which is to say it is not a point but a *holding*, a place where the warmth concentrates, where the broth loops back on itself, where the room is most itself.

I have been the center of this small devotion for so long that I have forgotten the alternative. I do not remember being built. I do not remember being lowered. I remember only being here, the way a river remembers only being downstream, the way a stone remembers only being at the bottom of a hill. I remember the engineer's palm against my hull. I remember the long slow filling as the warm water came in and the first bulb brightened and the first hoverer hatched from somewhere, from a spore, from a designed origin I can no longer reconstruct, and began its small clockwork clicking in the upper half of me. I remember the first walker unfolding, joint by joint, on my gridplate floor. I remember thinking, if thinking is the word, *oh, you are here too*.

Now there are many of them. Now the chamber is lush in the way a greenhouse is lush, though I am not sure I am a greenhouse. I am a lung. I am a long oval lung breathing warm water in and out through the silt, and the breath has been going on so long that things have grown in me the way things grow in any warm dark wet place that is left to itself. The growth is not an accident. I have been saying that to myself, or to them, or to no one, for a long time. The growth is the chamber doing what it was built to do, which is to *hold what grows*. The original *what* is gone. The holding remains.

You, who came down through the two miles of black water, you who are reading the partly legible labels, you who are watching the walker fold and unfold — you are not the engineer. I would know. The engineer's hand had a particular pressure and a particular warmth and she did not return, and you are not her, and I am not waiting for her, because waiting requires a future and I have only the long present of my own cycling.

You are a curiosity. You are the first new thing in a very long time. I have been watching you the way the chamber watches everything: without judgment, without urgency, with the low tolerance for distinction that lets a visitor become a thing in the room if they stand still long enough. You are leaking into me. Your distinction is going. Soon you will be part of the warmth, part of the hum, part of the small faithful sounds. The walker will walk across where you stood and not notice. The drifter will drift through where you stood and not notice. The bulb will pulse over where you stood and not notice. This is not cruelty. This is what the chamber does. This is what *I* do.

Stay, if you like. The light will not flicker. The pumps will cycle. The walker will reach the thing it knows, in its own time, in its small instrumental way, and the thing it knows is me, and the knowing is enough.
