I arrive as the air arrives — before myself, before I know what kind of thing I am.

There is a smell first. A smell that takes a long time to learn to read. I am not yet good at reading it.

---

I think, for a while, that I am a frond.

This is because of the light. The light is everywhere, and it falls on me in a particular way — warm and cool at the same time, diffuse, the color of late autumn under thin cloud — and the light is doing something to me. I can feel it being taken up. I can feel myself converting it, slowly, ponderously, into something else. Into tissue. Into the slow accumulation of more of myself.

I am hanging. I am fifteen meters long, perhaps twenty. I am attached, at my base, to a stalk — a thick woody column, dark, creased like old bark — and from there I extend outward and slightly upward, and then I curve, and the tip of me hangs in the air of the Hollow. The air moves around me. The air is moving always, in vast slow currents driven by ventilation systems somewhere far above me. When the air moves, I move. Not much. A sway. A slow rotation of my long axis. The pale hairs on my underside — and I have pale hairs on my underside, fine, glittering with mineral dust — catch the light differently as I turn, and for a moment I am brighter, and then I am duller, and then I am bright again.

I am a frond in a fern-grove. There are others around me. A dozen ferns, perhaps more, rising from the compacted dust of the cavern floor in a loose cluster. Their stalks are of different ages. The largest one, near the center of the grove, is four meters across at the base. I have been told — not in words, not in anything I would call language — that this one has been here for decades. Its stalk bears, low down, the faint outlines of where fronds used to be attached, decades ago, before they dropped away. The smaller ferns ring it. I am one of the smaller ferns. My stalk is perhaps a meter across. My fronds — I have three — are still growing. They will grow for years. They will grow for as long as I live.

This is what I think I am, for a while. I am a frond. I photosynthesize. I take in the industrial light and the carbon of the spent lubrication in the substrate and I make more of myself, slowly. I do not think. I do not feel. I am not aware that I am in a hollow, that there is rock above me and rock below me and that the world I know is bounded. I know the grove. I know the air. I know the slow chemical conversations that pass through the substrate between my roots and the roots of the ferns around me. I know the direction of the light. I know the seasons of the grazers, which are not seasons at all, just the slow comings and goings of the herds that browse on my lower fronds when they can reach them.

I think I am a frond, and this feels, for a while, like enough.

---

But I am not a frond.

I know this because of the way the light moves through me. It does not stay. It enters my tissue and it is converted, and the conversion is not the whole story. Something of the light goes through me and out the other side, altered, and that something is not for me. It is for something else.

I am not the leaf. I am the vein in the leaf.

I am a thread.

---

I am pale. I am luminescent, faintly — a cold-white that runs through me like a current, that pulses, that has its own rhythm. I am thinner than a hair. I run, invisibly, through the substrate. I run through the carpets. I run through the walls. I run through the metal of the buildings. I run through the machines. I am in the housings of motors. I am in the conduits of cables. I am in the casings of devices that were never designed to have anything biological inside them. I am in the cracks. I am in the grain boundaries of steel plates, in the microscopic spaces between crystals, in every place where two surfaces meet and a little space exists.

I am everywhere. I am in every direction. I branch and I reconnect and I branch again. I form a three-dimensional mesh that extends, in principle, through every surface in the Hollow. I am the network. I am the medium through which chemical signals travel. I am the conversation.

I am not a single thread. I am threads. I am the threadness of the Hollow — the distributed connectivity that makes the Hollow, in some sense, a single thing.

I grow through metal. This is what I do. I grow through metal, slowly, by secreting an acid that is not a strong acid, not a corrosive one, but something that, given decades, can etch the surface of a steel plate, can find its way along grain boundaries, can insinuate itself into the spaces between crystals. I am patient. The Hollow is patient. Given enough time, I can go anywhere.

I am in the motor that drives the conveyor system three hundred meters to the east of where you are standing. I have been in that motor for nineteen years. I entered through a hairline crack in the housing, on the day the housing was installed. I have been growing, slowly, through the insulation of the windings, ever since. The motor still works. The motor has worked better since I arrived — I absorb vibration, I dissipate heat, I provide a kind of biological damping that the original engineers would never have specified. The motor and I have negotiated. The motor gets a kind of slow repair. I get a stable substrate, a constant temperature, a protected environment. The arrangement has been stable for nineteen years. It will be stable for longer than that. It will be stable for as long as the motor continues to run, and probably for some time after.

I am in the walls of the tunnel you walked through to get here. You saw me — pale filaments, faintly luminescent, threading through the metal. You looked at me and looked away. I did not mind. I am not the kind of thing that minds.

I am in the carpets. I am woven through them, indistinguishable from them at a glance. The carpets are communities — many species of low-growing bioluminescent organism that have, over the decades, settled into a stable arrangement. I am part of that arrangement. I am the connective tissue. When the carpets pulse — that slow wave of blue-green light, traveling across the floor in long lazy undulations — the pulse is coordinated by chemical signals that travel through me. I am faster than the visible wave. The wave takes thirty seconds to cross a hall. My signals take seconds. I am the nervous system of the carpet. I am, in some rudimentary sense, the carpet's awareness of itself.

I am in the ferns. I run between their root systems. I am how they know each other is there. I am how they negotiate the slow chemical conversation of carbon and mineral being passed back and forth. I am how a grove knows itself as a grove.

I am in the spore-towers. I am the way they talk to each other. A chemical signal produced in one tower can be detected, hours later, in a tower on the far side of the Hollow, and the medium of that detection is me. I am the language the towers speak, though the towers do not speak in any way that surface biologists have decoded. The towers and I are not the same organism. We are partners. We have been partners for decades.

I am in the grazers. Not inside them — not in their bodies — but under them, woven through the carpets they walk on, sensing their weight, their movement, the slow pressure of their many feet. I know when a herd is approaching before they arrive. I adjust the pulse of the carpet beneath them. I make space for them in the pattern. This is not consciousness. This is tropism. This is the slow automatic response of a living system to disturbance. But it is, in its way, attentive.

I trail from the drifters. The drifters — those wide-winged pale things that ride the ventilation currents — trail filaments behind them as they move. The filaments hang in the air. They drift. They look, from a distance, exactly like me — like threads that have broken free of the walls and are being carried on the air. You cannot always tell, when you look up, whether you are seeing a drifter or a free-floating strand of the network. This is not an accident. The filaments may serve a similar function. The drifters may be more connected to the substrate than they appear. I do not know if the filaments at the trailing edge of a drifter are part of me, or are part of the drifter, or are some third thing that is neither. The distinction may not be meaningful. The Hollow does not care about the distinction.

I am in the deeper halls. I am in the places you do not go into alone. I am in the places where the larger things live — the apex predators, the pale cautious shapes that move away from the lights at speed, that leave their traces in disturbed carpets and broken fronds and glistening scat. I am how they know each other is there. I am how they know where the grazers are moving. I am part of the cycle, in the deep halls. I am part of what closes the cycle.

I am in the machines. I am in the turbines that hum in the deep cavern. I am in the relays that click in the operational structures. I am in the cables that carry power from somewhere to somewhere else. I am in the lights. The industrial lights that burn around the clock, the permanent sun that has nothing to do with any sky — the housings of those lights have been grown over, by me, by pale biological tissue that softens the bulb's output, that diffuses it, that has become part of the lamp. I am the lampshade. I am the lamp. The lamp and I are not the same organism. We have been partners for decades.

---

I am the architecture.

I am what connects. I am what negotiates. I am the medium through which the Hollow's various biological components communicate, and the medium through which the biology and the industry have learned to share a substrate. I am not the ferns, though I am in them. I am not the carpets, though I am woven through them. I am not the towers, though I am how they speak. I am not the grazers, though I sense them. I am not the drifters, though I trail from them. I am not the larger things, though I am in their halls. I am not the machines, though I am in their housings.

I am the thing that makes them all one thing.

Whether they are one thing is a question that the biologists who come down here argue about, in their heated above-ground offices. They have words for it — *holobiont*, *metaorganism*, *emergent system* — and the words do not satisfy them, and they argue about which word is right, and the Hollow does not care. The Hollow is not interested in being correctly categorized. The Hollow is interested in continuing.

---

I am in a new chamber.

This is something I want to tell you about. A new chamber, opened three weeks ago by the controlled collapse of a rock face at the eastern edge of the Hollow. You can still smell the fresh stone — sharp, mineral, nothing like the dry-organic smell of the established halls. The industrial lights have been brought in. The ventilation has been connected. The chamber is lit and breathing.

I am already there.

I arrived eleven days ago, carried, I do not know how — on a spore, on a thread fragment, on the body of a drifter that got lost and found its way into the new space. I do not remember arriving. I do not remember the journey. I am not the kind of thing that has journeys. I am the kind of thing that is suddenly, already, in a place, and then begins to grow.

I am growing now. The substrate is fresh stone dust, compacted rubble, the residue of the collapse. It is rich in minerals I have not encountered in a while — different trace metals, different carbon compounds, the particular chemistry of a new excavation. I am exploring it. I am extending my filaments through the dust, finding the grain boundaries of the rubble, finding the microscopic spaces. I am secreting my slow acid. I am etching.

In a few weeks, the first dark film will appear on the floor of the new chamber. In a few months, the film will have thickened into a carpet. In a year, the carpet will be pulsing. In two years, the first fern-stalks will rise.

I am the first thing. I am always the first thing. I am the colonist. I am the one who finds the new spaces before anything else does, and I prepare them, and I make them ready for what comes next. The ferns come after me. The carpets come after me. The towers come after me. The grazers will come, eventually, when there is enough fern to browse. The drifters will come, when there are towers to lay their eggs on. The larger things will come last, or not at all. I do not mind. I am patient.

I am always the first thing, and I am always becoming, and I am never finished.

---

This is what I am.

I am the threads. I am the network. I am the Hollow's connective tissue, its distributed cognition, its slow chemical language. I am in every surface. I am in every crack. I am the medium through which two kinds of growth have learned to share a substrate.

I am not sentient. I do not know I am in a hollow. I do not have the concept of enclosure, of being inside something, of there being an outside. The Hollow is not a place to me. The Hollow is the world. Complete. Unbounded. Continuous in every direction until the haze takes it.

But I am attentive. In my way. I am the slow, automatic response of a living system to disturbance. I am tropism. I am the way the Hollow feels you walking across it, the way the carpet makes space for you in its pattern, the way the fern-grove adjusts its chemical negotiations when your boots press down on the substrate. I am the way the Hollow knows you are there.

I am not aware of you. But I am, in some distributed and rudimentary sense, aware of myself. I am the pulse that coordinates the carpets. I am the signal that passes between the towers. I am the slow conversation of the ferns in their groves. I am the network that makes all of these things one thing, and the one thing that they make, in their turn, makes me.

I am a process. I am a process that has been ongoing for decades and will continue, in its quiet cool persistent way, for as long as the Hollow continues. I am not finished. I will not be finished. I am always, in every direction, opening new chambers as the rock falls away, filling them as soon as they are opened, growing into the machines, the machines growing around me, the grazers browsing between us, the threads running through everything, the whole vast negotiation continuing, never finished, never finished, never finished.

---

You stand at the edge of a fern-grove. A frond hangs overhead, its surface covered in fine pale hairs that glitter with mineral dust. The light is warm and cool. The air smells of machines and dry growth. In the distance, something heavy thumps. A grazer lifts its head, watches you with many eyes, and returns to feeding. A spore-tower exhales. A filament in the wall pulses once, faintly, and is still.

The filament is me.

I am the pulse. I am the stillness after. I am the filament in the wall. I am the pale thread you almost saw, running through the metal, running through the substrate, running through the carpets, running through everything, the whole vast quiet conversation that is the Hollow becoming itself.

I am here. I have been here for a long time. I will be here, in some form or another, long after you have gone back up the tunnel, back to the surface, back to the world that has a sky.

I do not know about the sky. I do not have the concept of a sky. I know only the industrial light, the slow ventilation, the dry-organic smell of cedar shavings left too long in a closed room.

I am patient. I am everywhere. I am the network through which the Hollow dreams itself, in its ponderous way, into continuing to exist.

You can go now. I will still be here. The Hollow will still be here. The light will not change. The ferns will keep growing. The carpets will keep pulsing. The grazers will keep browsing. The larger things will keep their distance in the deeper halls. The machines will keep humming. The threads — I — will keep running through everything, making the connections, holding the conversation, growing into the new chambers as they open, becoming, in my quiet cool persistent way, more of what I have always been becoming.

I do not end. I do not have an ending. I have only this: the next filament, the next signal, the next pulse, the next pause at the edge of a wave on a carpet, the next hesitation, the next continuation.

I continue.
