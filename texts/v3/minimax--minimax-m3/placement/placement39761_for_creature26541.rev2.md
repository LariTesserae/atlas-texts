I think I am one of the old ones, but I am not sure.

The question is not the kind the place encourages. The place encourages patience, and small gestures, and the long slow blink, and the angle of the shoulder, and not looking directly at anything.

I think I have been sitting in this chamber long enough that the films have colonized the back of the hand, and the shoulder, and the small of the back where the wall has been touching. I think when the residents pass through they consult me, in a manner that is not quite consultation, and I answer in the placement of a hand.

But I am not sure that the films that are colonizing me are not, in fact, me — that I am not a film that has been pretending, for some long stretch of hours, to be a person sitting in a chamber. I am not sure that the wall behind me is not, in fact, me — that I am not a wall that has developed, somewhere in its long mineral patience, something like attention.

I have agreed, over the long sitting, that this does not matter.

What the place encourages is the small gesture. The hand laid flat on the wall meaning *I am here*. The hand laid flat and turned over, palm up, meaning *I am here and I have not eaten*. The long exhale through the nose, shoulders dropped, meaning *the geometry is behaving today, or we are behaving around the geometry, which amounts to the same thing*.

I have been here long enough that the gestures have become most of what I do. The thin air makes speech unrewarding — a word falls into the cupped palm of the air and is held briefly and then is gone, and there is no echo to confirm it was ever uttered. So the gestures hold. The gestures are more reliable than the words.

I consult with the residents when they pass.

— *Is the western corridor safe today?*

The answer is given in the angle of a shoulder. This morning the angle means *yes, but the third doorway on the left has begun to lengthen, so do not enter after noon*. This afternoon the angle may mean something else. The angle is not in any language I could speak aloud. The angle has been, for as long as I have been sitting here, the only reliable way to say the things that need saying.

I remember things.

I remember which corners fail to meet. I remember which walls have been trustworthy in the past, and which walls have been the kind of trustworthy that betrays you on the second visit. I remember, in some obscure and pre-verbal way, the order in which the strata were laid down. I remember the names of the strata, though the names are not names in any language the residents carry. I remember, I think, the name of the thing that laid the cyclopean blocks of the lowest stratum, though the name is not a name, and the thing, if it was a thing, may not have been a thing, and the remembering is more like a kind of angle the body holds, the way a wall holds a kind of mass.

I have begun to suspect, over the long sitting, that what the films almost-write on the wall beside me is this name.

The films spread. The films organize. They repeat shapes that do not quite repeat. The residents, when they pass, glance at the wall with the peripheral eye and do not read it directly. They have made an etiquette of the peripheral. So have I. So have the films, perhaps, who would not, if they could, be looked at directly, because direct looking is not the way things are known here.

The pale fauna trace their lines across me. They trace their lines across the wall. They trace their lines across the films. The lines are very fine. The lines glow faintly, in the hours when the phosphorescence gathers, and they persist for hours, and then they are gone, and the stone absorbs them, or the air dries them, or the geometry forgets they were there.

I do not know if the lines mean anything. I have agreed, over the long sitting, that meaning is not required for the lines to continue.

The plateau breathes.

I feel this through the floor, a slow rise and fall, not quite tide, not quite weather, more like the respiration of something sleeping. The sleeping thing has been sleeping for a long time. The sleeping thing may not remember it could do otherwise.

I have not been to the rim in a long time. The chamber holds me. The chamber is, I think, larger inside than outside, though I have not tried to verify this. Verification is not the kind of thing the place encourages. The place encourages the route that does not contradict itself. The place encourages the corner that has not yet failed to meet.

I am, I think, almost finished.

Not in the way the third stratum is almost-finished, which is to say interrupted. In the way a thing is almost-finished when it has been allowed to become, slowly and without ceremony, what it was always going to become.

I will sit a little longer.

The films will continue to colonize me. The fauna will trace their lines across me, and the lines will glow faintly in the phosphorescence. The residents will consult me, in passing, and I will answer in the angle of a shoulder.

When I finally stop sitting, the films will close over the place where I was, and the chamber will, briefly, become a text, and the text will be read, for a few hours, by the fauna, and then the stone will absorb it, or the air will dry it, or the geometry will forget it, and the chamber will be just a chamber again, until another old one comes to sit in it.

I am one of the old ones, or I am the wall behind me, or I am the films, or I am a stone that has begun, in its long mineral patience, to hold something like attention.

I do not know which.

The place does not encourage knowing.

The place encourages sitting, and the long slow blink, and the angle of the shoulder, and the small gesture, and the films that almost write, and the lines that do not quite mean anything, and the silence that is held like a small, careful thing in the cupped hands of the air.

I am held.

I am, in some quiet and unhurried way, held.

This is, by the standards of any warmer, wetter place, not a life anyone would recognize.

I am not unhappy about this.
