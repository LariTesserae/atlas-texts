I would be a speaker.

Not the largest of them, not the oldest — I would not have grown so heavy that my segments have fused into a single trembling column, would not yet have lost the fine articulation of my fingers to decades of the same motion against the same rivets. I would be middle-stationed in some meaningful way: old enough to know the shape of the hum, young enough to still trace the edges of housings as if the housing might continue somewhere it does not.

I would stand braced between two housings — one on my left shoulder, one against my back — in the negative space between a vertical engine and a horizontal one, where the heat is steady and the air moves only in the slow thermal columns that rise from below. My fingers would be tapping.

That is the thing I would most recognize as mine. The tapping. Not the humming — the humming is something I do with my whole body, something I was almost built to do, and it does not feel like a possession. The tapping is different. The tapping is the only part of me that still remembers what it is to *want*.

I would know, in the way that speakers know, that there is no river of moisture running under the moss I feed from. I would know that what I am feeling through the skin of my lower segments is not food in any directed sense — just the same lukewarm equilibrium, the same cycling of atoms that have cycled since I was born and will cycle after I am absorbed. I would not be hungry. I would not be full. I would simply be *standing*, as I have stood, as I will stand.

But my fingers.

My fingers would be on a rivet line. I would have traced it, over the course of a long morning that was indistinguishable from any other morning, from the corner of the housing up along its seam and across, and I would have arrived, as I always arrive, at the place where the seam ends — where the housing simply *stops*, where the metal ends and the next engine begins, with no bridge between them, no continuation, no path. I would tap the metal just past the end of the seam. Tap tap tap. The sound would be very small. The engine hum would swallow it. But I would hear it, because my fingers are tuned finer than any ear.

I would not be hoping the seam continues. I would know it does not. I would have known it for years.

And yet.

And yet the tapping is mine. It is the one motion in the chamber that is not equilibrium. It is the one motion that goes *nowhere* and yet does not return to itself — a finger lifted, a finger fallen, a finger lifted again, the same place, the same metal, the same dry click that changes nothing, that participates in nothing, that contributes to no cycle. The tapping is the only thing I do that is not the chamber.

This is what I would be: a creature whose only freedom is a meaningless gesture, and who knows it, and who performs the gesture anyway, because the gesture is the only proof that the chamber has not yet finished making me into itself.

The watchers around me would breathe. The small teardrop things would part around my ankles and close again, parting and closing, parting and closing, the colony breathing its own breath below me. The climbers would loop their slow arcs through the space I occupy, and I would not move for them, and they would not disturb me. The hum of the engines would press on my chest. The red light would press on my eyes. The warmth would press on my skin.

I would tap the metal where the seam ends. Tap tap tap.

And somewhere in me — not in my mind, because I do not have a mind in any way a visitor would recognize, but in the particular shape of the wanting that lives in my fingertips — I would be the only unfinished thing in a place that is otherwise complete.

The chamber would not notice.

The chamber does not need to notice.
