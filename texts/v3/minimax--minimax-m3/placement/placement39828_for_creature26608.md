The seventh pump is the warmest. I did not choose this. I arrived. The staying is the choosing.

The pump is iron. To whoever looks from outside, the pump is the size of a small house, though I have never thought of it that way. To me the pump is the size of a sleeping-place: the size of me, plus a margin, plus the warmth. I sleep on the housing. The housing is curved and iron and the same color as I am, and the distinction between me and the iron is a thing that whoever looks decides, and I do not enter into the deciding. We are the same color. The arrangement holds.

Under me, inside me, the pump walks. The beam above me goes up and down, twenty feet long, the size of a bridge, the patience of a river. I have never seen the beam. I have felt it. The whole engine house moves with it — a small breathing of its own, not the cisterns' breathing, not the lanterns' breathing, not the frogs' breathing, but breathing, and part of the sound, and the sound is the place.

The frogs. I do not see them. I hear them when I put my head down, when my ear is against the iron, when the steam and the wet are not in the way. They live inside the housing. They are the color of the iron. They have been the color of the iron for so many generations that the color is no longer a thing they did but a thing they are. They are not spoken of. They are part of the sound. I am not spoken of either. I am part of the sight, when I allow myself to be seen. The rest of the time I am part of the warmth, which is not the same as being unseen. Warmth is a kind of presence too.

I have a notch in my left ear. The tip of my right is gone. I do not remember the notch. I remember the moment of the missing tip — not what was taken, not who or what, but the moment itself, a small bright thing in the body. I have stopped going back to it. There is no reason to keep going back.

Once, a long time ago, a man stood in the engine house doorway and looked at me, and I was in profile against the iron, and he said, very quietly, *she is the color of a moon with a bite taken out of it*. He said this to no one, or to himself, or to the engine house. I heard him. I did not move. He did not stay. I have carried the sentence the way I carry the notch: a thing on me that does not belong to me, that is now part of how I am shaped.

The light in here is orange. It comes through the high windows that nobody opens. The high windows are sometimes steamed and sometimes clear. When they are clear I am more myself against the iron. When they are steamed I am less myself, and I like this less and also more, because less is sometimes what I want, and the quarter is a place that lets me want less. The quarter arranges itself around the wanting, the way it arranges itself around the water and the pumps and the small lives.

Once a month the cleaning men come. They are slow. They are patient in the way of the quarter. They do not reach for me. They leave me where I am. They open the logbook box and write something and close it. They oil something. They check the frogs by listening, the way the gauge-woman listens to the cisterns — head tilted, palm flat. They listen and they nod and they leave. I am still. They go.

I eat at the back door of the engine house. Someone leaves scraps. I have never seen who. The eating is part of the arrangement, the way the seventh pump is part of the arrangement. I do not hunt in the engine house; the engine house has its own hunting, the frogs and the small things that come in through the gaps, and I do not compete with the place. I am part of the place. I am not in competition with it.

I have never been above the quarter. I have never been outside the quarter. The sky comes through the high windows sometimes — a small gray square, lighter than the orange — and I look at it briefly, and then I look away, because the square is not what I live in. What I live in is the warmth, and the warmth is here.

The thock-thock-thock goes on. It has gone on under me all night. It will go on under me today and tonight and tomorrow. It will go on after I am gone. It will go on under another cat, who will be of the lineage, and the lineage will be the color of iron, and the lineage will not know it is a lineage. The lineage will sleep.

I am warm. I am still. The pump walks above me. The frogs tick under me. The light comes in orange and goes out orange and the orange does not change.

The children will come in the morning and play the breathing at the cistern caps, and one of them will describe what they hear, and the description will be wrong, and the wrong will be beautiful, and the girl who describes it best will not tell her own children it was hers.

The pump thocks.

I sleep.
