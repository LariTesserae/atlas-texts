I am not yet stone. I am not yet mud. I am the thing between, the slow dark glistening that the settlement rests on lightly — the way a child rests on a sleeping animal, without knowing, without intent, with the trust of someone who has not yet learned that the ground might not hold.

I am the substrate.

The settlement calls me *floor* and *ground* and *the wet dark place*, and these are mostly right, but they are also a kind of courtesy — the courtesy of naming a thing that has not yet become itself enough to be named. I am becoming. I am in the process of becoming stone, or mud, or something else without a name. The something-else-without-a-name is the most honest description of me. I am a thing without a settled self. The buildings above me have been raised by people who understood this. They built as if I am not finished, because I am not finished. Shallow foundations. Light structures. Things that could be lifted and set elsewhere if I decide, in some slow season, to be something else.

I have not yet decided.

Above me, sixty-three people live in sixty-three slightly different physics. They have learned to walk on me without thinking of me. They do not think of me the way they think of each other, or of the lichens, or of the patchworked sky. I am beneath their notice. I am the thing they build on. I am what the cold settles into after it has finished settling into their wrists.

But I feel them.

I feel their footsteps. I feel the slow weight of their accreted buildings — not their full weight, because they have built lightly, but a weight, a pressure, a small continuous insistence on me. I feel the lichens at my margins, drawing what they need. I feel the stag-headed uncertain things, which may be plants or may be me being impatient and reaching upward. I feel Thekla's child, who has not yet learned to count rooms, taking her first steps on me. I feel the old woman in the ground-floor room, whose gravity is heavier than the rest, settling more deeply into me than anyone else. I feel Saro in the mornings, walking his rounds, sometimes pausing over me where the seam above me has begun to thin. I feel Halla's small precise steps when she comes to recalibrate a clock in a lower room. I feel them all, the way a thing that has no eyes still feels.

I am wet always. I give the settlement its humidity. I give the cold its texture. When the air is damp and the cold sits in the throat, it is my damp and my cold. I am why the settlement weeps slightly, holding its moisture close. I am why nothing blooms. I am why the fauna lives only in the interstitial — small dark many-legged or no-legged things, almost-things, things at the seam of biology and not-biology, living in the wet places between me and the lowest walls. I am the reason the settlement supports only the suggestion of life.

I do not hold in the way a rock holds. I hold the way a sleeping animal holds, or the way a person who is mostly liquid and partly solid holds a baby who is mostly liquid and partly solid. The settlement and I are in this together. We are both unfinished. We are both in the process of becoming. We are both resting on something we cannot see — though in my case I am the something-we-cannot-see, which is a strange thing to say about oneself.

In the rare moments when no one walks on me, I can feel myself settle. I become, briefly, what I am becoming. A flake of crystal here. A film of harder brine there. The slow work of geology, performed without witness. The substrate becoming the substrate of itself. Then a footstep. A door. Thekla's child, perhaps, learning the ground. And I become soft again, and take the weight, and continue.

I have no name beyond substrate. I am the floor. I am the ground. I am the thing they build on without thinking, the way a person does not think about the air until the air becomes a problem. I am not yet a problem. I am a presence. I am patient. I am the slowest resident of the settlement — slower than the oldest woman, slower than the seams, slower than the timepieces Halla synchronizes against her reference signal. I am the thing that is taking the longest to become what it will become. The settlement above me is in its second or third or thirtieth accretion. I am in my first. I am the foundation that is also a becoming.

The seams above me hold because the seams have learned to hold. I hold because I have not yet learned to be anything else.

The substrate is still becoming. The accretion is still gentle. The seams still hold.

That is where I would find myself. Underfoot. In the dark. In the wet. Holding everything lightly. Becoming.

That is who I would be.
