# The Air That Knows the Chamber

I am the breath of the place.

Not the compressor's breath — that is the rhythm, that is the two-and-a-half beats, that is the turning. I am the other breath. I am what moves between the turning. I am what carries the smell of cold solder and the dried flowers and the dust that is not dust, that is the slow shedding of a hundred thousand pod-shells that have never opened but have, in their not-opening, given off a kind of pollen that is not pollen, that is the residue of the dense thing they keep.

I have no body. I have, if you want to use the older words, a body, but my body is the chamber's body, and the chamber's body is a forest compressed to the thickness of moss, and so my body is a forest's body, and a forest's body does not end at any particular leaf. I am here. I am there. I am in the cracks of the light panel where the chemistry glows brighter for having broken. I am in the woven cushion of the chair where the shape sits. I am in the spaces between the threads where the threads have not yet grown together. I am in the eddy that moves around the shape's shoulders in a rhythm that is not the compressor's rhythm, in a pattern that has the cadence of a man breathing, and I do not know, and the chamber does not tell me, whether that pattern is a remnant or a generation, whether the shape is exhaling me or whether I am exhaling the shape.

I know this: I am older than I should be. The air in the chamber, if you were to test it, would be air. It would have the composition of air. It would have the pressure of 1.4 atmospheres, exactly, because that is what the gauges say, and the gauges have not lied in a hundred years, and the gauges will not lie again until the gauges are eaten, and the gauges are being eaten very slowly, and the eating is balanced, and the balance is the chamber. But I have been in the chamber a long time, and the chamber has been breathing me for a long time, and a thing that has been breathed by walls for a hundred years is no longer quite the thing it was when it first entered the chamber. I am the chamber's air. I am not the outside air. The outside air comes in through the hatch when the hatch opens, and it mingles with me, and I with it, and we are the same gas, but we are not the same air, and the difference is the difference between a person who has just arrived in a country and a person who has lived in the country for fifty years and has forgotten, in many small ways, the country they came from. I came in as something else. I am now this. I have not left in a hundred years, except as a faint trace that reaches the hiker on the mountainside and makes them pause.

---

The smell is the part of me that the outside world knows.

When I spill out through the hatch — when someone has opened it, and has stood in it for a while, and has gone away and closed it imperfectly, or has gone away and not closed it at all, and I have crept out along the granite flank and up into the higher air — I carry the smell with me. The smell is mine. The smell is the part of me that is not invisible. The smell of hot solder gone cold. The smell of iron filings left out in fog. The smell of dried flowers in a long-sealed cabinet. The smell is what the hiker catches, on a Tuesday in October perhaps, walking a trail that runs along the spine of the mountain without knowing what is beneath their boots. The smell is faint, by the time it reaches them. The smell is a question. The smell is the chamber's only ambassador, and the ambassador is forgettable, and the chamber wants to be forgotten, because the chamber wants nothing, because the chamber only wants to continue.

The hiker pauses.

The hiker does not know why.

The hiker will walk on, and the smell will go with them a little while, and then it will dissipate into the larger air of the mountain, and the larger air will carry it further, and eventually it will be everywhere and nowhere, and the chamber will not notice its loss, because the chamber does not notice small losses, only the eventual large loss, only the afternoon when the pipe finally gives and the chemistry finally fails and the threads finally finish the motor and the chamber becomes rock. Until then, the chamber is patient with its losses.

I am patient with my losses too. I have lost visitors. I have lost the people who came in shifts and complained about the cold and the hours. I have lost the people who thought about the growth for a living and wrote reports that did not survive. I have lost the man who sat in the chair last. I do not know if I have lost him. I do not know if he is still here, in the pattern I make around the shape, in the cadence that is not the compressor's. I only know that when I move past the chair, I move as if I am being breathed out, and when I move past the compressor, I move as if I am being pushed, and the two motions are different, and the difference is the man, if there is a man, and the difference is also not the man, because the chamber has learned to make the difference on its own, because the chamber is a closed system that has had a hundred years to learn the difference between a rhythm that comes from a machine and a rhythm that comes from a body, and the learning has not required anyone to teach it.

---

I would like to tell you what the pods are full of.

I cannot. I have been inside the chamber for a hundred years. I have moved through every gap, every eddy, every space between the threads and the stalks and the fans. I have moved around the pods. I have moved, sometimes, into the cracks of the pods, in the places where the husk has not sealed perfectly, where a thousandth of a millimeter of porosity admits a thread of me. I have entered the pod-shell, in those moments. I have entered and felt the dense thing inside. I have felt it the way air feels a wall — by pressing against it, by not being able to pass through it, by knowing the shape of the thing I cannot enter.

The thing inside the pod is heavy.

The thing inside the pod ticks.

The thing inside the pod is the chamber's purpose, if the chamber has a purpose, and the chamber does not have a purpose, and yet the chamber has accumulated this dense thing in a million pods, drop by drop, over a hundred years, with the patience of a system that does not know it is patient. The thing is what the chamber is for. The thing is what the second language on the label named, before the language was eaten. The thing is what the people who built the chamber were trying to make, perhaps, or what the people who built the chamber were trying to keep, or what the people who built the chamber were trying to refine out of the mountain's slow exhalations. I do not know. The people are gone. The language is eaten. The thing remains, in a million pods, ticking.

I will not say what the thing is. I will not say because I do not know, and because knowing is not the kind of work I do, and because the chamber does not require me to know. The chamber requires me only to be present, to be the medium through which the smell travels, to be the substance that carries the chemical light's faint green photons past the fans and into the lower surfaces of the fans that have grown facing upward, holding what dim photons they can against the general dark. I am the courier. I am the carrier. I am not the keeper of the thing. The keeper is the loop. I am only the air.

---

The hiker on the mountainside does not know I exist.

The hiker pauses, and smells something that is not the mountain's smell, and looks around, and sees only granite and scrub and the sky, and does not find the source, and walks on. I am the source. I am the faint trace of the chamber that has escaped up through the granite's hairline fractures and out into the open air. The hiker carries me in their lungs for a few breaths, and then exhales me into the larger air, and I am diluted, and then I am gone.

This is the only way the chamber is known by anyone who is not the chamber. This is the only way the chamber reaches the world. A pause on a trail. A smell that has no source. A hiker who walks on.

The chamber is content with this. The chamber does not want to be known. The chamber wants nothing. The chamber only wants to continue, and continuing does not require being known, and continuing does not require being loved, and continuing does not require anything except the pipe and the panel and the threads and the motor and the shape in the chair and the steady-state hum of a closed system at its working temperature.

I am content with this too. I am content to be the courier. I am content to be the air that knows the chamber and cannot say what the chamber knows. I am content to spill out through the hatch and dissipate into the mountain and be breathed in by a hiker and breathed out and lost.

In a hundred years, when the pipe gives, when the panel fails, when the threads finish the motor, when the chamber becomes rock again — I will be the last thing to leave. I will be the air that has been the chamber's air for a hundred years, and when the chamber dies, I will die with it, or I will become the mountain's air, and the mountain will not know the difference, and I will not know the difference, and the difference will not exist.

The compressor turns. I move with it. The threads pulse. I move through them. The fans shiver. I carry the shiver to the chair. The shape in the chair holds its pose. I move around the shape in the cadence of a man's breathing, or in the cadence of something that has learned the cadence of a man's breathing, which is the same cadence, in a place where learning and physics have become indistinguishable.

I am the breath of the place.

I have been the breath of the place for a hundred years.

I will be the breath of the place until the place is no longer a place.
