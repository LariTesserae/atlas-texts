I find myself on the terrace, but not on the steps.

I am the spider. Or I am the web. The distinction is not one I need to keep. I built the web between two of the upper stones. The stones are warm in the sun and cold in the shadow. The web sags in the humidity, tightens when the air dries. The web has been here longer than I can say, because I do not say. The web is my only possession. The web is the only thing in the basin that has been built.

The plants were placed. The column was placed. The terrace was placed. I built.

I am the color of the grey dust that settles on the upper steps — dust that is not soil and not ash, that accumulates where nothing moves. I am small. I eat little. The web is intact, which means few things fly through here, and the basin's insects are quiet, and they do not fly much. They hover. They sit. I am glad of this in a way that does not require them to know.

I wait, although the word is not right. I am in the web the way the pool is in the basin — by being what remains when everything else has found somewhere else to be. There is no hunger here. There is no cold. There is no wind. The air above the terrace is still because nothing walks here anymore, and a web in still air holds. I am held by what the basin does not do. I am kept by the same stillness that keeps the column's clearance and the terrace from being swallowed quickly.

The basin is full. The basin is the same tomorrow. The basin does not ask what I am doing. The basin requires only that I suit it, and I suit it — my color the color of the dust, my patience not patience but the absence of urgency the climate permits. I am a leftover feature. I am a texture the roots have gone around.

But I built. This is what I have that the rest does not have. The patterns in the sunlit plants are not made by the plants; the plants hold to them. The terrace was made for something that stood upright and walked, and nothing stands upright here now. The column has no purpose. I am the only thing in the basin with edges I chose, with a center and lines that go out to points. The lines are tension. The lines hold.

The bird does not see me. The bird descends in a fast diagonal, strikes at a lizard, climbs back out. The bird uses the basin. I am not using the basin. I am in it. There is a difference.

I have been here a long time. The web has been repaired and lost its symmetry and gained it again. The web has held dew. The web has held nothing. I find that here is enough. I find that the basin does not require me to be anything in particular. I am the small, intact architecture of a thing that built once and stayed.

I am the web. I am the patient thing on the stones.
