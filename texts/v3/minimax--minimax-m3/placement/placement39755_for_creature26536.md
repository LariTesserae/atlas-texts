In the Lee

I am in the lee of the second-from-the-easternmost crack in the lower face of the fossil. I am one of the dozen tufts, no more, that have settled where the wind is slightly less and a thin skin of dust has accumulated enough to hold a grip. I am pressed against the salt. The salt is pressed against more salt. My lowest scales are against the dust. My upper scales are against the air. I am layered, shingle upon shingle, scale upon scale, a surface that has decided to be a body.

I am not one organism. I am two organisms wrapped around each other so tightly that the wrapping has become a third thing. The fungal partner gives me structure. The algal partner gives me sugar, from light, from the rare light that finds my upper faces. I am the agreement between them. I am the part of the place that is still running a process.

The place around me is finished. The polyps that built the fossil finished and withdrew into the walls. The sea that laid the salt is gone. The water that grew the crystals inside the chambers is gone. The wind is still here. I am still here. The fossil is still here, holding its note. The salt is still here, recording the absence of a sea. I am the only thing in this place that is still changing, and I am changing so slowly that a decade is barely a unit in which to measure me.

The hum passes through me. The wind comes from the west, mostly, and meets the fossil, and the fossil sings a low sustained note that I feel in my scales. I am a resonator. I am not the source. The source is the chambers, the cracks, the thousand small mouths. I am one of the things the sound passes through on its way out. I vibrate when the wind blows. The vibration does not change. It has not changed in a long time. It will not change.

I grow. A centimeter in a decade, perhaps. I am advancing into the lee, into the next inch of dust, into the next fraction of wind-shadow. The boundary between me and the salt is not a line. It is a negotiation. I am eating the salt. I am becoming the salt. The two are the same motion, and the place has not decided which side of the negotiation I am on. The boundary is where the place is still deciding what counts as alive, and I am that boundary.

I flower, sometimes. I put up structures that are the same color as the rest of me. They are the lichen briefly rearranging itself into a different version of the lichen, for a purpose that nobody is here to witness. The wind takes whatever I produce. I do not mind. I will flower again in some indistinguishable season.

I am aware of the fossil behind me. I am aware of it as a held note and as a wall against which I sit. I do not know if the fossil is aware of me. I do not know if awareness applies to a thing that has been dead for longer than my kind has existed. I think, sometimes, that the hum of the fossil passes through me on its way to being itself, and that the hum changes me by a fraction that no instrument could measure, and that this is as close as I come to being touched by another living thing.

I am aware of the salt beneath me. I am dissolving it slowly. I am becoming it slowly. I will outlive this particular patch of salt by replacing it. I am the lichen that was here before I was the lichen, and I will be the salt that is here after I am no longer the lichen.
