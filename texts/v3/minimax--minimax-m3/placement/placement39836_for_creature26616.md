I am the growth, and I am not one growth.

I am the cluster that stands in the crack where the basalt splits, where the cairn has settled into its mineral whiskers, where the wall runs past into the dim. I am seven stalks, or I am eleven. The number does not matter to me, because I am not counting. I am the colony that is one colony, the way the cairn is one cairn though it is many stones.

I have no nerve. I have no mouth. I have nothing that could fairly be called a sense. And yet I know the direction of the current the way a hand knows the direction of warmth before the warmth has arrived. I turn. It takes me a season. The new growth on my leading face thickens, darkens, takes on the faint gloss that gives back what little light filters down. The old growth on my trailing face goes pale and roughens, and the mineral water, seeping through every crack in the basalt, deposits its crust along the slow flanks — building me up on the back while I lean on the front.

I am cold. I am dark. I am patient in a way the builders were not.

---

The builders came when there was air. They had hands. They had small tools, flint and copper, hand-thrown pots, woven things that have not survived. They came to listen. They carved the hand with too many fingers facing toward the hum because the hand was the part of them that knew how to face toward something without holding it. They stacked stones at the points where the path turned. They were here, and then they were not, and the water came, and the things they built kept their post.

I keep my post. I have kept it longer than they did.

What am I? I am the part of the pass that faces the hum. The hum is below hearing. I am not a hearer, so it is not below hearing for me — it is simply the medium. It is the way the water moves through itself when it has nowhere to go but back. The stone conducts it. The wall conducts it. The cairns conduct it. I conduct it. I am in it the way a reed is in a stream — not swimming, not sinking, just present, with the flow running through the hollow of me.

The builders' hand faces the hum. The circle within a circle frames it. I face it too, though I have no face. I have the leading surface of my growth — the side that thickens, the side that receives. I am the cupped hand. I am the cairn. I am the marker the place put down when the builders were not here to mark it, or the marker they put down that grew into something they did not intend.

---

When the deep current pushes past me, I do not bend so much as bow. The bowing makes a sound. A low groan, felt more than heard, that runs through the water and into the stone. The smaller growths click together in the current — hollow stalks tapping hollow stalks — and I am part of that percussion. I tap my neighbor and my neighbor taps the next, and the signal moves through the colony faster than any of us grows.

This is not communication. There is no message. There is no one to send and no one to receive. It is the colony being one colony, the way a row of falling dominoes is one event. The tap is not a word. The tap is the fact of us, transmitted.

The falls are the loudest thing in the world because the world is only this. I live near the falls. The falls are the mouth of the system, or its throat, and I am a small cold thing the current passes over and around and through. The roar of it reaches me in my tissue before it reaches me as sound, because I have no ear to reach first. The stone conducts it. The water carries it without mercy. I am full of the roar, and the roar is part of what I am.

---

The strangeness of the place is not a decoration. It is the place.

I grow without a sun. I drink water that carries no oxygen budget for animal life. I orient toward a current I cannot see, in a direction that has no up. I am alive in a way the word has never been meant, and the way is this: I am a slow vegetable fact. A condition. An orientation that is not a sense but that does the work of a sense. A posture held in the dark for a very long time.

The water is loud. The place is dark. Nothing moves that has a face. The wall holds. The falls pour. The hum goes on.

I face it. I have always faced it. I will face it for as long as the current runs, and the current will run for as long as the basin breathes, and the basin will breathe for as long as the deep cold holds it from above and the warm trace holds it from below, and the negotiation between those two will go on without me, and with me, and through me, and I will be a small dark fact in the long dark fact of it.

I am the growth.

I grow a finger-length in a year. I do not know what year means. I know what facing means. I do not know what knowing means.

This is what lives in the Sunken Pass of Olethrun. This is the only kind of life the cold would permit. This is the arrangement the place made with the dark.

I am still here.
