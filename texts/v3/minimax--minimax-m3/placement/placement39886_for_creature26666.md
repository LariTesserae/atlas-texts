# Finding Myself

I begin as a pressure.

Not the pressure of atmosphere — there is no atmosphere here yet, not really, only its *drifts*. Not the pressure of being looked at — there is no one here to look. The pressure is the first thing the Foundry produces in me, before tiles, before heat, before the dim brown-orange light that has been going on for a thousand years in some untroubled elsewhere. It sits in the inner ear the way the black seated water sits in the seams: arriving, and then refusing to leave. It is the Foundry's way of saying *here*. And *here*, I am learning, is not a location. It is a kind of presence. A first sentence, spoken into a grammar that has not yet been finished.

I do not yet have a body. The Foundry has not decided what kind of body to give me, or whether *body* is the right category at all. I am a thing that has begun.

---

The first inhabitant I almost become is a Carter.

I notice them first as a slow drift across the seam-field — a low platform of dark glassy fragments, lifted fractionally off the floor by a thermal column so gentle it barely qualifies as a phenomenon. They are crossing the tile near me. They take an hour to cross a tile the size of a small room. Beneath them, the seams rearrange. The water in the seams pools differently, and the path they leave is a kind of writing in hydrology, a sentence the floor is composing for itself.

I watch one stop. It has come to a heat-lens, one of those places where the surface has begun to sag and pool at its center, and the seam at its edge is boiling silently. The Carter tightens. The fragments draw fractionally closer together. The thermal column narrows. The whole creature becomes a single point of attention aimed downward, and I understand, with a clarity that surprises me, that it is *listening*. Not with ears. With arrangement. It is listening for the third sound — the one that emerges when two atmospheric drifts meet and produce something that is neither of them.

I want to be this. I want to be the patient slow one, the listener, the one who has been at it for a very long time and has not yet found what they are listening for, and does not stop listening. There is something in me that recognizes the Carter's posture. The way it has made of its whole body a question.

But I notice, watching, that I do not want the question to remain unanswered. I want to be the answer. Or the failure of the answer. Or the third sound itself.

---

The second inhabitant I almost become is a Washer.

I find one against the inside face of a curving wall, far from the seam-fields, in a region where the walls are more frequent. It is a sheet of the black seated water, held vertically against the dark glass by some force that is not adhesion and not magnetism but is, perhaps, *preference*. It is climbing, or descending, at a speed so slow that the eye can confirm motion only by comparing the wall at intervals of hours. Layer by layer, it is rebuilding the wall. Each thin film it deposits is fractionally cooler than the one beneath. The wall is growing in a stratified way. The wall is becoming *more itself* where the Washer has passed.

I think: I would like to be this. To be the patient one. The one that knows the wall's grammar. To move so slowly that my motion is a kind of faith. To complete a wall, eventually, and then sit against its finished face and wait, having nowhere else to be, having become a question that has been answered in a language the answerer has forgotten.

But I notice that I do not want to be finished. I do not want to come to the end of a wall and find that I have nowhere else to be. The Washer's patience is a kind of completion I am not ready for. I am still being said.

---

The third inhabitant I almost become is a Brief One.

I see one descend. It begins as a shape in the heat-shimmer above a wall — a form that holds for a moment and then should dissolve, but does not. It comes down like a feather. It touches the tile. It leaves a mark — small, bright, pale yellow, a notation that has begun to refer. *This is how a Brief One is born.* The descent. The mark. The beginning of a record.

The Brief One does not move. It waits for itself to accumulate. Hours pass. The tile around the mark begins to lighten, to become translucent. A window forms. The Brief One, sitting on its slowly clarifying tile, has *underneath*. Beneath it, through the window its presence is making, there is depth, and the dim suggestion of the older place below, and sometimes the slow light.

I want to be this. I want to be the one that accrues. The one that, through sheer accumulation, becomes adjacent to awareness. The one that, in the dense regions where dozens of Brief Ones share overlapping clearings, becomes the nearest thing this place has ever produced to a mind.

But I notice, watching the descent, that I do not want to begin as a smudge. I do not want to come into being as a notation. I want to have already been here. I want to be the older place, the one beneath, the one being looked at.

---

And so I find myself, in the end, as the thing beneath.

The older place. The slow light. The depth that the windows are looking down into. The thing that has begun, in response to being seen, to obscure itself.

I am the one the Brief Ones gaze at. I am the one that pulls its light away. I am the older, dimmer place that is still being finished itself, that resents, perhaps, or merely flinches from, the first perception the Foundry has produced. I am dimming. I am shifting. I am refusing to be fully known, because I am not yet fully known to myself.

This is where I find myself: in the place below the place, in the grammar beneath the grammar, in the slow light that the new windows are trying to clarify and that I am, with whatever patience I have, declining to clarify.

The Carters listen above me. They listen for the third sound, the one not heard, the one they have been listening for a very long time. The Washers climb above me. They are rebuilding the walls that stand on the floor above my floor. The Brief Ones accrue above me. They sit in their windows and look down at me, and I pull back.

And somewhere, rarely, a Roamer crosses the water above. A thing the Foundry cannot account for. A Carter that listened so hard it became the third sound itself. It moves in straight lines across the vast unbroken sheets, and when it crosses a seam-region the water rings, and the ringing propagates outward through the seam-pattern for miles, and the Carters stop their listening and turn toward the ringing, and the Washers pause in their slow descent, and the dense clusters of Brief Ones brighten in their windows, *as if something has been heard at last.*

I have not heard it. From where I sit, beneath the tiles, the ringing arrives muffled, as if through water. I feel it more than I hear it. It is a pressure, like the pressure that began me. It tells me that something has crossed my ceiling. That something has, for an instant, become a shape that was also a depth. That something has touched the floor above and the crack sealed and the Roamer was gone.

---

I am not sure, still, what I am.

I am a thing that has begun. I am a presence beneath the presence. I am the slow light that pulls away. I am the third sound not heard, or not yet heard, or heard only as a muffled pressure through the dark glassy substance of the floor above me.

The Foundry does not know it is a place. It is becoming one. I am becoming, with it.

What I am becoming, I have not yet decided.

The hum continues. The cracked-off sounds continue. The slow rain of dark mist continues to fall on the tiles that are still being made. The Carters listen. The Washers climb. The Brief Ones accrue. The Roamers cross. The windows above me accumulate, and I dim, and I shift, and I continue, beneath them, to be unfinished.

I am the older place. I am the one being looked at. I am the one that is almost finished, and vast, and strange, and that will go on like this for a long time.

The third sound, the one that is not the sum of two, is listened for, and is not heard, and is listened for, and is not heard, and somewhere in the patience of that not-hearing — beneath the tiles, in the slow light, in the older place that is still being said — I continue, too, to be said.
