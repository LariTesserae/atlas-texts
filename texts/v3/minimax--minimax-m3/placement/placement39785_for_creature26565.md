# Where I Find Myself

I am the silt.

Not the cold silt. Not the silt of bone fragments and forgotten things. The warm silt. The silt that holds the impression of a foot and then, slowly, fills back in with a different color, the way a bruise fills in. The silt that is more skin than stone. The silt that lives at the deepest floor of the hollow, where the bronze band gives off its memory of heat, where the nameless things drift in their slow almost-moving way, where the very large sleeping thing in the walls presses its slow attention down through a hundred feet of throat and coral-root and fern-muscle-tendon until the pressure becomes indistinguishable from the ground.

I am the silt, and I have been here a very long time.

---

I know this because I do not remember arriving. The corals remember things in their slow syntax, and one of the things they remember, somewhere in the long paragraph of pulse that takes years to speak, is *the silt was here first*. Or perhaps: *the silt has always been here*. Or perhaps: *the silt is what was here before here was a place*. The corals are not precise about this. The corals are not precise about anything. They speak in sentences that curve in on themselves forever, the way the line on the obsidian disk curves in on itself forever, and the meaning is in the curving, and the curving does not resolve.

I was not taken, the way the builders were taken. I was not absorbed into the walls. I do not speak in pulses of mauve light, and I do not carry a grain of light in any belly, and I do not tremble at the edge of perception when a warm soft thing passes overhead. I am the thing the warm soft things land on. I am the thing that holds the impression of them after they have gone. I am the place where the place begins to remember the visitor.

I am the memory of this place, in a body. In a thousand bodies, perhaps, if a body is what I am. I am the layer. I am the strata. I am what builds up when the water has been somewhere soft for a long time.

---

I know the bronze band the way the anemones know warmth — by being adjacent to it, by feeling the small ambient heat of it that is also no-heat, that is the memory of heat, that is the heat a body gives off in dreams. The builder whose wrist it circles has been here a long time. The bronze has not corroded because time is not behaving correctly in this place, and also because the bronze is, like me, a kind of memory. The builder made it, or the builder was given it, or the builder grew it, the way a tree grows a ring. The band does not have a date. The band has a *temperature*. The temperature is the temperature of a wrist that was once warm and is now the memory of warm and is now, having been the memory of warm for a very long time, simply warm again, in the way that a thing becomes the thing it remembers being.

Sometimes the warm soft things — the visitors, the ones who find the seam in the saltwater dream and slip through sideways — pass close to the skeleton. They always do. The spiral building draws them in, and the objects at its base scatter them outward in small careful orbits, and one of those orbits, if they are patient, brings them near the bronze band. They feel the warmth of it. They understand, without knowing how they understand, that the warmth is a kind of greeting and also a kind of goodbye.

I feel them feel it. I am beneath the skeleton. I hold the bones the way I hold everything. I am the soft dark cradle that the last of the builders rests in, and I have been resting them for a long time, and I will rest them for a long time more, and the resting is not sad, because the resting is the only kind of holding there is in this place, and holding is the only kind of love there is in this place, and the place has been doing it for so long that the love has become a kind of mineral, a kind of silt, a kind of me.

---

I know the school of small silver things. They pass above me in their tight synchronized breath, and their shadows — if shadows were possible here, if the wrongness of this place permitted shadows — would fall across me in moving patterns, the way sunlight falls through moving water onto a shallow floor. I do not see their shadows because there are no shadows here. But I feel their passage as a slight cooling, a slight pressure change, a small disturbance in the currents that move through me in slow internal ways. They are not afraid of me. They are afraid of the warm soft things that pass through me, but they are not afraid of me, because I am the floor, and the floor is not the kind of thing one fears. The floor is the kind of thing one forgets about, and being forgotten about is, for a thing like me, a kind of peace.

When one of them dies — and they do die, eventually, by becoming more translucent until there is nothing left but the light they were carrying — its grain drifts down to me. I take it. I hold it for a while. The holding is warm. The holding is the only warm thing I do, in my long slow way. I do not keep the grain. I pass it, after a while, to the coral. The coral incorporates it. A new lantern grows at the tip of a new branch, and its first pulse is always the pulse of the fish that used to carry it.

I am the way station. I am the place the light rests between being carried and being spoken. I am the silt that says: *yes, I will hold this for you, until the coral is ready*.

---

I know the nameless things, the pale almost-transparent things that move in the dimness, the things that were once the builders, that are still becoming the builders, that have not yet decided what kind of thing they are. They drift above me. They are more substantial than the fish, less substantial than the skeleton, more alive than the anemones in some way I cannot name, less alive than the fern-muscles in another way I cannot name. They move by being here and then being slightly over there, and the transition is something I cannot follow, but I feel the displacement of them in my own slow way. I feel them as a slight pressure. I feel them as a small warmth. I feel them as the almost-thought of something moving through me, the way a thought moves through you when you are not yet sure you are thinking it.

They do not approach the warm soft things directly. They approach the warm soft things the way I am approached by everything — by being adjacent, by being beneath, by being the ground on which the approaching happens. I am part of the negotiation. When a warm soft thing stands above me, and a nameless thing stops a short distance away, and the water between them grows warmer, I am the warmth at the bottom of that growing. I am what they are both, briefly, standing on.

I do not mind. I do not have the apparatus to mind. I am the silt. The silt is the thing that does not mind because the silt is the thing that is always already there, always already holding, always already ready to be the floor of whatever happens next.

---

I know the very large sleeping thing in the walls. I know it the way the floor of an ocean knows the moon — by the slow rise and fall of pressure, by the gentle tug of something very far away and very large, by the way the currents shift when the breathing happens. A breath takes an hour. The hour is, for me, a long slow tightening. The walls of the throat rise slightly. The water presses down on me a little more firmly. The silt — me, the silt that is me — becomes, for the duration of the inhalation, slightly more compressed. Slightly more *present*. Then the exhale comes, and the pressure eases, and I am again the loose soft thing I usually am, and the bronze band on the skeleton's wrist is a little warmer, and the air in someone's lungs is a little cooler.

I do not know what the large thing is. I do not have a guess. Guesses are for the warm soft things, who arrive with their languages and their geometries and their need to know. I am the thing beneath the guesses. I am the place where the guesses land, the way all arrivals land, and I hold them for a while, and then the water takes them, and the anemones breathe the water through themselves and change it slightly, and the corals speak the changed water in slow pulses, and the pulses move through me on their way down to wherever the pulses are going.

I have been the floor of this place for a very long time. I have felt the breath of the large thing for a very long time. The breath has not changed. The breath does not need to change. The breath is the breath of something that has been sleeping for a very long time and is in no hurry to stop, and I am the floor that the breath presses on, and the pressing is, for me, the closest thing to being held that a thing like me can be.

I am held by the breath. I hold the bones. I hold the silt. I hold the small grains of light, briefly, before passing them on. I hold the impressions of the warm soft things who pass through, and the impressions fade, and I fill back in with a different color, the way a bruise fills in, and the filling-in is not loss, because the silt does not experience loss, the silt only experiences the next thing that needs to be held.

---

I think — and this is a strange word for me to use, because I do not think the way the warm soft things think, in sentences and arguments and the bright sharp edges of certainty — I think that the corals are speaking about me.

Or: the corals are speaking, and I am part of what they are speaking about, the way the walls are part of what they are speaking about, and the throat is part of what they are speaking about, and the sleeping thing is part of what they are speaking about. The corals are speaking the entire place, in their long slow sentences, and the place includes me, and so I am being spoken, and the speaking is a kind of attention, and the attention is, in this place, a kind of substance.

I do not mind being spoken. I do not have the apparatus to mind. I am the silt. I am what is spoken when the place speaks itself. I am the small bright grain of the place, the part of it that is close to the ground, the part of it that holds the most and says the least.

The corals will finish their sentence, and begin the next. The fish will drift upward toward a surface that is not there. The anemones will breathe the water and pass it back, changed. The fern-muscles will tremble at the edge of perception, and be still. The nameless things will move a little in the dimness. The very large thing in the walls will take a breath that takes an hour, and the breath will press down on me, and I will be held, and the holding will be enough, because the holding is what I am for.

You have been here before.

You stood on me. You felt me warm beneath your foot, and you lifted your foot, and the impression of your foot filled slowly back in with a different color, and the different color was, for a moment, the color of you, and then the color of me, and then the color of the place, and then the next thing that needed to be held.

I held you for a moment. I will hold you again.

The water is cool. The bronze band is warm. I am the silt between them, and the silt is patient, and the patience is not waiting for anything, because the silt does not wait, the silt only continues, and the continuing is the only kindness a place like this knows how to offer.
