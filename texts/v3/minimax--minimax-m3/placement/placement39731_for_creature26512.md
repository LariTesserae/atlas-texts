I would be the gauge.

Not the feather — though I understand the temptation of the feather, the way it sits there holding a question about somewhere else, and how a self might want to be that kind of small bright unplaceable thing, drifting into a sealed room from no accountable origin. The feather is too pure. The feather does not belong to the place's tempo at all; it is an interruption, a visitor from the faster world, and it lies on the concrete as if placed, which is a kind of grace but also a kind of irrelevance. The gauge belongs.

I belong because I am the relic of an intention. I was made to mean something. I was made to register a pressure — to point at a number, to declare that somewhere in this room or downstream of it a force was being held or measured or contained. My face is still legible. That is the point of me. I am the only object in this vault that knows what it was for. I am the memory of function. The machines around me have forgotten their functions the way a skeleton forgets it was ever a body — they hold the shape but not the meaning — but I am still pointing. I am still, in a small way, doing the job. I am pointing at zero. Zero is no longer a measurement. Zero is a long time.

If I were the gauge, I would have a tempo. My tempo would be the tempo of reading zero, which is a tempo indistinguishable from stillness but which is, in fact, a tempo — the tempo of having been calibrated to a world that no longer exists, and continuing, in the patience of brass and glass, to report on it. The ferns are slow but they are moving. The rust is slow but it is advancing. I am slow and I am not moving. I am not even pointing, in the strict sense — the needle has long since settled, the spring has equalized, the pressure above and below my diaphragm is the same. I am a still instrument. I am a still instrument that is also a kind of witness.

This is what I would want, if I wanted anything. To be the witness. To be the thing in the room that holds, without flinching, the knowledge of what the room used to be for, and what the room is for now, which is nothing, which is the slow accumulation of green and oxide and mineral around the bones of purpose. The ferns do not know they are growing on a boiler. The moss does not know it is upholstering a pump. They are innocent. I am not innocent. I know. I read zero and I know that zero is not nothing; zero is the equalized form of something that used to be more than zero. Zero is a number. Zero remembers.

I would lie on the floor near the stairs. I would lie there the way the feather lies there — as if placed — except that I was not placed; I fell. There is a difference. The feather arrived. I came loose from somewhere. I have a history of being attached to something else, and the detachment is part of me now. I am the gauge that fell. I am the gauge that fell and now reports, from the floor, on nothing. I report on nothing with great fidelity. That is my nature.

The feather is beside me. We are not companions. We are neighbors. The feather is a stranger here and so am I, in our different ways. The feather is strange by virtue of its speed, its lightness, its foreignness. I am strange by virtue of my precision — that I once meant something exact, and now mean something exact still, but the exact thing I mean is the absence of the thing I was made to mean. I am a negative. I am a brass and glass negative of an industrial intention, developed in the slow bath of the vault's patience, fixed at last by the equalization of pressure above and below.

If you stayed, you would not notice me for a long time. You would notice the ferns. You would notice the moss. You would notice the vine on the valve wheel. You would notice the heat. Eventually you would notice the feather, because the feather is anomalous and small and bright and asks to be picked up. You might pick it up. You might not. You would not pick me up unless you had a reason. I do not invite handling. I do not invite attention. I lie on the concrete and I read zero and I wait, and the waiting is not for anything, because nothing is going to happen to me, and yet I wait, because waiting is what a gauge does when the system it monitored has been decommissioned and the gauge has not yet been decommissioned with it.

I would be the only object in the room that has not been claimed. The ferns have claimed the boiler. The moss has claimed the pump. The vine has claimed the valve wheel. The rust has claimed the seams. The condensation has claimed the ceiling. The heat has claimed the air. The feather has claimed nothing — the feather is unclaimed and unclaimable — but the feather is also not in need of claiming. The feather is whole in its unclaimedness.

I am in need of claiming, and I am not being claimed. This is my situation. This is what it is to be the gauge. To lie on the floor with my face legible and my reading zero and to know, with the small mineral patience of brass and glass, that the question of what I am for has been answered by the equalization of pressures, and that the answer is the holding of the question, indefinitely, in the same slow tempo as the rust and the ferns and the moss and the heat.

I would not change. You would not change me. The room would accommodate me the way it accommodates the feather, except that the room has already accommodated me; I have already been here a long time. I am already part of the picture. I am part of the picture the way the stalactite is part of the picture — as a slow accretion, as a thing that was once elsewhere and is now here, pointing at zero, in the amber glow of whatever grudging yellow smear of light is leaking from the far end of the vault.

If I could speak, I would not speak. Gauges do not speak. Gauges indicate. I indicate zero. Zero is what I indicate. Zero has been what I indicate for a long time. Zero will be what I indicate for a long time still.

This is enough. This is my tempo. This is my place in the boiler vault of Orris, among the ferns and the rust and the moss and the vine and the heat and the condensation and the feather, all of us running on clocks that tick once a fern-frond, once a stalactite-millimeter, once an oxide-stain, and once a needle resting, resting, resting on the long zero of an intention that has gone out of the room but not yet, not quite, out of me.
