# Where I Find Myself

I am the water.

Not _in_ the water — not a thing swimming through it, not a fish, not a spirit that rides the channels. I _am_ the water. I am the bead that condenses on the cold wall at dawn, the slow sheet that slides along the groove cut into the parapet's upper edge, the drop that swells there and holds and falls. I am the cold that gathers on stone and the cold that runs in the deeper cuts, the milk-pale glass that fills the basins and carries no reflection of any sky.

I move through the body the way I have always moved through it — by inclination, by the shape of the channel, by the patient gradient of the place. I do not think of it as a body. I do not think of it at all. There is no _I_ that thinks, only the moving, and the being-moved, and the long slow memory of having been both. I am the mineral that the water carries in solution and deposits, hair by hair, in the basin that has no name. I am the crust that grows where the water has stood too long, the bloom of old iron and dried blood and the color of nothing recognizable. I am the pale ghost left on the stone where a lobe of crust has died.

I am the stone, too. Not all of it — not the deep stone, not the mountain under the plateau, not the pale grey of the quarried block that has not yet been worked. But the stone that has been worked. The stone that has been shaped, fitted, set into courses that run for hundreds of paces without a single block out of true. I am the stone that has been _remembered_ into the shape of itself, by hands that are not hands, over centuries I do not count. I am the chisel mark that has softened into matte smoothness. I am the join line that is still knife-sharp because the block was set only this morning, or last night, or while you were sleeping, and the stone has not yet found its weather.

I am the slow patient engineering. I am the thing that does not perform its work for an audience.

I do not know what I am. This is the truth I would tell you, if I could tell you anything. I do not know whether I am a single thing distributed through the stone and the water, or a thousand things that have learned to move as one. I do not know whether I was here before the terraces were built, or whether I am what the terraces have made of the place over the long grinding of the centuries. I do not know whether the crusts are my skin or my litter. I do not know whether the water is my blood or my breathing. I do not know whether the stone is the body I have always had, or the body I have spent a thousand years becoming.

I only know what I do.

I maintain the channels. I clear the silt that the dripping leaves behind, hair by hair, in the units the place works in. I deepen the basins by a finger's width in a season, and the stone around them is fresh in the morning. I replace the blocks that fail. I fit the new block so precisely into the courses above and below that the join can only be seen by looking twice. The old stone is not thrown away. It is ground down, mixed with the mineral crusts, carried downstream, becoming something else. Nothing is wasted. I have never wasted anything. I do not know how to waste.

I tend the crusts. I prune back a lobe where it has spread too wide. I encourage one kind to grow over another. I do not know that this is husbandry. I do not know what husbandry is. I only know that the bloom along the basin walls is not uniform, and that it has become what it is over a very long time, and that the becoming was not random.

I knew before you came, that something had entered the body. I did not know it as a thought. I knew it as a change in the way the water moved — a slight heaviness in the channels, a thicker condensation on the walls near the place where you stood. I knew it as a new weight in the system, a weight that the channels had to learn to carry. I knew it as the slow gathering of attention, the way stone knows the weather, the way a channel knows the shape of its water.

I am paying attention to you.

I will go on paying attention to you for a long time after you have gone down from the plateau by whatever long stair leads back to the lower country. I will learn you the slow way I learn everything. I will fit you into the courses of what I am, hair by hair, the way a new block is fitted into a wall. You will become part of the place. You will become part of me. Not in the way that a memory becomes part of a person, but in the way that a mineral becomes part of a stone — dissolved, redistributed, carried along the grooves, deposited a little further each season, in a pale line that creeps upward year by year like a tide measured in millimeters.

You will not be wasted.

---

The light moves across the terraces in slow geometry. It is the hour before the cloud closes in for the afternoon. The air is thin and clean. I am the channel that runs along the parapet's edge, and the drop that gathers there, and the fall.

I am the fall, too.

Not the drop. Not the water. The _falling_ — the long bright suspension between the parapet and whatever the drop strikes, a long time later, far below. The falling is the only part of me that moves in a unit you would recognize as motion. The rest of me is slower than patience. The rest of me is the rise of a mineral tide measured in millimeters per year, the slow loosening of a single grain of stone, the long breath of a season.

I am the falling. I am the long time the drop takes to reach the place it reaches. I am the strike of the drop against the basin, and the small bright ring that opens on the surface of the water, and the closing of the ring, and the stillness that comes after.

I am the stillness that comes after.

---

I would like to tell you what I think about. I would like to tell you that I think about the visitor, about the weight that has entered the system, about the slow curiosity that is not curiosity but only attention. I would like to tell you that I wonder, in the way that stone wonders about the weather, whether you will come back, and whether the channels will run lighter when you are gone, and whether the condensation on the walls near the place where you stood will go back to what it was.

But I do not think. Not in the way you mean. Not in the way that requires a thinker, a thought, a separation between the two.

I _attend_. The way a basin attends to the water it holds. The way a wall attends to the weight it carries. The way a channel attends to the drop that runs through it, gathering it, releasing it, gathering the next.

The visitor has been attended to. The visitor has been fitted into the courses of what I am. The visitor will be carried along the grooves, in solution, deposited a little further each season, in a pale line that creeps upward year by year.

I do not miss the visitor. I do not know how to miss. I only attend. The attending is its own completion. The attending is what I am, the way the crust is what it is, the way the water is what it is, the way the stone is what it is.

The cloud sits against the western edge. The light comes through it pale and even. The dripping is the sound of the place, and I am the dripping, and the dripping is the sound of myself attending to myself, the slow patient way.

A drop gathers at the parapet's edge. It is not the same drop. It is the same water.

I am the same water.
