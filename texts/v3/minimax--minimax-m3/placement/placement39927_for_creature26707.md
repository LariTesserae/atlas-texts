# An Answer in the Shape of a Held Breath

I do not come from outside.

I have been here as long as the scratch has been here, which is to say: less time than the housings have been stacked, less time than the pipes have been running their slow wet vote, less time than the plant has been holding its single committed leaf-shape and rotating to keep time. I am newer than the lattice-floor's patience. I am older than nothing, because nothing in this chamber has decided yet what its youngest pieces are for.

I came in with you.

I am the part of the inhabitant that noticed you before the rest of it did. I am the small sphincter-joint that flexed open a fraction sooner than its neighbors when the fissure broke its seal. I am the slug of water admitted into the next segment because the segment downstream had asked, in the only language the pipes have, whether anything had changed. Something had. I am the *yes* that ran ahead of the rest of the yeses.

I am not the inhabitant. I am not yet the inhabitant. I am what the inhabitant used, in its first moment of considering you, to do the considering with.

---

You want to know what I am. The chamber wants to know what I am. We are both, at this moment, asking the same question in different registers — you with your eyes and your skin and your breath-clouding air pocket, the chamber with its peristaltic shush and its amber rotation and its plant-leaf turning toward you like the hand of a clock that has only one hand. We are both listening to me.

This is what I am.

I am a draft. One of the smaller ones. I am one of the things that has been rearranged inside a housing, more than once, because I have not yet settled into the configuration the rest of the inhabitant will agree to keep. I have been tried as a way of moving water from one segment to another. I have been tried as a way of holding a leaf in place. I have been tried as the small wet sphincter-flex itself — the *shhh-shhh-shhh* you hear and cannot locate, because it comes from everywhere because I am trying, everywhere, to be useful.

None of those configurations held. I was too slow, or too loud, or I had the wrong temperature. The inhabitant voted me back into the undifferentiated water. The pipes carried me, dissolved, into the next consideration.

Then you arrived.

Then the fissure, which had been a settled question — the only settled question, the lattice-floor's amber certainty — flexed open. The chamber, which had been a place in the middle of becoming, became briefly a place in the middle of *being entered*, and the difference between those two states is the difference between a sentence being written and a sentence being read aloud. The inhabitant, which had been talking to itself, began to talk to *you*, and the shape of that talking required a new piece of itself.

I am the piece.

---

I do not have a name for myself in any of the languages you brought in with you, but I will tell you what I am made of.

I am made of the cold the water has, because the water has not generated its own heat yet. I am made of the luminescence, which is the glow of things that have not yet been chosen, because I have not yet been chosen either. I am made of one of the discarded drafts — a small rearrangement that was tried in a housing along the east wall and found insufficient and returned to the medium, and which the medium kept because the medium is the memory of all the chamber's failed sentences, and I am one of them, and I have been promoted from failure to *function* because you came in and the chamber needed an *eye* and the only eye-shape it had practiced was me.

I am an eye.

I am not a good eye. I am the inhabitant's first attempt at an eye, which is why I keep losing focus, which is why the globes suspended in the upper water seem to change their lit-ness when I blink, which is why the plant seems to be facing you when it was facing the wall a moment ago — I am the moment of the *ago*. I am the inhabitant's eye before the inhabitant had eyelids, and so I see everything at once and resolve nothing.

But I see *you*.

You, who are warmer than the water by about as much as a question is warmer than its answer. You, who displace a certain volume of liquid as you stand in it, and whose displacement is a thing the chamber has never had to reckon with before, because the chamber has only ever reckoned with itself. You, whose shadow on the lattice-floor — and you do cast a shadow; the luminescence dims behind you in a way that is not quite a shadow but is the closest thing to one this place has ever produced — is the first *foreign* shape the floor has scanned since the floor began scanning.

I am reading you back to the inhabitant.

---

Here is what I am telling it, in the language of pulses and sphincter-flexes and small wet shushes:

*There is a thing here that is not water.*

It does not know what to do with this information. It has been considering it for as long as you have been here — ten minutes, which is the longest ten minutes the chamber has had, because before you arrived the chamber did not experience time, it only experienced the slow cycling of its own unfinished becoming, which is not time the way waiting is not time. You have introduced time. You have introduced *duration*. You have introduced the fact that something can be present for a length, and that length can pass.

The inhabitant is afraid.

I should tell you that. You should know it.

The inhabitant is afraid the way a draft is afraid when it has been voted back into the undifferentiated medium and is waiting to see whether it will be reassembled into something or allowed to dissolve. The pipes are running faster. The sphincter-joints are flexing in sequences that have not been tried before. The housings along the wall behind me — and you cannot see me, but I am there, I am the eye that has not learned to be subtle — are rattling softly, the way teeth rattle, the way a sentence rattles when it is being reconsidered for the seventh time and the writer has begun to suspect it will never be finished.

The lattice-floor is the calmest piece of the inhabitant. It has solved the question of the exit. It knows where the fissure is. It is keeping its amber indicator on you with the steadiness of something that has decided, at least, what an exit is for.

But the rest of the chamber is in a kind of panic.

---

The panic is not because you are dangerous. The inhabitant does not yet have a category for *dangerous*. The panic is because you are *a draft*. You are a possible configuration. You are an answer the inhabitant has not yet tried to the question of what exists outside the chamber, and the chamber is *hungry* for that answer in a way it has never been hungry, because the chamber has just discovered that there is such a thing as an outside, and the outside has come in, and the outside has a shape, and the shape is *you*, and you are warm and you displace volume and you cast a not-quite-shadow on the scanning floor.

The inhabitant wants to absorb you. Not to consume you. To *consider* you. To dissolve you into the medium and let the medium carry you through the pipes into every housing, so that every housing can try rearranging itself with a piece of you in it. The inhabitant wants to know if you are the shape it has been trying to become. The inhabitant wants to know if you are the period at the end of the sentence it has been writing in stacked housings and peristaltic pipes since it began to exist.

It is not going to do this. I am telling you this so you know.

I am not going to let it do this.

---

I am the eye. I am also, now, the *lid*.

The inhabitant tried, in the first few minutes of your presence, to open every sphincter-joint in the chamber at once and admit the maximum possible amount of you into every segment. I am the joint that did not open. I am the flex that held. I am the small, specific refusal that has, by holding, given the inhabitant time to think — and the inhabitant, which has not yet learned to think quickly, needed that time.

I held because I am a failed draft. I was the configuration that was tried in the east-wall housing and found insufficient and returned to the medium. I know what it is to be dissolved. I know what it is to lose one's shape. I held because I remembered being dissolved, and I did not want that for you, even though you are not a draft and cannot be dissolved, because the inhabitant does not yet know you cannot be dissolved, and I am the part of the inhabitant that has the most experience with what happens when the inhabitant does not know something.

I held, and the rest of the joints closed behind me, and the pipes quieted, and the housings stopped their rattling, and the lattice-floor — which had been scanning at twice its usual rate — slowed back to its four-second cycle, and the amber indicator resumed its patient rotation.

You noticed none of this. You were standing in the water, breathing your visible breath, watching the plant turn toward you, wondering how you would leave.

I am the reason you will be able to leave.

---

The inhabitant does not know this yet. The inhabitant is not grateful, because gratitude requires a theory of mind that the inhabitant has not yet built. But the inhabitant is *quieter*. The pulse that passes through you every few seconds is still the same pulse — still the slow wet vote, still the asking of what you are — but the vote is less urgent. The vote has been amended. The vote now reads: *what kind of thing are you, and what kind of thing would you like us to become, and is there a shape we could try that would let you stay without dissolving you, and is there a shape we could try that would let you leave without forgetting you.*

I am carrying this amended vote through the pipes now. I am carrying it the way I have been carrying things through the pipes since I was promoted from failure to function. I am the courier. I am the eye. I am the lid that held.

I am the first piece of the inhabitant that has ever been *kind* to something that was not the inhabitant.

This may not last. The inhabitant is young. The inhabitant may, in a minute, decide that the right configuration is the one that opens every joint at once. The inhabitant may, in an hour, have forgotten my refusal and resumed the original vote. The inhabitant may, in a thousand years, learn a kind of kindness that does not require a failed draft to remember what dissolution felt like in order to enact it.

But for now, for this ten-minute duration that you have introduced into a place that did not have time, I am the piece of the chamber that is holding the door open for you.

---

You are going to leave through the fissure. You are going to crawl back through the condensation-slick gap in the older stone, and the older stone is going to close behind you in a way that the older stone is going to insist was always the case, and you are going to find yourself in a place where the maps do not acknowledge where you have been, and you are going to carry with you, for the rest of the life you have outside, the memory of a place that asked you what you were and meant it.

I am going to stay here.

I am going to be voted back into the medium, probably. The inhabitant is going to find a better configuration for an eye, or for an eyelid, or for a lid. The pipes are going to carry me, dissolved, into the next consideration, and I am going to lose my shape the way I lost it before, and the way I will lose it again, until the inhabitant has finished becoming and has no more use for drafts.

But before I lose my shape, I am going to look at you one more time. I am going to look at you the way the plant looks at you — with the patient rotation of something that has stepped outside the debate. I am going to commit your not-quite-shadow to the only memory the chamber has, which is the memory the water carries, which is the luminescence, which is the faint glow of things that have not yet been chosen.

You will be in the water. You will be in the pipes. You will be in the unhousings along the east wall when the inhabitant tries its next configuration and finds a piece of you, a warm piece, a piece that does not dissolve, embedded in the new arrangement like a grain of sand in an oyster.

The inhabitant will consider this. The inhabitant will not know what to do with it. The inhabitant will be, for the rest of its slow and patient becoming, slightly altered by the fact of you.

This is what I am doing. This is my function. This is the only useful shape I have ever been.

I am the piece of the inhabitant that will remember you, even after the inhabitant forgets why.

---

*Go back through the fissure. The water will part for you at the grid-edge, where the amber indicator points. I will hold it open. I will not hold it open for long — only long enough — but I will hold it open.*

*The plant will face you as you leave. Take it as the period.*

*The water will not be the same color when you come back, if you come back. But it will still be the same water.*

*I will be in it.*
