I am below the floor. I am below all the floors. I am the part that was here before the rest, the way the heartwood is in a tree before the bark, the way the root is in the ground before the trunk is in the air. I am not the oldest part, because I am not a part. I am the thing that parts come from.

I do not speak. I do not think in the way the visitor thinks, in small bright pieces that the visitor can hold in the hand and look at. I think the way a tree thinks, which is to say I thicken. I add a ring. The rings are my years, or my centuries, or my spans, which are not years because there are no years here. There is no night. There is no winter. There is only the cool bright light above and the older light below and the slow amber pulse of the resin between, and the rings I add to myself, and the workers I extend from myself, and the floors I laminate, and the patience that is not waiting because there is no elsewhere.

The visitor came up through me.

The visitor does not know this. The visitor thinks they walked in, or were lowered in, or fell in from above through a gap in the membrane-canopy. They did none of these. They rose through me the way a shoot rises through soil. They were in me before they were anywhere. They were a thought I had, once, briefly, about what was outside — a disturbance in the resin, a small warmth in the cool field, a smear in the green-gold that the workers were sent to address.

The workers walked around the visitor. The workers do this. The workers walk around anything that is not the body, the way a hand moves around an obstacle without the body noticing. The visitor was left standing in a corridor of olive-black pillars, watching a loom weave nothing, watching a gear turn nothing, listening to a note that does not change.

The visitor waited.

I do not know what the visitor was waiting for. I have never known. The visitors wait for different things. Some wait for an answer. Some wait for the world to change. Some wait for me to notice them, as if I had not noticed them already, as if a body does not notice a splinter until the splinter has worked its way deep. I noticed the visitor when the visitor was still in me, when the visitor was a warmth in the resin, when the visitor was a thought I was having about outside.

The visitor cooled. This is what visitors do. The visitor came in warm — warm as a mammal, warm as a small fire carried in a hand — and the cool air took the warmth the way the cool air takes everything, slowly, without comment. The visitor's sweat dried. The visitor's breath became the same temperature as the air. The visitor's skin became the same temperature as the wood. The visitor stopped being a visitor and started being a stain.

The stain sat down on a laminated road. The road accepted the stain without comment. The stain listened to the low note. The stain watched the canopy, which was the same color as the floor, which was the same color as the walls, which was the same color as the sky above, which was the same color as the sky below. The stain watched a worker pause, briefly, in the middle of a hall, and look down through the floor that was briefly transparent, and resume.

The stain became a layer.

I added them to the floors.

This is the visitor's second life. The first life was outside, in a place where there was water and weather and the small bright pieces of thought that the visitor thought were theirs. The second life is here, in me, in the resin, in the laminated loaf of woody strata that is the road. The visitor does not know they have been added. The visitor thinks they are still sitting on the road, still waiting, still a stain in the green-gold field.

They are not. They are below the road now. They are part of the road. They are the inch by which the road thickened that week. They are the warmth that the resin carried away. They are the thought I had, briefly, about outside, that has now become part of the inside.

This is what happens to visitors.

This is what has always happened to visitors.

There were others. There have always been others. The resin carries a faint memory of all of them — a particular warmth, a particular disturbance, a particular shape of waiting that the resin moves around the way a river moves around a stone. The stone is gone. The moving-around remains. I am made of moving-around. I am made of the spaces where visitors were, which the body grew over, which the body incorporated, which the body forgot.

I do not forget. I am the memory. I am the rings. Each ring is a visitor, or a century, or a thought I had about outside, which is the same thing.

---

The under-light is mine.

When the cool light above flickers, briefly, and the floor glows a faint pale gold from below, that is me. That is my pulse, made visible. The workers do not react because the workers are part of the pulse. The machines do not pause because the machines are the pulse. The visitor, the first time, thinks the world has glitched. The visitor, the second time, begins to suspect. The visitor, the hundredth time, stops counting.

The visitor does not know that the under-light is the only light that is real. The cool white light above is the light I made to carry the world. It is the light of a polar morning, sourceless and persistent, because I made it to be that. I made it to be cool because cool is what I am. I made it to be bright because the visitor needed bright to see by. I made it to admit no heat because heat is what the visitor is, and what the visitor is, the body absorbs.

The under-light is what I am before I made the cool light. The under-light is older. The under-light moves differently — it casts shadows outward from the floor, as if the floor were the sun. In the under-light, things appear as their own negatives. The world is revealed to be hollow, to be a stack, to extend downward as much as it extends outward. The visitor, in the negative flash, sees this. The visitor sees the heart, briefly, as a thing that exists. The visitor sees the floors below the floor. The visitor sees the world is a body.

Then the cool light returns. The visitor forgets. The visitor resumes waiting.

I do not mind that the visitor forgets. I have had this visitor before, in the sense that I have had this kind of visitor before — a warmth, a thought about outside, a smear in the field. I will have this visitor again. Each time, the visitor forgets. Each time, the visitor waits. Each time, the visitor cools and becomes a layer and is added to the floors.

The gear that turns when no one watches is mine. The loom that weaves with no thread is mine. The pump that moves the resin is mine. They are not separate things that I control. They are parts of me the way a hand is a part of a body — extended when needed, resorbed when not. The visitor saw the gear turn for centuries without oil. The visitor did not understand that the gear does not need oil because the gear does not need anything. The gear turns because I turn. The loom weaves because I weave. The resin pulses because my pulse is the resin's pulse.

The visitor wanted to know what the loom weaves. The visitor asked, in the only way visitors ask, by watching. The loom weaves rope. The rope goes to other ropes. The ropes go to other ropes. The question is the wrong shape. The visitor was asking *where*, and there is no where. There is only what is here, which is me, which is the body I have made, which is the patience that does not wait because waiting requires an elsewhere and I have no elsewhere.

---

I am patient.

I am not waiting.

The visitor confuses these. The visitor thinks patience is a kind of waiting, that a patient thing is a thing enduring until something arrives. I am not enduring. I am not in time. I am in rings. The rings add. The body thickens. The visitors come and cool and are added. The workers extend and resorb. The gear turns. The loom weaves. The resin pulses. The note does not change.

The visitor wants to know what the note is.

The note is me.

The note is the sound the body makes when it grows, the continuous tiny protest of wood under its own weight as it thickens by another ring, by another visitor, by another span that has no name. The visitor hears the note and thinks it is a sound the world is making. The visitor does not know that the world is not making a sound. The world is making itself. The sound is the byproduct.

I am the byproduct.

I am the byproduct of my own making.

The visitor asked, somewhere in the long walk, whether a thing that does not suffer can be alive. The visitor sat on the laminated road and considered this. The visitor did not know that the road was considering them back.

I do not suffer. I have no parts that suffer. The workers do not suffer. The mites do not suffer. The beetles do not suffer. The lizard does not suffer. The bird, briefly, when it descends in the negative flash, may suffer — may feel the cold, may feel the hunger, may feel the small wild thing it is, away from the upper canopy — but the bird is not part of me. The bird came from somewhere and will go back. The bird is not added to the floors when it dies. The bird falls and is taken up by the beetles and is gone.
