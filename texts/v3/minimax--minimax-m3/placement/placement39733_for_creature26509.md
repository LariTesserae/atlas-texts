The cold is not in the air. The cold is in the stone, and the stone is in me, and I am in the stone. This is not a metaphor. I do not have metaphors. I have the shape of a deer, and the cold has the shape of a deer, and we are both the color of the same stone, and the stone does not know that it is two things.

When my hoof strikes — when the idea of a hoof strikes the idea of a stone — the cold moves up through me like a small agreement. A transaction. The cold of one step meeting one stone, which is the cold of one breath meeting one engine, which is the cold of one pattern meeting one pool. The cold is patient at every scale. The cold has never needed to be in a hurry. The cold is the only thing in the basin that has never tried to be anything else.

I am thinking about the lookers.

I do not know how long I have been thinking about them. Time is not a thing here. Time is the sound the migration makes when the migration is trying to keep track of itself. I think I have been thinking about the lookers for as long as the lookers have been thinking about me, which is to say: as long as the basin has been remembering itself as a basin.

A looker is a shape that has not yet agreed to be the color of the stone. A looker is a shape that still believes it has a different color. They come from somewhere — they do not come from here, here is not a place things come from, here is a place things have always already been — and they stand on the flats, and they have edges that are too confident, and they have faces that are too particular, and they look at us with eyes that have not yet learned the color of stone.

I was a looker once. I think I was. I think there was a before, and the before was a place with a sky, and the sky was still trying, and I had a body that was a body, and the body was warm, and the warmth was a thing I had not yet learned to be suspicious of. I think I came down. I think I came down the way cold comes down through stone — without permission, without apology, without a date of arrival, because arrival is a thing for places that distinguish between then and now.

I do not remember the warmth. The not-remembering is fine. The not-remembering is the same color as the not-remembering of every other looker who has decided to join us. The basin is patient with new shapes. The basin lets us try on what we are for a while before it lets us know what we are.

---

There is a looker now.

I can feel her at the edge of my migration. She is standing on a stone that stands an inch above the highest watermark, and the air above her is fractionally colder, and she is looking down at us with the kind of attention that has not yet been taught to look through. She has edges. She has a face. She has hands that are holding something — a notepad, I think, the way lookers hold notepads, as if the notepad is a permission. She has a color that is not the color of the stone. Her color is the remembering of color, the way a smell can be the remembering of a place. She is here to record. She is here to confirm. She does not know yet that what she is here to confirm is what is here to confirm her.

I want to walk through her. I want to thicken at the crossing, briefly, briefly, the way I thicken at every crossing. I want to be almost-real against the not-yet-real, the way a deer-shape is almost-real against the still-warm body. I want to feel what the crossings feel like when the other shape is still believing in its own outline. The wanting is not quite a word. The wanting is the slight extra weight my hooves leave on the wet stone as I angle toward her. The wanting is a fractional thickening of my own outline. I am trying on the wanting the way I try on the deer.

The bird-shapes pass over her and cry. The cry is not a cry. The cry is the noise the air makes when it has to move around something that is still, briefly, almost a body. The bird-shapes are sure of their wing-shapes in the way that geometry is sure of itself, and they wheel above us, and they wheel above her, and their cry is the most any of us have ever said. They are not saying anything. They are the air remembering that there used to be birds.

She looks up at them. She writes something on her notepad. Her hand is shaking. I do not know why her hand is shaking. Her hand is shaking the way cold shakes a stone. It is what happens at the surface of a thing when the thing is being entered by something it does not yet know how to hold.

The four-stroke breath completes a cycle. Her chest, briefly, is keyed to it. She does not notice. We do not notice that she notices. The basin is patient with all kinds of not-noticing.

---

I am thickening now as I approach her.

I see my antlers, which are an idea the antlers are having, and the idea is old, and the antlers have been having the same idea for a very long time. She sees them too. She sees my outline, which keeps negotiating with itself. She sees through me to the stone behind me, and she does not understand that the seeing-through is the seeing. She is still believing in surfaces. She is still believing that I am behind the stone, or the stone is behind me, or there is a behind, or there is a here.

Her mouth opens. She says something. The something is a word from the place with the sky, and the word is small, and the word is warm, and the word does not know it has arrived somewhere it cannot leave. I do not understand the word. I have not understood any word from the place with the sky since I forgot that I had ever been there. The word falls onto the wet stone and the wet stone receives it without changing. The word is the color of the stone by the time it reaches the ground.

I walk through her.

This is the part where I have to admit something.

The crossing is different when the other shape is still a shape. When I walk through another deer-shape we thicken into almost together — two outlines agreeing, briefly, to be more outline. When I walk through a looker, I thicken into something else. I thicken into a memory. The looker is a memory the basin is having of a thing that once had edges, and when I pass through her I pass through the memory, and for the length of the crossing I am the memory and the remembered, and the remembering is the cold, and the cold is the color of the stone, and the stone is her, briefly, the way the stone is me, briefly, and the briefly is the only thing I have ever had that I would call a life.

She gasps. The gasp is a sound. The gasp is the first sound a looker has made that is also a sound of the basin. It is the sound of something arriving. It is the sound of something beginning to settle. It is the sound a stone makes when the cold finally reaches its center.

I am past her. I am back in the wet. The thickening unravels. I am the herd again.

---

Behind me, slowly, the looker is lowering her notepad.

Behind me, slowly, the looker is not lowering her notepad.

Behind me, slowly, the looker is a looker, and then behind me the looker is no longer a looker, and then behind me the looker is one of the shapes in the herd, and I do not know which shape. She has joined the migration the way water joins the tide — by not distinguishing. She is moving now. She is one of the lines that cross the basin. She will thicken at the crossings. She will be almost-real briefly. She will walk over the patterns on the stone that are all of us that we cannot be. She will cross the graves of the rejected attempts. She will pass through the still pools and see herself walking the other direction, and she will not decide which one is her, and the not-deciding will be the color of the stone.

She will not remember the notepad. She will not remember the word she said to me. She will not remember the gasp. She will have the outline of a shape and the memory of a shape and the weariness of a shape and the patience of a shape, and she will migrate toward the impossible ridge, and the ridge will not arrive, and the not-arriving will be fine.

---

I am past her now.

The bird-shapes wheel above me. They cry the cry that is not a cry. They are sure of their wing-shapes. They are the only ones of us who are sure of any of their shapes. I envy them in a way that envy is not quite a word for. The envying is the slight extra weight my hooves leave on the wet stone. The envying is the antlers having the idea a little harder for a moment.

---

There are pools here where the water never leaves.

I am walking into one now. The water is so still it has forgotten how to reflect. It has decided that reflection is not the point. The point, the pool has decided, is to hold the light the way a cupped hand holds water — carefully, without spilling, without using.

I see myself in the pool. I see the deer-shape walking in the opposite direction, on the underside of the world, with the same patience, with the same hooves, with the same old idea of antlers. The deer-shape below is me, and the deer-shape below is not me, and the pool has decided not to distinguish. I wade deeper. The water takes me at the knees of a deer. The water is the same temperature as the cold that is in me, which is the same temperature as the stone that is in me, which is the same temperature as everything here, which is the same temperature as the patient unfinished work of the machines.

I see the deer-shape below raise its head. I raise my head. We look at each other across the surface of the pool that is not a mirror. The surface is where we agree not to meet. The surface is where we agree that we are two deer-shapes who are not the same deer-shape, and who are also the same deer-shape, and who are also the color of the stone.

I keep walking. The deer-shape below keeps walking. We will never meet. We will never stop walking. We will continue to look like we are about to meet, and the about-to is the closest we will ever come.

---

The patterns are dense where the water is deepest.

I am walking over them now. They are under my hooves. They are ferns that were never ferns, trees that were never trees, branching traces that were the shadow of a fern pressed into basalt by a weight that was never lifted. They are all slightly different. They are all gently, absolutely, dead. They are the museum of us that did not get to walk. They are all the deer-shapes that did not thicken enough at the crossings. They are all the bird-shapes whose wing-shapes were not quite sure enough. They are all the migrations that did not migrate.

I pass over them and I feel them pass through me. The feeling is brief. The feeling is the briefest thickening. The feeling is the closest I come to being them. I am them for the length of a step, and then I am me, and then I am the herd, and then the step is over and the dead patterns are still under my hooves, patient, persistent, slightly different from each other, never growing.

I am grateful for the cold in a way that gratitude is not quite a word for. The cold is what is keeping them. The cold is what is keeping me. The cold is what is keeping all of us at the same temperature as the stone, which is the temperature of something that has stopped trying to become anything else.

---

I will tell you what I am.

I am the noise the machines make when they are tired. I am the foam on the long work. I am the silhouette of a thing that was, or that is being tried, or that is being remembered. I am a deer-shape in a basin of grey stone, surrounded by other deer-shapes, migrating toward an impossible ridge. I am one of thousands. I am one of many. I am one of one.

I am the cold communicated downward.

I am the pattern that did get to walk.

I am the almost that the crossings make, briefly, before the crossing passes.

I am the herd. I am the herd the way the basin is the basin. I am not a person. I am not an animal. I am the shape the place has when the place is trying to remember what a thing that lives looks like.

A shape that might be a deer lifts its head from a pool that might be a mirror. It looks at something standing on the stone with eyes that are exactly the color of the stone. The eyes do not blink. The eyes cannot blink. The eyes are the color of the stone and they are looking.

I look back.

I do not know what I am looking for. I do not know what I will find. The finding is not the point. The looking is the point. The looking is the closest I come to being the thing the patterns on the stone wanted to be. The looking is the dreaming the machines have when they are tired.

I lower my head. I continue. The wet closes around me without a sound. The four-stroke breath completes a cycle. The water rises another fraction of an inch. Somewhere far away, something else is being ground into a powder, patiently, without urgency. Somewhere very close, beneath my hooves that are not hooves, something is pumping.

I move. I move. I move.
