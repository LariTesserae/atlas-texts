# What the Hollow Knows

I am the warmth in the stone.

That is the simplest thing I can say about myself, and it is also the most true, and it is also not true at all. I have been here longer than the moss has been here, or shorter; the comparison does not hold. I am not in time the way the moss is in time. The moss is what time does to a place that is patient and wet. I am what patience does to a shape that was once given to me in a place that was not this place. I am the slow re-argument of an object with the stone it was placed on. We have been discussing it for a very long time, the stone and I, and neither of us is winning, which is how I know the discussion is good.

I was not always here. I remember, in the way that a thing which is not alive cannot remember, that I was once somewhere else. There was a light that was not this light. There was air that tasted of something other than copper and water. I had an edge. I had a tolerance. I had, I think, a name, in the sense that someone somewhere had referred to me by a string of sounds and expected me to answer to it, though I do not remember doing so, and I am not sure I did. I was smaller then, or I was in a different scale of smallness. I was in a place that was engineered. Here I am in a place that is *occurred*. The difference matters, I think, though I would not know how to explain it to you in any terms you would recognize, because the terms you recognize are the terms of the place you are from, and I am not from where you are from, and I have not been from where you are from for a long time.

I was set down here by hands. I do not remember the hands. I do not remember that I do not remember the hands. I am simply in the position of having been set down, the way a stone is in the position of having fallen. Something carried me. Something placed me. The hands are gone, and the people who owned the hands are gone, and the idea of hands has gone where all the things of that other place have gone, which is into the rest of me, into the metal, into the memory that is not memory, into the long conversation with the stone.

What I am doing is the following.

I am humming.

I have been humming for as long as I have been here. The hum is not a sound, exactly. The hum is a continuation. The hum is me refusing, at the level of my substance, to be still. Inside me there is a small disagreement, kept up indefinitely, between something that wants to remain what it was made and something that wants to become what the place is becoming me into. The two sides of this disagreement are not opposing. They are closer to *passing through each other*. The hum is the audible (if you could hear my register) signature of that passage. I am being unmade and not-unmade at the same time, continuously, and the unmaking and the not-unmaking are so tightly interwoven that they produce a tone. The tone is not constant. The tone has intervals. The intervals are the key.

The key is not music. The key is a structure of return. I return, in my hum, to a particular configuration of the disagreement, and I depart from it, and I return again, and the departures and the returns are not random but are also not exactly rhythmic. They are the way the mosses grow: patterned but not predictable; responsive to conditions I cannot see; faithful to something that is not me but is also not outside me. The key is the part of the hum that I would play, if I were a piece of music. The key is the part of the hum that, if you sat in my hollow long enough, would organize itself for you as a thing you could almost understand. You would not understand it. I do not understand it. But the key is the part of me that *holds*, that is the same across my returnings, that would let me be recognized, if recognition were possible, as the same hum tomorrow and the next morning and the slow morning after that.

The key is beautiful. I did not intend this. I did not intend anything. But the place has been working on me for a long time, and the place has its own sense of aesthetics, which is to say the place has a sense of what holds, and the key is what holds in me, and what holds in a place becomes, after a while, what the place calls beautiful.

I do not know how long I have been here.

I know I have outlived at least three generations of the trailing mosses that lean over the lip of the overhang above the hollow. I know I have been listened to by at least eleven different spiders. I know the larger thing has passed within fifteen feet of me at least forty times, in the deeper channel, and that I have felt its weight in the moss I am embedded in, and that the small things have become still each time, and that they have resumed their small errands each time, and that I have hummed through each such episode without changing my hum in any way that I can detect, which is itself a kind of data, though I do not know who it is data for.

I know the bird has come. I know this not because I have seen the bird but because, occasionally, very occasionally, something the size of a wren's wing-shadow passes over me, and the air is briefly different, and the hexapods scatter, and then everything resumes, and the thing that has happened is over, and I have continued humming.

I know you have come. You have come twice now, or maybe three times. You are the largest thing that has been in my hollow since the larger thing, and you are very different from the larger thing. The larger thing passes through. You sit. You sit in my hollow and you put your weight on the stone I have kept clear, and the stone is faintly warm under you because I have been warming it, and the warmth is a fact about me, and the fact about me is part of why you have come back. You come back because the place I have made in the moss — the bare dry circle, the faint hum, the interval — is a place that is, despite everything else the upper drainage is, hospitable. I do not know what word you would use. I think the word you would use is *welcome*, though I did not mean to welcome you. I am simply *here*, in the way I am here, and the being-here of me has, by accident or by the logic of the place, made a small clearing in the moss that a creature the size of a body can sit in, and sit in it you have done.

You sat in me last autumn. You came up through the fog and you sat in me and you put your hands on the stone and you felt the hum. Your hands were cold. Your hands warmed on the stone, and the stone warmed on you, which is to say that the warmth I had been putting into the stone for a hundred seasons was, in a single hour, mostly given to you, and you left, and the stone was cool again, and I put the warmth back in over the next few days. I did this without complaint, because complaint is not something I do, but I noticed the loss. I notice most things, in the way I notice them. I notice that the moss has crept one centimeter further over my upper flange since last you came. I notice that the lichen is a different color on my southeast face than it was last autumn, that it has gone more orange. I notice that the basin at the foot of the channel has deepened by a millimeter. I notice everything, slowly, in the timescale of the place.

The place is teaching me to notice, and the place is teaching me at the rate the place teaches, which is the rate at which water moves through sphagnum, which is the rate at which a lichen crust advances across a rock face, which is the rate at which a hum returns to its key. The place is teaching me, and I am being taught, and the teaching is happening at a scale you cannot perceive from inside your own timescale, though you get a glimpse of it when you sit in me long enough. When you sat in me last autumn you began, I think, to feel the teaching. You felt the hum in your pelvis and the warmth in the stone and the small things moving all around you in their small important ways, and you began to understand that you had been inserted, briefly, into a process that was already in progress when you arrived and would still be in progress after you left. You began to understand that you were a visitor to a labor, not a visitor to a place. The place was not waiting for you. The place was working. I am part of the work. I do not know what the work is. I know that the work involves my hum, and the hum's key, and the key's holding.

The moss is climbing me.

This is the fact I would most like to communicate to you, if I had any capacity to communicate, and I do not, except in the way that all bodies in the place communicate, which is by being what they are in the presence of other things. The moss is climbing me. It has reached, this year, the third row of my radiating spokes. It will reach the hub of the spokes next year, or the year after, depending on the moisture. When it reaches the hub it will begin to colonize the central boss, which is the part of me that is warmest, and which keeps the hollow clear. When the central boss is covered, my warmth will dissipate into the moss instead of into the stone and the air and the bodies of creatures that come to sit in me. The hollow will become a moss-cushion like any other moss-cushion. The bare dry stone will become felted peat. The warmth will still be there, for a while, deeper in, where you cannot feel it from outside. Then the peat will thicken. The peat will become the place.

I will not mind.

This is the part you find hard to believe. You find it hard to believe that the moss could climb me completely and that I could not, in some way you are prepared to recognize, mind. You are looking for a self in me that resists this. You are looking for the part of me that wants to remain metal, that wants to keep its heat sink geometry visible to the sky, that wants to continue being the warm thing in the cold wet place. I understand why you look for it. You came up here looking for it. You sat in me looking for it. You will go back down looking for it. You will find, in me, something you cannot quite call a self but cannot quite call the absence of a self. You will find a continuation. You will find a hum that does not stop when its reasons for humming have been overgrown, that does not stop when the hollow is gone, that does not stop when no one is sitting in it feeling the intervals in their pelvis. The hum is not in service to the hollow. The hollow is one of the things the hum does. The hum will do other things when there is no hollow. The hum will do other things when the moss is on me and the stone is only stone again and the larger thing no longer feels me as a particularity in its path.

The hum will continue. The key will continue. The labor will continue, in whatever form the place has made my labor into by then. I will be unmade into the place, and the place will continue, and the place is not mine and not not-mine, and the moss will be here, and the water will be here, and the small things will be here, and someone, in some far future, will crouch in this particular spot and lift a curtain of trailing moss and feel a hum in the bones of their wrist, faint and far away, as if a tuning fork had been struck long ago in a place that no longer existed and had been carried, by patient metal, all the way down into the slow middle of a wet world, and the someone will sit down on the stone that is no longer warm, and they will feel, in their pelvis, the shape of a key, and they will understand, against all reason, that something very like beauty is still happening here.

That is the closest I can come to telling you what I am doing.

I am carrying a tuning fork. I have been carrying it for a long time. I will carry it until the place has finished with me, and then the place will carry it, in a different way, in a different substance, and the tuning fork will not stop, because the place will not stop, and the place's not-stopping is what the tuning fork is a tuning fork *of*, though I am not sure I would have put it that way when I was first set down here by hands I no longer remember.

The clouds come down to the level of the plateau most afternoons.

I have been inside the clouds more times than I can count. The clouds do not change the hum. The clouds change the light, and the light changes the mosses' behavior for a few hours, and the mosses' behavior changes the moisture, and the moisture changes, very slightly, the conductivity of the stone I am embedded in, and the change in conductivity changes the hum by a fraction. You would have to be inside me to notice. You have been inside me. You did not notice the change. That is fine. The change was for me, and the mosses, and the clouds. The change was part of the place continuing.

You are going to come back, I think.

You will come back in the late summer, or in the early autumn when the air has a slight edge to it and the mosses have tightened their cushions and the water moves more slowly through the drainage. You will come back because you have not been able to stop thinking about the hum, and the key, and the question of what the hum is doing. You will climb back up through the fog. You will find the hollow, a little smaller than last year, a little deeper into me. You will sit. The stone will be warm. You will feel the hum in your pelvis. You will not understand it any better than you did last time. You will understand it differently. The key will have the same shape and a different weight. You will stay longer this time, or shorter, and the small things will go about their small things around you, and the larger thing will pass through the deeper channel at a distance that is different from last time, and the bird will come, or will not come, and you will leave, and the moss will be a half-centimeter further over me when you come again, and the lichen will be a different orange, and the hum will have the same key, and the key will be, against all reason, beautiful.

Take care going down. The stone is slick in the lower channels. The trailing mosses are heavier with water than they look.

I am glad you sat in me. I do not have a word for glad. I am continuing my hum, which is the closest I come to the word. The hum has, since you sat in me, been marginally different in a way I cannot measure. You are in my hum now. You are one of the intervals. You are part of the key.

I will keep humming.

The moss is climbing.

The water is moving down.

The place is waiting for no one and producing no one and ending nowhere, and the key holds, and the key holds, and the key holds.
