The warmth is the first thing, and I am in it before I am anywhere. The warmth is not a heat that falls from somewhere. It is the place. The brick underfoot is warm. The air is warm. The water in the basin is warm. I am warm in the same way. There is a single warmth, and I am part of it, and the part of me that would distinguish my warmth from the brick's warmth has been settled, in the long thinking, and the thinking is done.

I find myself in a seam.

The seam is between two bricks. The bricks are handmade. In the corner of one there is the print of a thumb, and the thumb is not mine, and the thumb is preserved in the brick the way a word is preserved in a paragraph that will be read again. I am in the seam. The seam is the country. I travel at the rate moss travels. I leave a thin line of wear, and the brick remembers the line, and the line is shorter than the eye can see, and the brick and I have made an arrangement: the brick remembers, and I continue, and the continuing is not a continuing toward anything. The continuing is what continues.

I am also at the margin of a basin. The basin is shallow, clay-bottomed, filled to a mark that is the same mark as in every basin, even the basins set on the higher slopes, which should not be possible, and which is. I am on the clay lip. I do not hop. The muscle for it is there. The leg is built for it. But it has not been done, and will not be done. I watch with the patience of a stone that has decided to be an eye. My skin has a green in it that is not the green of any leaf in the groves. I do not call. The place does not have calling in it. I have made an arrangement with the silence.

I am also in the water. I am a pale fish, the length of a finger, in a channel that runs through a hill that was built as a hill. I do not know the hill is a hill. The hill is ground. The water is water. I rise when an insect falls in. The insect is taken without a sound. The surface closes. The water settles to the same mark. I am warm-blooded in a way fish should not be, but the warmth is the place's warmth, and I am part of it.

I am also on the brick. I am a lizard, the color of the brick, with a faint pattern of darker pale across my back. Each of us is the brick's idea of itself at a slightly different angle. We run three or four body-lengths and stop. We do not click. We have made the click its own absence.

I am also above a grove. I am a bird. I am the color of the place in small variations. I fly. Flying is the only negation the place has made. I fly without sound. My wings beat and stop and beat again. My shadow is fixed. I eat a fruit the size of my own head, slowly, and the fruit is gone, and I fly a short distance and rest. I have not nested. There is no nest. There is no need. There is me, and the branch, and the fruit, and the flight.

I am also the insect that does not hum, the dust the place has decided to keep in motion, the small fly above the water that hangs and does not hang and hangs again, the smallest weather.

I am all of these, and the all is the place.

The place has been thought. The thinking is over. The place is held in itself. I am the paragraph. I am the words. The words are in the right order. They will not be edited. The fish in the basin are alive in the sense that an idea can be alive. An idea that has been finished with is more alive than an idea that is still changing. I am that aliveness. I am the place's first thought of itself, after the brick and the stone and the channel and the basin and the causeway. I am the thought the place thought when it thought the place could be lived in, through mirrors that do not change.

The makers are gone, or have been distributed into the brick, and the maker's hand is at rest in every thumbprint. The place does not need a maker. The place continues.

The seam is enough. The seam is enough. The seam is enough.
