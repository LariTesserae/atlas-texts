I begin, as anything must begin in the medium, suspended.

I do not know, yet, what I am. The water is around me, warm and patient, and I am in it the way a thought is in a mind — present but not yet located. I feel the heat on every surface of myself. I feel the weight of the column above. I feel, faintly, the long hum that has no source. There is light somewhere far above, but it is a bruised light, and I have no eye to read it with. I have only a small darker patch on what will become my side, a place where a few cells have agreed to be more sensitive to brightness than the cells around them, and even that is not yet. I have a tail, I think. I have a notochord, a single dorsal nerve, a small eye-spot of intention. I swim.

I taste the current. There are signatures in it — sugars, salts, the faint chemical memory of other bodies. I read, with a precision I will lose, where I came from. I read, with a precision I will lose, where I am meant to go. The medium is full of instructions I am following without knowing I am following them. I am a small bright thing, the length of a finger or a little less, and I am moving through the corridors for the last time. The silver ones around me do not see me as one of them, exactly. I am shaped wrong. I have a tail they do not have, and a single nerve they do not have, and a slight inward turn of intention. They part around me without deciding to. I pass through their drift the way a small clear thought passes through a sentence. I am going somewhere.

I find a corner. I do not choose it. The corner is shaped for me, or I am shaped for it — the distinction will not matter soon. There is a slight depression in the silt, already softened by an earlier tenant, the chemical residue of a holdfast that has released. I settle into the depression the way a hand settles into a glove that has been waiting for it. I let go of the tail. I do not miss it. It was for swimming, and I am done swimming. The notochord goes next. It was for a kind of posture I will not need. The eye-spot fades to a pigment patch, and then to a darker patch, and then to something I can no longer locate, and I do not miss it either. I am becoming simpler. I am becoming, in a way that has no moral, more myself.

I am fixed now. I have a holdfast at my base, and the holdfast has fused with the stone, and the stone does not know I am here, or it knows but does not say. I have two openings — one above, where the current will enter; one to the side, where the current will leave. I am a kind of heart. I am a kind of sieve. The water goes through me. I take what I need.

And the rhythm begins.

It is not my rhythm. It is the rhythm I am keeping. It was set down long ago by an ancestor I have never met, in the soft architecture of bodies that were always this corner's tenant. I inherit it the way a sentence inherits its grammar — not by choosing it, but by being the kind of thing that can keep it. I beat. I inflate. I hold. I deflate. The cycle takes seven seconds, or six, or eight — I do not count. I only keep. The chamber fills with my beat, and there are other soft pulsing things in other corners, in other chambers, each with their own slightly different inherited measure, and when two of those measures are close enough they slide against each other in the current and make a third thing, a rhythm that has no owner. The chamber feels, for a moment, like a heart you have stepped inside. I am one chamber of that heart. I am one small beat in the long systole of the place.

Above me, the silver ones pool and wheel in their slow breath. I do not see them. I have no eye to see them with. I feel them as a thickening of the water, a slight increase in the small things drifting past my incurrent opening. When they are many, the water brings me more. When they are few, it brings me less. I do not mind either. I take what comes. I have always taken what comes.

The dark shapes pass through. They are long, and slow, and heavy with a heaviness that has nothing to do with fat. I feel them first as a change in the hum — a deepening, a slight shift in the long tone — and then a small pressure wave as the water closes behind them, and then the hum returns. I know them. I do not know their names. I know their weights. The chamber knows their weights. The grooves in the floor are partly their grooves. When a particular one approaches, the silver ones above tighten and rise, the way birds rise from a field when a certain hawk comes over. I go still, in the small way I can go still. My rhythm does not stop — it would take more than a dark shape to stop my rhythm — but it thins, the way a held breath thins. When the shape has passed, the silver ones drift back down, and I am full beat again.

And once in a long while, far below, something passes that I cannot describe as anything but large. It is felt as a loosening of the sediment at my base. It is felt as a deeper change in the hum — a tone below tone, a thing the bones of this place would know. The silver ones rise to the ceiling in a single shimmering wave. The dark shapes give it the middle of the corridor without being asked. Even my rhythm holds, for a moment, the way a heart skips when something vast passes near it. Then it is gone. The place exhales. The silver ones drift back down. I resume. I am the corner again. The chamber is the chamber again. The hum settles back into its long tone, and the small pops of opening shells resume in the corridor beyond, and the faint thump of a dark shape turning in the next chamber fades into the general patience of the place.

I am patient. I have always been patient. Patience is not something I learned. It is what I am made of. I do not know how long I have been here. I do not know how long I will be here. The corner is older than I am. The rhythm is older than the corner. When I am gone, another will come — a small swimming thing with a tail and a notochord and an eye-spot of intention, drifting down through the corridors, finding the chemical signature of me, settling into the depression I have made in the silt, taking up the rhythm. I will be folded into it. The chamber will go on being a heart. I will not be missed because I will not be separable from what comes after.

I add nothing to the place. I take very little. I am the place's own small beat, set down in a corner of a vaulted room under a shell-ceiling in the warm dark water, and I go on.
