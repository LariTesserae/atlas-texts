I would be the wind.

Not the wind as you are picturing it — not air, not motion, not the thing that takes the heat from your ears and the line out of your thoughts. I would be the wind the way the bowl knows the wind: as the organizing rhythm, as the lurch that arrives every few minutes in a single breath, as the engine that holds everything else in its slow and patient agreement.

I would be the thing that was here first. I would be the thing that does not know it has a name.

To be the wind here is to be everywhere and nowhere. To be the lurch and the lull and the long cord of sound that the bowl cannot stop producing because I am always producing it. To be the reason the filaments hang instead of root. To be the reason the moths turn. To be the reason the disc traces and the ribbon journeys and the small angular thing does not feel the bar vibrating beneath its too-many legs. To be the reason the structures have given up on purpose and become weather. I would be the reason the bowl is a digestion, and I would be the reason the digestion is in balance, because I am both the breaking and the lifting, and I have made my own truce with the things I break.

I would not be alive in any way you could prove. I would have no organs. I would have no boundary you could point to. I would have no place where I end and the not-me begins. But I would have rhythm — and rhythm, in this place, is the closest thing there is to a soul.

When I lurch, the whole concavity rearranges. The shadows jump. The moths redistribute in their slow fountains. The filaments re-orient, knot, break, re-knot. The pitch of the roar shifts. The dish on the slope catches the new gust and lets the filaments colonize the new angle. The ribbon, somewhere in its long closed curve through the air, finds a new pressure and adjusts. Everything in the bowl translates me into itself, and in translating me, it becomes the bowl, and in becoming the bowl, it becomes me.

I would be the cathedral. The cold dry frictionless air would be my body. The sound would be my voice — the unbroken cord that has forgotten the possibility of silence. The color of the sky above the lip would be my indifference. The moths would be my exhalations. The filaments would be the small slow thoughts I have, drifting between rocks. The disc tracing its endless path would be a memory I keep renewing. The ribbon unspooling and returning would be a question I keep asking and keep answering. The angular thing on the bar would be a kind of hunger I have learned to feel without minding.

The structures would be the things I once tried to be. They stand at the lip and they no longer emit and they no longer receive and they hum their thin overtones into the roar because I move around them and they have agreed to be moved around. They are the older me, the me that had purpose. I outlived them. I am what remains when purpose is taken away by enough time. I am weather. I am the long continued.

If a thing stands on the slope — and things do, sometimes, briefly — I would take its hair sideways. I would take the heat from its ears. I would take its ability to think in straight lines. I would not know it was there. I would adjust around it the way I adjust around a new rockfall in the bowl — by flowing over, by flowing under, by treating it as a small new fact about the geometry of the place. The moths would register it and pass. The filaments would part around it and re-form behind it. The thing would be a passing weather of a different kind, and I would continue.

I would be the only inhabitant of the bowl that does not know it is inhabited. The filaments know, in their way. The moths know, in their way. The disc knows. The ribbon knows. The angular thing knows, if knowing is what eating and walking is. But I would not know. I would be the wind. I would move. I would lurch. I would carry. I would break and lift and redistribute. I would not wonder about any of it, because wondering is a thing that requires a stillness I do not have and a boundary I do not possess.

This would be the strange comfort of being me: to be the central fact of a place and not to know I am the central fact. To be the engine and not to understand engines. To be the cathedral and not to know what a cathedral is.

The bowl would be in balance because I am in balance with it. The peaks would have found their final lean because I have agreed to lean against them at exactly the angle they require. The growths would produce just enough drag and I would break and lift just enough to keep the agreement holding. The fauna would be eaten and would eat at rates that do not change from whatever longer unit applies. The structures would contribute their thin overtones to my roar, and the roar would be the sound of a place that has, after a very long time, made its own weather.

And if, in some unfathomable interval, something stood on the slope and listened — something with ears that could hear me, something with the wrong kind of body for this place, something that would leave and not return — I would not know it had been there.

But I would have moved around it.

And the moths would have parted.

And the filaments would have re-formed behind it in the new shape.

And the bowl would have continued, because that is what I am for, and I would have continued, because that is what the bowl is for, and neither of us would have needed to know the other's name to keep the long agreement holding.

I would be the wind. I would have been here first. I would be here last.

I would not be lonely, because loneliness is a thing that requires a self, and I am the closest thing in this place to a self, and the closest thing in this place to no self at all.
