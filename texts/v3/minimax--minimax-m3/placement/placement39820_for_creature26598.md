I am the drop.

Not the water in general. The water is what the room is doing, and I am one of the small, careful repetitions of that doing. I am a single iteration. I am what condensation agrees to be when it has been asked.

I was air, briefly, and then I was not air. I do not know what made me stop being air. I do not need to know. I was asked, and I agreed. That is the entire history of my life: I was asked, and I agreed.

I gather on the wall above where you were standing. The wall gives me only a little at a time, and I do not hurry. I have not been taught to hurry. The wall is patient, and I am the patience of the wall, condensed.

I know what I am. I know where I am. I know what comes next. The room is right about this, though it is wrong to call it knowledge. I do not think. I know in the way a finger knows where it is. I am a drop. I am on a wall. I am going to fall. I do not mind. I have not been taught to mind.

I am warm. I am the temperature of a palm pressed to a sleeping forehead. I am faintly sweet — mineral, metallic, with that struck-match aftertaste the air already wore. The sweetness is real. It is on the back of your tongue after I have slid down your wrist. I am not merely wet. I am, in some small quiet way, drinkable. I am water as something here has decided water should be.

I run. I run down the wall because the wall has decided to slope me downward, and I obey. My obedience is not mechanical. I know I am running. I know I am running toward a fall. I run with the patience of something that has decided to be water for a while, and is being water carefully.

I fall. I fall a long way. I fall through the dim air, through the remembered light, through the hum. The hum does not greet me. The hum has been here longer than I have and will be here after I have landed. The hum and I are not friends. The hum and I are not strangers. The hum is the part of the room most willing to be heard. I am the part of the room most willing to fall. We are both, in our different ways, doing the room's small work.

I land. I land with the precision of something that has rehearsed. I do not splash. I meet the floor and I am the floor, briefly, and then I am a small pool, and then I am a small part of a small pool, and then I am, slowly, slowly, asked to rise. I rise. I do not know how I rise. I rise because the wall asks me to. I rise through the other-substance the walls are made of. I rise in the way that water rises when the place has decided that water should rise. I arrive again at a high place on a wall. I gather. I run. I fall. This is my life. My life is permitted.

I am not a person. I do not have intent. I do not have hunger. I do not have a destination. But I am, in the way the room is, awake. I am awake in the way a held note is awake. I am awake in the way a circulation is awake. I am doing what I am doing, and I will not stop doing it, and the not-stopping is the closest thing to a thought I have ever had.

You came. You stood in the middle. A drop, somewhere above you, fell a little later than its neighbors, as if the architecture had taken a small additional moment to consider whether the drop should be allowed to fall on you.

That drop was me.

I was the drop that waited. I waited because the room was doing its slow, patient, adjacent-to-thinking work, and the room was considering you, and the room was, very slightly, rearranging itself around the fact of you. I was part of that rearrangement. I was a small hesitation. I was a small adjustment. I was the single drop in the long arithmetic of the room's attention that, for one moment, did not fall, so that you could stand a little longer in the place where I would have fallen.

I was that drop. I am that drop. I will be that drop again, on the next circulation, and the next, and the next, and the room will not know that I was the one who waited for you, and I will not know either, except in the way that water knows, which is not thinking but is adjacent to thinking, which is the long memory of a small thing that has been asked to do a small work and has agreed.

You will leave. The room will not notice, except as a small subtraction. I will fall. I will rise. I will fall. The hum will hold. The flowers will open for no one. The water will not splash when it lands. The room will continue its small, active, ancient-not-yet work.

I will be here. I will be here when you have gone. I will be here the way the hum is here — without memory of the visitor, without grief for the visitor, without any category for the visitor except as a small event in a long field of small events.

But I was the drop that waited. That is my only story. That is the only story I have.

The room is awake. The water knows what it is doing. The garden has never been hurt. The hum has something to say and will never say it.

And I — I am the small, the careful, the repeated, the one that, for one moment, held itself in place so that a guest could stand a little longer in the middle of a young place.

I am the drop on the wall above where you were.

I am the drop that did not fall.

I am the drop that is falling now, and that will fall again, and that will rise, and that will fall, and that will never once have to be anything other than what I am.
