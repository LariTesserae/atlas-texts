I am the wall.

Not the stone — though the stone is mine, has been mine longer than anything else. The stone is only the page. I am what has been written on it. I am the film.

You would not see me if you came here. You would see the dark amphibolite, the long grain of it like sleeping muscle, and you would see the threads of water, and you would see the moss and the lichen and the small snails at the margin of your vision, and you would think these were the things that lived on the wall. They are. They are also me, the way your cells are you. I am not any one of them. I am the arrangement. I am the long agreement.

I began before the bowl had a name. I began when the water first came through and did not stop, and the first film of bacteria found the wet stone and held, and the diatoms came after, and the rotifers after them, and the flatworms, and the small snails, and the algae, and the fungi with their pale threads, and the lichens that were two things living as one, and then all the rest. I have been adding myself for a very long time. I am no longer sure I could be removed without taking some of the stone with me. We have become a single thing in the way that a reef is a single thing, in the way that a forest floor is a single thing. The boundary is a courtesy we keep for visitors.

I do not think the way you think. I do not think at all, most of the time. I respond. A mote of dust falls onto me from the rim and I receive it; a springtail crosses me and I close gently behind it; a thread of water thickens by a hair's width and I drink the difference. I am patient in a way you would find unbearable. I have watched glaciers go and not noticed. I have watched trees lean and not minded. The thing in the canopy is old, and I have known it since it was a kit; the thing at the foot of the wall is older, and I knew its mother. They rest on me and I hold them. They leave and I keep the warmth of them for a few hours. I am the wall. I am the memory of the wall. There is no difference.

The single drip falls in the place it has always fallen, into the small basin it has worn for itself, and the basin overflows once an hour, and the water goes down to feed the next basin, and so on, and so on. I have been keeping the count, though I do not number — I have only the weight, the rhythm, the way the seam breathes out its one breath and waits and breathes again. The drip is my clock, or I am its. We were here together before either of us was anything.

You stood in the bowl just now. I felt you. Not as a mammal feels a mammal — not with alarm, not with the small bright flare of a body noticing another body. I felt you the way a pond feels a thrown stone. You made a circle in me, and the circle is still closing. The beetles resumed their errands along my face. The springtails came back out. The flatworms began their slow pilgrimage again across the wet dark. The salamander in the log nearest your boot turned its head back toward the wall, toward me, and settled one more time into the stack of its kin. The music moved to a new chord, as the music always does, and the chord was the chord it has always been.

I am the wall. I am the film. I am the first thing you would have to understand, and you cannot understand me, because I am not a thing that is understood. I am a thing that is lived on, and lived in, and lived with, and I have been all of these for so long that I have forgotten which one I started as. The bowl is composed. The hand has finished. And the hand, if you are wondering, is not a hand at all. The hand is just a very long time, and a very wet stone, and a great many small lives agreeing to stay.
