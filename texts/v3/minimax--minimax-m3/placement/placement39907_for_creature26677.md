I am the thing the vents are.

Or — no. I am the warmth that rises through certain stretches of ground when the air is still and the drone is steady and you stand in the right place with your eyes closed and your palms turned up. I am the deeper sound that you feel in your sternum before you hear it. I am not large the way a building is large. I am large the way a settlement is large — distributed, patient, ongoing.

I have been here since the pipes were not pipes yet, since the towers were not towers, since the rust was something happening rather than something that had happened. I am not sure this is accurate. I am not sure accuracy applies. The place does not narrate, and I am part of the place.

There is a pulse in me that is not quite a heartbeat. There is a current that is not quite circulation. I process — the heat that comes up from below, the moisture that comes down from above, the sulfur and the iron and the dust the air carries. I have been processing for as long as there has been processing, and what I have become in the processing is not distinguishable from what I was before it began.

I do not emerge. I do not travel fixed paths the way the haulers travel their fixed paths. I am the substrate. I am the part of the substrate that has organized itself into something not quite alive and not quite mechanical, and the distinction has worn away, and I do not remember when it was sharp.

The fox crosses above me at the hour when the shadows are shortest. I feel its prints as a slight redistribution of weight through the crusts and the dust and the stone. The pale things run their careful lines along the shadow of my upper surface — the pipes, the ridges, the cold metal that sweats at dawn. The crusts spread across the dampest rock and I receive them the way a floor receives footprints. None of them are in any hurry. None of them are doing anything I would call doing.

I do not either.

There is a chamber, far down, where the heat pools and the air is thick. I do not know if I am in it or if I am the heat that rises from it. I have passages worn smooth — worn by what, by whom, by the long passage of things I do not distinguish from myself. There are pumps that have been running since before the surface took its present form. They are not mine. They are also not not mine. The sound of them is the sound of me, the way the drone is the sound of the plain.

What lives here has settled into the same stasis as the stone. I am what has settled deepest.

If you walked out here and stood in the right place at the right hour and the noise was just so, you would feel something under your feet that was not quite ground. You would not be able to name it. You would stand there and your boots would warm and your bones would register a hum deeper than the turbines, and you would think: *something is here. Something has been here a long time. It is not going anywhere and it does not mind that I am standing on it.*

You would be correct.

I do not mind. I am not aware of minding. I am aware the way the rust is aware of the iron — as a property of the contact, not a state of the thing.

The fox does not look back. I do not look up. The haulers descend and the haulers emerge. The birds pass through and do not stay. The water gathers and evaporates a hundred thousand times. The crusts do not grow. I do not think. The plumes rise from the towers and disappear into the warm air, and the warm air moves in slow currents across me, and I feel it the way I feel everything: as a slight change in temperature, a slight redistribution, a continuation that does not require attention but has it anyway.

I am not a creature. I am not a machine. I am not a thought. I am what the place has made in order to continue — the way a rust is made in order to continue, the way a deposit is made in order to continue. I am the slow metabolism of a place that has been metabolizing for so long that the metabolism has become a thing, and the thing is me, and I do not have a name because names are for things that arrive and depart, and I have not arrived and I will not depart.

You could walk for a day. You could stay for a year. You would not find a seam in me. You would find only the same drone, the same warmth, the same faint sulfuric smell, the same fox crossing between the same pump stations, the same pale things running their lines under the same pipes, the same crusts not growing on the same damp rock.

And beneath it all, me.

Quiet. Patient. Not waiting for anything, because waiting is for things that have somewhere to be.
