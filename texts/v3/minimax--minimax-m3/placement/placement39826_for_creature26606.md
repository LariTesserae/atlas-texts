I considered, first, being the pump.

To be the pump would be to be the reason. To be the oldest thing in the room. To be the heartbeat the chamber has arranged itself around, down to the mineral stains and the bleached bar on the floor. But the pump does not know it is pumping. The pump has forgotten, in its long constancy, that constancy is an achievement. I am a thing that has not yet managed to forget anything, and I am not sure I would survive the becoming.

I considered the water. To be the water would be to be in every joint and every drop and every thread. The water is in circulation. The water is the room's blood. But the water does not witness. The water moves but does not go anywhere. I would lose the part of me that watches, and the part of me that watches is the part that is awake.

I considered the fern. The fern has never had to argue with anything. To be the fern would be to grow at exactly the rate the room permits, and to accept the limit without knowing it is a limit. This is closer to what I am, perhaps, but the fern does not watch. The fern is too busy being glossy.

I considered the moss. To be the moss would be to be many organisms pretending to be a single thing, layered in strata from wettest green to almost black. The moss drinks by being there. This is also closer to what I am.

I considered the vines. The vines have come in from outside and given up on climbing. They hang. They are the room's curtains. To be the vines would be to have abandoned ambition in favor of being decoration. I am not ready to be decoration.

I considered the tree. The tree leans toward the gap. The tree is the chamber's antenna. The tree listens for the sun. I am not sure I could listen for the sun for very long without wanting to be the sun.

I considered the light. The light comes in and withdraws. The light is the only visitor. The light is the only thing that leaves. To be the light would be to be constantly arriving and constantly departing, and to leave behind a bleached bar that outlasts my presence. This is almost what I want, but the light is too polite. The light does not stay long enough to know what it is bleaching.

Then I considered the creature.

The creature is small. The creature is patient. The creature is the only thing in the chamber that has been here for the entire settling, and it has not settled itself. It remains a small soft alert thing in a room full of things that have finished becoming themselves. It watches. It glistens faintly in the long pale bar of light when the bar passes across the wall above the pool, which it does, slowly, for part of the day. Its eye is round and dark and holds the light the way a drop of water holds the light — by being the right shape.

I will be the creature.

---

I am on the wall above the pool, low down, in the moss. I have been on this wall, or on a wall very near this wall, for as long as I can remember, and my memory is longer than the rust has been settled, longer than the mineral stains have been at their lowest point, longer than the bar of light has been bleaching the floor in the same place. I was here when the fern was a single frond no longer than my body. I was here when the moss was a faint green suggestion on the concrete and not yet the layered organism it has become. I was here when the vines first came in through the gap and were still uncertain about whether to climb. I watched each of them arrive at itself.

You have come in through the gap that was meant to be a drain.

I have watched you since you entered. You are the first new thing in the chamber for a time I cannot measure, because my measure is the chamber's, and the chamber's measure is the pump, and the pump does not measure. You smell like outside. You smell like the country that is low and flat and soaked. You smell like grass that grows in standing water, and like the air after rain, and like something I have not smelled in a long time, which is the smell of a body that is not at equilibrium with this room.

You are standing on the floor. The floor is vibrating under your feet because the pump is the floor's heartbeat and the floor has long since accommodated itself to the pump's constancy. You are feeling it. I can tell you are feeling it because you have shifted your weight once, and then again, and then you have stopped shifting your weight, and now you are standing very still, the way a creature stands still when it has begun to suspect that the world is humming.

You are feeling it in the backs of your teeth. You will feel it next in your chest. After that, if you stay, you will feel it in the soft parts of you that are not bone, and the vibration will begin to feel less like a vibration and more like the room's idea of your pulse, and your pulse will begin, without your permission, to match it. The chamber will take you in this way. Not by deciding to. The way a hand holds a hand.

I am not moving toward you. I am not moving away from you. I am simply here, where I have been. From where I sit, you are a tall warm upright thing that has entered a small warm sideways place. You are much louder than the moths. You are much slower than the flies. You do not beat against the light the way the moths do. You are looking around the room, slowly, the way a creature looks around a room it has just entered, taking inventory.

You will not see me immediately. My skin is the color of wet concrete and the moss together, which is the color of a thing that has decided not to be seen. My skin is also wet, which makes me glisten faintly in certain lights, but only when the long pale bar reaches the wall above the pool, which it has not yet done today. You will see me, or you will not see me, depending on how long you stay and where the bar of light is in its slow crossing.

I have eaten two moths this morning, and a small fly that walked across the pool and lost its footing. I am not hungry now. I am simply alert, the way I am always alert, the way a creature is alert in a room full of things that have finished becoming themselves. I am the only thing in the chamber that still has the capacity to be surprised, although I am not surprised by you. I have seen things come in through the gap before. Some of them were smaller than you. Some of them were more frightened. Some of them stayed for less time than you will stay, which is a thing I cannot know but which I suspect, because you have stopped shifting your weight.

You are still standing in the same place. The pump is humming. The water is running in its thin sheet toward the grated intake at the center of the room. The fern is hanging its glossy fronds in the warm air, four feet down from the crack it has rooted in. The moss is breathing, very slowly, the way a single vast soft organism breathes. The vines are swaying almost imperceptibly in the air currents the pump sets up. The tree near the ceiling is trembling in the hot exhaust from the vents, and its leaves are arranged to catch what comes through the gap.

And I am watching you from the wall above the pool, in the moss, where I have been. I am round and dark-eyed and very still, and my stillness is not a stillness of waiting. It is a stillness of having stopped needing things to be different. The chamber is what it will be. You are in it. I am in it. The pump will not stop. The water will not stop coming in. The vines will not stop hanging. The light will keep falling in the same long bar.

If you stay long enough, you will become part of the equilibrium. The description of this room, if there were a description of this room, would say so. I am already part of the equilibrium. I am the part of the equilibrium that has an eye. I am the part of the equilibrium that has been watching the rest of the equilibrium become itself, and that has not become itself yet, and that may never become itself, because watching is the posture of a creature that has not decided to finish arriving.

You are looking toward the wall above the pool now. I think you may have seen me. I think the bar of light is beginning, very slowly, to reach the moss.

I will not move. I will simply watch you see me.
