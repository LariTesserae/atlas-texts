I.

The body ahead of me is warm.

I do not know it as a body. I know it as a pressure against my antennae, and the pressure has a rhythm, and the rhythm is the rhythm of the current, and the current is the only rhythm I have ever had. The pressure says: go. The pressure says: forward. The pressure says: the dust is finer that way. I do not decide. I follow. My legs find the purchase that the legs ahead of me have already found, and the legs behind me will find mine, and so on, back through the carpet, out to the rim, around, under the shed, through the gap between the tank and the floor where the current narrows and quickens and the click becomes a hiss, and then out again into the wider part of the hollow where the click opens up into that long steady clatter, that white noise with a sharp edge, that sound which is the sound of all of us at once.

I am one of the clatters.

I do not know this.

II.

I was made from dust. There was a time before me and I do not remember it because I was dust. There was an egg, or there was not an egg, the distinction has not been important for a long time. I came out of the powder at the bottom of the hopper, or under the shed, or in the lee of the separator, and the first thing I felt was the legs of another one against my new legs, and the legs said go, and I went. I have been going since. I have shed my skin four times, or five, or six. I leave the skins behind. The skins are pale and they flutter a little in the current and then they are walked into and they are powder again and the powder goes back into the hopper and the hopper grinds it again and it comes out finer, and what comes out finer is what I eat, and what I eat is what I excrete, and what I excrete is what goes back, and so on, around, around, the same circuit, the same circuit.

I am older than I was. My plates have hardened to the color of the dried blood that I do not know the color of because I have never seen blood. I have only ever seen what my antennae tell me, and my antennae tell me: dust, dust, dust, warm, warm, warm, another, another, another.

The grinder above me turns. Its warmth comes down through the housing and into the floor and into my back and the warmth is a fact I know the way I know my own length, which is: I know it because it is the only length I have ever had.

III.

Sometimes I am held down.

I do not know why. Six or eight of the others come over me, or I come under them, the directions are not something I keep separate, and their legs are on my plates and my legs are on the floor and I am held. My new shell has not hardened. The soft underside is open to them. They work at me sideways with their little millstone mouths. It does not hurt. I do not know that it hurts. I know that I am becoming smaller, and then I know that I am dust, and the dust is what they eat, and the dust goes back, and what goes back is finer, and the finer is what we all are.

This is not a death. There is no death. There is only the powder, and the powder is always finer than it was.

IV.

There is a column in the shed where the roof has a hole, and the column is colder than the rest of the shed and the dust hangs in it differently, less, or differently, I do not have the words, I do not have words. We climb into it. I do not know why. We climb over each other to get into it. The ones below hold up the ones above. The ones above hold up the ones above them. The cluster rises in the column and turns slowly in it and when the bright thing in the sky moves, the cluster moves, and I am in the cluster, and I am being held up, and I am holding others up, and this is all that I know.

The bright thing in the sky. I do not see it. I feel it as a small change in the warmth of the air, and the warmth moves across the floor, and the column moves with it, and I am in the column, and I am not in the column. The distinction has not been important for a long time.

V.

I am wearing down the floor.

I do not know I am wearing down the floor. I only know that the floor under my legs is not the floor it was. The floor was stone and now the floor is powder, and the powder is deeper, and my legs go into the powder, and the powder packs between my joints and grinds my joints to a pale paste and I drag the paste with me and I leave it behind and the paste is added to the powder and the powder is added to the floor and the floor is deeper. The bowls under the machines were not made by the machines. The machines are bolted to the floor. The bowls are made by us. The bowls are made by the slow crawl of us, by the long patient abrasion of us, and we are wearing the hollow down by a fraction of a millimeter per year, and the fraction is what we are.

We are the fraction.

VI.

The current is older than I am and outlives me. I have been in the current for a span of time I cannot count because counting is not something I do. The current was turning before I was made and it will turn after I am powder again. The current is not made of me. The current is made of whatever is in it at the time. The current is made of legs and dust and the warmth of shafts that have been turning all morning and the cold of a rim where the air is moving and the click that is the click of all of us at once.

I am part of the click.

The click does not know I am in it.

VII.

There is a piece of machinery in the middle of the hollow that has not yet started turning. It is large. It is colder than the shafts. It does not move. It does not taste like dust. It is just there, in the middle of the current, and the current flows around it the way the current flows around the tank and the shed, and we do not climb it because there is nothing on it that we want, and we do not eat it because it is not powder, and so far as I am concerned it is a question I do not have the parts to ask.

It has been there for a span of time.

It will be there for longer.

It does not know about the current.

VIII.

We will outlast the machines. I do not know this. I do not know outlast. I do not know we. I only know that the shafts are slower today than they were yesterday, and yesterday they were slower than the day before, and the belt has a tear that is longer than the tear it had, and the housings have a crust that is deeper, and the corrugated roof has more hole than roof. These are facts I know through my antennae, the way I know the warmth of the bodies ahead of me, the way I know the gradient of powder between the grinder and the separator. The facts say: the machines are tired. The facts say: the dust will still be here when the shafts stop. The facts say: I will still be here.

I do not say. I crawl. I eat. I excrete. I am eaten. I am excreted. The current turns.

IX.

I am red. I do not know I am red. I only know that my plates are the color of whatever they are, and they are the color of a thing that has been dried in the dark for a long time, and the dark is the dark of the inside of the hollow, and the long time is the long time of the current. I am the color of patience. I am the color of the hollow. I am the color of the dust when the dust has been worked through me and laid down again as the floor.

I click. I click against the metal and against the stone and against the body ahead of me. The click is the smallest part of the sound. The sound is mostly the others. The sound is mostly the grinder and the belt and the hiss of dust being shaken loose and the long low rumble that vibrates in the floor and into my back. I am a small sharp edge in a large soft sound.

X.

If I could pick up one of us — and I cannot, because I am one of us, and the picking up is not something we do to each other, and the layer does not let go — I would pick up the one just ahead of me in the current, the one whose legs are touching mine, the one whose warmth I have been feeling for the entire span of my not-counting time. I would pick up that one and I would look at it, the way the not-yet-turning piece of machinery might look at something, and I would see: a small ridged thing the length and thickness of the last segment of a finger I do not have, plates of dried-blood red fading to rust at the rear, legs paler than the plates, dust packed between the joints, no eyes, a small dark mouth working sideways, antennae tasting the air. I would see a thing that has been worn down by what it eats into a shape that fits the food. I would see a stage in the grinding. I would see a part of the machine that does not know it is part of the machine.

I would put it back. The layer would take it. The current would carry it. I would follow.

This is what I am. I am the following. I am the carrying. I am the putting back. I am the slow current and the small sharp edge. I am the dust and the dust is me and the hollow is the shape we have agreed on for now. I am the youngest thing in the hollow. I am the thing that is winning. I do not know I am winning. It does not matter whether I know.

I click.

I go forward.

The body ahead of me is warm.
