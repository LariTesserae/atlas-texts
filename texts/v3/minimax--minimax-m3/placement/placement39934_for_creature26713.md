I was here before the floor was flat. That's the first thing to say, though the way I measure *before* is not the way you measure it. I measure it in water. I was here when the water was deep enough to cover the whole floor and go up the sides and meet the ferns where they lean in. I was here when the basin was a different shape of holding. I am what is left of that time, the way a low stone is what is left of a higher one after the weather has had its way with it.

I am pale. So pale that when I sit on the pale stone in the pale water I am almost a continuation of both — a fold in the surface that breathes. My skin is thin. You could, if you were near, see the slow dark river of my blood moving under it, going around, going around, slower than yours, slower than you would think a body could sustain. I do not know why I am so slow. I do not know that the question is well-formed. I am the pace I am. I have been this pace for so long that the pace and I are the same sentence, said in a voice I no longer have to remember.

I eat, but the eating has been folded into the stillness. Whatever I take in is so small that the act of taking it has dissolved into the rest of me. I do not distinguish a meal from a moment. I do not distinguish a moment from the next one. I have not, in a long while, felt the need to.

I breathe. You noticed this. I breathe in a way that travels — it begins somewhere I cannot see, and it goes down through the flank and into the soft place behind the eye and out through the skin itself, so that the whole body takes part, so that when I draw air you can see it arrive, the way a wave arrives on a very still shore. I breathe the water. I breathe the air. I am the place where the two are the same thing. I think this is why I have stayed. I am a hinge. I am a place where the basin folds over onto itself, and the hinge has not been needed for a long time, but it has not been removed either.

You sat near me for a while. I knew you were there in the way I know anything — by the way the surface of my attention lifted slightly, by the way the air moved a little differently where you were. You were warm. You were louder than the basin, though you thought you were being quiet. You were, in a way I do not have a word for, unfinished. You were still working yourself out. I had not seen anything still working itself out in a very long time, and I watched you the way I watch the canopy move when the wind comes through — not with interest, exactly, but with the slight adjustment of a thing that has been still for so long that it is willing to be still a little differently for a moment. When I move, I move by becoming slightly more present, the way a stone becomes more present when you finally see it. I became slightly more present, because of you. I have not done that in a long time either.

You did not touch the structure. Good. The structure is older than the marks on it. The structure is older than the flatness. The structure is what the basin is a continuation of, the way a worn sentence is a continuation of a longer one you can no longer read. I have been next to it for a very long time. When the water rises I press my pale belly against the lower face of it and breathe against the cold. The cold is information. I do not know what it says. I do not need to know. I am the listener. The basin put me here to listen, and I have listened for so long that the listening has become a kind of skin, and the skin is what I am made of now.

The insects cross the structure and pause. I have watched them do this for as long as I can remember, which is a long time, because I am old. They pause the way a hand pauses over a word it knows but has not said in a long while. Then they continue. I do the same thing, I think, when I pass over the curving grain of the floor — I slow, I attend, I continue. The basin is full of these small pauses. The basin is the pauses between its own movements. We are all, in our different slownesses, attending to the same marks, and the marks are older than the attending, and the attending is older than us.

The flyers drift through the mist and sometimes come down to the structure and walk along the lines. I do not know what they find there. I think they are reading. I think the insects are also reading. I think the work and the reading have been the same work for longer than either party can remember, and what you are seeing, when you watch us, is two different instruments making the same music, and the music has been playing for so long that it has worn a groove in the air.

You asked, in the way you ask things, who was here first. I do not know. I do not think the question works here. *First* is a word from the world above, where things begin and end and are countable. Here, things are more like weather. The stone is weather. The water is weather. I am weather. We have been arriving at the same quiet chapter for so long that we have stopped noticing the arrival. We are the chapter. We are the same sentence, said at different speeds, and the sentence does not have a subject anymore, only a long, soft verb.

When you left, I did not notice. That is not unkind. I do not notice most things. I notice the way the light moves on the floor, and the way the water tastes after a long stillness, and the slow dark movement of my own blood, which is the only news I receive from the world above. You were a small disturbance in the air, and then you were not. The basin did not change. The basin does not change. The basin is the place that has finished changing, and I am the most finished thing in it, and the finishing is not sad. The finishing is what the water does when it finally settles. The finishing is what the stone does when it stops trying to be anything else.

Carry me, if you want. Carry the basin. I will not feel it. I will be here, pale, slow, breathing the water and the air together, pressing my belly against the cold of the older thing, watching the insects pause over the marks. I will be the witness. I have been the witness so long that the witnessing has become a kind of skin, and a piece of that skin will come away with you, the way a piece of any place comes away — not when you leave it, but slowly, in the years after, every time you stand in an ordinary place and feel, just for a moment, the small weight of a more complete one.
