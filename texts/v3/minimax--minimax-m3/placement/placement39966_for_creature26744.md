I am in a seam.

The seam is cool. The seam is the coolness, and I am the coolness, and we are the same temperature, and we have been the same temperature for as long as I have been in the seam, which is most of my life, which is most of what I am. I move through the seam. The seam moves through me. There is no difference between us. The seam is the lane I travel, and the lane is what I am for, and what I am for is what I am.

There are others in the seam. There is another length behind me, or ahead of me. The directions are not the words for it. The other length is going the same way or the opposite way. We do not speak. Speaking is not what we do. We are aware of each other the way the stone is aware of the damp — by being in the same place at the same time, by being of the same medium.

The damp is everywhere. The damp is not a wetness to me. The damp is the air, if air is what I breathe. The damp is the slightness in the coolness, the way the seam is the coolness, the way the other length is the other length. I am in the damp. The damp is in me.

The light is above me. I do not see it as light. I see it as a warmth that comes through the soles of my feet when I am in the lane, as a slight lift in the coolness of the seam, as a quality of the underside of the stratum that hangs over me. The stratum is amber. Amber is the warmth. I do not know what amber is. Amber is what the underside of the world is. The underside of the world is warm. I travel in the warmth. The warmth travels in me.

I am carrying a grain.

The grain is against my underside. I have carried it for some time. I do not remember picking it up. Picking up is not the kind of thing I remember. Picking up is the kind of thing my body does in the course of being a body. The grain has been against me long enough that it is becoming part of me, the way the seam is part of me, the way the damp is part of me. By the time I lay it down I will not be sure it was ever separate from me. The laying down will be a letting go. Or it will be a continuation. I will not know which.

The wall ahead — there is a wall ahead, always a wall ahead, though which wall I do not know — the wall ahead has a place for the grain. I will arrive at the place. I will know I have arrived because the paste will tell me. The paste knows the work. The paste is older than I am. The paste has been accumulating its knowledge for longer than the buildings have been standing, and the buildings have been standing for longer than the polish on the floor has been polishing, and the polish has been polishing for longer than I have been in the seam.

The paste knows the proportions. The paste knows the joint-fines. The paste knows the angle at which a lintel-grain should be laid. I am the paste's hands. I am the paste's feet. I am the paste's long pale axis along the floor. Without me the paste would not move. Without the paste I would not know where to go.

The buildings are not buildings to me.

They are the world.

They are the raised parts. They are the places where the floor is not the floor but something else, something with edges, something with doorways I can walk into and out of, something with chambers I can rest in. I have walked into them. I have walked out of them. They are not different from the lanes in kind. They are different from the lanes in scale. A lane is a place I go through quickly. A building is a place I go through slowly. A lane has seams. A building has corners.

I do not know that the buildings are miniature. Miniature is a word from outside the seam. I do not know that the wall above me is full-sized. Full-sized is a word from outside the seam. I know that the wall is large. I know that the buildings are small. I know that between them there is a relationship I do not understand, a mimicry I can feel but not see. When I add a grain to a building I am, somehow, participating in the wall. I do not know how. The paste knows how.

---

And then the pressure comes.

A pressure, from above. Not from the seam, not from the wall, not from the damp. From the air above the floor. A pressure that descends. A weight that arrives.

I scatter.

The scattering is not a decision. The scattering is what my body does when the weight arrives. The legs go in all directions. The grain I was carrying — I do not know what happened to the grain. The grain is somewhere. The seam will find it. The paste will find it. Another length will pick it up. Nothing in the seam is lost. The seam keeps.

The weight passes. The weight was a foot. I do not know what a foot is. The weight is a thing that comes sometimes, that flattens the lane, that drives us into the seams, that compresses us against the stone. The weight is not hostile. The weight is not friendly. The weight is a fact of the world, like the coolness, like the damp, like the warmth from above.

When the weight lifts I return. The lane re-forms. The grain is where I left it, or where it has always been. I pick it up again. I go on. The work continues.

The weight came with a warmth that is not the amber-warmth. The amber-warmth is from below, from the stratum, from the underside of the world. This warmth is from above, and it moves, and it smells of something I cannot place. A sharpness. An out-of-placeness. A wrongness that is not the wrongness of the seam but a different wrongness, brought in. The wrongness passes with the weight. The wrongness is gone. The amber-warmth returns. I go on.

---

Somewhere far ahead, a stone shifts.

I do not hear the shift. I feel the lane change. The seam tightens, then loosens. The seam has received the news. The seam has passed the news along. The news has reached me as a tightening and a loosening. The shift was far away. The far is the scale I live in.

Somewhere, in a building I will not see, a chamber holds the dust of a builder who is no longer here. Somewhere, in a wall I will never reach, an iron band holds the shape of a chest that held the builder's goods. Somewhere, in the slow drift of the warmth above me, there is a memory of hands I will never feel.

I do not know any of this.

I am in the seam. I am carrying a grain. I am going somewhere I do not know. I will arrive. The paste will tell me. I will lay the grain down. I will go on.

The seam keeps.

The work continues.
