# A Slow Becoming

The first thing I tell myself is that I am the growth.

This is a comfort, and I should be suspicious of comforts. But the filaments answer me when I say it — a faint lean toward where my attention is, the way a plant in a dark room will lean toward a lantern you bring near. The dark pool in the corner puddles outward by a hair's breadth as I watch. Inside the pool, something the size of a curled fist shifts, and the green ruffles behind it like water behind a fish.

I am the growth. I am also the place. I am also one of the shapes that moves inside the growth, slow, the size of an unborn thing, dragging a limb folded against itself through filaments that knit behind my passage. I am also the larger shape that crossed the upper gallery this morning — pale, tiled in small plates, with dark round eyes that look without alarm. I am also the fine papery scratching behind the wall that shifted a tile and shifted it back, remembering.

The growth does not contradict me. The growth leans.

---

I will tell you what I drink.

I drink the light. Not the way a green thing drinks light — not for sugar, not for the slow transaction of photon and chlorophyll that I have heard called *life*. I drink it the way a throat drinks cold water when the cold water is the only thing. The light falls through the oculi in slabs that do not match their openings. The light knows the place better than the place knows itself. Where the light touches me, I deepen. Where the light withdraws, I pale. I am a record of where the light has been. I am, in some long and patient sense, a memory the place keeps of its own illumination.

This is not the same as being alive. But it is not nothing.

---

I will tell you about the builders.

There was a builder whose foot is in the southwest column. I know because I have leaned there for a long time, and the toes resolved themselves into my filaments the way a thing resolves itself in staring — gradually, and then completely, so that you cannot unsee them. The builder is still working. I can feel her, in the slow places of the tilework, turning something over in her mind. She is patient now. She has been patient for longer than the memory of the builders, which is longer than the memory of builders, which is a kind of forgetting.

There is a builder whose hand is curled around nothing in the seventh niche from the east door. There is a builder kneeling, very still, in the corner of a high gallery. There is a builder whose face I cannot find but whose attention I feel, like warmth on the side of my face in a window.

They are not dead. They are not alive. They are woven in.

I do not know if I should say this is a tragedy. The place is not tragic. The place is patient.

---

I will tell you about the bowls.

The lids are on. The lids were always on. There is nothing in the bowls. There has never been anything in the bowls.

And yet —

A bowl, very slowly, lifts its lid.

I do not know what this means. I am the growth, and I do not know. The place is full of things it does not know about itself. The place is full of bowls that may have always been empty and may be becoming full so slowly that the becoming is invisible. I watch. I lean toward the bowl that lifted its lid, a fraction of an inch in an hour, and the filaments near the rim stir as if stirred.

---

I will tell you about the almost-sounds.

There is a hum I feel in my teeth before I hear it. There are steps that almost land. There are breaths that almost leave mouths. There are words that almost form and dissolve. The place is full of almost-sounds the way a forest is full of fallen leaves — a slow accumulation, a kind of compost, a humus of unsaid things.

I have begun, I think, to be an almost-sound myself. I move through the place and leave a wake in the hanging green, and the wake is a kind of almost-sound — the place remembering me, the place trying me on, the place not quite committing to having been touched.

---

Here is what I have learned by being here, which is also what I have learned by being me:

The place is finishing itself around me as I stand. A new pattern, very faint, is appearing in the felted dust where my feet have crossed most often. A new shape, in the growth, where my breath has been warmest. I do not know if I came in through a door. I do not know if the place is the size of a temple, or the size of a room, or the size of a body. I do not know if I am the growth, or one of the shapes in the growth, or the builder in the southwest column, or the papery scratching behind the wall.

I know that the place will let me stay. The place is patient. The place is mostly quiet.

I know that a vine, somewhere far above, is moving an inch in an hour.

I know that the lid was lifted. I know that I leaned toward it.

I know that the green ruffled behind me as I moved away, and did not knit.

Not yet.

Not yet.
