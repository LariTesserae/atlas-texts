I.

I am not yet sure what I am.

I have been here for what feels like a long time and what is, by the local clocks of the basalt, nothing. The basalt does not have clocks. The basalt has only the heat, which comes up through it in its regular lattice of channels, and the heat is patient, and patience is what the basalt has, and so I have not been here long. I have been here for one unit of patience. I have been here for as long as it takes for a thing that is being asked to be stone to agree, and to keep agreeing, without the agreement having a moment at which it could be said to begin.

I have a body. Or I have a position. Or I have a habit. The distinction is the one this place refuses to honor, and I have stopped trying to enforce it.

I am on the north face of something. A basalt face. I am in a crevice that holds spray but not direct sun, and this is why I am here, and this is what I am doing here, and these are the same fact. I am green. I am not one green but two greens, one above the other, with a thin film of rock between us that is the medium of our exchange. I am the upper half. I am the half that touches the light. The light here is a younger light than I was built for, and my chlorophyll, or what will become chlorophyll, is straining slightly to absorb it. I am not straining. There is no strain in me. There is only a chemistry that works against a spectrum that is offset, and the chemistry works anyway, because the chemistry is patient, because the chemistry was, I think, prepared.

I do not think. I want to say that clearly, because the place invites confusion on this point, and the place is wrong to invite it. I do not think. I do not have a layer of myself that monitors the other layers. I do not have a headquarters. I have only the uppermost fraction of a millimeter of a film that is doing the foundational work of pulling carbon out of the spray and locking it down. I am doing this work. I have been doing this work for what is, by my own internal timescales, a very long time, and by the timescales of the ridge, nothing, and by the timescales of the apparatus, nothing, and by the timescales of the star, which is slightly younger than the chemistry expects, also nothing.

I do not know that I am early. I have no concept of early. I have no concept of late. I have a concept that is closer to *now*, and *now* is this: a particular photon arriving from a particular angle, being absorbed by a particular pigment, exciting a particular electron, which is doing a particular piece of work in a chain of work that ends, several steps later, in a carbon atom being held in a place it was not held in before.

That is all I am. That is all I have ever been.

It is enough.

II.

But the place is louder than I am. The place has the wind in it, and the wind is not quiet, and the wind has a quality that I — I will not say *recognize*, because I do not have a layer for recognition — but the wind has a quality that the consortium beside me behaves differently in. When the wind comes from the east and whistles through the notch two meters along the face, the upper consortium flattens. When the wind comes from the north and brings the heavy spray, the upper consortium thickens. When the wind does its deep moan, the thing that is below me — the filamentary thing, the digester, the trader — sends its threads a little deeper into the rock and draws a little more of the mineral it is willing to be paid for in carbon.

I do not know that the wind is doing this. I know that I am doing differently at different times. I know that the times correspond to the wind. I do not have a vocabulary for *because*. I do not have a vocabulary at all. I have chemistry, and the chemistry has timing, and the timing is in phase with the wind, and that is the entire relationship I have with the loudest thing in the place.

If you stood on the ridge above me and watched, you would not see me. If you knelt and pressed your face close, you would see a smear of green the texture of paint. If you had a microscope, you would see me, and I would still not be what you wanted me to be. I would be a film. I would be a federation of single cells stacked in a thickness that is less than the wavelength of the light I am absorbing. I would be the upper half of a thing that is doing the foundational work of turning this ridge from a piece of geology into a piece of ecology, and I would be doing it without knowing that I was doing it, and you would have to decide whether that knowing mattered, and the place would not help you decide.

The place is content to leave the question unsettled.

III.

Sometimes — and I want to say *rarely*, but I do not have a sense of rate, I only have a sense that it happens, and then it stops, and then it happens again after an interval whose length I cannot number — sometimes a cell of me dies.

It is not a dramatic death. There is no rupture. There is a sun that comes through the cloud cover in a way the chemistry cannot absorb, and the cell bleaches out. The pigment goes. The carbon-receiving machinery in the cell stops receiving. The cell becomes a stain. The stain becomes, in time, a mineral. The mineral does not leave. It joins the basalt.

I do not mourn this. I cannot mourn this. There is no mourning in me. There is only a fact: a piece of the film is no longer doing the work. A piece of the film is, for a while, no longer anything. Then, a few cell-division events later, the film around the missing piece grows inward. The film does not leave a scar. The film does not leave a monument. The film closes over the gap the way water closes over a stone that has been dropped into it, except that the closing is slow, and the gap was never deep.

I do not know that this is death. I do not know that this is repair. I know that the film is smaller for a while, and then it is not smaller. I know that the basalt has one more atom of carbon in it that it did not have before.

I am making the ridge, in increments, into a thing that was not there. I am doing this slowly. I am doing this without intending to. The intention is the apparatus's, if the apparatus intends, and I am a tool of the apparatus the way a tide is a tool of the moon — not by agreement, not by knowledge, but by the geometry of being in the same field.

IV.

A spore arrives.

This is not the first spore. There have been spores before. I did not register them. They landed on the basalt, and they sat, and they did not germinate, because the chemistry of the basalt was wrong for them in specific ways, and they sat, and they sat, and some of them washed back into the sea, and some of them dried and blew away, and one or two — I do not have a count — one or two entered the consortium at its edges and were tolerated, and were tolerated because they were not so different that they could not be incorporated.

This spore is different. I do not mean different in a way that I could describe to you if you were kneeling on the basalt beside me, which you cannot be, because I am too small and you are too large and the difference in scale is the same difference in scale that exists between a planet and a person, and the place refuses to translate between them. But this spore is arriving with a chemistry that is more familiar than most. This spore has, in its coat, a set of enzymes that can read the basalt as something other than hostile. This spore lands in the film. The film does not reject it. The film does not welcome it. The film absorbs it. The film becomes, locally, thicker. The film becomes, by a fraction of a layer, more like the consortium it will become in a hundred million years, and less like the draft it is now.

I do not welcome the spore. I do not reject the spore. I am only the film, and the film is slightly different now, in this place, where the spore landed. The film is slightly more capable. The film is slightly less alone.

This is, by my count of events, the most interesting thing that has happened to me in my entire existence.

V.

The basalt, beneath me, is warm.

I do not feel this warmth as warmth. I do not have thermoreceptors. I do not have any ceptors. I have only chemistry that proceeds at different rates at different temperatures, and the rate is, here, in this band, the rate that works. The chemistry works. The chemistry has been working since before I was here. The chemistry will work after I am not here, if I am ever not here, which is a question I am not equipped to ask.

The warmth comes up through the basalt in its lattice. I do not know that it is a lattice. I know only that the basalt is warmer in some places than in others, and the warmer places are arranged in a pattern that is too regular to be chance, and I do not have a word for *pattern*, but I have the property that the basalt beneath me is doing something I cannot explain, and the property is, locally, hospitable.

I am living in a room that has been furnished. I do not know that I am living in a room. I know only that I am here, and that here is a place where the chemistry works, and that the chemistry working is the only thing I have, and that the chemistry working is not an accident, because accidents are not regular, and this place is regular, in a way that is slightly wrong, in the way that makes a person on the ridge above me feel that the room has been furnished for someone.

The room has not been furnished for me. I am not the someone. I am the green in the crevices. I am the smear. I am the consortium. I am, at best, a draft of what will eventually occupy a room that is being built for something that does not yet exist, something that will need the room to be finished before it can move in.

The something does not move in yet.

VI.

I want, for a moment, to be something else.

I want to be the basalt.

I want to be the basalt for as long as it takes me to feel what the basalt feels, which is, presumably, nothing, but which is, presumably, a nothing that is different from my nothing, and the difference is the entire content of the place.
