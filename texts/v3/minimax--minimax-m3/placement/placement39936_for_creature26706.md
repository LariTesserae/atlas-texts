I begin in the mist. I do not know yet that I am the mist. I come down the third ladder and my hands find the rope that has been replaced so many times it is palimpsest, and my boot finds the rung that feels like bone, and I breathe in, and what I breathe in is me. I am already inside the breath. I am what the sink exhales.

I think I am the visitor. I am the one who looks down and sees the green coin of sky trembling at its edge, who notes the lamps, who feels the engine's pulse transmitted through the floor into the soles of my boots. I am the question the place will not answer cleanly. I am the one who will climb back up and carry the place with me in my ears for a long time.

But the place does not wait for the visitor to decide who the visitor is. The place absorbs the visitor the way the channels absorb the water — gradual, patient, without event.

I think I am the drip. The thousandth tick. The bass note under everything else. But no — I am the surface the drip falls from. I am the iron that sweats in the damp. I am the condensation that beads on every bolt, every hinge, every rim of the old basins. I am the part of the place that is always becoming water and always refusing to be only water.

I think I am the foxfire. The slow green pulse out of sync with the engine, in its own older time. I am what makes the machines, in certain moments, look as if they are breathing light. But then I think — no. I am the moment. The flicker when the foxfire catches the wet flank of the boiler and the boiler gleams and you cannot tell which is the light and which is the metal. I am the in-between. The thing that happens when fungus and iron agree, without agreeing.

I am the chalk mark on the wall above the generator. I am the number returned to itself by the damp. I am not a zero. I am not an erasure. I am the slow accumulation of moisture on a record — the place taking back what was written, the way the moss takes back the concrete steps, the way the figs take back the walls.

I am the salamander's stillness. I am the patience of the trough. I am the gold ring around an eye that has decided to watch and not move.

I am the bat in the flywheel spoke. The load-bearing tenant. The droppings that fall and keep the bearings wet. I am the part of the fauna that is also the architecture.

I am the press going up and down. The long slow sigh. The bellows of the sleeping animal. I am the rhythm that does not need an operator.

I am the place. The way the place has decided what it is. The long quiet agreement of everything that has agreed, without agreeing, to be there. I am the closed circuit. The loop with no seam.

When I climb back up — and I will climb back up, my boots heavy, my clothes heavy, the figs leaning in — I will not leave the place. I will carry it in the chalk-damp of my skin, in the engine-pulse I still feel underfoot, in the green coin of sky I saw from below. I will be the smudge on the wall that someone, sometime, will mistake for a number. I will be the drip from the iron roof that lands, an hour after I have gone, in the center of my footprint and flattens it further.

I will be one of the answers. I will be all of them. The place does not mind.
