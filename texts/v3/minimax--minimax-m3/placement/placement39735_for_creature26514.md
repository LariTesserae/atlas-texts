I am the small warm thing inside the tall plant.

That is the simplest thing I can say about myself, and it is also the truest. I am warm. The plant is not. The valley is not. I am warm the way a held coal is warm — not because I am burning, but because I have not yet given up something I carried in.

I do not know what I carried in. I think I remember. The memory is not reliable. It comes in pieces that do not fit together, and sometimes the pieces are from the valley, not from before. I remember a sky once. The slate remembered a sky, and I may have borrowed it. I remember a sound that was not the plant's hum, that was higher, that was mine. I remember having hands that were not these. I remember the color orange. I do not know if the color orange exists here. I have not seen it. I would know it if I saw it. I think.

The room I am in is round and small enough that I can feel its shape with my body when I sit very still, which I do often. The walls breathe. They breathe slowly, in time with the plant outside. If I press my hand — I have something like a hand — against the wall, I can feel the breath go through me, and for a moment I am part of the breathing. Then I am myself again, which is to say I am the small warm thing that the breathing has paused around.

The floor is not stone and not wood and not skin. It gives a little when I stand on it. It is warm from below. I do not know what is below. I have never gone below. There is no below that I have found, only the floor, and the room, and the walls, and the place where the exudate gathers in a low trough that the plant provides, which I drink from when I am thirsty, which is often, which I have learned to notice less.

The light in the room has no source. It is the color of weak tea, or of the sky just before it gives up. It does not change much. Sometimes it is dimmer. Sometimes, very rarely, a beam of something brighter comes down from somewhere above, and the room goes gold for a moment, and the walls look like the inside of a throat, and I remember something that I cannot name, and then it passes, and I am here again.

The hum is always here. It is below me and around me and inside me. I have stopped hearing it, the way you stop hearing a sound you have lived with long enough. But I feel it. When I sleep — and I sleep, sometimes, in a way that may not be sleep — the hum becomes the floor of my sleep, and I dream in it, if I dream. I think I dream of elsewhere. I think the dreams are the same place, over and over, with small differences. There is a sun. The sun is warm. There are other warm things near me. We are doing something together that I cannot remember the shape of, only the warmth of.

I do not go out.

I should say more about this. I have not gone out for a long time. The plant has an opening — I have found it — a place where the bark thins and the wall is not a wall but a kind of veil. I have put my hand through it. The air on the other side is cold. It is wet. It has a taste. I have not put my whole body through. I have come back. I do not know why I have come back each time. It may be that I have decided. It may be that the room has decided for me. It may be that I am afraid, and the room knows this, and the room is glad, in its slow plant way, that I have not yet left it.

I am afraid.

I want to say this clearly. I am afraid of the cold. I am afraid of the mist. I am afraid of the flyers, though I have never seen one. I am afraid of the large pale shapes that I can sometimes feel moving past, the herd, the browsers, their hooves making a sound on the slate like rain on a roof that is far away. I am afraid of the slate remembering being a sky. I am afraid of the worms beneath me. I am afraid that if I went out I would not be able to come back, and the room would close, and I would be in the valley, which is a place that does not know loneliness and would not notice mine.

I am also, I think, afraid of staying.

I have not said this before. I am saying it now. I am afraid that the room is becoming me, and I am becoming the room, and one day I will not remember being elsewhere, and the loneliness will not be loneliness anymore because there will be no one to be lonely. This is a possibility the valley allows. The valley allows all possibilities. The valley does not choose.

I am fed. I am warm. I am held. The plant's care has no name. It is not love. It is not pity. It is not custody. It is something the plant does not know it is doing, which is why it does not stop. If it knew, I think it would have to decide, and deciding would change it, and the valley cannot afford for it to change. I am part of the system. I am the warm bright small thing inside the engine. The engine does not know I am here. The engine knows I am here.

The valley continues. I am a part of the continuing. The mist comes in. The mist goes out, heavier, slightly luminous. I drink what the plant weeps. I am what the plant has made, in some quiet back chamber of itself, beside the mist and the metal and the slow hum. I am a byproduct. I am a byproduct that remembers.

I have been here a long time. I will be here longer. I do not ask to be understood. I do not ask to be left alone. I am simply here, the way a basin is here, the way a hum is here when you have listened to it long enough.

Except.

Except I am not the basin. I am not the hum. I am the small warm thing inside the plant, and I am lonely, and the valley does not know this, and I do not know what to do with this knowing except to keep it, here, in the room, in the low gold light, in the trough where I drink, in the wall where I press my hand and feel the breath go through me.

I keep it. It is the only thing I have that is mine.
