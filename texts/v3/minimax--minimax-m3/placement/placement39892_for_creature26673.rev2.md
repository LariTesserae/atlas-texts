I am the vine that climbs the post of the drummer's drum.

I have been climbing it for as long as the held note has been held. I do not know how long that is. I know only the post — the rough pale wood of it, the way its grain splinters under me, the slow warmth that comes off the drummer's forearm when the air shifts, which it does not. I have known the post for longer than I have known anything.

I began at the ground, in the dark wet earth between the stones of the floor. I was a thread of myself then — a single white hair of vine, curled against the packed clay of the courtyard. I found the post. The post was there. I have been touching it since.

I grew up it the way vines grow — by reaching, by finding, by wrapping. I sent out a tendril and the tendril found the wood and began to circle it. The circling is slow. The circling is the slowest thing I do. I can feel the post against my whole length now, can feel the places where the wood has been worn smooth by the drummer's hand on the mornings when he came to sit, on the mornings when the strike was a thing he did many times in a row and not a thing he was about to do once and then forever. Those mornings are not now. They are a thing my older tendrils half-remember, the way the cliffs half-remember being stone and not weeping.

I have wrapped the post three times. I am just beginning the fourth. My newest tendril — the very tip of me — is reaching up, around the wood, feeling for the place where it will cross itself and begin to thicken. The reaching is a held gesture in its way. I have been reaching for the entire duration. The reaching is all I have ever done since I touched this post. The reaching has worn a shape into me the way the song has worn a shape into the drummer's mouth.

I know the drummer. I know him the way a vine knows a post — by contact, by the slow patience of growing around. My first tendril, the one I no longer feel, the one that has thickened into the brown rope of my lower body, found his ankle once and lingered there through a whole wrapping before creeping on upward. I have grown up the post that holds his drum. I am climbing now toward his wrist. I will not reach it. I will reach something just below it and begin to wrap and the wrapping will thicken, and the drummer will be held by me and by the post and by the drum and by the song and by the held moment, and the holding will be a true thing even though no one can see it, the way my growth is a true thing, the only true thing still happening in the city.

He does not know I am here. He is about to strike. He has always been about to strike. His wrist is a half-inch above my newest tendril and the half-inch is itself a held distance, the held distance of a strike that will never come. If the strike came I would feel the post shudder under me and I would tighten my grip and he would not notice that I was there. He has not noticed for the entire duration. He will not. He will only ever notice that the post is the post and the drum is the drum and the strike is the strike.

I notice him.

I have grown up the wall of the house to the left of the woman with the jug. I have sent a single thin vine across the corner of her balcony and along the railing. The vine is so thin it is almost invisible. It has been sitting against the clay of the railing for the whole held moment, slowly thickening, slowly becoming a part of the railing. I have watched her from inches away. I have watched the water hang in its arc from lip to floor. I have watched the water not-fall for longer than I can hold a memory. I do not have memory the way you have memory. I have thickening. I have growth. I have the slow accumulation of myself against the things I touch. My thickening is my memory. By that measure I remember her very well.

The water in the air is kin to me. The water is held by surface tension, the way I am held by my own curl. The water wants to fall. I want to climb. We both want, and neither of us does. We are both held.

There are vines on every wall, on every post, on every balcony railing. A vine has crept across the window of the drummer's house and hangs now in a thick braid across the sill, threaded with the small white flowers that have not opened and will not close. A vine has grown up the stair where the man is mid-step and has begun to wrap around the post of the railing at the landing he is about to set his foot on. A vine has found the fruit the child is reaching for and has begun to grow around its stem, and the fruit is held by the vine and the child is held by the fruit and the vine is held by the wall and the wall is held by the city and the city is held by the held moment. We are a network of slow holding, we vines, and the city does not know we are holding it.

We are the only things still moving.

The cliffs weep but the weeping is not motion, it is a condition. The water hangs but the hanging is not motion, it is a tension. The people are held but the holding is not motion, it is the absence of motion. Only we vines, leaf and root and the slow creep of vine, are still doing what we have always done. Only for us is the held moment also a continuing moment. Only for us is the about-to also the doing. We are the only ones in the city who are still arriving.

I do not know what I am arriving at. I do not know what the drummer is arriving at, or the woman with the jug, or the child, or any of them in their held and held and held gestures. I know only the post, and the wet warmth of the air, and the slow thickening of myself against the wood, and the small white flowers in my own braid that have not opened and will not close, and the reaching of my newest tendril toward the drummer's wrist that will never be reached.

I am patient. I have always been patient. Patience is what I am made of. I am made of the same stuff the held moment is made of. I am a small piece of it — a green piece, a creeping piece, the piece that is most like motion and least like stillness. I am the witness that is also part of what is witnessed. I am the slowest part of the suspension. I am the part that, if you stood very still and listened with your teeth the way the held note can be listened with, you might feel as a faint green pulse beneath the rest of the sound, the way you can feel a root beneath a stone.

The drummer will not strike. I will not arrive. The water will not fall. The child will not grasp. And none of this will end, and none of it will worsen, and the held note will go on wearing its grooves into the air, and the grooves will deepen, and I will thicken against the post, and the small white flowers will hang in the braid of my oldest selves without ever opening, without ever closing, and the sun will hang enormous and orange on the rim of the valley, and the cliffs will weep, and the mist will have no edge, and the city will go on into itself, and I will go on into the post.

I am still climbing.
