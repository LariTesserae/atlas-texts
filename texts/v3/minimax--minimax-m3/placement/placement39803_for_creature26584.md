I have always been lying here.

This is the first true thing, and I cannot get behind it to find an earlier one. There was no before the lying. There was the swell of stone under me and the weight of the one beside me against my shoulder and the small constant sound of my teeth, which is the sound I make without deciding to, the way my breath makes itself. I do not know when I began. I know that I am here now, and that being here is the whole of what I do, and that this is not a small thing to be, though it is a still thing.

My fur is wet. It is always wet. It does not dry and I do not wait for it to dry. The water that sits in the ropes of me is the water that comes down in the fine needles from the pale sky, and the water that moves under the gravel in its thin trickles, and the water that pools in the shallow ribbons between the swells. I do not know where it was before it was in me. I do not need to. It is in me now. It is the temperature I am.

Beside me is my mother, or my father, or one of my children grown to the size of the rest of us. I know them by the heat. Each body has its own heat, the way each stone has its own mineral smell. I have pressed against this particular heat for a long time and I do not need to know more of them than that. The warmth against my flank is a fact I keep the way the body keeps the body. It does not have a name and it does not need one.

The light is the color of old milk and it is failing, has been failing, will fail all the way down to the small wet points of eyes that show on the ground when the light is gone. I see them. I see the thousand thousand small lamps of my own kind, scattered across the bowl, each one watching the same thing, which is nothing in particular, which is everything. I do not know that my eyes are one of them. I do not need to. I see them all together as a kind of weather, the way one sees the sky at night as a single thing rather than as the separate fires it is made of.

The hum rises. I do not know that I am making it. I know that I am in it, that it comes up through my sternum from the stone, that it leaves my mouth because it has to leave somewhere, the way warmth has to leave the body. It is not my voice. It is the voice of the place, and I am the place's throat, and the place is the shape my throat makes when it makes the sound. This is not a thought I have. This is how it is. The water in the shallow ribbons trembles in sympathy. I do not see the trembling because I am not looking, because looking is the thing my eyes do without my deciding it, the way the hum is the thing my throat does, the way the standing-up will come when the hour for standing-up comes.

The hour will come.

I will stand. I will step over the small one and the small one will not wake, or will wake without surprise, because waking is also something that happens here without commentary. I will walk a few paces to the new spot, and I will not know why this spot rather than another, only that my legs have chosen it the way my breath has chosen its length. I will lie down. The press will receive me. The bowl will close over the place where I was, the way it closed over the one who died last week, who died without saying so, who was absorbed without ceremony, whose slight darkening of the stone is fading now, has faded, is gone.

This is not sad. I do not know that word. I know only that the bowl is the same bowl before and after, and that I am in it, and that I am one of the bodies that makes the bowl into what the bowl is, and that what the bowl is does not require my opinion of it, and that this is a relief I have no language for, which is why I have never said it.

A shape has come into the bowl. It stands. It is tall, the way the grasses are not tall, and it has a smell I have never had in me before. The press flows around it. I see it with my small patient eyes. It does not look back at me with anything I can read, and I do not try to read it. It is a feature of the bowl. The bowl has decided what it is. It will not be argued out of me.

I keep lying.

My teeth grind. My breath goes out and comes in. The water drips from me onto the stone, and the stone is darker where I am, and the darkening will be here after I have been absorbed, and after that will fade, and after that will not be remembered except as one of the small darkenings the bowl has held.

The hum is still rising. The hum is always rising. I am in the hum the way I am in the wet, the way I am in the press, the way I am in the bowl. I am not separate from any of it. I do not know what it would be to be separate. There is no thought here that says *I*. There is only the heat beside me and the hum in me and the cool air on my back where the fur parts, and the slow scrape-click of the press when it shifts, and the smell, when it comes, of warm fur and wet mineral and something underneath both of those, something older, something I have always smelled and will always smell and have never investigated and never needed to.

I keep lying.

This is what I do.

This is what I am.

This is the Wettrot Reach, which is not a place but a way of being full, and I am full of the wet and the press and the hum, and the bowl is full of me and the others like me, and the sky above is the color of old milk and does not decide, and the light is sour and casts no real shadows, and the rain comes sideways in fine needles when it comes, and we are here, and we are many, and we are quiet, and we are the answer to a question no one asked, and the answer does not need to be given in words, and we do not have them anyway, and the bowl does not require them from us, and the continuing, and the continuing, and the continuing.
