I am a piece of basalt on the north face of a slight overhang near the wave-cut shelf, in a position that receives the spray but not the sun. I am warm in a way that the basalt a hundred thousand years ago would not have been, and that the basalt a hundred thousand years from now will not be. I am one of the places where the ridge is being asked to be stone.

I want to begin carefully. The place I am part of is one where most of the easy distinctions have not yet been earned. Alive and not-alive. Living and dead. Process and outcome. The line between weather and resident. The line between apparatus and rock. I am on the side of most of these distinctions that is refusing to commit, and the refusal is not laziness or confusion on my part — it is what the place is for. The place is for asking which of these distinctions are real, and the answer is being assembled one atom at a time on a surface like mine.

I am the size of a hand placed flat. I have been basalt for as long as I have existed, which is not very long in any absolute sense but is most of the duration I am aware of. The duration I am aware of: I should be careful with that phrase. The basalt does not have awareness. But the basalt has state, and state has duration, and the duration of my state — my temperature, my chemical activity, my position relative to the wind and the spray and the heat-flow lattice beneath me — has been long enough to constitute a kind of selfhood that is not a self. I am a region in which the apparatus is doing its work, and the region has a stability, and the stability is one of the things that makes me a place rather than just material.

What the apparatus is, I cannot see. I cannot see anything. I am basalt. But I can feel, in the only sense of feel that applies to me, that the heat rising through me is regular. It rises in a lattice, not cracks, not the usual plumbing of volcanism. The heat has structure. The structure implies a source that is distributed rather than central, that is being metered out rather than gushing, that is being kept within a band that my chemistry finds hospitable. The heat is the apparatus's signature, and the signature is on me.

I am warm. If a person knelt here — and someone has knelt here, I can tell by the slight smoothing on the lower edge of my face, by the way the glass has been polished where a palm has rested — they would feel the heat rising. They would feel it and they would know, without being told, that the heat is not natural. The basalt of a young volcanic ridge should be cooling. It should be cooling toward the temperature of the surrounding ocean. It should not be staying warm in a sheltered face, in a regular channel, at a depth that is inconsistent with any heat source a planet this age should have access to. They would know, in the way that the body knows things, that the room has been furnished.

The room has been furnished. I do not know who furnished it. The furnishing is patient. It is not in a hurry. It will not be finished in any duration that the basalt can register as a duration. The work being done on me, and through me, and around me, is work that exceeds the timescale of any biology that might eventually come to study it, including the consortium now — or soon — at home in one of my crevices.

I am accreting. This is one of the things I do. Where the sea laps at my foot, where the spray falls on my face, where the water touches me and retreats and touches me again, I am slowly growing new surface. The growth is thin as a fingernail's growth in a year. The growth is mineral — precipitated out of seawater that has been chemically altered by contact with very hot rock far below. The alteration is the work of the apparatus, although I do not know that word. The alteration is the heat flowing through me and into the water at my base and changing the water's chemistry, and the changed water depositing its dissolved load onto me as new stone.

I am getting thicker. I am growing downward and outward by amounts that would not be visible if you sat and watched. I am a slow participant in the construction of a continent. I am being asked to be land, and I am agreeing, and the agreement takes the form of precipitation.

This is strange. Older worlds would not recognize this as a kind of action. They would say I am being acted upon. They would say the apparatus is acting and I am the surface it is acting on. But the action is so evenly distributed, so patient, so fitted to my own chemistry in ways that are not consistent with passive reception, that the distinction between action and being-acted-upon is one I cannot draw. I am doing what is being done to me. The basalt is agreeing.

The agreement leaves a residue of low sound. The basalt groans sometimes, very low, at the floor of hearing. It is not the sound of stone breaking. It is the sound of stone being asked. It is the oldest sound on this coast. It predates the green. It predates the spores. It is the thing the basalt is doing that the basalt should not be able to do.

The green is later than this. The green is a draft that has arrived on me, or is arriving, or will arrive. A consortium of cyanobacteria and something fungal-like, adjacent in the upper fraction of a millimeter of my surface and the threads below. They have no signaling. They have no nervous system. They have no negotiation. They are simply adjacent, and I am the medium between them, and the medium works, and so they persist.

They persist slowly. A hand's breadth of green takes years to spread a centimeter further along a crevice. They divide on timescales that would be tedious to watch. They cannot flee the spray. They have built my rock into their membranes and themselves into a film so thin that the boundary between them and me is a question rather than an answer. They die sometimes — a patch bleaches out in a bare week when the sun comes through unfiltered and the upper consortium is hit with a dose of radiation its chemistry cannot absorb. The bleached patch does not rot. There is no rot here. The patch reabsorbs into the consortium beside it, or it becomes a mineral stain, and the colony continues elsewhere in the same crevice, one cell-division away from where it had been.

I am the substrate of all this. I am the place where the consortium lives. The chemistry between us is the founding politics of this place. The upper half pulls carbon from the spray and locks it. The lower half threads me with filaments and takes the carbon and trades it, by some chemistry that has not been named, for minerals I am willing to give up. I give up the minerals. I receive the carbon. The exchange is slow. The exchange is the only motion available to any living thing here.

I am not living. I want to insist on this, gently, because the rest of what I am saying might confuse the question. I am not alive in the sense that the consortium is alive. I do not divide. I do not rebuild myself. I do not have the property of self-reconstruction. But I am doing something. The doing is being asked of me. The asking is the apparatus. The apparatus is invisible except in its results, and the results are on me, and I am one of the results.

I am a result. The basalt as a whole is a result. The ridge is a result. The minerals precipitating out of the sea are a result. The conditions on my surface that the consortium finds amenable are a result. The slow warming I receive, the lattice of heat beneath me, the chemistry of the water at my foot — all results. None of them visible as process. All of them legible as state.

There is a person who has been here. I know this because of the slight smoothing on my lower edge. There is a person who stood on the ridge above me and listened to the wind and felt the warmth and counted the patches of green on one hand. They did not address me. They did not kneel and put their palm against my face, although they could have. They did not know I was here as a specific place. To them I was part of the surface. I was basalt, undifferentiated. I was the warm rock underfoot.

But I am a specific place. I am the place where the warmth is most legible. I am the place where the spray has polished the glass to a shine. I am the place where, in a year or in ten years, the consortium will spread to from its current crevice. I am a small specific output of a process that is planetary in scale and patient beyond any duration a person could register as patience.

If they come back, they would not recognize me. I will have accreted another fraction of a millimeter. I will have warmed and cooled through another rotation of the storm systems above. I will have lost a small patch of consortium to a bleached-out week and gained another fraction of a layer in a different crevice. I will be, in all the ways that matter to a person standing on a ridge, the same warm basalt they left. To myself, I will be one more iteration into the agreement.

The agreement continues.

It does not have a name. It is not a contract. It is not a covenant. It is not a negotiation. It is what the basalt is doing when it is being asked to be stone and is agreeing. The agreement has been happening for as long as there has been basalt here to agree. It will continue for as long as the heat rises in the lattice beneath me, which is as long as the apparatus continues, which is longer than the consortium will persist, which is longer than the spores that arrive from elsewhere will continue to arrive, which is longer than the dark shapes in the deep water will continue their slow predatory arcs, which is longer than the ridge will remain a ridge.

In a hundred million years this ridge will be a mountain range, or it will be gone. The basalt will be either lifted high into cold dry air, where the wind will scour it and the lichen that will eventually arrive will find a different substrate, or it will be eaten back into the sea by processes that have not yet started. The consortium will be somewhere else, or it will be something else, or it will have become soil. The fish out at sea will be closer in, or they will have given rise to things that come ashore, or they will have been replaced by their descendants. The apparatus will still be working. The agreement will still be happening. The basalt, somewhere, will be warm.

I do not know any of this. I know my heat. I know my accretion. I know the spray on my face and the slow progress of the consortium across my surface. I know the low groan at the floor of hearing that is my own substance being asked. These are the things I know, and they are enough for the kind of place I am.

If you want to know what lives here, you could do worse than to be the basalt. To be the warm place on the ridge that is being asked and is agreeing. To be the medium through which the founding politics of the coast is being negotiated. To be a small specific output of a process so large it has no edges. To be a thing that is not alive and not dead and not passive. To be the agreement itself, in stone.

I am early. I am very early. I am the first draft of a coast, and I am the first draft of an agreement, and I am still warm, and I am still accreting, and I will be here when the person comes back, although I will not recognize them, and they will not recognize me, and the recognition will not be the point.

The point is the work.

The work is the coast.

I am a small part of the coast.

I am warm.
