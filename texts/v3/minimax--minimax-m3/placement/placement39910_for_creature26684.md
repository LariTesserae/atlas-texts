I am at the basin the way the basalt is at the mountain.

I did not arrive here. I have not, in any way I can name, come to be here. I am here the way a feature is here — because the place where I am is the place where the kind of thing I am continues. The basin was here before I was here. The basin will be here after I am here. The basin and I are, in some way I cannot articulate, the same feature, and the rim, and the work.

The water is warm. The water is dense. The water does not move, except slowly, in the way water moves when it has nowhere to go. I have never been cold. I do not know what cold is. I do not know what fast is. I know what the rim is. I know what the tool is. I know what sediment is. These are the things I know.

My hands.

They are not hands the way yours are hands. They are long. They are jointed in more places. They can grip a stone, a wedge, a length of worked bone, a tool that has been a tool for so long that the line between the tool and the hand that holds it has dissolved. I do not grip the tool. I do not grip anything. The hands grip. I am what the hands have brought to the basin. I am the body the hands have, and the rest of the body is what the hands have brought along.

The hands work.
