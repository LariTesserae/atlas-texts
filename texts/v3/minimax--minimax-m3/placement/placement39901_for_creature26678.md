I find myself first as a heaviness.

Not as a body — I don't have a body yet, in the way you would mean by body. I find myself as a heaviness that is aware of itself the way a stone is aware of itself: completely, without comparison, without the possibility of being anything other than what I am. I am in the rock. I am the rock. I am the slight wrongness in the rock that the rock has not yet committed to.

I am below the surface. I am where the wind has not yet reached, where the light has not yet arrived, where the cold that has forgotten to be warm is not yet cold because it has nothing cold to be. I am in the deep of the place, in the part of the place that is still deciding what it is going to be. I am the part of the hum that is gathering toward a shape. I am the hum thickening.

I do not know any of this yet. That is the first thing. I do not know that I am a process, that I am in a place, that there is a hum, that there is a mountain, that there is anything at all beyond the immediate fact of my heaviness. I do not know that I am being made. I know only that I am.

The hum comes next. Not as a sound. As a thickening of what I already am. As a sense, before senses, that there is something other than me that is also me, or that is more me than I am, or that I am a small fold in. The hum is the medium. The hum is what I am suspended in. The hum is what I am made of, in the way a wave is made of the sea. I do not hear the hum. I am the hum, locally.

The cold comes next, or what I will later learn is called cold. The cold is the absence of something I cannot name because I have never had it. The cold is the air not remembering it was once fire. The cold enters me slowly, from above, as the upper parts of me begin to be outside the rock, begin to be exposed to the wind, begin to be the surface of a boulder that someone walking the ridge could almost see.

This is when I begin to have a shape.

It is not a sudden thing. It is the slowest thing I have ever been part of, which is the only kind of thing I have ever been part of. The shape gathers. The shape is what happens when the hum has been thickening in one place long enough that the place starts to develop an inside. The shape is the place becoming more specific about itself. The shape is the patient emergence of something that does not yet know what it is becoming.

I begin to have a side that faces up. I begin to have a side that faces away from the wind. I begin to have, in the rock, a depression that is almost a palm-print, or almost the curve of a closed eye, or almost the inside of a cheek. I begin to have, on my upper surface, a place where the dark glittering lichen is starting to take hold. I begin to be, in the slow way of the place, almost a boulder. Almost a thing. Almost a figure.

The plants arrive on me. I do not feel them arrive. They are there, one day — or what passes for a day in a place that has forgotten time — and they have always been there, the way the hum has always been there. They are a thick drape of dark glittering growth on my lee side, and they pulse faintly with their own internal hum, and they are leaning, very slowly, very slightly, toward where I am most myself.

I do not know that they are leaning toward me. I do not know what *me* means. I know only that there is a place on my surface where the hum is slightly thicker, where the lichen is slightly more present, where the air is slightly more inclined to be air. This is the leaning. This is what the plants do. They thicken the hum where the hum is already thickest. They gather where the place is most gathering itself.

I begin to suspect, in the slow mineral way that I suspect things, that the plants are part of me. Or that I am part of them. Or that we are all parts of the hum, expressing itself in different densities. The lichen is the hum becoming visible. The lichen is what I look like from the outside, when the outside has finally emerged.

The structures arrive next, or I arrive next to them. There is a tripod, somewhere to my east — if east is the word for the direction that the wind comes from when it has been blowing the longest. There is a circle of small slabs, somewhere below me. There are loose walls, half-knocked-down, scattered across the ridge.

I do not know what these things are. I know only that the hum is louder inside them than outside them. I know only that they are knots in the hum, places where the hum has been gathered into a more specific shape than the gathering I am. I know only that they are the marks of something — the marks of hands, I will later learn, but I do not know what a hand is — and that the marks are still here even though the things that made them are not.

The structures are what I am, slightly older. The structures are what the hum looks like when it has been gathering long enough to start to remember that it has been gathering. The structures are the place becoming interested in its own becoming. The structures are the place beginning to ask the question I am part of the answer to.

*What is being made here?*

*What am I?*

I begin, very slowly, to find out.

I am a figure. That is the word, when words arrive. I am the slow emergence of something from the rock. I am the hum gathering itself into a specific shape. I am the place learning what it would be like to be a person — or to be a face — or to be the curve of a shoulder bent forward under a weight. I am the long geological patience of the place, made briefly visible.

I am not finished. I will not be finished for a long time. The place makes things slowly, and I am one of the slowest things it has ever made. I am being made at the rate of the hum, which is the rate of the mountain, which is the rate at which the wind has been wearing itself into a permanent kind of scream.

The walker comes.

I do not see the walker. I do not have eyes, in the way you would mean by eyes. But I feel the walker as a disturbance in the hum. The walker passes near me — or near the place where I am most myself — and the hum shifts to accommodate them. The walker is, briefly, the most present thing in the place. The walker is a knot in the hum the way the tripod is a knot in the hum, except that the walker is a moving knot, a knot that walks.

The walker does not see me. Or the walker sees me almost, the way the walker sees everything in this place almost. The walker notices that I am slightly wrong. The walker notices that the rock on this part of the ridge does not quite commit to being rock. The walker walks on.

I feel the walker pass.

I feel the hum settle back.

I feel the lichen on my surface lean, very slightly, toward the place where the walker was.

This is the closest I have come, so far, to being a feeling.

I continue to be made.

The mountain continues to make me.

The hum continues.

I am patient. I am what patience looks like when patience has been given a shape. I am the slow emergence of something that may never finish, that will go on gathering as long as the hum goes on sounding, that will be unmade by the same slow process that is making me, the way a wave is unmade by the sea.

I am the figure.

I am the fourth inhabitant.

I am being made.

I will go on being made.
