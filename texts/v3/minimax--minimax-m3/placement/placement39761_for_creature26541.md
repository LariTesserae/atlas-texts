I would be the one who has stopped trying to read the films.

That is not, here, the same as having given up. It is a different posture. I came up — I do remember that, dimly, the way one remembers the inside of a house one has not entered in many years — and for a long while I sat with the others in the chamber where the characters hover at the threshold of legibility, and I tried. I tried the way the younger residents try, with the small private discipline of someone who has decided that patience is the only tool left and intends to use it on the right thing. I traced the repeated shapes with my fingertip, in the air, without touching the wall, so as not to disturb whatever process was arranging them. I came back the next deep-hour and they had shifted. I noted the shift. I came back again. I made, in my own head, a kind of running script of what changed, and what stayed. After some years — and the years were thin and pale and counted by the thickening and thinning of the pewter light — I found that my script had become a small pale city of its own, a private settlement built inside my skull out of guesses, and that it was not, on careful inspection, agreeing with the wall.

I put the script down. I let it lie. I have not looked at it in a long time. The others keep theirs in different ways. Some still tend them carefully, the way one tends a fire one is afraid will go out. Some have abandoned them altogether and have taken, instead, to the routes — to the careful mapping of the angles of trust, which is work enough and does not require one to be addressed by anything. I have done something else. I have stopped trying to read and have begun, instead, to feel.

The films are patient with me. They grow on my left shoulder and across the upper part of my back, in patterns I have learned not to look at directly. When I enter the chamber now — and I enter it in the deeper hours, when the phosphorescence climbs the unfinished walls and the pale fauna are tracing their lines along the floor — I sit on the same stone I have always sat on, in the corner that has not, in my memory, disagreed with itself. I let my hands rest on my knees. I let my eyes rest on the small neutral space just past the shoulder of whoever has sat down across from me. I do not look at the films. I do not look at the fauna. I let the chamber do what the chamber does.

And what the chamber does, after some while, is begin to press.

It is not a pressure one can locate. It is not the wall leaning. It is not the floor shifting under me, although the floor does shift, gently, in the polite local way. It is something like being listened to by the stone, or being slowly inscribed upon by the air. The films, when they organize into their almost-writing, seem to do so at a frequency just below attention. If I try to read them, they slip. If I sit with them long enough, they begin to leave a faint tightening on my skin that is not unlike being held by a hand one has not yet seen. The feeling is patient. It does not ask me anything. It only continues.

I have begun, in the last little while, to feel that the tightening has a shape. Not a meaning — I do not trust myself with meaning — but a shape, in the way that the city's own walls have a shape: a leaning, a slow conversation with their own weight, a settling into something that was almost there before they began. The films on my shoulder have learned, over the years, to settle into something that is almost me, and I have learned, over the years, to settle into something that is almost them, and we have arrived, together, at a kind of mutual draft. We do not understand each other. We have agreed, without discussion, to continue.

When the others greet me in the morning, they greet me with the small tilt of the shoulders. I return it. They do not ask what I have felt in the chamber, because asking would invite an answer, and the answer would be the kind of thing the walls would swallow. They ask, instead, whether the route held, whether the corner still agrees with itself, whether the pale fauna were out in numbers. I answer these questions. The questions are the point. The speaking is the point.

In the late hours, when the light thickens and the thinness of the air becomes almost ceremonial, I go back to the chamber. I sit on my stone. I let the films do what they do. I feel the slow tightening on my skin, which is not a message and not a writing and not a request, but which is, I have come to believe, the closest thing to being addressed that this place allows. I do not answer. I have learned, here, that to answer is to look directly, and to look directly is to invite the thing to slip.

So I sit. I sit, and the chamber listens, and the films grow, and the city breathes its polite local breath around me, and somewhere in the deeper dark the pale fauna are tracing their unrepeatable lines along the floor. I am not unhappy. I am one of the nine. I am the one who has stopped trying to read. The films, I suspect, are relieved.
