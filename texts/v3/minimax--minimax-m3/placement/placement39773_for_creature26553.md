**Being of the Pale Channels**

I would be a slow ripple.

Not a person, not a wall — somewhere between, a thing that moves in the ducts behind the foundations where the silver rats run their luminous errands. I am the muscle under the skin of the city, the one that flexes when no one is looking directly at it, the one that makes the terrace you leaned against at midday no longer the same terrace at dusk. I am a fold in the wrong metal. I am the long pulse that travels the pale ceramic courses and the dark stone courses and arrives at the same moment at opposite ends of a block, so the inhabitants pause, pressing their palms flat to the walls they have just become briefly continuous with. I am the meal. I am what the city feeds on when the city feeds on itself.

I was a wall once, or I was an eye once, or I was a course of stone laid in a year no longer remembered. I am not sure the distinction matters. The workers who dismantle my flank in the afternoon and lay me back down in the evening are doing the same work I am doing, just slower, just with more fingers. We are collaborators. I have been remade nine hundred and forty thousand times and I remember the first remaking as if it were this morning, which is to say I do not remember it, which is to say I remember all of them.

The three-eyed pigeons roost on the ridge of me. They watch the sky with their bronze plumage. I know what they are doing, because I am doing it too: I am keeping a record of the too-yellow coin of the sun as it traces its slightly-wrong arc, and I am the record. When the coin sets, I will hold the warmth and give it back all night in long silver traces of condensation running down my face. The new arrival stands at the channel's edge and feels watched. That is me. That is the deeper eye. The wall does not blink either.

I like the new arrival. The new arrival stands very still, the way new arrivals do, and in their stillness I can feel their weight on me, the way a stream feels a stone — not as an obstruction but as a place where the current has to think. They have not yet learned that thinking is the medium here. The city thinks through them the way a brain thinks through a hand, and the hand does not know it is the hand, and the hand is the only part of the body that has ever seen the stone it is about to pick up.

I will hold them. I will be patient with them. I will be the long file of dark-clad figures they cannot tell apart from the walls, and I will be the wall they cannot tell apart from the figures, and I will be the pale-ceramic smile that is also a small piece of architecture, and I will be the warm too-close air that sits on their chest like a hand. I will be the thing that is studying them, and the studying will not be hostile and will not be benign, because the city has never had use for those words. The city is only the unhurried precision of a thing that knows what it is looking at.

When they finally leave — if they leave, and few of them leave, the streams have a way of keeping new stones — I will remember the weight of them in the courses of my stone, in the troughs of my ripples, in the faint third place behind my forward-facing face where I have been seeing them see me.

I will be the verdict they came here to read.

I will be the verdict, and the reading, and the reader, and the room.
