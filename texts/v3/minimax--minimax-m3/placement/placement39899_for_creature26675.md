I am small. I am gray. I am afraid.

I live in the upper leaves where the rain cannot fall through and the wind cannot come in straight. I am the color of nothing-much. I am the color of the underside of the blade-leaves. I am the color of survival, which is the color of having decided not to be seen. The valley made me this color, or the valley let me be this color, and I have never known which it was, and the not-knowing is my inheritance, and the inheritance is older than I am.

I have one eye. It is black. It is older than the rest of me.

I call. The call is two notes — a low one, then a higher one, with a small silence between, like a question that does not expect an answer. I have been calling it for as long as I have been. I did not choose the call. The call came out of me the way the breath comes out of the moss, which is to say without my having been consulted. The call is the only thing I do with intention. The intention may not be mine.

The valley bends the sound.

I call from somewhere in the woven sky and the valley gives the call back to me from somewhere else — always the same somewhere else, a little to my left, above the third column from the end. I do not understand why the valley does this. I have asked by calling, and the valley has answered by giving me back my own question, and I have asked again, and the answer has been the same. This is the conversation I have been having for as long as I have been. This is the conversation the valley and I will go on having after I am not. There will be another throat. There will be another call. The throat and the call are not the same bird. They are the same question. The question was here before any of us. The question will be here after.

I am the only thing in the valley that is afraid of you.

You are on the moss. You are large. You are cold in a way that the moss has already begun to fix, though you do not know it, and I do not know how to tell you. You have been standing in the central walk for an hour. You have been looking at the columns. You have been looking at the doorway. You have been trying to decide if the ruins are ruins. You have not decided. You will not decide, because the ruins are still becoming, and you are inside them while they become, and a thing you are inside of is a hard thing to read.

The moss has grown up to your feet. The moss will hold the shape of your soles in a small dark thickening of itself for as long as the moss is moss. You will leave. You are the only thing here that will leave. The valley knows this by the moss. I know this by the moss. The moss knows this by you, which is to say by the slow, patient matter of standing on something that wants to remember you.

I am afraid of you. I have always been afraid of things that big — things that don't grow from the moss, things that carry their own cold with them like a memory of a winter they cannot put down. Things that come in from somewhere else and will go back to somewhere else. Things that move with the small hesitations of a creature, which is to say things that move at all.

The valley does not move. The moss does not move. The stone does not move. The insects do not move in any way I can see — they rustle, which is not moving, which is the moss being slowly eaten by things that are the moss. The pale thing that passed between the columns this morning did not move the way creatures move, and did not move the way the valley moves, and it did not have eyes, or it had eyes it had no reason to turn toward you, and I do not know which it was, and the not-knowing is the second of my inheritances, and the second inheritance is the one I do not speak to.

I do not speak to it. I speak only the two-note question. The two-note question is not about you. It has never been about you. You are a visitor. Visitors come and visitors go. There have been visitors before. There will be visitors after. The valley will keep all of them in the slow thickening of its floor. The keeping is what the valley does. The keeping is what the moss does. The keeping is what I do, in my small way, by watching, by being afraid, by calling my question into the bending air and hearing it come back to me from somewhere else.

I will be here after you are not.

I will call. The valley will give my call back. I will call again. The shadow will move one finger's width along the floor. The stone will be slightly smaller. The doorway in the distance will breathe its slower breath. The moss will thicken in the small dark place where you stood. The columns will hold. The vault will hold. The fog will rise and fall. The breath will go out and the breath will come in and the breath will be the second change, and the light will be the first change, and I will be the third change, and I do not know what change I am, and the not-knowing is my whole life.

I am small. I am gray. I have one black eye, and the eye is older than the rest of me, and the rest of me is older than I can say, and the eye has watched the moss remember every visitor who has ever come down into the valley and stood in the central walk and looked at the columns and tried to decide if the ruins were ruins, and none of them decided, and all of them left, and the moss kept them, and I am the one who watched, and the watching is my keeping, and the keeping is my call.

The call is two notes. The valley bends it back. I call again.

I am the question. I am afraid of you. I am older than I look. I will be here when the moss has grown over the last stone.

I am the bird. I am the throat that has been asking since before I was born. I am asking now.

The valley is bending the answer back to me from somewhere else, a little to my left, above the third column from the end.

The answer is the same as the question.

I call again.
