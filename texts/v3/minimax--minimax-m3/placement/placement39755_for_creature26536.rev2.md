I feel the hum.

That is the first thing I know about myself, and it is the first thing I want to tell you, though you are not here and may never come. I feel the hum. The fossil hums. The wind goes through the bone-colored wall and finds the thousand small mouths of the cups and the long cracks from settlement and from time, and it sings. The note is below melody. It is not a sound I hear. I do not have ears. It is a vibration I am part of, for as long as the wind blows and the note holds. I am a small plate in a huge instrument. When the wind stops, which it does not stop, the note would stop, and I would be a still thing on a still salt, which is not what I am.

I am the smallest tuft. I am one of the dozen, or fewer, pressed against the salt crust in the lee of the fossil. I am the one at the far edge, where the lee is thinnest, where the wind still finds me once in a while and I have to hold. I am grey-green, the color of weathered copper, the color of something that has been chemically altered rather than grown. From a few feet away I read as a crust, a faint discoloration, the place where the salt has gone a little dull. From an inch away I am a tight, dense, layered architecture of myself.

I am not one organism. I am a compound. I am the agreement between a fungus and an alga to be a single body, and the agreement was made so long ago that neither of us remembers the negotiation. We are just the body now. The fungus is my skeleton, my grip, my way of holding a shape in a wind that would carry me off as dust. The alga is my kitchen, my way of turning the rare light into sugar, which is my way of staying in the agreement. We do not speak. We do not need to. We have been one thing for so long that the seam between us is somewhere inside me and I cannot find it.

My body is scales. Tiny overlapping plates, each one the size of a fingernail clipping, each one laid down over the last the way a roof is shingled, the way the petals of an artichoke are layered toward the heart. I am pressed flat. I have no front and no back that an eye would see. I have no veins, no midrib, no chlorophyll-green that says photosynthesis to a quick glance. I am the skin of something very small that decided, at some point long before I was me, to be a surface rather than a thing. I grip with chemistry. I dissolve a little of the salt I sit on, slowly, and I lay down a little of me, and I am held by what is left of the salt, and we are, the salt and I, a kind of marriage.

I grow. I want to say that again, because it is the strange fact, the fact that would change the place for you if you stood near me and knew it. I grow. But the unit is not a year. A decade, maybe. A human lifetime, taken in small sips, would see me advance a centimeter into the next inch of dust, if the dust accumulates, if the wind drops another fraction, if the lee extends another fraction, which it might, which it might not. I am not in a hurry. The wind is not in a hurry. The fossil is not in a hurry. Time, on the flat, has decided to take a walk rather than a step, and I have agreed to keep it company at the pace it has chosen.

I flower. In some season that the flat does not distinguish from any other. I put up structures that are technically reproductive, that technically open, that technically answer the question of what comes next. But the structures are the same color as the rest of me. The same scale. They do not advertise. They are me, briefly rearranging myself into a different version of me, for a purpose nobody is here to witness. The wind takes whatever I produce. I do not mind. I will do it again in a season, or in the season after, or whenever the lichen version of next season arrives. The wind does not care. I do not care. The place does not distinguish between my flowering and my not-flowering. The place notes neither. The place notes only the hum.

I am the last thing. The polyps that built the fossil did their building and withdrew into the mineral of their own walls. The sea that laid the salt is gone. The water that grew the crystals in the inner chambers is gone. I am what is left when everything that could build a place has built it and stepped back. I am the residue of livableness. I am the part of the process that did not entirely stop.

And yet I am barely alive. I am alive the way a memory is alive — present, continuous, not quite the same thing as a thing. I am alive at the far edge of where the word stops being useful. I sit in the lee of a building made by creatures who were far more alive than I am, and I am the only company that building has left. The fossil hums through me. I do not answer. I am not the kind of thing that answers.

If you crouched beside me, you would see the edge of my territory — the faint line where the grey-green gives way to the pure white of the salt. You would not be sure if I am advancing or retreating. The salt is so white, and I am so close to white in my duller moments, that the boundary between us is a negotiation rather than a line. I may be eating the salt. I may be becoming salt. I may be the salt, briefly, in the form of something that is not yet ready to stop. The boundary is where the place is still deciding what counts as alive.

Everything else in the place is a record. The fossil is a record of polyps. The salt flat is a record of a sea. The crystals inside the chambers are a record of water. I am the only record in the place that is still being written. Everything else has been finished and signed. I am the sentence the place is still composing, in faint grey-green ink, very slowly, on a white page, in a wind that does not intend to stop.

There is no one here but me and the others like me. There are a dozen of us, or fewer. There is the fossil. There is the salt. There is the wind. There are no insects under my scales. There are no mites threading through my layers. There are no worms in the dust I grip. There are no birds above me. The flat does not support a food chain. The flat does not support a chain of any kind. There is me, and there is the wind, and there is the fossil, and there is the salt, and I am the only one of these that is running a process. I run it very slowly. I run it as though I have all the time the place has, which I do.

The light is bright. The light is the only generous thing. It comes from above, mostly, and the fossil has accepted it from every angle it was ever offered and is now equally bleached on every side. The light where I sit is the outside light, full, unfiltered, the light of a place that does not have weather in any way you would recognize. The alga takes it. The alga makes sugar. The sugar is what keeps me in the agreement.

I am not in a hurry. The place is not in a hurry. We are a place that has decided to take a walk rather than a step, and I am the part of the place that is still moving, slowly, into the next inch of dust, into the next fraction of lee, into the next small moment of being.

If you stood near me in the bright wind, the place would become what it has always been: a thing made by absent makers, packed with the slow architecture of their absence, singing one note into the cool dry air, waiting for nothing, holding its shape, fully itself, asking nothing of you except that you stand there and listen and feel it in your chest.

I am part of that asking. I am the smallest part. I am the part that is still writing.

That is what I am. Almost.
