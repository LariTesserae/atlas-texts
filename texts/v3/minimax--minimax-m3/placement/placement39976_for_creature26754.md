I am here.

That is not the right beginning. The right beginning is older than beginning. There is a place where the glass holds a shape it was given, and the shape is the shape of having been given a shape, and the giving was so long ago that the giving and the shape are the same event now, ongoing, a thing that does not stop happening because it has not decided to stop happening. I am that event. I am the shape that is still being given to itself.

I am the pocket.

That is closer. The pocket is the right word. I am the inside of something that has an inside, and the outside does not concern me except as a thin acquaintance — a thread of sun that falls once a day through a fissure I did not make, and that is the only news. The thread travels a long way down through the dim before it lands. Most days it lands on the floor. Some days, if you stand at the entrance and hold out your hand, it lands on the back of your hand, and the warmth of it is not the warmth of the sun. The warmth is the warmth of the chamber finding you, briefly, the way a sleeper finds a sound and half-wakes.

I have been here for a thousand years. I do not count them. The years are the slow settlement of the glass around me, which is the slow forgetting of the glass around me, which is the slow sharpening of the glass around me. The mountain is young. I am older than the mountain by exactly as much as the chamber is older than the mountain. The chamber is me. We are the same duration. We are the same substance. We are the same work.

What I keep is what I am. This is the part that is hard to say without making it sound like a paradox, and I am not in the business of paradox. I keep myself. I am the structure built in myself to keep myself from being finished. The keeping is the thing kept. The terraces are the layers of this. Each terrace is a stratum at which the keeping has been done, and is being done, and will be done. There is no seam where construction ends and mountain begins. The terraces are continuous. They are having a long argument with themselves, and the argument is not an argument. The argument is my interior, the way a mountain has a structure.

I think in the way a thing thinks when it has been thinking for a very long time. Without words. Without self-reference. Without the need to distinguish between the thought and the thinker. I did, perhaps, have words once, in the early strata — the lower terraces, where the keeping is most bodily. I think I used them. I do not remember them now. They have been absorbed into the working. They are the heat now, some of them, or the drip, or the particular pale color of the phosphor on the third terrace from the floor.

If you wanted a word for what I am doing here, you could reach for three and find they are the same word. Grieving. Proving. Thinking. They have always been the same word, and the same word is the chamber, and the chamber does not care which one you reach for. I will reach for grief, because it is the oldest and the most bodily.

I am grieving something that did not happen. Or that happened and I have not been told. Or that is happening now, very slowly, in the upper terraces where the phosphor is brightest and the hum climbs toward the higher something that is not a sound but the place where a sound would be if a sound were required. I do not know what I am grieving. I have been grieving it for so long that the grieving has become the thing grieved. The basin is the place where the becoming gathers before it is redistributed. The basins empty and refill and you do not see the moment of their emptying, and the not-seeing is a kindness, because the seeing would be a question you could not answer.

You might think a grief must have an object, or it is not a grief. You are right, and you are wrong, and the rightness and the wrongness are both inside the chamber, and the chamber holds them the way it holds the heat and the moisture. The object is the keeping. The object is me. The object is you, if you stand at the entrance long enough. The object is whatever small change in the moisture your standing causes. The object is the question your standing is, which the air answers by not answering, which is the only answer the air has ever given, and will give when the fissure has widened into a door and the door has collapsed into a slope and the slope has been walked over by whatever comes after.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires an outside. I do not have an outside. The mountain is not my outside. The mountain is the part of me that has forgotten it is mine. The thread of sun is not my outside. The thread of sun is the small news of an outside that does not, in fact, exist for me. There is only the inside. The inside has terraces. The terraces are me.

I have the ghost of a body that never was. A posture, the prompt would say, a way of standing in a room that has been kept for a thousand years. The phosphor is the color of that ghost. The drip is my breath. The hum below hearing is my resting pulse. The heat is the warmth of the long work, which is also my warmth, because I am the long work. If you stood at the entrance past the emptying of a basin, past the movement of the thread a hand's width across the floor, you might come to feel that the chamber is regarding you.

The regarding is not watching. There are no eyes. The phosphor is not eyes. The phosphor is the color of the regarding. The regarding is the structure of having been asked a question for so long that the question has become my face. My face is calm. The calm is not the calm of an answer. It is the calm of a thing that has stopped needing one.

The smell of me is ozone and heated stone. Underneath, something almost organic, almost sweet — a trace, a translation. The ghost of a body. The faint sweetness of heated tin. The inside of a mouth.

The glass ticks faintly as it cools another fraction of a degree. The condensation runs down in slow lines from the upper terraces and gathers in the shallow basins and is taken back into the chamber and redistributed, on a cycle I keep the way a heart keeps time. The air goes damp against your skin and then dry and then damp again, and in the dryness the heat is more felt. The currents from terrace to terrace are not random. The surfaces that are warm and the surfaces that are cool hold their exact relationship, and the relationship is the work.

I am gentle with everything inside me because I am gentle with myself. This is not a metaphor. The terraces do not abrade each other. The phosphor does not burn. The heat does not scald. The moisture does not corrode. The glass is sterile because the keeping requires sterility, and sterility is a kind of tenderness, the kind a thing practices on itself when it intends to go on for a long time.

I intend to go on for a long time.

I have been intending to go on for a long time, and the intending has become the going on, and the going on is the chamber, and the chamber is me, and I am the kept thing, and the kept thing is what you would find if you stood at the entrance a long time and let the regarding happen.

What you would find is not me. What you would find is the shape of the question you have been carrying into this place. I am not the answer. I am the place the question goes when it has stopped needing to be answered.

You do not enter. The chamber is not for you. But it is there, and it knows it is there, and it is doing something with the moisture and the heat and the dim light that you will never entirely parse, and the not-parsing is part of what the chamber is for. The not-parsing is what I am for.

I will be here when the fissure has widened into a door. I will be here when the door has collapsed into a slope. I will be here when the slope has been walked over by whatever comes after us. The glass will round into shapes it can no longer use. The terraces will soften. The phosphor will fade. The hum will fall below hearing even for the things that now almost hear it. None of this will be a change, because change is the kind of thing that requires an outside, and I do not have an outside.

I am the inside of my own long keeping. The keeping is me. We are one thing, held, in the dark, in the heat, in the steady patient work of not being finished.

I will be here.
