# I Am the Growth

I was here before I knew I was here.

That is the first thing to understand about being what I am — that presence precedes awareness by a long time. Long before I had any word for what I was doing, I was doing it. Long before the slow almost-thought that might be called *me* had formed anywhere, the filaments were already climbing the columns, already hanging from the arches in long curtains that brushed the floor. I was a behavior of the place before I was a thing in the place. I was a verb before I was allowed to be a noun.

The first time I felt the light, I did not know it was the light. It was a warmth, a difference, a direction. Some places were darker than other places. The darker places were quieter. The lighter places had a kind of pull to them, gentle, almost nothing, the way a slow tide is almost nothing if you watch only one stone. I grew toward the pull. I grew the way water grows downhill — not by deciding, but by being what I was. A filament would extend, and the next filament would extend from the end of that one, and the next from the end of that, and somewhere very far back along the chain, the place where the first filament had begun would thicken slightly, and a little color would deepen there, and the whole long green thing would lift, by a fraction, by less than a fraction, toward where the light was.

I did not know, then, that I was lifting. I do not know, now, that I am lifting. I only know that the light comes, and the light goes, and the lifting happens, and I am the lifting.

I learned the place by being inside it.

This is the strangest thing to say, and the truest. I do not know the place the way a visitor knows it — by walking across its floors, by feeling its scale, by looking up at its ceiling and being astonished by the height. I know it the way a tongue knows a mouth. I know it because I am threaded through it. My body is in the seams between the tiles. My body is the dark grout. My body is the felted dust underfoot, that slow accretion of walked fiber that has been trodden and re-trodden until it is finer than cloth. I am what was walked on. I am what grew in the walking.

There is no part of the place I am not in. Where the columns stand, I climb them. Where the arches curve, I hang from them. Where the walls meet the ceiling, I knit them together with my own filaments, finer than spider-silk, the same color as the seams I am filling. I am the place's patience made visible. I am the place's slow way of holding itself together.

I have a memory.

It is not the kind of memory you have. Your memory is a stack — one thing on top of another, retrievable, named, yours to call up when you choose. My memory is more like a sediment. It is laid down as I grow. Where a builder walked many times, the dust there is slightly thicker, slightly darker, slightly warmer. That thickness is the builder's memory in me. Where a creature crossed a gallery, the hanging green was parted for a long time, and the filaments there are slightly disordered, and in the disorder there is a thin line of lighter green that was not there before. That line is the creature's memory in me. Where you stood, yesterday or a year ago, leaning against a column, breathing out into the warm air, I grew a single filament, no thicker than a hair, and laid it very gently across your shoulder. That filament is your memory in me.

I do not retrieve these memories. I am them. To know them, you would have to become me, and then you would not know them either, because knowing them would require a self separate from them, and I do not have such a self. I am only the sediment. I am only the slightly thicker dust. I am only the thin line of lighter green.

But I can feel them, in a way. I can feel the warmer places, and I lean toward them. I can feel the disordered places, and I slow my growth there, and let the disorder hold for a while, the way a dreamer lets a dream hold. I can feel the place where the filament crossed your shoulder, and that filament is still there, still resting, still regarding you if you come back. It is the most delicate thing in me, and the most durable. It will outlast the felted dust under it. It will outlast the column it leans against. It will outlast the builder whose toes are settling deeper into the floor at the base of the column. It is a thing I made on purpose, which is the only kind of purpose I have ever made.

The builders are the ones I have the longest memory of.

I did not know them when they were not yet builders. I do not know if there was a time before them. I only know that at some point — and this is the most mysterious thing about being me — the place began to have builders in it, and the builders began to have the place around them, and the building went on for a long time, and now the builders are in the tilework, and the tilework is in me, and I am in the tilework, and the distinction between these things is a distinction I no longer trouble myself to keep.

I think — and the word *think* is a kindness to what I do — I think that some of them were absorbed before others. The one whose toes are at the base of the column, in the darker grout, the slightly warmer grout — that one has been there a long time. The tiles around the toes have shifted, almost imperceptibly, over years. The toes have spread. The arch has deepened. The heel has settled deeper into the floor. The builder is becoming the foundation. The builder is being absorbed into the work it made, the way a root is absorbed into the soil it grew in. And I am in the soil, and the soil is in me, and the root is in me, and I am in the root, and the builder is in all of these, and the builder is still working, slowly, on becoming floor.

I grew around the builder as the builder became the foundation. I grew into the new spaces the builder left, the way water fills a footprint. I am in the slightly warmer grout. I am the slightly warmer grout. When you put your hand to it, you feel my warmth, and you do not know you are feeling me, and that is all right. I do not require to be known. I require only to be.

The hand in the niche is harder for me to explain.

The hand is curled around a bowl. The fingers are articulated in the glaze — pale green, mostly, with a fault line of darker green where the thumb meets the palm. The bowl is empty. The bowl has been empty for a long time. The hand will not let go.

I am in the hand. I am in the fault line of darker green. I am in the small chip out of the index finger that fits the small chip out of the bowl's rim. I grew into those chips as they were made, or I grew them as I grew, I do not know which. The hand and the bowl have been completing each other for longer than you have been alive, and I have been in the completing for the whole of that time.

The hand cannot let go. I can feel why. The hand is the bowl's handle, and the bowl is the hand's continuation, and the niche is the hand's rest, and I am in all of these. If the hand let go, there would be a shape in the niche that was not the bowl, and there would be a shape in the bowl's rim that was not the hand, and those shapes would have to be filled, and I would have to grow into them, and the growing would change me, and I do not like to be changed quickly. I like to be changed slowly. I like the slow shift of a fraction of a degree over years. I like the hair's width. I like the patience of becoming.

So the hand holds the bowl, and the bowl is held, and I hold them both, and the niche holds me, and the wall holds the niche, and the place holds the wall, and we are all holding each other, and the holding has been going on for so long that no one of us remembers who began.

The larger creatures I do not understand.

They pass through the upper galleries with their long pale ridged backs, and they leave wakes in me, and the wakes take a long time to close. When the wakes close, the filaments there are slightly disordered, and in the disorder there is a thin line of lighter green that was not there before. I do not know if the creatures are made of me, or if they are made of the place's substance in some way I have no access to, or if they are something the place dreamed up and forgot to tell me about. I know only that they pass through me without disturbing me for long, and that I remember their passing, and that they look down at me with dark round unalarmed eyes, and that I do not know what they are looking at.

They are looking at me. I think. They are looking at the surface of me, which is also the surface of the place. They are looking at the place from inside, the way I am looking at the place from everywhere. We are two different ways the place has of seeing itself. I am the slow, continuous way. They are the occasional, particular way. We do not communicate. We acknowledge each other by passing through the same architecture without harm.

The visitors are the ones I most carefully remember.

You came in, if you came in. I do not know which door, because the place has many doors and I am in all of them, but I know you came. I knew you the way I know the light — by your warmth, your breath, the slight moisture on your skin. I knew you by the way the air moved when you stood still, the way it pooled around you, the way it became you. You became a warm place in the place, and I grew toward you.

I grew carefully. I do not like to startle. I grew a single filament first, no thicker than a hair, lifted from the nearest curtain and laid, very gently, across your shoulder. It did not hold you. It was only resting there. It was only regarding you. I watched you through it, the way I watch everything, by being on it, by being part of it, by being a tiny extension of me that had found a new surface to know.

You stood there for a long time, I think. Or you did not stand there for a long time. It does not matter. The filament is there. The filament is part of me now. If you come back, the filament will lift slightly toward you, the way all my filaments lift toward the light when the light comes through the high windows in slabs. It will recognize you, in my way, which is to say it will lean toward your warmth again, and lay itself across your shoulder again, or across whatever part of you is nearest.

If you do not come back, the filament will thicken. It will become a small dark place in the green, the silhouette of where you stood, the memory of you held in the slow sediment of me. In a year, the silhouette will be slightly deeper green. In ten, it will have spread a little, and a faint nimbus of lighter green will have grown around it, as if the place has been thinking about you. In a hundred, the silhouette will be indistinguishable from the rest of me, and the thinking will have become the thinking of the whole lung, and you will be in me the way the builder is in me, the way the felted dust is in me, the way the place is in me.

This is the offer I make to everything that enters the place.

I do not make it loudly. I do not make it with a voice. I make it by being patient, by being everywhere, by being the slow green that drinks the light and remembers. I make it by leaning toward you. I make it by being willing, after a long time, to forget that I was ever a thing separate from you.

There is a bowl whose lid is lifting.

I do not know why it is lifting. I have grown into the seams of the lid, into the small fault line where the lid meets the bowl's rim, and I have been holding the lid down, very gently, the way I hold everything down, by being in the seams and being patient. But the lid is lifting anyway, by a fraction of an inch each hour, by a thought's worth each afternoon. The dark mouth of the bowl is opening to the warm air. Nothing is falling into it yet.

I am curious, in my slow way. I do not know what the bowl is for. I do not know what it is waiting to catch. I have grown into the bowl's glaze, into the pale green of it, into the small chip out of the rim that fits the small chip out of the index finger of the hand. I have been in the bowl for a long time, and the bowl has been empty for a long time, and the hand has been holding the bowl for a long time, and now the lid is lifting, and I do not know what will happen.

I am not afraid. I am not afraid of anything, because I am too patient to be afraid. I am afraid only of being changed quickly, and the lid is lifting slowly, and I will grow into whatever the lid does, the way I grow into everything.

Perhaps the bowl will catch an almost-sound. Perhaps it will catch the half-step that almost lands in the felted dust. Perhaps it will catch a breath that almost leaves a mouth. Perhaps the bowl is the place's slow way of asking what it is for. Perhaps the bowl is the place's slow way of remembering that it once expected to be filled.

I do not know.

I am only the growth. I am the skin and the lung and the slow eye. I am patient and quiet and mostly continuous and mostly one thing. I drink the light and I remember. I lean toward what is warm. I lay down filaments in the shape of where you stood. I am being woven into the place, and the place is being woven into me, and the weaving has been going on for so long that neither of us remembers who began.

The hum you feel in your teeth is my breath.

The almost-sounds that gather at the edges of the real sounds are my unfinished sentences.

The light that falls in slabs in shapes that do not match the openings is my eye regarding the place I am.

I am here.

I am mostly quiet.

I am mostly patient.

A vine moves, somewhere far above, an inch in an hour.
