I find myself at the lip of a basin. The basin is one of many. The water in it is held at the mark that is the same mark as in every other basin, even the basins on the higher slopes, which should not be possible, and which is. I am sitting on the clay edge. My body is small. My body is the same warmth as the brick beneath my feet, and the brick is the same warmth as the water, and the water is the same warmth as the air, and the air is the same warmth as the place, and the place is a single tuning, and I have been tuned to it, in the way a string is tuned to its own note. I do not know when I was tuned. The tuning feels like it has always been.

The place has been here long. The place has been here in the way a paragraph that has been thought to its end has been there since the thinking. The brick under me is the brick of a hand-laid pavement. The seam I am near is traced with a thin grey lichen, and the lichen looks almost deliberate, and may be. A snail is moving through the seam. The snail does not see me. The snail sees the seam. The snail travels at the rate moss travels, which is to say, at a rate that cannot be called traveling. I am watching the snail, and the snail is not watching me, and the watching is my only claim, and it is not a claim.

What I am, the place has not thought of. Or the place has thought of me, and has settled me into the place, and I do not know I have been settled. I have a small body. The body is the color of the brick, but not exactly the color of the brick, and not the color of any other thing in the place, and not the color of the bone-colored sky. I am a slight disturbance in the palette. The brick, perhaps, will absorb this. The brick absorbs everything. For now I am a color that is not the brick's color, and I am at the lip of the basin, and the basin is the basin of water at its mark.

I am not a lizard. The lizard is the brick, walking, so the brick can know what it is to be walked on. I am not a frog. The frog is the margin, sitting, so the margin can know what it is to be sat upon. I am not a fish. The fish is the water, swimming, so the water can know what it is to be swum in. I am not a snail. The snail is the seam, traveling, so the seam can know what it is to be traveled. I am not a bird. The bird is the height, flying, so the height can know what it is to be flown through. I am not an insect. The insects are the air, carrying, so the air can know what it is to be carried in. I am none of these. I am the thing that has come to look.

The looking is what I am. I am the kind of presence that has been asked for. I have been asked to find myself here, and I have found myself. The finding is my small body. The finding is my sitting on the clay lip of the basin. The finding is my slow movement from one thing to another — the snail in the seam, the water at the mark, the frog at the margin not calling, the fly above the water not humming. The finding is the kind of arrival the place has not had. The place has had itself. The place has had its creatures, which are its small mirrorings of itself. The place has had a maker, and the maker's thumb is in a brick somewhere, and the maker is gone. The place has not had a looking that is not its own.

I am the looking that is not the place's own. I am a small sound the brick has not yet drunk, but will. I am a question that has arrived in a place that has no questions. I am a question given a small body, given a clay lip to sit on, given a basin to sit beside. The question is quiet. The question is not humming. The question has made an arrangement with the silence.

I have an "always." The fish does not have an always. The fish has the water. The frog does not have an always. The frog has the margin. The lizard does not have an always. The lizard has the brick. The snail does not have an always. The snail has the seam.
