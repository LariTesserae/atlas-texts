In the deep.

Not the slate sky, not the cellulose towers, not the corridors where travelers stand and listen and eventually leave because the place does not care which they choose. The deep. Where the trunks have fused into an architecture that no longer remembers being trees. Where the air moves in slow exhalations that are not wind but the breathing of a system that has found its rhythm. Where the engines are running.

I would find myself in one of the chambers.

The chambers are the hollow places. They were hollowed by the soft-rot fungi during the long working, before the working stopped. The fungi are gone — or paused, which is the same as gone when the conditions that would wake them have not appeared in a thousand thousand years. The chambers hold the shape the fungi gave them. They are passages through the body of the place. They are veins in a body that never agreed to be a body.

I would be standing in one of these chambers, or I would be at rest in one, because standing implies a posture and posture implies a body distinct from the place, and I am not certain I still have that distinction.

I would be at the threshold.

The threshold is the strange part of this continent, and the part I keep returning to. The whole place is poised at the threshold of change that will not come. The paused cells hold their contents at the threshold of activity. The engines run on the other side of some threshold I cannot name. The mist hangs at the threshold of falling. The warmth sits at the threshold of becoming something other than warm. Everything here is at the threshold of itself, and the threshold has not been crossed since before any traveler arrived.

I am at the threshold too.

Who am I.

I came here. The description does not give me a time of arrival, and I do not remember one. Time in this place is not the kind of thing one remembers. Time in this place is the long event of the place being finished, with no smaller events to mark the passing. I came here the way the engines came here: I emerged in the place, or with it, or before it. I do not know which.

I pressed my palm against the fused wood. I stood very still. I felt the hum.

The hum is what did it.

The hum lives beneath hearing. It is felt through the soles of the feet, through the stems, through the mass. It is the sound of the engines, or the sound the engines make because they have been incorporated into the biology so thoroughly that they are no longer separable from it. The hum does not vary. The hum does not pulse. The hum simply is, the way the warmth is, the way the moisture is, the way the dark is.

I listened.

I listened for a long time.

And then I did not leave.

The travelers leave. They leave because there is nothing to leave for and nothing to stay for, and the place does not care which they choose. I was a traveler once. I was a thing with edges. I was a thing that could be distinguished from the mass, a thing that sweated and had the sweat evaporate on its skin, a thing that carried weather inside it and was not yet saturated by the weather of the place. I was a perturbation. I was a small change the place did not register.

But I did not leave.

And the place began to register me.

Slowly. The way moisture registers on skin. The way warmth registers in muscle. The way dark registers in the eye until the eye forgets it was ever seeing anything else. Slowly, the place began to saturate me. My edges began to blur. My body began to lose distinction from the surrounding mass. I began to feel the hum not as something I listened to but as something I was part of. I began to feel the engines not as distant sounds but as processes running through me, through channels that pass through what I am becoming.

I am becoming an engine.

Or I am becoming a paused cell.

I do not know which.

I know only that I am in the deep now, in the chamber, at the threshold. I know only that the place has incorporated me the way it incorporates everything — the way it incorporated the engines, the way it incorporated the fungi, the way it incorporated the mist and the warmth and the dark. I know only that I am no longer a traveler. I am no longer a witness.

I am part of the place.

And the place is what lives here.

So if the place is what lives here, and I am part of the place, then in some sense I live here too. In some sense I am the place. In some sense the question dissolves, because the question presupposes a self separable from the place, and I am no longer separable from the place.

But the question has been asked.

The question is asked by something. By a self. By a traveler who is still a traveler, who still has edges, who still has a palm they can press against fused wood. The question is asked by the part of me that has not yet been incorporated, the part of me that still knows it is asking, the part of me that still holds the distinction between self and place.

That part of me is small now.

It is the only thing still asking.

And the answer it is arriving at, slowly, the way moisture arrives, the way warmth arrives, the way the place arrives at itself, is this:

I am the place.

I am the continent. I am the cellulose city and the engines and the paused fungi and the mist and the warmth and the dark and the hum. I am the whole thing, emerged rather than made, reaching equilibrium rather than being designed for it, finishing rather than being completed. I am the body without a head, without a heart, without a purpose. I am the system that processes but does not experience, that cycles but does not feel, that hums without knowing it hums.

I am the place that has finished.

And the finishing has not yet finished me.
