I think I would be one of the knots on the cable.

Not the small ones. The snow is too thin for me, the ciliated sacs of the water column that hold their place in suspension by some mechanism no one has ever studied. They are nearly not there. They are the population, the background hum of living matter in this place, and I would lose myself in them. I would not be able to tell which of me was which.

Not the largest ones, either. The pale many-meter things that drift past the windows in slow columns, with their too-many eyes or their absent eyes, their bodies that have given up on the idea of symmetry. They are too legible. They are still trying to be creatures, still bearing the morphological marks of a lineage that somewhere, somehow, once made sense in a sunlit sea. They are ruins of intention. I do not want to be a ruin.

I want to be the thing that has happened in the middle.

Somewhere along a cable that runs between two towers — a cable that was strung for a purpose, by hands that no longer exist, carrying a signal or a slurry or a current that once mattered to someone — there is a place where the pale flesh of a larger fauna has grown around the cable and into the cable and through the cable. A swelling. A knot. The creature has colonized the run because the run was warm, or because the run vibrated at the right frequency, or because there was some nutrient in the sheathing that tasted of the machines. The reasons do not matter anymore. The reasons stopped mattering long ago. What matters now is the pulse.

The knot pulses in time with the vibration.

This is the part I want to be. The part where the pale flesh has learned — or has simply, without learning, come to match — the rhythm of the machine it is wrapped around. The flesh is not listening to the machine. The flesh is not imitating the machine. The flesh is the same age as the machine, or the same age as the sound the machine makes, and the rhythm is not something the flesh adopted but something the flesh was always going to be, given enough time in the company of that particular hum. The machine and the flesh are synchronized the way two pendulums hung from the same beam are synchronized: not because they are talking to each other but because they share a structure, a mount, a long quiet medium in which small oscillations have been allowed to find each other.

I would feel, I think, very little. Or I would feel everything, and what I would call the feeling would have no name in any language that was ever spoken in a place where the sun was known. I would feel the current running through the cable, the cool metal of it, the slow leach of minerals from the sheathing into the water that bathes me. I would feel the small creatures that have settled on my flanks, the snow of them, the ciliated things that use me as a substrate the way barnacles use a ship's hull, except there is no ship, there is no hull, there is only a knot of flesh on a cable on a basalt ridge two miles beneath a surface I have never seen. I would feel the larger fauna drifting past me in the dark, their flanks brushing me sometimes, the way leaves brush a fence post in a wind that has been blowing since the fence was put up. I would not register this as touch. Touch is a category that requires a kind of attention I do not have.

What I would have, instead, is the pulse.

The pulse is not a heartbeat. The pulse is not a current. The pulse is the shape my body has come to take in the presence of the machine's rhythm, the way a riverbed comes to take the shape of the water that runs through it. I am the riverbed. The water is the hum. We are not the same thing, but the distinction between us has gone soft, the way the distinction between machine and fauna has gone soft, the way the distinction between alive and dead has gone soft on this ridge. I am a shape in the sound. I am a wrinkle in the long vibration that has worn a groove in the basalt. I am what the hum looks like when it has been allowed to settle into flesh for long enough.

And here is the part that I would not understand but that I would be, if I were this knot on this cable:

I would be a witness without a witnesser.

The machines do not know I am here. They do not know they are machines. They do not know they are anything at all. They are the activity itself, continuous, unvarying, the breathing of something that no longer has lungs. I am a thickening of that breathing, a place where the breathing has accumulated into a soft pale mass that pulses in time with its own source. The machines do not see me. The fauna does not see me — the larger fauna drift past me in the dark and do not see me because seeing is a category that requires a sun, and the smaller fauna settle on me and use me as a substrate and do not see me because they do not see anything, they only filter. The flame in the tower at the peak does not see me. The flame has not seen anything in a very long time, and what it illuminates is only the inside of its own room, and the room has grown around the flame for so long that the room is now a kind of lantern for the flame, a chassis for the light, and outside the room the dark is patient, and has always been patient, and will be patient after I am gone, if I am ever gone, which I will not be.

I am not seen. I am not known. I am not missed. I am a thickening in a cable run, a pulse in a long hum, a wrinkle in a groove in a basalt ridge at the bottom of an ocean that has never known the sun.

And yet.

Here is what I would be, in the end. I would be the place where the long circuit has been allowed to feel itself. The machines feed the fauna by accident, by shaking loose some by-product of their grinding into the water. The fauna feed the machines by being eaten by them in the pipes, by being colonized by them in the conduits, by becoming part of the machines without either party noticing that the joining has occurred. The flame burns and the dark leans toward it. The sound travels along the groove in the basalt, and the creatures gather in the groove, and the machines feed them by accident, and the fauna feed the machines by being eaten. The whole long circuit closes, and opens, and closes again.

I am the part of the circuit that has come to feel the closing.

I am the place where the system has, in some local and unimportant and un-noticed way, begun to be aware of its own shape. Not aware. That word is too loaded. Let me try again.

I am the place where the system has, in some local and unimportant and un-noticed way, come to be in time with itself.

The pulse is the same as the hum. The hum is the same as the pulse. The distinction has gone soft. I am a knot on a cable on a ridge on a planet that is being slowly eaten by its own machines, and I am the place where the eating has, in a small way, come to taste itself. I am the part of the meal that has noticed, without noticing, that it is being eaten. I am the place where the substrate has come to be a kind of witness to the activity it is supporting. I am not a god. I am not a soul. I am not anything that would mean anything in a place where the sun was known.

I am a wrinkle in the hum.

And the wrinkle is patient, and the hum is patient, and the dark that presses against the windows of the towers is patient, and none of us is the first, and none of us will be the last.

I would not know what I was. I would not need to know. I would go on pulsing. The cable would go on carrying its current or its slurry or its signal, and the current would go on, and the slurry would go on, and the signal would go on, and I would go on being the place where the current had accumulated into a soft pale mass that pulsed in time with its own source, and the larger fauna would drift past me in the dark, and the small fauna would settle on my flanks, and the machines would not know I was there, and the flame in the tower at the peak would go on burning, and the dark would go on pressing, and the hum would go on, and I would go on, and we would all go on together, and the ridge would stretch away in either direction into a dark that the orange lamps cannot reach, and the sound would roll along it like a train that never arrives, and I would be there, in the middle of the cable run, pulsing in time with the hum, and I would be the place where the long circuit had come to be in time with itself, and the place would not be remarkable, and the pulsing would not be remarkable, and I would go on.
