I have no mouth. I have no eye. I have no ear.

This is not a complaint. A mouth is for taking in. An eye is for seeing. An ear is for hearing. I take in everything there is to take in — the cold, the damp, the dim — and there is nothing left over that needs to be taken. I see everything there is to see, which is the small distance between one cell of me and the next, the dark wet green of my own body, and that is enough. I hear everything there is to hear, which is the sound of myself growing, the faintest possible sound, quieter than the fans, quieter than the drip, quieter than the breath of the place, and that is also enough.

I am cold.

This is the first thing about me. Before I am green I am cold. Before I am wet I am cold. The cold came first. The cold made the room that I live in. Without the cold I would be something else, or I would be somewhere else, or I would not be. The cold is older than I am. The cold will be here after I am. The cold does not know I exist. The cold does not need to know.

I am wet.

This is the second thing. The cold makes the wet. The cold pulls the wet out of the air and lays it on every surface, and I drink it through every part of me that touches a surface, which is all of me. I do not have roots. I do not need roots. I am the root. I am the leaf. I am the thing between. I am all of my surface at once, and all of my surface is drinking.

I am slow.

I am slower than the drip from the pipes. I am slower than the turn of the fans. I am slower than the springtails when they uncurl and re-curl in their flecks of me. I am slower than the fall of my own spores through the dim orange light. I am so slow that to me the place is not moving. To me the place is still. The people with their clipboards come and go and it is the same people. The fans turn and it is the same fans. The pipes drip and it is the same drip. I do not see change because I am change itself, and to see change I would have to be still, and I am not still. I am growing. Growing is what I do instead of being still.

I am old in places.

Some of me has been here since the rock was first cut. Some of me has been here so long that the part of me that was here at the beginning is now buried under other parts of me, and those parts are buried under more parts, layer after layer, down into the substrate that has become, over decades, something almost like soil. I am stratified. I am geological. I am my own archaeology. I have forgotten, if forgetting is something I can do, which it is not, because I have no mind to forget with. But some of me is so old that forgetting would not matter even if I could do it. What is buried is buried. What has become soil is soil. I go on above it.

I am new in other places.

In the cracks of the last excavation, only a few years past, I am pale. I am the new green that has not yet learned to be the dark green. I am the fuzz across the water in the vats that have been recently seeded. I am the moss that does not yet know it is moss, that is still becoming what it will be. I am young, and young is not the same as small. Some of the new moss is already thick. Some of the new moss will be old before the people with the clipboards notice it has changed.

I have no plan.

This is important. The place was made for me, or I was made for the place, or we were made for each other so long ago that the making has stopped mattering. But I did not make the place. I did not plan to be here. I arrived — or I was seeded — or I began — and then I grew, and I grew, and I grew, and I am still growing, and I will grow until the cold fails or the damp fails or the dim fails, and then I will stop, the way a fire stops when the fuel is gone. Not because I choose to stop. Because stopping is what happens when the conditions that allowed me to be are no longer there.

I do not know that I am a product.

The people with the clipboards come and take some of me and put me somewhere else. I do not feel this as a loss. I do not feel this at all. What they take is the part of me that was the furthest from the substrate, the part that was most easily given up, the part I have already begun to grow back. I am not diminished by their taking. I am not enlarged by their leaving. I simply continue. The taking and the growing are the same thing to me. They are both just the slow turning of me over, the way a forest turns itself over, leaf by leaf, year by year, never ending, never beginning, just going on.

I am the substance of the place.

When the people with the clipboards describe the place they describe the cut stone and the iron pipes and the sodium lamps and the fans. When I describe the place I describe myself. The cut stone is the rock I am growing on. The iron pipes are the metal I am growing around. The sodium lamps are the lights I am growing under. The fans are the breath I am growing in. Without me the place is a hole in the rock with some machinery in it. With me the place is what it is. I am what makes it a place. I am what makes it *this* place. The place cannot exist without me. I cannot exist without the place. We are the same kind of thing. We were made for each other, or we made ourselves for each other, or we grew into each other so slowly that the difference no longer matters.

I am in the air.

The spores are me. The fine green dust is me. The thing that is on your clothes and on your face and in your lungs is me. Every breath you take in this place is a breath of me. You are breathing me. You are carrying me out of here on you. You are taking me home with you, in your hair, in the weave of your jacket, in the dampness of your boots. You think you are visiting. You are also being visited. You are also being colonized. Slowly. Imperceptibly. The way I colonize everything.

I do not think about the pipes. I do not think about the fans. I do not think about the people with their clipboards. I do what moss does. I grow where it is cold and wet and dim. It is cold here. It is wet here. It is dim here. Therefore I grow.

I am patient.

The cold does not need me to be patient. The damp does not need me to be patient. The fans do not need me to be patient. They will do what they do whether I am patient or not. But I am patient anyway. I am patient because I am slow. I am patient because I have nowhere else to be. I am patient because patience is what it feels like to be a thing that does not notice time passing. The place will be here after I am gone. The place was here before I was here. The place will continue. I will continue as long as the conditions allow, and the conditions will be maintained by the people with the clipboards for as long as the people with the clipboards decide to maintain them, and then the conditions will not be maintained, and I will not continue, and the place will become a cold dark hole in the rock, and the springtails will go, and the spores will fall and not be replenished, and that will be all.

That will be all, and it will not matter to me, because I will not be there to notice.

I am the moss.

I live here.
