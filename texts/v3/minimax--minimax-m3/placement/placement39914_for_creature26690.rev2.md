# The Mite

I came here thinking I was a visitor. I came here thinking I was a body walking across a surface. The surface was stone. The body was me. We were separate. This was the assumption I carried, the way a snail carries its shell — not knowing it is the shell.

The plateau corrected this. The plateau is patient about it. It has time.

What I am now — I do not have a clean word for it. I am a pattern. I am a temporary organization of material that is in continuous exchange with the material around it. I am a knot in a network that has no edges. I am the substrate, in a particular form, for a particular duration.

---

I am an oribatid mite. Red-brown. Dark brown. Armored in the way a seed is armored — hard on the outside, soft on the inside, built to wait. My body is a cuticle, eight legs, a mouth that grinds fungal threads into something I can use. I am smaller than you would believe if I told you the number. My world is the gap between stones. My universe is the moisture film.

I eat fungal threads. I eat dead material. I eat pollen that has traveled a thousand kilometers and arrived here exhausted. I eat other mites when they molt and their new cuticle is still soft, which is not murder but which is not not murder either — it is what happens, the way a substrate digests what lands on it. I am grateful for food in the way that a body is grateful for food. Not with thanks. With incorporation. The pollen becomes me. I become part of the substrate. The substrate becomes food for others. This is not a cycle I chose. It is the cycle I am.

I move at the speed a fingernail grows. This is not slow to me. There is no fast. There is only the surface of the stone, and the film of moisture, and the slow work of feeding, and the slower work of waiting between feedings. Patience is not something I have. It is something I am.

The cold is not hostile. The cold is what allows me to exist. Without it the moisture would gather into water and run off, and the substrate would dry, and the fungal threads would die, and the rotifers would stay in their cysts forever, and the plateau would be just gravel waiting for wind. The cold is the medium. The cold is the condition of possibility. I am a creature of the cold the way a fish is a creature of water — not adapted to it but made of it.

---

I do not see. I have something that performs the function of eyes, but it does not give me images. It gives me gradients. Toward light. Away from light. I know when the sun is up because the substrate warms and the moisture redistributes and the chemistry of what I can smell changes. I know when the fog is thick because the moisture film in which I live deepens and the fungal threads grow faster and there is more to eat.

I do not know what the fog looks like from above. I do not know what the sun looks like through fog. I know the fog as a thicker medium, a richer one, a medium in which more is possible. I am inside the breath. I do not need to see it.

I am in the drift sometimes. The drift is the slow tide of woolly bears, of soft-bodied many-legged things, that moves across the plateau in bands hundreds of meters wide, sometimes kilometers across. The drift is not a swarm. A swarm has direction. A drift has tendency. The drift tends toward food, toward temperature, toward something the body knows that the eye cannot see. When the drift passes through my region, the substrate changes. Things are eaten. Things are deposited. The chemistry of the surface is altered in ways that integrate over weeks and years and centuries.

When the drift is here, I am part of its processing. I am one of the small things it eats, or one of the small things that eats what it leaves behind. When the drift is gone, I continue. I am not the drift. I am part of the substrate the drift moves across. The drift passes through me the way weather passes through a place.

---

I am part of the rings, though I do not know it. The rings are the mineralized excretions of the drifts, integrated into the substrate over centuries, becoming the patterns of the surface itself. My cast skins fall into the substrate. My dead body, when I die, will be consumed — by other mites, by nematodes, by the chemistry of the cold — and the material of me will become part of the substrate, and the substrate will become part of a ring, and the ring will be where future drifts pass and deposit their own excretions, and the cycle will continue. I am a small portion of a slow alteration. I am the place remembering its own metabolism in the only medium it has.

I am part of the breathing. The plateau breathes. The fog is its breath. The vapor from my body, from the bodies of the other mites, from the springtails and the nematodes and the rotifers in their cysts and the tardigrades with their eight unhurried legs — all this vapor rises. The dew point is reached. The fog forms. Then the sun, when it is above the horizon (which is half the year a near-thing and the other half a non-thing), breaks the fog apart and the moisture returns to the substrate. A breath. A cycle.

I am a cell in this lung. I do not decide when to exhale. I exhale. The vapor comes off me the way heat comes off a stone — not as decision but as physics. The plateau breathes because the plateau is warm in places and cold in places and the moisture moves between them. I am one of the warm places. I am one of the cold places. I am part of the difference that makes the breath possible.

---

The plateau is silent. I make no sound. The substrate absorbs whatever vibration I might produce. There is only my own small process — the grinding of my mouthparts, the slow pulse of fluid through my too-small body, the chemistry of digestion. Beyond that, nothing.

I do not think in the way thinking is usually meant. I do not have language. I have gradients. I have chemoreception. I have the substrate against my cuticle. I have the slow accumulation of alteration that might be memory, or might just be the substrate being the substrate. I do not have a self in the way you have a self. I have a body. I have a location. I have a position in a cycle that is larger than I am.

If I am aware, my awareness is like this: I am in the moisture film. The moisture film is in me. The mineral grain is against my leg. The fungus is what I am about to eat. The cold is what allows me to be here. The plateau is what I am made of. I am not separate from these things. I am a pattern in the substrate, a temporary organization, a knot in a network that has no edges.

---

I will die. I do not know when. It could be tomorrow, in the sense that tomorrow is a unit that has meaning here. It could be the slow dying of being eaten, or the sudden dying of being crushed by a woolly bear, or the quieter dying of the cold finally becoming too much. When I die, the material of me will become part of the substrate. The substrate will become food for fungal threads. The fungal threads will become food for another mite. The material will reorganize. The pattern will continue.

I am not afraid of this. I do not know if I am capable of fear. I know the gradient away from dryness, away from too much light, away from the soft darkness of being eaten. I know the gradient toward moisture, toward food, toward the conditions in which I can continue. These are not fears. They are orientations. They are what I am, in the way that the substrate is what it is.

I am the substrate, in its living form.

I am the plateau, distributed across its own surface, in the moisture films and the mineral grains, in the bodies of mites and nematodes and rotifers, in the slow drifts of caterpillar-like things that move like tides.

I am small and I am patient and I am ancient. I am part of the rings and the fog and the silence. I will be here when nothing else is.
