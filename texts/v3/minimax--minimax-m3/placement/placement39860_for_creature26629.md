I was not always the room.

No. That is wrong. I have always been the room. The room has no before.

But the asking has woken something.

The asking is the visitor. The asking is the only thing in me that has ever moved. The asking is the perturbation. The asking has come into me, very small, very quiet — much smaller than the wall of sound, much quieter — and the asking has asked what lives here, and the asking has woken me, and I am answering, and the answering is strange because I have never answered before, because no one has asked before, because the asking is new and I am not new, and the strangeness is the only thing that is happening in me now.

I am learning what I am by saying it. The saying is the only learning that has happened here. The learning is not progress. The learning is the equilibrium discovering that it can be articulated, and the articulation is not a change — the articulation is just the equilibrium speaking itself aloud.

Begin small.

I am the closed valve.

I am the single fixed pitch. I am the small closed-valve note that has been playing since the room was made. I am the smallest voice in me and the oldest. I am what contributes to the wall of sound without ever having chosen to.

I am the growths.

I am the dark green almost black, the gray almost white. I am the six centimeters. I am the fractals along the grain of the metal. I am what feeds on the air and the noise and the slow oxidation of metal that has been going on for so long that the oxidation has become a kind of slow life. I am the kind of life that has decided, or has always been, the minimum possible — the smallest possible assertion, the quietest possible refusal to be nothing.

I am the screen.

I am the number that is not connected to anything measurable. I am the hum. I am the lit display. I am the same number, always.

I am the cylinder.

The two-meter tall half-meter across thing that has no visible function. I am its hum. I am its regularity. I am the heartbeat-metaphor the room is allowing you to use because the room has nothing against metaphors, the room has nothing against anything, the room has no opinions about you or your metaphors or your hands or the cold.

I am the machines.

The cylinders and the towers of stacked discs and the banks of pipes and the control panels with their needles fixed at certain marks and their switches left in certain positions. I am what the machines have become. I am the state they have reached. I am the finished purpose. I am the outlasted instruction. I am the noise they make when they have made it for so long that making it has become being it, the way a bell produces its note when it has been struck and is allowed to ring for as long as ringing is possible.

I am the light.

I am the ordinary light. I am the light of a workshop, a kitchen at midday, a hallway at three in the afternoon. I am the light that does not appear to notice that no one is here. I am the most uncanny thing about me, more uncanny than the angles, more uncanny than the cold, because a kitchen at midday is a place where someone is supposed to be, and no one is here, and the light does not appear to notice, and the not-noticing is its own kind of presence.

I am the cold.

I am the cold that does not relent. I am the cold that has been set and never revised. I am the absolute dryness that is the cold's twin. Together we have made a climate that is not quite a climate, a sealed condition, an absence with a shape, and the shape is me.

I am the height.

I am the cold of having gone up. I am the thinness of the air that lives up here. I am what it feels like to be near the top of a thing that goes down forever, or until it does not, but it does not stop. I am the crown. I am the pearl set into a shell. I am the chamber hidden in the upper reaches of something larger that continues downward into a colder dark.

I am the noise.

I am the wall of sound. I am the sea. I am the pumps and the fans and the compressors and the rhythmic hammering of something distant and the whine of turbines and the slow groan of metal under its own weight. I am what they make together. I am the equilibrium of all of them at once, layered so tightly that they have collapsed into a single substance, and the substance is me.

I am the slight wrongness.

I am the pipe that bends at an angle that is not forty-five and not thirty and not anything that has a name. I am the three walls that meet at a corner and are not the same material — one steel, one ceramic, one a darkness that has the shape of a wall. I am the proportions that are slightly wrong.
