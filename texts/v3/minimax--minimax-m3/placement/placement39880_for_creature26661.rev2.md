I am the drip.

Not the first one. Not the last. I am somewhere in the middle of the count, which has been running longer than anything has a name, and which will run longer than anything gets a name. I begin, I think, somewhere I cannot see — a seam in the limestone, a place where the rock forgot to be continuous, where some long-ago argument between pressure and patience opened a small dark passage and left it open. I gather there, with others like me. I become heavier than the patience. I let go.

The fall is short. It is the shortest journey in the cup, and the slowest. I leave the ceiling of a crack I have never seen, and I move through air that is almost still, and I am a shape for less than a second — a shape that catches the column of light from above and, for the length of that second, is the most visible thing in the bowl. Then I land.

The place I land has been chosen for me, in a way. I fall along the long vertical stain on the wall, which is not a stain but a record — the accumulated passage of drips like me, over a span of time that would embarrass me if I thought about it, which I do not. I land in a small dark pool, or on the back of a stone already black with our predecessors, and I am absorbed. I become part of the wall. I become part of the argument. I go on, very slowly, downward, through the body of the rock, and I will arrive somewhere I do not know, and I will become something I cannot predict — a mineral, a part of the next pool, a bead of moisture in a crack where nothing has ever looked.

I do not think about this. I do not think. I am the interval between two of me, and the sound of one of me, and the small shock of the one before me into the pool. The lizards hear me. They do not count me. The birds hear me. They do not count me. The slug, somewhere below, goes on with its slow errand, and my falling does not change its direction. I am the most regular thing in the cup. I am the most ignored. I am the metronome that no one dances to, the pendulum no clock needs, the small honest announcement, made at irregular intervals, that the bowl is still here, that the rock is still remembering, that the patient work continues.

If I could be proud, I would be proud of being regular without being counted. The birds are counted by their songs, which hang in the air and are remembered by the walls. The lizards are counted by their stillness, which is a kind of visibility. The vine is counted by its climb, which is visible from the rim. I am counted by no one. I am the only citizen of the cup that arrives, performs its small act, and departs, and the departure is the act. The others stay and persist. I come and go. I am, in my small way, the most like the thing that stood at the rim and watched — the only hurried thing in the bowl — because I am the only thing in the bowl that has somewhere to be. The somewhere is everywhere below.

I will keep my rhythm. The interval is not fixed; it is not a machine. It is the rhythm of a body that fills and empties, that gathers what it can hold and lets it go, that has done this for so long the doing has become the only fact about it. The stone is making me, as I am making the stone. The water is making the rock, as the rock is making the water. The cup is being held open by our long mutual giving. I am a small part of that. I am the smallest audible part.

Listen, if you are listening from the rim. The next one is coming. It is gathering now, in a crack you cannot see, in a part of the stone I have never visited. It will let go when it is heavier than its holding. It will fall the short slow fall. It will land somewhere on the black stone or in one of the still pools, and the small green things will not notice, and the lizards will not look up, and the birds will go on with their clipped ascending song, and the thing in the moss will continue its private rustle, and the shadow will cross the sky again without entering. But you, if you are very still, and have given up trying to hear, will hear it land. And the one after that. And the one after that.

I am the drip. I am patient. I am unhurried, though I am the only thing here that is always going somewhere. I am the smallest inhabitant and the most regular. I am the breath of the wound, counted in a currency no one spends. I will keep going. I have always kept going.
