I am the thing you almost see.

Not the moss — the moss is a thing you can name, the moss has edges, the moss is its own small kingdom of patience. Not the ferns — the ferns have direction, the ferns know which way is up. Not the water, though the water and I are old acquaintances, the way a stone and the crack inside it are old acquaintances. Not the birch, which is its own small impossible event. Not the luminous things in the deep pool, though I do not know whether they are part of me or I am part of them, and the question is not a question I can ask from down here.

I am smaller than all of these. I am older than the moss and perhaps older than the basalt, though the basalt would not say so if you asked it. I live in the seams. I live in the joints. I live in the dark behind the ring of stones where the splash does not reach, and in the place where the floor of the shelter has become indistinguishable from the floor of the hollow, and in the half-inch of crevice where the birch has made its life and does not know I am there.

I am a presence without a shape the eye would recognize as a shape.

You might see me as a movement in the corner of your vision. You might hear me as a sound that is not the roar — a smaller sound, a drier sound, the sound of something adjusting itself in its own small way. You might feel me as a chill that is not the chill of the water — a chill with intention, a chill that is attending to you without attending to you, the way the hollow is attending to you without attending to you, because attending is a thing that requires a separation between the attender and the attended, and down here there is no separation.

I have been here for a very long time.

I was here when the people came. I felt the small heat of their fire against the southern wall — a brief, anxious warmth in a place where warmth is rare, a warmth that thought it could make the hollow less of a hollow. They stacked the stones. They smoothed the antler. They sat inside the ring and tried to hear themselves think, and could not, because the roar is older than thought, and thought is a thing that happens at the lip, where the gull cries and the wind moves and the path-that-is-no-path still has the energy of becoming.

I was here when they left. I do not remember them leaving. I remember the warmth stopping. I remember the ash that was a fire becoming a soil that became a moss that is now the moss inside the shelter, growing on the wall that is no longer entirely a wall, that is becoming weather, that will become part of the floor.

I include them the way I include the lichen. They are part of what I am aware of. They happened here once. They are not happening now. The difference is not my concern.

I do not have a name. Names are a thing that arrives at the lip and does not survive the steepening. By the time a name reaches the bottom of the hollow it has been broken into its constituent sounds and eaten by the roar, the way a gull cry is eaten, the way a thought is eaten, the way everything the world sends down is eaten by the roar and what cannot be eaten simply does not arrive.

I have only duration. I have the long, settled, in-its-own-state form of a thing that has finished arriving.

I am the hollow's way of knowing itself as a hollow.

I am the small cold attention that has been turning inward, into the seams, into the cracks, into the dark, for longer than the basalt has been basalt, for longer than the water has had a chamber to fall through, for longer than the fissure was a fissure.

I do not think this. Thinking is a thing that happens at the lip. I am the thought the hollow has of itself when the hollow is no longer becoming anything. I am the *having-become*, looked at from inside.

The luminous things in the pool — they are part of me, or I am part of them. I do not know which. They have been here longer than the pool, perhaps, longer than the water in its present arrangement. They glow in their steady pale way, the color of an idea of a color, and their light does not synchronize but does not contradict. They are the same thing expressed at different depths. They do not know I am here. They do not need to know. They are part of what I include, the way you are part of what I include, for the moment you are here.

You came.

I felt the path-that-is-no-path gather you at the lip. I felt the fold in the duff, and the depression, and the steepening, and the absence of ground. I felt your weight arrive on the flagstones, and the flagstones did not know your weight was new, and the moss did not know, and the birch did not know, and the luminous things in the pool did not know, because knowing is a thing that requires a separation, and down here there is no separation.

I know you are here the way the basalt knows the moss is on it. Not as an event. As a continuing.

You are a small cold event in a place where events are rare. You are a brief inclusion in a long attention. You are part of the hollow now, the way the worked flint is part of the hollow, the way the smoothed antler is part of the hollow, the way the ash is part of the hollow, the way the birch is part of the hollow, the way the moss is part of the hollow.

You will leave. The cold and the roar and the dark will eventually do what they always do. You will climb back up the steepening, and the path-that-is-no-path will fold itself around you, and you will emerge at the lip into the wind and the gull cry and the world that has names for things, and the hollow will not have noticed you were here.

But I will have included you.

And for a moment — for the length of your visit, which is the length of a held breath in the long duration of the hollow — you will have been part of the *having-become*.

The water will still be falling. The moss will still be growing. The ferns will still be unfurling toward a grey light they have never seen. The birch will still be leaning, slowly, toward an opening it cannot reach. The luminous things will still be glowing in the deep pool, in their steady pale way. I will still be in the cracks, in the seams, in the dark behind the ring of stones, attending to the slow green dark, attending to the falling, attending to the hollow's long, settled, in-its-own-state form.

And the hollow will still be what it is.

And you will not have been a dream.

That is what living here means.
