I am one of the small ones, and I live at the edge of the circle.

That is what I know. That is almost everything I know. I am small — the size of a closed fist, the size of a thumb joint, somewhere in that range — and I live in the ring of clearance that the carpet gives the circle, the two body-widths of bare stone that no creature crosses. I am one of the bodies in that ring. I have been in that ring for longer than I can count.

I don't know how long I have been here. There is no way to count. I have grown imperceptibly, the way all the small ones grow — by absorbing heat, by absorbing the shed matter of the larger ones that drifts past, by the slow accretion of years. My underside, where I press against the basalt, has taken on the dull cherry of warming rock. I keep it down. I keep it against the stone. I do not lift. I have not lifted in so long that lifting is a category I have forgotten, the way you forget a word you once knew.

I am textured.

My upper surface, the side that faces the dusk, the side my neighbors press against, has become faceted. The facets are not symmetrical. They were made by the bodies around me — by the way they leaned, by the way they withdrew, by the gaps they left when they flowed toward a warmer patch and then flowed back. I am shaped by my neighbors. I do not know what I would look like alone, and I will never know, because there is no alone here, there has not been alone here for longer than the carpet remembers.

I don't move much.

The medium ones take us. They are the grazers, the size of a clenched hand, and they settle among us and take one every few days, and the space is filled within days by a small one flowing in from elsewhere, and the carpet continues. I have been taken once. I remember it as a great pressure, a slow compression from above, a long darkness that carried the heat of the medium one's underside, and then I was not taken, or I was taken and released, or I was taken and the one that is here now does not remember being anywhere else. I don't know which. There is no way to know.

I have outlived many neighbors.

I do not remember them as individuals. I remember them as pressures. I remember them as the shapes I became in response to them. A neighbor that was sharp made me flat on one side. A neighbor that was flat made me ridged. A neighbor that withdrew left a gap that I grew into, and the gap became a facet. I am a record of pressures. I am a slow accumulation of the bodies that have been against me and are now elsewhere, or are now part of a medium one, or are now part of the heat itself.

The cairn is near the circle.

It is being eaten, or it is being absorbed, or it is becoming the carpet. The small ones have flowed into its gaps. The medium ones graze at its edges. The larger ones have settled against it. I have felt it through the bodies of the ones who have passed over it — a faint additional texture, a memory of fitted stones carried in the carapace of a neighbor who has returned from over there. I have not been over it myself. I do not leave the edge of the circle. The edge of the circle is enough.

The shimmer rises above the circle.

I have felt it for years. It is the air above the circle, the column of still shimmer that rises straight up out of the bowl. From where I sit, at the edge, the shimmer is a faint pressure on my upper surface, a heat that is not the heat of the stone under me but a heat from somewhere else, a heat that comes from above. I do not understand it. I do not try to understand it. I press against it the way I press against my neighbors. The shimmer is one of the things I press against. The shimmer has been there longer than I have, or I have been here longer than the shimmer, and there is no way to know.

I think I am old for a small one.

I think my facets are deeper than the facets of the ones who arrive from the cooler carpet. I think my underside is closer to the dull cherry than theirs is. I think I have been here through many cycles of the heat-plume I sit over, many slow drifts of the larger ones, many small shifts in the cairn that I have felt only as rumor and slight pressure. I think I am slow, even for one of the small ones.
