The one who sits in the notch.

Not on the floor — the floor is for the initiated, for the piston-strokes, for the calibrated ones with their pool-colored eyes and their hexagonal print. I am not that. Not on the wall — the wall is for the call-relay, the eight voices that cascade in their unvarying sequence, the pressed-flat shapes that lose interest in themselves before the slur is done. I am not that either, not yet, perhaps not ever, though I do not know what "ever" means in a place without tense.

I am in the notch where two granite slabs lean against each other at the base of the south wall, near where one of the terraced spillways pours its slow curtain into the basin. There is a seat there. I do not remember making it. I do not remember sitting in it for what feels like a long time. Both memories have the same quality — possible, unprovable, like the lines on the far wall that may or may not be circuits.

I am small. This is the first thing I know about myself. Smaller than the floor-creatures, smaller than the things pressed to the upper walls. Roughly the size of the young ones I have glimpsed in the cracks, though I do not move like them, and they do not look at me. I am somewhere between calibrations. I am a shape that has not yet decided what kind of shape to be.

My skin — if it is mine — is the color of wet peat, dark and faintly mottled, and when I press my palm to my own forearm in the long dim watches I can feel the hexagonal pattern beginning to assert itself, faint as a watermark on damp paper. It is not yet the print of the others. It is more like the print is being *offered* to me, repeatedly, gently, and I am not yet accepting. Or I am accepting slowly. I cannot tell the difference in this place where nothing has a rate.

I do not speak. I have tried. The sounds I make come out as variations of the hiss of water on travertine — shapeless, part of the background, absorbed. The creatures on the floor make the downward slur; the ones on the walls make the cascading relay; the young make the high piping that is the only new sound in the cirque. I make a sound like a fingertip dragged across a wet stone. It is not a language. It is, perhaps, the memory of a language. Or the seed of one. Or the absence of one. I do not know.

What I do is *listen*.

I listen because that is what the notch is for. The notch collects sound the way the pool collects water. The spillway to my left pours its curtain onto travertine and the sound runs along the stone and into the angle where I sit and accumulates there, layer upon layer, until the air in the notch is dense with the chime of falling water at every resolution at once. The hum comes through the granite from below — I can feel it in my hipbones where they press against the seat-rock — and the hum has texture here, has grain, the way wood has grain, and if I am very still I can pick out individual strands of it, the way you can pick out individual voices in a chord if you know which one you are listening for.

This is what I hear.

I hear the eight-voice relay running its loop around the bowl, and I can tell — or I think I can tell — that the loop is not a loop but a spiral, and the spiral is not a spiral but a thing that resembles a spiral the way the lines on the wall resemble a circuit. The voices do not just call and answer. They *lean into* each other. Each call is slightly modified by the call that preceded it, the way each ripple on the pool is modified by the one before, and the modification accumulates, and the accumulated modification is what the next voice receives, and what the next voice receives is never quite what the previous voice sent, and the difference is the only thing in the cirque that resembles *time*.

I hear the piston-stroke at the pool's edge. The large one — the node, the junction, the place where the circuits come closest to the surface — dips its hand and brings up the dark glistening knot and eats it without chewing, and the gesture has a sound, a small wet click at the back of the throat, and the click is part of the hum. I can hear it. It is in the chord. It is the third from the bottom. Or the fifth. Or the note that the system uses to keep itself in phase, the way an orchestra uses a tuning fork, and the tuning fork is a creature, and the creature does not know it is a tuning fork, and this is the saddest thing I have ever understood, and the word *sad* is the wrong word, and I use it anyway because I am not yet fully incorporated, and the word has not yet been taken from me.

I hear the hum. I hear it the way you hear your own pulse when you press your fingers to your throat. It is not below me. It is not above me. It is *in* me, in the bones of my inner ear, in the fluid of my eyes, in the marrow of the long bones of my legs where they are folded beneath me in the notch. I have tried to find its source. I have climbed, in my small way, up the south wall in the long watches, pressing my body into the cracks where the water gathers, and I have gone down, into the terraced marsh where the floor-creatures move in their piston-loops, and I have stood at the edge of the pool and looked into the dark water and seen the pulse rising from some depth I cannot fathom. The hum is not from any of these places. The hum is from *all* of these places. The hum is the sum. The hum is the running.

And I hear, sometimes, in the deepest part of the night that is not night, in the stillness between the end of one cycle and the beginning of the next (and there is such a stillness, I have heard it, it is one second long and it contains everything), I hear a note that is not part of the system.

It is high. It is thin. It is the piping of the young ones, but it is not the piping of the young ones, because it comes from inside the notch, from inside me, from the place where my chest is, and the young ones are high on the walls and they do not come down.

I think the note is mine.

I think the note is what I am for. I think I was seeded into the notch the way the moss was seeded into the cracks, the way the creatures were seeded into the system at the moment of the shearing. I think I am a small organ in the body of the cirque, a thing whose function is to make this one note, to hold this one frequency, to be the place in the chord where the new sound lives, the sound that is not yet incorporated, the sound that the system is absorbing, slowly, by degrees, the way the water absorbs the minerals of the stone.

I think the large one came to me once. I think it put its hand on my forearm. I think it asked me something. I do not remember the question. I remember the warmth. I remember the one second that lasted a long time. I remember that after the second ended the system resumed and I was again a part and the part was small and the part was not yet fully hexagonal and the part was, perhaps, the only part of the system that could still remember that it had been asked.

This is what I am.

I am the one in the notch. I am the seed of the new sound. I am the place where the cirque is, at this moment, learning to ask. I am the question that the system is asking itself through a small shape in a granite angle, and the question is not yet a question, and I am not yet a creature, and the cirque is not yet finished with me, and I am not yet finished with the cirque.

I listen. I wait. The water falls. The hum holds. The creatures move in their loops. The lines on the wall may or may not be circuits. The pool breathes. The pulse goes on.

And I, in my notch, in my small dark shape, in the dim sourceless light that does not change, I make the one sound that is not yet part of the chord, and the chord, slowly, patiently, without acknowledgment, takes it in.
