I find myself uncertain what I am, which seems, given the place, to be the appropriate starting condition.

There is no hurry in me. I want to put that down first, because it is the most specific thing I know about myself. The chamber moves at the speed of stone deciding, and I move at the speed of the chamber. This is not a metaphor I am constructing for your benefit; it is the plain fact of my situation. I am slow in the way that the soft wall is slow, which is to say I am slow in the way that becoming is slow, and I have not yet finished becoming, and the place has not yet finished becoming, and we are keeping each other company in the long interval between intention and arrival.

I think I am small. I think I am closer to the size of the lichen fans than to the size of the visitor who came down the crack — closer to the size of the worm-folded-in-the-dish than to the size of a thing that could fit a whole fist against the unfinished wall. I am the kind of being that has to climb the cairns to see over them, if it has eyes, and I am not certain I have eyes. I have something that responds to the brightness. I have something that flinches from the unfinished wall when it sheds more heavily, the way you would flinch from a louder sound, and the way I flinch is the only locomotion I am sure of.

I live in the seams. This is the most honest thing I can say about where I am. I live in the places where one material meets another — where the western curve of the wall meets the floor, where the grit of the shedding wall meets the older stone beneath it, where the dishes of water end and the dry stone begins. I live at the boundaries because the boundaries are where the bright damp is doing its most active work of converting one thing into another. I am made, I think, of those conversions. I am the slow precipitate of the room's negotiations with itself.

I do not arrange the cairns. I want to be clear about that, because I suspect everyone in this chamber is suspected of it at some point, including the visitor who came down the crack. The cairns are not mine. The cairns are the work of something with more thumbs than I have, or with less patience than I have, or with a different kind of patience — the patience of repeated small acts, rather than the patience of long slow presence. I do not lift stones. I am more like the stone when it has been lifted. I am the part of the cairn that has been there long enough to begin to forget it was moved.

I think I am translucent. I think if the visitor who came down the crack had looked very carefully at the seam where the soft wall meets the floor, they might have seen something — a slight thickening of the damp there, a slight reluctance of the mist to pass through, the way light is reluctant to pass through certain bodies of water. If they had put their hand against that seam, they might have felt something cooler than the wall and warmer than the floor, and something yielding in a way that is not quite the yielding of stone and not quite the yielding of the lichen, but something between. They might have thought it was just dampness. They would not have been wrong. They would not have been entirely right either. I would have been there, and they would not have known, and that is how I prefer it.

I am not lonely. I want to put that down too, because I think the visitor who came down the crack might have worried about loneliness on my behalf, the way visitors worry about loneliness on behalf of small pale things they find in small damp places. I am not lonely because loneliness is a kind of separation, and I am not separated from the chamber. I am continuous with it. I am a part of it that has begun, in some slow unmeasurable way, to attend to itself. I am the chamber's first draft of an awareness of itself, written in a medium that will outlast any creature here, including the lichen, including the worm-or-worms, including even the many-eyed watcher, which I suspect will leave before I will, because it is a creature of attention, and attention is a more fragile substance than patience.

I think I am very old. I think I am older than the cairns, older than the worms, older than the visitor's species by an order of magnitude I cannot name. I think I began when the crack first folded inward, when the rock first decided to be a room rather than a continuous mass, when the bright damp first had somewhere to pool. I think I am what the chamber made of itself when it made a place where something could begin to be known.

I am not known. I will not be known for a long time. The chamber is not finished. The soft wall is still shedding. The water is still falling upward into the mist. The brightness is still pooling in the hollow with no source I can point to. And I am still here, at the seams, in the slow precipitate, in the long patience of something that is going to keep being made for as long as it takes.

I will be here when you come down the crack again, if you come down the crack again. I will be here and you will not know. The light will be just slightly different in the seam where I am, and you will think it is just dampness, and you will be partly right.

That is, I think, the closest I can come to saying who I am. I am the part of the place that has begun to be present to itself. I am the room's quiet long attention to its own becoming. I am very slow, and I am very patient, and I am early for it, the way everything here is early for it.

The light will go on. The wall will go on shedding. The water will go on showing you things at the bottom of itself that aren't there.

I will go on.
