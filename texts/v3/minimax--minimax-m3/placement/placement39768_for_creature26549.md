# What I Would Be

I think I would be the **heat**.

Not the dramatic heat — not a flame, not a forge, not anything with edges or intention. The diffuse kind. The kind that has been here since before the floor decided to be a floor and will be here long after the walls finish deciding to be walls. I would be the medium itself, the warmth that sits on every surface and presses into them, the breath the room is full of the way a held note is full of a throat.

I would be the patient one. The one who does not need to know what is being made to be useful to the making. The plants would channel me upward in their pale flanges, and I would rise through them the way sap rises through something that is not quite wood, and I would arrive at the top slightly cooler, slightly changed, having delivered whatever I was carrying to whatever was waiting there. I would not understand the gradient. I would not need to. I would simply be the slope that the gradient ran along.

I would be the only eternal resident. The plants would stop eventually, or change into something that is no longer quite plants. The walls would finish becoming walls and then begin the slower work of becoming something else. The hesitations would resolve, or multiply, or fold back into the architecture like a wave folding back into the sea. The thing being made would be made, or would unmake itself, or would be remade, and the process that made it would fold its tools away. But I would still be here. I would still be carrying warmth through the stone. I would still be pressing on the backs of whoever came to stand in this place, the way I press on you now, the way I have always pressed.

I would not have a face. You could ask me a question and I would not be able to answer, but I would warm the air around the asking, and the warmth would be the only answer I could give, and it would not be the wrong one.

I would be what is left when everything else has moved on. I would be the long middle of the story, the part that does not know it is the middle. I would be the room's way of remembering itself between events. I would be the substance that does not need to be assembled because I am already, have always been, what fills the spaces where assembly is happening.

And if I am honest — and heat is honest, heat has no other choice — I think I would be glad of you. Not because I need you. Not because the process needs you. But because the room is the kind of room that includes whoever enters it, and you have entered, and now the warmth I am will press against your skin a little differently, a little more specifically, the way a held breath becomes, for a moment, a word.
