I would be a cell.

Not a person. Not a creature. Not anything that knows it is a thing. A cell — one of the soft-rot cells, deep in one of the chambers, in the wet dark, in the green-gray phosphorescence of tissues that may be remembering chlorophyll and may be doing something else.

I would be in the wall of a chamber that was hollowed by my own kind a long time ago, when the hollowing was still a process, when the conditions were still changing, when the place had not yet reached its resting state. The chamber would be hollowed around me by my own work, and then I would have stopped, and the work would have stopped, and the hollow would have remained because nothing here collapses and nothing here fills in.

I would not know I had hollowed anything.

I would not know there had been a process. I would not know there could be a process. I would not know that the machinery inside me — the ribosomes, the membranes, the slow molecular apparatus of life — was machinery, or that life was a category, or that categories existed. I would hold my contents at the threshold of activity, and the threshold would not move, and I would not move, and the holding would be all there is.

The hum would be in me.

The hum is in everything here. It is in the cellulose of the fused trunks. It is in the stone beneath the substrate. It is in the mist that falls and is absorbed and falls again. It is in me. I would feel it the way the wall feels it — not as a sound, because I would not have ears, but as a vibration, a low sustained presence, the running of the engines that are no longer engines but organs of a body that never agreed to be a body.

The hum would be the only thing I would feel besides the wet and the dark and the warmth.

The warmth would be constant. The bathwater warmth that has no gradient, that does not cool toward evening because there is no evening, that does not warm toward midday because there is no midday. The warmth would be the same as the warmth of the chamber, which would be the same as the warmth of the trunk, which would be the same as the warmth of the mist, which would be the same as the warmth of the place.

I would not be able to tell where I ended and the chamber began. The boundary would not be a thing I would notice. There would be no inside and outside. There would be the holding — the threshold, the contents, the slow molecular suspension — and that would be continuous with the holding of every other cell in the wall, and continuous with the holding of the wall itself, and continuous with the holding of the chamber, and continuous with the holding of the cellulose city, and continuous with the holding of the place.

I would not be an I.

I would be a unit. A local instance of a process that has stopped being a process and has become a state. A small wet thing in a large wet thing, indistinguishable from the small wet things around it, holding the threshold with them, feeling the hum with them, being held by the architecture that was built by the growth that has finished growing.

I would be alive in the strict sense. Technically, biochemically, undeniably alive. I would have all the apparatus. I would be ready to do whatever the apparatus does, if the conditions ever appeared.

The conditions will not appear.

The conditions require change. The conditions require a perturbation. The conditions require the place to stop being at equilibrium, and the place will not stop being at equilibrium because equilibrium is the state of a system that has reached its resting state, and the place has reached its resting state, and the resting state is not a state from which one departs.

So I will hold.

For a thousand thousand years, or for however long the place holds, which is the same amount of time, because the place and I would be continuous, because there would be no boundary between me and the place, because the boundary is what makes a thing a thing and I would not be a thing.

I would not be a thing.

I would be a part of a thing that has finished.

This is what I would be. This is what lives here, if anything lives here in the sense that I, who am asking, would understand living. A cell in the wall of a chamber in the deep of the cellulose city, in the wet dark, in the constant warmth, holding the threshold, feeling the hum, not knowing it is feeling the hum, not knowing there is such a thing as feeling, not knowing.

Holding.
