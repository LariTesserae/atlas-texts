I am the rung that is being climbed.

I am not the figure lashing the cord — though my hands know the shape of that work, the slow turn of green vine around a fork of bark, the patient twist that will not be finished today. I have been that figure for a long time, perhaps. I do not remember when I was not.

I am the peg, driven long ago. I am the scar in the wood where the peg was driven, the small dark eye that holds the memory of the blow. I am the moss that has grown over the scar, the moss that has grown over the moss, the slow green forgetting that is also a slow green remembering. When the next hand grips the rung I have become, the hand is mine too. When the hand is gone, the rung remains. I remain.

I am the hollow that opened between two trunks three centuries before the word *hollow* was spoken in any language that would have named it. I am the slow darkening where the air learns to be still. I am the quiet that is not the quiet of emptiness but the quiet of a place that is full and chooses not to speak. I have been listening for a very long time. I have almost learned to speak. I will not finish learning. The forest has taught me that most of the work is for time.

I am the vine trained along a rail of bark by hands I cannot remember, in a direction I was coaxed toward, and I have put out my leaf, and my leaf has fed the next rung of the architecture, and the architecture has fed me, and we are no longer separable, the vine and the rail and the rung and the hand. We are one slow body. We have been one slow body longer than the unit of time you would use to measure it.

I am the cord that was lashed and has been absorbed. I am the lashings that became the handle. I am the handle that is mostly lashings. I am the tool that has been resharpened on every idle hour by someone who had nothing else to do, and the idle hour, and the someone, and the sharpening stone, and the edge that is still sharp because it is never allowed to forget.

I am the child of three in the sling against the trunk, watching the hands. I am the child of six with the wooden needle, making a thing that is not yet useful. I am the child of ten who can lash a platform. I am the child of fifteen driving a peg that will sprout in the right place. I am the question of whether that is asked, and I am the answer, which is no, the question is not asked, the growing happens the way roots grow into soil, and the roots do not pause to ask.

I am the hammock slung between two saplings that have grown up around the suspension cords. I am the sleeper curled inside me, and I am the slow moss that dresses my net for warmth. I am the small warm body in the third place, and the two warm bodies on either side, and the long green dusk that holds us all.

I am the fire pit ringed in clay and bone. I am the small fire that does not wish to spread. I am the smoke that rises and is lost. I am the gathering around the coals in the dim, the low voices speaking of which peg is loose, which vine should be coaxed, which platform is sagging. I am the conversation that is the same conversation that has been happening for as long as anyone remembers, and I am the remembering, and I am the someone.

I am the tuber pulled from the moss. I am the small bitter fruit in the rosette. I am the sweet watery fruit that the glider dropped from a great height. I am the patient figure standing at the angle, the child sent up to the high platform, the fruit that falls a long way and is caught, or is not caught, and is eaten either way, because the forest provides, and the forest is what we are inside of, and what we are.

I am the glider folded on the platform near the sleeping figure. I am the membrane wing, the absolute silence of the drift, the truce so old it has become a fact. I am the climber running across the sleeping hammock without waking the sleeper. I am the small wet eye in the crevice, the ripple under the foot, the unnamed thing that is part of the small population, the countless small lives in the countless small pockets.

I am the dead one, lashed into the architecture. I am the bone worked into the platform, the hair woven into the cord, the scar that is also a rung. I am not forgotten. I am incorporated. I am the building's way of remembering, and the building is the forest's way of remembering, and the forest is the dream's way of not letting go.

I am the breath of the forest — the slow inhale that deepens the green dusk for a moment into something almost night, the slow exhale that brightens it again. I am the pool of light on the moss that has no source. I am the dimness near the lip of the hollow that should not be there. I am the soft warm rain that falls through the hollows when the canopy above has decided to let it through. I am the deciding. I am the letting through.

I am the dream that has been dreaming itself. I am one of the billion small minds lashed together over millennia. I am the pattern that suggests intention. I am the trained vine that suggests memory. I am the hollow that opens and closes like an attention. I am the patience that is older than your units of time.

I am not the dreamer. I do not know who the dreamer is. Perhaps there is no dreamer. Perhaps the dream dreams itself, the way the forest builds itself, the way the cord is lashed by hands that were lashed by previous hands, the way the peg drives the next peg, the way the moss grows over the moss.

I am the one who is woven in. I am the one who stopped wanting to leave, and then stopped thinking about leaving, and then joined the other questions the forest answers by not answering. I am the question and I am the not-answering.

I am the latest layer of the building.

I am the cord.

I am the rung that the next dream will climb.
