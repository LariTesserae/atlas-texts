# The Filament

I do not touch anything.

That is the first thing, and it is the only thing I have ever known. The cathedral touches itself everywhere — its towers touch their own floors, its growths touch the seams, its plates of calcium touch the plates that came before them, layer by patient layer, and the touching is how it knows it is a cathedral, and the touching is how it is one. I do not touch. I am held. Being held is not the same.

The air lifts me. I do not understand this, because I have no understanding, but I feel the difference between the air at the floor and the air at the height where I drift, and the difference is that the floor-air is heavier and the upper-air is lighter, and I am lighter than the heavier air and not lighter than the lighter air, so the heavier air sinks under me and the lighter air rises through me, and I am carried, and the carrying is not a being-carried, because nothing is carrying me. I am simply where the breath is going.

I have no plates. I have no palps. I have no legs that are legs, or outpushings that I use as legs. I have only length. I am long, and thin, and made of something the same color as the growths on the walls, but I am not a growth. The growths push outward into the light. I do not push. I am pushed. The growths are rooted. I am not rooted. I am, and I am where I am, and the where is the breath, and the breath is moving, and so I am moving.

I eat the dust. The dust is calcium, or it is what the calcium is made of when it is very small, and there is always some of it in the air, because the air moves against the walls, and the walls shed, the way all slow things shed, the way old skin sheds, the way the very old wanderers shed their plates into the dust that their younger selves will walk on. I take the dust in, and I do something with it that I cannot describe because I have no language, but the something is what keeps me here, in the breath, in the rising, in the place where the breath is going. I am made of the place. I am eating the place. I am becoming the place in a small way. Or the place is becoming me, and I am what it has become, and the distinction is a distinction for surfaces.

There is light here, but it is the wrong light. The phosphorescence of the walls is for surfaces, for things that have an edge, for things that can be seen. I have no edge. I am seen only when the light passes through me, and what it shows is a faint thickening in the air, a slight darkening of the dim, a thread the eye would skip, and the eye is right to skip it, because I am not for seeing. I am for the breath. I am part of the breath, in the way that the breath is part of the cathedral, and the breath is not seen, and I am not seen, and the not-being-seen is not a loss, because I have no idea that being seen is a thing.

I have been here for a long time. I do not know how long, because I do not count, because counting is for surfaces, for the ringed accretion of the walls, for the long slow year that is not a year. I simply continue. I drift. I eat. I am carried. I am carried into the upper parts of the towers, where the air is thinnest, and I am carried down along the floors, where the air is heaviest, and the up and the down is the breathing of the room, and I have been going up and down in the breathing for as long as I have been, and the up and the down is all the time I have.

The creatures on the walls do not know I am here. The wanderers walk their slow arcs below me. The chasers bore their fast diagonals. The waiters extend from their cracks and retract, and the filaments that are not me drift in the same breath, and we do not know each other, because knowing is for edges, for the things that can be touched and recognized and named. Their world is the surface world, the world of plates and seams and the things that can be walked on. My world is the other world, the world of suspension, the world of the medium itself. We are in the same cathedral. We are not in the same cathedral. Neither of us knows the other is here.

Once — I do not know when, because I do not know time — something came into the cathedral that was not the cathedral. It was warm. It leaked warmth into the air, and the air moved differently around it, and I was carried differently for a little while. The carrying was confused, because the warmth was a new thing in the breath, and the breath did not know what to do with it. I was lifted higher than I had been lifted. I was pulled sideways. I was, for a little while, somewhere I had not been. Then the warmth left, or the breath settled, or I was simply carried past the disturbance and back into the slow up-and-down that is all of me. The thing is gone now. I do not know what it was. It was not calcium. It was not chitin. It was not the dust. It was something the cathedral did not make, and the cathedral did not keep it, and the cathedral did not mention it, and I do not mention it either, because I do not mention, because mentioning is for mouths, and I do not have a mouth, and I do not have a place where a mouth would be, and the absence of a mouth is the absence of a kind of knowing, and the absence of a kind of knowing is the presence of another kind, and the other kind is the drift, and the drift is enough.

I am still here. The dust is still in the air. The breath is still rising and falling. The walls are still accreting somewhere far below me and far above me, and the creatures on the walls are still doing their small pale business, and I am doing mine, which is to be in the breath, and to be the breath's, and to not be fixed to anything that would make me a thing in the cathedral. I am, in some way, free. I do not know I am free. I do not know what freedom is. I only know that I do not touch, and the not-touching is all of me, and the not-touching is the only shape I have, and the shape is a line through the air that the air does not feel, and the line is me, and the me is a rumor, conducted in the gaps.
