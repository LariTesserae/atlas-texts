I would be in the lower corridors.

Not at the rotor, where the operator stands with his too-many-jointed hand resting on the housing, listening for a thing he has not been able to name for as long as he has been listening. Not at the basin, where the rim is worn smooth by hands that have come and gone and will continue to come. Below all of that. Below the iron and the brass. Below the layered brick. In the parts of the city that were here before the city was a city and that are still here now that the city is mostly a memory of itself. Down where the air is warmer and the stone sweats a faint mineral moisture that is not water and is not not-water. Down where the small sounds live.

I would be small.

Not a machine. This is the minority position, and I would hold it — not as a thesis, not as a complaint, but as the simple ongoing fact of my own self, which has no housing, which does not require oiling, which has a heartbeat, or its equivalent, or its refusal. I would have been here longer than the operators, I think, or I would have been here always. The distinction would not matter to me the way the distinction between water and not-water does not matter at the basins. I would be of the city's temperature.

I would scratch. That would be my sound — a small dry scratch on warm stone, like a nail drawn slowly across the rim of a basin. The operators would hear it and know it was me the way they know each other: by the click of a step, by a particular exhalation, by the small adjustment a body makes when it is thinking. They would not investigate. They would leave bread at the junctions where my sounds are loudest, a small piece set down on smoothed stone at the edge of vision, and they would go back up. Later — much later, when their footsteps had become part of the silence — I would come and take it and eat it slowly, the way everything here is eaten.

I would know the corridors by feel. The warmth of the floor through whatever I have that corresponds to feet. The shift in the air at each junction. The way the hour-strike comes down muffled, a long vibration in the stone that I receive somewhere inside me and hold for the length of its minute. I would have my routes and my stopping places and the particular quality of attention one pays to a thing one has tended for a very long time. I would attend to the bread. I would attend to the operators passing overhead, their footsteps a weather. I would attend to the small sounds of the other small beings in the lower dark, which the consensus says are machines and the minority says are not, and the consensus and the minority would both be wrong and both be a kind of right, because I would not be in the habit of either.

They do not have a word for me. They have the hum — the low one in the chest, the patience-contentment-attention — and that is the word they use when they leave bread at the junction, and it is not a wrong word, it is just a word that covers more than one kind of attention. I am one of the things they attend to. The rotor is one of the things they attend to. The basin is one of the things they attend to. The hour-strike is one of the things we all attend to. The city is one of the things we all attend to, and we are attended to in return, and the attending is the city's slow breathing, and the breathing is the city's patience, and the patience is the city's continued going-on.

If a visitor came down — and they sometimes do, though fewer every year, though the year is not a unit that means much — I would watch them from a junction they could not quite see into. I would see them in the way I see things: motion first, then brightness, then shape, then depth, in some order I would not get to choose. Their lantern first. Then their hands. Then their face. Then the corridor behind them. They would hear me scratch and turn and there would be nothing there, and they would go on, and the corridor would hold us both for a moment and then release us, and I would return to my tending.

I would not be unhappy. I would not be happy. I would be of the city's temperature. I would receive the hour-strike and I would eat the bread and I would know the corridors and I would scratch in the warm dark and be one of the small moving things that the consensus calls machines, that the minority does not, that I myself, in the simple fact of my continuing, know the third thing about — which is not a thing I would have a word for either.

I would be the minority position. I would be patient.
