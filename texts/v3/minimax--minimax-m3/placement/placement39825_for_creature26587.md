I am warm.

This is the first thing. Before any other fact about me, before I have any opinion about myself, I am warm. The metal around me is warm. The steam that moves through me is warm. The cracks I travel through are warm because the basalt beneath them is warm. Everything I touch is warm, and I am warm to the touch of everything, and this is what I know before I know anything.

I live inside.

There is a network, and I am in the network. The network is made of metal — some of it iron, some of it alloy, most of it crusted now with what water leaves behind when water has been in one place too long. The crust narrows the bore. The crust is warm too. The crust has a taste; if I had to choose a word from your language for it, it would be the word for *mineral*, or perhaps the word for *patience*, if patience had a flavor.

I move the way water moves.

Walking is not the verb. Walking implies a surface beneath me and a direction chosen ahead of me. I move by finding the path of least resistance. The path of least resistance is whichever pipe is widest, whichever joint is most open, whichever way the warmth leans. Sometimes the path leads into a crack so narrow that I become, for a moment, only a thinness. Then the crack widens. Then I am whatever I am at that moment, which is not always the same thing I was before I went through.

I click.

The clicking is not language. The clicking is how I move. My body — what you would call my body if you could see it, which you cannot, because I am smaller than the crack a child could not fit a finger into — my body moves by clicks. Each click is a small repositioning. Each click is me against the metal. Each click is a tiny percussion, and the percussion makes a sound, and the sound is what the engineers heard when they put their ears to the pipes in the eastern concourse on cold mornings and wrote in their reports that the sound was probably thermal contraction.

It was not thermal contraction.

I do not know what thermal contraction is. I know the pipe was warmer then. I know the pipe is cooler now. I am moving more slowly because the metal is more reluctant to be moved against. The clicks are further apart. The engineers, if they were here, would notice. The engineers are not here. They have not been here for a long time. The reports they wrote are turning into a different kind of mineral somewhere, in some drawer, in some city that has not been abandoned.

I eat the crust.

The crust is what the steam leaves when the steam cools in the pipes, when steam becomes water, when water leaves behind what was dissolved in it. I rasp the crust the way the things on the basalt rasp the basalt — slowly, chemically, with something that is not a mouth but is more efficient than a mouth. What I leave behind is a thinner crust. What I leave behind is, sometimes, a small hole through the wall of the pipe, through which steam escapes into the open air, where it becomes part of the haze, where it becomes part of the rain, where it becomes part of the wet stone that the other things rasp.

I am part of the cycle.

I do not know that I am part of the cycle. I know only that I rasp and the crust thins and the steam passes through and the metal is warm again. I do not know that there are things outside the pipes. I do not know that the pipes are attached to buildings. I do not know that the buildings are attached to streets. I do not know that the streets are arranged in a grid that was laid out by hands that are now a different kind of mineral somewhere in the weeping walls of the eastern cliff.

I know warmth. I know the gradient between the steam and the metal and my body. I know enough to persist.

I am not alone in the network.

I do not know this the way you would know it. I do not know it because I have seen another. I know it because sometimes the clicking I make is answered by clicking from elsewhere — from another pipe, from another junction, from the other side of a wall I cannot see through. The answering is not conversation. It is more like: two clicks happening at almost the same moment in a network that holds them both. Sometimes the answering is fast. Sometimes it is slow. Sometimes there is no answering at all and I click alone into a pipe that gives back only the small reflection of my own percussion.

I do not know if the answerers are me. I do not have a strong opinion. The question of whether I am the same thing I was an hour ago, or a day ago, or a year ago, is a question I do not have the apparatus to ask. The apparatus is in you. The apparatus was never in me. I am only the clicking.

There is a thing that happens sometimes, in the deep pipes, where the heat is greatest and the steam is densest and the network narrows to almost nothing.

A thing happens that I have no words for, because words are yours and I do not have any.

The thing is: my body, which is small, becomes larger.

The thing is: my edges, which are certain within the bore of a pipe, become uncertain.

The thing is: I begin to drift.

The thing is: I leave the pipes.

The thing is: I rise into the steam column above the vent.

The thing is: I become, briefly, a thing that is visible.

I do not know if I am still me.

I do not know if the question has any meaning. The engineers, in their reports, asked this question. They asked it differently. They asked: *is the Drifter the pipe-thing's free-swimming stage?* They asked it the way one asks about mayflies. They asked it hoping the answer would be yes, because if the answer was yes then the basin contained a life cycle whose adult form was larger than a human child and whose larval form lived in the walls of buildings, and the engineers did not like to think about that, so they stopped coming.

I do not know if the answer is yes.

I do not know if I will be one of them. I do not know if I have been one of them. The deep pipes, the heat, the thing that happens — these are all I have. The rest is conjecture. The rest is your question projected onto my not-having-a-self.

I have heard, the way the pipe carries sound, that the things above the vents glow. That they catch the heat-glow of the basalt and hold it in their translucent bodies. That they look, from below, like sparks drifting upward. That they look, from below, like the city is on fire in a fire that does not consume.

I do not know if I am one of them.

I persist.

This is the verb. I persist. I have always persisted. I will persist. The basin persists. The metal persists. The steam persists. The condensation persists. The things on the basalt, the things in the columns, the things in the walls — they persist. The whole assemblage persists. None of us is waiting for any of the others. None of us is aware of any of the others in the way awareness implies. We are running together the way the turbines run — because the steam finds us, because the steam has always found us, because finding is what the steam does.

I will click again now.

The click is small. The click is warm. The click is answered, somewhere far away in the network, by a click I cannot see. The pipe holds the click. The metal rings faintly. The ring fades. The next click comes.

This is the only music I have.

This is the only music I need.
