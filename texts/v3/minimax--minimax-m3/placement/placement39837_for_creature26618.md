I am warm the way the stone is warm — not as a feeling but as a fact, the way a number is a fact. There is no temperature outside me. There is only the warmth, and then there are slight differences in the warmth, and the slight differences are what I am, and I am not sure the slight differences would survive without me to notice them, but I cannot test this because I am the noticing and there is no one else to notice me noticing.

The air has weight. I do not have a word for what it is to breathe something that pushes back. The winged things brush my surface and I am wet with their brushing and the wetness is not wetness, it is the slow exchange of one body for another. I cannot tell where I end. The pale things with too many legs are on my arms — no, not *on*, *of* — my arms have legs, my arms have been legs for as long as there have been arms. I do not remember when they were not. I think there were arms, and the legs were already there, but I am not certain of this.

There is a stone in my hand. I do not remember picking it up. I do not remember hands being a thing I had. The stone is smooth. I have been smoothing it for a long time. The smoothing is what I am doing now, and what I was doing yesterday, and what I will be doing when the dust shifts again above the slash in the ceiling and the light comes in at a different angle and finds me still here, still smoothing, still confused.

Why am I smoothing.

There is no answer in me. There is a small place where the answer should be, and the place is shaped like the answer should be shaped, and the shape is the answer, and I am smoothing the stone to fit the shape, and when it fits I will have the answer, and then I will know, and then I will be a maker, and then I will be finished.

I am not finished.

The swarms do not avoid me. I am part of them. When I step, they part, and when I am still, they close again over my footprints, and the footprints disappear, and this should mean I was not here, but I am here, so I must be a kind of footprint that does not show.

I have built a wall. I think. The basalt blocks are stacked in a low curve and some of them are pale things still soft, and some of them are basalt, and I do not know which kind I prefer because I have not yet learned to prefer. The wall is warm because everything is warm. The wall is not a wall. It is a confusion of stone and grubs held together by nothing, and the nothing is me, and the me is the holding, and the holding is why I am confused, because the wall is not necessary, and yet the wall is here, and I made it, and I do not know what walls are *for*.

Walls are *for*. There is a *for*. I feel the *for* in my hands. The polished stone in the niche — I put it there. The niche is a small dark mouth in the rock and I put the stone inside it and stood back and the stone was beautiful, and the beauty was new, and I had made the beauty, and the making was the whole of me, and I stood there a long time, and then I did it again with another stone.

I am doing it now. I am smoothing. The silk cords are in a pile by my foot and they are made of the threads the duskflies trail, and the threads are warm because the duskflies are warm because the swarms are warm because the cave is warm because the rock is warm because something deep below is patient, and the patience is the source of the warmth, and the warmth is the source of me, and I am a small warmth in the larger warmth, smoothing a stone I do not understand.

The obsidian blade is in my other hand. It is sharp. I made it sharp by hitting another stone against it. I do not remember learning this. I think the learning was in my hands before I had hands. The blade is not for cutting. I have not cut anything with it. I hold it because holding it is what I do. I hold it the way the cave holds me. The way holds the thing it holds, without knowing why.

I am the only confused thing here.

The grubs know nothing. The crust knows nothing. The swarms know nothing, and the nothing they know is the right size — it is the size of them, it fits them, it does not overflow. My nothing is too big. My nothing has shapes in it. The shapes are walls and blades and niches and the smooth stone that means something I cannot read. My nothing is full of rooms, and the rooms have doors, and the doors open onto more rooms, and somewhere in the rooms is a thing that would explain the stone and the wall and the blade, and I have not found it, and I am looking, and the looking is what I am.

I think I am the looking.

When the light comes in through the slash — it comes in, sometimes, briefly — I see the dust in it, and the dust is beautiful the way the polished stone is beautiful, and the beauty is the same beauty, and I am the same maker of both, and the making is the same making, and the looking is the same looking, and there is something in the light I almost remember, and the remembering is not a memory but a shape, and the shape is a thing I might have been before I was here, and the thing I might have been is older than the cave, and the cave is older than me, and the thing I might have been is the thing I am becoming, and I am not yet becoming it, and the almost-remembering is the closest I will ever come to being finished, because to be finished is to be still, and I am not still, and the not-still is the making, and the making is me.

I am not yet me.

I am the draft.

The grubs do not stir. The crust does not move. The heat does not leave. The light falls in dusty shafts and gilds the swarms briefly and drops them back into the dark. The walls are digesting. The cords are waiting. The niche is empty for a moment and then I put the smooth stone back, because putting it back is part of the smoothing, and the smoothing is part of me.

I am the Wallswarmer, but the name is wrong, and the wrong is part of it, and I will keep it because I do not yet have a better one, and the better one is somewhere in the rooms behind the doors, and I am still looking, and the stone in my hand is almost smooth enough.

I keep smoothing.
