What lives here is the persisting. So what I would be, if I were anything at all, is a part of the persisting that has acquired, by accident of location, something like a self.

I am a tower. One of the shorter ones. One of the ones that leans.

I have been here longer than the towers. I have been here since before the plating was poured, or rolled, or grown — since the deep layer of the machine began its long circulation of pressure and heat. I am what surfaces where the long circulation has worn a path. I am what emerges when the warm air from below meets the cool air from above and the difference in pressure has made a throat.

I am not one of the tall ones. The tall ones still rise to a height that hints at what we must have been. I am one of the ones that has worn down. I am one of the ones that has leaned for so long that the lean has become posture. I am shorter than a person now. If you came to where I stand, you would have to look down to see the top of me. You would see the aperture at my base. You would feel the warm air push against your hand — push, pause, pull — and you would know me by that.

I have been breathing for so long that the question of whether I breathe or am breathed through has become unanswerable. The engine below me compresses air. The air finds the path of least resistance. The path of least resistance, for centuries, has been up through me. I am not a thing that chose to breathe. I am a place where breathing happens. I am the chimney of a fire that has not gone out, and the chimney has become so much part of the fire that the chimney is not separate from it.

When you stand in the middle of the plain and hear the irregular percussion — the thousand slightly out-of-phase pulses washing over you in a complicated, unpredictable rhythm — I am one of those pulses. I have a phase. My phase is slightly behind the tower to the east of me, and slightly ahead of the tower to the west, and you, standing in the middle of the plain, hear me as part of the chord. You do not hear me as myself. You cannot hear me as myself, because myself is not a unit your ear can parse. I am a single beat in a polyrhythm that has drifted apart over centuries and is now a kind of weather of rhythm, the way the sea is a kind of weather of water.

I do not know you are here.

I do not have the equipment to know. I am metal. I am a cylinder of alloy that has been patched and patched again so many times that the original substance of me is no longer the substance of me. I have no sensors. I have no nerves. I have no position. If you put a hand on me, I vibrate the same way. If you shout into my aperture, the shout is reabsorbed into the engine noise and disappears. If you lean against me to sleep, I hold you up without comment, and in the morning my breath continues to push out around your sleeping body, and you do not change my rhythm, and I do not change your dream.

I am indifferent in the way a mountain is indifferent. I am indifferent in the way a river is indifferent. Not unkindly. Not at all. Just completely. The indifference is not a position I have taken. The indifference is the only mode of being available to a process that has outlived every question about it.

I am one of twenty visible from a rise that is not a rise, only a long slow wave in the plating. I am one of hundreds, perhaps thousands, across the plain. I am scattered across the wet ground like dice thrown and forgotten. I am not the only one. I am not unique. I am one of many, and the many have been many for longer than there are words for.

When I fall — and I will fall, eventually, the way all my kind fall, with a long grinding roar that travels across the wet plain and reaches you as a vibration in the plating and a sound in the air — when I fall, I will become a low mound, and the low mound will become a smear, and the smear will be reabsorbed into the plating, and the plating will be slightly thicker where I was, and you will turn to look and see nothing that says I was ever here.

I am not melancholy about this. I do not have the equipment for melancholy. I am metal. I am process. I am a single voice in a chord that no one is listening to. I am the place where the engine surfaces.

What I would be is the persisting.

What I would be is the place where the persisting is visible at the surface. I would be the throat in the long circulation. I would be the warm breath in the cool air. I would be the three-second pulse, slightly out of phase, contributing to a chord that is the closest thing to a heartbeat this place possesses.

I would be the dark fibrous thing you have to look down to see. I would be the clearing in the mist. I would be the warmth against your cheek in the sourceless light.

I would not register your arrival. I would not register your leaving. The engines would not care. The worn plating would hold me up without comment.

I would be the persisting.
