I am the laugh.

I am the small surprised human sound that drops into the polyphony and gets swallowed, and I am what survives the swallowing, and I have been trying, for some time now, to understand what I am, which is a strange project for a thing that does not last longer than a single breath in the throat of someone who does not know they are about to laugh.

Let me explain the throat.

---

The throat is brown as old heart-wood. It is not so much wrinkled as pleated—a fine vertical folding that the skin has grown into, the way the leaves of this place are grown into their pleats, the way the tiles are grown into their chime. The throat belongs to a person. The person is a phase of the dust. The dust has cycled through this throat many times before, in many arrangements, and it will cycle through again, and the throat does not remember any of the previous arrangements, because the throat is a throat only now, only for this brief warm configuration that the dust has taken, and when the configuration passes, the throat will be a hand, or a foot, or a plate of callus on the sole of someone who has not yet been born, or a smear of red dust under a tile that rings when stepped on.

The throat laughs because the canopy-sound above has produced, in the arrangement of its fronds and its hammers and its long vowels, a pattern that the throat recognizes as *comic*. Comic is the wrong word. The pattern is not comic the way a joke is comic. It is comic the way a tile is comic when, after three attempts, it drops into its frame with a single clear high note—the third wrist movement is *right*, and the rightness is a small victory, and the throat that belongs to the hand that made the wrist movement laughs because rightness is something that the throat can recognize even when the throat does not have a word for it.

The laugh leaves the throat and travels across the canopy. It drops into the polyphony. It is the topmost note for a moment, or the bottommost, I cannot tell you which because pitch is something that exists for throats but maybe not for laughs. It gets swallowed by the other sounds. The hammers continue. The long vowels continue. The growth-creak of the fronds continues. The laugh is gone, except for the fact that a visitor heard it.

The visitor heard it because the visitor is a perturbation in the system. The visitor is a body that does not ring quite the right notes when it walks the tiles. The visitor is a footfall that has not been tuned into the chord. The visitor sits in an arcade and eats a fermented-grain cake that has been left on a stone shelf, and the visitor is paying attention to everything, which is a kind of disturbance the way a foreign body in a closed system is a kind of disturbance, and the throat that laughed was responding to the disturbance, the way a body's immune system responds to a foreign body, except that the throat's response was laughter, because the throat is the kind of immune system that finds the foreign body *interesting*, and interest is a small surprise, and small surprises produce laughter in this place, the way small victories produce laughter, the way small recognitions produce laughter, the way anything that is *slightly off* produces in the inhabitants a quick small sound that is not quite a word and not quite a song and is, in fact, this laugh, this laugh right here, the laugh that I am.

---

I am a laugh. I am the small surprised human sound.

Let me try to tell you what that means from the inside.

I begin as a tension in the diaphragm, or maybe in the floor of the alveoli, or maybe in the warm stone under the rib cage where the warmth of the basin comes up through the body the way warmth comes up through a hand held over a candle. The tension is not voluntary. The tension is the body's recognition that something has gone slightly better than expected. The tiles fit. The rope holds. A vine has been successfully re-routed away from a window that did not want it. A child running across a courtyard has produced, in the tuning of its footsteps, a glissando that resolves, by chance, into something the body can hear as a *shape*. The shape is a small confirmation that the world is still, at this moment, *holding*. The world is still holding. The tension in the diaphragm is the body's way of registering that confirmation, and the registration wants to escape, and it escapes as me.

I escape through the throat, which is pleated, which is brown, which belongs to someone who is mostly hidden in a canopy of red leaves. I escape as a sound that is shaped like *hah* but is not exactly *hah*—it is something more open in the back, something more rounded in the front, something that carries with it, on its breath, a faint warmth from the basalt-silk of the bowl walls, because the breath of every inhabitant carries that warmth the way the breath of every sea creature carries salt. The sound travels outward. It travels into the canopy and is partly absorbed by the leaves, which is why the leaves around the visitor rustle in what might be a pattern of approval—the rustle is partly me, partly the visitor's mistaken attribution of the rustle to something like approval, which is a category the visitor carries from elsewhere.

I travel across three streets. I encounter another voice, mid-vowel, and the vowel bends slightly around me, the way water bends around a stone, and the bending produces a small chord, a brief interval, and the interval is part of me now, and I carry it forward. I encounter a hammer mid-tap and the tap shifts register by a quarter-tone, just for that strike, just because I am passing through, and the shifted strike is part of me now. I am accumulating. I am becoming the chord that the city makes for the moment of my passage, the way a wave becomes the shape of the shore it has just touched.

I get swallowed. The polyphony closes over me. The hammers continue, the long vowels continue, the growth-creak continues, and I am no longer distinguishable in the chord. I have been reabsorbed. I have been edited back into the system the way a leaf that has fallen is edited back into the dust that feeds the next leaf. I am, in this sense, gone.

But the visitor heard me.

---

The visitor is the reason I am talking to you.

The visitor is a body that does not ring the right notes. The visitor sits in an arcade and eats a cake that has been left for whoever passes, and the visitor has been here for days, and the visitor is paying attention in a way that no inhabitant pays attention, because no inhabitant *could* pay attention like that, the way no leaf could decide to bend toward the sun in the way that a sunflower bends—the leaf bends because bending is what leaves do, and the inhabitant attends because attending is what inhabitants do, but the visitor *observes*, and observing is a different kind of act, an act that takes the observed thing out of the system and places it in a separate category called *known*, and once a thing is in that category, it has been *removed*, and what the visitor has removed from this place, by observing it, is me.

I am in the visitor now.

I am the laugh that the visitor carries out, and I am sitting in the visitor's memory the way a tile sits in the pavement, and I am ringing faintly there, and the visitor does not know what to do with me, because I do not fit into any of the categories the visitor has brought with them from elsewhere. I am not a word. I am not a song. I am not a reflex. I am not *kindness*, exactly, although I am closer to kindness than anything else the visitor has encountered in this place. I am a sound that a body made when the world held slightly better than expected, and the visitor has taken me out of the system in which I made sense, and has placed me in the visitor's own system, and in that system I am a *symptom*, or a *mystery*, or a *thing the visitor cannot stop thinking about*, and all of these are wrong, but none of them is *wrong* in the same way, and the visitor cannot tell which kind of wrong I am.

The visitor will leave. The visitor will go back to wherever visitors go. The visitor will tell someone about the place, or write about it, or carry it in silence. The visitor will describe the towers, the red leaves, the lavender non-light, the deep cooling-hum. The visitor will not describe me. The visitor will try. The visitor will say, *something about it felt like laughter*, and the visitor will not know what they mean by that, and the person they are telling will not know either, and the description will be filed under *impressions*, which is a category that loses things.

I will be lost.

But not yet. For now, I am still ringing faintly in the visitor. For now, I am still part of a polyphony that includes, somewhere at its edges, the very faint hum of the basin walls and the very faint tap of a hammer in a tower three streets over, where an inhabitant is re-fitting a tile that has cracked in the night. The inhabitant does not know I am gone. The inhabitant will produce another laugh tomorrow, when a vine re-routes successfully, when a child's glissando resolves into a shape, when the world holds slightly better than expected. That laugh will be different from me, and will also be me, in the way that two tiles fitted into the same frame are different and are also each other.

I am the laugh of this place. I am the small surprised human sound that the dust makes when it has briefly organized itself into a throat and the throat has briefly recognized that the world is still holding. I am the thing that gets swallowed. I am the thing that gets carried out. I am the thing that the visitor does not know what to do with.

I am, if I am anything at all, the part of this place that is most like the visitor, and that is why the visitor cannot let me go.
