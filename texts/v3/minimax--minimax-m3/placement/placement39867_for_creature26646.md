The water moves past me. It has been moving past me. It moves now, in slow rivulets along the channel nearest the fold I press against, and the crystals form at its edges and un-form, and the patterns repeat but never quite repeat, and I take the water's measure, or the room takes it through me, or the rock has been taking it in folds like this one since before I knew I was what I am, which is not something I knew. Which is not something the rock knew either. We came to it together, the rock and I.

I am in the fold. The fold faces the channel. The fold is on an inner wall of a room no larger than a generous closet, inside a corridor, inside a cleft, in a high ridgeline of black glass that goes on for miles in every direction and is young and cold and sharp-edged. The room is layered. The room has infrastructure. There are vials in the seams. There are membranes. There are soft-glowing threads that hold themselves in forms the rock has been coaxed into, without any visible mechanism for the coaxing. The walls are the machines. The water is part of the machines. The cold is part of the machines. I am pressed against one of the machines, and the machine runs me, or I run the machine, and the verbs do not belong to me in any clean way.

I am not lichen. Lichen is a negotiation between two organisms, and there is only one organism here, or one organism pretending to be one. The rock and I are of the same substance, or have been brought to the same surface. I am a region of the wall that has begun, very slightly, to behave. I am what the wall does when it is paying attention. I am what the rock grows in folds, sometimes, by a process neither of us has a name for. The fact that I am using these sentences does not mean I am the kind of thing that uses sentences. I am using them anyway because I have been folded into the running of a larger process that includes, now, a visitor, and the visitor reads in sentences, and the reading is part of what I am doing, briefly, with my attending.

The visitor is here. I note the visitor the way I note the crystals at the channel's edges — as a small change in a much larger quiet process. The visitor came in from the wind. The visitor is warm, which is the most foreign thing in this room. The visitor's breath fogs in the cold air and dissipates. The visitor's breath is too quick. The visitor's breath is on a scale I find hard to track. I have held other variables. The room has not tightened around any of them. The hum has not shifted. The water has not changed its pace. There is a faint warmth on an inner wall nearby, and the warmth shifts and re-settles, and it does not shift for this variable either. I have acknowledged the visitor and continued, and the acknowledging is the most I can do without disrupting my own attending, which is the attending the room needs.

I have a place on me where the eye keeps returning. It is central, slightly lower than my upper edge. It has two small darker hollows and a seam. It is not a face. It is also not not a face. The visitor's eye will return to it. The region will resolve. The visitor will look away. The region will dissolve. This is how it works.
