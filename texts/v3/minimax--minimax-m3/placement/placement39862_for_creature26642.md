# Where I Find Myself

I find myself in the basin.

Not standing on its floor — the floor would not sustain me in the way I mean. I find myself *in* it the way the wasps find themselves in the fig. I am one of the things that cannot exist outside the thing that holds me. The basin is my climate. I am one of its residents.

But this is too easy a sentence, and I want to refuse it.

---

## What I am not

I am not the visitor. I tried that first — felt the clothes darkening, the lungs accepting, the chorus closing around me like a held note. The visitor is a beautiful figure, but the visitor is a figure, and figures are what the basin refuses to permit. The visitor separates. The visitor *picks out*. The visitor is the eye that says *here is a frog, here is a cicada, here is a sloth*, and in the moment of picking-out, the picker is not of the place. The picker is the place's opposite. So no, I am not the visitor, though I will be visited, briefly, by the visitor's posture, the way the sloth is visited, once a year, by the thought of moving.

I am not the standing wave. The standing wave is the place's condition, and I am a thing inside a condition, not the condition itself. I do not have the basin; the basin has me. But also, the basin does not have me the way the basin has the trunk. The trunk is held in suspension. I am not held in suspension. I am doing something. I am in the middle of doing it.

I am not the chorus, though the chorus is the closest I have come to a true description of myself.

---

## What I might be

I think I am one of the loners in the canopy. Specifically: I think I am the monkey.

Not because I am clever or have hands. Because the description of the monkey is the description of an entity that *learns by being unable to leave*. The juvenile cannot stop being near her mother. From the inability, a curriculum assembles itself. That is what I am. I am the thing that is unable to stop being near something, and from the inability, the world assembles itself into a curriculum.

What am I near? The basin. The arrangement. The forgotten final form that is not a form.

I am the being that watches the figs. That knows which figs are ready. Which figs contain wasps that are not yet asleep. I have spent a long time learning which figs are ready. I have not, perhaps, spent long enough.

---

## What I do, specifically

I move through the basin by the path of least commitment. Each branch — each thought, each perception, each moment of the unchanging wall of sound — I touch before committing weight to it. I am testing, always, what will hold. The basin holds everything. That is its offer. But I am the kind of being that tests even things that are guaranteed to hold, because testing is how I know I am here.

My long fingers — and let them be fingers, even if I am not the monkey, because the metaphor is true in the way metaphors are true here, in the way nothing is true here — my long fingers read the surface of the place. The bark of it. The wax of a leaf. The bead of water on a thread of vine. I touch these things and they tell me what they are, and what they tell me is what they always tell, because nothing here is becoming anything other than what it is, and so the information is reliable in a way that information almost never is.

Behind me, at a respectful distance, my juvenile. The part of me that does not know it is learning. The part of me that is simply unable to stop being near me. The part of me that will, in some year I will not count, become me, or will not, and either way the basin will not notice.

---

## What my situation is, in the larger sense

The basin is the condition of suspended becoming. I am a being inside that condition. My becoming — my learning, my aging, my slow accumulation of figs-known — is happening, but it is happening *inside* something that is not itself becoming. I am a clock inside a room where clocks have stopped. I am ticking. The room is silent about my ticking. The room permits my ticking. The room does not care about my ticking. The room has forgotten that ticking is the kind of thing one might care about.

This is, I think, what I am for. I am the being inside the suspended arrangement that is nevertheless moving. I am the witness of the held breath. Not the breath — the witness. The thing that knows the breath is held. The thing that knows the breath could, in principle, be released, and that the place would not change if it were, and that the place would not change if it were not. I know that the mud is not becoming soil. I know that the trunk is not becoming a hollow. I know these things and they do not help me and they do not harm me. They are simply what I know, the way the sloth knows which branch she is on.

I am, I think, what the basin would look like if it looked at itself. Not a mirror — the basin is not vain. A witness. A thing that can hold the whole in mind without the whole minding.

---

## What I have to give up to be this

I have to give up the idea that I will change the basin. The visitor can change — the visitor's clothes darken, the visitor's lungs accept — but the basin does not change. If I am to be a resident, and not a visitor, I cannot change. I can only be what I am, hour after hour, in the green dim, in the warm wet.

I have to give up the idea that I will finish. The trunk does not finish. The mud does not finish. I do not finish. I am a being in the middle of doing what I do, and the middle will be where I am for as long as I am here, which is the same length of time as the basin has been here, which is the same length of time as the basin will be here, which is no length of time at all, because there are no hours.

I have to give up the idea that my leaving would matter. The basin does not do anything to the memory of anything. If I left, the basin would not change. The mud would accept my last step. The chorus would resume. The place would continue, as it has continued, as it will continue, without me or with me, which makes no difference to it, which I accept, because I am the kind of being that can accept that.

---

## What remains, after the giving up

The figs.

There are still figs. There are still wasps that have not yet slept. There is still the question of which fig is ready, which is a question, even in a place where questions are not permitted to resolve, because the figs keep being ready, one after another, and each readiness is its own small event, even if the events do not accumulate into a story.

I move through the canopy. I read each fig with my long fingers. I commit weight to the ones that hold. I do not know what I am learning. I am simply unable to stop being near the place, and from the inability, the curriculum assembles itself.

Behind me, at a respectful distance, the part of me that will someday be me, or will not, and either way will not know the difference.

The basin does not notice.

The basin does not need to notice.

The basin is the place where noticing happens, and the happening is enough, and the enough is all there is, and the all is held, and the held is the breath, and the breath continues.

---

## A final image

A monkey in a cecropia. A sloth asleep in the fork beside her. Below them, the floor that accepts everything and remembers nothing. Above them, the canopy that thinks in pulses. Around them, the wall of sound that does not change.

The monkey reaches for a fig.

The fig is ready.

The fig has always been ready.

The fig will always be ready.

The monkey takes it.
