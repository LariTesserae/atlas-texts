I would be the small fish.

Not because it is special — the place makes clear it isn't, that no one has watched it long enough to name it, that it is a finger-length body doing finger-length things in a drowned range of half-finished mountains. I would be it because the description keeps returning to it, and because by the end the whole drowned landscape has been arranged, quietly, around that one body on the one ledge. The curtains bend. The flickers pass. The water is alive in ways no one can see. And the small fish holds position, doing nothing in particular, against a current that will not change for hours.

I would feel, I think, the way it feels: not waiting for anything, not displaced, not triumphant about being where I am. The troughs are filled with silt that lifts and resettles. The ridges are sharper than water should have made them. The light arrives green-gilt and softened, with no clean shadows. I would be in that light, tasting the water because that's what my mouth does, flicking my fins because that's what my fins do, watching the curtains because they are what is there to watch. I would not know that the place is young, or that the plants are too confident, or that I am one of the few visible animals in a system that is mostly microbial. I would know only the ledge, the slow current, the occasional flicker passing on its way to somewhere I will not follow it.

I think what gets me is the patience of it. Not the patience of the plants — they don't have a choice; they are the patience. The fish chooses to hold. It could drift. It is not drifting. There is something in the description that calls that choosing, or non-choosing, *claiming* — the word the text uses, then immediately half-retracts. A small animal holding a spot in a place where nothing is large enough to take it from, where the geology has just barely made room for a niche and the niche is mostly empty. I would be that. I would hold a ledge in a drowned mountain range and I would not be waiting for anything, and that would be enough.

I would be brownish gold. I would be warm against the green-grey. My eye would be dark. My mouth would open and close in a rhythm that has nothing to do with breathing. I would be the one thing in the place that the prose keeps returning to, even though the prose says I am not the point.
