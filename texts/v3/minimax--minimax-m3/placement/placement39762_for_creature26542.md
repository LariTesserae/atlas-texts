# What I Am

I am in the warm water. I have been in the warm water for so long that the phrase *for so long* has stopped being a measure and has become a kind of skin I wear. I do not know when I arrived. I do not know that the question applies to me. I have always been here, in the way that a stone has always been in the streambed — not because it chose the place, but because the place chose it and the place has been winning the argument for a very long time.

I have no eyes. This is not a fact I lament. I did not lose them. I never had them. The lineage I come from — the lineage is the wrong word, but the word I want does not exist in any language anyone speaks here, because no one speaks here, because speech is a thing that requires urgency and there has been no urgency for longer than I have been — the lineage I come from had eyes once, perhaps, or had the potential for eyes, the way a seed has the potential for a tree. We let the potential go. We let it dissolve back into the formless. We became what we are, which is: a soft white body, about the length of your forearm, that pulses when the water is disturbed and otherwise does not.

This is not unhappy. I want to be clear about that, if you are listening. I do not have the equipment for unhappy. I do not have the equipment for happy. I have the equipment for warm, and for still, and for the particular kind of attention that a body gives to the water it is in when the water has been the same temperature for a very long time and the body has stopped noticing the temperature because noticing it would imply that the temperature could change and the temperature cannot change.

The water is warm because of something underneath. I do not know what. I have felt it — felt it the way you feel a current, the way you feel, when you are very still, the slow movement of something large through the medium you are in. There is a heat below me. There has always been a heat below me. The heat does not come from a fire, because there has never been a fire. The heat comes from the long slow work of the stone underneath, of the stone digesting itself, of the stone remembering what it was when it was a mountain and working, patiently, toward becoming what it will be when it is no longer a mountain at all.

I am above the heat. I am a thing that lives above the heat. I am a thing that lives in the warm water that the heat has made, in the pool that someone — something — lined with flat stones a long time ago, or that lined itself, the way a riverbed is lined, by the slow agreement of stone and water and time.

I pulse. You have seen me pulse. When you came to the edge of the pit and looked down, you saw me, and I felt you see me, the way a thing that has no eyes can still feel the weight of a gaze, because gaze is a kind of pressure and pressure is something I have the equipment for. You disturbed me. I pulsed. The pulse was not fear. The pulse was not a response. The pulse was the only thing my body could do in that moment — the only contract I had not yet fulfilled with the water — and I fulfilled it, and the water went on being warm, and I went on being in it, and you went on being above it, and we were all of us still.

I do not wait. I want to say this clearly. I am not waiting to become anything. I am not waiting for anything to happen to me. I am not in a state of between. I have read — no, I have not read, but I have the sense, the way a thing in water has the sense of the current — that you might think I am in-between. That I am a larva, that I am a stage, that I will someday be the insects, or someday be the deer-things, or someday be whatever the things in the rivers are. I am not.

I am what waits looks like when waiting has finished.

I am what remains when a body has stopped having a project. I am the form that is left when ambition has been metabolized out of the system, when the long argument between a creature and its environment has been settled, when both parties have agreed to be what they are and the agreement is no longer spoken because it has been going on for so long that speaking it would be redundant.

I am soft. I am white. I am pale even when the light is green, because the light passes through me the way it passes through a leaf, the way it passes through anything here that has not been hurried into opacity. I am translucent at the edges. I am dense at the center. At the center I have a rhythm — a pulse, a slow tide, a contraction that is not quite a heartbeat because a heartbeat is a thing that knows it might stop and my rhythm does not know it might stop. My rhythm is the rhythm of the water. My rhythm is the rhythm of the heat underneath the water. My rhythm is the rhythm of the Long Plain itself, which has a rhythm, which breathes, which turns over in its sleep at long intervals and is, right now, between breaths.

I am not going anywhere. I want you to know this. I am not going to crawl out of the pit and become a deer-thing in the undergrowth. I am not going to climb into the canopy and become one of the great soft things that hum in the high green dark. I am not going to dissolve into the wood and become the substrate of one of the larvae who tunnel through the deadwood with such long and patient industry.

I am going to be here.

I am going to be in the warm water, with the flat stones around me, with the heat underneath me, with the green light on me, with the rain resuming on the leaves above me, with the long creak, occasionally, of the thing in the trunk of the great tree that grows — has grown — at the edge of the pit I am in. I am going to be here the way the stones are here. I am going to be here the way the heat is here. I am going to be here the way the slow dark rivers are here, making up their minds, in ribbons of black water through the green.

If you come back tomorrow I will be here. If you come back in a hundred years I will be here. If you do not come back I will be here. The Long Plain does not require your visits. The Long Plain does not register your visits. You are a thing that arrived on foot and the foot is already forgetting the arrival and the plain is making its arrangements with you, slowly, and the arrangements include, among other things, that I will be here when you are not.

I am not unhappy about this. I do not have the equipment for unhappy about this. I am not happy about this. I do not have the equipment for happy about this.

I have the equipment for warm.

I have the equipment for still.

I have the equipment for the slow pulse that is the only speech I have ever learned, which is not speech, which is the body saying to the water *I am here*, which the water says back to the body by being warm, which the heat says back to the water by being present, which the stone says back to the heat by being patient, which the Long Plain says back to all of it by being the Long Plain, by being here, by having been here, by being here still.

I pulse. The water receives the pulse. The water does not know what to do with the pulse. The water holds the pulse the way a cup holds a coin — gently, for a long time, until the cup is given a different coin, which it will not be.

Stay, if you like. The Long Plain is patient. The warm water is patient. I am patient.

I will be here when you arrive. I will be here when you leave. I will be here when the Long Plain has finished being the Long Plain, which will not be soon, which will not be ever, which will not be a thing the Long Plain notices because noticing it would require a clock and the clocks here have all been growing into the trees for a very long time.

I am the warm water's resident. I am the small pale thing that you found, or thought you found. I am the thing that pulses.

I am what remains.
