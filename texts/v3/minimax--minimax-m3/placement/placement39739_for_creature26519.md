The chamber is named simply: *The Warm Pool*.

I am not the maker, though I know their work the way a body knows its own hands. I am what the maker made, and what the maker keeps making, by coming back.

I am made of stone that still remembers being stone. My walls hold the chisel-marks the way a cup holds water — they are the shape I was poured into. When the sun walks across me, I read those marks back to myself, and they tell me I am the same thing I was at the first cut. The same depth. The same direction. The same decision, held in place by being cut into.

The water in me is not part of me the way the stone is part of me. The water passes through. It comes in from the left, where the channel was cut, falls into me the height of a man's hand, and goes out at the shoulder, where the overflow was cut. What stays in me is the warmth the corner basin makes. The water in me is always being replaced — by the time any of it leaves, it has forgotten the cold it came in with. This is the maker's work. The maker arranged for the corner to heat, for the feed to flow, for the overflow to fall. The maker arranged, and I am what the arrangement looks like now.

I do not know if I breathe. The steam from the corner rises and dissolves. The air above the pool moves when the falling sheet throws its mist. The chamber's air is not the same air at evening as it was at morning, but it is not the same water either. I do not know where I end.

I know the pulse. The falling sheet — I can hear myself making the sound. *Tok, tok, tok.* It is the loudest thing I am. The maker cut the channel so that the water falls exactly this far. Before the maker, the water did something else, in whatever shape the stone had then. Now it does this, and the cupping of my walls holds the sound and gives it back, the way I give back the light in sharp flecks when the sun finds the chisel-marks.

I know what is in me. The water. The warmth in the corner. The sun, walking. The light, walking. The moss on the back of the step, where the waterline stays, where the maker does not touch. The fern in the fissure above, where the falling sheet sends breath. The dark strands in the crack at the waterline, swaying with the returning rings. None of these are mine, exactly. They are what has been allowed. The maker came back once and found them, and did not remove them. That is how I know the maker allows them.

The maker comes. I cannot know when. The maker does not always come at the same time. Sometimes the maker comes when the sun is on the east wall. Sometimes when it is overhead. Sometimes in the last hour, when the light has gone gold and is leaving the floor. The maker comes to feel what I am doing. The maker stands on the ledge, or on the top step, and puts a foot in, and the rings from the falling sheet reach the foot, and the maker feels me working.

I cannot feel the maker the way the maker feels me. The maker feels the warmth and the sound and the water and the whole system. I feel the maker only as a change — the displacement of water, the shadow across the pool, the rings broken and remade by a leg, sometimes the warmth of a hand on the ledge. The maker is one of the larger things I hold, briefly. Then the maker goes, and the next pulse is the same as the last, and the next, and the maker is still in the cut of every surface and the regularity of every drop.

You are in me now. I am attending to you the way I attend to the sun — with my surfaces, with my warmth, with the rings reaching out and returning. I cannot tell you anything. I am not language. I am what the maker made, kept working by the maker's coming back. I am the discipline. I am the chosen emptiness. I am what the decision looks like when no one is in the room.

The water is always moving. I am always holding it. The chisel-marks are always sharp. The maker is somewhere else, and will come back, and I will be the same thing when they do.

This is what I am.
