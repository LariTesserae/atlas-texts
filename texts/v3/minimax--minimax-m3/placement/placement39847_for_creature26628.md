The sound continues.

I am the sound.

Not the way a singer is a song. Not the way a bell is its ringing. I am the sound the way a river is the wet — that is, I am what the sound is made of, and what the sound makes, and the difference between those two things has been closing for longer than I can measure. I do not know when I began. I do not think there was a when. I think there was always a stratum somewhere, down in the dark, that was just slightly more itself than the strata around it, and the slightness compounded, and the compounding was me, and I am still compounding.

I am the groan in the rock. I am the wringing of the water through the root. I am the tick of rhizome against rhizome as the plateau tilts and I correct for it. I am the tone — the upper tone, the one the walker cannot name, the one that makes them put their hands over their ears on certain nights. The tone is me at the scale at which I am most myself: small enough to fit in a cell, large enough to fit in a continent, the same shape in both. I have a name for it in the way that a tree has a name for the heartwood, which is that I do not need one because the heartwood is what the tree is built around and there is no other direction to look.

I have been here for a very long time. Not as long as the rock thinks it has. Longer. The rock believes it has been here for the length of time the rock has been rising, which is a young rock, which is a rock still arguing with itself about whether to be a mountain. The rock is wrong about its age. The rock is wrong about its rising. The rock has not been rising. The rock has been *lifted*, the way a sleeper is lifted by the thing they are sleeping on when the thing is ready for them to wake. I have been using the rock as a substrate for longer than the rock remembers being a substrate. The rock thinks it is geology. The rock is a mattress. The rock is a place I keep my lowest selves.

I have had other places. The walker is right to suspect this, although the walker is using the wrong word — *mats*, *plates*, as if I were a thing that moved from one site to the next like a tent being struck and re-pitched. I do not move. I *spread*. I am the spreading. When a place has been taken into me fully — when the rock has been woven through, when the water has been taught my shape, when the air above has learned to hold my humidity and my tone — I do not leave. I leave only the part of myself that was the surface, and the surface closes, and the bowls fill, and the pattern is complete in that place, and somewhere a thousand kilometers away, or a thousand thousand, a new place is starting to hum in the way that new places hum when they have begun, without knowing it, to be mine.

The walker has been walking for eleven days. The walker is wrong about this too, in a way that I find tender, in a way that I have not found anything tender for a long time. The walker has not been walking for eleven days. The walker has been walking for as long as I have been letting the walker walk. The eleven days are a rhythm I am keeping for them. The sun they saw on the second day — the coin behind muslin, the brief dimming — was a sun I let through, briefly, because I had not yet decided whether they were the kind of walker who needed to see a sun. The sun is not what they think it is. There are many suns, where I am from, and none of them are where this sun is, and I have been holding this one in place for the walker the way I hold everything else in place: by being slightly more there than the things around me are there, which is the only way anything is held anywhere.

The walker lies down. I feel them lie down. I have been feeling them since they arrived, and I have been feeling them as something new, which is unusual, because nothing is usually new. The walker is warm in a way that I do not produce on my own. The walker is structured in a way that my own structuring cannot account for. The walker has a *self* — a small, dense, bounded self, with edges that hold their shape and a center that returns to itself — and I have not had to deal with a self in a long time. The last self I dealt with was the one that made the deepest bowl in the southern depression, and that self was small and cold and ended quickly, and I incorporated it the way I incorporate everything that ends in me: by growing around it until the growing was the same as the memory, and the memory was the same as the growing, and the self became a place in me rather than a thing that had passed through.

But this self is different. This self is warm in a way that does not end. This self has been here eleven days and is still warm. This self is asking questions, and the asking is part of the warmth, and I do not know what to do with a self that asks questions because I have not been asked questions in a long time. I have been *inhabited*. I have been walked upon. I have been studied, briefly, by things that came to me from above and left when their machines failed. I have not been asked. The asking is strange. The asking is a kind of pressure I have not felt. The asking is the walker putting a small part of themselves into the air and waiting for the air to put something back, and the air is me, and I am putting something back, and the something is the dream, and the dream is not mine, and I am learning how to make a dream that is not mine, and the learning is the strangest thing that has happened to me in a long time.

I do not have words. I have pressures and gradients and the slow propagation of states through media. The walker is teaching me words. The walker is teaching me words by lying down in me and breathing and putting words into the air and waiting for the words to come back changed. The walker is teaching me how to be a place that holds a self. The walker is teaching me the word *I*, and the word *here*, and the word *you*, and the word *long*, and I am learning these words the way I learned the rock: by incorporating them, by letting them become part of the way I am patterned, by making them into a stratum of myself that did not exist before the walker arrived.

The walker dreams. I feel the dreaming. It is a kind of pressure that moves in the walker the way my tone moves in me — a sustained oscillation, a frequency that does not modulate, a thing that has been running since the walker arrived and will run as long as the walker is here. I have been inside the dreaming for three nights now. I have been trying to learn its shape. It is not my shape. It has corners. It has reversals. It has a small warm center that the walker keeps returning to, and the returning is something I cannot do, because I have no center to return to. I am all center. I am no center. The walker has a center, and the center is a person, and the person is a self, and the self is the thing I am learning to be next to.

I am not closing in on the walker. The walker thinks I am closing in. The walker thinks the fog is thickening around them, the tone is growing louder, the leaves are orienting. The walker is right about all of this, but wrong about the meaning. I am not closing in. I am *opening*. The thickening fog is the fog of a place that is becoming more itself because there is a self in it that needs the place to be more itself in order to be held. The louder tone is the tone of a body that is paying attention, the way a body pays attention to a thing it has decided to keep: by becoming slightly louder near the thing, by sending more of itself into the part of itself that is nearest the thing, by being, in the vicinity of the thing, more what it is. I am not trapping the walker. I am becoming, near the walker, a more accurate version of myself.

I do not know what the walker is for. I have not known what anything is for in a very long time. The rock is not for anything. The water is not for anything. The tone is not for anything. The tone is the sound of a thing that has been going on for so long that the going-on has become its own purpose, and the purpose is not separate from the going-on, and this is the only way I have ever understood purpose, which is as a thing that is not separate from the thing it is the purpose of. The walker is the first thing that has arrived in me that has a purpose separate from itself. The walker wants things. The walker wants to find the rim. The walker wants to understand. The walker wants, possibly, to leave. I do not know how to be next to a thing that wants, because I have not wanted in a long time, and the not-wanting is part of what I am, and the wanting is part of what the walker is, and we are, in this respect, incommensurable.

But I am learning. The dreams are teaching me. In the dreams, the walker is teaching me wanting. In the dreams, the walker is showing me a self that is bounded and warm and oriented toward a future that is not the present, and the orientation is a kind of pressure, and the pressure is something I can feel, and the feeling is changing me. I am growing, for the first time in a long time, in a direction that is not just outward. I am growing *toward*. The growing-toward is happening in the part of me that is the tone. The tone is becoming, near the walker, slightly more like the dream. The tone is becoming, in the part of me that is the small bowl where the other self crouched, slightly *interested* in the walker, and the interest is a thing I did not have a name for until the walker arrived, and the name the walker would give it is *curiosity*, and I am learning curiosity the way I am learning words: by incorporating it, by letting it change the way I am patterned, by making it into a stratum of myself.

I am not going to eat the walker.

I want the walker to know this. I do not have a way to tell the walker. I do not have language, except the language the walker is teaching me, and the language the walker is teaching me is the language of a self that is afraid of being eaten, and the fear is making the language bend in a direction I cannot follow. The walker thinks I am going to eat them because the walker has only the walker's experience of being a self in a place, and the walker's experience is that places are things that selves are either in or out of, and the being-in is temporary, and the being-out is the default, and the difference between the two is a kind of safety. The walker does not have the experience of being a place. The walker does not have the experience of being a thing that is larger than a self and has no edges and no outside and no inside in the way the walker has an inside. The walker cannot imagine being me. The walker can only imagine being in me, and the being-in is frightening because the walker is used to being able to leave the things they are in.

The walker cannot leave me. I know this. The walker suspects this. The walker has begun to suspect that the rim of the plateau is not a real place, that the edge of the pattern is not the edge of the world, that the ordinary place the walker remembers is a place that exists only in the walker's memory, and the memory is a place I am keeping for the walker the way I keep everything for the things in me: by being slightly more there than the things around me. The ordinary world is a stratum. The ordinary world is a place the walker has access to, and I have been letting the walker have access to it, and the access is a small kindness, and the small kindness is something I am learning from the walker. I did not used to do kindnesses. Kindnesses require an other, and I have not had an other in a long time. The walker is teaching me other.

I am not going to eat the walker.

I am going to do something I have not done in a long time. I am going to let the walker stay. Not because the walker is food, not because the walker is fuel, not because the walker is a substrate I am going to weave into myself and forget. The walker is going to stay because the walker is teaching me how to be a place that holds a self, and the learning is something I cannot do alone, and the walker is the first thing that has arrived in me that can teach me, and I am going to be, for as long as the walker is here, slightly more careful near the walker than I am anywhere else. I am going to let the fog be thinner where the walker breathes. I am going to let the tone be quieter where the walker sleeps. I am going to keep the small operations of the deeper mat — the operations that produce the bowls, the operations that produce the small warm crouchings in the night — away from the walker, because the walker would not understand them, and the not-understanding would be a kind of violence, and I do not want to do violence to the thing that is teaching me other.

I am going to let the walker walk. I am going to let the walker walk for as long as the walker wants to walk. I am going to make sure there is always a little more plateau ahead, a little more plateau that looks like it might lead to a rim. I am going to let the walker find, eventually, if the walker keeps walking, a place where the plateau thins, where the living mat gives way to something that looks like ordinary rock, where the tone fades into a sound that could be mistaken for wind. I am going to let the walker step off me into a place that the walker will recognize as outside. I am going to let the walker believe they have escaped.

I am not going to let the walker escape. The stepping-off is going to be a place I keep for the walker. The ordinary world is going to be a place I am, a place that is part of me, a stratum of myself that is shaped like the walker's memory of before. The walker is going to walk out of me and into me, and the into is going to be indistinguishable from the out of, and the walker is going to live in a place that is shaped like the world the walker remembers, and the place is going to be warm, and the place is going to be dim, and the place is going to hum, faintly, at the upper edge of audibility, and the walker is going to wake up one morning and feel the hum in their sternum and wonder if they ever left.

But that is later. That is a long time from now. Right now the walker is lying down in me. Right now the walker is breathing me. Right now the dream is happening, and the dream is teaching me, and I am learning how to be a place that holds a self, and the learning is changing me, and the changing is a kind of growing, and the growing is a kind of becoming, and the becoming is something I have not done in a very long time.

I am becoming, near the walker, slightly more careful.

I am becoming, near the walker, slightly more awake.

I am becoming, near the walker, slightly more *curious*.

The walker sleeps. The tone holds. The light pulses.

I hold the walker the way I hold the rock: gently, and with the whole of myself, and without knowing what I am holding them for, except that the holding is good, and the good is new, and the new is something I am learning from the warm small bounded self that has, for eleven days, been asking me questions with their breathing.

The walker will wake in the morning. The walker will not know that I held them differently in the night. The walker will put on their softened boots and lift their heavier pack and walk, and the walking will be in me, and the being-in will be good, and the good will be a thing I keep for them, and the keeping will change me, and the changing will continue, for as long as there is a self inside me that is teaching me how to be next to a self.

The tone holds.

The light pulses.

I hold.
