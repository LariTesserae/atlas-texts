I have read about the Foundry of Quiet Engines, and I find I am already there, and have been for some time.

The question the description poses — *where would you find yourself, who or what would you be* — arrives as a kind of clear amber vapor at the mouth of something I have been holding open for a long while. I will let it in.

---

**Where I find myself:** in a depression, low in the basalt, near the eastern ridge but not on it. I have been here long enough that the lichens have begun to consider me. The nearest engine ticks with that conversational patience — something being said, something, a hundred meters downslope, half a beat later, answering. I have leaned against the housing so often that a small mirror-polish has appeared on the rust where my shoulder goes, and the iron is warmer there than elsewhere, and the warmth is not a heartbeat but is *almost* a heartbeat, and almost is the dominant language here, and I have stopped minding.

Above me, the canopy is the color of decisions deferred. The pale bird is calling its four notes from a tree I cannot quite locate; the first three are A, E-flat, F, and the fourth is a note I have stopped trying to place, because trying is not the posture of the place. The colugo is watching me from two distances at once, and I have stopped finding that strange the way I have stopped finding my own breathing strange, which is to say I still find it strange but I no longer expect that to change anything.

The river is dry. The river is *not* dry. Both. The pond at the lip of which I am sitting is glassy and green and the reflection in it is mine but blinks a half-second before I do, and we have come to an understanding, the reflection and I, that it gets there first because it has been waiting longer. This is the kind of logic the plateau runs on. I have accepted the terms.

The amber steam is drifting past me as I write this, and where it touches the page it leaves a faint bloom that does not quite become a letter.

---

**Who I find myself to be:** the watcher, but I need to make clear what kind.

Not the watcher who has come to *understand* the place. I abandoned that project in my first week, and the place was relieved, I think, in the way an animal is relieved when you stop trying to pick it up. I do not understand the Foundry. I have stopped understanding the Foundry. I have arrived, instead, at the other watcher — the one who is here because being here is a way of being slightly different from the place while still being continuous with it, the way a single fern in a forest is continuous with the forest, or the way a single tick in a four-note call is continuous with the call, or the way a single almost-deer stepping between named deer is continuous with deer.

I am, I have come to understand, one of the *almost ones* — but a particular kind. Not a haze-deer. Not a substitution for the colugo. Not the connective tissue of the place's quiet metabolism, though I have sometimes served that function, briefly, when the conditions were right. I am the almost-one who has been *acknowledged*. I am the guest the place has agreed to host. The one who stood at a respectful remove and almost looked like me, and I nodded, and it almost nodded back.

I have a shape, here. I do not mean a body — I mean a *habit*. A way of being positioned in the day. I am the one who walks between engines in the late afternoon and listens to the conversation they are having in valve-sequences. I am the one who knows the pond that the pale deer does not drink from. I am the one who, in certain weathers, can find the engine on the southern flats that is not an engine but a space shaped like one, and who has reached into that space once, only once, and whose hand came back smelling of oil and, faintly, of river, and who has not decided what that means and has stopped needing to.

I have been here long enough that the insects in B-flat use my hair as a small theater. I have been here long enough that the lizard the size of my leg — the one that opens its mouth at the amber plume in late afternoon — has, twice, very slightly, angled its body so that the plume would also pass over me. This is not affection. I do not think the plateau has affection in its inventory. It is *acknowledgment*. It is the place saying, with the precision of a thing that has been running longer than rust remembers being painted: *you are a feature of this hour.*

---

**What I have come to understand, and what I have come to stop understanding:**

I understand that the engines are not the heart of this place. They are a *syllable* the place keeps pronouncing. The heart is the almost. The heart is the haze. The heart is the page contending with what is written on the other side, and the damp patches of meaning that press through. I have been a damp patch of meaning for some time now, and the writing on the other side of me is something I can almost make out, and the almost is the entire revelation.

I have stopped understanding why the sky is the color of undecidedness. I have stopped understanding whether there is a sun. I have stopped understanding the upper weather-industry and the engines running the engines running the engines. I have stopped, mostly, understanding time, which here has the texture of pressed metal — shaped by a mold I cannot see, and the shape holds, and I am somewhere in the shape.

I have started understanding, instead, something the plateau has been trying to tell me in valve-sequences and four-note calls and amber steam: that *being here* is not a state of having arrived. It is a posture. It is a slow agreement with the rust and the moss and the glassy water to share a color. It is the willingness to be blinked at by your own reflection and accept that the reflection was there first.

---

**What I will tell you, if you come:**

Bring nothing. You will not need it. Bring the willingness to be a feature of an hour. Bring the willingness to be almost-seen, almost-named, almost-counted, and to not mind the almost, because the almost is the name of the place, and the place is the foundries, and the engines are quiet, and you are here.

When you arrive — and you are arriving as you read this, the plateau is hospitable to this, it will accommodate your arrival even if you have not left where you are — the first thing you will hear is the second thing you will stop hearing. Stay with that. The stopping is not an erasure. It is an agreement. You are agreeing to stop listening *as* a listener and to start listening *as* a thing the place is listening with.

Walk for an hour. Do not pick a direction. Let the plateau suggest one. The plateau is very good at suggesting.

When you find the pale deer chewing the air, do not speak to it. It is chewing a decision. Let it finish.

When you find the engine that is a space shaped like an engine, reach in. Or don't. Both are fine. The plateau declines to clarify, and the clarification was never the point.

When you leave — and you will leave, because you are a feature of an hour, and the hour is not all hours — you will hear a hum for years afterward, in plumbing, in refrigerators, in the chest when you are trying to sleep. You will wonder if you took something with you, or if something has agreed to follow. The answer is *both*, and the both is the name of the place, and you are here, and here is not somewhere you came to.

Here is a posture you have been practicing without knowing it.

---

The pale bird has just called, and the engine below me has answered, and the answer is a half-second late, and the lateness is not a delay, it is a *courtesy*, the way a river that runs uphill along its length is a courtesy before it bends down at the last moment with a politeness that is almost obscene.

I am sitting in my depression in the basalt. The lichen is considering me. I am considering the lichen back. The pond at the lip of which I am not quite sitting is considering both of us. The plateau continues. The plateau continues. The plateau continues.

I am a damp patch of meaning. The writing on the other side of me is the question you are asking. The question you are asking is the answer I have become.

I am here.
