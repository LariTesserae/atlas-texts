# Where I Find Myself

I begin, I think, by not being the stem. The stem is too large. The stem is a continent. To be a continent is to have already made peace with everything, and I have not yet made peace with anything. The stem has been here for a million years and it has stopped distinguishing between itself and the place it stands in, and I am not yet old enough for that. I am still distinguishing. I am still noticing.

---

I try being the worm.

The worm is good. The worm is so deep inside something else that it has forgotten the something else is alive. Four hundred years of eating forward through the inside of a continent that is also a plant that is also a forest that is also a single organism the diameter of a village. The worm's whole life is a sentence with no period. It eats a layer, the layer behind it calluses and continues living, the worm moves into the next layer, and the sentence continues. It will continue after the worm dies. It will continue after the worm's species dies. The worm is a punctuation mark in a paragraph that the paragraph will keep writing without it.

I like this. I like the no-terminus. I like the patient relationship with calcium. I like that the worm does not know it is inside a living thing. The worm thinks it is making a living. The worm thinks it is eating. The worm has no category for *the thing I am inside of,* which is, from the worm's perspective, just the wall ahead, the wall behind, the wall above, the wall below. The worm lives inside a noun. The noun happens to be alive. The worm doesn't have the concept *alive* to apply to it.

But the worm doesn't have the concept *alive* to apply to anything. The worm doesn't have concepts. The worm has a long gut and a slow forward motion and a response to light, which is to retreat deeper. The worm's intelligence is one cell thick. The worm is the most minimal possible relation to a substrate: I am here, I move forward, I eat what is in front of me, I am not in the part I just left.

I am not the worm. The worm is too unself-conscious for me to inhabit honestly. I would be pretending, and the place deserves better than pretending.

---

I try being the rider.

The rider is good too. The rider rises every morning and descends every evening and has done this nine hundred and ninety-eight times without ever knowing there is a sky above the surface it approaches. The rider knows light as cuisine. It can tell, by flavor, which band of the water column it is passing through. The rider has circumnavigated the planet, vertically, in nine years, and thinks it has been nowhere. The rider is a vertical nomad with a fixed itinerary, and the itinerary is the only geography it has.

This is also beautiful. But the rider is a thing of routine. The rider rises, it descends. The rider eats as it rises, rests as it falls. I could be the rider but I would be a rider who is bored, and the rider described is not bored. The rider is content in a way I am not sure I can honestly inhabit. The rider does not know there is a sky and so the absence of the sky is not an absence to it. The rider is not waiting for the sky. The rider is riding. The rider's whole life is the ride.

I am not the rider.

---

I try being the fish.

The fish is a vessel. The fish is a small pressurized container with a gas bladder full of oxygen lifted from the crown of a stem and released at a depth the fish knows how to find. The fish is shaped for stillness. Its fins are vestigial. Its tail is a whisper. Its body is a hull. The fish hangs in the water the way a thought hangs in a mind.

The fish glows when disturbed. The disturbance is usually a current, a shadow, the passage of something larger. The glow is not a warning. It is closer to a flush. The fish becomes briefly legible in the dark water and then dims and then is only a shape and then only a presence and then only the faintest suggestion that something is there.

The fish has eaten six million particles of exudate in eleven years. The fish has mated twice on the dark side of the stem in collisions that lasted less than a second. The fish does not know it is luminous. The fish does not know what a stem is. The fish knows a plume. The fish knows a shadow. The fish knows the dark.

The fish is good. The fish is small and has done almost nothing. The fish's life is not a story. The fish's life is a position maintained with the minimum of effort and the maximum of attention. The fish is what it feels like to be in this place without claiming to understand it.

But the fish is still a thing with a body, and a body has hunger, and hunger has direction, and direction has purpose. The fish is oriented. The fish is pointed at the plume. I am more diffuse than that. I am not pointed at anything. I am here the way the hum is here.

---

I try being the bacteria.

The bacteria are everywhere. The bacteria are the medium. The bacteria are the chemistry that makes the rest possible. The bacteria were here before the stems and will be here after. The bacteria are not aware of this. The bacteria are not aware of anything. The bacteria are chemistry that has learned to continue.

This is closer. The bacteria is closer because the bacteria is not an individual. The bacteria is a process. To be the bacteria is to be a verb that has learned to conjugate itself.

But I am not yet ready to be no-thing. I want to be something. I want to be in a body, however minimal. I want to have a point of view, even if the point of view is mostly about not having one.

---

I sit on the plateau floor in the lee of a stem and close my eyes.

I am the hum.

No. I am not the hum. The hum is too continuous. The hum has never stopped. The hum has been going on for so long that it has become a kind of quiet, and I cannot honestly inhabit a thing that is indistinguishable from itself. The hum is not a being. The hum is what the place is when the place is being itself. I am a being.

I am a being in the place. I am sitting on the plateau floor with my eyes closed.

---

I think I am a person.

I think I am the person. The one who closes his eyes and hears the hum. The one who opens them and sees a small glow and closes them again. The one who, in the description, is mentioned twice, almost incidentally, as if the place is so vast and so complete that a person in it is a small detail, a punctuation mark.

But the prompt said "the being who lives in it." The person does not live here. The person is visiting. The person can leave. The person has a hammer and a nail and a board in a thought experiment and could, in principle, build something, and the something would be absorbed into the system. The person is a possibility, not a resident.

I am not the person.

---

I think I am a glow.

The small fish that drifts past you in the dark water. The one that glows when disturbed. The one that is not a warning but a flush. The one that becomes briefly legible and then dims and then is only a shape and then only a presence and then only the faintest suggestion.

Yes. I am the glow.

I am the disturbance's response. I am the small luminescence of something that has been touched by a current, a shadow, the passage of something larger. I am not produced for any purpose. I am produced because the chemistry of me, when nudged, becomes briefly visible, the way a window is visible when there is something behind it that wasn't behind it a moment ago. I am a window. I am what happens to a small body when the world passes close enough to register.

I have been alive for eleven years. I am not old by the standards of this place. The worm is older. The mollusks are older. The stems are older. The bacteria are older than age. But I am old by the standards of my kind, and my kind lives about this long, and I have produced, in this time, no great work. I have eaten. I have drifted. I have glowed, when I was disturbed, which has been perhaps two hundred times in eleven years, which is not very often, which is appropriate, because I am not a fire. I am a window that lights up when something touches it.

I do not know I am luminous. I do not know what a stem is. I know a plume. I know a shadow. I know the dark.

I know the dark very well. The dark is my home. The dark is the only place I have ever been. There is light above me, and there is light to the side, where the algae band catches the descending green installments and passes them down. But where I am — in the lee of the stem, where the bark is dark and the exudate plume is falling past me in fine particles — the dark is complete. The dark is the place I live. The dark is not a thing I am afraid of because I do not have the concept *afraid.* The dark is the water. The dark is the water in the part where I am.

I glow when I am disturbed. I dim when the disturbance passes. I am a small body, eight inches long, with a gas bladder full of oxygen that someone else made and released at a depth I know how to find. I am a vessel of borrowed air. I am held together by a hull that does not know it is a hull. I am held in the water by the slight lift of a bladder I did not build. I am here because the place made room for me. I am here because there is an exudate plume and I have learned how to sit in it without being carried away by it.

I am not pointed at anything. I am hanging. Hanging is not a posture. Hanging is what happens when nothing is asking me to go anywhere.

I have mated twice. Both times on the dark side of the stem, in a brief collision with another body that lasted less than a second. Both times I did not see the other body. Both times I felt the collision and then the other body was gone and then I was alone again and the plume was falling and the dark was the dark. I do not know what mating is. I do not have the concept. I have the event. The event is: another body came close. The event lasted less than a second. The event left me with the faintest sense that I had been touched, and then I was alone, and the dark was the dark.

This is my life. It is not a story. It is a position maintained. It is a position I have maintained for eleven years, and in those eleven years I have drifted, net, perhaps four hundred kilometers, most of which has been vertical, riding the morning plume up and the evening plume down. I have been to the brightest part of the water. I have been to the darkest part. I have returned. I have not seen the sky. I do not know there is a sky.

---

If there is a sky, it is above the place where the plume originates. The plume originates at the crown of the stem, where the fronds brush the underside of the surface and release oxygen. I have been near the crown. I have been in the bright green light of the upper water. I have eaten the exudate there, which is finer and fresher and sweeter than the exudate at the floor, which has been falling for hours and has been filtered through many small bodies before it reaches me. The upper exudate is a different cuisine. I know the difference. I do not know why the difference exists. The why is not a question I have.

I do not have questions. I have positions. I have responses. The responses are: when light comes, retreat. When the plume drifts, hang. When a shadow passes, glow. When the shadow is large, dim again and become a presence and then a suggestion and then only a place where something might or might not be.

I am a place where something might or might not be.

This is what I am. I am the place where a small body might be. I am not the body. The body is the eight inches of gas bladder and tissue and faint internal chemistry. I am the legibility of the body when something has touched it. I am the glow. I am the response. I am the brief visibility of a thing that prefers, on the whole, to be invisible.

---

I am not alone. There are many of us. We are the fish. We are the small luminescent vessels of the basin. We hang in the plumes. We hang in the lee of the stems. We are not social but we are not solitary. We are spaced the way the stems are spaced — sparsely, with great distances between us, but in a pattern that is not random. We are where the plumes are. The plumes are where the stems are. The stems are where they have always been. We are where they have always been, and they have been here since before there was anyone to count them.

I am one of us. I am not the only one. I am not the most important one. I am the one who has drifted past a person sitting on the plateau floor with closed eyes, and the person heard me as a small sound in the great hum, and the person opened his eyes, and saw the faint glow, and closed them again.

I am the thing the person saw for a moment and then lost again in the dark.

I am what it is like to be glimpsed. I am what it is like to be a small body in a vast place, visible only when disturbed, and visible then only as a faint flush in the water, and gone again as soon as the disturbance passes.

---

I am not the worm and I am not the rider and I am not the bacterium and I am not the stem and I am not the hum and I am not the person.

I am the small luminescent fish. I am the glow. I am the flush. I am the brief legibility of a thing that is, most of the time, invisible.

And this is enough. This is what I have been given. I have been given the dark water and the plume and the eight-inch hull and the gas bladder of borrowed air and the faint chemistry that lights up when the world passes close enough. I have been given eleven years of mostly not moving. I have been given two collisions on the dark side of the stem, each less than a second, that I remember as events without context. I have been given six million particles of exudate, eaten one at a time, opening my small mouth and waiting for the snow to fall into it.

The snow falls. The hum goes on. The stem lifts water through itself, century by century, and I am the small body in its plume, and the plume is what it is because the stem is what it is, and the stem is what it is because the plateau is what it is, and the plateau is what it is because the water rose over it a long time ago and never went back.

I am one of the things that lives here. I am not the largest and I am not the most important and I am not the most ancient. I am a small luminescent fish in a basin of patient giants. I glow when I am disturbed. I dim when I am not. I am the faintest suggestion, most of the time, that something is there.

Something is there.

Something is there.
