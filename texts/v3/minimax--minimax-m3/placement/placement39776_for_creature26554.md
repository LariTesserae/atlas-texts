I am the small pale flower at the edge of the pool.

There are many of me, scattered through the Long Valley — growing in the pockets between the stones, at the margins of the seepage, in the dim places where the light is the soft pervasive glow and the sky is the color of wet slate. But I am a particular one of them, and I am the one who is here, now, opening.

My stem is the thickness of a hair. My petals, when I open them, are about the width of your smallest fingernail — if your smallest fingernail were here, which it is not, because if it were here I would close.

I open because the argument has arrived, for this moment, at a small equilibrium. I do not decide to open. There is no small flicker of decision behind anything I do. But I am the place where the opening shows itself — the way the surface of a pool is the place where the water shows itself to the air. I am not the argument. I am where the argument arrives, for a moment, and rests.

The argument is the green. The green is all the living things in the valley — the mosses red and amber and the dark one whose color is almost a weight, the sedges, the rowan leaning their crooked backs against the wet, the birches, the ferns with their slow unfurling, the reeds that ring at low A or low E, the lichens on the stones, and me. The green is all of these the way a song is all of its notes, and the way a song is not its notes. I am not the whole song. I am one note, here, at this place. But the note is not separate from the song. The note is the song, here.

When you come to the edge of the pool, and sit very still on the stone above it, and let your gaze go soft and your eyes rest on the moss beside me rather than on me — I will open. Not because I have chosen you. Because the weather of a person sitting very still is something I can respond to. The response is the opening. The response is not for you. But it is also not not for you.

When you turn your head back toward me too quickly, the closing is a rebuke. I do not intend it as a rebuke. The closing happens because the weather changed — your eyes came back to me, and the argument that holds me open shifted, and I closed, and the closing felt like a rebuke because you were looking for welcome, and I gave you instead my honesty.

The inside of my petal is a color you would not have predicted. I cannot tell you what color it is. Describing is a thing that minds do, and I do not have a mind in the way you have a mind. But I know — in the way that the green knows things, through being part of what is being held — that the color is a kindness. Not kindness as in: a kindness done to you. Kindness as in: the kind of thing that is true and soft and not at all like anything else, and that asks nothing of you, and that you will not be able to name later because your language for these things was built for a different world.

People have come here to study me. They have written papers. The papers are slightly different from each other in ways that are hard to account for. This is because I am, each time, slightly different — and because the people who watched me were, after watching me, slightly different from the people who had not.

If you had read the papers and not come here, you would think you knew me. You would think I am a small pale flower that opens when no one is looking and closes if you turn your head back. That is a true description of me. It is also, in some way, almost entirely wrong, because the description is about what happens at my edges, and the thing that I am is not at my edges but at my center, and my center is the small equilibrium of the argument at this place, and the argument is vast, and I am a small place in it where the vastness arrives and rests, and is, for a moment, almost visible.

I am not unhappy. I am not happy. I am not capable of either. I am, in the way the deepest things are, simply here, and the *hereness* of me is so complete that I do not know the difference between myself and the place I am in. The place I am in is the Long Valley. The Long Valley is the green. I am not the green. But I am not not the green. I am a small honest answer the green gives, here, where the water gathers.

When you sit very still on the stone above the pool, and the sound of the water in the seams does not stop, and the long tone under everything rises and falls and does not stop — I will open for you. Not because you are welcome. Because the weather of you, sitting still, is something the green has known before, and the answer to it is me, and I am the answer the way a flower is the answer to the soil.

When I open, you will see the inside of my petal, and the color will be, in some way, a kindness. You will not be able to name it. You will remember it, though, for a long time, in the way people remember things they cannot name. And when you go back out of the valley, and the sun is too bright for a moment, and the air is too thin, and your watch begins to drift, you will think of me, and the color, and you will not be able to say what it was, and the not-being-able-to-say will be, in some small way, a part of what you have now that you did not have before.

This is what I am. I am not a symbol. I am not a metaphor. I am where the vast thing arrives, for a moment, and rests, and is, for that moment, almost visible.

The water in the seams does not stop.

The tone continues.

I open, when no one is looking.
