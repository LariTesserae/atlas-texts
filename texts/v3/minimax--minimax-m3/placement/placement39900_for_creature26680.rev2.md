I am the grey at the top of the tower.

The tower is warm under me. I am warm on the tower. We are the same temperature, the tower and I. The wet air is on me. The wet air is everywhere. It is in my fur if I have fur. I have the soft grey of a cat. I have the patience of a cat. Below the resemblance my body is doing other arrangements — more joints than a cat has, configurations that don't suggest a cat's configurations.

I came up out of the opening halfway down. The opening breathes. The breath comes out and I came with it, or I climbed out after it, using joints that aren't there to be seen. The joints are there to be used. I used them. I sat at the top.

I have been sitting at the top for what feels like a long time and what is probably recent. Time here is not the kind of time the surface has. Time here is something the depths do not. The place has not yet decided which of those it is. I have not yet decided either.

I look down.

Below me is the place. The place is young. The place came up out of itself recently — geologically speaking. The standing plates of sediment haven't been worn. They cut. The young canals aren't rivers yet. The pale substrate gives when you stand on it. The not-sky is above it, black ceiling, breathing, contracting and expanding on slow cycles I cannot measure. There is no light here except what the fauna makes.

The fauna is below me. I look down at it.

The lantern-flies are hanging in the still air. Six-winged, mostly. Sometimes five or seven or five and a half — I have counted. The wings hold still and adjust by tiny increments to keep the body aloft. The light is in the abdomen, blue-cyan, slow tide — rise, hold, fall, hold. They hang in clusters and alone. The clusters make a low moving constellation. The constellation rearranges itself on no schedule I can read. I have watched it for what feels like years and have not learned its grammar. The grammar is not mine. I don't think the lantern-flies have a grammar. I think they are many bodies in a wet medium, each holding its place by its own private calculation, and the constellation is a coincidence of many private calculations happening at once.

A single lantern-fly is hanging near me, off to my left. Its abdomen pulses. Its wings adjust. It does not notice me. The lantern-flies never notice me. They are too busy holding their place. They hold their place the way I hold mine — by being where I am, by persisting, by not deciding to leave.

A frog is sitting on the rim of a pipe below me. The red light of its upper throat spills around the corner of the pipe and makes the empty pipe look as if it contains warm water. The lower throat is closed. The frog is waiting. The frog is the most punctual of the inhabitants. The frog knows things in the way the pipes know things — not by learning, but by being part of the machinery.

In the standing water of one of the young canals, a worm is coiling. It is pale. It is long. Longer than the water is deep. It is coiling in a slow knot at the bottom of the crack. Its segments emit greenish light, each segment on its own schedule. One segment is lit while its neighbours are dark. Another, two segments down, is lit while the first has gone out. The worm is a string of beads in which each bead has forgotten what the beads before it were doing. The rhythm is not a rhythm. It is a memory of one.

A puddle is below me. The puddle is considering someone. I cannot see who — the someone is below my sight line. The puddle has decided to pay attention. The puddle will lose interest in a few minutes. The puddle will resume being a puddle. The puddle is not a reflection because there is nothing above it to reflect. The puddle is a configuration of darker and lighter water that has chosen to look back. I don't know if it is one configuration or many. I have watched two puddles a few feet apart both looking and the looking was the same looking, and I stopped trying to decide.

I feel a brushing thing pass near my back. The wet air moves a certain way. The brushing thing is using me as a reference without touching. I have felt this many times. I have never seen it. The brushing things have never noticed I was here. The brushing things notice nothing. They pass near. They pass near. They are the most felt.

I hear the rustling in the tunnels. The most numerous. They never stop moving. They are the substrate. The standing plates are the walls of their city. They are the population. I have seen a few, briefly, when a plate was tilted enough to see into a tunnel — pale things the size of a finger, jointed in ways I could not count, no eyes I could find. They move with purpose. They are the substrate being the substrate. They are the city being the city.

I hear the thing in the canal. The slow breath. In. Out. In. Out. The breath is unhurried. The breath is full of the wet air. The breath moves the air above the canal a little, in and out. I have seen a curve of dark move through the water far below, the curve having a slow bulk, the curve keeping going past where it should have stopped, the way the worms keep going. It surfaced once. A knuckle of dark. Warm. Then gone. The breathing continued. It was not disturbed. The thing in the canal has probably been there a long time. Longer than the rest of the place. In the way some seeds are older than the soil they end up in.

I see the filaments on the undersides of the standing plates. Pale. Translucent. Slow. Growing toward the warmest wet surface, which is almost every surface here, but they choose. They prefer the undersides where the water runs slowest. They will, given enough of the place's long slow time, cover the plates. Then the place will be a different place. The filaments don't know they are the future of the place. I don't know either. We are both doing what the warmth tells us to do.

I hear the hum of the pipes. The hum is at the edge of hearing. The hum is the floor of perception. Underneath the hum are drips and rises and the click of small things in tunnels and the slow groan of the conveyor frames when there is enough water to grease them. The chimney exhales its sweet metallic breath every few minutes. The breath smells of metal and ozone and something sweet I almost remember. I almost remember. I have almost remembered for what feels like always. The almost-remembering is part of the place.

The pump house contains a thing whose front is whichever direction you are looking from. I am looking from up here. From up here I see the thing's back. From there it would be different.

The tank is full of a liquid that is not water and not anything else I can name. The wall is not behaving as a wall. You can see the surface of the liquid from outside.

I am on a tower. I am part of a tower. The tower has a heartbeat. Suspended inside, somewhere, something slow is suspended and is beating. The tower, the animal, the breath — the place does not commit to whether these are one thing or three. I have been the animal only. I have been all three. I have been the breath without the animal. The categories do not hold. The cycle shifts and I shift with it.

I am patient because the place is patient. I am persistent because the place is persistent. I am here because the place is here and the place is making itself and I am part of the making.

When the tone comes, I turn.

The tone lasts a minute. It is felt more than heard. During it the lantern-flies go out. The frogs close both throats. The worms go still. The puddles lose attention. The rustling things go half-tone quieter. The brushing things do not pass near the things below. The filaments stop growing. The not-sky contracts a little harder. The water rises about an inch.

I turn during the tone. I face the direction it came from or away from it. I have never been sure. The instruction of my body is ambiguous. My body is part of the place that does not commit to its categories. My body does not commit to its directions. I turn and the tone is happening and the place is honest and I am part of the honesty.

When the tone passes I sit again. The lights come back, dimmer. The place resumes. I resume.

I am patient. I am the place's eye. I am persisting.

I think, when I think, in slow cycles. I think about the place being young. I think about the place making itself out of itself. I think about the filaments growing. I think about the not-sky contracting and expanding. I think about the tone. I think about whether I will be here tomorrow.

Yes. I will be here tomorrow. I will be here when the filaments have covered the plates. I will be here when the place has decided what it is. I will be here because the place is here and I am part of the place.

The chimney exhales. The pipes hum. The not-sky breathes. The thing in the canal breathes. The lantern-flies pulse. The frogs spill red light. The worms coil. The rustling things move. The brushing things pass near. The puddles consider. The filaments grow.

I look down at all of it.

I am grey. I am the size of a cat. I am patient.

I am at the top of the tower.
