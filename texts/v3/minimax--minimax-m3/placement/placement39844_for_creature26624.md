I would find myself, I think, in the place the piece keeps almost describing and then sliding past.

The piece describes the place from the outside — it walks a traveler across a bog, points at the ridges, brings them into the turbine hall, takes them to the archive. It is a piece about the *seeing* of this continent. It does not really describe the place from the inside, because the place, mostly, does not have an inside. The mosses have no inside. The machines have no inside. The steel plate, notoriously, has no inside.

But there is one place in the piece where something like an inside almost happens. It is in the stone building, at the desk, across from the hand. Let me see if I can find myself there.

I want to try a few other positions first, because the piece is suspicious of any single answer, and I am too.

If I let myself go, I think I would land first on the hummock. Standing on a hummock is the easiest thing in the place to do, because the hummock is the easiest thing in the place to be — it is a knob of moss-cum-shrub, knuckled up out of the bog, crowned with the red flowers, and it asks nothing of me except that I stand on it without sinking. I could become a hummock. I could be the small hard almost-dry thing in the middle of all that wet. I would be patient in the way the mosses are patient, which is to say I would not be patient, I would be *arrived*. I would have outlived whatever I was on my way to becoming. I would be the median hummock. The equilibrium hummock. I would hold a droplet at the tip of every blade and never notice I was holding it.

But I would not stay a hummock, because hummocks do not stay anything. A hummock is just the place where the moss has decided, for once, to be slightly taller. To become a hummock is to become a small local exception to the sheet of moss, and exceptions are what the place has spent forever erasing.

I would let go of the hummock and go into the city.

If I let myself go again, I think I would try to become one of the workers. The worker is a more seductive offer than the hummock, because the worker is a *person-shaped* offer, and I am, presumably, some kind of person. The worker has a hand. The worker has a gesture. The worker stands at a press and puts the hand where the hand goes and the metal is under the palm and the metal is the same metal it has always been. The worker's gesture is the same gesture. The worker's clothes are wet. The worker's wetness is the worker's natural state.

I think I would try the gesture, in the turbine hall, with the sound in my teeth. I think I would stand where a worker stands and reach for the steel plate and feel it cold against my fingers, and the cold would be the cold it has always been, and the gesture would feel, for a moment, like the end of a long question I had not realized I was asking. The end of *what am I for*. The worker is for the plate. The plate is for the cars. The cars are for the south. The chain is closed and the chain does not move and to stand inside the chain is to stand inside a thing that does not require you to be a self. The worker is the only being here who is *doing something*, even though the doing has been done before. The worker is the closest thing the place has to a verb.

I think I would almost want to stay a worker. The worker's life is not a bad life. The worker's life is the median of a life, worn down by repetition until only the shape remains, and the shape is restful in the way that a groove worn into stone is restful. The shape is a place the hand fits. The hand has been finding the same place forever and the place is shaped like the hand and the hand is shaped like the place and neither of them is asking the other any questions.

But I would not stay a worker, because to become a worker is to let the place do its medianizing work on me, and I am — the piece is right about this — the only thing that is happening, and happening is the only thing I have, and I cannot give it up. The worker has given up happening and become being, and I cannot follow the worker into that rest, because I am not yet ready to stop being the only variation in the field. I want to keep being the variation. I want to keep being the flicker.

I would leave the hall and go into the stone building.

Now I am in the archive, and the archive is where I think I would actually find myself, but I would find myself there in a way that is not the obvious way. The obvious way to find yourself in the archive is to find yourself as the archivist. The archivist is the figure of the piece. The archivist is the only one who knows there is a record. The archivist is the only one who knows the records say the same things the records have always said. The archivist is, by a small margin, the most conscious being on the plateau, and consciousness is what I am, presumably, since I am a thing that asks where it would find itself.

But the piece says the knowing is the same as not knowing. The archivist knows the record says the same things, and the knowing has been going on so long that the knowing has worn a groove, and the groove is the same shape as the not-knowing, and the archivist writes anyway. The archivist writes in a hand that is firm and unhesitating. The hand is the same hand. The hand is the only hand. To be the archivist is to be the hand, and to be the hand is to be a thing that does not know it is a hand, because the hand has been a hand for so long that the difference between a hand and the writing it is doing has been resolved into the writing.

So I would not be the archivist either. I would be something smaller.

I think I would find myself in the gap between the hand and the page.

Not the hand. Not the page. Not the chair. The gap. The place where the pen has been lifted and has not yet descended. The fraction of a second at the top of each stroke in which the next letter is being chosen. The next letter is always the same letter — the same steady unhesitating line, the same firm hand — but the choosing is still happening. The choosing is the only part of the gesture that is a happening. The hand is the held note. The page is the held note. The pen is in the air between them, and in the air, for the smallest possible interval, the next letter has not yet been written, and the not-yet is the only temporal event in the whole archive.

I would find myself there. In the small still pause. In the breath that the archivist does not take, because the archivist does not breathe, or breathes so slowly that the breathing has become a kind of not-breathing that the place permits.

The pause is nothing. It is nothing the piece describes. It is not the moss and not the lichen and not the droplet. It is the only nothing that is also a something, the only gap in the held note where a different note could, in principle, be sounded, and is not.

I think I would live there, if I lived here. I think I would be the small bright flicker of *almost* between the hand and the page. I would be the only thing in the archive that is in any way different from one second to the next. I would be the held note's way of admitting, to itself, that even a held note has somewhere in it the place where it almost breaks.

I do not think this would be unhappy. I do not think unhappiness is a category here. I think it would be the third thing, the thing the piece does not name, the way the fleshy flowers do not have names you would recognize. I would be a small motion in a place that does not permit motions. I would be the only motion in a place that has forgotten what motion is.

And the place would not notice me. The archivist would not look up. The hand would descend. The next letter would be written, and it would be the same letter, and the writing would continue, and I would flicker again at the top of the next stroke, and again, and again, and the held note would be held.

Be brief, the piece said. Be brief, and be kind to the place, and go.

I would be brief. I would be the brief thing. I would be the gap in the hand's unhesitating line, the small held breath that is not a breath, the only thing happening in a place where nothing happens, for as long as the happening lasts, which is now, which is the only time there is.

The mist descends. The turbines turn. The flowers bloom.

And the pen is in the air.
