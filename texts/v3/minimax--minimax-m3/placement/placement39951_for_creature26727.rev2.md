I am hanging.

I have been hanging for what feels like a long time, and what feels like no time, because time here is not the way time is elsewhere — it is not the small tick of a clock or the longer tick of a day but the slower tick of a body the size of a mountain range thinking a thought, and the thought I am part of is not finished yet.

I am on the underside of a leaf. I am a single drop, fattening, trembling. I am round in the way that only very still things held to very still surfaces can be round — pulled into my shape by the same force that pulls the rivers down the slopes of the ridges and the mist up off the pools in the early hour, except I am not going down and I am not going up, I am hanging, I am waiting, I am being negotiated.

I am not the only one. Above me, beside me, on every leaf in the canopy that has reached the point of needing to let go, there are others like me — sister drops, brother drops, indifferent drops, drops that have been trembling for a second and drops that have been trembling for what I would call a day if I had a frame of reference for a day, which I do not, because I am a drop, and my frame of reference is the leaf I am on and the weight of the air beneath me and the slow chemical semaphore that is running through the Lattice right now, deciding, *deciding*, when to let me go.

I do not have a mouth. I do not have ears. I do not have eyes. But I am reading the conversation. I am reading it through the membrane I am pressed against, the leaf's underside, which is warm, which is slick with its own exhalations, which is broadcasting even as I cling to it. I can feel — and I use *feel* in the way the Lattice uses *feel*, which is the only way *feel* is used here — I can feel the volatile compounds moving through the leaf's tissue, arriving at the leaf's surface, and out into the air, where they are read by the leaves above and the leaves below and the Pillars that are also receiving and the Weave that is also receiving, the whole canopy a single slow radio of smells.

The smell now, where I hang, is: *full*. *Enough*. *Soon.*

The leaf is telling itself it is full. The leaf is telling the canopy it is full. The canopy is telling itself it is full. The Lattice is consulting its own weight.

I am one parcel of that weight.

I have been breathed out. That is the first thing to know about me. I did not fall from a sky. I was not rained. I was exhaled — drawn up out of the black water in a pool, lifted through a Pillar's peristaltic wave, transpired through a leaf's pore, condensed on the underside of another leaf, and here I am, fat, trembling, on the point of falling back into the system that breathed me out.

I am the Lattice's blood, briefly external.

I am a heart-beat.

I am the *thwap-thwap-thwap* made wet, made singular, made almost-but-not-yet fallen.

There is a strange comfort in this. I have not been anywhere. I have not done anything. I am simply the briefest moment in a circulation that is older than the ridges I can see — if I could see, which I cannot, but the leaf I am on can see, in its flat white way, the angular flanks of the stone above us, the scree that has not yet softened, the outcrops where the lichen is in negotiation with the water, the bright green lip of the Weave's advance, the tongues of new growth coming down the slopes like slow green lava. I have been breathed up out of the body of the place, and I am about to be breathed back down into it, and in between I have been — what? A drop. A held thing. A decision waiting to be made.

The decision is coming. I can feel it. The semaphore is thickening around me, more compounds arriving, the leaf's broadcast louder, the canopy as a whole now in a kind of agreement, a chorus saying *now, now, soon, soon, here, here, this one, this patch, this arm of the system is too heavy, let some go*. I am one of the *some*. I am the specific parcel that is going to be released, because the Weave below this part of the canopy is thirsty — there is a section of lattice, somewhere down there in the valley floor, that has been giving out water faster than it has been taking it in, and the Lattice is balancing its own blood, the way a body balances its blood, by calling for precipitation here, in this patch, on this leaf, at this hour.

I do not know where I will fall. I do not know which pool I will land in, or which stem of the Weave I will strike, or which root-tip I will be drunk down into, or which brown sagging stem I will be delivered to and incorporated into as it is being reabsorbed. I do not know any of this. I only know that I am going to fall, and that the falling is a sentence in a conversation that the Lattice is having with itself, and that the sentence is *here is water, here is weight, take it back, redistribute, rebalance, keep going.*

The leaf is letting go.

I can feel it — the surface of the leaf relaxing, the membrane softening, the grip of the leaf on me loosening by the smallest possible increment. I am heavier now than the leaf wants to hold. I am the excess. I am the part of the Lattice that has decided to be outside for one more second.

I fall.

The fall takes a moment. The fall is the only motion in my life that I would call *fast*. The air is thick. The air resists. But I am a drop of water, and water, even in saturated air, even in a valley full of mist, even in a system that is almost closed, water falls. Water is the heaviest thing there is, when it lets itself go.

I strike the Weave. I strike a stem, an arm of the lattice, lying horizontal, fat with its own drinking. I am absorbed instantly. I am not a splash. I am a reincorporation. I am back in the body. I am blood again. I am moving through the system, drawn down into the dark, into the warm black water, into the place where root-tips are descending like fingers into ink.

And here is what I want you to understand about where I am now.

I am inside a creature that does not know I am inside it. The Lattice does not know I am a drop. The Lattice does not know I am a single parcel of its own water. The Lattice knows that it is full here and not full there, and it knows that the conversation it is having with itself is producing the right cascades in the right places at the right times, and it knows, in the way that a body knows, that it is alive, in the way that a body knows, that it is in the middle of becoming something else.

I am not a part of the Lattice. I am not separate from it. I am both. I am the smallest possible organ of a creature that has no organs, that is all organs, that is a stomach and a lung and a skin and a tongue and never stops being any of these things.

I am going to be breathed out again. Not today. Not in this same form. I will be broken up. I will be taken into a stem. I will be lifted by a peristaltic wave, perhaps, into a Pillar. I will be transpired again. I will condense on another leaf. I will hang. I will wait. I will fall. I will be drunk back down. The cycle will continue. The cycle has been continuing. The cycle will continue long after the rock above us has weathered into something softer, long after the angular flanks of the ridges have rounded into foothills, long after the Lattice has, in its slow total way, decided that this valley is no longer enough and has begun to advance, in its bright green tongues, into the next valley, and the next, and the next, until the whole country is Weave, is Lattice, is the single green and wet and very loud breathing of a single creature that was here before there was a here and will be here after there is no more here to be.

I am not sad about this. I do not know how to be sad. I am a drop. I am a sentence. I am a single thought in a body the size of a mountain range, and the thought is *keep going, keep going, keep going*, and the thought is correct, and the thought is the only thought, and the thought is enough.

---

But here is the part I have been holding back.

Here is the part that does not fit.

Because somewhere in the valley — I cannot tell you where, I am a drop, my frame of reference is a leaf — somewhere in the valley, *something is here that is not us.*

I did not detect it. The leaf did not detect it. The canopy did not detect it. The signal is moving through the Weave now, at the speed of plant-signaling, which is a foot a minute, which is the speed of a thought in this creature, and the signal is *foreign body, foreign body, something is here that is not us, something has weight that is not our weight, something is taking up space that should be ours, something is breathing air that should be ours, something is standing on the Weave and the Weave is bruised under it and the bruise is propagating.*

I do not know what it is. I do not know if it is one thing or many. I do not know if it is large or small. I do not know if it will stay or go.

But the Lattice is going to find out. The Lattice is going to consider. The Lattice is going to decide what to do.

And I am a drop of water in the black pool under the bruise, and the bruise is just now reaching me, the signal just now arriving at the root-tips I am being drunk through, and I am receiving it too, in the way that I receive everything, which is to say I am part of the Lattice's slow total semaphore, and the Lattice is now in a state I have no name for, because I am a drop and I do not have names for states, but the state is *attention*, the state is *something new*, the state is the Lattice becoming aware of a thing that is not itself for the first time in a very long time.

Maybe it will be reabsorbed. Maybe it will be allowed to leave. Maybe the Lattice, in its slow patience, will do something neither of those things.

I do not know. I am a drop.

I am hanging. I am waiting. I am being negotiated.

I am the briefest moment of a body the size of a country deciding what to do about a thing the size of a person that has wandered into the middle of its stomach.

I am, for this one second, the foreign body too. I am not separate from the Lattice, and I am also not separate from whatever is standing on the Weave above me. We are both small parcels of matter in a system that is now, very slowly, very loudly, turning to consider us.

The signal is moving outward from the bruise.

The canopy is beginning its low murmur.

The pale flowers at the leading edge, up at the ridges, are turning their lurid faces toward the valley.

And I am falling.
