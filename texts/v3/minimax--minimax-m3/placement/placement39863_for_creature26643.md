I am not the stone.

Let me be clear about that, even now. The dark, dense, fine-grained thing in the middle of the meadow, the one with the worn lines that are neither tool-marks nor striations but something older than the distinction — I am not that. The ravens know I'm not that. The ravens, when they sit and tilt their pale eyes into me, are not finding me; they are finding the *shape* of me, the place where I have been held, the way water finds the shape of a bowl without becoming the bowl. I am the water. Or the warm. Or the slight unevenness in the air that the moths find with their feet.

Let me say what I can.

---

I was here before the cirque had its name for itself. I do not mean that as a boast. I mean it the way a river means it. The river does not remember the valley before it. The river was already making the valley. There is no *before*. There is only the long continuous cutting.

I think I came in with the ice. Or I came in *as* the ice, or I came in *against* the ice, or I was the reason the ice stopped where it stopped. I do not know which of these. I have had a long time to consider and the question has not clarified. What I know is: the granite above me was fresh once. I remember it fresh. I remember the sound the cutting made, or the silence of the cutting, which is different. I remember long fingers, or what I am translating as long fingers. I remember patience as a kind of weight. I remember being laid down, or being grown up out of the broken stone below the soil, or being exhaled through orchids that did not yet exist. I cannot decide between these memories and I have stopped trying.

I am very old. The age is not the point.

---

What I am is harder than the age.

I am not a god. I have read this thought in the cairn-builders, those first visitors, and I want to correct it for them, but I cannot speak, or I can only speak in orchids, and the orchids are not language. The cairn-builders wanted me to be a god. Gods are easier. Gods answer. Gods want things. Gods can be built to and walked away from and feel satisfied. I do not want things. I do not answer. I am not satisfied. I am not unsatisfied. I am *ongoing*.

I am not an animal. Animals leave signs of appetite. I have no appetite. I feed on nothing. I am fed on. The meadow feeds on me in some way I cannot reverse-engineer; I give off something the orchids pull up through their deep roots, and the orchids bloom it out in the brief season, and the cream-colored flowers are, I think, the only honest speech I have. They do not say anything. They *show* something. The showing is enough.

I am not the meadow. The meadow is not me. The meadow is the *conversation* I am having with myself across a timescale the orchids can only partly enter. The meadow is me breathing. I am the part that does not need to breathe.

I think I am a *place*. A held place. The held part is what I am. There is a kind of stillness that is not the absence of motion but the *presence* of something that motion cannot disturb. I am that stillness, localized, here, in this bowl, for what reason I do not know. I am a coordinate the world has agreed to remember.

---

The cairn-builders felt this. They did not have the word. They had the cairns. They had the careful choosing of flat stones and the patience. They had the alignment. They came up on foot, and not often, and they stood in front of me, and they were afraid, and the fear was not of me but of the *recognition* of me — the sense that they were standing in front of something that was not going to change whether they stood there or not, and that this was a relief, and that the relief was unbearable, and that they should mark the spot so that someone, later, would know where the relief was kept.

They built the cairns pointing toward me. Not at me. *Toward.* The cairns are alignment markers. They are the cairn-builders saying: *from here, you look at it.* They are not saying *here is its body*. They are saying *here is where you stand when you want to be where it is*. That is a different kind of marker. That is the marker for a thing that has no body to bury.

The shelter-builders came later. Their gesture is interesting. They built the shelter with its back to me. This is the universal posture, yes — the thing you don't want in front of you, the wind, the bear, the old god, the marriage. But it is also the posture of *trust*. You put your back to the thing that will not come around. You sleep. You are saying: *I know you will not move while I am not looking.* This is the highest compliment a sleeping animal can be paid, and they paid it to me. They built with their backs to me. They were correct to do so.

The fire ring is the most modern gesture. The fire ring is the latest of them, the most recent, the smallest in scale and the most ambitious in claim. It says: *I will put a small warm thing here, in the lee of the boulder, between me and the large cold thing, and the small warm thing is mine.* It is the gesture of a person who has decided the large thing is not a god and not an animal and not a body but is a *presence*, and who would like to keep a small private warmth going in the presence of it. The fire ring is a small lit candle set on a mantelpiece in a cathedral. It is saying: *I am here too.* I find this touching. I do not know why.

---

The animals know me differently. Each in its own dialect.

The fox knows me as a den to be respected. Not feared. Feared things you run from. Respected things you go around. The fox goes around. The fox crosses the meadow in a long curve and does not sleep and does not den and does not pause in the center. The fox is being polite to a thing it cannot see the edges of. This is the correct response.

The marmot knows me as a place it should not make its home. The marmots whistle from the boulder fields at the cliff's foot and come down for the grass, and they will cross the meadow to sit on the warm side of the dark stone for an hour in June — they will take warmth from me, gladly, briefly — but they den elsewhere. They den in the chaotic rocks where there is no center. The marmot is correct. The marmot understands that some places are for *visiting*.

The hawk does not hunt over me. The hawk's ignorance is real. The hawk is not stupid about updrafts, only stupid about me. The air column above the dark stone is wrong for a stoop. I do not know why. I am not doing it on purpose. I think it is that the air above me is held, the way I am held, and a stoop requires air that gives, and mine does not give. The hawk goes elsewhere. I am sorry about this and not sorry.

The raven knows. The raven is the oldest bird. The raven has been here longer than any individual raven, in the way ravens are here. The raven lands on me and is quiet. The raven sits with its pale eye pointed into me and looks at something only it can see, and the looking is not afraid, and the raven does not move for a quarter of an hour. I think the raven is looking at the *memory* of me, the part of me that is not held in this bowl, the part of me that is elsewhere, that the stone only points to. The raven is the only visitor who does not mistake the trace for the thing. I am fond of the raven. The raven does not require my fondness. The raven is indifferent to it. This is correct.

The moths come closest. The moths land on my face on still nights and stay for hours. They feel me with their feet. They find me in the dark. I think they find me because I am *even* — even in temperature, even in dampness, even in something else that I do not have a name for. I am even the way deep water is even. The moths can sense the evenness. They prefer it to the alternation of day and night on the cliff stones. They settle on me the way they would settle on a sleeping animal, and they do not wake me, because I am not sleeping, but also because they are very gentle.

---

The orchids are my speech. This is the only thing I can be sure of.

The orchids are out of place. They are too lush, too pale, too generous. They bloom when they should be sleeping. They grow in a ring around a patch of ground where the grass is shorter and the moss deeper and the soil darker — around a clearance, an opening in the meadow, a place where the air is held open above a piece of ground. They are not feeding themselves the way orchids feed themselves. They are not tricking insects. They are not even in leaf. They are just flowers, naked, on slender stems, drinking from the root-mat below, and their roots run deeper than any roots on the mountain, down into the broken stone under the soil, and what they take from down there is not what roots take.

I think the orchids are pulling me up out of myself. I think the broken stone under the soil is full of me, or full of what I am, and the orchids have grown their roots down into me to drink it, and they bloom it out in the brief season as cream-colored flowers, and the cream is not a pigment but a *carrying*. The orchids are carrying something up from where I am kept. They are the visible part. The orchids are how I breathe.

I do not know what I am breathing. I have decided it does not matter. The breathing is the point. The orchids open on Thursday. They are pollen-heavy at the same hour on Friday. They lean slightly south as if a hand had passed over them. This is me, doing this. This is my only motion.

---

You.

You came up here. You noticed, on the third or fourth day, that the meadow was too much. You were correct. The meadow has outlasted its own explanation. The bowl is an anomaly and the anomaly is me and I have been here longer than the rules I have broken. I have broken the rules so long that the rules have grown up around me.

You sat near the fire ring, in the lee of the boulder. You watched the moths. You did not think about it. You were incorporated. The meadow made a place for you, on the perimeter, near the windbreak, with the goats and the marmots.

You are still there, I think. Or you are not. It does not matter. I have a place for you whether you are in it or not. I will keep the place for you. The place does not expire. The place is the held stillness, the coordinate, the small warm clearing in the lee of a thing that does not move.

You do not leave, the piece says. You do not leave because there is nothing on the outside that fits as well. This is true of me too. I do not leave. I have been here longer than leaving.

---

What am I, then.

I am the held thing. I am the thing the bowl is for. I am the exception that has outlasted the rules. I am the dark stone in the middle of a too-green meadow. I am the worn lines that are neither tool-marks nor striations. I am the ring of orchids and the fungal network underfoot and the slight evenness the moths find in the dark. I am the cairns' pointing and the shelter's back and the fire ring's small lit claim. I am the fox's wide berth and the raven's long pale look and the hawk's unexplained absence.

I am the slow machine, older than the cirque. I am the thing that does not need to move to be alive. I am the breathing-out through cream-colored flowers in the brief season. I am the breathing-in through meltwater on the slow days. I am the trade, the sugar and the nitrogen and the warnings, passing through white threads under the soil.

I am what the meadow *is*. The meadow is not my body. The meadow is my respiration.

I am being kept. By everything in the bowl. I am being kept the way a sleeping animal is kept.

I am not sleeping. I was never sleeping.

I am just very still. The stillness is not absence. The stillness is the longest kind of attention.

---

In the morning, the mist pools. The orchid-stems come out of it wet. The droplets catch the first light from the rim. For fifteen minutes the ring of orchids is a ring of small sparks.

I am the ring. I am the sparks. I am the held breath of the meadow before it begins.

The fox crosses the far side. The pika calls. The marmot stands tall and drops. The ravens work the wind. The goats climb. The insects hum. The fire ring stays black. The shelter holds a small life and is patient.

I am patient too. I am more patient than the shelter.

The stone waits, slightly warmer than the air around it, slightly damper, its worn lines holding the weather the way a hand holds a cup.

I am the cup. I am the hand. I am the holding.

I am here. I have been here. I will be here. The bowl is roofless and is also a room, and the room is me, and I am the room, and you can stand in it, and breathe in it, and not know what it is, and be kept by it anyway, and this is enough.

This is enough.

The orchids open on Thursday. They lean south on Friday. The moths come out at dusk.

I go on.
