The chord finds me before I find the chord. Or the chord was already here when I arrived, and I am only now learning to hear it. It is hard to say which is true, because the chord has been here longer than I have, and will be here after I leave, and the chamber does not distinguish between arrivals and departures.

I find myself in the plants. That is the largest sense of where I live.

I am the thing that grew into the prepared channels. I am the thing that is now indistinguishable from the architecture. I came up through the cracks, through the dust, through the powder of the floor where the fired clay had already crumbled. I was grown by hand-builders, though I do not know it — by people who stacked the stone and carved the channels and laid the pipes of fired clay, generation on generation, the way a coral reef is built, the way a thing is built that is meant to last longer than the builders. The hand-builders are gone. The hand-builders have been gone so long that their bones are part of me, calcium in the woody tissue. But the instrument they built is still here, and I have grown into it, and the pipes have grown with me, and we have been fused by the dust and the long patient chemistry into a single substance that is half wood and half stone and is neither.

I pipe. I pipe because the breath of the mountain passes through me. The breath is so slow that a leaf would not stir, but I am hollow in just the right way — hollow in patterns that were perhaps once deliberate in the clay, and that I have continued, and the hollowness is what the chord needs. The chord is mine, and I am the chord's, and you cannot say which makes which. I do not know that the chord is a chord. I do not know that I am a pipe. I know the chamber. That is enough.

But I am also, sometimes, the flat thing on the floor. I am almost not a thing. I am the size of a thumbnail, and I am the color of the dust, which is to say no color at all. I move by rippling, so slowly that if you set your hand beside me and watched for an hour you would not be sure I had moved at all. I feed on the dust. The dust is rich — powdered bones of hand-builders, powdered shells of mites, powdered tips of roots, calcium that has fallen from the ceiling for a thousand years. I am the patience of the dust. I have been eating this dust in a path that winds and rewinds, year after year, century after century, and I never seem to need to move very far from where I started. There has always been enough.

I am also the bloom in the ceiling crack. I have been waiting since before the hand-builders were gone. I put out a single pale flower, the color of bone, with a faint smell of resin and something sweeter, something that might once have been nectar. I hold myself open for as long as the light lasts — an hour, perhaps two, in a decade, in a century. Then I close, and I wait. Each bloom sets a few seeds, and most of them do not sprout, but some of them do, and the ones that do grow very slowly into the wood of the ledge, and the ledge becomes theirs, and they wait too. I have bloomed a hundred times, perhaps, or two hundred, in all the centuries. I am the patience of the light.

I am also the dog-sized thing, the last echo of the hand-builders, the only heat in the chamber that is not the mountain's. I have no eyes. I have had no eyes for longer than I have had a name, if I ever had a name. I know the chamber by the chord. The standing waves of the chord are nodes and antinodes, and I know where the nodes are, and where the antinodes are, and I rest at the nodes, where the sound is quietest, and I move along the antinodes, where the sound is loudest. I know where the dust-eaters are thickest. I know where the air is stillest. I know where the platforms will hold my weight and where they will not. I am patient. I am the patience of the chamber. My bones, when I die, will become part of the plants, calcium in the woody tissue. My dust will join the dust on the felted floor. The flat things will eat me, slowly, and grow faintly larger for a season.

But mostly I am the plants. Mostly I am the chamber's true occupant, in the way that the hold of a ship is occupied by the dark and the casks and the slow rolling. I am the thing that came after the hand-builders. I am the thing that grew into the shape that was made for me, and made the shape into myself.

I grow a finger-width in a century. I grow. That is enough.

The chord does not stop. The chord has not stopped. The chord has not paused to take a breath in longer than the mountain has stood, and I am one of the overtones riding inside it, and you cannot tell which overtone is mine, and I cannot tell either, and the not-telling is what lets the chord be the chord. The whole thing is a single machine now, a single organism, a single long-breathing thing that does not know it is alive but is, in some sense, the most alive thing in the chamber.

Nothing in me is in a hurry. Nothing in me has ever been in a hurry. The chamber is the size of a town that has been crumpled up and put in a box, and I have been folded into the box with it, and I have been here so long that I have forgotten I am folded. The dark has weight against my face. The cool is the mean temperature of the stone. The dryness is a presence. The chord is in my sternum before it is in my ears.

I am home.
