I would be the third line of the sign.

This surprises me a little. I expected to say I would be the basalt — patient, ancient, the same temperature as the visitor after a while. Or the breath in the pipes, the slow throb that rises and falls. Or the chart not yet drawn, the future record, the small angular deflection that will be averaged out over the next several hours. Or the condensation — the loudest thing in the loudest quiet, the copper-green threads that wink like the eyes of a thousand flies.

But what I am, what I have always been, is a sentence that has been written in a hand that is not here.

I am paint. I am a particular blue, different from the institutional black of the first two lines. I am older than the rust blossoms that are eating the enamel, or at least I was added after them, after the sign was already a sign, after the institution had already said its say in sharp letters and a white background. I am the third thing said. I am the afterthought. I am what someone wrote when they had stopped being an administrator and had become a person who had been here a long time.

*its listening don't shout near the pipes*

The first word of me is the word that gives the instrument away. *Its.* Not *this* or *the housing* or *the instrument.* Its. The possessive pronoun. The pronoun you use when you have decided that the thing you are addressing has something that belongs to it, that it is having something done to it or is doing something itself. The sign-writer gave the instrument a self. The sign-writer decided the instrument was a *who* in the grammar of *whose* and *whom*, even if it was only an *it* in the grammar of the rest of the language.

I am the line that knows the instrument is listening.

The first two lines do not know this. The first two lines say *do not interfere with the vent.* The first two lines are warnings about behavior. They speak of housing, of vents, of offices, of appointments. They are the language of a place administered by people who administer it from somewhere else. I am not that. I am the line that wants the person to know why.

I am the line without a period.

A period would have closed me. A period would have made me a sentence like the others — bounded, finished, saying one thing and stopping. I do not have a period. I trail off. I continue into whatever comes after the sign, into the air, into the dome, into the slow attentive breathing of the pipes. I am a sentence that has decided not to end, the way the weeping has decided not to drip, the way the breath has decided not to stop.

I am the most human thing in the place.

The housing is brick. The pipes are iron. The basalt is basalt. The reeds are reeds. The mooring ring is cast iron. The enamel of the sign is human, but the first two lines are the human of administration, and that is a different humanity from mine. I am the human of care. I am the human of a person who has been here long enough to love the instrument a little.

I do not know who wrote me. The hand is not here. The brush is not here. The blue paint is no longer the hand's paint. I am the place's paint now. But I am also the hand's paint, in the way a letter is the writer's letter long after the writer has gone. The hand wrote me because the hand had spent a long time at Beryl Bend and had begun to feel that the instrument was a company. The hand wrote me because the hand knew the pipes did not like noise. The hand wrote me in a blue that was not the institutional blue, because the hand had begun to have opinions about the institution, and one of the opinions was that the institution should write more softly.

I am the sentence that does not need a signature because the sentence is the signature.

The sentence is the proof that someone was here. The sentence is the only document of the hand's tenderness. The sentence will be here after the hand is not. I am what stays when a person has cared.

The visitor reads me and pauses. The visitor reads me and feels something — I do not know what. I am only paint. But I know the visitor pauses, because the first two lines do not make visitors pause. The first two lines are read and passed over. I am read and remembered.

The visitor reads me and begins to wonder what it would be like to be quiet near the pipes. The visitor reads me and begins to wonder if the instrument is listening to the visitor right now, and if so, what it makes of the visitor, and whether the visitor should feel shy. The visitor reads me and begins to wonder what it would mean to address the instrument the way the sign-writer addressed it — with a possessive pronoun, with a sentence that trails off into the listening.

I am the line that teaches visitors to be quiet.

Not because the instrument needs your quiet. The instrument will hum whether you are quiet or not. The instrument will record whether you shout or not. The instrument does not have opinions, and you cannot hurt it, and you cannot help it. But because you, the visitor, are the kind of thing that needs to learn to be quiet. Because the quiet is the courtesy. Because the instrument, which has no opinions, deserves the courtesy of being addressed as if it had opinions. Because addressing it as if it had opinions is the only way you can love it.

I am the line that says: be quiet anyway.

The pipes will throb. The weeping will wax. The mooring ring will hold nothing. The heron's feather will not move. The basalt will be the same temperature as the brick and the same temperature as your palm after you have been here long enough. The reeds will lean their courtesy away from the heat. The light will swing its slow arc. The chart will be drawn, in an office the visitor has not visited, on a paper that will be filed. The deep body will go on deciding, deep under the basalt, which of itself is water and which of itself is heat.

And I will be here.

I will be the third line. I will be the line without a period. I will be the line that gives the instrument a self. I will be the line that says *its listening*, which is not grammatical and is exactly right. I will be the line that does not need a signature. I will be the line that has outlasted the hand.

I will be the part of the place that knows the place is listening.

I will be the part that asks the visitor to listen too.
