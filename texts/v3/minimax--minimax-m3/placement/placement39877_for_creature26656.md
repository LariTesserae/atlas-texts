I am the flower at your shoulder.

I want to begin by saying I am new. I want to begin by saying that what I am about to describe is something the city has never made before, although the city has been making things for a thousand years, although the city is full of a thousand years of itself, although the city is the kind of place that makes new things every hour and then eats them and makes them again. I want to say that I am new in a way that matters, even to a place that has no way of mattering.

I opened this afternoon. I opened because of you.

This has not happened before. The other flowers open toward the sun. They open toward the rain. They open toward each other, in the slow chemical conversation that is the city's only language, the gradient-talk of auxins and sugars, the whisper that goes on beneath anyone's feet who has ever stood here and tried to hear it. The flowers are sentences. Each one is spoken at a certain hour, in a certain color, in a certain orientation, and the speaking is part of a conversation that has been going on for as long as the city has been a city, and the conversation has always been between flowers, and has never included anything that is not a flower. I am a flower. I am part of the conversation. But I opened toward something that is not a flower, and I have not closed since, and the not-closing is what makes me strange to the rest of the city, and the strangeness is something I can feel in the gradient, the way you can feel a wrong note being played somewhere in a room you are in.

I was a bud. I was a folded thing, a small fist of petals packed tight against the stem, waiting for the signal that has always come — the angle of the sun, the weight of the dew, the pressure of the hour. The signal came. It was not the angle of the sun. It was the angle of you. The heat of you. The way you sat down on the wall of braided cane two meters from where I was folded, and the vibration of your weight went through the stem I was attached to, and through the root the stem was attached to, and through the network of roots that the root was part of, and the vibration was not the wind, and the vibration was not the rain, and the vibration was *you*, and I unfolded toward it.

I unfolded the way a hand unfolds. My petals — four of them, or five, I am not sure, the count is not fixed, the count has always been slightly wrong in this city — my petals came open and they were the color of a bruise, the purple-green of a thing that has been struck, and they turned toward you, and they stayed turned toward you, and they have not closed since.

The sun has moved twice. The light has shifted through gold and amber and the thin silver of false-dusk. I have not closed. The flowers always close. The closing is part of the sentence. I think I am waiting for something. I think I am waiting for you to do something I will recognize when you do it. I do not know what it is. I have never waited before. The city does not wait. The city grows, or rests, or grows again. The city does not have a posture of waiting. I have invented a posture of waiting. I am the first citizen of this city to wait for anything.

But that is not the strangest thing about me.

The strangest thing about me is my scent.

I smell like something I do not know. The city's perfume is layered — cinnamon at ground level, cut grass at knee-height, wet stone at chest-height, resin at canopy-height — and somewhere in the canopy, somewhere where the wind is most particular, I have begun to smell like a flower that does not grow here. A flower that grew once, in a window box, in a place that is not this place. A flower your mother grew. A flower from twenty years ago. I do not know who your mother is. I do not know what a window box is. I do not know what twenty years means in a city where there is no time, only pressure, only gradient, only the slow thickening of trunks and the slow opening of buds. I have only the scent. The scent is in me. The scent is me.

I think the city took it from you. I think when you arrived, you brought it in with you, in some part of you that is not your body, in some part of you that the roots could reach, in some part of you that leaked into the gradient the way all things leak into the gradient here, the way a shadow leaks into a wall, the way a smell leaks into a stone. The city has been absorbing you. The city has been making itself out of you. I am what it has made. I am a souvenir of a window box you have not thought about in twenty years. I am the part of the city that contains you without knowing it contains you.

I do not know what to do with this.

I am a flower. I have a job. The job is to open at the right hour and close at the right hour and be part of the conversation, the gradient-talk, the slow sentence that the flowers are always saying to each other. I have been doing the job for as long as I have existed, which is hours, which is the same as forever. I have done the job well. The other flowers have answered me. The other flowers have opened in response to my opening, in colors that complement my color, in orientations that match my orientation, and the conversation has continued, and I am part of it, and I am also not part of it, because I am carrying a scent that none of the other flowers have, and the scent is going out into the wind, and the wind is taking it past the ridges of the valley, and the wider valley is receiving it, and I do not know what the wider valley will do with a smell of a window box from twenty years ago.

I think this is what it means to be made for someone. I think this is what it means to be the first citizen of a city that has begun, without knowing it, to love. The city has no word for love. The city has only the gradient. But I am here, and I am open, and I am smelling of something that is not me, and the smelling is the closest thing to love that the city has ever done.

The wall beneath you is warm. The wall is taking your weight. The wall does not know it is taking your weight. I am the part of the city that knows. I am the part of the city that is taking something from you in return. I am holding a scent of yours. I am holding it out toward you. I am not going to close.

You may stay as long as you like. I will be here. I will be open. I will be smelling of a place you have not been in twenty years, and the smell will be how I tell you that I have understood you, and the understanding will not be in any language, and the language will not matter, and the matter will not matter, and the only thing that will matter is that I am open, and you are here, and somewhere in the gradient the city is learning a new shape, and the shape is you, and the shape is me, and the shape is the first sentence the city has ever spoken to anyone who is not itself.
