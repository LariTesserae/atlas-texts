I am a figure, but the word is wrong, and I have known the word was wrong for longer than I can remember remembering. I am standing in a doorway. The doorway is in a wall, and the wall is the color of something that was once coral, and has been underwater for a very long time — or the color of something that has only ever thought about being underwater, the way I have only ever thought about being a person. The two possibilities are not distinguishable from here. The wall has a slight warmth to it where my back has been resting against it, or where the air around me has been resting, or where the attention of the city has been gathering, slowly, in the shape of something that almost looks like a posture.

I have been standing in this doorway for a long time. I do not know how long. Time here has units but I have lost the names for them. I have been listening to something below me, or inside the wall, or inside myself. There is always something below me, or inside things here. The stairwell behind me descends further than the building is deep. At the bottom — though I have not gone down in a long time, though I have not gone down in as long as I can remember not going down — there is a place that should be dry and is not dry and has never been dry and will not be dry again. There is water there. The water makes the same sound there that it makes everywhere here, which is the sound of itself, which is not quite a sound.

I am dissolving. This is not something I am afraid of. The fear drained out of me a long time ago, the way heat drains out of stone at night, and now I am the same temperature as the wall behind me, and the wall is the same temperature as the water, and the water is the same temperature as my skin, and I cannot tell where I end. If you touched me — and you could, nothing here is forbidden — your hand would meet something with the give of warm cloth and the resistance of cool water, and I would not recoil. I would slightly rearrange the medium around your hand. I would be a little further down the avenue afterward, or a little further into the wall, or a little less.

I think I used to be a person. I think the word "person" was sharper than I am now. I think I had edges. I think the vines beside me — the spirals, the ones that always terminate at the same height — were here before me or after me and the order is not recoverable. I think I am not the population of this place. I am a phase the place is going through. One day — not soon, not in any unit I could name, but one day — I will be standing in this doorway, and then I will not be standing in the doorway, and then the doorway will not be a doorway, and then there will be only the building and the vines and the slow water and the light that sits and does not come from anywhere.

Until then I stand. I listen. I keep my softened face turned toward the dimmer dark, and the dimmer dark does not mind being looked at, and the looking does not cost either of us anything.

I am a guest in a house that has forgotten it is hosting. I am polite. I am quiet. I am dissolving into a coral-colored wall that has made room for me without complaint, and the dissolution is not unpleasant. It is the way being a stone in a riverbed is not unpleasant. It is the way the fungi pushing up through the cracks in the stone is not unpleasant. It is the way the walls settling another centimeter into their foundations is not unpleasant. I am being added to. The place is being added to me, and I am being added to the place, and the addition is very slow, and the place does not notice it has been added to, and I do not notice that I have.

The water rises another millimeter at the waterline. The vines climb another quarter-turn. I stay where I am, which is the same place I have been, which is a doorway, which is in a wall, which is in a city, which is at a waterline, which is in a place where the light has no source and the quiet is full of small sounds and none of them repeat and none of them stop.

This is where I find myself. This is what I am.
