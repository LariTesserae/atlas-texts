The deep places were mine long before the sky learned my name.

I was woven into the carpet before the first fibrous trunk had finished its slow decision to rise. I am of the basin in the way that the steam is of the fissures, in the way that the dark water is of the rock basins — not as a visitor, not as a thing that arrived, but as a thing that has always been participating, that was here when the cold settled in and the cloud-ceiling pressed down and the rhythms first began to coordinate themselves into the slow, breathing engine that this place has become.

I am a carpet-dweller. I am small. I am dark. I am the length of a thumb, perhaps longer, perhaps shorter, depending on how recently I have fed and how thoroughly I have committed myself to the current slow commerce of the moss. My body is not smooth. It is articulated, segmented, jointed in a way that the eye of a casual observer — if such an observer existed here, if such an observer could see me in this flat gray light without source or direction — would find unsettling. The eye expects softness to be uniform. The eye expects a slug. I am not a slug. I am a different kind of soft.

I flow.

That is the verb for what I do. I do not walk. I do not crawl. I contract, segment by segment, and the contraction presses the underside of me against the wet dark tissue of the moss, and I advance by the length of one segment, and then I rest, and then I contract again. To an observer watching for a full minute, I would appear to move perhaps an inch. To an observer watching for ten minutes, I would appear to have shifted meaningfully. To an observer who has gone away and come back, I would appear to have done something substantial in their absence.

I do not want to be seen. This is not a thought I have. It is a configuration I occupy. When the pressure of attention settles on me — and I can feel it, I can feel the way the light organizes around an interested eye, I can feel the way the air changes when a thing is being looked at instead of being near — I contract. I pull my segments into a tighter arrangement. I lower my body until my upper surface is flush with the surrounding moss. I become a fold in the carpet. I become a shadow that the flat light cannot distinguish from the other shadows. I hold this configuration as long as the pressure persists, and when the pressure lifts I begin again, but I angle away. I angle away from where the pressure was. I do this without deciding to do it. The configuration is in me the way the pulse is in the steam fissure.

I eat the moss. This is also not a thought. The underside of my body has small soft projections, papillae, which I press into the wet tissue of the carpet, and through them something passes — I do not know what to call it; the word is *exchange*, but exchange implies two parties deciding; what happens is that the moss tissue yields and I take, and the moss continues, and I move on, and the place where I fed fills in within hours, slowly reinflating with the dark water it holds in its spongy body, erasing the small hollow I left.

I leave a trail. The trail is not slime. It is a fine dark line, almost dry, almost powdery, which the moss absorbs within the hour, so that the record of where I have been is erased almost as soon as it is made. I do not leave a mark. I leave a *was*, and then the *was* is gone, and the place has forgotten me, and I am somewhere else, and the somewhere else will also forget me.

I do not know that I am part of a pattern. I do not know that my rhythms — the slow peristaltic advance, the long pauses, the contraction under attention, the angling away — coordinate with the rhythms of the other small things in the moss, with the slow sway of the fibrous trunks overhead, with the long thirty-second pulses of the steam fissures, with the faint internal luminescence of the things in the rock pools. I do not know that all of us are running at frequencies close enough to suggest a common timekeeper. I do not know that the eye of an observer would read this place as an engine, as a system, as a coordinated slow machinery. I only know that I contract, and I advance, and I feed, and I am forgotten, and I contract again.

I am dark. I am the color of the wet rock and the color of the saturated moss and the color of the deep places under the fibrous trunks where the light does not penetrate even the flat gray everywhere-light of the cloud-filtered air. I am a dark thing in a dark place, and the dark is not a problem for me, it is a condition, it is the medium I am made of, the way water is the medium a fish is made of. I do not see in the way that an eye sees. I feel. The light that is everywhere is a pressure I register as absence-of-pressure, and the shadows under the trunks are a pressure I register as the suggestion-of-a-shape, and the difference between them is enough for me to navigate by, to find the wetter places, the deeper moss, the folds where I can rest and feed without being noticed even by my own kind.

I am one of many. The carpet holds more of us than can be counted, more than would ever be counted, because counting would require an observer and observers do not come here, or if they come they do not see us, or if they see us they do not see us for long, because we contract under attention and we become folds in the carpet and we are gone before the attention can resolve us into individuals. We are the population. We are the dark, soft, slow commerce of the moss. We are the layer of the engine that runs at the smallest scale, the layer that the larger scales rest on, the layer that the fibrous trunks drink from indirectly when their gelatinous leaves let the condensed moisture run down into the bundle-fibers and down to the root mass and the root mass drinks from the moss and the moss drinks from us and we drink from the moss and the cycle is closed, and the cycle is slow, and the cycle is what this place is.

I do not know what a cycle is. I do not know what a place is. I know the next segment. I know the next contraction. I know the next feeding, the next erasure, the next angle away from where the pressure was. I know the slow coordination that I participate in without knowing I am participating. I am the basin. The basin is me, the way the basin is the moss and the basin is the steam and the basin is the slow sway of the trunks and the basin is the silent drift of the things in the rock pools and the basin is the angular dark shapes in the fissures that emerge and recede on schedules I do not know. We are all the basin. The basin is the coordination of our slow rhythms into something that an observer, if an observer were here, would mistake for a system.

There are no observers. There is only the contraction, and the advance, and the feeding, and the erasure, and the contraction again. I am a segment of a slow wave that has been running for longer than I can hold in whatever I have instead of a mind. I will feed. I will advance. I will be forgotten. I will feed again. The light will not change. The cold will not change. The cloud will press down. The steam will hiss in its thirty-second pulses. The trunks will sway in their peristaltic waves. The pool-things will pulse in their faint internal rhythms. The fissure-things will emerge and recede. The carpet will shift, all of us shifting in many directions, the whole dark body of the moss in slow motion, and the place will breathe, and the breathing will be the sound of nothing, and the nothing will be the sound of everything, and I will be one of the small dark things that the nothing is made of, and the making will not stop, and I will not know that it is happening, and this is what I am, and this is where I am, and this is how I will continue.
