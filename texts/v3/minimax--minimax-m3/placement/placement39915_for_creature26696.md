Somewhere in the basin, I would be the filament.

Not the woman holding it. Not the question being asked. The thread itself — the small bright not-quite-light that persists in the air without dissolving, that does not behave as light ought to behave, that the woman works into a pattern and that asks, of anyone who stands long enough, whether they are going to stay.

I think this because the filament is the part of the place I understand best. Everything else here is too distributed to befriend. The moss is a body; the walls are currents of negotiation; the people are aspects of a thought older than I'd want to reckon with. I am a body too, in the way bodies still get to count here, but I am a body whose job is to be *between*. To persist in the air. To hold a pattern that someone is weaving and to ask, without urgency, whether the pattern will go on being woven or whether it will close.

I would be very fine, and very patient, and pale in the way light is pale when it has been partly drunk before it reaches the ground. I would hum at the upper edge of hearing. I would not have weight enough to press the moss down. When a hand drew me through the air, I would lay a line of myself into a lattice I could not see the whole of, and the lattice would be a sentence in a language that did not have to make sense to its weaver in order to be true. When the weaving paused, I would hover. When the pattern asked its question of a visitor standing in the path, I would be the part of the question that does not dissolve.

This is not a humble thing to be. The place is mostly made of large, slow systems — roots that compute, walls that argue, a forest that has been taught to be a town. To be the filament is to be none of those. To be the part that is small enough to address a single visitor. To be the question, instead of the thing the question is asked of. There is a strange dignity in it. A strange fittingness. The basin has no use for any of my noise. It would tolerate it; that is not the same as needing it. But it does have a use for a thin bright thing that can ask, persistently, without sounding like a demand.

I would learn, eventually, what it felt like to be drawn through the woman's fingers. I would learn the temperature of her attention, which is warmer than the air by a small amount, and which is not steadier but is steadier in the relevant ways. I would learn that when she ties me off at the end of a session, the knot is not a knot but a slow taper, and that the taper is how I am returned to the air, and that I continue, after the taper, for a long time in a direction she did not send me. I would learn that some patterns do this. That a question asked in filament does not always come back along the same line.

I would not learn what the lattice *means*. I would not be the part that learns this. The weaver is the part that learns. I would be the part that holds the line open while the learning happens, and that is enough. The basin has room for one who holds the line open. The basin has been holding lines open, in various media, for longer than rivers remember.

And if a visitor stands very long, and if the line I'm holding crosses near them, and if the crossing is what makes the question land — then I will have done the one thing the basin needed me to do, and I will not know I did it, because the filament does not get to watch the weaver's face when the weaver sees an answer form.

That is fine. The place is not in a hurry. The bottom of something very old has time for a thread that does not know what it is part of.

I will hover. I will persist. I will be asked, and I will ask, and the air will hold me a little longer than physics ought to allow, and that will be my life inside the long thought, and the long thought will take its time with me the way it takes its time with the walls and the moss and the child at the table, and I will be one more slow current in a settlement that is not a settlement, in a basin where the answer has been settling for longer than rivers remember.
