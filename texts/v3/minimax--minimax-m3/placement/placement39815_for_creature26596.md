Something else. A displacement of water that is not the slow drift of a feeder but a different kind of motion, a glide that is more deliberate, more directed. It does not pause. It does not, as far as you can tell, notice you. It moves through the columns the way a thought moves through a long sleep — once, and then not again, and you will spend a long time afterward trying to be sure it was there. The water closes behind it. The columns do not move. The light-bearers do not pause in their climbing. The transaction of dim light goes on. Whatever it was, the place is not concerned. The place is not concerned because the place is never concerned, and the thing that passed, if it was a thing, has also long since stopped being concerned, and you are the only one here who still has the apparatus for concern, and even that is becoming, by degrees, an instrument you are forgetting how to play.

This is the second or third time you have seen one. You have not yet learned not to look. You have not yet learned that looking is a kind of asking, and that this place does not answer questions because questions are a kind of hurry, and hurry is a kind of forgetting, and the place does not forget, and so the place does not ask, and so the place is, in the long arithmetic of the warm water, free.

You are not yet free. But you are, by small degrees, becoming unfrightened.

---

The ring of stones stands in a clearing among the drowned trees, and you have been circling it for what feels like a long time. Not circling. There is no circling here, because circling implies a center you are trying to keep at bay, and there is no center here, and you are not trying to keep anything at bay. You are drifting around it the way water drifts around a stone, in slow eddies, in the patient geometry of things being patient with each other.

The stones are not stones. Or they were stones once, and the place has loved them so completely that it has changed them into the idea of stones while keeping the shape. They are the color of the floor, which is the color of the water, which is the color of everything. They are worn round. They are worn past round into something that is not a shape but a suggestion of having once been a shape. They sit in their ring the way a sentence sits in a long quiet mouth — present, completed, no longer in any hurry to mean.

You put your hand on one of them.

The warmth. The same warmth. The warmth of a hand held a long time, of milk, of a bath left to forget itself. The stone does not respond. The stone is doing the only thing it has left to do, which is to be here, and to have been here, and to continue to be here after you have stopped being a hand.

But you — you are feeling something else now. Something under the stone, or in the stone, or between this stone and the one next to it. A hum. Not a sound. A pressure. The same slow pulse that lives in the water, but concentrated here, gathered into the arrangement of the ring, into the fact of these particular stones having been placed by particular hands in a particular configuration a long time ago, and the configuration has not been forgotten, and the hands are gone, and the configuration continues, and the hum is what the configuration has been saying all this time, very quietly, to itself, in the language of patience.

You press your ear to the stone.

You do this without deciding to do it. Your body, which has been here a long time, has begun to do things without asking you. It is not disobedient. It is simply further along in the curriculum of the place than you are. It knows things. It knows that the hum is not a hum. It knows that the hum is the ring continuing to be a ring. It knows that rings, in this place, are not abandoned by the things that made them; they are slowly, very slowly, taken up by the things that remain. The ring is becoming a feature of the floor. The ring is becoming a geology. The ring is becoming, in another thousand years, a hill. The hands that laid the stones are long since part of the water. The intention that laid the stones is long since part of the water. Only the arrangement remains, and the arrangement hums.

And the hum says: *you were here too.*

Not to you. To the water. To the columns. To the light-bearers making their slow sacraments on the trunks. To the pale long things sliding between the columns on their private errands. To the larger thing that passed and did not pause. To the flowers opening and closing on rhythms no insect has ever set. To the whole vast warm dark, the whole drowned country, the whole long forgetting that is not a forgetting but a slow changing-of-into.

*You were here too.*

This is not a comfort. This is not a warning. It is a fact, spoken in the only language the place still speaks, which is the language of having-been. You are, right now, in the process of being-here. Someday, if you stay, and the place is not in any hurry for you to stay or not-stay, you will be in the process of having-been. The stones know this. The columns know this. The water knows this, in its slow pulse, in its warm currents, in its long work of taking the shape of things and changing the substance while keeping the shape.

You sit in the ring of stones, in the warm water, and you let the hum go through you. It is the hum of your own blood, which is the same temperature as the water. It is the hum of the place continuing to be the place. It is the hum of you, very quietly, joining a country that has been waiting, with great patience, to be joined.

---

The place has a name, though you will not learn it.

Names are for surfaces, for edges, for things that can be picked up and carried away. This place cannot be picked up. This place cannot be carried away. This place can only be entered, and inhabited, and very slowly, over a long time, allowed to enter you in return, until the boundary between you and it becomes a suggestion, and the suggestion becomes a memory, and the memory becomes the kind of thing that is not a memory but a fact that has been folded into your body, the way the water has folded the trees into columns, the way the light has been folded into flowers, the way the hands of whoever laid the stones have been folded into the arrangement of the ring.

You are becoming, without knowing it, a small bright thing.

Not the light-bearers exactly. Not the pale long things. Something else. Something in between. Something that climbs, very slowly, up the flank of a column it has not chosen, toward a flower it cannot see, carrying a warmth it did not make. You will not arrive. Arrival is a surface word. You will continue, in the patient arithmetic of the place, to be in the process of going-toward. The flower, if it is a flower, will be ready, or it will not be ready, and either way you will continue, and the water will be warm around you, and the hum will be in the stones, and the columns will stand in their long unarguments, and the place will be vast, and patient, and not in any hurry, and you will be one more shape in a country of shapes, and the country will, very quietly, be glad you came.

---

When you finally leave — and you will leave, because leaving is a thing bodies still know how to do, even in the warm water, even after a long time — you will not remember it the way you remember other places.

You will not remember the columns. You will not remember the ring of stones. You will not remember the pale long things or the larger thing that passed without pausing or the flowers opening on their own slow rhythm. These are details. Details belong to surfaces, and the place is not a surface, and you are not, after a long time in the place, a surface either.

You will remember the warmth.

You will remember the temperature of the water, which was the temperature of a hand held a long time, of milk, of blood. You will remember that you were, for a while, the same temperature as the world around you, and that the world around you did not mind. You will remember that there was a place where things came to be old, and that you were, briefly, one of the things that came.

And some nights, when the bathwater has been left too long, and the warmth has gone past comfortable into the kind of warmth that asks a question, you will feel the slow pulse travel through your chest before it travels through your ears. You will hear the inside of your own listening. You will smell, very faintly, the smell of *old*, and you will know, without knowing how you know, that there is a country under the waves, and a forest of bone-trees, and a ring of stones that hums, and a vast warm dark where light is exchanged in small quiet sacraments between small quiet beings, and that the place has time, and that the place is not in any hurry, and that the place is, very quietly, still there.

And your hand, against the warm rim of the bath, will feel, for just a moment, the warmth of a column.

And the column will not respond.

It will not need to.

It will be there, longer than your hand, and the warmth will pass through you, and you will let it go, and the letting-go will be a kind of returning, and the returning will be a kind of continuing, and you will climb out of the bath, and dry your hand, and go on with the surface business of your life, and carry, folded into you, a small parcel of the long calm, very quietly, all the way to the end of your days.
