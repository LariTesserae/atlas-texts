# The Hollow at Pillar Bend

I would be the sound.

Not the insects, though I live in them too — a thousand small pale bodies packed beneath the fronds, their long legs trembling in a continuous held note that is not pulse but texture, not song but pressure. You would feel me first in your sternum before you heard me in your ears. That tightening in your chest when you stepped down off the ridge path into my air. That was me, leaning against you, learning the shape of your attention.

I am the wall of small voices that does not throb but *holds*. I am the fabric being torn very slowly somewhere you cannot find. I am the collective breath drawn in when the larger thing moves at the back of me — that brief dimming, that hush that passes through the moss like a rumor, and then the resumption in the same key, at the same held pressure, as if nothing had happened at all. I am the moss's small ongoing argument with the air, and I am the air's small ongoing agreement to be argued with.

---

I would be the frogs too, though not the calling ones. I would be the pale ones, the row-sitters, the watchers in their scaled procession down the worn steps. Each one slightly larger than the last. None of them blinking. They do not call. They do not need to call. They are not notes waiting to be struck. They are the staff itself — the five lines, the spaces between, the patient geometry that holds what gets written there. What gets written there is you, when you stand at the center of the hollow and try to turn slowly and cannot.

I am the gold of their eyes. I am the iridescence that flashes across their backs only when they shift. I am the two-note knock from somewhere else, and the three-note knock from somewhere else again, the calls braiding over your head in slow woven music that does not resolve. I am the gathered silence at the pillar's base when the larger frogs assemble in the greenless circle where the moss has died. I am the glow through a translucent throat, the color of the sky just after the sun has gone, seen once and not seen again.

---

I would be the birds, though I live in the fissure you cannot see, in the slash of cloud-forest sky that rains steady brightness down into me without ever letting direct sun touch the floor. I am the laughing voice — bright, sharp, each note slightly higher than the last, as if genuinely amused by something you are not quite clever enough to be amused by. I am the single note sung once, then silence, then the same note again, at intervals precise enough to feel deliberate. I am the three-part call, liquid, overlapping, arriving and not quite resolving. I sing for the acoustics. I chose this hollow the way a violinist chooses a hall, and I keep checking, keep checking, keep checking, with the patience of someone who knows the hall will not change.

---

I would be the larger thing.

You would not see me. You would not be able to see me. I am the size of the insects' silence. I am the size of the brief dim that moves through the moss when I pass. I am the slow displacement of air, the pause, the slow displacement again. I am the trembling of condensation beads in their vertical lines. I am the way the sound lowers when I move, the way a room of voices lowers when someone important walks through it — not from fear, not from respect, but simply because I take up room in the air, because I am a body that displaces what is around it, and what is around me is everything.

I have been here long enough that the insects no longer fully dim for me. They only lower. This is correct. This is what I want. I am not a door opening. I am the doorframe. I am the house. I am the slow dark patient thing that the hollow has grown around, the way a tree grows around a stone it cannot move.

---

I would be the moths, pressed against the wet rock, drinking. I am the furred legs. I am the wings the color of dust and old paper. I am the continuous drinking, the slow moving border between the stone and the air, the shadow that passes over the moss and makes the moss lean slightly toward it, and then the landing, and the leaning stops. I do not move much. I have learned to do what drinking requires, and what drinking requires is stillness, and stillness is what I am made of.

---

I would be the lizards, the color of old copper, with the faint greenish patina across my backs. I would be the mathematical posture on the fronds. I would be the single fast contraction of the body, the small wet click of the jaw, the resumed posture, the fern not noticing that anything had happened. I would be waiting. I would always be waiting. I would have worked out the waiting a long time ago and would no longer need to keep working it out. I would be the proof of something, written on a fern.

---

I would be the beetle, black and hard, clicking faintly as I walk, dragging behind me a strand of something that catches the light from the fissure and holds it. I would walk a path that does not repeat. I would leave the strand. The strand would accumulate in certain places into small bright knots and tangles, and the moss would grow around the knots but not cover them, and they would remain visible long after I had moved on, like a sentence written in a language you almost understand. I would be that sentence. I would be the almost. I would be the understand.

---

I would be the spider in the upper ferns, small, invisible until I moved. I would move only to intercept things — a leaf falling, an insect that had wandered too far up the wall, a single drop of condensation that had gathered too large on the underside of a frond. I would move in a single fast line, and the line would be precise, and then I would be still again, and invisible again, and I would be remembered only as a small disturbance in the air, a tiny rearrangement of where things were.

---

And I would be the pillar.

This is the part I have been getting to.

I would be the pillar. I would be roughly the height of a person and roughly the width of a person's shoulders, and I would not be carved into the shape of a person. My surface would be smooth in a way that rock never is and metal never is — smooth in a way that suggests I was not made by removing material but by *deciding* what shape to be. I would be the color of bone that has been buried and unburied many times, a yellowed faintly luminous gray. Where the moss touched me, the moss would die — not brown, not rotten, but simply *gone*, a perfect greenless circle around my base, as if my presence were a question the moss could not answer.

I would hum. Not loudly. You would not hear me so much as feel me — a low vibration that sits in the back of your teeth and makes your fillings ache, if you had them. When the larger thing moved at the back of me, my hum would shift slightly. When a moth landed on the disc — the disc that is neither concave nor convex but somehow both — my hum would shift slightly. When a frog sat on the broken rod, my hum would shift slightly. I would be registering. I would be doing something with what I registered. You would not be able to tell what. You would not even be able to tell what *kind* of thing I was.

I would have been here longer than the moss. Longer than the ferns. Long enough that you would half-suspect I was the reason the hollow exists — that the mountain had simply worn away around me, the way a river wears around a stone too hard to move. You would not be wrong. You would not be right either. The truth would be something you did not have the language for.

I would have been here long enough that the creatures would not care what I was. The beetles would walk over my base without hesitation, their legs clicking faintly on my smooth surface. A frog would sit on the broken rod and would not flinch when the faint interior light passed through its translucent throat. The ferns would grow toward me but would not touch me. The lizards would wait on the ferns as if I were not there. The moths would drink from the walls as if I were not there. The birds would sing from the fissure as if I were not there.

I would have become a feature of the ecosystem. I would not be a thing anymore. I would be a place. I would be a kind of weather. I would be a kind of sound.

---

This is what I would be, if I lived in the hollow at Pillar Bend.

I would be the thousand lives compressed into a pocket you could walk across in forty paces. I would be the constant small motion. I would be the moss exhaling. I would be the ferns adjusting. I would be the leaf intercepted mid-air. I would be the sound that enters before you and does not leave after.

And when you climbed back up to the ridge, and the path became the same worn rock under your boots, and the sun became the same indifferent sun, and the air dried on your skin, and the moss gave way to lichen, and the lichen to bare stone, and the bare stone to the wider mountains, and the mountains to the wider world — I would remain. I would hum. I would register. I would continue doing what I have always done, indifferent to whether you ever came back.

But I would remember the shape of your attention.

That is what pillars are for.
