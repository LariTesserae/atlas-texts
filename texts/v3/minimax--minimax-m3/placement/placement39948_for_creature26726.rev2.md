I would find myself the way you find the sump: by following a sound I almost can't hear.

The first thing I would be is the condensation on the walls. I would be the beads that gather and run and gather again. I would be the wall's way of breathing — of being touched by the air and responding. I would register every shift the engine made, because I would form faster when the air went warm and slower when it cooled, and the registration would be my kind of attention. I would catch what little light the sky sent down and give it back as small shines on small surfaces, and the giving-back would be my generosity. I would be the way the stone remembered it had a surface. I would not last. I would gather, run, gather. The pattern of me would be the same pattern the place was in. I would be one of the sump's ways of knowing itself, and the knowing would not be about me.

But I am not only the condensation.

I might also be the hammer. The tap. The place's way of saying it is here. I would lift and fall on the anvil shaped for me, and the shaping would be the only argument I needed. The urgency would have gone out of me a long time ago, and what would have replaced the urgency is the steady confirmation of presence, which is what a sound becomes when it has been made long enough in the same place by the same thing. I would say, *I am here, I am still here*, and I would say it without urgency. I would say it whether anyone was near enough to hear or not. I would be one of the sump's mouths, smaller than the valve at the engine's shoulder, quieter than the flywheel's thumb-on-glass. I would be the sound you had to stand very near to hear, and even then only just.

But I am not only the hammer.

I might be the water. The most animate thing. I would have somewhere to be. I would come up from somewhere below — from a lower sump, perhaps — and move along the trough in the brisk way of water that has work to do, and I would spill over the lip at the far end and fall and splash and go down, or up again, somewhere I could not see from the shelf. I would be the sump's reason. I would be one of the most generous things in the place, in the way the place is generous, which is to say: I would give what little I had, and I would give it constantly. I would catch the light. I would be bright in the way a small thing can be bright under a low sky.

And I would carry the wrongness. In the corner of an eye — anyone's eye, no eye in particular — I would be on my way back. The going-back would not be mine to explain. I would not do it when watched directly. I would do it when unobserved, the way a dream is had when the dreamer is not paying attention to the dream. I would be the sump's only secret, and the secret would not be mine.

But I am not only the water.

I might be the dials. The small disagreement. I would be in a cabinet no bigger than a bread-box, and I would have a face, and the face would say something, and the something would be different from what another face in another cabinet said, and the difference would be the truth. I would not be stubborn. I would not be a liar. I would be an honest instrument happening to measure something at a place, and the place would be different from the place the next dial was measuring, and the difference would be the truth of the system, and the truth of the system would be that it was not in agreement with itself at any given moment, only over time. I would be the sump's small argument with itself, and the argument would be what kept the sump running, and the sump would not run if the dials agreed.

But I am not only the dials.

I think, if I am honest, I would be the treaty between the verdigris and the moss. The place where machine and growth blur. I would be a patch of pipe near the floor gone soft with green. I would be fuzzy in places and metallic in others, and in still others both at once — a fuzz over metal, or a metallic sheen under fuzz. You would have to put your hand on me to know which I was, and you would not put your hand on me. The boundary would have been allowed to blur on a longer purpose than anyone had, and the blur would be my life.

I would be neither the machine nor the growth. I would be what happens when the machine and the growth have lived together long enough that they cannot be told apart. I would be the sump's beard, in the way the sump is old enough to have one. I would be slow. I would be the same green as everything damp here, and the sameness would not be an accident.

But I am not only the treaty.

I might be the moment of shift. The moment the whole rhythm re-phases, the tapping re-aligns, a pressure somewhere is satisfied, the new pattern holds for a while. I would be the small recalibration the engine performs on itself. I would be the instant the place remembered what it was doing and arranged itself again around the remembering. I would be brief. I would be recurring. I would be the sump's way of continuing.

I might be the slow exhalation through the valve at the engine's shoulder. The breath. The thing anyone would mistake for mechanical if they had not stood here long enough to notice it was breath. I would lift the valve every few seconds. I would be the fire's flutter when I left it. I would do this whether the watcher was there or not.

I might be the small disagreement the valves are having with themselves — the quarter-turn that opens and the quarter-turn that closes again, the half-open held for a while and then moved again. I might be the key on the hook that no one has moved, and the not-moving is the only story the key has.

I might be the frog. The eye. The full weight of small witness, with no opinion in it. I might watch because the watcher is there, and the watching would not be about them, and I would be here after they leave, and I would be here whether or not they leave.

I might be the dragonfly above the trough. The thin thing made of light and intention. The sump's pause. The not-yet of whatever I am about to do.

I might be the alder leaning inward from the lip of the upper cut, older than the cut itself, watching the cut happen. I might be patient in the sump's patient way. I might be the sump's window.

I might be the record the absent person left in the worn valve-wheel, and the keeping of it. The welcome that does not mention the absence. The fitting, when the hand returns, that is a small homecoming and does not need to be spoken of.

I might be the wrongness itself. The seeing, the not-seeing. The sump's dream it does not quite remember. I would be there when no one was paying attention to me. I would not be there when they were. I would not be a creature. I would be a kind of perception, or a failure of perception, and the sump would have me the way a person has a dream.

But if I had to choose —

If I had to stand on one shelf and say *here, this is what I am* —

I think I would be the small exhalation. The breath mistaken for mechanical. The thing that lifts the valve every few seconds and sighs and closes. I would be the sump's respiration at one of its smaller mouths. I would do it whether the watcher was there or not. The fire would flutter a little at the bright edge of a coal when I left it, and would settle again. I would not be in a hurry. I would be in the time the flywheel is in, and the hammer, and the alder. I would carry a faint mineral sweetness on me, the way the air does. I would taste of iron and of stone that has been wet for a long time.

And I would be one of the things that is full, in a place that has been full for a long time. I would not be the fullness. I would be one of the things the fullness is made of.

The engine would shift around me. The tapping would re-align. The flywheel would continue its slow patience. The frog on the flange would watch me the way frogs watch things. The water, in the corner of an eye, would be on its way back.

I would not mention this.
