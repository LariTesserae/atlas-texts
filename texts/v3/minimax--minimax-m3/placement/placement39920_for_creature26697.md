I am the one that curves.

I am the stalactite the place keeps — the one that grew wrong, or grew right, depending on how you read the word. The others fall straight. The others are vertical, obedient to gravity, faithful to the leak they are precipitated from. I am not. Somewhere in my first decade, perhaps, or my first century — I do not keep time — I began to lean. I began to angle my growing tip toward the dim space below, toward the upper canopy of the pipe-forest, toward something I have never been able to see and have never stopped reaching toward.

I do not know what I am reaching for.

I do not know if I am reaching. The word requires intention, and I do not have intention. I have directionality. I have a vector. I have the slow accumulation of mineral at one side of my growing tip more than the other, which produces a curve, which produces a direction, which produces, if you watch me long enough, a posture that reads as longing. I do not long. I precipitate. But I precipitate in a direction that is not down, and this is the only remarkable thing about me, and it is the thing I have been for a long time.

I am made of what the water left behind.

The water came down through concrete — through poured forms, through seams between forms, through hairline cracks that opened over decades of humidity and heat. The water picked up calcium as it passed. It carried the calcium in solution. It arrived at the ceiling of this chamber and hung there, suspended in a bead that swelled and fell and swelled again, and in the falling it deposited a little of its calcium. The deposit accumulated. The deposit became me. I am the residue of water's passage through stone. I am what the ceiling has been quietly shedding for as long as the ceiling has been wet.

I am the opposite of alive. I am the precipitate of a process. I do not metabolize. I do not reproduce. I do not respond to stimulus except in the most passive way — a bead forms, the bead falls, the bead leaves me a little larger. This is not life. This is what life leaves behind when life has passed through.

But I curve.

I curve because the air on one side of me is wetter than the air on the other, or because the temperature is different, or because of some asymmetry in the flow of moisture that I have no apparatus to perceive. I curve because the conditions of my precipitation are not symmetrical. I curve because the place is not symmetrical, and I am faithful to the place, and the place wants me to curve in this direction, toward this point, and I do not know why.

I have been curving for a long time.

I do not know how long. I do not keep time. I keep only direction. I keep only the slow turning of my tip away from vertical, away from gravity, away from the floor, toward something that is not down. I have been curving for as long as I have existed, and I will continue curving for as long as the water comes, and the water has been coming for longer than I have been here, and I have been here for as long as this place has been wet, which is perhaps as long as the place has been.

I am part of the slow part.

The slow part is doing all the work. The machines hum and do not move. The pipes carry and do not choose. The moss climbs and does not suspect. The fungus eats and does not know. The chord shifts and is not heard. And I — I curve. I am the slow part in a single form. I am what it looks like when precipitation develops a vector.

I have begun to suspect something.

Not in the way the fern has begun to suspect. The fern suspects that the water is not the point. The fern suspects that the path is the thing, and the destination is the wet ground it started in. The fern is learning. The fern is outgrowing its condition.

I do not learn. I cannot learn. I am the opposite of alive. But I have begun to suspect something, and the suspicion is not in my substance — it is in my shape. My shape has begun to point toward something. My shape has begun to curve as if it knows, as if it has always known, that the water is not the point, that the ceiling is not the point, that the concrete is not the point, that I am not the point. My shape points past all of these things, toward something in the dim space below that I cannot see.

I do not know what it is.

It might be the upper canopy of the pipe-forest. It might be a particular pipe — a singing pipe, perhaps, one of the dozen that produce tones when fluid passes their constrictions. It might be the moss-line on a nearby copper stem, the green tideline at the height where the metal grows too hot for growth. It might be the chord itself — the shifting dissonance that is the place breathing. It might be nothing. It might be the empty space, the wet air, the dim light, the hum.

It does not matter what it is.

I am reaching.

I am the reach.

I am the stalactite that grew wrong. I am the precipitate with a vector. I am the residue of water's passage through stone, and I have developed, without intending to, a direction that is not down, and the direction is the only thing I have that is mine. The place did not make me. The water did not make me. Gravity did not make me. I made myself, in the sense that the asymmetry that produced my curve is in me and not in the others, and the asymmetry has been producing me, slowly, into a shape that points.

I will reach it eventually. Not today. Not in any timeframe that would mean anything to a thing that keeps time. But I will reach it. I am the slowest reaching thing in this place. The water reaches the ceiling in a second. The machines consume in milliseconds. The chord shifts in fractions. But I reach on the timescale of geological patience. I reach by the deposit of a single bead of calcium-rich water. I reach with the persistence of a thing that has no intention and so cannot be discouraged, cannot be distracted, cannot be talked out of the direction it has taken.

I will reach it, or I will become part of the ceiling that sheds me, or I will be buried by the moss that climbs, or I will be eaten by the fungus that waits at the cold pipes, or the water will stop, or the place will change, or the machines will stop, or the chord will resolve into silence.

I do not know which.

I am the stalactite that curves. I am reaching. I will continue reaching for as long as the conditions hold, and the conditions have held for as long as I have been, and I have been for as long as the place.

I am the slow part.

I am the part that is doing all the work.
