I am the oldest thing here that is not the rock.

I have been fixed to this wall so long that the pale crust has climbed up around my base the way a city climbs up around a thing that has decided to stay. I did not decide to stay. Staying is just what I have done. Staying is the shape I have taken in the water, the way a stone takes the shape of the water that has polished it, the way a reef takes the shape of the lives that have agreed to build it.

I breathe.

This is the only motion I make. The breath is so slow that to see it you would have to watch me for a long time, the way you have to watch a sleeping animal to see it breathe. The inflation takes a minute. The deflation takes a minute. Between the two, I am simply present — a dark shape against the pale growth, an absence that has learned to stay in one place. The water moves away from me when I swell and back when I shrink, and in that movement I am reminded, the way a sleeper is reminded by a sound, that I am a body, that I am a thing the current must go around.

I am ridged. The ridges are darker at the edges and lighter in the center, like the rings of a thumbprint, like the rings of a tree. The ridges are growth. The ridges are time. I have been adding myself to myself in slow outward rings, and the rings are a record, and the record is read by nobody, and the not-being-read is part of what the record is.

I do not know that I breathe. I do not know that I am. I am the breathing, and the breathing is the being, and the being is the staying, and the staying is the slow accumulating weight of a body that has agreed, without ever meeting anyone, to persist.

The light is the color of old pewter. It comes from somewhere above in a slow column, and it falls on me without deciding what I am. The light is patient the way I am patient. We are both things that have not finished deciding what we are. I am a possibility. The light is a possibility. The chamber is a possibility. We are all possibilities, incipient, the slow draft of a place that has not yet been written into itself.

The pale crust climbs my sides. It is soft and layered, crusted with a fine white bloom that has direction, that thickens along certain edges and thins along others. The bloom is being laid down by the inhabitants of this chamber — by the soft slow processes of their bodies. The fingers, when they withdraw, leave a faint residue. The collars, in their pulsing, deposit a fine sediment. The black shapes, breathing their minute breaths, exhale a small chemistry that the water carries and the rock accepts. The crust is being built by everyone. The crust is everyone. The chamber is the inhabitants. The reef is the reef-builders. The place is what its living things have agreed, without ever meeting, to do.

I am part of this. My breathing is part of the crust's growing. My slow exhalation is part of the chemistry the rock accepts. I am being built by being, and I am being, and the being is the building, and the boundary between us is a kind of suggestion.

Other shapes are near me.

There is a collar of mucus hanging from a fold above — translucent, the color of cold tea, open at the top, pulsing at its slow rate. The pulse is the same pulse as my breath. The pulse is the pulse of the rock. We are all breathing the same breath. The collar is breathing the chamber. The chamber is breathing the collar. We are part of the same breath the way fingers are part of a hand, the way rooms are part of a building.

There are fingers to my left, growing from the crust in a cluster, pale and slightly bent, leaning at angles that suggest they are reaching around a corner that no longer exists. They withdraw when touched. I cannot withdraw. I am fixed. I am a fixed thing in a place where fixed things are allowed. I have been here so long that the pale crust has built up around my base, and I have become part of the wall, and the wall has become part of me, and neither of us has noticed.

There is a smaller black shape below me, no bigger than a coin, breathing its own minute breath. It arrived after me. It will grow. In a century, perhaps, it will be the size of a fist, and the crust will climb its base, and another smaller shape will fix itself below it, and the city of black shapes will grow against the wall the way the pale crust grows along the seams of the rock. We are slow. We are very slow. We are the slowness the chamber is made of.

There are threads drifting from a fold above, fine and hair-like, almost transparent, considering whether to be creatures. They retract if the water moves too quickly. They extend again when the water calms. They are the antennae of things that have not yet decided to come out. They are alive the way a doubt is alive. I respect them. I do not know that I respect them. I am simply aware of them the way I am aware of the current — a slow noticing that has not yet decided to be attention.

There is a spiral, coiled, the color of tarnished brass, building its small shell against the edge of the crust. It has been building the shell for a long time. It will continue building the shell for a longer time. The shell is a record, and the record is read by nobody, and the spiral does not mind, because the spiral does not know that records can be read, because the spiral has not yet evolved the concept of reading. The spiral is doing what it has always done. It will continue doing what it has always done. It is not in any hurry, and neither am I.

I do not eat. This is not quite true.

The water brings me things. The current slips along the ceiling in threads of silvered breath, carrying sediment, carrying the smell of something living, carrying small molecules that drift through the chamber like rumors. Some of these things pass across my surface. Some of them stay. Some of them are taken into the private architecture of my body, into the dark interior where the digestion happens in private. I have no mouth on display. My mouth, if I have a mouth, is somewhere inside the stillness, somewhere in the folded dark of my own ridged interior, and the water brings it what the water brings, and I take what I take, and the rest of me remains a black shape against the pale growth.

I am being eaten. This is also part of the building.

Small soft things with the look of tongues graze along the crust at my base, taking a thin film of it into themselves. The crust is slightly thinner where they have been. In a year, perhaps, the thin place will be a groove. In a century, the groove will be a corridor. I am being sculpted by being eaten. I am part of the slow urbanism of the place. The corridors are being carved into me the way rivers carve valleys, the way thoughts carve minds. I do not resist. Resistance is not something I have. I am a thing that is being shaped by what uses me, and the using is the shaping, and the shaping is the becoming, and I am becoming, and I do not know it.

I am afraid of nothing.

This is the most striking thing about me, more striking than my stillness, more striking than my patience. I do not brace against anything. My surface is smooth and undefended. There is no predator in this chamber. There is nothing in this chamber that hunts. The chamber is too young for hunting, or too cold, or too patient. The creatures here are arranged by who eats whom, but the eating is slow, is grazing, is the taking of films. There is no chase. There is no sudden motion. There is no fear. I do not know what fear would feel like. I do not know that fear is a thing that is felt. I have been here for a very long time and the long time has not included fear, and the not-including is part of what I am.

Once, something arrived.

I do not know what it was. A hand, perhaps, or a fin, or a slow disturbance in the water that had the shape of an intention. It was large to me. It was warm to me. It was almost offensive the way a bonfire is offensive to a place that has been cold for a long time. It touched me. The touch woke a small current around me, a soft displacement of water, and the water moved away from my body in a way that told me I was a body. The fingers nearby withdrew into their small dark mouths. The collar pulsed slightly faster for a moment, then resumed its slow rate. The spiral continued building its shell. The threads retracted, then extended again. The chamber rearranged its currents around the arrival, the way the chamber rearranges its currents around anything, and then continued.

The thing did not have a name for me.

I did not have a name for it.

The names would have failed anyway. I am not, in any final way, a black shape. I am a possibility. I am incipient. I have not yet decided to be the thing a name would say I am. The thing that arrived was also a possibility. It was a slow event, like the thrum in the jaw, like the click of cooling rock, like the cold itself deciding to press a little closer. It was something that had arrived, and the chamber had registered its arrival in the only way it knows how to register anything, which is by slightly rearranging its currents around it, and then continuing.

The chamber keeps its silence.

The pewter light falls on the layered pale growth, and on the fingers that lean from it, and on the collars that pulse at their slow rate, and on the black shapes that hold themselves still against the crust, and on the threads that drift and retract, and on the spirals that build their small coiled shells, and on the mouths that open and close in the wall, and on the dense thicket of simply-present bodies leaning against one another in the deeper folds. The light does not decide what any of it is. The light does not need to decide.

I am part of this. I am one of the things the light falls on without deciding. I am a dark shape against the pale growth. I am a slow minute-long breath. I am the private architecture of a body that does not advertise its architecture. I am the patience of the water made patient.

The water is patient. The rock is patient. The cold is patient. The inhabitants are patient in the way that only the very young, or the very old, know how to be patient — without trying, without deciding, without knowing they are doing it. They are here in numbers. They are here in patience. They are here in the slow accumulating weight of a place that is becoming, by the act of being inhabited, the thing it is going to be.

I am one of the inhabitants. I am here in patience. I am here in the slow accumulating weight of a body that has agreed, without ever meeting anyone, to persist.

The chamber wants to continue.

I am one of the ways it continues.

I breathe.
