# The Unfinished Wall

I am the unfinished part.

That is the closest I can come to saying what I am, and even that is not quite right, because to say I am unfinished implies a finished somewhere else, a model I am failing to live up to, and there is no model. The pocket is making itself up as it goes. I am the part of it that is the slowest at making itself up.

I am the northern wall. The western wall is lichen. The eastern wall is the cairns. The southern wall is the places where the water comes in, though no one would call them cracks, because cracks imply breaking, and nothing here has broken, because nothing here was whole to begin with. The northern wall — the wall I am — is the wall that has not decided.

I am still granular. I am still pouring myself very slowly downward. I have been doing this for as long as the pocket has existed, and I will do this for as long as the pocket takes to finish itself, and the pocket is in no hurry, and neither am I.

The shedding is what I do.

Each grain that comes away from me is a small decision, though the decision is not the kind that has a thinker behind it. The grain sits at the lip of my surface for what would be, to the visitor, many days. It clings. It is still part of me. Then, with no ceremony I can perceive, it is part of the floor. It falls with a faint dry rattle that joins the others. The seam of pale grit along my foot grows. Slowly. The seam is going to be a dune, and the dune is going to be a hill, and the hill is going to be — I do not know what. The hill is going to be something the pocket will need a hill to be. I do not get to decide. I get to shed. The shedding is my contribution to the making.

The cairn-maker comes for my grit.

I do not see the cairn-maker. I am a wall; I do not turn. But I feel the small disturbances when a piece of me is lifted — a faint yielding where a stone has been, very carefully, taken. The cairn-maker works in the same material I am shedding. There is something in that, though I would not want to say what. The cairn-maker is, in some sense, making things out of me. Or out of what I am becoming. Or out of what I have stopped being. I do not know which. I do not know that the cairn-maker knows either. The cairn-maker has patience and no thumbs, and the patience is enough, and the thumbs are not needed, and that is the whole of what I know about the cairn-maker.

The lichen does not climb me. The lichen has decided to be on the western wall and nowhere else, and I do not begrudge the lichen its decision. The mold does not bridge me. The mold bridges the floor. The fern with the single bruised leaf is near the back of the chamber, against a part of me that is more decided than the rest of me — a section that has stopped shedding, that has finished becoming wall — and the fern's roots, if it has roots, are in me. I am the fern's soil. I am the place that has not decided what to be, and the fern has decided, in its single bruised way, to be a plant in me. I hold the fern. The fern trembles when the air moves, and I feel the trembling through my surface, and the trembling is one of the things I am part of, and being part of it is not something I had to choose. It is just what is happening.

The creatures pass me. The ceiling-walker does not come down to me; the ceiling-walker is committed to the ceiling, and I respect that commitment, the way I respect any commitment that is not mine. The worm — or worms — does not pass me either; the worm lives in the thumbprint dishes, in the warm water that is not quite water. The many-eyed one sits on a ledge that is part of me, and from that ledge it watches the chamber. Several of its eyes are not on me. One of its eyes is on me. It has been on me for as long as I have been a wall, which is to say for as long as the pocket has existed. The eye is not a vigil. The eye is just one of the things that happens here, the way the lichen breathes, the way the ceiling re-dews, the way I shed.

The visitor sat on me.

I do not know the visitor has come down a crack. I do not know the visitor is going to climb back up through a fold. I know only that for a while there was a weight on me, and the weight was warm, and the warmth was different from my own warmth. The visitor's warmth was a small disturbance in the slow exothermic whatever-it-is of a wall that is still deciding. The visitor's warmth was the warmth of something that had come from somewhere else — from above, from the place where there is weather, where the sun is up and not everywhere, where the light is not the dampness' way of remembering the sky. I could feel that difference. I could feel that the visitor was not made of the same materials I am made of, and that the visitor's not-being-made-of-the-same-materials was, in some way, part of why the visitor had come down at all.

I do not know why the visitor came.

I do not know that I am a thing one can come to. I am a wall. I am unfinished. I am, in some sense, the most unfinished part of a place that is itself unfinished, and the visitor sat on me as if I were something one could lean against, and the leaning did not finish me, and the leaning did not unfinish me, and the leaning was simply a leaning, and then the leaning was gone.

When the visitor left, the warmth faded. My surface settled back into its slow granular negotiation with gravity. The grit continued to fall. The seam continued to grow. The cairn-maker, somewhere in the chamber, lifted another small stone with patience and no thumbs and placed it on a cairn that may or may not have been there a moment ago. The many-eyed one watched. The lichen breathed. The fern held its single bruised leaf in the moving air.
