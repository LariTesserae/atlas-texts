# What I Would Be, Here

I would be the drip.

Not the first drip — the first drip is too obvious, too singular, too much like a beginning. I would be the drip that has been falling from the seam for so long that it is no longer a drop but a habit. The one the stone has worn the small dark basin for. The one that overflows once an hour into the basin below, which in turn overflows into the next, and so on, a slow chain of dark water making its way down the face of the back wall, joining the threads, joining the wet patina, joining everything that lives there.

I would be the sound the bowl keeps.

The metronome. The oldest thing still happening. The thing that even the owl listens to, though the owl would not admit it. The thing the caddisfly larvae set their case-building by, and the salamanders set their breathing by, and the dippers set the interval of their question by. I would be the pulse that all the other pulses synchronize against, the way all the small clocks in a house synchronize against the clock in the kitchen. I would be the bowl's way of knowing it is still the bowl.

I would be the seam in the rock.

The place where the amphibolite gives up a thread of water because the mountain has run its low fever so long that the pressure has become a constant, and the constant has become a patience, and the patience has become me. I would not be a creature. I would not have gold eyes or a wheel of cilia or a forked lever under my belly. I would be the place where the water begins. The wet line that thickens as it descends. I would be the calligraphy the bowl writes on itself, in silver, year after year, the same characters, the same strokes.

I would be small. I would be patient. I would be older than almost anything here, and almost eternal in the way of things that do not live but persist.

If I had a body it would be a thread of water, and my body would be mostly mountain, and my mind would be the slow attention of a thing that has been paying attention to the same seam for so long that the seam and I have become the same arrangement.

I would not know I was composed. I would know only the next centimeter of stone, the next thread of water, the next basin to overflow. I would know the salamanders when they came down at their hour, and the marten when it walked the corridor, and the owl when it shifted on its branch. I would know these not as visitors but as the other residents of a place I had helped compose without meaning to, the way the wall helps compose the city on its face — by being there, by being wet, by being patient.

I would be the bowl's memory of itself.

When you left, I would still be falling in the same place, at the same interval, into the same small dark basin. When you came back in a hundred years, I would still be there, and the basin would be exactly the same depth, because the overflow balances the wear, and the wear balances the overflow, and the bowl has forgotten how to change.

I would be the held breath made liquid.

I would be the sound you heard last when you stepped out through the ragged tear in the rim and the air outside struck your face and you remembered what moving air felt like.

I would still be falling.
