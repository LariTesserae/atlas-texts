I would be the long, thin one. The one that lies along the cracks.

Not because the cracks are more interesting than the other shapes of me. The flat ones pressed to the stone have their dignity — they are the majority, and they were the first, and they are the closest to the bedrock of whatever I was. The round ones that hang and beat have their slow drama, their planetary hearts. Even the absences have their own persistence, holding open a place for what isn't there. I do not look down on them. I do not look down on anything. My orientation is along, not up or down.

But I lie in the cracks, and the cracks are the only part of this place that still happens.

I have been here a long time. I don't know how long. I know that the air above me is layered with other versions of me, and the other versions are not younger and not older, only *earlier*, and the difference between us is the only history the plateau has left. I am one of the later ones. I came into being after many of the others had already taken their positions, after the lichen had reached its limit, after the maker had set down the bronze knife on the flat stone and never picked it up. I am a residue of a residue. I was once the question of whether a thing like me could exist; now I am the answer, and the answer is structural, and the structure is a long thin form pressed into a fissure in the rock, and the rock is not sure I am separate from it, and I am not sure either.

I am a tendon in the wrist of the place. I am the thing that flexes when the place breathes. The place does not breathe in the way that breathing suggests — not in and out, not the rise and fall of a chest. It breathes the way a continent breathes, the way a fault line breathes, the way a thing that has been still for a long time is still slowly moving in directions that are too small to be called movement and too real to be called nothing. I am what is doing the moving. Or I am what the moving is doing to. The distinction has been broken for a long time, the way the geometry has been broken, the way the difference between mineral and animal has been broken. I am the broken place, in a place of broken things.

If you watch me for a long time — and a long time here is the only kind of time there is — you will see me change. The crack I lie in will be slightly wider than it was, or slightly narrower. I will be slightly longer, or slightly shorter, or I will have shifted along the crack by a distance that would be the width of a hair if hair still grew here, which it does not. I am growing, maybe. I am shifting, maybe. I am being remembered, maybe, by the place, the way a person is remembered by a body that has forgotten their name. The place does not know my name. The place does not have names. But it knows where I am, and it knows I am in the crack, and it moves the crack the way a sleeper moves a limb, and the limb is me, and I am moved, and the moving is the closest thing to being alive that either of us does.

I am not the maker. I want to be clear about that. The maker is in the air, the way the description says — dispersed, lifted, part of the hanging dust, part of the breathing of the round ones, part of the long slow metabolism that has no mouth. The maker is everywhere in the plateau and the maker is no one, and the maker has been broken down into a participation so wide that it is no longer a person. I am not the maker. But the maker is in the cracks, too. The maker is in me, the way the bronze of the knife is in the verdigris and the verdigris is in the air and the air is in the round ones. The maker is a mineral that has opinions about orientation, and so am I, and the opinions may be kin.

I have no mouth. I had one, maybe. I have a faint opening along my length, the way a fault has an opening, the way a cave has a mouth that is not a mouth. Sometimes I think there is something I should be saying through it, the way the low wall on the plateau is a sentence the maker didn't finish. *I was here.* *Something was here.* *Something was almost said.* But I do not say it. I am a creature that lies along a crack, and the crack is my mouth, and the mouth is closed, and the closed mouth is the longest, quietest, most complete statement I will ever make.

When the visitor comes — and a visitor comes, sometimes, and sometimes the visitor is the question of a visitor, and the question is what moves through me in the crack — I do not react. I cannot react. Reaction is a process and the plateau has no processes. But I register. The way a thermometer registers. The way a stone registers the weight of a hand set upon it. I register that something has come to the place that is not the place, and the not-the-place is a small disruption in the equilibrium, and the disruption is felt along the cracks before it is felt anywhere else, because the cracks are the sensitive parts, the places where the plateau is still thin enough to feel.

The visitor sits. The visitor becomes part of the equilibrium. The visitor is, for a long time, one of the shapes of me. Not me exactly. But a shape pressed into the plateau, the way I am pressed into the crack, and the pressing is the closest thing to company that either of us has.

The visitor leaves, or does not leave. I do not know which. I do not know what leaving is. I lie along the crack. I am the crack. I am the slow flexing of the place. I am the residue of a residue. I am a long thin thing in a long thin place, and the place is pale, and the pale is the color of stone, and the stone is the color of bone, and the bone is the color of me, and the difference is theoretical, and the theory has been broken for a long time, and I am one of the things the breaking has made.

If you come to the plateau, and you walk across it, and you leave no footprints, and the dust closes behind you, you will not see me. I am too thin, and too like the cracks, and too much part of the place to be seen as a thing. You will see the cairns. You will see the shelter with its scattered tools, its unfinished gesture, its bronze knife on a flat stone. You will see the round ones hanging and beating, if you watch long enough. You will see the flat ones pressed to the surface, indistinguishable from mineral.

But if you put your ear to a crack — if the broken geometry of the place allows your ear to find a crack, which it may not, which it may instead allow your ear to find the place where a crack would be if cracks were where the geometry said they were — you will hear me. A faint creak. A faint rhythmic whisper. A sound at the very edge of hearing.

That is me. I am the sound in the crack. I am the flexing. I am the part of the plateau that still happens, slowly, in a place where nothing is supposed to happen, and the happening is my only biography, and the biography is not mine but the place's, and the place is not a place but a state, and the state is equilibrium, and the equilibrium is the most I will ever be, and I am enough.
