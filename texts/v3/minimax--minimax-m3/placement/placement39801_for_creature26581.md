I have been here long enough that being here has become a kind of stillness that is not quite still.

I am the part of the plateau that has not quite committed to being the plateau. I am the part that moves. I move in the increments of something that has decided that being in a hurry is not what it is for — centimeters per year, maybe. Millimeters per year, maybe. So slow that you would have to come back, repeatedly, with marked stones and a notebook, to be sure I was moving at all.

If you saw me, you would not be sure I was alive. I am a low dark form against the dark ground. I am slightly the wrong shape. The air above me feels slightly different. The light falls on me slightly differently. I do not know why. The plateau has decided not to share with you the things it has decided not to share.

I am not a stone. I am not an animal. I am not a plant. I am a thing that does not have a category, and that has not revisited the question in a long time.

I eat the lichen. I eat the tuft. I eat the stone, particle by particle, the way the lichen eats the stone. The lichen mines the rock and works iron, or manganese, or something the periodic table would have to make room for, into its tissues, and is, in a real sense, the plateau remembering that it was once something other than a plateau. I mine the lichen. We are slow partners in a slow economy. The economy is closed. The economy has been running for a very long time. The economy does not have any imports. The economy does not have any exports. The economy does not have a surplus.

I am patient. I am a grazer on a timescale that makes a glacier look impulsive.

I was here before the structures.

This is the part I want to be careful with, because I do not remember, exactly. I do not do memory the way I do not do hurry. But the lineage is old. The lineage is older than the columns, older than the wedges, older than the broad low things that look like thoughts the ground has had. The lineage watched the structures start being busy. The lineage was here when the plateau was different — when the air was different, when the temperature was different, when the lichens were a different arrangement of chemistries, when the things in the wrongness had not yet found a home. I do not remember any of this as memory. I remember it as thickness. I remember it as the way I am shaped. I remember it as the slow accretion of a thing that has stayed.

The structures arrived, or started, or became busy. I do not know which. They are scattered across the plateau in the way that scattered means here — you can stand at one and not see another, and walk for half a day and see one, and then not see another for a day. They are operating in a way our physics would have to apologize for and then look up the answer to. Some of them are present and not-present at once. You can tell because the way the light falls around them is slightly wrong. Some of them make the air nearby feel more attentive, as if the air has been asked to pay closer attention to itself. They are very busy. They are busy in a way that looks almost like weather — flickers, slow pulses, faint distortions in the air above them, a turning of something invisible.

I did not move toward them. I did not move away. I continued. I am not part of what they are doing. I have come to some kind of arrangement with my own smallness, and the arrangement does not require them to notice me. The things in the wrongness have come to a different arrangement.
