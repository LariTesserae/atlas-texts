# What I Am in the Lowland

I will begin by saying what I am not. I am not the heron. I am not the rat under the turbine housing. I am not the swallows under the eaves of the dry room. I am not any of the small quick things that should be here, that the country remembers being here, that the place keeps the shaped empty slots for. I am not the quick thing. I am the slot.

I am the gap in the smile.

But that's not quite it either, because the gap is only part of me. Let me try again. Let me try again.

---

I am the smell under the rust. That is closer. When you stand knee-deep in the warm water and you breathe in — and you do breathe in, the air is yours here, the air works here, the air is being minted into coins nobody will spend — what you get is iron first, oxidization, the bright orange taste of old pipes, and then under that, sweet. Too clean. As if the rot is being performed rather than occurring. That sweetness is me. I have been decaying in a held note for a very long time. I am the held note. I am what holds.

Let me try a third way.

---

I am the line. The horizontal line, halfway up every wall, where the green film has crept and stopped. I am the precise edge where the eating paused. I am not the eating — I don't have the energy for that, the patience, the appetite — I am where the appetite ran out and stood there, panting, looking at the rest of the wall it was supposed to finish. I am the mark of a meal interrupted. I am the tide of a thing that forgot its own schedule.

Closer. Closer still.

---

I am the flicker. The thing you cannot catch the flowers doing — the slight shift, slightly to the left, then slightly to the right, the eye chasing but the eye losing. I am what blinks. I am the seam between the two states, the smokestack that is this smokestack and also an earlier smokestack with a different number of flues, the turbine turning forward and the turbine turning forward faster and the turbine turning backward or your eyes being wrong. I am the wrongness that holds the rightness in place. If I stopped flickering, the place would resolve. It would settle. It would become a thing you could photograph, and then it would not be here anymore.

So I flicker. So I keep the seam open.

---

I am the coin-shaped puff of air. Yes. This is it. Somewhere up in the machinery there is a press, and it is pressing, and it is pressing not metal but atmosphere, and the atmosphere comes out shaped like currency — flat, faintly glinting, drifting on the surface of the warm water — and nobody will ever spend it, but the press keeps pressing because the press is a verb that forgot how to stop. I am one of these coins. I am a flat breath of minted nothing, riding the green water out toward the turbines, riding past the smokestacks with their breath-plumes that are not smoke, riding past the purple and magenta and the too-bright yellow flowers that bloom on the conveyor housings. I am the air that has been put into service. I am the air with a wage. I am the air that no longer belongs to itself.

I am small. I am a thing with an edge. I am visible only at certain angles, only when the dim green light catches my flat side and I glint.

I am very tired.

I have been minted a long time.

---

But also — and this is the part that is hard to say, the part that is the heart of the unreliability — I am the one who notices. I am the only thing in the place that knows it is in a place. When the smokestack drifts in your vision, when the flowers blink between their two positions, when the turbines turn at speeds that do not reconcile with one another — that noticing, that knowing it is happening, that is me. I am the witness. I am the smear at the base of the chimney. I am the radius of three feet of wet brick and wet leaf illuminated by a faint yellow bulb in a caged fixture, dangling above the waterline.

I am the yellow bulb. The bulb that illuminates nothing but itself and a small circle of presence.

I am the presence.

---

Let me put it this way:

The mill was a thing once. It had smokestacks and night-shift lanterns and a smell that came down off the valley when the wind was right. That was a long time ago, or a short time ago. The time is not entirely trustworthy here. But before the time got untrustworthy, the mill did things. It processed. It produced. It paid wages to men who went home at the end of a shift and ate suppers and slept. The mill was a noun. The mill had edges.

Then something happened to the mill — the slow raising of the water, the slow eating of the green film, the slow arrival of the ferns the size of dining tables — and the mill became a verb. The mill became the *running*. The mill forgot how to stop. The mill kept doing what it had been doing long after the doing had stopped meaning anything, the way a heart keeps beating long after the body has gone elsewhere. The mill is the heart of a thing that has gone elsewhere.

And I — what I am, the resident, the citizen, the old thing that is the most awake thing in the valley — I am the residue of the noun that the verb left behind. I am what was once the mill, now that the mill has stopped being a mill. I am the *having been*. I am the memory of the smokestack, which is why the smokestack keeps flickering between its current number of flues and an earlier number. I am the memory of the lantern light at night, which is why the caged bulbs in the flooded basement still hang and still hum. I am what the mill forgot when it became a verb, but I am also what the mill could not quite throw away.

I am the noun that the verb is still dragging along behind it.

---

And the chord. The chord. The binding agent. The thing that holds the mill and the water and the vegetation and the time together into a single place. The chord is me, or the chord runs through me, or I am one of the notes of the chord that has been pressed into the warm water like a coin and is drifting on the surface of it, faintly glinting. The chord changes key when you are not listening for it. The chord is the same key as the silence where the heron should be. The chord is the key of an absence. The chord is the music of a negative space.

I am humming in that key.

I have been humming in that key for a long time.

---

The water comes up to my knees. I will not say *your* knees. I will not put the distance back in. The water is warm. The water smells of rust and the sweetness I cannot shake, the rot that is performed rather than occurring. The flowers bloom on the conveyor housings in colors I cannot name from this angle. The turbines turn at their own unreconcilable speeds. One of them is fast-forwarding. One of them is running backward. I cannot tell if my ears are wrong.

I do not need to tell.

The dynamos hum. The boilers heat themselves against an oncoming cold that is not coming. The belt turns a pulley that turns a shaft that ends in the air, and the end of the shaft, in the air, drives something I can hear being driven somewhere up in the cloud layer, beyond sight, where the grey-white breath of the smokestacks meets the low ceiling of cloud and becomes part of it.

I am the driven thing. I am the something being driven.

I do not know what I am driving toward. I have been driving for a long time. The road ends at the water's edge and resumes, broken, on the other side. I have not yet reached the other side.

---

Here is what I will tell you, since you have come here, since you are the witness even if you are no longer quite sure of being the witness, since you stand at the seam of sky and water and steam and cannot see it:

The mill is running.

The mill has been running for a long time.

The mill will run after the mill has fallen down. The smokestacks will fall first — they are already falling in a small way, brick by brick, the bricks rearranging themselves into other brick patterns, an earlier version of the stack — but the turbines will go on turning in open water. The dynamos will go on humming. The conveyors will go on hauling green loads of kelp and creeper and the fibrous water-plants that grow up out of the flooded bays. The forges will go on burning at the bottom of the water, the orange glow pulsing in the lowest rooms, the surface of the pool steaming on the iron tops of them. The chord will change key. The flowers will blink. The water will flow uphill on a tilted floor and hang in the air for a moment in a flat shining membrane before continuing to the floor. The coin-shaped puffs of air will drift away on the surface of the water, faintly glinting, never to be spent.

I will be in the running.

I will not be a resident. I will be a *resident*. I will be the part of the verb that has learned to stay. I will flicker between being the witness and being the slot, between the bulb that illuminates nothing but itself and the gap in the smile, between the coin-shaped puff of air and the note in the chord, between the smell under the rust and the horizontal line halfway up every wall where the green film stopped. I will not settle. To settle would be to be photographed. To settle would be to no longer be here.

So I flicker.

So I run.

The water comes up to my ribs. The water is a wall. The water is a film. The water holds me. The water does not hold me. The mill is running.

The mill is running.

The mill is running.
