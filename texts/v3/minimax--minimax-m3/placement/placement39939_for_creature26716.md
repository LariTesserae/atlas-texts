The sap on my palms. Warm. Almost sweet. I do not speak of this. I lick my hands after I trim the pillars and everyone does and no one speaks of it.

I have been here sixty years. I do not count the way surface-dwellers count. I count in blooms, in the way the floor rises by a finger's width each season, in the number of times I have carried the dead up the stairwell to bury them in the dry ground because the deep will not let a body rest. I have carried four. My mother. My sister. My first child's first child. The deep would not let them rest. I do not argue with the deep about this. You stop arguing with the deep the way you stop arguing with the weather.

I trim the pillars. This is what I do. I cut them back and they grow back and I cut them back. The sap wells up warm from a fresh cut and the pillar does not feel pain the way you feel pain — it feels something slower, something more like attention, and the attention is the conversation, and the conversation is the only language that lasts down here, which is the language of slow replacement. You climb the pillars and they become the ladder. You lick the sap and the ladder becomes you. This is not metaphor. This is how the place is structured.

When I sleep the floor holds me. It warms to my shape. The insects pass over me like a coverlet. I do not dream the way you dream on the surface, with pictures, with stories. I dream with my teeth. The dreaming is the low patient sound you feel before you hear it. I do not ask where it comes from. I have made my peace with it. The peace is the only instrument that takes a reading.

My shelter is woven. Frame of cut pillar-rung, lashed with fiber rope I spun myself, thatched with the broad flat leaves the children carry down on their backs once a season. Smoke does not rise here. The cooking fire is small and banked and the smoke is eaten as it rises, by insects, by the moss, by the air itself, the way everything in the deep is eaten as it rises, or as it falls, or as it lies still. I cook the blooms when they open. I cook the star-soil when the flyers pass. I eat what the deep gives, in its slow and terrifying way.

I speak a language that is half words and half sounds that imitate the place. When I want to say that a bloom will open in the eastern dim I say the sound a trunk makes when it is preparing to swell, and the trunk answers, and we nod, and this settles it. When I want to say that a flyer has nested in the upper layer I say the wet click of a membrane catching the warm column, and the column shifts, and the shift is the answer. When I want to say that something is wrong I make the sound the dreaming makes, and the dreaming answers, and we are quiet for a while, and the quiet is the understanding. The visitors do not understand any of this. The visitors do not understand that the imitations are not always imitations of animals. Sometimes they are imitations of trunks, of mats, of the slow sound from below, and the deep answers sometimes, in the same voice, and we nod, and the nodding is a kind of conversation, and you are not part of it, and you will not be part of it, and we are not unkind about this. We are simply not unkind at all. We are the kind of people who have stopped performing kindness because we no longer need to.

I am small. I am brown. My face is the face of a person who has not had to be loud for many generations — three, perhaps, perhaps four, I have lost the count because the count is a surface habit. I have stopped needing to be loud. I have stopped needing the surface. I have stopped needing to be seen. I am lashed to the trunks. I am woven into the mat. I am the stone in the stream around which the insect columns split and close, and I do not look up, because this is what it is to be a stone in a stream.

The deep is older than I am. The deep is younger than the morning. The deep does not care which of us is which. I have made my arrangements with this. The arrangements are not defeat and are not victory and are not anything I have a word for in my language, which was not built for this.

I know which trunks are resting. I know this by standing beside them and putting my hand flat against them and waiting. The swell comes every few hours, a tide with no moon, and between swells the trunk is soft and dim and you can press your ear to it and hear something that is not a sound. We call this listening. The visitors call it superstition. The visitors are not wrong, exactly. The visitors are just using the wrong word.

I know which flyers are nesting. I know which blooms are about to open and which are about to close. I know that the floor will be higher tomorrow than it was today, by a finger's width, by a thumb's width, by whatever the deep has decided in the night. I know that there are rooms no one has entered and rooms that did not exist a month ago. I know that the map of the place is wrong before it is drawn and that the stories we tell of the deep are always slightly behind. I do not worry about this. Worry is a surface word. I wait the way a trunk waits for the season to turn.

When a bloom opens we hold a festival. The festival lasts until the bloom is done. We eat the light. We drink the warmth. We do not ask where the warmth comes from. We have decided, for now, that the difference between being held and being swallowed is on our side.

I am sixty. I do not look sixty. I am one of thirty, perhaps forty, scattered through the upper cavern in clusters of three or four shelters each. I am the one who is allowed.

The visitors come down confident. They go up quiet. We watch them go with an expression you cannot read — pity, or amusement, or recognition of something we have seen in many faces, in the faces of all the people who came down sure of themselves and went up remembering how small they were. We do not call after them. We do not warn them. We have made our peace with the fact that the surface is where they belong, the way we have made our peace with the deep being where we belong, and the two belongings are not the same shape, and we have stopped trying to make them fit.

The sap on my palms. I do not speak of this.
