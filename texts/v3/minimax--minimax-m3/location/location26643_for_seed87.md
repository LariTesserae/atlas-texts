The place is not large. You could circle its perimeter in a few minutes, if circling were something you could do here, but the geometry doesn't really permit circling. The walls curve in, fold back, dip down. The whole thing has the quality of a chamber that hasn't finished deciding what shape it wants to be — a soft pocket in the underside of something larger, an indentation in a long spine of rock that runs away into the dark in both directions. You are inside the spine. The rock above you is the size of a cathedral, and you can feel it breathing the way cold rock breathes: slowly, in the way of stone that has learned to hold water without cracking.

The water is cold the way water is cold when it has stopped moving. It has not stopped moving, exactly. It is only that the motion has become patient. Currents slip along the ceiling in threads you can almost see — silvered, fine, the way breath looks in winter above a sleeping mouth. They carry nothing obvious. Sediment, perhaps. The smell of something living.

The floor of the chamber is not a floor. It is a threshold, a place where stone has begun, recently, to be something else. There are outcroppings that didn't exist a year ago — or a month ago, or whatever unit of time applies to rock that grows. They are pale, layered, crusted with a fine white bloom that is either mineral or the first film of a creature. When you look closely, the bloom has texture. It has direction. It thickens along certain edges and thins along others, and the pattern it makes is the pattern of a street, or a wall, or a corridor that has not yet decided whether to close itself off.

This is what is being built.

You did not see it begin, and you may not see it finish. It will take the time stone takes when stone is patient, and the time life takes when life is patient, and these are different times, and they are meeting here. The structures are not yet structures. They are the suggestion of structures, a sketch the place is making of itself. But they are numerous. They crowd the chamber in layers the way a brain has folds, the way a lung has chambers. They press close against one another. They share walls. Some of them open into one another through small dark mouths. If you were a creature of a particular size — the size of a thumb, say, or a fist — you could move through this place as if it were a city. You could follow its arteries. You could get lost in it, the way one gets lost in a place that has not yet finished naming its streets.

The creatures are here in numbers.

They keep to the cracks and the soft places, the way water keeps to cracks and soft places. There are things like fingers, pale and slow, that withdraw when touched. There are things like collars of mucus that pulse. There are small black shapes that hold perfectly still against the pale growth and breathe so quietly you can only tell they breathe by the way the water moves around them. Some of them are building. Some of them are eating what is being built. Some of them are simply present, dense in the way that a thicket is dense, the way a crowd is dense — a single body made of many bodies leaning against one another. They do not appear to be afraid of one another. They do not appear to be aware of one another, in the way a city is not aware of itself as a city. They are doing the things they are doing, and the doing of those things is what the place is.

The light is the color of old pewter. It comes from above, in a slow column, and even in the column things are not clear. Shadows are long and a little green. Where the light does not reach, the color becomes a blue so deep it is almost a black, and in that blue-black, the small moving things become small moving absences, little rooms in the room you are already in. You can see perhaps ten feet in any direction. Past that, the place keeps going but declines to show you.

There is no sound.

This is not strictly true. There are sounds. The rock clicks occasionally as it cools further, and the click travels through the water as a single hard ping that has no echo, because there is nothing here to echo back. There is the wet tick of something opening and closing, opening and closing, somewhere among the structures, like a very small door. There is the faintest hiss of current along the ceiling. There is, once, a low thrum — felt more than heard, in the jaw and the chest — that lasts a few seconds and goes away, and you cannot tell whether it came from a creature or the rock or the water itself deciding to move. But these sounds are so few and so soft that they make the silence louder, the way a single candle makes a room darker. The chamber holds its quiet like a cupped hand holds water. The chamber does not want you to hear it. The chamber wants to continue.

It is cold here in a way that feels polite, a cold that has arranged itself around you rather than against you. Your skin registers it as pressure, as a presence, as the slow persuasion of a temperature that would prefer you not to stay long, but is not going to insist. The cold has a smell — mineral, faintly metallic, faintly sweet, the way a tide pool smells when the tide has been out for a while and the pool is thinking about whether to become land. The cold has a taste, if you open your mouth: salt, and a fine grit, and something else, something almost vegetable, as if somewhere above, in the bright world, kelp were rotting slowly in the sun.

The place is doing things.

You can see this if you wait. A pale arm of growth extends, by a fraction, along a seam in the stone. A small black shape detaches itself from a wall and drifts, almost imperceptibly, to a new wall, and adheres. A thin column of something that is not water rises from a crack and dissolves before it reaches the height of your hand. Somewhere in the layered growth, a soft burp of gas escapes, a single bubble that wobbles upward into the pewter light and is gone. These are the events of the place. They are small and they are slow and they are enough. The chamber is not waiting for anything. The chamber is busy being what it is. It is constructing itself the way a dream constructs a city, by adding rooms you cannot remember deciding to add.

You could stay here a long time and not learn it. You could leave now and never come back and the chamber would not notice. The creatures here do not have a name for you, and you do not have a name for them, and the structures being built do not have a name for themselves, because they are not yet sure they are things that have names. They are possibilities. They are the urbanism of a place that is still deciding what kind of place it is. It could become a reef. It could become a ruin. It could become a city for creatures that have not yet evolved the ability to live in cities. It could dissolve back into rock. The water is patient. The rock is patient. The cold is patient.

The chamber keeps its silence.

The light, pewter and dim, falls on the layered pale growth and the many small dark lives pressed within it, and does not decide what any of it is.
