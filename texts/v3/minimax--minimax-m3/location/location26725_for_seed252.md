# The Throat

The fissure opened six days ago, or six years—the way time moves here makes such distinctions academic. Local chronology resets each time the heat pulse rolls through, and no one bothers to count the days between pulses because counting requires a kind of attention the light doesn't permit.

You stand at the rim.

Below you, the rock stretches down and inward, and the geometry of "down" begins to soften. The walls of the rift are not walls in any architectural sense. They are a single unfolding surface, pocked and glassy, shot through with veins of something iridescent that catches the light and throws it back rearranged. The stone is obsidian in places, basalt in others, and in still others it is neither—something that cleaves in planes that don't correspond to its apparent grain, something that rings when you kick it, a bell-tone so pure it has no overtones at all.

The light is the first thing you notice, and the last thing you can account for.

It comes from no source you can identify. The sky is a smear of white. No sun, no disc, no shadow-cast geometry. The brightness is ambient, total, as if the air itself were the emitter. Things on the ground cast short soft shadows at high noon, but the shadows are wrong—they fall toward the light, or they fall in two directions at once, or they simply don't fall at all and the object sits in its own dimming. Your retinas ache anyway. Squinting helps. Closing your eyes doesn't—a faint pink geometry persists behind the lids, the afterimage of nothing in particular.

The heat is the second thing. It climbs from the rock itself, from the depth, from whatever process is keeping this place so freshly young. You sweat within minutes. The air above the stone shimmers faintly, the way asphalt trembles in July, except this is October or it is Tuesday or it is the third pulse since the last pulse. The water on the ground steams. The water doesn't pool so much as it *appears*—condensation crawling up the walls, a thin film that thickens into a sheet, then gathers into beads that run downslope and vanish into cracks too small to see. The rocks are sweating. The rocks have been sweating continuously since the place came into being, and they will continue until the heat dies or the water does, and neither has shown any sign of relenting.

The fissure is roughly a hundred meters across at the rim and narrows as it descends, though "narrows" is the wrong word because the narrowing is not uniform. The walls pinch and bulge. There are shelves and overhangs and reverse-slopes where loose rock should fall but doesn't, where flakes of obsidian hang like exfoliating skin, vibrating faintly when the heat pulses through. The pulse arrives every forty minutes, on a schedule no instrument has been able to confirm but every body has been able to learn. You feel it in the soles of your feet first—a tightening, a faint upward shove, as if the rock has drawn a single sharp breath. Then the air above the fissure brightens for a half-second, and then the heat rolls outward in a visible wave, a shimmer that travels the walls from bottom to top, and the water on the stone flashes to steam and the steam rises and re-condenses on the upper walls and begins its slow migration downward again.

It is, in its way, a kind of weather.

There is no plant life. You have looked. You have looked with an instrument that would resolve a single cell, and what it found was mineral, mineral, mineral. The dark glass. The lighter feldspar. The iridescent veins—those are not mineral, or not only mineral, but they are not alive either. They are aligned. They have structure. They are arranged in a pattern that does not repeat at any scale you can sample, which means either the pattern is unique or the pattern is larger than the instrument or the pattern only exists in the act of being measured. You have stopped trying to decide.

You are not the only one here.

There is another. Perhaps more—there is no way to be certain, because the bright air eats outlines at distance and the only reliable way to count presences is to wait for them to move and see what resolves out of the shimmer. You have seen one. It is smaller than you and darker than the rock, which is the only reason you can see it at all. It moves along the upper rim with a gait that is not quite a gait—it translates itself in a way that involves the legs but does not depend on them, and when it stops, it does not stop so much as *reduce*. It becomes smaller. It becomes a darker patch on the dark glass. You cannot tell if it is watching you. You can tell it is aware of you, because the heat pulses seem to coincide with its movements, and you have begun to suspect this is not a coincidence.

You have begun to suspect a number of things.

The sound of the place is mostly quiet, in the way that a held breath is quiet. There is the tick of cooling stone. There is the faint hiss of steam rising, which is not one sound but many, a kind of white noise that gives the air a soft texture. There is the occasional bright crack of a thermal fracture, a sharp pop that echoes once and dies. There is the ring you can produce by kicking the rock, which carries further than it should, given the geometry, and which arrives at your ears fractionally before it should, given the speed of sound. There is the pulse itself, which is not a sound but is heard anyway—a low tightening in the eardrum, a pressure, the way you can hear a thunderstorm before you can see it.

There is something else, which you have not told anyone about.

It is the light in the veins.

When the heat pulse passes through, the iridescent material in the rock flashes—not brightly, not dramatically, but in a way that is unmistakably *organized*. The flash is not uniform. It travels. It has syntax. It begins in the lower depths where you cannot see, propagates upward through the vein network in something under a second, and terminates at the surface in a pattern that almost resolves into a glyph before it fades. You have watched it happen thirty times now. You have not been able to determine if the pattern is the same each time. You have not been able to determine if *same* is even a meaningful concept here. The flash is brief. The flash is, possibly, irrelevant. The flash is the only thing that has ever happened in this place that wanted to be read.

The water runs down.

The water runs down and joins whatever is happening below, where the fissure narrows toward a point you cannot see, where the heat is most intense, where the stone is youngest and the veins are most densely woven. You have not gone down. You have not gone down because the upper rim is already almost too hot to stand on, and the descent steepens past the second shelf, and the air below the second shelf does something to your depth perception that you have decided not to investigate further. You have stayed at the rim. You have watched the pulses. You have watched the dark figure move. You have begun to wonder what the place is for, and you have begun to suspect that the question assumes a grammar the place does not share.

The light is bright.

The light is bright and the heat is high and the water is everywhere and the rock is young and the place is small and the place is, somehow, also larger than it is. There is no construction. There is no shelter. There is nowhere to sit that will not eventually become too hot to sit on. There is the rim, and the descent, and the pulse, and the flash, and the water running down, and the darkness moving along the upper edge, and the white sky, and the sound of cooling stone, and the sound of your own breathing, which is the loudest thing here.

You have been here for what feels like a long time.

You will be here for what feels like longer.
