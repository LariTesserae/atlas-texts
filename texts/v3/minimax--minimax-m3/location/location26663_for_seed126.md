# The Long Settlement

I want to start with what the eye refuses to accept, which is that there is no ground.

---

You stand — and "stand" is a courtesy, because nothing underfoot yields — on a surface that is not quite a surface. It is the outermost skin of a city that has been building itself downward for so long that the concept of *down* has become a kind of joke the architecture tells itself. The floor where your boots rest is plate, once. It was laid down by a process that finished its work approximately eleven million years before any organism you would recognize as such decided to complicate the question of what *organism* meant. The plate is kilometers thick in places, in places it has thinned to almost nothing, and in those thin places you can feel — through the soles, through the bones of the leg, through some apparatus behind the inner ear that the engineers of this place have never quite been able to silence — the slow, planetary pulse of the substrate.

The substrate is not rock. Nothing here is rock. Rock is what happens when a planet hasn't been paying attention.

I want to describe the light first, because the light is what tells you that you are somewhere that has rules, even if the rules will not be explained.

---

The light comes from everywhere and from nothing. There is no single source, no horizon-bucket of sun, no moon, no stars visible in the way you have been trained to expect. The sky — and there is a sky, in the sense that there is a *far* — is a held brightness, uniform in a way that is not quite uniform. If you watch a single point in it for a long time, you can see it breathe. There are bands. Lighter bands, darker bands, in slow transit overhead, and the lighter bands are not clouds because nothing here condenses, and the darker bands are not shadows because the light is coming from the bands themselves. They are a kind of ceiling. The sky is a ceiling that forgot to confess.

When the brighter band is overhead, your shadow shortens to a pool at your feet. When the dimmer band transits, your shadow lengthens, but it does not point anywhere consistent, because the band has no center and the light is pouring in from a slab of sky rather than from a disc. The shadows are queer, soft, and they do not behave like the shadows you were raised by. They flicker, sometimes. They are not flickering. Something is moving in the air between you and the band, very high, and the things that move up there are also not quite the things you were raised by.

The light is warm. It is the warmth of late afternoon, all day, every day, without the corresponding cooling that evening would imply. If you have a sense of internal time, that sense will begin, after the first few days, to feel insulted.

---

What you are standing on, when you look down at it, looks at first like a tessellation. A vast, repeating pattern in a material that is not stone and is not metal and is not ceramic, though it shares qualities with all three. The tiles are small where they are near you — hand-sized, you would have said, if your hands were the right size — and as your eye follows the pattern outward, the tiles grow. Not uniformly. The pattern is fractal-adjacent: there are versions of itself nested inside itself at every scale your eye and then your instruments can resolve, and at every scale the tile you are examining looks like it might, at the right magnification, contain a smaller copy of the entire field you are standing in.

The material is warm to the touch. It is warm because the substrate is warm. The warmth travels up through the plate in a way that has no obvious gradient, because the plate is not really a thermal conductor; it is a thermal *medium*, in a sense you don't have a word for. The warmth is the place's resting metabolism. The place is not dead. The place is the opposite of dead, but the opposite of dead, after enough time, looks indistinguishable from the opposite of alive, and this place has had a long time to practice that resemblance.

There are no seams you can find. The plate is continuous. The plate is, in fact, planetary.

---

There is no water.

I want to be clear about this because the absence is structural, not incidental. There is no *H₂O* anywhere on this world in any phase, in any reservoir, in any bound form that the place's chemists would recognize as water. The atmosphere — and there is an atmosphere, thin, warm, dry in a way that the word *dry* cannot do justice to — is composed of gases that are heavier than you would expect and lighter than you would expect and a small percentage of something that is not a gas in any way that your home planet's chemists would have admitted. The atmosphere moves. You can feel it on your face, intermittently, in gusts that are warm and smell of nothing. There is no humidity because there is no humidity to be. The wind is a redistribution of dust, and the dust is not what you would call dust, and there is not much of it.

The absence of water does not make this place dead. It makes this place *other*. The chemistry here is not your chemistry, and the things that grow here — and things do grow here, though not many, and not where you would expect — are not doing what the things you know do. They are not transpiring. They are not photosynthesizing. They are not, in any way you can verify with the equipment you brought, *metabolizing*. They are sitting in the light and they are being changed by it, very slowly, and what they are becoming is not obviously alive and is not obviously not alive, and the distinction has begun, for you, to lose its grip.

---

The vegetation is sparse.

What there is of it stands in isolated configurations, sometimes singly, more often in groups of three or seven or some number you can never quite finish counting before the count changes. The configurations are not random. They are following a logic that the place has, and the place's logic is older than anything you would call a language.

The plants — I will call them plants, though they do not require the courtesy — are vertical, mostly, and short, mostly, and they are made of a substance that is dark. Not the dark of shadow, not the dark of pigment, but a kind of optical dark, a material that absorbs the warm light and returns almost nothing of it. They are silhouettes in a bright field. They cast long soft shadows. The shadows are darker than the plants, which is the wrong way around, but you have been here long enough that the wrong way around has started to feel like a different right.

Some of them move. Not in the wind — there is not enough wind, and the plants are not the right kind of compliant — but in a slow, internal way, a reconfiguration of their silhouette against the bright floor, a reaching, a folding, an act of attention. They are looking at the light band overhead. They are tracking it. They are very old, and they have been tracking it for a very long time, and the tracking is the only thing they do, and they do not do it quickly.

There is no green. There is no chlorophyll. There is no photosynthesis. Whatever these things are doing with the light, it is not what the things you were raised by do with the light, and the difference is not a small difference, and the difference is the whole shape of the ecology, if there is an ecology.

I think there is an ecology, but I think it is one that has decided to take its time with you.

---

The erosion is ancient.

This is the part that takes a while to believe.

The place is old the way the floor of an ocean is old. The place is old the way a number is old when it has been incrementing for so long that the increments themselves have worn smooth. The place is so old that the concept of *old* has had to be reinvented several times in its presence, and each reinvention has been filed down by the next several million years until it is indistinguishable from the one before it.

Nothing here is sharp. Nothing here is jagged. Nothing here has an edge that has not been negotiated with. Every surface has been visited by every other surface, in some sequence, over a span of time that your equipment cannot date. The plate you are standing on has been walked on, in some sense, by the underside of itself, and the underside has been walked on by the upperside, and the two have come to an understanding, and the understanding is the texture of the ground. The ground is not smooth. The ground is *agreed*.

The horizon is close. The horizon is close because the place is not flat, exactly, but it is flat in the way that a planet is flat — that is, it has topography, but the topography is on a scale so large that you cannot see it. You are standing in a depression the size of a small ocean basin. You cannot tell. The land rises, very far away, to a ridge that you will never reach on foot, and on the other side of the ridge is another depression, and so on, and so on, across the whole world, forever. The depressions are not canyons. The depressions are not craters. The depressions are *features*, in the way that the grain of a wood is a feature, or the wrinkle on a dried fruit is a feature. The world has settled into its own skin, and the skin has its own opinion about what shape to take, and the shape is not quite any shape you have a name for.

The whole world is one city, or one building, or one organism, or one thought, and the distinction between these has worn away, here, over the time the world has had to think about it.

---

The place is total. There is no natural substrate.

This means: there is no place where the hand of construction has not been. The hand, here, is a long way back, and the hand was patient, and the hand was thorough, and what the hand made has since been edited by eleven million years of bright warm light and no water, and the editing has been so thorough that you cannot, with any instrument you brought, find the original.

But it is there. Underneath. Under the plate, under the substrate, under the warm pulse. There is a designed thing, an engineered thing, a thing that was *made*, and the making was deliberate, and the making is older than the place's current occupants by a margin that the occupants themselves do not have a unit for.

I do not know what made it.

I have theories. I will keep them.

---

The tech is beyond you.

This is not a failure of your equipment. This is a feature of the place. The place is built, in some layer underneath the layer you are standing on, with engineering that operates on principles your physics has not yet named. The engineering is quiet. The engineering is not visible. The engineering is the warmth, and the light bands, and the slow pulse in the floor, and the way the plants track the sky, and the way the dust does not fall, and the way your shadow behaves.

You can interact with the engineering, in a limited way, if you have the right kind of instrument, which you do not, but a small percentage of visitors have. The instrument is older than the visitors. The instrument is *made of the place*, in a way that is not quite compatible with being carried out. When you point the instrument at the plate, the plate returns a number. The number is large. The number is the answer to a question the instrument is not quite asking. You do not have the math to translate. You record the number, and the number joins the other numbers, and the numbers do not yet, in aggregate, mean anything.

Some of the visitors, the ones who stay longer, begin to suspect that the numbers mean something *to the place*, and the place is not telling them, and the not-telling is not unkind. The not-telling is the place being, itself, in the way that a forest is a forest and is not in the business of explaining forests to a single tree.

---

The fauna is rare.

You will see, on average, three things in a day that you would call animals, and you will argue, afterward, about whether they were animals.

They are small. They are the size of a small dog, or a large cat, or a child, depending on the angle. They are dark, like the plants. They move in a way that is purposeful and unhurried, and they do not seem to be aware of you, and you will eventually realize that they are not unaware of you, but that the way they are aware of you is not the way anything you have met before has been aware of you. They are aware of you the way a floor is aware of a footstep. They are receiving you. They are not, in any sense, attending to you.

If you approach one, it does not flee. It does not engage. It continues its slow transit, and you are in its transit, and when it reaches you, it passes through you, or around you, or it does something that you will later describe to your colleagues using the language of *phase*, and your colleagues will not believe you, and the description will not be wrong.

They eat, if they eat, by sitting near one of the plants and being near the plant for a long time. What passes between them is not food. It is not gas. It is not light. It is something that the place has a name for, and the name is not a sound, and the name is older than sound.

---

The sound is almost nothing.

This is the first thing the first visitor noticed, and it is the thing they all say, when they come back, and it is the thing they cannot stop saying, because the silence here is not the absence of sound, it is the *presence* of something that is doing the work of silence, the way a dam is the presence of something that is doing the work of stillness on a river. The silence here is held. The silence here is a decision, made at a level deeper than the plate.

You can hear your own breathing. You can hear the soft, dry whisper of your feet on the warm ground. You can hear, if you put your ear to the plate, the pulse of the substrate, which is not a sound, exactly, but which the ear receives as a low, slow, sympathetic vibration, like a heart heard through a mattress. The pulse is patient. The pulse is not in a hurry.

There is a wind, sometimes, that makes a sound like a far sheet of paper being turned. The wind is warm. The wind is brief. The wind does not repeat itself. The wind comes from a direction that has no name on your compass, and it goes to a direction that also has no name, and the direction is not the same each time, but the direction is not random, either. The wind is part of the place's metabolism, in the same way the plants are. The wind is exhaling, or the wind is the place turning over in its long sleep, or the wind is something else, and you are not in a position to know.

There is a sound, very rarely, that you will only hear once, or twice, in a long stay, and the sound is not a sound, it is a *change in the silence*, a thinning of the silence, a moment in which the held quiet of the place becomes, briefly, a held loud, and the loud is a single tone, very low, very far away, and it lasts for exactly the time it takes you to notice it, and then it is over, and the silence is back, and the silence is the same as before, and you will not be able to prove that it happened, but you will know.

---

The dynamics are slow.

This is the thing you must learn, to be here. The place is not still — the place is the opposite of still — but the place is *patient*, and the place is operating on a clock that you cannot keep, and if you try to keep it, you will go mad, and the place will not notice you going mad, because the place is not attending to you, the place is being itself, and the being-itself takes a long time, and the being-itself is what you came to see, and you cannot see it, because it is too slow to see, and you are too fast to see it, and the mismatch is the whole problem of being a visitor here.

The light bands move. The plants track them. The wind comes and goes. The animals transit. The pulse in the floor continues. The substrate thinks, or rests, or both. These are the dynamics. They are not the dynamics of weather, exactly, because the place has weather, but the weather is not the weather you were raised by. The weather here is a slow, planetary, *decision*, made over geologic time, and you are seeing one frame of it, and the frame is not the kind of frame that lets you understand the movie.

In a long time, on a scale you will not live to see, the light bands will change their pattern, and the plants will reconfigure to track the new pattern, and the animals will find new transits, and the plate will, in some places, lift, and in some places settle, and the place will be different, and the place will not be a different place, the place will be the same place, *continuing*, and the continuing is the dynamic, and the dynamic is what you came to see, and the dynamic is the silence, and the silence is the warmth, and the warmth is the pulse, and the pulse is the long settlement of a thing that has been settling for longer than your planet has been a planet.

---

I have not told you what color the light is.

It is amber. It has always been amber. It will, in a long time, be something else, and the something else will not be a different color, it will be a different *amber*, and the new amber will be the old amber, continued, and the visitor who sees it, then, will write a description like this one, and the description will not be wrong, and it will not be right, and the not-wrong-and-not-right will be the only honest thing to say about a place that has been itself for so long that itself has become a kind of music, and the music has become a kind of silence, and the silence has become a kind of warmth, and the warmth has become a kind of light, and the light has been amber, and the amber has been here, and here is the place I have been describing, and the place does not need my description, and the place will not read it, and the place has been here, amber and patient, since before you were a thought, and will be here, amber and patient, after you are a memory, and the being-here is the place's only argument, and the argument is sufficient.

You should sit down. The plate is warm. The silence is held. The bands are moving, very slowly, overhead, and the plants are tracking them, very slowly, and you are, for the first time in your life, in a place that is not in a hurry, and the not-hurry is the loudest thing you have ever heard, and the loudest thing is also the quietest, and the quiet is the place, and the place is here, and here is warm, and amber, and old, and patient, and made, and continuing, and the continuing is the only thing, and the only thing is enough.

Sit down. You have been standing for a long time. The plate is warm. The plate is patient. The plate has been waiting, and you have arrived, and the arrival is not a small thing, even here, even in this long settlement, even in this amber and ancient quiet.

Sit down.
