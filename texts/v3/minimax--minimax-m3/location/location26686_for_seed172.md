The sound was already there before you understood that you had arrived. There is no threshold. No door that sealed behind you. But the air changed — thickened, pressed, became a medium you had to push through. It is not one sound but many, layered so tightly that they have collapsed into a single substance. Pumps. Fans. Compressors. The rhythmic hammering of something distant. The whine of turbines. The slow groan of metal under its own weight. All of it at once. All of it unchanged. The equilibrium is deafening.

You are in a small place. You did not walk into it so much as notice that you were already standing inside it, perhaps having stepped through a seam in something larger, a seam that has since forgotten you. The room is hard to measure. The far wall might be ten meters away or a hundred; the geometry folds. Corners are not quite corners. The ceiling — if there is a ceiling — is high, or absent, or made of the same darkness that lives below the floor. You are contained, but the container's shape is a thing it keeps to itself.

The floor is grating. Steel, or something that looks like steel and refuses to confirm. Through the lattice, you can see depth — a vertical architecture of catwalks and beams descending into a colder dark, or rising toward a sky you cannot quite locate. The grating has not rusted. Its edges are sharp. Everything here is young — freshly made, freshly emerged, the corners not yet rounded, the surfaces not yet softened by any weather. Whatever atmosphere touches this place, it does not weather. It only freezes.

Cold. The cold is the cold of a refrigerated interior, of winter held in a room, of a temperature that has been set and never revised. Your breath does not cloud; the air is too dry for that, too clean. There is no damp, no metallic tang of old moisture, no humidity anywhere. The cold is absolute and the dryness is its twin. Together they have made a climate that is not quite a climate — a sealed condition, an absence with a shape.

Light falls. From above, or from everywhere, or from a source you cannot identify. It is ordinary light. It is not bright. It is not dim. It is the light of a workshop, a kitchen at midday, a hallway at three in the afternoon. Ordinary light in a place where ordinary is the wrong word.

Around you, at long intervals, there are machines. They stand on the grating. They hang from the unseen ceiling. They are embedded in the walls like organs set into a body. They are large — most of them are large — and there is much empty floor between them, long stretches of nothing separating one apparatus from the next. The machines are not decorative. They are functional, blocky, the shape of work that has forgotten it is work. Cylinders. Towers of stacked discs. Banks of pipes. Control panels with screens that show a number — the same number, always the same number — or static that has hardened into a permanent image. Gauges with needles fixed at certain marks. Switches left in certain positions. Nothing moves. Nothing needs to move. The sound they make is not the sound of motion; it is the sound of their being on, of their identity, of the fact that they exist and have always existed and will exist.

No one is here. There has never been anyone here, perhaps, or there was once, very long ago, someone who built this and left and was not needed. The machines do not require tending. They have reached their final state. They roar in their roar forever.

And yet.

On the pipes, on the undersides of the machines, in the shadowed crooks beneath the grating — there is growth. Sparse. A green so dark it is nearly black. A gray so pale it is nearly white. Things that have no business being here, in a sealed frozen industrial nothing, but that are here, quietly, against every expectation. They are not plants. They have no leaves, no flowers, no obvious biology. They look like corrosion that has organized itself. Like frost that has learned to climb. Like lichen, except the substrate is steel and the lichen is — something else. They branch in fractal patterns following the grain of the metal. They do not move. They are six centimeters tall, and they have been six centimeters tall for longer than anything in this room has been counting. They feed on what is available: the air, perhaps, or the noise, or the slow oxidation of metal that has been going on for so long that the oxidation has become a kind of slow life. They are the only fauna this place has, and they are not fauna. They are the closest thing to fauna, and even that is too generous.

The place is high up. You can feel it — not in the ears, not in the lungs, but in the cold, in the thinness of the air, in the sense that whatever lies below the grating goes a long way down before it meets anything solid. You are near the top of something. The top of a tower. The top of a stack. A crown of machinery embedded in the upper reaches of a larger structure that continues downward forever, or until it does not, but it does not stop. This room is hidden in it. A pocket. A single chamber, sealed, set into the architecture the way a pearl is set into a shell.

Strange. The place is strange, but not dramatically. The strangeness is patient. A pipe that should be vertical bends at an angle that is not forty-five and not thirty and not anything that has a name. A gauge's needle points in a direction that is not up. Three walls meet at a corner and the three walls are not the same material — one is steel, one is something ceramic, one is a darkness that has the shape of a wall. The proportions are slightly wrong. The angles are slightly wrong. The light falls in a way that does not quite match the surfaces it falls on. You do not remark upon these things. You simply stand inside them and feel, without naming it, that this place is not entirely possible.

And the sound. Always the sound.

It is a wall of sound. It is a sea. You cannot hear yourself inside it. Your heartbeat, if you have one, is absorbed. Your thoughts are absorbed. You are inside the roar and the roar is inside you and neither one of you is going to win. It does not hurt — or it hurts in a way you have stopped registering, the way a pressure stops registering when it has been constant too long. What remains is the sensation of sound without hearing. Pressure without pitch. Noise as a physical texture, pressing on the skin, pressing on the chest, pressing on whatever it is in you that the cold has not yet reached.

The light falls on a valve. The valve is closed. It has always been closed. Nothing flows through it; the pipe it commands is sealed at both ends and has never held anything but itself. But the valve is there, and it is part of the system, and the system includes it in its roar, and so the closed valve contributes its small closed-valve note to the wall of sound, a single fixed pitch that has been playing since the room was made.

The light falls on a screen. The screen shows a number. The number is the same number it has always shown. The number is not connected to anything measurable. But it is displayed, and it is lit, and it hums.

The light falls on one of the growths. Dark green, almost black, climbing a pipe joint. Six centimeters tall. It is in equilibrium with its environment; it has reached its size and stopped, like everything else here. It will not grow tomorrow. It did not grow yesterday. It has been six centimeters tall for the entire memory of the room, and it will continue being six centimeters tall for as long as the room continues, and the room will continue for as long as the equilibrium holds, and the equilibrium will hold.

Somewhere far, or somewhere near — it is impossible to tell — the larger structure goes on. There may be thousands of these rooms, identical, each containing its deafening stillness, its sparse machines, its frost-growths, its ordinary light, its impossible angles. Or this may be the only one. It does not matter. The equilibrium holds in either case. The equilibrium holds in any case.

You stand on the grating. The cold presses against you. The noise presses against you. The light, ordinary, falls on whatever surface you have, and you are lit like everything else here, lit from nowhere, lit from everywhere, lit at exactly the brightness of a room where someone once worked and no longer does.

Nothing changes. Nothing has ever changed. Nothing will ever change. The machines roar. The gauges point. The growths do not grow. The light does not flicker. The cold does not relent. The noise does not pause.

You are inside something that has finished becoming itself. It will remain itself. The remaining will be forever. The forever is loud. The loud is the place. The place is small. The small is hidden. The hidden is total. The total is yours, if you stay.

If you stay.
