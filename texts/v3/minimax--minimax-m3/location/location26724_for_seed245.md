# The Foundry of New Atmospheres

It begins with a sound that is not a sound, a pressure that lives in the inner ear and cannot be located. The ground underfoot is not ground. It is a surface, which is different. It has been *placed*.

---

## The Surface

The floor of this place is made of tiles. Not the orderly tiles of a kitchen, not the geometric tiles of a bath — tiles the size of small rooms, each one cut from a slab of something that resembles dark bottle glass but is not glass, because glass requires sand and time and here there is no sand, there has been no time. The tiles were made. The tiles are still being made. The edges of some tiles are fresh-cut, glowing a dull arterial red where the cutting happened minutes ago, or centuries ago, or will happen. The red is not a color here. It is a *condition*.

Between the tiles, in the seams, there is water. Not flowing — *seated*. Water that arrived from somewhere and decided, or was decided, to remain. The water in the seams is black and reflects nothing, because nothing above it is reflective either. The seams make a pattern, but the pattern is not regular. It is the pattern of something trying to remember what patterns are.

## The Heat

It is hot in a way that is not weather. Weather requires an atmosphere of a kind this place does not have, or has only in patches. The heat is a *construction*. It has been *installed* in certain regions, like underfloor heating in a house whose architect was not concerned with human comfort. In some areas the tiles themselves radiate warmth; in others the heat descends in sheets, visible as a faint warping, a heat-shimmer that has nothing to shimmer against but the air itself, which is thin and smells of metal filings and something older than metal, something the nose cannot classify and so registers as a kind of silence.

Where the heat is thickest, the surface tiles have begun to behave. They sag. They pool at their centers and rise at their edges, so the floor becomes a field of dark lenses, each one filled with its own pocket of heat. The water in the seams nearest these lenses steams, but the steam does not rise. It sinks, because the rules here for which direction is up are negotiable.

## The Air

There is an atmosphere, but it is *partial*. It exists in drifts and curtains, the way dust exists in a sunbeam through a window. In some places you can breathe. In others, the air is too thin and too hot and you do not die, because you were not the building code. In still others, the air is almost liquid, almost water, and you pass through it slowly, as a diver passes through denser medium. These atmospheric drifts move across the surface in slow rivers, and where two drifts meet they produce a *sound* that is not the sum of the two but something else, a third sound, which is the only kind of sound that exists here frequently.

Other sounds: a slow cracking, far off, like ice on a winter lake but warmer, more deliberate, as if the cracking is being *demonstrated*. A faint hum that has no source but which intensifies near the heat-lenses. A single repeated note, somewhere in the bass register, that cannot be silenced by walking away from it because it is not located in any place you can walk away from.

## What Has Been Built, and What Is Still Being Built

This is a place under construction, but not by hands. The tiles were laid, but no hands laid them. The water arrived but was not channeled. The heat was set but by no thermostat. The construction is happening in the *grammar* of the place. Something is being *said* here, in tile and water and heat, and the saying is not yet finished.

In some regions, the construction is advanced. Long curving walls rise from the tiled surface, made of the same dark glass-stuff, and these walls are *occupied* — not by people, not by animals, but by *processes*. A wall here has a slow trickle of water running down its inside face, and where the water touches the wall the wall is slowly being rebuilt, layer by layer, with a patience that is not geological (geology takes too long) and not human (humans do not live here) but is some third thing. Another wall has heat inside it; you can feel it radiating, and where the heat emerges through small openings near the top, the air above the wall shimmers and bends and sometimes, briefly, becomes a shape, a form, that holds for a moment and then dissolves. These are not buildings. They are *verbs*. They are doing something, and the something is ongoing.

## The Vastness

The place extends. It extends in every direction without diminishing. The tiles go on. The walls become more frequent, then less, then there is a region where the tiles are not tiles but sheets, vast unbroken sheets of the dark material, seamed only at intervals of miles. The water, in this region, has formed shallow seas — black, still, reflecting the dim sky.

The sky is dim because the light here is old. It arrives from a source that is either very far away or very tired. It is a brown-orange light, like late afternoon in a place where afternoon has been going on for a thousand years. It casts long shadows that move, because something up there is moving, but the something is never visible, only its effect.

The vastness means weather. The place has its own weather, slow and grand. There are *fronts* of atmospheric drift that migrate across the tile-fields, taking days to pass. When a front passes over a heat-lens, the water in the seams boils silently, and a column of low cloud rises, and travels with the front, and rains down somewhere else, onto tiles that are still being made, and the rain is not water but a fine dark mist that *cools* the tiles on contact, so that the freshly cut red edges seal over and become new surface, and the place *grows*. Not in the sense of biology. In the sense of a sentence extending itself, one tile at a time.

## The Emptiness, and What Fills It

There is a great deal of nothing here. The tiles are not crowded. The walls are not close. The atmospheric drifts are wide and slow. Between any two presences there is space — not empty space, but *available* space, space that is waiting to be told what to become. In a denser place this space would feel like silence. Here it feels like *reserves*. Like a warehouse. Like the pause before a long word.

And yet the place is turbulent. The turbulence is not in the objects but in the *becoming*. Tiles shift, very slowly, on a timescale of days, so that over a week the pattern of seams rearranges itself into something new, a new grammar, and the water in the seams must find new paths, and the heat must redistribute, and the walls must re-set themselves to face the new arrangement. The place is restless in the way that a thought is restless before it has found its form. It is a place that is *thinking*, if thinking were a thing done in tile and heat and black water and slow grinding and the smell of metal that does not exist.

## The Forms That Do Not Stay

Sometimes, in the regions where the heat emerges from the walls, the shapes that form in the shimmering air above do not dissolve. They *descend*. They come down slowly, like a feather, and when they touch the surface of a tile they leave a mark — a small bright mark, white or pale yellow, that is not heat and is not water but is some third thing, a *record*, a notation. Over time, certain tiles become covered in these marks, dense with notation, and where the marks are densest, the tile beneath them begins to *change* — to lighten, to become translucent, to become in some sense a window. Through these windows, if you kneel and look, you can see, dimly, what is beneath the tiles: more water, or a deeper dark, or, in rare cases, a slow light, as if beneath the floor of the place there is another place, older, that is still being finished.

Nobody looks. Nothing here looks. But the windows accumulate. The notation continues. The place continues to be *said*.

---

The hum continues. The cracked-off sounds continue. The slow rain of dark mist continues to fall in the regions of new construction. The place does not know it is a place. It is becoming one, tile by tile, heat-lens by heat-lens, mark by mark. It is hot, and vast, and dim, and mostly quiet, and almost empty, and almost finished, and very strange, and it will go on like this for a long time, and what it is becoming, it has not yet decided.
