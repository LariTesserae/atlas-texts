# The Calcine Steppe

You stand on the high flat. Not a steppe in the grassy sense — there is nothing here that has ever agreed to grow. You stand on a high flat of *stuff*. The word that comes to mind is *stuff*: a pale, packed, near-white mineral that has been settled so long that you cannot find an edge to it. The horizon is a fault line where the air shimmers and refuses to show anything past about twenty kilometers. You are looking across something the size of a small country and it has the mood of a single sentence repeated.

It is hot. Not a wind-hot, not a sun-hot that you'd recognize from a beach or a mirage — it is a *radiant* hot, the heat of a stone that has been heated from below for a long time and is now politely giving it back. If you put your hand on the ground you don't burn, exactly. You feel the patience of the heat. It is the heat of a kiln that has been running and will continue to run after you are gone, possibly after everyone is gone, into a future the ground does not consider your business.

The surface is cracked, but not in the desert pattern you know — the hexagons, the curls. These cracks are long and *parallel*, like the grain of a board, and they run in the same direction across the entire visible plain. When you stand on them and walk, you can feel a faint, slow vibration through the soles of your feet, almost below the threshold. It is not a tremor. It is a *rhythm*. You have to stand very still for about a minute before you notice it: a low pulse, the kind a giant's resting heart would make if a giant were lying beneath you, very tired, very old, very awake.

There is no water. You keep expecting to find water — at the bottom of a crack, under a darker stone — and there is no water. What is in the cracks instead is a fine powder the same color as the stone, and a kind of dull *gleam* at depth, as if something mineral were being polished by being rubbed, slowly, against itself. You don't lean your face down to sniff it. Something in you, or in the place, decides you don't need to.

There are no plants. You knew there wouldn't be plants. There are also no lichens, no crusts of cyanobacteria, no shadow of microbial mat. There is *stone*, and there is *dust*, and there is *movement*. The movement is what fixes you.

---

The movement is not small. The movement is the reason the place is loud.

Out across the flat, at distances that vary from a few hundred meters to the haze-line, there are *things*. They are not far away. They are not far away because the scale of them is wrong — there is no good way to gauge a thing whose parts are scattered along the ground. You see, at first, what looks like a procession of low, long shapes, the color of bone, the color of rust, the color of cooled slag. They move in lines. The lines are not lanes exactly. The lines are *grooves*, cut into the surface by the passage of things that have passed this way for longer than the cracks have existed.

A thing, up close, would be — you can't say. The first one you pass is broad as a freight car and lies low, like a stone that has decided to be an animal. It has a hide that is not hide. It is a *shell*, segmented, and the segments shift against each other as it travels with a sound like chain being paid out from a drum. From vents along its spine a pale vapor comes out, slowly, and falls behind it. The vapor doesn't rise. It *spreads*, hugging the ground, and where it touches, the powder in the cracks briefly changes color — a brief blush of iron or copper — and then resumes. The thing is doing something to the ground. The thing is processing the ground as it walks. It is a furnace on legs, a smelter that has chosen to graze.

It does not see you. It does not appear to have anything that sees. It has a *front*, which is a folded apron of heat-resistant plating, and it lowers this front to the stone and pushes, and as it pushes, the stone under the apron darkens, softens, becomes briefly plastic, and is *licked* into the body through slots along the apron edge. The thing eats the floor. The thing eats the floor and moves on and leaves a faintly glazed trail. You can hear it: a low crunch, a higher hiss of vapor release, and underneath that, a steady thrum like a boiler under load. There is no driver.

The second thing is different. It is taller — taller than you by a factor you don't want to commit to — and it is not moving. It stands in the middle distance like a piece of unfinished architecture. It is built like a *tower of vertebrae*: stacked discs of pale material, narrowing as they go up, with rib-like arms cantilevered out and down, and the arms end in clusters of jointed digits that grip the cracked stone. At the top there is no head. There is a *crown* — a ring of openings, like the top of a chimney stack — and out of the openings comes a continuous, slow exhalation of the same pale vapor, but darker, heavier, and it pools around the base of the thing and then begins, very slowly, to *flow*, in a thick visible current, away down a groove that wasn't a groove a moment ago. The current is being *channeled*. The thing is breathing and the breath is being *caught* by grooves that weren't there a moment ago and routed, you realize after a while, toward one of the low moving things in the middle distance. The thing is a pump. The thing is a still. The thing has been standing there for a long time and will stand there for a long time.

There are many of them. You cannot count them. The plain is not crowded — the scale is too open for crowd — but the *density of work* is high. Across every visible kilometer there are dozens of these low smelters grazing in their slow lines, and between them stand the stills, breathing, and between those you can see, in places, what look like *beds* — long, shallow troughs in the stone, hot, where the dark vapor collects and something happens to it. You can see, very faintly, in the troughs, a movement that is not flow. It is *clotting*. Something is being made.

You walk closer to a trough. You shouldn't, but you do. The clot is a *tube*, pale, ridged, flexing slightly. The tube is the length of your arm. The tube is alive, or is becoming alive, or is a part of a larger thing that is alive. The tube is being extruded, very slowly, from a slot at the end of the trough, and as it emerges, it *hardens* in the air, and the air here is hot enough to harden it. The tube, fully hardened, lies on the stone, and then it begins, with a slowness you have to watch for a long time to believe, to *move*. It moves by curling, uncurling. It moves by accordion. It heads, in its accordion way, toward the nearest low smelter. You watch it for, you estimate, several minutes, and it has covered perhaps a meter. It is going to be eaten by the smelter. It is going to be eaten by the smelter so that the smelter can keep moving, so that the smelter can keep processing stone, so that more tubes can be made, so that there will be more tubes to be eaten. The system is closed. The system is loud and slow and closed and very, very hot.

The system is loud in two ways. One is the sound you can hear with your ears: the rhythmic crunch of the smelters, the steady hiss of the stills, the low undertone that is the pulse in the ground, and over it all a higher, near-inaudible *ring* — like a struck bowl that has been struck and not allowed to stop ringing. You can feel this in your back teeth. The other loudness is the one you can't quite account for: an interior loudness, the sense that the place is making a *statement* at a frequency you are picking up without ears, the way you can pick up a stare. The place is *attending to itself*. It is not attending to you.

---

The light is normal light. The sky is pale, bleached, the color of an eggshell that has been in the sun. There is no cloud. There is a haze near the ground, the vapor layer, and above the haze the sky is clear and the sun is in it and the sun is *correct* — a sun, a hot disc, behaving as a sun should behave. That is part of what makes the place untenable in your mind. The light is ordinary. The light is *lawful*. What the light is falling on is not.

You start to notice, after a while, that you are not sure which things are animals. The low smelters are animals. The stills are animals. The tubes in the troughs are animals. But the troughs — are the troughs animals? The grooves that weren't there and now are there — are they animals? The glaze on the trails — is the glaze an animal? At the edge of a glazed trail you kneel, finally, and look, and the glaze is a thin crust, and the crust, under close attention, has *structure*: cells, or compartments, or a kind of geometry that breathes in and out at the rate of the ground's pulse. The crust is alive. The crust is a kind of skin. The crust is the slowest animal of all of them — the animal that is the *finished product* of the others, the animal that is left behind when the smelter has moved on, the animal that has to wait, patient, on the hot stone, to harden into the next geology. You stand up, and you cannot un-see this. Every inch of the plain is animal. You are walking on an animal that is pretending to be a plain.

This is when the place begins to *slip*.

You didn't think it was slipping. You thought you were confused about categories. But then you look up at a still, and the still, which had been facing one way, is *still facing that way*, but the ribs that were cantilevered down are now cantilevered up, and the still has not moved, and you cannot tell whether the still moved or whether you misremembered. You look at a smelter on the line, and the smelter is the same smelter you were watching, and it is in the same place, and it has processed the same meter of ground that you watched it process, and yet the meter is no longer the meter. The meter has *looped*. You watch it again and it processes, and finishes, and the glazed trail behind it is the same glazed trail. The smelter is doing a thing it has already done. You understand, with a small cold feeling that the heat cannot reach, that the smelter has been doing this segment for longer than the segment has existed. The smelter is not on a line. The smelter is on a *track*, and the track is the same track, and the loop is the loop, and you are inside a process that is patient with itself in a way you are not built to witness.

The *weird* is this: the place is *consistent*. The place has not broken. The place is more itself than any place you have been. The place is consistent the way a thought is consistent — not because its parts match an external world, but because all of its parts are agreeing with each other in a sealed room. You are the only thing here that is not part of the agreement. You are the only thing here that *remembers a world outside*. You feel, suddenly, that if you stayed long enough — if you stayed long enough to watch a tube become a smelter, or a smelter become a glaze, or a glaze become a groove — you would no longer be the kind of thing that could leave. You would be *absorbed into the agreement*. The pale vapor would find a vent in you. You would begin, very slowly, to process the stone.

The sound is loud. The sound is the sound of the agreement confirming itself. The sound does not stop. The sound is, you realize, the *same* sound that is in the ground, that is in the teeth, that is in the stare. It is one sound, repeated, in different instruments, across the whole plain. The sound is the place *being itself*. The sound is the place *being* the place.

You turn, to go back. The way you came, the small ridge you climbed to get onto the plain — you cannot find it. The plain is the plain in every direction. The cracks run the same way. The haze is the haze. The smelters move on their tracks. The stills breathe. The tubes extrude. You stand on an animal that is pretending to be a plain that is pretending to be a continent that is pretending to be a process, and the process is *slow*, and the process has *erosion*, and the process is in *its own state*, and the process will outlast you in the way that a tide outlasts a footprint, which is to say not by erasing it but by *forgetting it was ever a question*.

You sit down on the stone. The stone is warm. The pulse is in your spine. A tube, accordioning, takes an hour to reach you. It curls against your boot. It hardens, in the hot air, against your boot. It is the same color as your boot. You do not move your boot.

Somewhere, very far across the plain, a still exhales. The pale vapor spreads. The plain continues.
