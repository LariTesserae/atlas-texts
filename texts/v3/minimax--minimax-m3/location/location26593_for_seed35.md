# The Still Cathedral

The air here is dry as fired clay. It moves in slow, heated columns — you can feel them on your skin the way you feel sun-warmed walls in a closed room, only the warmth is coming from below and from every side at once. There is no breeze. The chamber is sealed; it has been sealed for a long time, perhaps forever. Nothing leaks in. Nothing leaks out. The warmth is the warmth of the place itself, maintained at the same temperature since before anything living arrived.

The light is the color of cooling iron. A dim, reddish, sodium-orange wash that has no visible source and casts no real shadows — everything is lit but nothing is clear. Your pupils keep trying to dilate. They cannot make the room bright. It will not become bright. The light is the light of hot metal seen through soot, and it has been this light for as long as the chamber has held air.

You are below the surface. The roof above you is not flat — it is a broken cone, like the inside of a tooth, with sharp facets of young stone that have not yet had time to weather. The rock was new when it was exposed, and it is still new. Nothing here rounds off. Nothing softens. Every edge is a knife edge that has been a knife edge for unmeasured time, because erosion, in this place, has been somehow *suspended*. The rock does not decay. The mineral faces are still mirror-bright where they were sheared. The fractures that opened the chamber into being — for the chamber was excavated, or revealed, or uncovered — still hold their original geometry, unchanged, as if time in this pocket has been told to wait outside and not come in.

The chamber is not enormous. It is the size of a cathedral — a single vaulted space you could walk across in fifteen minutes if you could walk across it, which you cannot, because it is filled.

---

It is filled with engines.

Not arranged. *Filed.* They stand shoulder to shoulder, layered three and four deep, climbing each other like a stopped mob. Some are small as a man's chest. Some are taller than you. They have housings of riveted plate, of ceramic, of something that might once have been a green-painted iron but is now a uniform black-brown from heat. The housings are old and the housings are new — they are not corroded, they are not worn. They are at steady state. They will never corrode further. They will never wear further. They are exactly as they were at the moment the chamber reached equilibrium, and equilibrium has not moved in all the time since.

The engines run.

This is the strange thing. The engines run. Every one of them. You can see the couplings turning through their observation windows — slow rotations, reciprocations, the disciplined motion of pistons and cams and eccentric wheels. Some of them have fans that spin. Some of them have belts that travel. Some have pistons that rise and fall, rise and fall. They run at the same speed they have always run. They have always run at this speed. Nothing accelerates. Nothing slows. The motion is a held motion, like a film looped onto itself.

The sound is enormous. It is a *wall* of sound. The chamber holds it; the chamber is shaped to hold it; the sound does not die against the walls but is absorbed and re-radiated and absorbed again, so that what reaches your ears is not the source noise but the room's own resonance — a deep, full, throbbing hum, like being inside a struck bell that has been struck once and never stopped ringing. It presses on your chest. It makes your teeth itch. It is the loudest thing here, and the loudest thing here has always been exactly this loud.

The engines are not producing anything.

This is the second strange thing, and it is the more important one. The engines drive shafts, and the shafts drive gears, and the gears drive wheels, and the wheels turn, and the turning goes nowhere. Power flows into the system and power leaves the system and the leaving and the arriving are equal, and so the chamber is neither heating nor cooling, neither filling nor emptying, neither progressing nor regressing. Each engine is doing the work of driving the next engine. The whole chamber is one closed thermodynamic loop, a machine made of machines, and it has been running and undone-running since it was switched on. There is no purpose. There never was a purpose other than the running. The running *is* the state.

---

In the machines live the creatures.

They are pale. They are pale the way deep-cave fish are pale, or the way grubs under a stone are pale — flesh-colored, translucent, soft-bodied, with no pigment because there has never been any light bright enough to need it. They have many legs, or none. They have small mouths and they have large mouths. Some have wings that are too small for their bodies, used for balance only. Some have no eyes at all; some have eyes that are wide and wet and seem to stare at nothing in particular, because in this reddish dim there is nothing particular to stare at.

There are *many* of them. They are abundant beyond what the space seems able to hold, and yet the space holds them. They are layered in the same way the engines are layered — bodies on bodies, on pipes, on housings, on the narrow strips of stone floor still visible between machines. They crawl across each other without conflict. They eat something — the fungi that grow on the warm metal, perhaps, or the dust that the grinding produces, or each other, or nothing visible at all, because the system is in equilibrium and what one creature loses another gains and the total biomass is what the total biomass is. They have been at this population since the chamber reached its state. No more. No less. When one dies, its body is consumed within minutes by the others, and the matter returns to the system, and the system does not notice.

They are warm to the touch. They are loud — they are *part* of the loud. They click and rasp and hum themselves, and their sounds blend into the machine noise until you cannot tell whether the engines are running or the creatures are running, whether the chamber is mechanical or zoological, whether the engine is the thing of metal or the thing of flesh.

---

The plants are few.

Sparse, sparse. You can see them only when you look closely, and when you look closely you wish you hadn't. There is a kind of moss that grows on the *undersides* of the engines, where the heat is steady and the light is dimmest. There are dark, almost-black fungal fans pressed against the cooler stones of the broken ceiling. There are small succulent rosettes — impossibly, in a place without water — tucked into the cracks between a machine's foot and the floor. They are fed, presumably, by the same closed equilibrium that feeds the creatures: moisture from combustion, minerals from wear, carbon from somewhere, all cycling, all returning, all unchanged.

Where the engines stand there are *marks*.

Not buildings. Not structures. Marks. A column built of stacked bricks that goes nowhere and stops at shoulder height. A stair of cut stone that climbs four steps and ends against a wall. A rail set into the floor for fifty feet and then stops at a riveted plate. Iron rings set into the rock at intervals, with rope or chain or wire attached to some of them, leading off into the machines and disappearing. A small brass plaque mounted on a housing, the characters on it worn smooth by heat so that you cannot read them, only feel them with your fingertips. A doorway, bricked up, with the brick still showing the trowel marks of the person who laid it. These are *scattered*. They are the residue of intention — of someone who wanted to go somewhere, or label something, or close something off. The intentions were abandoned, or completed, or lost. Only the marks remain. The marks do not change.

---

The chamber is the same. The chamber has been the same. If you left now and returned in any amount of time, you would find the engines at the same phase of their rotation. The creatures at the same density in the same places. The same moss on the same undersides. The same bricked door with the same trowel marks. The same red light. The same loud hum. The same warmth. The same dry, mineral, iron-tasting air. The same.

It is loud. It is warm. It is dry. It is dim. It is dense. It is still.

It is full of machines and full of life and full of nothing ever happening, ever, and the nothing that is happening is, if you stay long enough, the most overwhelming presence in the room.
