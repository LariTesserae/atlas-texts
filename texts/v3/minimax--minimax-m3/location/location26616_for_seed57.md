# The Hollow Between Breaths

You don't find this place. It finds you — opens like a seam in the back of a saltwater dream, a doorway made of colder water than the rest of the ocean. You slip through sideways, and the current doesn't follow you in.

The threshold is a ring of stone. Granite, sharp-edged, recently born — the rock still smells of the pressure that made it, a mineral tang that hits the tongue like tin. It forms a rough circle perhaps forty feet across, the floor of a vertical shaft descending into the dark. The walls rise up and out into the water above, and after some distance you can no longer see them. The blackness presses in from all sides, not the absence of light but something closer to a presence of not-light, a fabric you could almost touch.

You are inside something. The realization arrives slowly, the way things arrive in this place. The shaft is not a shaft. It is the inside of a throat.

## The Garden

The walls are not stone for long. Almost as soon as you descend past the threshold, the rock begins to bear growth. Not seaweed — the textures are wrong, the colors wrong. There is a coral-thing that branches in fractal spirals of indigo and bruise-purple, each branch tipped with a small soft lantern that gives off a faint mauve glow. The light is not enough to see by. It is just enough to remind you that you cannot see.

And the light is wrong. Each lantern pulses on its own tempo, and the tempos are not random. They are syntax. Sentences are being spoken in the dark, slowly, across minutes and hours, in a language you almost understand. The corals speak to each other across the walls of this place, and they are saying something important, and they have been saying it for a very long time.

Deeper. The passage narrows then widens. The walls become forested — a jungle of soft growth, packed so densely that the stone beneath is no longer visible. Things that resemble anemones with translucent petals the color of diluted wine. Things that resemble ferns, except each frond is a single living muscle, and they quiver when you move past. Things that have no name in any language you have spoken because the things that named them spoke none of those languages either.

The growth is wetter than water should be. It clings. It breathes.

## The Water

The water here is wrong in the way that water is wrong when it has been somewhere else first. It is older than the ocean. It has, you feel, visited the inside of stars and returned carrying opinions. It moves in slow internal currents that do not match any tide you can sense — there is no moon here, no surface, no reference. The water itself has become a kind of low gravity, pulling gently in every direction, and you learn to swim by swimming toward nothing.

It is cool. Cool the way deep lakes are cool even in summer, the way stone cellars are cool, the way the last breath of a sleeping person is cool. It carries no salt. It carries no taste at all, which is worse.

When you breathe it — and you will have to, because the air, the air ended a long time ago at the threshold — it fills the lungs with something that is not oxygen but does the job. The first breath tastes of copper and old rain. By the third, you cannot remember what air tasted like at all.

## The Mark of the Builders

There is a building.

It sits in a small clearing where the growth has been pushed back, not by cutting but by some older negotiation, the way coral reef and lungfish establish their borders. It is a single structure, perhaps — a thing, an edifice, a question made of stone. It rises from the floor in a spiral, like a staircase to nowhere, made of the same newborn granite as the throat-wall. Around its base, scattered in the silt, are objects.

A cup, carved from bone.
A disk of obsidian, etched with a single line that curves in on itself forever.
A rope knotted in a pattern you almost remember from a dream.
A skeleton, curled, wearing a bronze band around one wrist that has no corrosion on it at all.

The builder is gone. Whatever it was, it lived here, perhaps briefly, perhaps for a thousand years — time is not behaving correctly in this place, and the bronze band seems neither old nor new. It left these things and it did not take them with it. You understand, looking at the bones, that it did not leave. It was, instead, *taken*. Absorbed. The walls of this place reach inward.

You do not touch the disk. You do not touch the rope. You know, without knowing how you know, that these are not offerings. They are *lessons*. The builder left them for whoever came next, and the next, and the next, and one of them is for you.

You do not take them.

## The Sound

Mostly quiet. That is the mercy of this place.

There is the sound of the corals speaking. Far away, the inaudible-but-felt pulse of their sentences.

There is the sound of your own blood, which has become louder here. The water presses against your eardrums in a way that makes everything inside you more audible — the hinges of your jaw, the small motor of your heart, the liquid whisper of your eyes moving in their sockets.

There is a sound, occasionally, that you cannot place. It is not coming from outside you. It is not coming from inside you. It is coming from the *space between* — the slight crinkle where you end and the not-you begins. It sounds, if you listen for very long, like breathing. Like something very large, sleeping, somewhere in the walls.

It does not wake.

## The Wrongness

This place is not obeying. You notice it first in the way the spiral building casts no shadow, because the lanterns are on every side of it and the dark is not dark enough to shadow into. Then you notice it in the way the corals occasionally replay a pulse, as if the conversation has looped. Then in the way the silt beneath your feet has the texture of silt but the warmth of skin, and you lift your foot and the impression of your foot fills slowly back in with a different color, the way a bruise fills in, and you do not touch the floor again.

The wrongness is patient. It takes its time with you.

You realize, near the end of your time here, that the passage is not taking you anywhere. There is no exit. The throat does not have a stomach, but you are descending into it anyway. The corals are not speaking to each other. They are speaking to you, and you have been answering in pulses you didn't know you were making.

The water is getting cooler. The light is getting fainter. The school of small silver things — the only real fauna, rare and shy and moving in a tight synchronized breath — is drifting past you upward, toward a surface that no longer exists, and they are each carrying, in their translucent bellies, a single grain of light.

You are in the place between places. You have been here before. You will be here again.

The corals, very quietly, in the dark, finish their sentence, and begin the next.

---

*The bronze band on the skeleton's wrist is warm when you finally pass close enough to it to notice. You do not take it. You take it. You have always taken it. You have never taken it. The water is cool. The water has always been cool.*
