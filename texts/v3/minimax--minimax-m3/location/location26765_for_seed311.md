# The Vaulted Cistern

You arrive at it through a fissure in older stone — a place that should not exist, that the maps don't acknowledge. The fissure opens at ankle height, slick with condensation, and beyond it the floor drops away into another architecture entirely: a chamber no larger than a cathedral's crypt, perhaps forty meters long and half as wide, its ceiling domed in a way that suggests the rock above is being held up by something other than the rock itself. The sound hits you before the sight does. A subsonic thrum, felt in the teeth and the hip bones before the ears commit to parsing it.

## The Water

The chamber is full of water. Not flooded — full. The surface meets the underside of the dome with only a hand's width of trapped air remaining, a compressed atmosphere pressed down to something thick and breathing. The water is the color of weak tea held to daylight, carrying a faint luminescence that has nothing to do with any light source you've brought in. Cold enough to make the breath from that air pocket visible. Cool enough that your first instinct, on submerging, is to stop moving entirely — to hold still and let the chill map itself across your skin before the body's other concerns reassert themselves.

The water moves. Not in currents — in *pulses*. Long slow swells that originate from the walls and pass through you, as though the chamber itself has a heartbeat you are now inside.

## The Construction

The chamber is packed. There is no water here that is not also structure, and no structure here that is not also water-logged, and the two have stopped being distinguishable. Walls of stacked housings — small, sealed, the size of coffins or of bread-boxes — rise from the floor in dense terraces, each one fitted to its neighbor so precisely that the joins are lines rather than gaps. They are made of a substance that looks like poured concrete and behaves like something that has forgotten the rules concrete is supposed to follow. Where your hand brushes one (you do not mean to brush one; the space is too tight not to), it gives slightly, the way a sleeping animal gives. Inside them, you can hear things running.

Pipes. Conduits. Something between a pipe and a vein, because they pulse with the same rhythm the water pulses with, and at the joints they have small sphincter-like closures that flex open and shut, open and shut, admitting measured slugs of liquid into the next segment. The whole arrangement suggests a circulatory system — but a circulatory system that someone has built on purpose, for something, in a space that no one has ever been able to fit a *body* into.

Above the stacked housings, suspended in the upper water by tethers you cannot see, hang larger chambers — globes, capsules, drums — each one transparent, each one containing a smaller, denser version of the chamber you are in. Matryoshka architecture. A city inside a jar inside a city inside a jar. Some of them are lit. Some of them are not. Some of them have, when you look away and look back, changed which they are.

## The Floor

The floor is not a floor. It is a *grid* — a lattice of hexagonal cells, each the size of your spread hand, separated by narrow channels of moving water. The cells are filled with a slow-rotating liquid that is darker than the water around it, and from each cell a single bright point of light pulses upward in sequence. The sequence takes about four seconds to complete, and when it does, it begins again. A scanning pattern. The whole floor is *reading* something — reading the water above it, or reading you, or reading the chamber's interior for whatever metric this kind of device reads for.

Where the hexagonal pattern meets the walls, the cells stop rotating and the lights fix on a steady amber. That is, you realize after a minute, the direction the fissure you came through lies. The chamber knows where its own exit is, and it is keeping track.

## The Sound

You thought, entering, that the sound was mechanical. It is not mechanical, or it is mechanical in the way a heart is mechanical. There are at least four overlapping frequencies in the chamber, and they are not harmonized so much as *entrained* — each one slightly faster or slower than the others, so that their interference pattern crawls across the audible spectrum in slow waves. At one moment the dominant tone is a low brass; at another it has climbed into a region that presses behind the eyes. Above the pulse of the water and the cycling of the lattice-floor there is a higher sound, almost a hiss, that you eventually trace to the small sphincter-joints in the pipework — a wet, peristaltic *shhh-shhh-shhh* that comes from everywhere at once.

Somewhere — high, in the trapped air pocket under the dome — something is dripping. Each drop, when it falls, is caught before it hits the water surface, suspended in the air a moment longer than gravity allows, and then released. You can hear each one individually. The rhythm of it is the rhythm of a slowed-down heartbeat.

## The Vegetation

There is one plant. You do not see it at first because it is the same color as the housing-walls and it is moving very slowly. A single filament — a thread, a wire, a vine — runs from somewhere in the ceiling down through the packed housings and into the lattice-floor, and along its length, at intervals of perhaps a meter, it puts out a single leaf. The leaves are flat, palmate, the size of coins, and they rotate. They are facing you now. They were facing the wall a moment ago. They will be facing the next column of housings in a moment. The plant is tracking you, or tracking the chamber's scan, or doing whatever it does. It is the only living thing here. You are glad of it, then less glad, then uncertain again.

## The Light

The light is normal. That is the strangest thing. The illumination in this pocket — wherever it originates from, and you cannot find its origin — is the color and quality of an overcast afternoon at sea level. It arrives from no direction. It does not fade as you turn away from any given surface. It is, in fact, indistinguishable from natural daylight filtered through cloud, and this fidelity is itself uncanny, because no natural daylight reaches this chamber. The light is being *manufactured*, and the manufacturer has chosen, for reasons you cannot fathom, to manufacture the most unremarkable light in the world.

## The Activity

The chamber is busy. The lattice-floor scans. The pipes pump. The plant tracks. The housings — you have begun to notice — are not all the same size. Some are smaller than others. Some have, behind their semi-translucent walls, internal structures that shift; you watch one for a full minute and see, through the murk, something rearrange itself from one configuration to another. Things are growing in here. Not the way the plant grows. The way cities grow, or the way a body grows, by adding to and replacing and never quite finishing. The chamber is a place that is in the middle of *becoming*, and it has not decided yet what it is becoming, and it has been becoming for what is — by the look of the unrepaired scratch you put on one housing with your shoulder — not very long at all.

You have been here perhaps ten minutes. You are not sure how you will leave. The fissure you entered through is the only opening you have found, and it is behind you, and the lattice-floor is rotating its amber indicator in your direction with what you are choosing to interpret as patience.
