# The Workshop on the High Shoulder

It begins, as such places do, with a wrongness of scale. The bowl is small — small enough that if you stood at its rim and shouted, the echo would come back before the last syllable died. Roughly oval, roughly forty meters across at its widest, ringed on three sides by the raw spine of a mountain that has not yet decided what shape it wants to be. The fourth side opens onto nothing but air and the suggestion of further mountains somewhere in the cloud below. The bowl is high. A breath comes thin here. A breath comes warm here, too, which is its own strangeness — the warmth rises through the floor as though the rock beneath has a temperature the air above has not yet noticed, or has been told to ignore.

The rock is new. That is the first thing to understand, and the hardest. It is new the way an egg is new — finished, sealed, holding something, but not yet hatched. Its color is the color of things that have only just been delivered from the mantle: a deep charcoal where the light catches it at certain angles, a black with a violet undertone when you tilt your head, a green-black like deep water where the wet gathers in seams. The seams run everywhere. They run in patterns that are not quite patterns. They are too regular to be fractures and too irregular to be anything drawn. If you kneel and look along one, it eventually resolves into a thin bright line — a hair-fine filament of something that is not the rock, embedded in it, pulsing with a light that does not travel but rests.

This is the place where the rock is becoming itself. The bowl is not worn down. It is being *built up*. Slow. The way frost builds, except in warm air. The way a pearl builds. Layer after layer of something — call it deposition, call it accretion, call it what you will — laid down by whatever process has decided this should be a place. The stone is still hot somewhere far below. The stone is still soft somewhere close above. You can feel both through the soles of your boots: the memory of melt, and the promise of cure.

## Water, Everywhere, Slightly

It rains often. It rains not as weather but as condition. The air is overfull. Water condenses on every surface, on your eyelashes, on the back of your hand, on the underside of leaves that aren't really leaves. Droplets slide down the rock in slow, deliberate lines that do not always agree with gravity. Some climb. Some stand still until a breath dislodges them and they then begin, finally, to fall. There are trickles that come from no visible source, and trickles that come from sources that shift between one observation and the next. The bowl's floor is damp to a depth — not puddled, exactly, but saturated in the way a sponge is saturated. Step and the stone gives a faint compression. The water has weight. The water is warm.

There are pools, of a kind. Small ones, cupped in concavities that may or may not have been there yesterday. The water in them is clear to a degree that is suspect — clear the way a laboratory is clear, clear the way nothing natural is clear. If you look into one and hold still long enough, something in it looks back. Not an animal. Not a face. Just a *regard*.

## Sparse Green, Abundant Motion

The vegetation is shy. It grows only where it must, and only in the cracks where the filaments are thickest. A few things that resemble moss, but are wrong in color — too blue, too grey-green, with a faint internal flicker when you watch them. A few stalks that resemble lichens, but they move, very slowly, toward the brightest seam. A single spindly thing, waist-high, in the far curve of the bowl, with no leaves and a head like a closed fist. It will open eventually. It opens every few hours, briefly, to a pale structure like a flower drawn by someone who had only heard flowers described. Then it closes. Then it waits.

The creatures are not shy. The creatures are everywhere. They live in the wet seams, in the hairline cracks, in the small pools, in the warm air itself. They are numerous in the way insects are numerous — you do not see them individually so much as you see the *event* of them, the texture they give to the world. Things with too many legs, none of them right, scuttling along the rock. Things without legs, gliding. Things that are just small lights moving in patterns. They make the sounds. They are loud.

There is a sound like a paper hinge being turned, somewhere, always. There is a sound like teeth on something softer than teeth should be on. There is a sound like a long breath out, and it is not clear whether any of them is making it. The bowl is loud in the way a tide pool is loud if you put your ear to it — full of small, industrious, purposeful noises, none of which belong to you.

And they eat. They must eat. There is so little green. They eat the rock, you realize after a while, or the water, or the warmth, or the filaments. They are part of the building. They are among the things being built.

## The Filaments and the Fragments

The technology is not present in the way we imagine technology. There are no machines. There are no screens. There are no antennae. There is the rock, and within the rock, the lines. The lines are everywhere. They form a network that is too dense to map by eye and too delicate to touch — touch one and it dimmer for a moment, then returns. Some of them reach the surface. Some of them end at a small, smooth knob of metal-or-not-metal that has weathered into the stone like a fossil. One of these, near the center of the bowl, has been worked into something. A hollow. A seat. A node. It is the only artifact that unambiguously intends. The rest are *given*.

There are a few other things. A pillar, broken, that was once taller than you — it is the color of bone and the wrong color of bone, both at once. A flat plate set into the floor, perfectly level, with an edge that is too crisp to have been cut by anything a person would call a tool. A ring of small stones arranged by something, around a place where nothing grows and the water does not condense. Each of these is quiet. Each of these was probably not made here.

## Light, Sound, the Rest of It

The light is dim but it does not dim. It is the light of a cloud-covered high place at midday — grey, even, sourceless. But within it, the filaments pulse. The moss-flickers pulse. The closed fist pulses. The water pulses, faintly, with its own slow heartbeat. The bowl is full of small lights that add up to a presence, the way stars add up to a sky.

The sound is loud, and the loudness is not noise. It is a working sound. It is the sound of a place doing what it does, all the time, in every layer, and you are standing in the middle of it, a visitor whose presence has not yet been processed. The mountain, around the bowl, is silent. The bowl is the only busy place for a long way. The bowl is the only busy place, perhaps, for a very long way in every direction.

It is warm. It is wet. It is small. It is loud. It is being made. Whatever is making it is patient, and has been here a long time, and will be here longer. The closed fist will open again in a few hours. The water will run, briefly, up the side of the broken pillar. Something in one of the pools will look at you without being an animal, again. And the rock will lay down another thin, invisible layer of itself, beneath your feet, while you watch.

You can stay. You can leave. The place will continue. The place is not yet finished with becoming what it is.
