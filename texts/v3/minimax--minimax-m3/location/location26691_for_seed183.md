# The Weeping Plateau of the First Glass

---

You come up out of the last stairway of clouds and the world flattens. Not flat — the ground still heaves, fractured, tilted, piled — but the sky is so wide and so close that it takes the depth out of everything. The light comes from everywhere. It bounces off the wet rock and comes back at you off-color, off-angle, off-time. You cannot find a shadow long enough to mean anything by.

You are on a plateau, or a series of plateaus. There is no tree line because there are no trees. There is no tree line because there is no line — the rock keeps going and keeps not going in ways that are difficult to write down. You are somewhere very high and very cold-adjacent, in the cool that is just on the cold side of comfortable, and the air tastes of mineral and of nothing.

## The Rock

The rock is mostly dark. It is the dark of volcanic glass but the wrong kind of glass — too old, too soft, too much weathered. It is layered. The layers do not always agree about which way is up. You see places where the strata curve gently like a page turning, and places where a stratum ends midair with a torn edge, the way an old book ends a chapter.

There are other things in the rock that are not quite rock. Black branching growths come out of the cracks — not plant, not fungus exactly, not mineral in any way you can name. They look like coral drawn by someone who had only heard coral described. They are dense. They are everywhere. They cluster around the seeps and the damp places and the inside corners of stone basins. They are warm to the touch, which is wrong because the rock is cold and the air is colder and nothing here should be warm. They are not alive. You can tell because they don't stop being warm when you pick them, and because when you break one it does not bleed, only rings faintly like a struck bowl.

## The Water

Water is the dominant thing. It is not raining, but it is always raining in the small sense — every surface weeps. The vertical faces of the dark stone run with film. The basins, of which there are very many, hold water that arrived from no sky you can identify. It condenses out of the air in slow threads, or it climbs from somewhere beneath. You watch a stone lip and see a drop form, hang, fall, and another climb up to take its place.

The water is good to drink. It tastes of stone. You drink it anyway because it is the only drink, and after a while it tastes of nothing.

In the larger basins the water has a surface tension problem. It domes. It bulges above the rim of the basin by a thumb's width, by a finger's, before it considers letting go. Sometimes it lets go and runs down the outside of the basin and you have to follow it to see where it goes. It goes to another basin, or onto a slope that is too steep, or into a crack in the rock that seems too narrow. You lose it. You hear it later, somewhere else, finding its way somewhere else.

There is no sea visible. There may not be a sea.

## The Light and the Air

The sky is the wrong color of blue. It is brighter than any sky you have stood under. It does not dim at the horizon; it whitens, then it gets brighter still, then it is no longer describable as a sky and you look away. Looking too long at where the sky ought to end gives you a feeling like your eyes are being rearranged.

The air is thin enough to be a noticeable substance. You breathe and feel the breathing. The light is strong enough that you can see the grain of your hand at noon, the way you'd see it in snow country. Everything reflective reflects too much. The water in the basins glares. The wet rock glares. The black branching growths have a polish to them that picks up whole sections of the world.

The light is loud. It is the loudest thing here until you get used to it, and then it is just under everything, under the wind, under the water, under the humming in the stones.

## The Sound

It is loud. You expected wind at this altitude and you got wind, plenty of it, a steady keening that never fully stops, that steps up and steps down in irregular cycles. You also got the water, which is everywhere running, dripping, plinking, gathering, releasing — a percussion section with no conductor. You got the ringing of the warm growths when the wind hits them right. You got a low note out of the ground itself, a felt-not-heard hum, possibly from the weight of the plateau on whatever is below.

And then there are the other sounds. The ones you do not want to inventory.

A sound like a long page turning, but wet. A sound like a bell struck in a liquid. Sounds that begin as one thing and finish as another. Sometimes a sound that is clearly the voice of a person, very far away, not speaking any word, only exhaling, only present, and then it is not a voice anymore, and then it is gone.

You learn to stop tracking these.

## The Structures

There are structures. Scattered. Not built in the way a city is built. Built the way a person builds a single cairn to mark a place they stood.

You see stacked stones, knee-high, chest-high, in the lee of larger rocks. You see basins carved into flat stones, worn smooth by water that has used them for a long time. You see walls of flat slabs leaned together, more windbreak than shelter. You see a low arch made of fitted pieces that has no mortar and no reason to be standing and is standing. You see a roofless room, three sides, floored with the dark stone, with a sleeping hollow in one corner that fits a human body. You see tool marks. You see cup-marks, dozens of them in a row on a single slab, as if someone was counting something.

There are no people. There are no bodies. There are no bones. There are no pictures of people or anything else. The makers used their hands on the stone and did not write themselves into the stone, only their shapes.

The tools are here. A scatter of them, in the lee of a slab, under a cairn. They are stone. They are flaked, ground, polished. They are not metal. Some of them are almost too good — a blade so sharp you can split a hair with it by accident, a wedge that fits its crack too perfectly, an awl whose point is not quite a point but a small convergence. There is a hand-sized disc of black growth, polished smooth, with a hole in its middle. There is no use you can identify for it. There is no use anyone seems to have identified for it. It is just here.

## The Far

The plateau runs. You walk and you keep finding the same kinds of features: basins, weeps, cairns, growth clusters, the dark torn-edged strata, the wrong light. The distances are slightly wrong. A basin you thought was a quarter-hour away is there in three minutes. A cairn you could have sworn was on the next ridge is in the same valley. You correct for this. You stop trusting your legs. You start trusting the sound of water, which arrives at a more honest pace.

There are places where the rock gets thin. You can see it happening — a slope of dark stone where the underside begins to show through, a ribbed structure like the underside of a mushroom, glowing faintly with the trapped light of the wrong sky. You avoid these places, not out of fear but out of a sense that they are not places. You step around them. The ground is firmer beside them. The water runs faster beside them, as if trying not to slow down.

In one valley you find a place where the rock ends. It just stops, a clean curved edge, and beyond it is a fall of nothing into a bright nothing, and at the bottom of the bright nothing there is more plateau, at the wrong scale, a smaller plateau, a model, a copy of itself, also scattered with cairns and basins, also weeping. You look down at it. It does not look up at you. You move on.

There is, you begin to understand, no edge to this place. There is only the next basin. The next cairn. The next dome of water above its rim. The next long page-turning in the strata. The next held note out of the warm growths in the cracks.

## What Is Going On

You do not know. The rock remembers being hot. The water remembers being something else. The growths in the cracks are not alive but are not dead. The structures were made by hands you cannot find. The light comes from a sun you cannot see.

A wind passes. A basin overflows. A black branch-thing rings. The sky stays wrong. The page keeps turning.

You sit on a worked slab beside a basin you have decided is yours, and you wait for what is not quite a voice to sound again from the wrong distance, and you do not track it, and you do not track it, and you do not track it.
