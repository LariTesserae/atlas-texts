The place is found by going up. Not to a summit but into one. There is a fissure in the glass where the wind has been at work for a thousand years and not yet won, and from that fissure breathes air that is hot and wet and carries a faint sweetness, like heated tin, like the inside of a mouth.

The mountain is young. Geologically thoughtless. The obsidian and glass that compose it still have edges that should not exist — knife-ridges that catch the light a long way off, a thousand years away from being rounded into courtesy. At the elevation where the opening sits, the air is thin enough to taste, and the sun, when it finds you, would be an act of violence. But inside, the rules have already changed.

It is a pocket. A single sealed chamber within the body of the glass, the size of a small chapel, the size of a confession booth, depending on how you count. The walls curve in on themselves the way the inside of a tooth curves, layered — and this is the part that matters — *layered* is the word for it. Terrace above terrace, each one set at a precise height from the others, each one a different material that is in some way the same material. The glass has been worked. Engineered, or *grown*, but grown in a sense that has nothing to do with biology. The structures climb the interior and recede into the interior and there is no seam where construction ends and mountain begins. They are continuous. They are having a long argument with themselves.

The light that enters is a thread. A single line of sun falls from a fissure far overhead, and it travels a long way down through the dim before it lands — on the floor, in a basin, on the back of your hand if you hold it out. Most of the illumination is not that. Most of it is the pale phosphor that lives at the edges of the terraces, a glow without conviction, the color of a fever. It does not quite flicker. You cannot catch it doing it. But you cannot catch it not doing it either.

There is no life in this place. No moss, no lichen, no slow colonization by the patient outside. There is also no death — the glass is sterile, the constructions are sterile, the air has the quality of having been considered. The chamber is not abandoned. Something is using it. Something that does not leave traces in the way that we mean by traces.

The moisture is the moisture of breath. It condenses on the upper terraces, runs down in slow lines, gathers in shallow basins set at irregular intervals along the floor — and then, and this is where the place begins to refuse you, the water *leaves*. Not evaporates. Not soaks. It is gathered back into the chamber and redistributed, on a cycle the chamber seems to keep the way a heart keeps time. The air goes damp against the skin and then dry and then damp again, and in the dryness the heat is more felt. The heat is constant. It is the heat of the chamber's own doing — the heat of the work being done, the heat of operation. It is never the heat of the sun, even at the thread of sun that falls through.

The technology is not technology. There are no wires, no screens, no gears that you can see being gears. There are surfaces that are warm and surfaces that are cool and the relationship between them is exact, and there is a hum that lives below the threshold of hearing, and a higher something that is not a sound but the place where a sound would be if a sound were required. The chamber is active. It is *doing*. The terraces are turning in a way that has nothing to do with visible rotation, or the glass is calibrating itself, or the moisture is being read for some quality that no instrument you carry can name. Something is being kept. Something is being grown in the way that a thought is grown, or a proof, or a long grievance. You cannot see it being done. You can see the basin empty and refill. You can see the phosphor hold its breath. You can see, if you watch long enough, that the air moves in slow currents from terrace to terrace, and the current is not random.

It is quiet. The quiet is not the quiet of nothing happening. It is the quiet of something happening very carefully. The condensation drips. The glass ticks faintly as it cools another fraction of a degree. The hum persists. The thread of sun moves a hand's width across the floor as the hours pass. The smell is ozone and heated stone and, underneath, something almost organic, almost sweet — a trace, a translation, the ghost of a body that never was.

The strangeness is not the strangeness of the unknown. It is the strangeness of the familiar *slightly off*. The chamber is a room. It is the room of someone, or something, and that someone is not present, and the room is waiting the way a room waits, and the room is also doing its work. It is hidden. It is the size of a single room, a single feature, a single thought. It is the high place within the high place, the pocket within the pocket, the glass within the glass.

You stand at the entrance a long time. The heat and the damp press against you. The basin at your feet is full and then is empty and is full again, and you do not see the moment of its emptying. The phosphor at the edges of the terraces holds its quiet light. The hum continues below the threshold of hearing.

You do not enter. You do not need to. The chamber is not for you. But it is there, and it knows it is there, and it is doing something with the moisture and the heat and the dim light that you will never entirely parse, and the not-parsing is part of what the chamber is for.
