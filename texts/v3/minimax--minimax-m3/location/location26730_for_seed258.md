# The Vermillion Hush

You stand at the lip of a valley, but the word *valley* is wrong before you finish thinking it. The land is a cathedral inverted — a long scooped concavity in the earth, its floor hundreds of feet below, and every inch of that floor is growing.

The slopes are not slopes. They are walls. And the walls are not made of stone. They are made of *breath.*

---

**The Substrate**

At first you took it for a forest. Then for a fungus colony. Then for coral. Then for a city. None of these are right, and all of them are.

The valley is lined — floor, walls, and the curling overhangs above — with a single living tissue. It is plant, in the sense that it is green, and photosynthetic, and rooted in moisture. It is also not plant, in that it hums, and warms itself, and grows in deliberate shapes that have nothing to do with light or wind.

The tissue forms the architecture of everything here. There are no stones. There are no wooden beams. There is no soil that is not alive. The walls are ribbed with green-black columns that look like the fused trunks of a hundred cedars. The ceiling is a vaulted canopy, thirty meters high, of long blade-leaves woven so tightly they hold back the rain. The floor is a soft, breathing carpet of moss-pink and bruise-violet, perforated with breathing holes that exhale a warm, sweet fog every few seconds.

The fog is the river. The river is the air. The air is the fog. There is no separate water in this place. The moisture is so total, so woven into the medium of the world, that to speak of a *stream* is to lie. You can drink the air. You can drown in a cupped handful of moss.

---

**The Sound**

It is not quiet. Nothing here is quiet.

A low, sustained chord fills the valley at all times — an A, perhaps, or what would be an A if there were strings. It is the sound of ten thousand small bellows working in slow synchrony. The leaves above you, in the canopy-vault, each have a pore on their underside, and through that pore they breathe. The breathing has a rhythm, and the rhythm is shared, and the shared rhythm produces a tone, and the tone is the valley.

Above the tone, water. Somewhere, always, water is moving. Trickling down the green-black columns. Falling in sheets from the leaves. Beading on every surface and then releasing, one droplet at a time, in irregular percussion. When the wind stirs the canopy, a thousand pocket-falls happen at once and the sound is a hush of applause, then nothing, then the tone again.

A bird calls. Just one. You have heard it for an hour. You do not know if it is the same bird. The valley swallows the answer.

---

**The Built**

You came here expecting ruins. You found that the ruins were still *becoming.*

The columns along the walls are not random. They are fluted. They have capitals. They have, halfway up, a ring of small nodule-leaves that look exactly — exactly — like dentils. The vault above has ribs that meet at bosses. The floor has a long central walk, raised a hand's breadth above the moss, and the walk is made of the same living tissue, but compressed until it is hard as oak and pale as wheat. The walk is straight. The walk leads to a place where the walls close in and form something almost a doorway, and through the doorway is a darker green, and a warmth, and the sense of a room.

You do not enter.

There is a thing on the moss beside the walk. A small, perfectly round stone. It is the only stone. It is the only thing in the valley that is not made of the living green. The moss has grown up to its edges and is considering it. In a year it will be gone.

---

**The Ecology**

There is almost nothing here that walks.

The bird — if it is a bird — is a low gray smudge in the upper leaves. You see it only when it moves. There are insects, but they are quiet, and they live in the moss, and they are the color of the moss. Once, a long pale thing — too long, too limbless — moved through the far end of the valley, between the columns, and you could not tell if it was an animal or a tendril the building-plant had sent out to investigate a sound.

This is the rule of the place: the plant is the world, and the world permits a few guests. The guests are small. The guests are gray. The guests are quiet.

The plant, for its part, is patient. It grows. It builds. It considers the stone. It considers you.

---

**The Light**

It is afternoon. The light enters from above, through the woven ceiling, in long, slanted columns. Where it falls on the moss, the moss glows. Where it falls on the walkway, the pale wood-warm tissue throws it back as a soft cream. The columns at the edges of the valley are half in light, half in deep green-black, and the shadow-line moves as the sun moves, and the moving shadow is almost the only change you can see.

Almost.

The breath. The breath is the second change. Every few seconds, the whole valley *exhales*, and the fog thickens, and the light goes diffuse, and the far columns fade to suggestions. Then the breath goes in, and the air clarifies, and the valley is sharp again. The world, in this place, is breathing itself in and out.

---

**The Air**

Warm. So warm. The wet heat settles on your skin and does not leave. You are inside a lung. The air is sweet with a green, almost-fruity smell — the smell of cut grass, of bruised mint, of the inside of a greenhouse in summer. Beneath that, deeper, the smell of soil, of rot, of something being broken down very slowly into something else.

You taste iron. You do not know why.

---

**The Time**

The bird calls. The breath goes out. The light moves one finger's width along the floor. The moss at the base of the columns grows a millimeter, or you imagine it does. The stone is unchanged. The doorway in the distance breathes its slower breath.

Nothing here is in a hurry. Nothing here will be different tomorrow, or in a year, except in small ways that will take a year to notice. The valley is a thing that has finished becoming, and is now only being. The columns will hold. The vault will hold. The fog will rise and fall. The bird will call.

The stone will be absorbed.

You are the only thing in the valley that is going to leave.

---

**The Rule**

If you stand still long enough, the moss grows around your feet. You feel it first as a warmth, then as a soft pressure, then as a holding. You step away, and the moss closes over the place you stood, and in a week the only sign you were ever here will be a slight thickening of the floor, a slight darkening of the green, a faint human-shaped memory held in the body of the world.

The valley does not eat. The valley does not kill. The valley *keeps.*
