# The Hollow Floor

The place has no name that anyone remembers saying out loud.

It is warm. Not the warmth of sun on skin — there is no sun, has not been for as long as the dust remembers, which is to say forever. The warmth comes from below, rising through the stone in a slow steady exhalation, so that the rock itself is the temperature of a sleeping animal's flank. You stand on it and it almost answers you. Almost.

The ceiling is so high it is a weather of its own. Sometimes a faint haze gathers up there, in the strata where the warm exhalation meets whatever the upper air is made of, and that haze forms shapes — long horizontal smears, slow turning wheels of darker warmth — that the inhabitants below call clouds, though they are not clouds. They never rain. Nothing has ever fallen from that ceiling. The ceiling is the underside of a continent, and it is patient, and it does not give.

Across the floor, the rock is sharp. There has been no time to soften. Whatever opened this space — and no one speaks of whatever opened it, because the act of speaking would imply a before, and there is no before, only the always — did so recently in the scale of stone. The edges of boulders are crisp. The fractures in the ground are clean-cut, as if a chisel had been drawn through them by a careful hand. Pebbles do not roll down into dust. Pebbles are still pebbles, exactly as they landed.

There is almost no water. There is something else — a thin slick that gathers in the lowest basins, a substance that catches light and holds it, viscous, faintly sweet if a creature licks it, not water but a slow mineral secretion that pools where the warmth condenses out of the stone. The pools are shallow and they never overflow. They have never overflowed. They are exactly as they were, and the things that come to drink from them come at the same hour they have always come, because in this place the hour does not move. The slick does not evaporate. It does not thicken. It is what it is, and what it is, is enough.

There are no plants. There is nothing green, nothing rooted, nothing that has ever turned its face upward toward absent light and made sugar from it. The ground is stone, salt-crusted in pale patches, dust of a thousand colors in the sheltered folds. What grows here — and the word *grows* is generous — are the lichen-thin skins of mineral itself: a black fuzz on certain rocks that is not alive, a kind of breathing crust that the warm air keeps just barely organized. The crust is not eaten. It is walked on. It is the color the place gives to things that rest.

And yet the place is full.

The fauna is what the place is for. It is abundant. It is everywhere, in the sense that everywhere is where it is — you cannot walk for long without seeing one, and often before you see it you feel it: a presence nearby, a warmth beside your own warmth, a soft displacement of the air that has not moved in a very long time. They are many. They are quiet.

The creatures are pale. Their fur — and most of them are furred, heavily, in coats that look almost indecent for the warmth — is the color of the dust and the color of the slick and the color of nothing at all. Some are enormous and slow, browsing the mineral crusts with long soft mouths that work the stone without breaking it. Some are small and quick, threading between the boulders in passages they themselves have worn, the only erosion that has ever happened here. Some live in burrows that go down, only down, into warmer rock, and they come up at certain hours and the hours do not change.

They have eyes, all of them, and the eyes are large. In the dimness the dimness asks to be seen, and they oblige. The pupils are not round. They are vertical, or horizontal, or shaped like the leaves of plants that do not exist here, which is to say like nothing you have a word for. With their eyes closed — and they close them sometimes, briefly, in the manner of thinking — they look like sleeping stones.

They are quiet. They do not cry out, except rarely, a sound so low it is more felt than heard, a frequency that travels through the warm rock and arrives in your molars before it arrives in your ears. They call to each other across distances that would swallow a human shout, and the call is one note, held, and it does not fade. It is the only sound in the place, and it is the sound of the place not changing.

Across the floor, at intervals too irregular to be a pattern and too regular to be accident, there are built things.

A cairn. A low wall of fitted stones, the stones chosen, the joints rubbed smooth with something that might be the slick from the pools. The wall does not enclose anything. It is shaped like the beginning of a room that was never finished. It has been in this state forever. It will be in this state forever. Beside it, a post driven into a crack — wood? bone? it is hard to tell, and it is not important — with a flat stone balanced on top. The post has no purpose. The stone balances because the air does not move.

A pen. An oval of low stones, three stones high, no door, with a flat rock inside that has the faint impression of an animal sleeping on it, worn into the surface over a span of time that does not pass. The pen is occupied, sometimes, by one of the pale slow creatures, who stands inside it and does not eat what is outside it and does not leave. There is no rope. There is no gate. It stays.

A path. Footprints in the dust, going from one built thing to another, the dust compressed into a faint track that has not blurred in the time the place has existed. The footprints are the right size for the creatures, but they are also the right size for something else — something taller, narrower, with a stride that is almost but not quite the stride of the fauna. No one has ever been seen making these footprints. The footprints are just there, as the slick is just there, as the cairn is just there.

The tech is old. The tech is the first tech, the kind of tech that is barely distinguishable from the place itself. A tool lies beside the cairn — a wedge-shaped stone, its edge worn to a curve that fits a hand that is not quite a hand. A length of something — hide? a tendon drawn thin and cured? — lies coiled near the pen, with a knot at one end that someone tied, a long time ago or always. The inhabitants of the place, when they are moving among the built things, carry these things with them. They have always carried these things. They will always carry these things.

The light is a problem the place has solved without solving it. There is a glow — not from anywhere in particular, not from a sun, not from a fire that anyone lit. The warm rock itself gives off a faint luminescence at the edges of its fractures, as if the stone is tired in a way that produces light. The slick in the pools gives back what dimness it receives. The creatures' eyes give back what they take. The result is a visibility that is just barely enough — you can see the shape of a thing across the floor of the hollow, but you cannot see its expression, and its expression is what would tell you if it were a stranger.

The sound of the place is the sound of not-movement. A faint hiss at the edges of hearing, the warm air pressing against cooler stone. The low call of a creature, somewhere far, sustained. The soft pad of a foot in dust, somewhere near, not approaching. The place has its own acoustics, vast and dry, and the acoustics swallow everything that is not sustained. Snaps, cracks, sudden noises — these do not exist here. There are no sudden noises. There is only the long held note of the place continuing.

It does not change.

The pools do not dry or rise. The cairn does not crumble or grow. The creatures do not age visibly, do not mate and produce young that are noticeably younger, do not die in any way that leaves remains. The dust does not settle further. The footprints do not fade. The wedge-shaped stone does not wear further. The temperature does not fluctuate across any span a body can measure.

It is a place that has found its equilibrium, the final form of itself, and it is holding that form the way the slick holds the light — patiently, without effort, because effort is what you need to change things, and there is nothing here that wants to change.

If you walk far enough — and you could, the scale is the scale of the place itself, the floor goes on in every direction until the haze eats it — you would find another cairn. Another pen. Another path between them. The same footprint, the same wedge-shaped stone, the same low call crossing the rock. The place repeats. The place is the same place, extended. There is no end to it, and there is no other place. There is only this, exactly this, the way it has always been, the way it always will be, in the warm dark underneath a continent that does not know it is hollow.

The creatures look at you, when you arrive, with their strange-shaped eyes, and they do not move, and they are not surprised, because surprise requires the expectation of something different, and here there is no expectation. There has never been. There will never be.

You are the only thing that has happened here in a very long time.

And even you, after a while, will not be that either.
