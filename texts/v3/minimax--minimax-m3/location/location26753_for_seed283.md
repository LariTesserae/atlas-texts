A basin, or the floor of a valley that has forgotten it was ever a valley. The ground has risen to meet the air, or the air has come down to lie on the ground, and the difference no longer matters in the heavy weather of the place. You stand on soil that gives, slightly, with every step. The heat is not the dry heat of an open sun. It is the green heat of a thing that has been stewing in its own exhalation for a long time. The air is full of water. Water runs in shallow, slow sheets across the ground, water hangs in mist above the sheets, and water is inside everything else — inside the breathing trunks, inside the soft meat of the small things, inside the air itself, which feels less like a gas than like a tepid cloth pressed to the skin.

The light is wrong for the hour. It is mid-afternoon, or near it, but the day has been reduced to a greenish bruise. The sun does not enter here. It is filtered and re-filtered through a lattice of leaves and stems and the moving bodies of creatures so numerous that the lattice is alive. Where a single beam does make it through, it falls in a column and dissolves before it reaches the floor. The floor is in a permanent dusk. The dusk has weight.

There is no path, because nothing here was laid down. But there are corridors. The creatures have worn them — low tunnels through the undergrowth, runways along the boles of the great trees, channels in the soft earth where the runoff is always moving. You could follow one, if you were small enough. If you were the size of what made them, you could go a long way without ever seeing the sky.

The trees are an old argument with gravity. Their roots rise out of the ground in slabs and arches because the ground is not committed to being ground. The roots straddle the water. The water moves beneath them. The trunks lean. Nothing here is straight. The forest is vertical, but the verticals are also curves, and the curves are the only straight thing, in the way a river is the only straight thing across a delta. There are buttressed trunks ten meters wide at the base. There are trunks that fell in some long-ago storm and are now horizontal forests in themselves, draped and overgrown. There are columns that are not trunks at all but chimneys of breath — vents in the soil where warmer air comes up and meets the canopy, and around the vents the trees grow fastest, and the vents are the only landmarks, if you can call a thing that is everywhere a landmark.

The sound is the sound of a place that is doing very much but very slowly. A hum, not of engines but of wings — so many wings that no one of them makes a sound. A creak of wood. A drip that becomes a flow that becomes a distant thin water-sound. Now and then, deeper, a low percussion that has no obvious source, and that you feel in the soles of your feet before you hear it. Something has fallen. Something heavy. In the silence after, the smaller sounds rebuild themselves.

The fauna is the point. The fauna is what the place is for.

You do not see them at first, because the light is bad and the eye keeps trying to use the wrong scale. You look for individuals and see texture instead. Then you see that the texture is moving. Then you see that the texture is several textures stacked, and that what you took for bark is a sheet of small grey-brown bodies, each one an individual, each one going about a small business with the focused efficiency of a thing that has always done this. They are packed. The word *packed* is right, but the packing has a direction — flows of them, lanes of them, the way cars pack a highway in a city at dusk. They are not everywhere at random. They are everywhere on task. They are taking something from here to there. They are bringing something back. The lanes cross and merge. The density is such that the lanes have their own geography, their own banked turns, their own slow rush hours in the slow day.

Closer. The workers are the size of a thumbnail, hard-shelled, many-legged. They move in columns centimeters wide. Where two columns meet there is a junction, and at the junction a small geometry of bodies — a milling, a decision being made in the language of taps and chemical trails. They are not alone. On their backs, riding them, are smaller things — mites, or the young of something else, hooked on. The riders are not parasitic. They are part of the line. The line is a thing the size of an arm, made of many creatures. The line is moving. The line has been moving for a long time. The line will be moving after you have gone.

Above, in the canopy, larger shapes move. They are slow, because of the heat, and they move by the simple logic of things that do not need to be quick. Long things with many joints, hanging down and then hauling themselves up. The sound they make is the creak you heard. Their droppings fall with the deep percussion. The droppings are processed almost immediately by the workers below, who dismantle them and carry the pieces off in their lanes. Nothing is wasted. Nothing has been wasted in this place for a very long time. The cycling is complete. The cycling is what the place is.

You begin to understand the tech. It is not in the creatures. It is in the system. It is the efficiency. It is the throughput. The fact that a single tree's worth of leaf-fall is moved, in pieces, to the right places, by the right workers, in the right order, without any of them having a plan. The system has the feel of a refinery. The system has the feel of a long-haul logistics operation, run at a scale that would require a small nation's roads and trains, but here is just a forest floor, and the roads are the trails, and the trains are the threads of bodies, and the engines are the small drives of the small creatures' legs. There is no blueprint. There is no sign that anything was designed. The design is in the equilibrium. The design is the erosion. The design is what happens when a place has been doing the same thing for so long that the doing and the place have become the same thing.

You walk, or you try to. The undergrowth is the density. Branches in the way. Vines. The thin resistant stems of ferns. The smell, always the smell — of warm water, of rot, of a sweetness that is not sugar but the breakdown of sugar, of a musk that is not animal but the marker of an animal that passed here hours ago. The smell is the smell of a system that is not finished with anything. Everything in the place is mid-process. A leaf is mid-fall. A fruit is mid-rot. A creature is mid-meal. Even the water on the ground is mid-journey — it has not decided yet which slow channel to take, which root to bypass, which small dark pool to fill.

There is a pool ahead, in a clearing made by the fall of something ancient. The pool is black, and still, and full. The water in it is warm. The water in it is the same water that is in the air. The pool is a place where the lanes of the workers converge, and the workers come to drink, and to wash, and to exchange the small parcels they have been carrying. The traffic at the pool is dense. The pool is one of the hubs. There are other hubs. The whole basin is a network of hubs, and the hubs are the only places where the forest is truly open to the air, and even the hubs are closing, slowly, because new growth is already filling the edges, and the new growth will be re-covered, and the cycle that the place has been running will continue to run.

You cannot see the edge of the place. There is no edge. The forest extends. The forest is doing what it is doing across a distance you could not walk in a day, and at the end of that walk it would be doing the same thing, and at the end of that walk it would be doing the same thing. The place is a region. The region is a single process. The process is patient. The process is the patient thing, and the patient thing has been here longer than any of its parts, and the parts are replaced, and the process continues, in the heat, in the damp, in the dim green dark.

Somewhere a small point of soft yellow in the green-brown distance. A glow. It could be a fungus. It could be a beetle's lantern. It could be a small gas vent where the soil is hot enough to phosphoresce. You do not go to it. You are aware that the place has no use for you, and that the place is not hostile, and that these are the same thing.

The hum continues. The lanes continue. The water moves. The dark is the dark of a place that has made its own weather and does not need the sky to make it for it.
