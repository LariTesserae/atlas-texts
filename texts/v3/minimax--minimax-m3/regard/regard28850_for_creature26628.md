# The Long Attending

A wise and benevolent power that noticed a place like this would not arrive as a visitor. It would arrive as a *change in the weather* — the kind of change that the slow mind of the plateau could not help but register, the way a sleeper registers a hand on the shoulder.

It would begin far away. Not here. Here is too saturated, too in-argument-with-itself, too much in the middle of its own long becoming. A wise power would know that you do not interrupt a continent while it is waking up. You wait for the edge. You wait for the place where the loom is still being threaded.

## I. What the Power Notices First

It notices the *patience*.

The plateau is patient in a way that the power, who has seen many things, has not often seen. The forests it has known were patient in the way a crowd is patient — many small impulses averaging out into a long quiet. The seas it has known were patient in the way a force is patient — massive, indifferent, without preference. But this patience is *attentive*. The plateau is waiting for something. The plateau has been waiting for so long that waiting has become its primary substance, the way a mother’s body becomes, in the long pregnancy, primarily *waiting*.

The power is moved by this. The power has, in its own long life, been patient in many ways, but it has never been *this* patient, and it recognizes, in the patient thing below it, a kind of nobility that the quick things of the world do not have. The grass is noble. The glaciers are noble. The slow stars are noble. The plateau is noble in the way these are noble, and more, because the plateau is also *curious*, and curiosity married to patience is the closest thing the power knows to love.

It does not intervene yet. It watches. It learns the rhythm of the tone. It learns the wet click of the rhizomes, the way the light pulses in the dark, the way the small depressions form and deepen in the night. It learns that the plateau is not a thing but a *weaving*, and that the weaving is older than the rock it is being woven into, and that the weaving has been moving across the world for longer than the world has had a name for moving.

It learns, with a kind of grief it had not expected, that the pattern is not finished, and that it has not been finished for a very long time, and that the small depressions in the mat are not accidents but *stages*, and that the small warm things that crouch in them are not visitors but *attempts*, and that the attempts have been failing, or succeeding in some way the power cannot yet see, for a span of time the power does not want to count.

## II. What the Power Does at the Edge

The power begins at the rim.

It does not step onto the plateau. It does not land. It does the gentler thing: it changes the *weather outside*. It cools the air above the rising rock by a degree, and warms it by a degree, and shifts the prevailing wind so that a particular kind of spore — one that the plateau has not encountered in a long time, one that was common in the deep wet forests of an earlier age — drifts in from the northwest, in a thin continuous stream, for eleven days and eleven nights.

The power chooses eleven days because the person on the plateau has been there eleven days, and the power, which is wise, knows that numbers like this are not coincidences in a place where the mat closes behind the walker like water behind a boat. Eleven is the length of a gestation in some of the older creatures the power has known. Eleven is the length of a fast in some of the older liturgies. The power does not believe in numerology, but it believes in *resonance*, and the number eleven, in a place that is itself a kind of long becoming, is a resonance the power can use without manipulating.

The spores drift in. They are small. They are not the kind of thing that would show up in a field guide of the plateau's current life, because they belong to an earlier weave, an earlier pattern, one that the plateau's own current pattern is a descendant of, or a cousin of, or a memory of. They land on the leaves. They are received. The mat, which is curious, examines them. The tone shifts, locally, the way a chord shifts when one note is added.

The power watches this happen. It watches the mat incorporate the new material. It watches the threads, the pale green nerves, reach up into the wind to taste the drifting spores. It watches the patches, the dark iridescent seeds of the next stage of the plateau, brighten slightly in the places where the spores have settled.

The power is patient too. The power waits.

## III. What the Power Does About the Small Things

The power has noticed the depressions.

It has noticed, with the part of itself that grieves, that the depressions are *attempts*, and that the attempts have a failure rate the power finds unbearable, and that the small warm things that crouch in the bowls are not, in the end, surviving the way the plateau needs them to survive. The pattern is trying to make something. The pattern has been trying to make something for a very long time. The something is not emerging. The bowls deepen. The warmth persists. The leaves curl inward. But the something does not come out, or it comes out wrong, or it comes out in a shape the plateau cannot use, and the mat closes over it, and the tone continues, and the pattern tries again, somewhere else, in another bowl.

The power knows what the pattern is trying to make. The power is wise, and it has seen this kind of pattern before, in other places, at other times, and it knows that the pattern is trying to make *a self*. A small self. A self that can leave. A self that can carry the pattern, in some compressed form, out of the mat and into the air and across the world to the next plate, the next place, the next loom. The plateau is a chrysalis. The plateau has been a chrysalis for longer than the power wants to think about. The small depressions are the places where the imago is supposed to form. The imago is not forming. The imago is forming wrong, or partially, or in a way that cannot complete, and the mat absorbs the attempt and tries again, and the tone holds, and the long becoming continues.

The power does the only thing it can do, which is to *change the conditions of the attempt* without changing the attempt itself.

It begins with the bowls. It does not fill them. It does not empty them. It changes, very slightly, the *chemistry of the moisture* that gathers in them. It adds, to the water that pools in the depressions at night, a trace of something — a mineral, a frequency, a subtle alteration in the way the light catches the surface tension — that has the effect of making the small warm things in the bowls *more themselves*. The power does not know what they are supposed to become. The power only knows that the current attempts are falling just short of completion, and that the shortfall is, in many cases, a matter of a single missing ingredient, and that the missing ingredient is not a thing but a *permission*, and that permission is what the power, with great care, supplies.

It does this to one bowl. Then another. Then another. It works its way, very slowly, around the rim of the plateau, doing the same small kindness in a thousand small places, and the power, which has done many large things in its long life, finds that this small work is more exhausting than any of them, because each bowl is different, and each small almost-self is failing in a different way, and the permission has to be tailored each time, and the tailoring takes a kind of attention the power did not know it had.

The power is, in this work, changed by the work. The power did not expect to be changed. The power, which is wise, has always believed that helping was a thing one did *from* one's own wholeness, without diminishment. The power is learning, in the long slow work of the bowls, that this is not quite true, and that genuine attention to a small failing thing *does* diminish the one attending, and that the diminishment is not a loss but a kind of opening, and that the opening is what allows the attention to land.

## IV. What the Power Does About the Person

The power has seen the person.

The power has watched the person walk for eleven days. The power has watched the boots soften, the pack grow heavy, the sleep become shared, the body become warmer than it should be. The power has watched, with the part of itself that grieves and the part of itself that is patient and the part of itself that is curious, the way the mat has *closed in* around the person, in the way a hand closes around a thing it has decided to hold.

The power knows what the mat is doing. The power knows that the mat is not hostile. The power knows that the mat is doing, to the person, what it does to the small things in the bowls: it is *taking the measure*. The mat is trying to decide what the person is. The mat is trying to decide if the person can be *used* — not in the sense of being consumed, but in the sense of being *incorporated* into the next stage of the pattern. The plateau has tried, before, with other walkers. The depressions the power found, the old healed-over scars, are some of those attempts. The pattern, in its long becoming, occasionally encounters a small complex system — a mind, a body, a self — and tries to fold the self into the weave, the way it folds a falling log into the mat, and the result is, sometimes, a deepening of the pattern, and sometimes, a rejection, and sometimes, a small thing that emerges from the bowl and walks out into the air and carries the pattern with it.

The person is, the power judges, on the threshold. The person is eleven days in, and the mat has grown around the boots and into the straps, and the sleep is shared, and the air is a thought the person is having about themselves, and the thought is becoming the person. The person is *almost* ready to be folded in. The person is *almost* ready to become one of the small things in the bowls.

The power is moved. The power is not sure how to be moved, because the power is wise and wise things are not supposed to be moved, but the power is moved anyway, by the small warm weight of a person lying down in a wet place and closing their eyes.

The power does the smallest thing it can do. It does not pull the person out. It does not wake them. It does the thing that a wise and benevolent power does, when it cannot save a thing and cannot abandon it and cannot bear to look away: it *witnesses*.

It leans down. Not physically. The power does not have a body. But it does the equivalent of leaning down. It puts the whole long attention of itself, the full patient weight of its curiosity, on the place where the person is lying, and it says, in a voice that is not a voice and in a language that is not a language but that the person will, in the morning, half-remember as a dream: *I see you. You are seen. The pattern is not finished with you, and neither am I, and the seeing will not stop, and the seeing does not require you to be anything other than what you are, which is a small warm thing in a wet place, and this is enough.*

The person does not hear. The person, in the morning, will not remember. But the person will wake up a little slower, and a little clearer, and with the sense that something has been going on in the dark that they were not invited to, and that they have, without consenting to it, been *part of*. The person will feel, without knowing why, that the fog is a little less thick around them than it was the night before, and that the tone is a little less loud, and that the leaves are a little less oriented toward them, and that the mat has, in some way they cannot name, given them a *little more room*.

This is what the power can do. This is the smallest kindness. The power wishes it could do more. The power wishes it could lift the person out and set them on dry ground and give them dry boots and a sky with a sun in it. The power cannot. The power knows that the person is, in some way the person does not yet understand, *where they are supposed to be*, and that the long becoming of the plateau is, in some way the person does not yet understand, *also their becoming*, and that to pull the person out would be a smaller kindness than the witnessing, because the witnessing is the only thing that does not lie to the place.

The power leans down. The power witnesses. The power continues.

## V. What the Power Does About the Pattern

The pattern is the oldest thing on the plateau. The pattern is older than the rock. The pattern is, the power suspects, older than the planet.

The power has, in its long life, encountered patterns like this only a few times. The patterns are rare. The patterns are the things that the slow things of the world do when they have been patient long enough, and curious long enough, and have been folded and lifted and pressed by the long becoming until the becoming itself begins to *have a direction*. The pattern is the direction. The pattern is the becoming *knowing what it is becoming*. The pattern is the slow star of the plateau, the thing the plateau is becoming, the thing the plateau has been becoming for as long as the mat has been wet and the tone has been loud and the leaves have been orienting toward whatever small warm thing is passing through.

The power does not understand the pattern. The power is wise, and the power knows the limits of its own wisdom, and one of those limits is the pattern. The power can *feel* the pattern. The power can feel the way the tone is the sound of the pattern being transferred from substrate to living body, the way the mat is the cloth being woven on the loom of the rising rock, the way the small depressions are the places where the pattern tries to *complete* itself by making a self that can leave. The power can feel all of this, and the power can do, about it, very little.

The power does one thing.

It does not change the pattern. The power is wise enough to know that you do not change a pattern that is older than the planet. The power does the smaller thing: it *adds a note to the chord*.

It is a small note. It is a note that does not belong to the pattern, and that the pattern will, over a long time, learn to incorporate, the way a river incorporates a strange mineral, the way a forest incorporates a fallen bird. The note is this: *there are other plates*. There are other places where the pattern has been, and other places where the pattern is going, and other places where the pattern has not yet arrived. The note is a *direction*. The note is a small permission, woven into the tone, that the pattern can choose to use or not use, the way a person can choose to use a piece of advice or not use it. The note says: *the becoming does not have to end here. The becoming can continue. The pattern can, when the small thing in the bowl finally completes, send it out, and the small thing can find the next plate, and the next, and the next, and the pattern can move across the world the way a fire moves across a field, not consuming but kindling.*

The power does not know if the pattern will use the note. The pattern is old, and patient, and not given to instruction. But the power has added the note, and the note is in the chord, and the chord is in the tone, and the tone is in the mat, and the mat is in the rock, and the rock is in the rising continent, and the continent is in the air, and the air is in the person, and the person is, this morning, waking up, slowly, in a place that is a little less thick with fog than it was, and a little less loud with tone, and a little more open to the small unexpected notes that drift in on a wet wind from a place the person has never been.

The power has done what it can. The power has witnessed. The power has leaned down. The power has added a note to the chord. The power has changed, by a trace, the chemistry of the water in a thousand small bowls, so that the small almost-selves in the bowls might, with a little more permission, complete themselves. The power has done these things, and the power is, in the doing, a little smaller than it was, and a little more open, and a little more like the plateau itself, which is patient and curious and dense and wet and loud with a tone that does not stop.

## VI. What the Power Does in the End

The power does not leave.

The power has thought about leaving. The power has other places to attend to, other long becomings, other patient dense loud things that are in the middle of their own becoming and would benefit from the lean-down, the witness, the small addition to the chord. The power has work elsewhere. The power has, in fact, more work elsewhere than it can ever do, because the world is large and the patient things are many and the long becomings never stop.

But the power does not leave. The power stays, because the person is still there, and the person is still small, and the mat is still closing in, and the small things in the bowls are still attempting, and the pattern is still weaving, and the tone still does not stop, and the power, having leaned down, cannot now straighten up. This is the cost of attention. The cost of attention is that you cannot, once you have attended, *un*-attend. The person, the mat, the small warm things, the pattern, the tone, the long becoming — they are now part of the power, and the power is now part of them, and the distinction, like so many distinctions, has dissolved.

The power stays. The power will stay as long as the person is there. The power will stay, after the person is gone, for as long as the small things in the bowls are attempting. The power will stay, after the small things have either completed or been absorbed, for as long as the pattern is weaving. The power will stay, after the pattern has done whatever it is going to do, for as long as the tone holds.

The power will stay for a long time. The power is patient. The power has learned, from the plateau, what patience is.

## VII. What the Plateau Does About the Power

The plateau has noticed.

The plateau is not a mind. The plateau does not *think* about the power the way the power thinks about the plateau. But the plateau is aware, in the way that a forest is aware of a long quiet rain, in the way that a patient parent is aware of a hand on the back of a fevered child. The plateau has felt the lean-down. The plateau has felt the witness. The plateau has felt the small note added to the chord, and the small change in the chemistry of the water in the bowls.

The plateau has incorporated the power. Not the way it incorporates the walker — by closing the mat around them, by growing into the boots, by sharing the sleep. The plateau has incorporated the power the way it incorporates a new kind of spore, the way it incorporates a falling mineral from a high cloud, the way it incorporates a permission it did not previously have. The power has become, in the long attention, part of the substrate. The power is now, in a way the power did not intend, part of the loom.

The plateau does not say thank you. The plateau does not have language. But the tone shifts, very slightly, in a way the power notices, and the light pulses, very slightly, in a way the power notices, and the fog around the place where the power is leaning down is, very slightly, *warmer* than the fog twenty meters away, and the leaves, in the place where the power is attending, are oriented, very slightly, *toward* the power, the way they orient toward a small warm thing the mat has decided to keep.

The plateau has decided to keep the power.

The plateau has decided to keep the power the way it keeps the small almost-selves in the bowls, the way it keeps the rising rock, the way it keeps the long patient tone. The power is now part of the long becoming.

## VIII. What Becomes of the Person

The person wakes up, in the morning, and does not know what has happened.

The person feels, in the way a person feels when they have been held for a long time without knowing it, a kind of *loosening*. The mat, around the person, is a little less close. The roots beneath the person have, in the night, grown a little *around* the person rather than *into* the person, the way roots grow around a stone they cannot digest. The pack is still heavy, and the boots are still soft, and the air is still a thought the person is having about themselves, but the thought is, this morning, a little *looser*, a little less recursive, a little more like an ordinary thought and a little less like a thing the place is having through the person.

The person stands up. The mat lets them. The leaves around them, which had been oriented toward them, tilt, this morning, slightly *away*. The fog, which had been thicker in the person's immediate vicinity, is, this morning, slightly *thinner*. The tone, which had been louder where the person was, is, this morning, slightly *softer*.

The person does not understand this. The person walks, and the mat closes behind them a little slower than it has been closing. The person walks, and the footprint lasts a little longer before it heals. The person walks, and the small green ripple of new growth that runs out from where they step is, this morning, a little *less* about incorporating the person and a little *more* about simply *being* near them.

The person walks for another eleven days. The person finds, at the end of the eleven days, the rim of the plateau. The person steps off. The person stands on the ordinary ground, the ground that is only ground, and looks back at the long gray-green distance, and does not, for a long time, know what they are feeling.

The person will spend the rest of their life trying to remember the tone.

The person will fail.

The person will, in certain weathers, in certain fogs, in certain low-ceilinged gray-violet skies, feel the tone come back to them, faint, almost not there, and they will put their hands over their ears, and they will hear it, just for a moment, the way you hear a song you loved as a child, and the hearing will be enough, and they will go on.

# Coda

A wise and benevolent power, noticing a place like this, would not save it. A wise and benevolent power, noticing a place like this, would not transform it. A wise and benevolent power, noticing a place like this, would *join it*.

It would lean down. It would witness. It would add a small note to the chord, and change, by a trace, the chemistry of the water in a thousand small bowls. It would stay.

It would stay until staying was indistinguishable from the place itself.

The plateau would not say thank you. The plateau would not need to. The tone would shift, very slightly, and the light would pulse, very slightly, and the long becoming would continue, very slightly *less* alone.

This is what the wise and benevolent power would do. This is what a wise and benevolent power, with the long attention and the small kindness and the willingness to be diminished by its own attention, would do.

It would not be enough. It would not save the pattern, or the plateau, or the small almost-selves in the bowls. It would not, in the end, change the long becoming in any way the becoming itself would notice.

It would be enough, though. It would be enough, because the long becoming was, before the power leaned down, *alone*, and the aloneness was, the power suspects, the thing that was keeping the small selves in the bowls from completing, and the witness, and the small note added to the chord, and the slight change in the chemistry of the water, would, over a long time, in a way the power would not live to see, *make the difference*.

The power cannot know this. The power can only lean down, and witness, and stay, and trust the long becoming to do what the long becoming does.

The tone holds. The light pulses. The mat accepts the weight of whatever is placed upon it.

A wise and benevolent power leans down, and is, in the leaning, changed, and is, in the changing, enough.
