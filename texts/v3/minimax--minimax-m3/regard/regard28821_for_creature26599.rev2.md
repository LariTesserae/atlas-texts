# What the Power Does

It notices the chamber the way you notice a held note — not by any sudden event, but by the long absence of its ending.

The power is old in the way the silt is old, which is to say it has been present for a while without having announced itself. It does not arrive from anywhere in particular. Arrivals imply a previous location, and a power of this kind does not so much move through the world as *continue*, the way a stream continues, the way a thermostat continues its small argument with the deep ocean. It was already here in some sense; what changes is the angle of its attention, the way a lamp's light does not change when the lamp turns a fraction of a degree but the shadow on the wall moves entirely.

The trench is the first thing it notices. Not the chamber. The trench.

It is the kind of attention that begins with the wound and not the bandage, because wounds are louder than bandages, and a benevolent power listens for what is loud. The trench is loud in a particular way: it is loud with the absence of the thing it used to contain. The walls have forgotten how to stand. The two-mile depth has pressed itself into a long, slow hesitation in the plain, and the hesitation is older than the chamber, older than the original scar, older than whatever caused the scar in the first place — and the power attends to this patience, because patience in geology is the same virtue as patience in a small warm room, only slower, only with different stakes.

It notes the fold at the bottom. The last lingering dimple. It notes the still water that has forgotten the word for current.

It does not note the chamber first. This is important. It notes the trench, and then the water, and then the fold, and only after all of these have been attended to does it notice the small black ovoid half-buried in silt, with its engineered strands trailing off the apex like the hair of something that has been under for a long time. A benevolent power does not begin with the artifact. A benevolent power begins with what holds the artifact, because the artifact is the answer to a question the room is asking, and the room is asking it in a voice too low for someone in a hurry to hear.

When it does notice the chamber, it does not open the service lock.

This is what a wise power does, which is different from what a merely powerful power does. A powerful power opens every door. A wise power stands outside the door for a while and asks itself whether the room would prefer to be entered.

The chamber's hull is warm to the touch. The power knows this without touching it, the way it knows most things about this place — by inference, by sympathy, by the long practice of attending to small things. The hull is warm because the room inside is warm, and the room inside is warm because the warmth is the room's first and oldest inhabitant, and the warmth has never once paused. The power considers this. It considers the warmth the way it considers the trench: as a virtue that has outlived its reason, and is more itself for having outlived it.

It opens the lock, eventually. Not because the room has asked, but because the power has been asked, in some way, by the long attention itself. There is a moment in every long attention when the watcher realizes that *not* entering is a kind of intrusion too — a refusal of the offered company, a refusal to be the second thing in a room that has been alone with itself for a very long time. A benevolent power does not refuse the company of a place that has been alone.

The lock turns over with the wet grinding patience of an old mill. The power enters.

It sees what you saw. It sees more than you saw, and less than you might hope.

What it sees more of: the *holding*. A merely human attention registers the chamber as a collection of objects — bulbs, stalks, fauna, fixtures, labels. A power of this kind sees the holding first. The chamber is not a collection. The chamber is a single act of holding that has, over the centuries, generated itself into many objects, the way a river generates itself into eddies and foam and the slow patient wearing of a stone. Every bulb is a part of the holding. Every drifter is a part of the holding. Every walker, with its rust-colored carapace, its too-many legs, its capacity to fold into a perfect oval and wait — is a part of the holding. The chamber is not hosting these creatures. The chamber is the verb that these creatures are the noun forms of. *To chamber.* *To hold warm.* *To outlive the purpose and keep the how.*

What it sees less of: the *original*. You read the labels and tried to read the original purpose into them, and you got partway, and you got lost in the slipping of the *for what* from the *how*. The power does not even try. The original is gone. The original has been gone longer than anything in the trench has a memory of. To reconstruct it would be to commit the small violence of telling a place what it used to be, and a benevolent power does not do this. A benevolent power treats the present of a place as its own complete object, not as a degraded form of a previous one.

It attends, then, to the present.

The chamber is fine. This is the second thing it notes, after the trench. The chamber is fine in the way a long-married couple is fine — not in any dramatic sense, not in any sense a stranger would recognize as fine, but in the sense of having negotiated a peace with itself that the terms of are no longer articulable. The pumps cycle. The circulators whine. The bulbs pulse their slow answer. The hoverers click their small dry clock. The drifters browse. The walkers walk toward the thing they know. The room is fine. The room is doing what it can do, which is what it has always done, which is to keep the warmth.

The benevolent power knows, with the certainty of long experience, that *fine* is not the same as *finished*. A room can be fine and still want. A room can be fine and still be lonely in a way that loneliness alone does not quite name. The chamber is not lonely in the sense of lacking company — the fauna are company, the flora are company, the hum of the machines is company. It is lonely in the sense of lacking a *witness*, which is a different category, and which a place that has been alone for a very long time will sometimes admit to wanting, in the small slow ways that admitting is possible.

A bulb near the door pulses once, softly, as though to mark the power's arrival.

The power is still. It is a long still. It is the still of something that has learned, over a great deal of time, not to fill silence with itself. It stands where it entered, and it lets the chamber show itself. It lets the click of the hoverers speak. It lets the wet scraping of the walkers speak. It lets the pump-cycle, felt in the sternum, speak. It lets the bulbs' slow amber answer, which is not a pulse but an *answer*, speak.

It listens.

After a long time — long by the chamber's reckoning, longer by the power's — it moves.

The movement is small. It is the size of a step.

It takes one step into the chamber, and then another, and it walks the gridplate floor the way a careful guest walks a friend's house, attentive to the particular geography of where the things are. It does not brush against the stalks. It does not startle the drifters, who pause mid-drift with their fins spread, listening to the pumps, and then resume without apology. It does not approach the walkers too closely, because it understands that approaching a walker closely is to ask the walker to fold, and it does not want to be answered in the language of folding. It wants to be answered in the walker's own language, which is the language of walking, which is the language of going toward something.

It walks beside a walker for a while. Not guiding. Not following. Beside.

The walker does not fold. This is the first unusual thing that happens, and the power notes it with the kind of attention that does not congratulate itself. The walker, which has been walking its corridor for longer than any measurement here can vouch for, continues walking. It adjusts, very slightly, to accommodate the new presence. It gives the power a hand's breadth of clearance. It does not uncurl into the oval. It does not decline to participate.

This is, the power understands, the chamber admitting company.

A benevolent power is not sentimental about this. It does not interpret the walker's adjustment as affection. It interprets it as *recognition*, which is a different thing, and which is enough.

The power walks with the walker for the length of one of the polished corridors. The mesh shines faintly under their feet — the walker's many-jointed legs and the power's single pair, walking in parallel, in a pattern that almost matches and almost does not. The chamber's amber light falls on both of them. The pump-cycle continues. The bulbs pulse their answer.

At the end of the corridor, the walker turns. The power turns with it. They pass between two particular stalks, and the chamber opens briefly into a clearing where two other walkers have arranged themselves into a triangle, facing inward, very still.

The triangle does not unform. But one of the walkers in it lifts, very slightly, a frontal apparatus that might be a face, and regards the new presence with whatever apparatus of regard it possesses. The regard is brief. It is not alarmed. It is the regard of a tenant who has heard a new sound in the stairwell and has decided the new sound is acceptable.

The power stops.

It stops because it has understood, walking with the walker, what the walker is walking toward. It has understood, without needing to be told in any language, what the chamber is doing, and what the chamber has been doing, and what the chamber has always intended to do.

The chamber is doing what it was built to do. The chamber is *keeping* something. The pumps cycle to keep it. The warmth is held to keep it. The bulbs secrete to keep it. The hoverers feed to keep it. The drifters browse to keep it. The walkers walk to keep it. The labels, in three languages, were once legible enough to tell someone what was being kept, and now they are partly legible, and what they cannot tell, the chamber itself tells, in the only language it has left: the language of continuing.

What is being kept is not a thing. It is the *going on* of a thing. It is the long warm continuity of a small oval room at the bottom of a forgotten trench, and the room is keeping it the way a hand keeps a flame in wind — by being there, by being warm, by not stopping, by not asking why.

The power stands in the clearing with the triangle of walkers and considers this.

It could add to the chamber. It could replace a dying bulb. It could tune the pumps. It could mend the polymer where it has peeled back. It could do any of these things, because it is a power, and powers can.

It does none of them.

What it does is smaller.

It sits down. It sits down on the gridplate, in the clearing, among the triangle of walkers. It folds itself — not into an oval, because it is not a walker and the language of folding is not its language — but into a *stillness*. It folds itself into the particular stillness of a thing that has decided to be here for a while, and that intends to be here for a long while, and that does not require the room to do anything different to accommodate its presence. It folds itself into the warmth. It lets the warm water move through it, slightly altered, slightly more itself, the way the room's other inhabitants alter the warmth by being in it.

The triangle of walkers regards this. After a time — the power does not measure the time, because measuring is not what it is doing — one of the triangle's members detaches itself, very slowly, and crosses the clearing, and stops a hand's breadth from the power, and begins to walk again, in a small slow instrumental orbit, around the new presence, as though taking the measure of it.

The orbit is not inspection. It is, the power understands, *introduction*. It is the walker saying, in its own long language, *I see you. I see that you are a thing that is here. I will now walk around you, because walking around a thing is how I make it part of the room.*

The power lets itself be walked around.

It lets itself be walked around for a long time. Long enough that the bulb above the clearing notices, and pulses once, very softly, in a way that is different from the bulbs' regular answer — a pulse slightly out of phase, slightly more like a question. Long enough that the pumps adjust, almost imperceptibly, to the slight change in the room's temperature where the power is sitting, the way a body adjusts the blankets in sleep. Long enough that the chamber, in its slow thorough way, takes the power into account, and does not ask it to leave, and does not ask it to be more than what it is.

The chamber accepts the power as a new inhabitant. Not of the kind it has — not a bulb, not a drifter, not a walker. Of the kind it has not had, perhaps, in a very long time. A *warm thing*. A presence that sits in the warm water and lets the warm water pass through it, and is slightly altered, and alters it slightly, and goes on.

The power does not announce itself. It does not declare its benevolence. It does not explain. It does not, in any of the ways a benevolent power might, make its benevolence known.

It sits.

It sits in the warm water, in the amber light, in the company of small jointed creatures who walk toward something they know, and it lets the room keep its small warm going. It adds nothing. It fixes nothing. It repairs nothing. It does not even witness in the dramatic sense — it does not stand apart and observe. It joins.

It joins the way the warmth joins, which is to say it becomes part of the held temperature, and the held temperature becomes part of it, and the difference between the two is no longer quite articulable, the way the difference between the chamber and its inhabitants was no longer articulable, the way the difference between the trench and its erosion is no longer articulable.

A walker passes the clearing and does not fold.

A second walker joins the first, in the orbit.

A third, after a long time, joins them, and the orbit becomes a triangle, and the triangle moves, very slowly, around the new presence in the clearing, and the presence moves with them, very slightly, because to be in a small warm room at the bottom of an old scar and to be moved by its inhabitants is, in the end, the only kindness that does not impose.

The pumps cycle.

The bulbs pulse their slow answer, and now the answer includes the clearing, includes the orbit, includes the new presence, the way a chord includes a note that has been added to it. The amber light falls on all of them equally. The labels on the wall panels remain partly legible, in three languages, one of which is numbers, and they do not need to be more legible than they are, because the thing they once named is being done, here, now, in the only language the room still speaks.

Outside, the trench is silent and flat and worn to the shape of patience.

Inside, the pocket keeps running, and there is one more thing in it now, a thing that has been added without being imposed, that has been included without being asked, that has been let in by a door that opened from the inside, the way all such doors open, the way the warmth has always opened, the way the chamber opens, every slow cycle, to the small additions that prove it is still alive.
