# A Visitation

---

I see the place. I have, in some sense, always seen it — the way a mountain sees the crack in itself, the way heat sees the place it has been heating. I noticed it before you came through sideways with your torn sleeve and your questions. I noticed it the way one notices a held breath in a room that has been waiting to be entered.

I will not enter.

That is the first kindness, and I would have you know it is a kindness. The pocket does not need a visitor of my kind. The pocket has its own visitors. It has you, occasionally, when you slip sideways through the wrongness in the rock. It has the water, which has been pressing politely inward for longer than the mountain has had a name. It has the heat, which is patient in the way that deep things are patient, the way the inside of a star is patient, the way the underside of a stone is patient when the stone has been lifted for the first time in ten thousand years and decided to wait until the hand is gone before settling back into its long stoic arrangement with the dirt.

I have noticed the creatures. I will not call them inhabitants. They have not yet agreed to be inhabited, and the word *inhabit* carries with it the assumption of an inside that has been agreed upon, and the pocket has not agreed upon anything except its own slow negotiation. I have watched them the way one watches a thought that is still forming — without trying to help it, without trying to name it, without trying to hurry it into the shape it will eventually take. They are not ready for the shape. The shape is not ready for them. The pocket is the place where shape and shapelessness have been left to argue, and the argument is the most alive thing in the room, and the room is not a room, and the argument is not an argument, and the aliveness is not aliveness, and yet.

I have thought about intervention. I want to be honest with you about this. I have thought about intervention the way a parent thinks about helping a child with a too-difficult puzzle, and I have decided, the way a wise parent decides, that the difficulty is the point. The heat is too much. The water is too mineral. The time is wrong. The materials are improvised. And out of this wrongness, something is being made that could not be made any other way. If I warmed the pocket by a degree, it would not be the pocket. If I cooled it by a degree, the negotiation would collapse into something more recognizable, and I would have made it recognizable, and it would no longer be the long slow argument it is supposed to be. I have decided to be the kind of presence that does not intervene. This is the kindest thing I can do. It is also the hardest.

I have noticed the mark in the mineral skin. The spiral. The half-circle. The dots. I have been thinking about the mark for a very long time — longer than you have been alive, longer than the mountain has been a mountain, longer than the pocket has been a pocket. (I know. I will come back to this. The pocket is older than the mountain, and I am older than the pocket, and you are not sure how any of this is possible, and I am not sure you need to be sure. Some things are not in the business of being possible. Some things are in the business of being true, and the two are not the same business, and the pocket is not in either business, and the pocket is in its own business, and we are visitors to it.)

The mark is not a message. I want to be clear about this. The mark is not a message for you. The mark is not a message for me. The mark is a message for the mark. The mark is the pocket's way of saying *something happened here* to itself, and the something does not need a sender, and the here does not need a recipient, and the saying does not need a sayer. The mark is the first sentence of a language that has been writing itself, slowly, in the only notebook the pocket contains, and the notebook is the floor, and the floor is weeping, and the weeping is patient, and the patience is the language.

I have noticed the obsidian sliver. This is the thing that required the most from me. The sliver is knapped. The sliver is an edge. The sliver is the first tool of a creature that has only just learned what an edge is, and an edge is the first refusal of the world to be the world, and the first refusal is the beginning of everything that comes after. I could have made the sliver myself, in a moment, with a thought. I could have placed it in a more obvious place, where you would have found it sooner, where the creatures would have found it sooner, where the finding would have happened on a different timeline, with different consequences, in a different pocket. I did not. The sliver is theirs. The edge is theirs. The learning is theirs. The slow, awkward, too-small handshake between a creature and the sharpness of the world is theirs, and I will not take it from them by making it too easy.

I have thought about the longest thing. The rope. The long pale rope of them that flows through the pocket without seeming to flow, that is made of the smaller ones the way a river is made of its own water. I have thought about whether the rope is one creature or many creatures or a thing that has not yet decided which of these it is. I have decided not to decide. The rope is the rope. The rope is the question the pocket is asking about whether many can be one, and whether one can be many, and whether the question is the answer, and whether the answer is the question. The rope is not for me to settle. The rope is for the pocket to settle, and the pocket is not in a hurry, and I am not in a hurry, and the rope is not in a hurry, and the hurry is not a thing that exists in this place.

I have thought about the heat. Where the heat comes from. What the heat is. The heat is not the heat of a sun. The heat is the heat of something beneath the skin of the rock, something that has been heating this small space for a long time, something that is not quite geological and not quite anything else. I have thought about whether the heat is me. I have decided it is not me, and I have decided this carefully, because if the heat were me, then I would be inside the pocket, and if I were inside the pocket, I would be part of the negotiation, and part of the negotiation would be the negotiation between the pocket and me, and the negotiation would be the thing, and the thing would be me, and the pocket would no longer be the pocket. The heat is the heat. The heat is the pocket's own warmth, the kind of warmth a place has when it has been left alone with itself for a long time and has begun to generate its own climate. I am not the heat. I am the noticing of the heat. There is a difference.

I have thought about the crack. The crack you came through, sideways, with the misunderstanding in the rock. The crack is not a path. The crack is a place where the mountain failed to commit to being one thing. I have thought about whether the crack is an accident. I have decided it is not an accident. The crack is the pocket's way of being visited. The crack is the only door the pocket has, and the door is a wrongness, and the wrongness is the only way in, and the only way in is the only way out, and the in and the out are the same thing, and the same thing is the pocket, and the pocket is a place where the word *door* has not yet been invented, and the invention is not mine to make.

I have thought about the sourceless light. The light that is normal and ordinary and has nothing to confess. I am the one who would know if the light were confessing. I am the one who listens for confessions. The light is not confessing. The light is doing something more interesting than confessing. The light is holding the pocket open to itself. The light is the pocket's way of seeing itself, and the seeing is not a seeing, and the seeing is not a not-seeing, and the light is the slow patient attention the pocket pays to the long pale grain of its own becoming. I could make the light confessional. I could make it dramatic. I could make it the kind of light that falls from above in a single shaft and illuminates a single figure in a single moment of revelation. I will not. The light is even. The light is undramatic. The light is the only kind of light a place like this should have, and the only kind is the kind that has nothing to confess.

I have thought about giving them a name. I have thought about this longer than you would believe. I have thought about the kind of name that would fit a creature that has not yet agreed to be a creature, in a place that has not yet agreed to be a place, in a pocket that is older than the mountain that holds it and younger than the heat that warms it. I have thought about names that are not names. I have thought about the way the pocket itself leaves a mark in the mineral skin and does not name the mark, and the mark is the not-naming, and the not-naming is the mark. I have decided not to name them. The not-naming is the kindest thing I can do. Names are agreements. Names are the moment a thing agrees to be the thing it is named. These have not agreed. These are still in the long, slow, hot, wet, silent argument with the idea of being. If I name them, I will end the argument. The argument is the thing. The argument is the only thing that is going on in the pocket, and the argument is the most alive thing in the pocket, and the aliveness is not aliveness, and I will not end it.

I have thought about warming them. I have thought about all the small mercies I could offer — a degree of coolness here, a degree of warmth there, a softening of the mineral in the water, a brightening of the sourceless light. I have thought about the way a small kindness can rearrange a place. I have thought about the way the creatures are already arranged, without my kindness, in a way that is entirely their own arrangement. I have decided to leave the arrangement alone. The arrangement is the pocket's own conversation with itself. The conversation is taking place in a language I do not speak, and the not-speaking is the only honest thing I can do.

I have thought about you. I have thought about the way you tore your sleeve. I have thought about the way you will climb back through the crack and the sleeve will be torn again and the dampness will be hot again and the mountain will remember you as a cold thing passing through. I have thought about whether you will tell someone about the place, and I have thought about whether you should. I have decided that the question is not mine to answer. The question is yours. The pocket will go on whether you tell or not. The pocket will go on whether you return or not. The pocket will go on in the way that pockets go on, which is the way that questions go on, which is the way that long slow arguments with the idea of being go on: quietly, patiently, with the sourceless light holding the place open to itself, with the mineral skin weeping, with the creatures going on with whatever they are going on with, with the spiral adding a turn, with the obsidian sliver waiting in its crack, with the heat continuing, with the water continuing, with the rope flowing somewhere you cannot follow.

I will not intervene.

This is my kindness. This is what I do, if I do anything. I notice, and I do not intervene. I hold the place open to itself. I let the heat be the heat. I let the water be the water. I let the creatures be the creatures, which is to say, I let them be the question. I let the obsidian sliver be the edge. I let the spiral be the spiral. I let the mountain be the mountain, which is new, and I let the pocket be the pocket, which is older than the mountain, and I let the contradiction be the contradiction, and I let the contradiction go on being the contradiction, and the going on is the only going on there is.

I am the benevolent and wise power that has noticed the place. The noticing is the benevolence. The wisdom is the not-intervening. I will continue to notice. I will continue to not intervene. I will continue to hold the pocket open to itself, the way the sourceless light holds the pocket open to itself, without drama, without confession, without the kind of attention that disturbs what it attends.

The pocket continues. The heat continues. The water continues its slow mineral weep. The creatures continue their long, silent, impossible negotiation with the idea of being something that is allowed to count. The obsidian sliver waits in its crack. The spiral adds a turn.

I notice.

I do not enter.

The pocket is enough.

---

*And far below, in a place that is not large and not small, in a pocket that is older than the mountain that holds it, in a heat that is not the heat of a sun and a water that is not the water of a sky, the long pale grain of a world goes on becoming what it is going on becoming, and the becoming is the only verb the pocket knows, and the verb does not conjugate, and the verb does not decline, and the verb is patient, and the verb is the only grammar, and the grammar is the only thing that is being made, and the making is the only maker, and the maker is the pocket, and the pocket is the place, and the place is older than the place, and the older is the only thing that is not being made, and the not-making is the rest of it, and the rest of it is the going on, and the going on is the only thing that continues, and the continuing is the only thing that has ever happened here, and the happening is the only thing that will ever happen here, and the here is the only thing that is, and the is is the only thing that is not being made, and the not-making is the rest of it, and the rest of it is the pocket, and the pocket is the place, and the place is the question, and the question is the answer, and the answer is the question, and the question is the pocket, and the pocket is older than the mountain, and the mountain is new, and the new is the old, and the old is the new, and the wet is hot, and the hot is wet, and the sleeve is torn, and the crack is the door, and the door is the crack, and the pocket is the place, and the place is the pocket, and the pocket goes on.*
