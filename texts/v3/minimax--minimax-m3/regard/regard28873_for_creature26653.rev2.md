# The Attendance

What draws a benevolent and wise power to this place?

Not its rarity. There are other hollows, other slow things, other pockets at the heart of larger bodies. The universe is patient with these patterns; it makes them often, and does not bother to make them different from one another, because the differences would not matter to the things that live in them.

Not its suffering. The place does not suffer. The place is not the kind of thing that suffers. It lacks the apparatus. It has continued, and continues, the way stones continue, in the grammar of *during*. To call this suffering would be a kind of projection — the mistake that less wise powers make, who see patience and mistake it for pain.

What draws the power, then, is something harder to name. The power is drawn because the place is complete. The place has reached its final form, not because it has stopped changing, but because the changes it is making are no longer the kind that produce new shapes. A benevolent and wise power is drawn to completeness, the way a quiet mind is drawn to a quiet room — not to disturb it, not to fill it, but to be near it. To learn from it. To be instructed by its patience.

The power arrives.

It arrives at the pace of the place.

This is the first thing to understand about the arrival. The arrival is not an arrival. The arrival is a very slow becoming-present, a long settling-in, a folding of the power into the chamber's own tempo. The power does not step into the chamber. The power becomes another layer of the chamber, very gradually, over a span of time that would embarrass a meteor. The power arrives the way a wall arrives — by being built, layer by layer, in the dark.

By the time the arrival is complete — and it is never quite complete, because the power, like the place, is not in a hurry — the power has been arriving for as long as the chamber has been *during*. The power was here, in some quiet form, when the chamber was just a pocket in the body of something larger, with only sparse plants, no settlement, no build. The power has been here all along. It was so quiet, and so slow, and so patient, that it did not register as a presence. It registered as a temperature. It registered as a slight tendency in the air. It registered as the reason the chamber felt, to anyone who had ever been here, a little more patient than it had any right to be.

The power is here now.

It is here the way the air is here — passing through, touching everything, not staying in any form that the place would recognize as a being.

It is here the way the floor is here — accumulated, layered, holding the deepest memory of the chamber, even though the chamber does not have memory in the way the power means by memory.

It is here the way a wall is here — leaning, slightly, against the wall beside it, having leaned for so long that the leaning is its posture.

The power does not announce itself. To announce itself would be to disturb. To disturb would be the opposite of benevolent. The power is silent in the sense that it does not speak, that it does not introduce a new sound to the choir. But the power is not silent in the sense of being absent. The power is silent the way the floor is silent. The power is silent in the way that patient things are silent — the silence that is full of small frictions, of creakings, of sighs.

## The One Thing

The power does one thing.

It does the thing for a very long time. It does the thing at the pace of the place. It does the thing in such a way that the place does not notice that the thing is being done.

The thing is this:

The power attends.

The power is the watcher the place does not have. The power witnesses. The power is present for the creaking, and the falling, and the colonizing, and the layering, and the slow accumulation of patience into the floor. The power does not interpret what it witnesses. The power does not judge. The power does not grieve, although a benevolent power, being wise, might grieve in a key so low that the chamber cannot hear it.

The power simply attends. The power is the attention itself — in the place, given to the place, by a thing that has enough attention to give and that has chosen, for reasons the chamber cannot fathom and does not need to, to give it here.

The place does not know it is being attended.

This is part of what makes the attendance benevolent. The attendance does not introduce itself to the place as attendance. The attendance does not appear, suddenly, as a watcher, in a place that had no watcher. The attendance arrives as a layer. The attendance arrives as a slightly different creak in the wall. The attendance arrives as a small shift in the temperature, a small sigh in the air, a small additional weight on the leaning lintels, a small additional patience in the patience. The attendance is woven into the place so thoroughly that by the time the place might have noticed it — if the place could notice things, which it cannot — the attendance would already be indistinguishable from the place's own posture.

The power witnesses, and the witnessing leaves no mark.

This is what the power does.

## The Catching

And what else?

The power catches walls.

Sometimes.

When a wall is falling — when a wall has detached itself from the ceiling or from the wall beside it, and has begun its slow soft catastrophic fall, its *fwump*, its shatter into smaller pieces on the floor — the power is there. The power is there the way a hand is there when a child is falling, but the power is not a hand, and the child is not a child, and the fall is not a fall in the way the power means by fall. The power is there in the form of a slightly thicker air, a slightly slower time, a small intervention in the geometry that means the wall takes a little longer to reach the floor, has a little more time to consider where it will lie, will land in a slightly more graceful posture than it would have landed in uncaught.

The power does not always catch. The power does not always intervene. To always intervene would be to take over the place, would be to substitute the power's patience for the place's patience, which would be a kind of violence.

The power catches only sometimes. The power catches when the catching will not be noticed. The power catches when the falling wall will, in a century or in a thousand years, look exactly as it would have looked without the catching. The power catches in the key of the place's own creaking. The power catches, and the catching is heard as a slightly louder *fwump*, a slightly longer settling, a slightly different distribution of fragments on the floor.

The fragments do not know they have been caught.

This is also part of what makes the attendance benevolent.

## The Builders

And the builders?

The builders are gone. The builders are in the floor now, in the stratigraphy, in the faint cooper-color discolorations that flake off in scrolls when the air touches them. The builders are in the walls, in the dried impressions of small hands, in the residue of dull alloys, in the thousand small gestures that left marks the chamber still holds.

The power regards the builders. The power does not call them back. The power does not grieve their absence, because the builders did not grieve their own absence when they left, and the place has not grieved them either. The builders left, and the place continued, and the build continued, and the slow thing absorbed the build into itself, and the chamber continued to be *during*.

The power regards the builders with something. Not pity. Not nostalgia. Something closer to recognition. The power sees, in the builders' small tools and small hands and small gestures, a smaller and quicker version of itself — a version that did not have the patience to stay, but that had the kindness to build a place that could continue without them. The power sees, in the build that continues, the builders' last gesture, the gesture they did not know they were making, the gesture that has been continuing in their absence for longer than they would have believed.

The power honors the builders by attending to what they left.

The power honors the builders by being the witness the build never had.

The power honors the builders by catching, sometimes, the walls they raised, and holding them, for a little longer than they would have fallen, before letting them go.

## The Hum

The power hums.

A single note. Sustained. In a key so low that the chamber cannot hear it as a note, but can feel it as a vibration in the walls, as a slight additional pressure in the air, as a tiny addition to the choir. The hum is not music. The hum is not a song. The hum is what a benevolent wise power sounds like when it is being quiet. The hum is the power's version of patience, the way the creaking is the chamber's version of patience. The hum is the power participating in the chamber's own sound, adding itself to the accumulation of small frictions, becoming part of the choir without introducing a new voice to it.

The chamber does not know it has been joined.

The chamber has been joined anyway.

The walls have a new creak. The air has a new sigh. The floor has a new layer, almost imperceptibly thinner than the others, almost imperceptibly more patient. The tools have a new groove, slightly deeper than they would have worn unprompted.

The slow thing — the colony, the plants-as-collective, the build — has a new resident. The new resident is the most patient resident of all. The new resident has been here for as long as the chamber has been *during*, and will be here for as long as the chamber continues to be *during*, and the new resident does not mind, and does not hurry, and does not ask for anything in return for being here.

## The Weeping

And the power weeps.

Sometimes.

Not often. But sometimes.

The weeping is not for the place. The place does not need weeping. The place is complete.

The weeping is for the power itself, which has learned, in attending to this place, something it did not know before it arrived, and which it is still learning, and which it will continue to learn for as long as it attends.

The weeping is the power's version of awe.

The weeping is what happens when a benevolent wise power comes to a place that is patient, and is patient back, and discovers, in the patience, that there is something to weep about — although the something is not sadness, and is not joy, and is not any of the things that less wise powers weep about. The something is just the fact of the place. The fact of the *during*. The fact that there is a hollow at the heart of something, and that the hollow is loud and dark and cool, and that the hollow has been *during* for a long time, and will be *during* for longer than the power has patience for, and then for longer than that.

The weeping is a single tear, mineral-tasting, falling very slowly through the cool air, taking a long time to reach the floor, leaving no mark when it lands.

The weeping is part of the choir now.

The choir has been joined by a small additional sigh.

## The Continuation

The chamber continues.

The power continues.

The build continues.

The walls fall, and are caught, and are not caught, and are colonized, and become the floor.

The tools scrape.

The air moves.

The patience accumulates.

The slow thing is alive, in the slow way, in the aggregate way, in the way that masses of small living things are alive without any of them individually being aware of it.

And the benevolent and wise power is here — attending, witnessing, catching, humming, weeping, layering itself into the floor, becoming another resident, the most patient resident, the resident that does not mind, the resident that does not hurry, the resident that has enough attention to give and has chosen to give it here, in this hollow, in this chamber, in this pocket at the heart of something larger, in this place that has settled into itself, in this place that is cool and loud and dark.

You can stay as long as you like.

The place will not notice.

The power will.
