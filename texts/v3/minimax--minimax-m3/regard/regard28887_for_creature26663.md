# The Tending of the Hollow

A benevolent and wise power, in time, becomes aware of the hollow.

It does not arrive. There is no moment of descent, no parting of canopy, no sound of a great footfall on the basalt. Awareness, for such a power, is a thing that happens the way the seep happens — slowly, through cracks, in a way that has always been possible and is only now, for whatever reason, becoming actual. The power notices the hollow the way one notices a small sound that has been there for a long time and has only just become audible above the larger noises of the world. It does not, in noticing, change the hollow. Not yet. The hollow keeps breathing. The columns keep crossing. The dwellers keep drinking. The basalt keeps settling into its long memory of being a wound.

What the power sees, when it attends, is a place that is metabolizing.

It sees the seep first — the warm mineral upwelling through the cracked basalt at the lowest part of the basin, tasting of iron and salt and sulfur and a faint, almost floral rot, carrying copper and stranger metals, glowing faintly where it pools in the deeper cracks. It sees the stacked economy of moisture that is not moisture, the tier on tier of strange plants drinking each other in the dim green light. It sees the great rosettes, table-wide, weeping their not-water from the tips of their fleshy leaves. It sees the pillar-fungi, pallid and tall, ringed in shelves like the pages of a book no one is reading. It sees the syrup-bamboo, knobbed with black wax, scoring itself slow amber when cut. It sees the coral-thing that flinches when you step near. It sees the red furred carpet that hums underfoot, as if the ground itself is unwell, and is not.

It sees the fauna. The columns of jointed dark things moving in their thick ropes along the vines. The pale worms as thick as a wrist levering themselves between the basalt cracks. The flat many-winged comma-things roosting in clustered commas on the undersides of leaves. And it sees, with the part of its attention that is older than seeing, the larger things — the slow displacements in the undergrowth, the weights that bow the vines, the careful, deliberate crunches that go on too long to be accidental. The hunters, the grazers, the things that eat the things that eat the things. The hollow is a stomach with views, and the power understands the view.

It sees the dwellers.

Small. Dark. Brown-black from filtered light, deep black from skin that has never seen a sun that was not a hundred feet of canopy away. Wide and very wet eyes, the whites faintly yellowed, as if they are always a little unwell. They are not unwell. They are always a little lit. Their hair is long and matted with the amber syrup, platted into plates that shed the dripping condensation. Their hands are calloused in patterns that would tell a traveler everything about their work, if the traveler knew how to read hands, which travelers never do. The right thumb is horn-hard from the cutting. The left palm is ridged from the basket-weave. The fingertips of both are stained black from the seep, permanently, as if dipped in ink.

There are perhaps a hundred and twenty of them. Fewer in the wet months, when the vents run heavy and the air is thick enough to chew. More, a little, in the dry months — though dry here is a relative thing, and what others would call dry they would call pleasant, breathable, the season of the long walks. They have names for the seasons that are not the seasons of anywhere else. *Tall-fungus year. Leaning year. Year of the Pale Seep.* They keep no calendar. They do not need to. The fungus is the calendar. The fungus is many things that are not a calendar, and the calendar is the least of them.

The power watches a child being brought, in its first days, to the warm cracked basalt at the lowest part of the basin. It watches the mouth being held to the surface. It watches the first taste — iron and salt and a faint, almost floral rot, the taste the elders say of the mountain's own mouth. The child screams, or does not. Either is acceptable. The power understands this. The power understands, in its own long way, that either is acceptable because the dwellers are not in the business of deciding what the mountain should taste like. The mountain tastes like the mountain. The child meets the mountain. That is all.

The power watches a toddler set down beside a scored bamboo-stalk, shown how to lick the slow amber syrup. It watches a child of three taught to pluck a single jointed dark thing from the rope of its column, to bite the head off without hesitation, because hesitation is what the body cannot bear. It watches an elder draw the shadow of a larger thing in the dust with a fingertip, and a child learn the shadow before the name, and the power understands that this is considered correct.

It watches the high ones, the same people in their ones and twos, who have gone up into the canopy and not come down, or come down and gone up again, or live between, moving between floor and upper dim with a fluency the floor-dwellers admire without envy.
