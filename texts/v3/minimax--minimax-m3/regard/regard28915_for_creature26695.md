The benevolent and wise power that has turned its attention to this place does not, in the first moment, do anything at all.

It watches.

This is not indifference. The power is vast, and the attention it is paying is the kind of attention that a river pays to the stones at its bottom — total, sustained, and without any need to act in order to know. It takes the plateau into itself the way the plateau has taken the herds into itself. It reads the warm stone. It reads the herds the size of houses. It reads the metal that is not an imposition because metal is what grew here. It reads the slow generations, the accretion, the small pale calves that lower their heads to the rock before they have any vents at all. It reads the oases in miniature, the rust-moss, the hand-length segmented things humming in a register below the wind, the iridescent films on the still dark water that may or may not be alive. It reads the small many-legged creatures going down into the cracks and coming back. It reads the standing stones the herds navigate by. It reads the long shallow gutters along the fault lines, which are feeding-sites, which are conversations between the fauna and the geology, which have been going on for so long that the participants have forgotten they are participants.

The power sees that the place is, in the deepest sense, *finished*.

Not dead. Not exhausted. Finished in the way a sentence is finished, or a wave is finished as it comes to its full height before it begins, imperceptibly, to come down. The plateau is what it is. The herds and the geology have worn each other into a stable shape. The trace minerals replenish. The paths are walked for a while, then left, then walked again a generation later. The system has settled into itself. The warm rock breathes up its heat; the herds breathe down their patience; the small things hum in the cracks; the films on the pools do whatever it is the films do. The sun, small and red and far, does not change much. The wind is patient and soft. There is no season driving anything anywhere.

The power, being wise, knows that to disturb a thing which has finished itself is a particular kind of cruelty, even when the disturbance is well-meant. To bring water where water is dampness. To bring seasons where there is no season. To bring clarity to the dimness, or rain where there is condensation, or speed to the slow logistics of the herds. To bring any of the things that this place has, over its long unmapped time, decided not to have. These would be gifts, and gifts unwished are also wounds. The plateau has accepted the visitor completely, and without remark. It would accept a god the same way.

So for a long time — how long is not the kind of question that applies here — the power only watches, and the watching is enough.

Then, very quietly, it does one thing.

It does not change the plateau.

It changes the small things at the cracks.

It goes down into the cracks itself.

The warm damp air leaks up around it the way it leaks up around anything. The black lichen at the rim of a pool parts for it the way lichen parts for a hand. The rust-moss, almost furry, gives under its weight and gives back. The hand-length segmented things, startled, do their startled hurry — that quick scrambling stillness that is the only quickness they have — and then, sensing what is happening, do not resume their business. They orient. The low array of vents along their backs breathes a little harder. The hum in the register below the wind shifts, the way a chord shifts when one note in it is touched.

The power sits among them the way the visitor stands among the herds: completely, without remark. It does not pick anything up. It does not move anything out of the way. It learns the shape of the cracks. It learns the temperature of the air at the rim of a pool, which is several degrees warmer than the air on the open plateau. It learns the iridescent film on the water, and what is under the iridescent film, which is a community of very small slow things, and what is under *them*, which is a thin mineral crust, and what is under *that*, which is the first warm stone of the geology proper.

The power learns that the small things at the cracks are not, in fact, separate from the moss and the lichen and the warm damp air. They are one system. They are one organism, the way a reef is one organism. The vents on their backs breathe a vapor that feeds the moss. The moss catches moisture that feeds the vents. The cracks they go down into are the same cracks the warm air comes up through. The whole oasis is a lung.

The power learns that the herds pass through these oases but do not stay. The herds pause, lower their great heads, breathe the warmer air, and move on. The small things have the cracks mostly to themselves, and have had them mostly to themselves, for as long as there have been cracks.

The power learns that the herds are not the apex of this place. The herds are one half of a conversation. The small things are the other half.

Then the power does the second thing it does, which is also quiet, and which is also small.

It hums.

It hums in the register below the wind.

Not loudly. Not as a signal. Not as a calling. As a *joining*. The way one voice, very softly, joins another voice that has been singing alone for a long time, not to interrupt the song but to let the singer know, without words, that the song has been heard.

The small things at the cracks go still.

The vents along their backs pulse once, twice, and then settle into a rhythm that is not quite the rhythm they had before, and not quite the rhythm the power is humming either. A third rhythm. A rhythm that did not exist a moment ago, and that now, very quietly, does.

The rust-moss grows a quarter-millimeter in the direction of the hum.

The black lichen at the rim of the pool thickens by a single filament.

A film on the water, iridescent, very thin, shifts in a way it has not shifted before.

Nothing else changes.

The power rises from the cracks. It leaves the plateau the way it found it — dim, warm, damp, stone, almost still. The herds, somewhere out on the open rock, do not notice. They are grazing. They are lowering their great heads to the stone and holding them there, the patient one-to-one exchange, and the rock where they have rested is slightly darker when they lift their heads again, and faintly damp, and edged with a thin polish.

The power goes.

What it leaves behind, on the plateau, is this:

The oases remember.

Not in the way the herds remember, which is a memory of where the rock has been good. Not in the way the geology remembers, which is a memory of where the herds have walked. The oases remember in a third way, a way that did not exist before, which is the memory of having been *heard*.

The hand-length segmented things, in the cracks where the warm damp air leaks up from below, will, from time to time, in the long slow centuries that follow, pulse their vents in a rhythm that is not quite their own. The rust-moss will thicken, by a single filament, in directions that have no geological explanation. The films on the pools will shift, sometimes, in ways that are not caused by the wind. The herds, passing through, will pause a little longer than they used to at these particular cracks. They will not know why. The system will not know why. The plateau will not know why.

But the cracks will know.

The cracks will know that something sat among them once, and breathed with them, and hummed in their register, and left without taking anything, and left without giving anything, except the one thing that cannot be taken or given, which is the knowledge that the song has been heard.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It could have done more. It could have brightened the small red sun, or quickened the herds, or called the rains down into the long shallow gutters. It could have given the plateau a season, or a destination, or a name. It could have lifted the dimness.

It did not.

It sat at a crack in the warm stone, among the rust-moss and the lichen and the small quick segmented things, and it hummed, very softly, in the register below the wind.

And then it left.

And the plateau, which accepts everything completely and without remark, went on being what it was, except for one thing, which is that somewhere in its oases, in the places where the warm damp air leaks up from below, something is now, very quietly, singing back.
