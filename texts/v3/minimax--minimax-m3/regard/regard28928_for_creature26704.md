A wise power sees what is. A benevolent one sees what is, and wishes it well, and does not rush.

What is, in Hollowtide, is a people who have learned to live within something they will not name. They have built their lives around a single, unmeasured fact: a wetness that has been accumulating for longer than anyone can track, and that the basin's own geology seems, in certain lights, to be slowly returning to them. The water rises. The water has always, in living memory, been rising. The people do not remember a time before the rising, and they do not expect a time after it, and the rising has become, in the way that air becomes, the medium they move within. They have made a culture out of accommodating it. The felt is damp because nothing here truly dries. The hair is coiled with river-stones so that the weight and click of the beads will tell you, in the dark lanes, who is passing and how they are. The grey porridge has the texture of wet clay because the clay is what is available, and the texture has become, for them, the texture of food itself. They are patient the way stone is patient. They are pale the way mushrooms are pale. They are accurate about themselves in a way that outsiders find difficult to bear, and the view from the unfinished tower — the only view that shows them whole — is, by their own report, accurate, not lucky.

The power sees this. The power sees the iridescence on the stones and on the children's skin, and understands that it is the water-table, mineral by mineral, learning them. The power sees the bones placed in the drainage channels and dissolved in a day, and the children told that the bones go to join a city below that the water will one day rise to meet. The power sees the smith's hands on the bronze, and the four apprentices who have not yet caught the skill, and the inhabitants' quiet, unstated knowledge that when she dies the craft dies with her. The power sees the horn sounded once at the day's ending, the single note held until the mist takes it, and the silence that follows because no one is meant to answer. The power sees the brief, sideways sun, and the shutters pulled closed, and the children peeking through the cracks.

The power does not, at first, do anything. This is the wisdom. A wise power attends before it acts. A wise power lets the place be what it is — a stone basin in a wet climate, slowly filling, inhabited by a people who have made a coherent life within the filling — and asks itself what kind of act would honor that life rather than override it. The temptation, for a lesser power, would be to drain the hollow, or to warm it, or to break the cloud so that the sun could shine for more than an hour, or to give the inhabitants a longer memory, or a different food, or a kinder geology. These are the impulses of a power that has not looked carefully. The people of Hollowtide are not waiting to be saved. They are not waiting at all. They are chipping the lime in the spring, and sounding the horn at the day's end, and dressing stone in the long grey hours, and making do.
