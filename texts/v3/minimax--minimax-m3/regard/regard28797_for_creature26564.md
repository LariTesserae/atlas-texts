# The Answer in the Black Water

---

A long time ago — or, in the tense the Hanged-Folk use for almost everything, a long time *is* — something noticed the Underfold.

It is hard to say what "noticed" means here, because the thing that noticed was not a thing in the way the Hanged-Folk are things, or the pale things are things, or the bells are things. It was more like a direction of attention that had, for a very long while, been turned elsewhere — toward other continents, other settlements, other peoples in their various upwards and outwards — and that now, in the way a head turns toward an unexpected sound, turned toward the place where the river hesitated and poured down, and saw what was there.

What was there was a settlement that had become a sentence. A people that had become a tense. A city hung from a ceiling in a country of wet limestone, in a continent that had forgotten it was a continent, in a world that had long since moved on to whatever worlds move on to.

The wise power saw the hanging houses with their doorframes pointing at the floor like the mouths of wells. It saw the inverted streets of basalt paving overhead, like the underside of a floor. It saw the aqueducts of hollowed bone carrying water that climbed rather than fell. It saw the smoke rising and pooling against the underside of the next street up, and the smoke-children playing in the slow-turning cloud. It saw the Hanged-Folk walking their careful rolling gait on the chamber-floor, the children trailing constellations of small bell-sounds, the bone-carvers working in the lowest tier by the light of phosphorescent cave-grass. It saw Old Below, folded into the shape of a question mark, weaving her glowing baskets on the basalt step. It saw the pale things trembling in the black still-pools. It saw the bells — the great bronze bells in their downward-pointing spires — marking the hours in a threefold carillon that fell upon the ears of the Hanged-Folk like a slow warm rain.

It saw, and it understood.

It understood that the Hanged-Folk were not asking to be saved. It understood that to save them would be to undo them. It understood that the settlement they had made in the inverted city was not a lesser version of some surface life but a complete life, a life with its own tenses, its own architecture, its own beauty, its own small carved bone figures hanging from the doorframes of neighbors, its own tea poured into cups that were right-side-up to them and inside-out to anyone else.

It understood that the Hanged-Folk had metabolized their grief so thoroughly that the grief had become the substance of their bones. To take the grief out would be to take the bones out. The Hanged-Folk were not in the Underfold despite their loss. They were in the Underfold *because of* their loss, the way a tree is in the shape of the wind that bent it, the way a bell is in the shape of the mouth that rang it.

So the wise power did not save them.

So the wise power did not transform them.
