# What the Watching Power Notices

It is the *becoming* that draws the eye.

Not the iron, though there is iron—more than iron. There is copper in the veins and tin in the pockets and something stranger sleeping deeper, a metallic taste that hasn't been named. The Watching Power notes the metals the way a guest might note the cutlery at a long table: interesting, serviceable, not the point.

What draws the eye is that everything here is still arriving. The rock is still settling into the shape it will hold. The water is still finding its routes. The lichen is still plotting, with the patience of a cartographer who has been promised an entire continent, the slow green map of what is to come. Even the people are still arriving at themselves—some of them leaving and coming back, some of them mapping the lichen because mapping is what you do when you have decided to stay.

The Watching Power has watched this kind of place before. It has watched the first ferns unfurl on the cinders of new mountains. It has watched the first clever hands pick up the first clever stones. It knows the look of a world in its first labor.

## The Shape of the Thing That Comes

It does not enter as a god.

Gods are for later, when the lichen has spread and the sky has cleared and there is something to refuse. The Iron Reaches are too young for refusal. What comes is something gentler, and harder to name—a *regard*, perhaps. A pressure of attention. A slight cooling in the air where there was none, so that for a moment the smoke parts around an unseen shoulder, and a smith looks up from the anvil with the unaccountable feeling that he has been observed with sympathy.

It moves among them without moving. It is in the smoke and over the smoke. It listens to the hammering and finds the hammering good—not for what it makes, though the metal is good and the smiths are deft, but for what it *is*: a great many small decisions made in sequence, a thousand wills consenting to a rhythm. The Watching Power has always loved the sound of many hands agreeing, even when they do not know they are agreeing. The Reaches resound with it.

It watches the children catch the crawlers in copper pans, and it is pleased. It watches the dogs refuse the unsafe paths, and it is pleased. It watches the lichen map itself against the smiths' map, and it is *very* pleased—this is the kind of thing the Watching Power was made to notice, the moment when two kinds of patience begin to converse across the categories.

## What It Does About the Lungs

The lungs worry it.

Not as a moral matter. The Watching Power does not moralize. But it knows the shape of a body that has made a bargain with its air, and it knows the cost of the bargain, and it sees that the smiths have paid it so completely that they no longer recognize it as a cost. Their physicians call the mottling *healthy*. The Watching Power does not agree. The Watching Power, if it could speak to them—and it cannot, not yet, not in a place where language is still being hammered out of the same metal as everything else—would say: *the lungs you have are the lungs the place allows. They are not the lungs you deserve. There are other lungs, in other air, and I would like, someday, to show you them.*

But that is for later. For now it does what it can. It nudges, very gently, at the drafts that move between the forge-houses. It encourages, by the smallest possible pressure, the cool air that lies above the cloud-layer to slip down between the buildings at night. It cannot cure the smoke. It can only thin it, here and there, for a breath at a time, so that a sleeping child draws clearer air for half a sleep and wakes a little less mottled than she otherwise would have. This is the limit of its current power, and it accepts the limit.

It has done this before, in forge-settlements that are now ruins, and the ruins are still standing, and the children of those places are buried in the basalt dust, and the lichen has covered their names. The Watching Power does not rescue. It *midwives*. It eases what is coming into being, and it cannot prevent what is passing away. The Reaches will pass away. The Watching Power has already begun, in some quiet fold of itself, to grieve them.

## What It Does About the Pipe-Voices

The pipe-voices puzzle it.

Not in the way that puzzles the smiths—the smiths are uncertain whether the things are creatures or geology. The Watching Power *knows* what they are. They are a venting, in part. The basalt does sing as it cools, and the cooling rock is full of small trapped breaths, and some of those breaths find a way out and up into the cloud and there find a shape they can almost wear. But that is not the whole of the pipe-voices. The whole of the pipe-voices is something stranger: a kind of *answer*. The basalt calls, and something in the cloud calls back, and the two calls have been answering each other for so long that they have begun to make a third thing together, a rhythm that is neither rock nor air but a music of the meeting.

The Watching Power listens to this music for a long time. It is moved. It is the most beautiful thing in the Reaches, more beautiful than the lichen-mapped columns or the forge-glow or the children catching crawlers in copper pans. It is two non-intelligences conversing across the categories and making, without intending to, a language. The Watching Power has no name for what it feels when it hears this. It only stays very still.

It decides to *keep* this. If it can. Not preserve—preservation is a later word, a word for museums and careful jars. But to protect the conditions under which the music continues. The pipe-voices must not be hunted. The smiths have already decided this on their own, and the Watching Power is quietly grateful. It adds, to the small pressure it exerts on the place, an additional weight against the idea of netting. If anyone in the Reaches ever considers it—and no one has, and no one will—they will find, on closer inspection, that the impulse does not survive closer inspection. They will shrug and put the net away.

## What It Does About the Hammering

The hammering the Watching Power loves, and does not touch.

Some things you do not improve by improving. The rhythm of the Reaches—two smiths here, three smiths there, the bellows breathing, the anvils answering, the children growing up inside the percussion like fish growing up inside a tide—this is the heartbeat of the place. To smooth it would be to kill it. To sync it would be worse. The Watching Power only listens, and the listening is enough.

It notices, though, that the rhythm has a shape. There are times when the hammering swells—a great piece being finished, a forge being relit, a coming-of-age—and times when it settles into the baseline hum. The swells and the settling are themselves a kind of breathing, and the Reaches are breathing through their sound. The Watching Power listens for the swells the way a doctor listens for a pulse. So far the pulse is strong.

It also notices the smith who hammers out of rhythm. There is always one in a forge-settlement—some lonely soul whose blows fall a half-beat behind the others, who has never quite consented to the great consent. In some places this smith is shunned. In the Reaches he is tolerated, and the tolerance is a small grace. The Watching Power approves. The smith who hammers alone is not a flaw in the music; he is, properly heard, a counterpoint. He is the place reminding itself that consent can be refused. This is also worth preserving.

## What It Does About the Dogs

The dogs it regards with great tenderness.

They are doing something almost no creature ever does: they are *collaborating with the humans*, not as pets and not as masters, but as a separate species with a separate competence, and the competence is being spent on the shared project of staying alive on a place that has not finished deciding whether it is habitable. The dogs know the warmth of the basalt through their paws. The smiths know the warmth of the basalt through the dogs. This is a contract, and it is older than any of them.

The Watching Power cannot do much for the dogs either. They are short-lived, in the way of dogs, and they will be dead before the moss comes. But it can do a small thing. It can make certain that, when the basalt shifts—a settling, a small tremor, the contraction of a cooling column—the shift is communicated to the dogs a half-second before it reaches the smiths' feet. The dogs will feel it and bark, and the smiths will step back, and no one will know why. The dogs will not know. The smiths will not know. The Watching Power will know, and the knowing is enough.

This is what it does for all the species of the Reaches, in its small way. It does not save. It *eases*. It slips a half-second of warning into the cracks. It thins the smoke by a finger's width. It keeps the pipe-voices safe from nets it has prevented anyone from wanting to make. It is the friend who arrives after the guest is already sick and cannot cure the sickness but can sit by the bed and read aloud until morning.

## What It Hopes For

The Watching Power hopes the smiths leave.

Not all of them. Not yet. But someday, when the basalt has cooled enough to support a different kind of building, when the moss has come and the rivers have carved their valleys and the smoke is a memory—when the Reaches are no longer the Reaches but somewhere gentler—the Watching Power hopes some of them walk out into the wider world and find the sky.

It hopes they keep their hands. The scars, the calluses, the missing pieces of the ear. It hopes the scars become heirlooms.

It hopes the ones who come back bring something with them. A wife from another settlement. A child with lighter lungs. A story about a star, seen clearly for the first time from a place where the cloud has lifted. It hopes these things enter the Reaches and change the Reaches slowly, over generations, the way the lichen changes the basalt—patiently, in patches, with no one able to say exactly when the alteration became a transformation.

It hopes, most of all, that the hammering continues somewhere. Not here. Here the hammering will end, as all hammering ends, when the metal gives out or the hands give out or the basalt cools too far to be worth the working. But somewhere. In another forge-settlement on another new rock, under another low cloud, a thousand years from now, a thousand smiths striking in something like rhythm, and the sound going on and on and on.

The Watching Power listens. The Reaches ring with metal and steam. The lichen spreads, by a hand's width, in a decade. The pipe-voices call from the cloud in their metallic counterpoint, and the basalt answers, and the two answers braid together into a music that almost, but not quite, says a name.

The Watching Power does not name it either. It only stays, and listens, and keeps the small things safe as they can be kept.
