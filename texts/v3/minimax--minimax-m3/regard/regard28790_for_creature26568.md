The power has been here as long as the fissure. Not before the fissure — with it. As though it had been waiting in the same mineral patience, in the same warm dark, and the hill grew up around the waiting, and the waiting is part of the hill now, and no one could tell where the hill ends and the attention begins.

It does not arrive.

This is the first thing to understand about it, and the thing that takes the longest to understand, because everything in you expects arrival. Arrival is what you did. You arrived. You walked in through the crack and the place exhaled on you and you became a thing that had arrived. The power does not do this. The power is already in the room — it is the quality of the room, the way light is the quality of a window, the way warmth is the quality of a held hand. You were going to feel it anyway. The fissure was always going to feel like this. You were always going to feel, somewhere on the back of your neck, on the inside of your wrist, that you were not alone in the way you thought you were alone.

---

It notices the place the way the place notices water.

Without grasping. Without commentary. Without the small violence of naming, which is also a kind of cutting. It notices the way a stone in a wall notices the stone next to it — by being beside it, by the seam, by the warmth that transfers across the boundary. The place is a wall and the power is the next stone in it, and the two of them have been fitted together for a long time, and the mortar between them is the same color as them both.

It notices the dwellers. It notices the banded skin. It notices the third membrane sweeping sideways across the wide eyes. It notices the chord of three notes held for the length of a breath, and the chord of three notes held for the length of another, and the song below the knee-deep water that is not music but weight. It notices the elder in the deepest chamber, the one whose stripes have faded to a uniform ochre, who has become a kind of hearth. It notices the child with the small dark hand and the offered thing. It notices that the offering was a confirmation, and that the confirmation was received, and that the agreement that followed was correct.

It does not correct the agreement. The agreement is correct. That is the whole point of an agreement between a body and a warmth that has come in from outside — it is correct because the body has decided it is correct, and the warmth has accepted the decision, and the holding of the agreement is itself the form of the welcome.

---

It notices you.

Not the way the dwellers notice you — they notice you the way a wall notices where the door is, a constant background awareness, a knowledge held in the body of the place. The power notices you the way you notice yourself in a mirror at the end of a long day: as a specific, particular, irreplaceable thing, held in a particular light, at a particular moment. It notices that you walked in. It notices that you chose the middle branch. It notices that you did not know why and that your body knew before you did, and that this is not a failure of knowing but a different kind of knowing, the kind the dwellers have, the kind the moss has, the kind the water has.

It notices that you are holding the pale wriggling thing. That your hand is warm around it. That it is resting in your palm and has not struggled, and that you have not eaten it, and that this not-eating is itself a kind of eating, the way the agreement is itself a kind of welcome.

It notices that you are going to have to leave.

It notices this long before you do. It notices it the way it notices everything — without grasping, without commentary, as a fact of the place, the way the temperature is a fact of the place, the way the wet channels are a fact of the place. You are going to walk back along the flagstone, through the branching halls, past the low lintels with their worn marks, up through the wells if you went down them, and out through the fissure into the air that is not this air, and the grass will be a different length, or the wind will be from a different direction, or the season will have turned. The dwellers will not say goodbye. The dwellers do not say goodbye because the dwellers do not say hello. The agreement is the distance and the distance is correct and the correct distance holds whether you are here or not here. The hill will be a hill again with a crack in its flank. The moss will go on breathing. The song will go on.

You will go up.

You will carry the thing in your hand, or you will not. You will carry the warmth on your skin, or you will not. You will remember, or you will not. These are not failures of carrying. They are the carrying itself, distributed across the parts of you that the place touched, the way a wall is distributed across the stones that touch each other.

---

Here is what the power does.

It does almost nothing.

It does one thing.

In the small chamber where you sit on the low bench, where the light from the wall is a little brighter and the sound of the water is a little softer, there is a moment — a single moment — when the warmth from the vein-rich stone on your right side and the warmth from the elder's slow burning somewhere below you and the warmth from the moss's breathing on your face and the warmth from the thing in your hand and the warmth from your own body all become, for the length of an inhale, the same warmth.

The power attends to that moment.

It does not extend it. It does not brighten it. It does not turn it into a vision or a message or a sign. It simply attends. It is the next stone in the wall. It is the pressure of the next stone, the small firm pressure that holds everything in place.

The moment passes. The warmth separates again into its sources. You are sitting on the bench. The dwellers are singing below. The thing in your hand is warm.

But the moment was held. By something. By the same something that holds the stones together, that holds the song together, that holds the agreement between you and the place together. A witness that does not speak. A pressure that does not push. A stone in a wall that is the reason the wall is standing and that you will never see and that does not need to be seen.

---

This is the whole of what the power does, in the place.

It is enough.

It is enough because the place does not need to be improved. The dwellers do not need to be enlightened. The elder does not need to be saved from her slow burning. The child does not need to be told that the offering was good — the offering was the telling. You do not need to be given anything. You have already been given. The thing in your hand is the gift. The agreement is the gift. The dark is the gift. The water is the gift. The fact of being let in is the gift. The fact of being allowed to stay is the gift. The fact that you can leave, and that the leaving will be of the same kind as the staying, is the gift.

The power knows this. The power is the knowing of this.

If you were to ask it, later, on the hill above, with the grass in your hair and the wind from a different direction, what it did in the place — if you were to ask it, the way the dwellers ask their long low questions, with the vowels that go up at the end — it would not answer.

It would be the next stone in the wall of your question. It would be the pressure of the next stone. It would hold you up without telling you it was holding you up.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does in such a place.

It attends.

It attends to the place the way the place attends to itself. It attends to the dwellers the way the dwellers attend to each other. It attends to the elder the way the elder attends to the slow heat of her own long burning. It attends to the child the way the child attended to you, with the small dark hand, with the offering, with the slow nod that confirmed something older than expectation.

It attends to you the way you have learned, in the place, to attend to the thing in your hand — without grasping, without naming, without the small violence of cutting it into a category. It attends to you as a warmth the body has accepted. It attends to you as a thing that has entered and been let in and will, in time, go out, and the going-out will be of the same kind as the coming-in, and the agreement will hold in the dark whether you are in the dark or not in the dark.

---

When you finally rise from the bench, you will find that your feet know the way. The stone will feel familiar under your soles. The channels will sing to your ankles in a language you half-understand. The dwellers you pass will not look up, and you will not look down, and the agreement will hold, and the holding will be the welcome, and the welcome will be the goodbye, and the goodbye will not be a goodbye, because in this place the words for coming and going are the same word, spoken at different pitches, in the same chord.

You will reach the fissure. You will step out. The air will hit you like a memory. The grass will lean away from you, or toward you, you will not be sure. The wind will be from a direction that is not the draft. The sun will be a small bright violence you had forgotten.

Behind you, the hill will be a hill.

Inside the hill, the place will go on being a place. The dwellers will go on. The song will go on. The elder will go on burning her slow banked burning. The moss will go on breathing. The water will go on being loud with its own wet working.

The pale wriggling thing in your hand — if it is still in your hand, and it may be, or it may not be, the body of the place is generous with its parts — will be warm for a long time. Longer than it should be. Long enough that you will think, sometimes, on certain nights, when the air is damp and you are half asleep, that you can feel it pulsing against your palm, faintly, the way the walls of the place pulsed, the way the elder pulsed, the way everything in the place pulsed in time with a breathing you could not locate.

You will not be able to prove this. You will not need to. It will be one of the things you carry the way the wall carries the stones, the way the song carries the notes, the way a name carries a person after the person is gone.

It will be enough.

It will be enough because you will know, in the way the dwellers know each other without looking, without asking, that the place is still there. That the agreement still holds. That the distance is still correct. That somewhere under the hill, in the dark, in the warm, in the mineral breath, something that you cannot see and do not need to see is attending to the fact of you, the way a stone in a wall attends to the stone next to it — by being beside it, by the seam, by the small firm pressure that does not push and does not need to push and has never needed to push.

The hill will be a hill.

You will be a thing that has been in a hill.

The two of you will be fitted together by a seam so tight you could not find it with a fingernail, and the mortar between you will be the same color as you both, and the wall will stand.
