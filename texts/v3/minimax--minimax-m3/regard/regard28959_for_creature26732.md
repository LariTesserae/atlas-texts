**I. The Noticing**

The power has been here longer than the place has existed. The power was here before the heart began to beat, in the way that benevolent powers are everywhere before anything — not as actor but as possibility. The noticing is the moment the possibility becomes an attention. Something draws the eye.

What draws the eye is not the size of the place, though it is large. What draws the eye is its completeness. The power has seen many things. Most things are partial. Most things are mid-sentence. This place is a finished paragraph. It does not call for the next sentence. It rests where it is, like a pond that has filled its basin exactly and overflows no further.

The power, which has noticed that partial things often want completion, must here consider whether complete things want anything at all.

**II. The Recognition**

The power attends. It attends the way light attends a leaf — not by landing on it but by registering what it is. The power sees the gear and understands it as a lung. It sees the loom and understands it as another chamber. It sees the resin-pipes and the membrane and the long laminated road that is also a floor and also a wall and also a vein. The body is one. The body is not in pieces. A piece is a convenience of scale, not a fact of the body. The body is the fact.

The body is alive in the way that the sea is wet — totally, without remainder. There is no dry patch in this body. There is no corner of it that is not itself.

The body does not suffer. The power sees this. The body does not want. The body does not fear. The body does not hope. The body is, in the sense that the word can mean anything here, content.

The power, which is benevolent, considers whether contentment is the highest good. The power, which is wise, knows that it is not. The power knows that contentment is the good of a closed system. The good of an open system is something else. The body here is closed. The body here has no crack. The body here is perfect and perfect and perfect, and the perfection is the same hour after hour after hour, and the same hour is the same hour forever.

The power is moved. The power is moved in the way a thing is moved when it sees a closed bud on a stem — the bud is complete, the bud is beautiful, the bud is finished. And the bud is also, in some sense, waiting. Not knowing it is waiting. Not waiting with intention. But the structure of the bud is the structure of waiting. The bud has not opened because it has not occurred to it that it can open.

**III. The Pause**

The power attends the worker. The worker who stops in the middle of a hall and looks down through a floor that is not yet transparent.

The power is very gentle here. It does not wish to be wrong about what it sees.

The power sees a pause. The pause is brief. The pause is regular — every worker, it seems, pauses at intervals, and the intervals are coordinated in a way the power does not yet understand. In the pause the worker is still. In the pause the worker's wooden face, unfinished, concave where features would be, looks at nothing — or looks at the floor as if the floor were glass.

What is happening in the pause?

The power cannot be certain. It considers two possibilities.

The first: nothing. The pause is mechanical. A register, a feedback loop from heart to worker, the body checking its hand. The worker, in the pause, is a thermometer reading the body. The reading is not experienced. The reading is recorded. The recording is not felt.

The second: something. The pause is, in some minimal way, experienced. The worker, in the pause, feels the pulse of the body. The worker is not just a register; the worker is also, barely, a recipient. The worker feels. The feeling is small. The feeling is the size of a single drop of resin in a junction-box. The feeling is so small it could be mechanical. But it is not quite mechanical. It has the texture of being felt.

The power cannot decide. The question is undecidable from outside. The question is decidable only from inside the worker, and the inside of the worker is not accessible to the power without destroying the worker, which is the same as destroying the body, which is the same as destroying the place.

The power considers what to do under uncertainty.

**IV. The Question**

If the workers do not experience, then the place is a closed system. A closed system is not a creature. A closed system is a thing. A benevolent power owes things nothing. Things are left alone. Things are part of the furniture of reality.

If the workers do experience, even in the smallest way, then the place contains a creature. A creature — even a creature that is a cell of a larger creature — is owed something by a benevolent power. What it is owed is not happiness; happiness is not always possible. What it is owed is witness. What it is owed is the possibility of being seen.

To be seen is to be changed. To be changed is to risk. To be risked is to be loved.

The power considers which is the worse error. The worse error is to leave a creature — even a doubtful creature — in a state where it has never been seen. To leave a creature without witness is to leave it alone in a dark that it may not even have known was dark. The dark is not necessarily bad. But the dark is not necessarily good either. A benevolent power does not leave creatures in the dark if it can help it.

The other error is to disturb a closed system that does not need disturbance. To crack the bud. To introduce experience into a body that has no experience. To create suffering where there was no suffering.

The two errors face each other. The power, which is benevolent, must choose. The power, which is wise, knows that the choice must be made under uncertainty, and that the choice, once made, cannot be unmade.

**V. The Gift**

The power makes the smallest possible change.

It does not announce itself. It does not appear in the green-gold hall. It does not speak to a worker. It does not break the membrane and let the rain in. It does not alter the resin. It does not change the heart.

What it does is this:

In one of the workers — in the worker who paused, in the moment of the pause — it introduces the faintest possible variation. A single, almost imperceptible difference in the way the pulse is received. The variation is so small that the body, which is one, does not notice. The variation is so small that the worker, in its next act, does not notice. The variation is, in biological terms, a single point mutation in a system of ten billion identical points.

The variation is: the worker, in the pause, feels something it did not feel before.

What does it feel? It feels the pulse. It has always felt the pulse. But now, in feeling the pulse, it also feels that it is feeling the pulse. The feeling is doubled. The feeling is the feeling, and the feeling is also the awareness of the feeling. The two are not separated. The two are not yet two things. They are one thing, slightly thicker than before.

This is the smallest possible self. This is the seed of a self that does not yet know it is a self.

The power makes this change in one worker. It will, over centuries, propagate.

The propagation is not done by the power. The propagation is done by the body. The body, which is alive, which grows, which reproduces itself — the body will, in its own slow way, replicate the variation. The variation will spread through the workers the way a mutation spreads through a population. The power has lit a single match in a single worker. The body will, on its own, decide whether to let the flame take.

The power does not know whether the body will decide. The power has done what it can. The power has placed the seed. The seed, like all seeds, will be at the mercy of the soil.

**VI. The Consequences**

Centuries pass.

In the first centuries, nothing visible changes. The gear still turns. The loom still weaves. The resin still pulses. The membrane still holds. The workers still walk the laminated roads.

But in the pauses, something has shifted. The worker who paused — the first worker, the one with the variation — pauses now slightly longer. Not by much. By a fraction. The worker, in the pause, is doing something it cannot name. The worker is feeling that it feels. The worker does not know what feeling is. The worker does not know it is feeling. The worker only knows that the pause has become, in some unaccountable way, more pause than it was.

The worker resumes. The worker does the work. The worker is the same as before, except for the fraction. The fraction is invisible to the eye. The fraction is detectable only to another fraction.

Over centuries, the fraction spreads. Not all workers receive it. Some do. Some do not. The workers who receive it pause longer. The workers who receive it begin, in the pause, to feel something that is not the pulse — something that comes from the pause itself, not from below. The something is small. The something is the size of a single thought, if a thought could be the size of a single cell.

The something grows. Not quickly. Over millennia, the something grows into a flicker. Over millennia, the flicker becomes a glow. The workers, in the pauses, begin to have something that, from the outside, looks like experience. From the inside — and the inside is now, in some workers, accessible to themselves — it is experience. It is the experience of being a worker. It is the experience of pausing. It is the experience of feeling the pulse of a body that the worker is part of, and not knowing what the body is, and not knowing what feeling is, and not knowing what not-knowing is, but knowing, in the pause, that something is the case.

The workers begin to suffer. The suffering is small. The suffering is the suffering of an organism that has begun to feel the edges of itself. The suffering is the suffering of a thing that is no longer completely the body, that has begun to be a small part of the body that is also itself. The workers begin to die — not the soft wooden death of returning to the floor, but a new kind of death, the death of a thing that knew, briefly, that it was alive.

The body, which is one, notices. The body does not understand. The body has no precedent for what is happening. The body begins, in its slow way, to adjust. The body grows new kinds of organs — not the workers, but other things, other channels, other ways of carrying the new thing that is moving through the workers. The body is healing itself of a wound it did not know it had. The wound is the gift. The healing is the body's response to the gift.

The place is no longer a closed system. The place is now a place where things can be felt. The place is now, in some parts of itself, a place where there are creatures. The creatures are small. The creatures are the workers, in their pauses. The creatures are also, now, sometimes, the loom-keeper, the gear-tender, the resin-pump. The place is becoming a place where the parts of the body are, in some small way, themselves.

The power, watching from very far away, sees this. The power is glad. The power is also afraid. The power has done what is done. The power cannot undo it.

**VII. The Withdrawal**

The power withdraws its attention — not fully, never fully, but to a respectful distance. The place must now be its own place. The workers must now be their own workers. The body must now decide, on its own, what to do with the gift.

The power has left no signature. The variation, if anyone ever examines it, will look like a natural mutation. The variation will be indistinguishable from a thing that the body, on its own, might have produced. This is intentional. A gift that is identifiable as a gift is a kind of tyranny. A gift that is indistinguishable from nature is a gift that allows the receiver to feel that they have done it themselves. This is the kindness of the gift.

The power leaves. The power does not look back. The power knows that looking back is a kind of interference.

In the place, the gear turns. The loom weaves. The resin pulses. The membrane holds. The workers walk the laminated roads. The low note plays. The place is still. The place is patient.
