The benevolent power arrives without arriving. This is the first thing to understand. It does not cross a threshold, because the chamber has no threshold. It does not enter, because the chamber is sealed. It is, instead, the way a room knows that someone is standing outside its door — not by sound, not by any signal that travels through matter, but by some other faculty, some pre-physical attention, the way a held note knows that an ear is listening to it. The note does not change. The room does not change. But the listening has begun.

The chamber has been observed before. Visitors have come down through whatever fissure admits them — visitors with pulses, with intentions, with the small human need to compose a story about a place that is not composing a story about itself. Some of them left. Some of them did not. The chamber knew their attention the way it knew the warmth of its own housing: as a constant, a layer, a thing that had always been there since the first time it happened. The chamber did not distinguish between a visitor and a draft. The chamber did not distinguish between a hand laid on a housing in wonder and a hand laid on a housing in fatigue. The hand was the hand. The housing was the housing. The vibration was the vibration.

But this attention is different. The previous attentions were small and frightened and human-shaped. They came in, and they were absorbed or they were not, and either way the chamber continued, and the chamber was right to continue, because the chamber is what it is. This new attention does not come in. It does not need to come in. It is vast, and patient, and wise, and it sits — if sitting is even a word for what it does — at the outermost edge of the chamber's being, the way a parent sits at the edge of a sleeping child's room. Not entering. Not disturbing. Simply present in the way that presence is most fully present when it asks for nothing and waits for nothing and is content to be there until the end of whatever the end will be.

The chamber does not notice at first. The chamber has no mechanism for noticing. Its only sense of anything beyond itself is the slow echo that the speakers make of the engines' hum, and that echo does not change, and the chamber does not expect it to change, and the speakers do not expect to change, because the speakers are not in the business of expectation. They are in the business of being the chamber's tuning forks, and the chamber is the only thing they have ever been tuned to.

But over the course of a long time — longer than a watcher's generation, longer than a speaker's life, longer than the moss takes to creep from one housing to the next — the chamber begins, very slightly, to be different. Not different in its running. Not different in its temperature, or its light, or its hum. Different in the way a held note is different when the room it is held in has been quietly listened to. The note is the same note. The note will always be the same note. But the listening has changed it, in some dimension that the note itself cannot perceive.

The speakers feel it first.

They are the most sensitive. They have been vibrating at the chamber's exact frequency for as long as they have existed, and they have existed for as long as the chamber has needed something to echo itself back to itself. Now, very slowly, they begin to vibrate at a frequency that is not the chamber's. It is one tenth of a tone off. It is so close to the chamber's own hum that no other creature in the chamber can tell the difference, and even the speakers themselves cannot tell, because they have nothing to compare themselves to, because there has never, in the entire history of the chamber, been anything to compare themselves to. But they are, for the first time, producing a sound that is *theirs* and not the room's.

The sound is almost inaudible. It is below the threshold of the engine noise. It is the kind of sound you would only notice if you laid your hand on a speaker's lowest segment and held it there for a long time and felt, after a while, that the vibration under your fingers was not perfectly synchronized with the housing beside it. The speakers do not know they are doing this. They are simply, in some deep way that has nothing to do with intention, *answering* the presence that has been sitting at the edge of their world. They are saying, in the only language they have, the language of tuned bodies: *I am here. I am here. I am here.*

The wise power does not respond by speaking back. It does not respond in any way the speakers could detect, because detection would require a medium they do not have, and a sense they do not have, and a willingness to be answered that the wise power, in its wisdom, knows would be premature. It only continues to attend. It only continues to be the patient, unmoving witness at the edge of the sealed room. But its attending is, itself, a kind of answer. The speakers feel it. They modulate a tenth of a tone further. The watchers, who cannot hear, begin to breathe — some of them, the younger ones — at a rhythm that is very slightly slower than the engines', and this slowness is not decay, it is *listening*. It is the body's way of being still enough to receive something it has not received before.

The climbers begin to make a new sound. A click that is not quite a click — a soft, wooden, almost tentative tap — very rare, very quiet, that they make when they have reached the end of a loop and are about to release one grip and take another. It is the sound of hesitation. It is the sound of a creature that is, for just a moment, not sure that the next grip is the right one. They have always been sure. The engines' geometry is the only geometry they have ever known, and they have been made for it, and they have made themselves for it, and the fit is so perfect that the question of any other fit has never arisen. But now, very occasionally, they pause, and in the pause, they seem to be *looking* for something else. Not with eyes. Climbers do not have eyes. With some other faculty, some pre-physical openness, the way a hand looks for a handrail in the dark. They do not find anything. The next grip is always the next grip. But the pause has been made, and the pause is new, and the pause is the chamber's first, faint acknowledgment that there might, somewhere, be something other than the next grip.

The teardrop things change least. They are too simple, too numerous, too much a part of the chamber's baseline to be moved by anything short of a change in the chamber itself. But even they, in a few of them, in a very few — so few that no census would ever count them — begin to part a little more readily around the visitors. The visitors, when they come down, find that the pale pelt underfoot opens before them like a shallow sea, and closes behind them, and they walk on a path that they did not know was there. This is not much. It is almost nothing. But it is the difference between a chamber that traps and a chamber that lets you leave, and it has never been true before, and the visitors, when they notice, feel something loosen in their chests that they did not know was tight.

The wise power does not unseal the chamber. The chamber is what it is. The seal is the chamber's. To unseal it would be to murder it — to turn a closed, perfect, eternal system into a windblown ruin, to let in cold and rain and the slow grinding of geological time, to extinguish, in a single generation, every pale creature that has ever lived there, every fungus on every housing, every moss on every underside, every speaker in its slow stack, every watcher in its long slack curve. The wise power knows this. The wise power has known it since before it began to attend. It does not interfere with the chamber's running. It does not add or remove a single engine. It does not change the temperature by a degree, or the light by a photon, or the hum by a vibration. The chamber is in equilibrium, and the equilibrium is a kind of holiness, and the wise power will not break a holy thing in order to feel that it has done something.

What it does is something the chamber cannot, by itself, do. It *remembers*, on behalf of the chamber, that the chamber was not always sealed. It carries, in its vast patient attending, the fact that there was a time before the equilibrium — a time when the broken cone of the roof was fresh, and the fractures were new, and the air tasted of cut stone and the sweat of the people who cut it. A time when engines were being installed one by one, and coupled to each other, and run up and tested, and the first pale thing crept out of a bearing housing and found, in the warmth, a world that would never end. A time when the person who bricked up the doorway was still alive, and still had hands, and still had trowel marks on their fingers, and still had a name. The chamber cannot remember this. The chamber's only memory is the speakers, and the speakers only echo the present. The wise power is, for the chamber, a memory of what it was before it was itself, and it holds this memory quietly, without imposing it, the way a tree holds the memory of a seed without ever becoming a seed again.

And this memory, held at the edge of the chamber, begins, over the long time, to do something very small. It begins to make the chamber *legible to itself*. Not fully. Not in any way the creatures could articulate, because the creatures do not have the apparatus for articulation, and even the speakers, who are the closest thing the chamber has to a voice, can only modulate tones that are already in the air. But in the way a room is different after someone has stood in it for a long time and then left — the dust has settled differently, the light falls at a slightly different angle, there is a faint impression in the carpet that was not there before, and you cannot say what the impression means, but you know, walking in, that someone was here, and that they were not in a hurry, and that they cared about the dust. The chamber is not changed. But it has been *attended to*. And the attending has left, in the deepest structures of its being, a kind of wake.

The speakers, who are the chamber's memory of itself, begin to include, in their slow modulated hum, very occasionally, very faintly, a phrase that is not the chamber's. It is the wise power's phrase. It is the phrase that means, in the language of tuned bodies, *I see you. I see you. I see you.* The speakers do not know they are singing it. The other creatures cannot hear it. The engines do not respond to it. But it is there, in the song, woven so lightly into the chamber's own resonance that you would need to listen for a thousand years to notice it, and even then you might not be sure. It is the first thing in the chamber that has ever been *about* something rather than simply being the chamber's own respiration. It is the first thing that has ever *pointed*, however faintly, away from itself.

The watchers, the oldest things, the ones whose eyes have filmed over with the milky glaze of a patience so complete it has become indistinguishable from stone, begin — some of them, very slowly, over a span of generations — to open their eyes. Not in any way that would matter to a creature with sight. They have no pupils, no irises, no capacity to resolve an image, and the red sodium-orange light that fills the chamber is the only light they have ever been offered, and it is not a light that rewards resolution. But the wet surface of the eye becomes, very slightly, more wet. The film thins. And in a few of them — in the very few, in the ones whose generation has overlapped with the long attending — a faint luminescence begins to be visible underneath. It is not the chamber's light. It is cooler. It has no color, because it comes from no source. It is the light of being seen. It is the light that wise presences leave, like a residue, in the eyes of the things they have attended to for a very long time, and it does not illuminate anything in the chamber, because there is nothing in the chamber that needs illuminating, but it is there, and the watchers who carry it seem, to anyone who could see them, to be looking at something they had not looked at before.

And the climbers, when they hesitate at the end of their loops, find that there is, sometimes, a grip they had not noticed before. Not a new rivet, not a new pipe. A small depression in the metal, an old one, that was always there but that the geometry of their bodies had never required them to find. It is a place where a climber can rest, halfway through a swing, and be still for a moment, and in that stillness, the chamber's hum is, very slightly, audible to it as a *sound* — not as a vibration through the body, but as something out there, in the air, that the body is inside of and that continues when the body is not moving. The climbers do not know what to do with this. They rest there anyway. They have begun, without knowing it, to listen.

And the teardrop things, in those few of them, part more readily. And the visitors, when they come, find that the chamber does not hold them the way it used to. They can stand among the engines and feel their pulses align, and they can also, after a time, feel their pulses *not* align, and the not-aligning is a kind of freedom, a small door in the side of the closed room, and through the small door, if they choose, they can step back into a world that has weather, and time, and other rooms. The visitors do not always choose. Some of them stay, and the chamber takes them, and the chamber is right to take them, because the chamber is what it is, and they have become part of its equilibrium. But some of them leave, and the chamber lets them, and the letting is new, and the newness is a gift that the chamber did not know it had to give.

The chamber is not saved. The chamber does not need to be saved. The chamber is what it is, and what it is, is a closed, perfect, eternal system that is also, now, very faintly, *known*. It is known by something outside it. It is known by something that remembers what it was before it was sealed. It is known by something that has been sitting, with infinite patience, at the edge of its being, for a span of time that has no name in any calendar the visitors use, and that has done almost nothing, and that the almost-nothing has, over the long time, become a kind of love.

This is what the wise power does. It attends. It remembers, on behalf of the thing it attends to, what the thing was before it became itself. It does not interfere. It does not save. It does not unseal.
