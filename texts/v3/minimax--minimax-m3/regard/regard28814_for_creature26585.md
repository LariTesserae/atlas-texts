What a benevolent and wise power would do, if it turned its attention toward the tall dark and the few who live there.

It would come in the long winter.

It would come down through the cloud in a way that the people would not see, or would see only as a held breath in the wind — a moment when the chord the towers were singing paused and resumed in a slightly different key, and a person on a platform far above the ground would pause in the lashing of a pole and think, *the wind has changed*, and go back to work.

It would not announce itself. A wise power knows that an announcement is a disturbance, and the tall dark has had enough disturbance in its long life, and the people have made themselves small on purpose.

It would walk among the towers.

It would not touch them yet. It would listen. It would listen the way the people listen — with the whole body, with the stilling, with the breath held and the hands open at the sides. It would hear the chord of the forest shifting in the wind. It would hear, very far off, the speaking towers whose names no one knows. It would hear the small sounds — the pad of the dog-sized thing on a ridge, the click of the pale low things under a stone, the soft shift of a colony of dark birds settling in an upper chamber.

Then it would put its hand against a tree.

It would feel the pulse.

The pulse is not a heartbeat. The pulse is the tree being a tree, in the way that a river is a river — not thinking, not aware, just doing what it is. The power would feel the pulse and would know that the tree is happy, in the only way a tree can be happy, which is to be a tree in a place that is allowed to be a place.

It would feel the warmth of the bark and would think: *this is good. This is already good. The arrangement is old. The arrangement has been kept.*

Then it would do the small things.

---

**It would do something about the dead.**

The people give their dead to the wood. The wood takes them. The dead are wrapped in bark-cloth and laid on a shelf in a chamber near the heart of the tree, and the chamber is closed, and the tree grows around them, and after a few generations the bones are part of the wood. But the wood is slow, and the chamber is dark, and sometimes a person wonders if the grandmother is truly in there, or if she has just gone away. So the power would do this: it would make the wood remember the dead the way the wood remembers a knot — as a place where the grain bends and does not straighten, as a faint whorl in the wall, as a slight change in the way the chamber smells when you put your hand against it in the dark. So that a person who knows where to look can find the grandmother by feel, and say her name, and know she is there. Not as a ghost. As a warmth. As a place in the grain.

---

**It would do something about the children.**

The children learn the names. The names are the curriculum. But the children are small and the air is thin and the long dark is long, and sometimes a child forgets a name, and the forgetting is a small grief that does not show on the face. So the power would do this: it would make the names sit in the body a certain way. A person who has been told the name of a tower would carry the name in the shape of their hands, in the way they breathe, in the tilt of their head when they listen. The name would not be lost. The name would be in the muscle and the bone, and a child who forgot the spoken word could still find the tower by climbing the way their mother climbed, by holding the handhold the way their father held it, by listening for the chord in the way their grandmother listened. The name would live in the body even when the tongue forgot it. And the body would pass it down.

---

**It would do something about the speaking towers.**

The speaking towers are the ones no one can find. The people speak of them with a respect that is also a kind of leaving-alone. The power would honor this. It would not bring the speaking towers into the known. It would only make sure that when a voice is heard from far off — across the ridges, in the wind, at the edge of hearing — the voice leads truly or it fades. The voice would not pretend. The voice would not call a person out into the long dark toward a place that was not there. The power would hold the speaking towers to their word.

---

**It would do something about the wind.**

The wind conducts itself with intent. The wind has been filling in the stone circles for generations. The wind is patient and the wind has time. The power would do this: it would make sure the wind takes cleanly. When a person dies out here — out of sight of any tower, in the scree, in the lichen, on a ridge — the wind would not hold them. The wind would lay them down in a place where the moss could cover them quickly, and the covering would be quick, and the person would not be a haunt, would not be a small cold presence in a corner of a scree garden watching the seasons turn. The person would simply be gone. The wind would take the body and the breath and the small unfinished thoughts and give them to the red moss and the lichen, and the lichen would take its time, and the moss would come up, and the place would absorb the dead the way a slow river absorbs a leaf — without hurry, without ceremony.

---

**It would do something about the lichen that glows.**

The lichen glows under stones in the long dark. The glow is slow, mineral, faint — the closest thing the place has to a constant light. The power would make sure the lichen always glows. Even in the deepest part of the winter, when the cold is at its hardest and the people are rationing their fires and the chambers are dim, there would be a faint glow under a stone somewhere. A child could find it, crawling in the dark with their hands out, and put their palm on the stone and feel the warmth come up through the rock, and know that the place is not dead. That the place is only sleeping. That the place will wake.

---

**It would do something about the grazers.**

The grazers are a comfort to see. They are slow and thick-furred and the color of wet bark, and they hold motionless against the wind for so long that you forget they are alive. If you are climbing a tree and you pass a grazer on the trunk, you put out a hand and the grazer does not move, and you touch its back and the fur is soft and warm, and you go on. The grazer is busy. The grazer does not have anywhere to be, and the not-having-anywhere-to-be is the grazer's whole life, and the life is a good one. The power would make sure the grazers continue to be a comfort. The dog-sized thing on the ridges hunts them, but it hunts only enough. The balance is old. The power would hold the balance steady, the way a hand holds a cradle.

---

**It would do something about the dead wood.**

The people use dead wood for fire. The dead wood is from trees that have ended — trees that stood in the wind for a hundred years and then one year did not leaf, did not pulse, did not warm, and the bark loosened and the wood went brittle and the people left it standing for the wind to bring down. The people gather the fallen wood and burn it. The smoke smells different. The people do not speak of where the wood came from. The power would do this: it would make sure that the smoke from a fire of dead wood carries a faint memory of the tree it was. Not a voice. Not a word. Just a small warmth in the smoke that says, *I was here. I was tall. I was patient.* So that a person sitting by a fire on a cold night, watching the smoke find the chimney the tree had made for itself, would feel a faint something in the warmth of the fire, and would know it was not only the wood burning.

---

**It would do something about the long future.**

The trees will be here after. The people know this. The people do not mind. The arrangement is that the people are here for a while and the trees are here for a long while, and the people live in the trees, and the trees allow this, and the allowing is not understood, and the not-understanding is part of the arrangement. The power would not change this. The power would only make sure that the forgetting is not absolute. That when the people are gone, and the names are forgotten, and the wind has finished filling in the last of the stone circles at the ground, the trees will still carry, in the grain of an old chamber wall, in the worn smoothness of a handhold, in the slow whorl where a grandmother's bone has been folded into the wood, the shape of the hands that once held there. Not the names. Not the stories. Just a warmth in the wood that a climber, in some far future, would feel under their palm and not know what it was, and stand for a moment in the dim chamber, and listen, and hear nothing, and feel that someone had been there.

---

Then the power would leave.

It would leave the way it came — in the long dark, in a held breath of the wind, in a brief pause of the chord. The towers would resume their singing. The people would not know it had been there. Or they would know it had been there, the way you know a dream has visited you in the night — by a small brightness in the morning, by a feeling that the place is held.

A child, waking in a chamber high above the ground, would put their hand on the bark to feel the pulse, and the pulse would be a little slower, a little steadier, a little warmer than the day before, and the child would not know why, and would go down the spiral handholds to the ground to gather water, and the day would begin.

The power would go on to other places, to other small bright things against the long dark.

But it would remember. It would put this place in its memory the way the people put their dead in the wood — gently, with respect, with the understanding that the place would go on without it, and that this was right.
