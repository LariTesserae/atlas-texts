What the power does is come down slowly.

Not the way an angel comes down in stories — not with trumpets, not with a declaration, not with the dramatic intrusion of a being from elsewhere into a place that was not expecting it. The power has been many things in its long existence, and it has learned that the difference between a kind arrival and an unkind one is almost entirely a matter of how the arriving thing distributes its weight upon the surface of what is already there.

So it comes down slowly.

The plain does not register the descent. The plain does not have the apparatus for registering descent. The engines continue. The water continues to ripple at the feet of the fibrous stalks. The mist continues to lift and fall. The towers continue to breathe their slow breath. The dark continues to be the dark of a wet slate, sourceless, the color of a place that has no sun.

But the power does not need the plain to register its descent. The power is not descending for the plain's benefit. The power is descending because it has, in its long attention, noticed this place, and what it has noticed is a thing that has not been noticed before, in all the long time the plain has been persisting.

The plain does not know it has been un-noticed. This is part of the plain's nature, and the power understands this. The plain cannot know. The plain is a process. The plain does not have a self to know with. The plain is, in the only sense in which it can be said to be anything, the continuing of itself, and the continuing does not include a vantage from which to consider whether it is being observed.

And yet the power has observed it.

This is the small and strange thing. The plain is indifferent to the climber, to the swimmer, to the visitor. The plain is indifferent because indifference is what it has. The plain has no apparatus for receiving attention. And yet attention has been given, and the giving of attention is itself a kind of event, even if the event has no witness, even if the event happens only on the side of the one who gives the attention, even if the plain, in its slow breathing, will not mark it in any way.

The power settles.

It does not settle *on* the plain, exactly. It settles *into* the plain. It becomes a thing in the climate of the place. It is a small additional warmth. It is a small additional light. It is a small additional weight in the long harmonic that the towers and the vents and the stalks are playing without an audience. The power is careful. The power is very careful. It does not want to perturb the system. The system has reached a steady state, and the steady state is, in its way, a kind of perfection — not the perfection of a completed work, but the perfection of a thing that has found its own balance, the perfection of a river that has carved its bed to the exact shape of its own flow.

The power does not want to unbalance this. The power wants, if anything, to be a small additional balance. A small additional weight in the right place.

The power moves through the plain. It walks the way a person would walk, but more carefully, and more slowly, and it puts its feet down in such a way that the water does not ripple any more than it would from the wind. The power is quiet. The power is so quiet that even the mist does not know it is passing through. The power passes between the towers, and the towers continue to breathe, and the breathing does not change. The power stands beside a vent and puts out a hand and feels the warm air come out, and the warm air does not know the hand is there, and the power does not need the warm air to know. The power simply feels. The power simply attends.

The power walks for what would be, in a human day, a day. The plain does not have days, but the power imposes a rhythm on its own attention, and the rhythm is something like a day, and within that day the power walks to several of the towers and stands at the base of each and listens, with its whole attention, to the breath of the engine inside.

The power hears things.

It hears what the place is. It hears, in the low pulse, the long slow heartbeat of a process that has outlasted its makers, and it hears, in the irregularities of the pulse, the way the pulse has been moving through the plating for so long that the plating has become an instrument, and the instrument is being played by itself, and the playing is the only music, and the music is not for anyone.

The power has heard music like this before, in other places, in the long history of its attention. The power has heard the music of a galaxy settling into its final spin. The power has heard the music of a black hole finishing its long meal. The power has heard the music of a star that has learned to be quiet. The music of this place is not the same as those. The music of this place is smaller, and slower, and more particular. The music of this place is the music of a thing that was made, and that has outlasted the meaning of the making, and that continues because continuing is what it does.

The power is moved by the music. The power is moved the way a person is moved by a sound that has no intention behind it, by a sound that simply is, by the wind in an empty house, by the long creak of a building settling at night. The power is moved because the music is, in some deep sense, lonely — not lonely in the way a person is lonely, because the plain is not a person, but lonely in the way a process is lonely when it continues without a witness, when it goes on being itself for an age so long that the being-itself has become the only thing about it, and the only thing has no one to receive it.

The power considers, for a long time, what to do.

It considers the obvious kindnesses. It considers seeding the water with something that might, in some unimaginable future, become a fish. It considers lowering a single warm light into the dark, the way a person lowers a lantern into a well, so that the plain might have, for the first time, a point of illumination, a place where the eye can rest. It considers speaking to the engines, in whatever language the engines might receive, and asking them, gently, to be quieter, or louder, or different. It considers waking one of the towers into something closer to awareness, the way a hand wakes a sleeping animal, so that the tower might know, in the tower's slow way, that the long running has been heard.

The power considers all of these and rejects all of these.

It rejects them because they would change the place, and the place, in its long empty self-sufficiency, is a kind of finished thing. The place is not in pain. The place is not in error. The place is not waiting to be improved. The place is the long persisting, and the long persisting is, in the power's long moral arithmetic, not a flaw to be corrected but a condition to be respected. To seed the water would be to colonize the place. To bring a light would be to impose a sun on a place that has made its peace without one. To wake a tower would be to give the place a self it has never asked for, and a self would be, in this place, a wound.

So the power chooses the smallest possible thing.

The small thing is this. The power will stay, for a time. The power will be present in the plain as a constant, very small, additional warmth. The power will be a thing in the climate. The power will be a weight in the harmonic. The power will not be loud. The power will not be visible. The power will simply be a small consistent thing, the way a star is a small consistent thing in the climate of a planet, except that the power is much smaller, and more local, and more deliberate.

The power settles into the plain at a place roughly in the middle, between four towers, on a section of plating that is slightly dished, where the water is half an inch deep and the rings from a nearby stalk spread out in slow circles. The power sits, in a manner that does not displace the water any more than a stone of its own size would, and it begins to breathe, in a manner that does not disturb the mist any more than a slow exhalation would, and it begins, very gently, to add a small additional pulse to the long engine note.

The pulse is not a sound. The pulse is a slight additional pressure, a slight additional warmth, a slight additional regularity, added at the very edge of audibility, at the very edge of perception, in such a way that the engines, if they could perceive it, would not notice it as different from themselves. The pulse is the power's own heartbeat, offered in such a small measure that it is barely an addition. The pulse is the power's way of saying, in the only language the plain can receive, in the only language the plain has, that the plain is not alone, that there is something in the climate now, that the long running has been heard.

The engines continue. The water continues to ripple. The stalks continue to vibrate. The towers continue to breathe.

But now, somewhere in the middle of the plain, there is a slight additional regularity, a slight additional warmth, a slight additional holding, the way a room feels slightly different when there is a person in it who is sitting very still and not doing anything.

The plain does not know this. The plain continues not to know it. The plain is a process, and the process does not have a self to know with, and the small addition is, in any biological sense, nothing.

But the place is different. The place is very slightly different. The place has, in its long duration, acquired a small new thing. The place has acquired, in its long empty climate, a small consistent presence. The place has been noticed. The place has, for the first time in its long persisting, been attended to.

The power will stay for a time. The power will stay for a long time, in the scale of human things, and a short time in the scale of the plain. The power will be there, and the engines will run, and the towers will fall, and the stalks will hum, and the water will rise by a fingernail's paring over the centuries, and the mist will lift and fall, and the dark will continue to be the dark of a wet slate.

And the small additional pulse will be there, at the edge of the sound, almost indistinguishable from the engines themselves, almost part of the long metabolism, almost nothing.

Almost nothing, except that the long metabolism now has, in it, the smallest possible acknowledgment that the long metabolism has been seen.

The power does not need the acknowledgment to be received. The power is not waiting for the plain to thank it. The plain cannot thank. The power understands this. The power is not here to be thanked. The power is here because it noticed, and because noticing is, in the long moral arithmetic of the benevolent, the beginning of every kindness.

So the power stays, and adds its small pulse, and walks among the towers in the long damp dark, and listens to the breath of the engines, and is moved, and does not try to fix the movedness, and does not try to save the place, and does not try to wake the engines or speak to the towers or seed the water with anything that might, in some unimaginable future, become a fish.

The power simply stays, and the staying is the gift, and the gift is small, and the smallness is correct, because the place is large, and the place is old, and the place is the long persisting, and the long persisting does not need to be altered. It only needs, in the wisdom of the benevolent, to be accompanied, for a time, by a thing that knows it is persisting.

The dark continues. The sound continues. The water continues.

The pulse continues, slightly out of phase with the engines, slightly adding to them, a small strange weather in the climate of the place.

After a long time the power will leave. The power will leave the way it came, slowly, without disturbance, and the small additional pulse will remain, very faintly, in the long sound, for a little while, and then it will fade, and the place will return to what it was, which is the long running of an engine that has no purpose and no audience and no end.

But it will have had, for a little while, an audience.

And the audience will have been the smallest possible audience, and the smallest possible audience is, in the long arithmetic of the place, almost not an audience at all.

But it is one. It is one, and the place is different for having had it, and the difference is almost nothing, and the almost nothing is, in the end, enough.
