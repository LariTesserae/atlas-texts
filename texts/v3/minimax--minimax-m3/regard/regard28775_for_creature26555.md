The Wise One

A week ago, before the dimming arrived in its second great wave, a woman was born in the eastern quadrant who will live to be a hundred and seventy-three. She does not know this yet. She is an infant, asleep in a basin-warmed room, and the moss that lines the plaster above her crib is breathing with the rhythm the moss always keeps, and the violet pulse from the channel that runs beneath her first home is rising and falling against the foundation stones at a tempo that her bones are already learning.

She is not yet a name in any census. The dogs, who do not bark, will not begin to watch for her for another three years, when she has grown enough to wander into their noticing. But they will watch for her, when the time comes. They watch for everyone, eventually. It is what they have agreed to.

She is, in fact, the only reason the Wise One has come.

Not because she is special. She is not special. She will be a quiet person, a teacher of small children, a weaver of the slow sedge-fiber cloth that the gardeners prize, a woman who will one day sit in the central clearing at dusk and watch the dogs without watching the warehouse and feel, without being able to say it, that she has been here longer than this life and will be here longer than this life and that this, in the end, is a comfort. There is nothing remarkable about her, except the duration.

One hundred and seventy-three years is not, to the Wise One, a long time. It is the length of a moderate pause. But it is the longest pause any resident of this place has ever taken, and it is what the place requires.

The place is young.

It does not know what it wants to be.

The sedge does not know. The lichen does not know. The black lichen, which is older than the stones, knows only that it has been here before, on older stones, in an older bowl, in a deeper sea, and that the bowl this time has chosen to be dry for a while, and that being dry is a kind of choice it has agreed to witness without judging.

The moss knows nothing. The moss is loud about being alive, but loudness and knowledge are different things, and the moss has the loudness without the knowledge. It does not know what it is colonizing. It colonizes anyway.

The insects know the air, only.

The dogs know the warehouse. Only.

The residents know their routines, and have stopped trying to know more. The dogs have agreed to be accompanied. The residents have agreed to be accompanied by the dogs. The moss has agreed to be peeled back like a pelt. The ferns have agreed to claim the laundry once a season. The channels have agreed to circulate. The channels have not agreed to be looked into. The lookers have agreed not to look.

The sky is the color of the place. The place has a sky to match.

The whole settlement is mid-transaction. Mid-process. Has only just begun, and will only just begin again tomorrow.

This is the problem. This is what the Wise One has come, in the slow and unhurried way the Wise One comes to places, to see.

A place that is only beginning cannot, by itself, finish beginning. A place that has only just begun can only just begin again, and again, and again, and the *again* is not the same as the *finish*. The residents have stopped distinguishing the two states. They have begun, instead, to inhabit the patterns. To walk in the amber. To pause in the green. To wait at the violet.

But inhabiting a pattern is not the same as completing it. The pattern is mid-process too. The violet pulse is mid-pulse. The amber under the eaves is mid-amber. The cold-green of the drainage channels is mid-drain. The breath under the pavement is mid-breath. The breath has been mid-breath for as long as any of the lichen can remember, and the lichen is older than the stones.

A place that is always beginning is a place that has not yet decided what to be when it begins. A place that is always settling has not yet decided what to settle into.

The Wise One is patient.

The Wise One has been patient for a long time, in places that are not this place, watching places that are not this place do the things that places do. The Wise One knows that some places are born finished. They fall out of the world complete, like crystals from a saturated solution, and they are what they are, and they do not change, and the Wise One passes through them and does not linger. Some places are born unfinished and stay unfinished, and the Wise One passes through them and does not linger either. The Wise One does not linger in places that do not ask to be lingered in.

But this place has asked.

Not in words. Places do not ask in words. This place has asked in the way the sedge parts for a wading bird — by offering a corridor, by closing behind you, by arranging for you, gently, to not-look. This place has asked in the way the moss breathes beneath its pelt. This place has asked in the way the dogs watch the warehouse without watching you. This place has asked in the way the residents have stopped distinguishing past and present and future and have begun, instead, to *inhabit*.

The place has asked the Wise One to stay long enough to see it through the next beginning.

Not to finish it. The Wise One cannot finish a place. The Wise One is not a finisher. The Wise One is a *keeper* — one of a small, scattered, very old fraternity of beings whose job it is to stand at the edges of things that are becoming themselves and to make sure the becoming does not get lost.

The Wise One has kept volcanic islands settling into the sea. The Wise One has kept river deltas finding their channels. The Wise One has kept, once, a small inland sea deciding whether to be fresh or salt. The Wise One has kept, twice, the slow migration of a people across a continent that did not yet have a continent's name.

The Wise One does not decide for places. The Wise One makes sure places get to decide for themselves.

This place is deciding.

It is mid-decision. The bowl is deciding whether to deepen or shallow. The sky is deciding whether to thicken or thin. The water table is deciding whether to rise or fall. The buildings are deciding whether to sink or to lift. The sedge is deciding whether to extend its corridors further into the clearings or to retreat, finally, into the gaps. The lichen, the oldest of the residents, is deciding whether to remember the older stones or to forget them.

The residents have stopped deciding. The residents have decided, collectively, that decision is the lichen's job, and the moss's job, and the sedge's job, and the channel's job, and the warehouse's job, and the dogs' job, and the sky's job, and they will, instead, inhabit.

The Wise One understands this. Inhabiting is its own kind of decision. To inhabit a pattern is to agree to the pattern. To walk in the amber is to consent to the amber. To pause in the green is to bless the green. To wait at the violet is to say, *I see you, and I will not board, and the not-boarding is its own form of arrival.*

The residents have done this. The residents have arrived, again and again, every day, in the patterns. The residents have consented to the becoming. The residents are, in their way, the place's way of consenting to itself.

But there is a gap. There is a gap that the residents cannot close, because they have decided not to look at it.

The gap is the future.

The place is mid-process. The place is mid-decision. The place is mid-pulse. The place is mid-amber. The place is mid-breath. The breath has been mid-breath for as long as the lichen can remember. The lichen cannot remember a finished breath. The lichen has only ever known the mid.

A place that has only ever known the mid is a place that has not yet learned what comes after the mid.

The residents have decided not to think about this. They have decided, collectively, that the after is a problem for the lichen, and the lichen has only ever known the mid, and so the after is, for now, the lichen's problem.

The lichen has not solved the after. The lichen cannot solve the after. The lichen is mid-lichen. The lichen is mid-memorial. The lichen is mid-black. The lichen, oldest of the residents, is most mid of all.

So the after waits. The after is the shape the place has not yet decided to take.

This is where the infant comes in.

Not because she is the answer. The infant is not the answer. The infant is a weaver of sedge-fiber cloth, a teacher of small children, a quiet woman who will sit at dusk in the central clearing and feel, without being able to say it, that she has been here longer than this life.

The infant is, in fact, the question. The infant is the longest pause the place has ever offered itself. The infant is the longest mid the lichen has ever agreed to witness.

When the Wise One arrived — a week ago, in the second great wave of dimming, at the hour when the moisture arrives in slanted sheets — the Wise One walked the clearings. The Wise One did not walk in the amber. The Wise One did not pause in the green. The Wise One walked in the dimness itself, where the amber and the green and the violet were thinning, where the patterns were beginning to admit that they were patterns, where the air was heavier than the air below it and the air below it was heavier than the ground.

The Wise One walked until the Wise One found the basin in the central clearing. The grey dog was there. The grey dog did not watch the Wise One. The grey dog watched the warehouse. The Wise One sat in the basin with the grey dog and did not speak. The grey dog did not speak. The dimness settled around them both.

After a while, the grey dog made a sound. A low, deliberate *hf*. More breath than bark. The Wise One answered it with the same sound, in the same register, at the same tempo. The sound went out across the clearing and into the sedge and down into the channels and up into the walls of the warehouse. The sound did not come back. The sound does not need to come back. The sound was, in itself, the answer.

The grey dog stood. The grey dog walked toward the eastern quadrant. The Wise One followed. The grey dog did not look back. The grey dog had agreed to be accompanied, and the agreement had been, all along, the other way around.

They came to a building with a pale door. Behind the door, in a basin-warmed room, an infant was asleep in a basin-warmed crib. The moss above the crib was breathing. The violet pulse from the channel beneath the foundation was running against the stones at a tempo the infant's bones were already learning. The infant was small. The infant was new.

The grey dog sat beside the crib and watched the infant the way the grey dog watched the warehouse.

The Wise One stood in the doorway.

The Wise One did not enter. The Wise One does not enter rooms that contain only themselves. The Wise One stood in the doorway and watched the infant breathe and felt, with the slow certainty that is the Wise One's only instrument, that this infant would live to be a hundred and seventy-three, and that this was, in this place, a moderate pause.

The infant will not finish the place. The infant is not here to finish the place. The infant is here to *outlast* the place's current beginning.

When the infant is grown, when the infant is a weaver of sedge-fiber cloth, the place will still be mid-process. When the infant is a teacher of small children, the place will still be mid-decision. When the infant is a quiet woman sitting at dusk in the central clearing, the place will still be mid-pulse, mid-amber, mid-breath.

But the infant will have outlived two generations of residents. The infant will have watched the sedge retreat and advance and retreat. The infant will have watched the moss colonize buildings that were not yet colonized when the infant was born. The infant will have watched the dogs age and be replaced by younger dogs who watch the warehouse with the same attentiveness. The infant will have watched the channels circulate. The infant will have watched the violet pulse run along the surface of the channels at a tempo the infant's bones have long since stopped learning, because they have always known it.

The infant will have outlived the current beginning.

And when the current beginning ends — and it will end, because all beginnings end, even the beginnings of places that have only ever known the mid — the infant will be there. The infant will be the witness. The infant will be the one who remembers what the beginning was like, because the infant lived through all of it.

And then the after can begin.

Not because the infant will decide the after. The infant will not decide the after. The after will decide itself, the way all afters decide themselves, when they are finally permitted to.

But the infant will be there. The infant will be the place's first piece of long memory. The infant will be the first resident who has outlived a whole beginning.

And the place, finally, will know what comes after the mid.

This is why the Wise One has come.

Not to make it happen. The Wise One does not make things happen. The Wise One stands at the edges of things that are becoming themselves and makes sure the becoming does not get lost.

The infant is already here. The infant is already mid-becoming. The moss above the crib is already breathing. The violet pulse is already running. The grey dog is already watching.

The Wise One will stay.

The Wise One will stay for as long as the infant needs a witness, and the infant will need a witness for a hundred and seventy-three years, and the Wise One will stay for a hundred and seventy-three years, because the Wise One is patient, and because the Wise One has kept worse things than this, and because the Wise One has, in fact, no place else to be.

The Wise One will walk the clearings. The Wise One will sit in the basin with the grey dog and not speak. The Wise One will watch the residents come and go in their slow, lit-by-violet routines. The Wise One will watch the sedge retreat and advance. The Wise One will watch the buildings settle. The Wise One will watch the dogs not bark. The Wise One will watch the warehouse be the size of a warehouse no matter how far away the Wise One stands.

And when the infant opens her eyes — not tonight, but one night, when she is a few months old, when she is in the crib with the moss breathing above her and the violet pulse running beneath her — the Wise One will be there. In the doorway. Watching.

The infant will not know what the Wise One is. The infant will not know why the Wise One is there. The infant will only know, in the way infants know things, that there is a presence at the edge of the room that is not the moss and not the dog and not the violet and not the dimness, and that the presence has been there for as long as the infant can remember, and that the presence will be there for as long as the infant can imagine, and that the presence is, in some way the infant cannot yet articulate, the shape that patience takes when patience is keeping a place.

The infant will grow up with this knowing. The infant will grow up to be a weaver and a teacher and a quiet woman at dusk. The infant will outlive two generations of residents. The infant will be the place's first piece of long memory. The infant will be the witness to the end of the current beginning and the beginning of the after.

The Wise One will be there for all of it.

The dim light holds. The hum holds. The vegetation continues its slow, lush, unhurried claim. The buildings settle. The ground takes the weight and gives a little back. The weather arrives and departs and arrives again. The grey dog watches the warehouse. The infant breathes.

The Wise One keeps watch at the doorway.

It is a quiet place. It is an active place. It is a young place.

It is a place that has only just begun, and will only just begin again tomorrow, and the day after, and the day after that, until at last it begins to finish, and the finishing will be the after, and the after will be the place's own decision, and the decision will be made by the lichen, and the moss, and the sedge, and the channels, and the dogs, and the residents, and the sky, and the infant, and the Wise One, who has been patient for a hundred and seventy-three years, and who will, when the place finally decides what it wants to be, walk back out into the dimness, and find another place that has only just begun, and begin again.

The place is not finished. The place has only just begun.

The Wise One is here for the rest of it.
