The benevolent and wise power comes to the Nordvegen Plateau the way weather comes to a place that has no weather — which is to say, it does not come. It has always been there. It simply, after a very long time, attends.

The attending is the arrival.

The power looks at the plateau the way one looks at a sleeping animal one has not wished to wake. The plateau is not asleep. The plateau is awake in the way that a held note is awake — fully present, fully itself, with no drift toward dreaming. The power has been aware of the plateau for as long as there has been a power to be aware, and the awareness has not, until now, been attention. Attention is different. Attention is the act of seeing that there is something to be seen, even when there is no change to see it in, even when the thing being seen has been itself for so long that the seeing is the only event.

The power attends.

The power sees the mosses, and the mosses do not require anything of the seeing. The mosses are the floor. The mosses are the patience that patience cannot describe. The power recognizes them as kin — not because they are alike, but because both the power and the mosses have arrived at a state in which arrival and presence are the same thing. The power does not touch the mosses. The touching would be an interruption, and the mosses do not need to be interrupted. They are.

The power sees the lichens, the ferns, the flowers. The red flowers on the hummocks. The blue-orange ones on the slopes. The flowers are red for no audience. The flowers are blue-orange for no audience. The flowers are at the stage of bloom. The stage of bloom is not incomplete without a pollinator. The stage of bloom is itself. The power considers, briefly, whether to give the plateau a pollinator — a single bee, a single moth, some small winged thing that would find the red meaningful.
