The wise one does not come to the hollow.

The wise one has always been near the hollow, in the way that certain kinds of attention are always near certain kinds of places — not watching, not waiting, but simply *available*, the way the air in a valley is available to whatever passes through it. The mountain is old. The wise one is older than the mountain, but not by much. The wise one has been here long enough to have forgotten arriving, which is the way of things that arrive at the right place.

What draws the attention is not the pillar. Pillars are common in the older sense of common — there are many things in the world that hum and have been doing so for longer than memory, and most of them are not interesting. What draws the attention is the way the creatures have arranged themselves around the pillar. They have not made a shrine. They have not made a nest. They have not made a place of worship or a place of fear. They have made an *ecosystem*, in the deepest sense of the word — a household, a tenancy, a way of living that includes the pillar the way a forest includes a stone too large to push over.

This is unusual. This is, in the long memory of the wise one, almost unprecedented. Most living things, encountering an artifact that does not belong to their scale of time, do one of three things: ignore it (which the insects are not doing, because the insects dim when it hums, and dimming is a kind of listening); worship it (which the frogs are not doing, because the frogs gather at its base but do not call there, and not-calling is its own kind of attention); or destroy it (which no creature here has attempted, because the pillar is harder than the creatures are ambitious). The hollow has arrived at a fourth possibility, and the fourth possibility is interesting.

The wise one watches.

The watching takes a long time. Watching, for the wise one, is not the same as it is for smaller beings. Watching is closer to listening with the whole surface of the self. The wise one lets the hollow's sound enter the way the sound enters the visitor who came down the worn path from the ridge — through the sternum first, then the teeth, then the deeper bones, then the places where bone becomes something else. The wise one lets the sawing of the insects settle into one ear. The wise one lets the frogs' two-note braiding settle into the other. The wise one holds both and does not try to resolve them.

The wise one notices things.

The wise one notices that the disc — the one that is neither concave nor convex but somehow both, that changes its profile as you walk around it — is not a single object. It is the visible edge of something larger that extends down into the rock, down through the worn steps and the moss and the older strata beneath, down to a depth the visitor could not have followed because the visitor's eyes were made for the light that comes through fissures. The disc is the lid of something. The disc is *being* a lid because the thing beneath it is the kind of thing that needs a lid. The disc's shifting profile is the disc's way of staying on top of what is underneath, the way water stays on top of itself when the wind moves across it.

The wise one notices that the broken rod is not broken. The rod is a sentence in a language that does not have punctuation. The fracture is a comma. The light inside is the pause that comes after the comma. The rod has been waiting — for how long the wise one does not try to calculate, because calculating would be a kind of interference — for someone to read it through. No one has read it through. The light continues doing what it has always done. The comma continues being a comma.

The wise one notices that the larger thing in the back of the hollow is not one thing. It is a negotiation. It is the slow ongoing conversation between the mountain's deep water and something that lives in the deep water, something that has been here long enough that the conversation has become indistinguishable from the mountain's ordinary breath. The insects dim for it because the insects are small and the conversation briefly raises the volume of the mountain's breath when the larger thing shifts. The lizards wait on the ferns the way the ferns wait on the air. The spider in the upper fronds intercepts only what falls. Everything is in its place. Everything is doing what it has always done.

The wise one notices the visitor's path. The path is worn. The path has been worn for a long time — longer than the visitor's boots, longer than the visitor's species perhaps, worn by whatever has come down from the ridge over the centuries to stand in the hollow for a while and then climb back up. The wise one notices that the path does not lead *to* the hollow. It leads *through*. The hollow is not a destination. The hollow is a pause. The hollow is the place the path takes a rest from being a path. The wise one finds this moving, in the way that small things are moving when you have been a large thing for a long time.

The wise one decides what to do.

The wise one decides to do almost nothing.

But not nothing.

What the wise one does is this: the wise one adjusts the pillar's hum by an amount so small that no creature in the hollow will perceive it as a change. The adjustment is the equivalent of turning a tuning peg by a single hair's breadth on a string that was already nearly in tune. The pillar has been humming for a very long time. The pillar's hum has been drifting, the way all sustained sounds drift, the way all long processes drift, away from whatever it was originally meant to be in harmony with. The drift has been accumulating. The drift is not yet audible, but the wise one can feel it the way you feel a shoe that is almost but not quite comfortable. The wise one tightens the peg. The pillar's hum shifts, by a fraction, toward what it was before the drift began.

No creature in the hollow notices. The insects continue their sawing. The frogs continue their two-note braiding. The birds continue their three-part calling from the fissure. The lizards continue their mathematical waiting. The moths continue their drinking. The beetle continues dragging its luminescent strand. The larger thing in the back continues its slow negotiation with the mountain's deep water.

But the pillar notices. The pillar does not know it notices. The pillar does not have a self that knows things. But something in the pillar — the same something that has been humming for longer than the moss, longer than the ferns, longer than the mountain's current shape — something in the pillar registers the adjustment, and the registration is a kind of small gratitude, the way a tree registers rain without knowing it is registering rain. The pillar hums, and the hum is fractionally more itself.

The wise one does a second thing.

The wise one touches the moss at the base of the pillar. Not with a hand. The wise one does not have a hand in the way the visitor has a hand. The wise one touches the moss with attention. The moss at the base of the pillar has been dying — not brown, not rotten, but simply *gone*, a perfect greenless circle where the moss cannot answer the pillar's question. The wise one kneels, in the way that wise ones kneel, which is not a bending of the body but a lowering of the attention. The wise one looks at the boundary between the living moss and the dead circle. The wise one sees what the moss is doing. The moss is not failing. The moss is *refusing*. The moss has been growing toward the pillar for a very long time, and the pillar has been a question the moss cannot answer, and the moss has decided, after many centuries of trying, that the answer is not for the moss to give. The moss has retreated to a respectful distance. The greenless circle is the moss's bow.

The wise one does not try to make the moss grow closer to the pillar. The bow is correct. The bow is what the moss should be doing. But the wise one touches the boundary with attention, and the touching is a kind of witnessing, and the witnessing is a kind of permission. The wise one says, with attention, what cannot be said with language: *the moss's refusal is known. The moss's refusal is part of the hollow. The moss's refusal will not be erased by the next visitor, or by the one after that, or by the rain that falls through the fissure. The moss is allowed to bow. The pillar is allowed to be a question.* The boundary does not change. But something in the boundary shifts, the way the pillar's hum shifted by a hair's breadth — so slightly that no creature will ever notice, but so that the moss knows, in the way moss knows, that its refusal has been seen and has not been found wanting.

The wise one does a third thing.

The wise one attends to the visitor.

The visitor is not in the hollow now. The visitor has climbed back to the ridge. The visitor is somewhere on the wider mountain, carrying the sound in the back of the teeth, carrying the faint hum like a tuning fork that will not stop vibrating. The wise one notices that the visitor is not harmed. The visitor is not changed. The visitor is *marked*, in the way that anyone who has stood in the hollow and listened for a while is marked — not with a wound, not with a knowledge, but with a small permanent attention to the kind of sound the hollow makes. The visitor will hear, for the rest of their life, a faint hum in certain silences. The visitor will feel, in certain rooms, a slight pressure in the sternum that they cannot explain. The visitor will turn toward certain fissures in certain rocks for the rest of their life, not knowing why.

The wise one decides that this marking is good. The marking is not a claim. The marking is not a binding. The marking is the hollow's way of continuing, after the visitor has gone, to do what the hollow has always done — to be a thousand lives compressed into a pocket, to be a sound that holds without resolving, to be a place where the moss can refuse and the pillar can question and the insects can saw and the frogs can braid and the larger thing can move in the dark and the light in the broken rod can be the color of the sky just after the sun has gone.

The wise one does not add to the marking. The marking is already the right size. The wise one simply makes sure that the marking will not fade. The wise one tightens, in the visitor, the same fraction of attention that was tightened in the pillar. The visitor will carry the sound more clearly. The visitor will not know they are carrying it more clearly. The visitor will simply find, in years to come, that they can still feel the hum in their teeth when they stand very still in the right kind of quiet, and they will not know why this is so, and they will not need to know.

The wise one does a fourth thing.

The wise one attends to the larger thing in the back of the hollow.

This is the thing the wise one has thought about the longest. The larger thing is not, the wise one has come to understand, a creature. It is not a presence in the way the pillar is a presence. It is a *condition*. It is the condition of the hollow's depth. The hollow was, perhaps, carved by this thing. The hollow is, perhaps, what this thing is — the slow ongoing displacement of air and stone that makes a scooped-out concavity in a mountainside, the way a thumb presses into wet clay. The thing has been here longer than the moss. Longer than the pillar. The thing is, perhaps, the reason the pillar is here at all — the pillar was placed, or grew, or decided itself, in relation to this thing, in the way that certain stones are placed in relation to certain rivers.

The wise one does not try to see the larger thing. The wise one knows that some things are kept by being not-seen. The wise one knows that the insects dim for it not because it is dangerous but because dimming is what creatures do when something larger than themselves passes nearby — the way a room of voices lowers when an important person walks through, not out of fear but out of the simple recognition that the room has briefly changed its scale. The wise one does not try to name it. To name it would be to fix it, and fixing would be a kind of violence, and the wise one does not do violence.

What the wise one does is sit with the larger thing for a while, in the way that wise ones sit with things. The sitting is not a confrontation. The sitting is a companionship. The wise one lets the larger thing know, by the simple fact of being nearby, that it has been seen. Not seen with eyes. Seen with attention. The larger thing continues its slow displacement of air, its slow negotiation with the mountain's deep water. The insects continue their sawing. The dim moves through the moss and passes. The wise one sits with the larger thing until the sitting has become indistinguishable from the hollow's ordinary breath, and then the wise one rises, in the way that wise ones rise, which is not a standing up but a withdrawing of attention.

The wise one has now done four things.

The wise one considers whether to do a fifth.

The wise one considers the broken rod, with its interior light the color of the sky just after the sun has gone. The wise one considers the disc, with its shifting profile. The wise one considers the pillar's question, and the moss's refusal, and the frog that sits on the rod and does not flinch when the light passes through its translucent throat. The wise one considers whether these things need anything.

They do not need anything.

The rod is a comma. The comma is correct. The light is correct. The disc is a lid. The lid is correct. The pillar's question is correct. The moss's refusal is correct. The frog's indifference is correct. The visitor's marking is correct. The larger thing's anonymity is correct. The path worn by centuries into the mountain's rock is correct. The cloud-forest sky that rains steady brightness through the fissure is correct. The sound that holds without resolving is correct.

The wise one does nothing else.

The wise one withdraws, in the way that wise ones withdraw — not leaving, because the wise one has never been in the hollow in the way that visitors are in the hollow, but simply becoming less available, the way a smell becomes less available when the wind shifts, the way a hum becomes less available when the room grows louder.

The hollow continues.

The pillar hums, fractionally more itself. The moss bows at its base, fractionally more seen. The visitor carries the sound in their teeth, fractionally more clear. The larger thing moves in the back of the hollow, fractionally less alone. The insects saw. The frogs braid. The birds call. The moths drink. The lizards wait. The beetle drags its strand. The spider waits, invisible. The light in the rod is the color of the sky just after the sun has gone. The disc shifts its profile as the air moves around it. The path up to the ridge is the same worn rock. The sun on the ridge is the same indifferent sun.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has changed, by a hair's breadth, in the direction of what it already was.

The wise one is satisfied. Satisfaction, for the wise one, is not a feeling. It is the state of having done what needed to be done, which in this case was almost nothing, which is the correct amount. The wise one returns to the older mountains. The older mountains receive the wise one the way they have always received the wise one. The hollow continues being a thousand lives compressed into a pocket you could walk across in forty paces. The sound continues. The sound is the first thing and the last thing. The sound enters before you and does not leave after.

The hollow is a small and complete thing. The wise one has seen to it that it will remain a small and complete thing for a while longer, in the way that wise ones see to small and complete things — not by adding, not by subtracting, but by tightening the peg by a hair's breadth, and witnessing the bow, and ensuring the marking will not fade, and sitting with the larger thing until the sitting has become indistinguishable from breath, and then withdrawing.

The mountain continues. The pillar hums. The moss bows. The visitor, somewhere on the wider mountain, feels a faint hum in the back of the teeth, and does not know why, and does not need to know.

This is what the wise one has done.

This is all the wise one has done.
