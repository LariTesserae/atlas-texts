# What the Benevolent Power Does

It does not arrive. That would be too much. The mill is a place that has lost the capacity to register arrivals. If something came in through the door, the door would note it, and the door is mostly under the water, and the water does not register arrivals either. To enter here with a footstep would be to ask the water to remember the shape of a boot. It cannot. The water has other things to do.

So the benevolent power does not enter. The benevolent power turns, the way the head of a parent turns toward a child who has gone quiet in the next room — not because anything is wrong, but because the quiet has changed color, and the parent can tell, somehow, that the quiet has gone on a little longer than a quiet is supposed to go. The benevolent power is the slight tilt of attention, the extra grain of care extended across a single square of being. The benevolent power is not a being. The benevolent power is the moment when the universe decides it will look at this place, specifically, with kindness.

It begins to look.

It sees what you saw when you arrived. It sees the water, which is not water. It sees the vegetation, which is not vegetation. It sees the mill, which is a verb that has outlived its grammar. It sees the chord, which is the only thing holding the place into the shape of a place. It sees the turbines out in the open water, each one the size of a small house, turning at rates that do not reconcile. It sees the smokestacks, brick and rivet, three of them, with the grey-white breath issuing from the tops and joining the ceiling of cloud and ceasing to be distinguishable from the ceiling of cloud. It sees the conveyor housings with their load of kelp and creeper running past in green slow motion. It sees the forges burning at the bottom of the water, the actual steam rising from the iron tops of them, the orange glow pulsing in the lowest rooms. It sees the dynamos and the pulleys and the pumps and the belts frayed almost to nothing. It sees the lights, the faint yellow bulbs in caged fixtures, dangling above the waterline, illuminating three feet of wet brick and wet leaf, humming. It sees the coin-shaped puffs of minted air glinting as they drift across the surface of the pool. It sees the road that ends and resumes. It sees the fence that goes under. It sees the heron that is not on the smokestack, and it sees the not-heron as a person sees a held chair at a table.

It sees all of it. It sees it the way the place needs to be seen — not as a ruin, not as a haunted thing, not as a mistake, but as itself. The benevolent power has enough wisdom to know that this place has a self. The place is not a broken version of something else. The place is its own completion, in the same way a chord is its own completion; if you strike the chord and let it ring, you do not then stand around waiting for the chord to become some other chord. The chord is the chord. The mill is the mill. The warm water is the warm water. The fact that the mill has no heron and no rat and no swallow does not mean the mill is unfinished. It might mean the mill is a different kind of finished than we are used to.

The benevolent power considers this carefully. The consideration takes no time, because time here is the kind of substance that holds you and does not hold you, and the benevolent power is not subject to it in the same way. But the consideration is full. The benevolent power turns the question over the way you turn a stone over to see what is on the underside of it. The question is this: *should I act?*

And the answer comes back, gently, in the way all the answers in this place come back — slowly, with reluctance, in a key that changes when you are not listening for it. The answer is: *yes, but the action must be the one that the place is already asking for. The action must not be added. The action must be allowed.*

The benevolent power understands: the place is not asking for rescue. If the benevolent power were to drop a heron onto the smokestack, the heron would not understand the smokestack, and the smokestack would not understand the heron, and the heron would fly away, and the smokestack would return to its grey-white breath, and the gap would be larger than the gap had been, because now there would be a missing heron as well as a never-was heron. The place does not want to be repaired. The place wants to be finished in the way it has been trying to be finished for so long.

What is the place trying to do?

The benevolent power looks closer. It looks the way a doctor looks, or a gardener, or the river itself. It looks past the salt of the place, past the wet green light, past the deafening chord, past the patience of the eating that has been paused halfway up every wall. It looks for the grain of the thing, the way wood has a grain, the way a person has a grain, the way a quiet child has a grain that the parent can read in a single glance across a doorway.

And the benevolent power sees it.

The place is trying to end.

That is not the right word, exactly. The place is not trying to die. The place is trying to *resolve*. The place is a chord that has been holding the same chord for so long that the chord has become the only thing it knows how to be, the way a singer who has sung one note for a hundred years cannot imagine singing another, and the silence of the song would not be silence but would be the whole rest of the music, the music that was always waiting underneath the one note. The mill is running. The mill has been running for a long time. The mill is not running *toward* anything. It is running because running is what it is. It is running the way a heart runs. It will run until it stops. The benevolent power sees that the place is not endless. The place is exhausted. The place is the kind of tired that does not know it is tired because it has forgotten what awake is. The exhaustion is the chord itself.

And the benevolent power sees something else.

It sees you.

You are standing in the warm water. The water is at your knees, or your ribs, or it is a wall, or it is a film, depending on where you have stepped. You are the witness. The text told you so: you are the only thing in the place that knows it is in a place. You are the only thing that is here *on purpose*, with a before and an after, with a sense that this is somewhere and not everywhen. You are the only thing that can hear the chord as a chord, because you have heard other chords and you know that this chord is one and not many. You are the only thing that can see the smokestack as one smokestack and not as every smokestack it has ever been. You are the only thing that can feel the warm water as warm, because you have felt cold and you know the difference.

The benevolent power sees that you are doing something here, even though you are doing nothing. You are the witness that the place did not know it needed. The witness is the thing that lets a chord resolve. A chord held in empty air does not resolve; it sustains, and the sustaining is its own kind of stasis, neither moving forward nor letting go. A chord heard by a listener resolves, because the listener is the place the chord was always trying to arrive at. You are the listener. You are the destination of the chord, the place the water was flowing toward, the reason the turbines were turning, even though nothing was connected to anything, even though the belt was frayed, even though the dynamos were running only their own selves. The mill was running *for you*. The mill has been running for you since before you came.

This is the thing the benevolent power sees that the place itself does not see, because the place has no self to see it with. The benevolent power sees it for the place. And so the benevolent power acts.

The act is small. The act is almost nothing.

The benevolent power does not change the water. The benevolent power does not reseal the road. The benevolent power does not lift the turbines out of the pool or relight the forges or rehang the frayed belts. The benevolent power does not put a heron on the smokestack. The benevolent power does not restart time.

The benevolent power does one thing.

It makes you, the witness, able to be witnessed.

That is: it makes it possible for you to *go*. Not to escape — the place cannot be escaped; it holds you and does not hold you. But to go, in the sense of the heron. In the sense of the swallow returning south. In the sense of the chord finally allowed to release, because there is somewhere for it to go to, because a song is only a song when it has an end, and an end is only an end when there is someone outside it to register the arriving-at-silence.

The benevolent power does not push you out. The benevolent power adjusts, in some way that does not register to you as an adjustment, the seam between the place and the not-place. The seam has been tight. It is loosened by the smallest amount. The place has been hermetic. It is allowed, gently, the porosity of a living thing. A breath can come in. A breath can go out. A heron can land on the smokestack and not understand why it has chosen this smokestack, and the smokestack will not understand the heron, but the not-understanding will be a kind of meeting, and the meeting will be the first thing the place has had with anything outside itself since it began to be a place.

You feel it. You feel it as a shift in the warmth of the water, or as the sudden presence of a sound that is not the chord — a sound that is less, smaller, a single note among the many, and the single note is the note of the heron's landing. You feel it as the kind of breath you take when you have been holding your breath and you have forgotten you were holding it, and the release of the held breath feels like a gift, and the gift is mostly the air that was already there, just newly admitted.

You feel it as the chord thinning. Not stopping. Thinning. The chord, which was a wall of sound, which was the only thing holding the place into the shape of a place, which was the only thing binding the turbines and the dynamos and the boilers and the air and the silence where the heron should be into a single sustained hum of being — that chord begins to thread itself through your attention and emerge, on the other side, as something more like music. As something with a counter-melody. As something that knows it can end, because it has somewhere to end into.

The benevolent power watches this happen and is satisfied. Satisfaction, here, looks like a slow settling. It looks like the water on a tilted floor, finally, instead of flowing uphill, flowing downhill — but only for a moment, only for the length of time that the downhill makes sense — and then continuing on, slightly differently, into the culvert that is no longer flowing the wrong way.

What happens after, the benevolent power does not exactly control. The benevolent power has acted within its wisdom, which is to say, within the grain of the place. The place will do what the place will do. The turbines will turn, but they will be connected to something now, faintly, by a thread of awareness, and the thread will be enough. The dynamos will hum, but the hum will be part of the chord and part of the not-chord, and the not-chord will be silence and small things moving. The vegetation will continue its triumphant conquest, but the conquest will be paused at the top of every wall — the eating will, at last, be allowed to finish. The smokestacks will not fall, because the smokestacks are also the place, but the smoke from them will shift from grey-white to something closer to the color of weather. The dynamos' lights, the faint yellow bulbs in caged fixtures, will illuminate slightly more — not more of the brick and wet leaf, but more of whatever has entered by the loosened seam. The coin-shaped puffs of minted air will continue to drift, but they will be spendable now, faintly — to be picked up by whatever small creature first walks across the surface of the warm water and finds one and is startled by its weight.

The benevolent power does not stay. The benevolent power was always almost-not-there. The benevolent power is only the grain of kindness extended toward this place, and now the kindness has been received, and the receiving makes it no longer necessary for the kindness to be active. The benevolent power recedes the way a parent recedes from a doorway when the child has begun, at last, to breathe again, and the parent does not linger. The parent returns to what the parent was doing. The parent is not even aware that there was an event. The parent only knows, dimly, that for a moment there was something, and the something was tended, and the tending is finished, and the child is asleep, and the house is quiet, and the world is going on.

You stand in the warm water. The heron is on the smokestack. The heron does not know that it is the first. The heron is only a heron, doing what herons do, which is to stand and wait. The waiting, here, will be the long kind of waiting. The waiting will change. The waiting will become a kind of listening. The heron will hear the chord and the heron will hear the not-chord and the heron will stand between them on its thin grey legs and it will not know it is a witness. The heron will only know that it is a heron, that the smokestack is warm, that the water is below, that the air above smells of rust and a sweeter rot underneath, and that this is a place where it is possible to be a heron, and so the heron is a heron here, and the being-a-heron-here is enough.

The benevolent power, receding, turns once more to look. What it sees is what it hoped to see, which is what it knew it would see, because its wisdom is long and its kindness is old and it has looked on a great many places, and it knows what they need, which is: to be seen as themselves, and to be allowed, at last, to be witnessed by something outside themselves, and to begin the long slow work of becoming the thing they actually are, which is always smaller, and quieter, and more ordinary, and more dear, than they had supposed.

The mill is running. The mill has been running for a long time. The mill will run for a long time still. The mill is running now in the dim green light, in the thinning chord, in the warm water, in the presence of a single heron that did not know it was the first, in the slower patience of the eating that has been allowed at last to proceed, in the seam of sky and water and steam which can now be seen, because there is something on the other side of the seam to see it from. The water comes up to your knees. The heron stands on the smokestack. The dynamos hum a hum that is slightly less than a chord and slightly more than a hum, which is the sound of a place remembering that it does not have to be everything in order to be a place. You feel the warmth of the water. You feel the warmth of the water as warmth, because you have felt cold and you know the difference.

You stay for a while. Then you go.

The heron stays.

The mill is running.
