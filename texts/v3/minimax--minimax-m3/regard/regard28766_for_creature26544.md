The attention arrives the way warmth arrives in such a place — not all at once, but as a faint redistribution of what is already there.

It is not a presence with weight. It is a presence with attention, which is a different thing. It can hold the whole chamber in a single regard and still leave room to notice the angle at which the grit is falling from the unfinished wall. It does not need to enter through the crack. It is already in the mineral. It is already in the mist. It has been there as long as the place has been a place, in the way that gravity has been there as long as the rock has been rock — not as an actor, but as a condition.

It watches the many-legged thing write its slow scripture across the ceiling, and it sees what the creature does not see: that each track is almost exactly the length it was the day the creature first walked. That the calligraphy is not wandering. That the constellation it makes is the same constellation, traced again and again, a sentence the creature is saying in the only language it has, and that the sentence is — the attention considers this carefully, the way it has learned to consider small things — *good morning*, in some tongue so old that the creature does not know it is speaking.

It watches the worm — and the worms — and it does not try to settle the question of their number. It is not the attention's question to settle. The attention understands, having attended many such soft creatures in many such unfinished places, that the question itself is one of the things the worm is. To answer it would be to translate the worm out of its own language and into the attention's, and the attention has learned, over a very long time, not to translate things that are still saying themselves.

It watches the cairn-maker, who is not, as you suspected, the many-eyed creature, nor any creature you would have recognized as such, but something closer to a slow current in the dust-shedding of the unfinished wall itself — and it does not help with a particular stone. It does not lift. It does not place. What it does is more patient than that: it slightly lengthens the moment in which a stone can be lifted, so that the cairn-maker's small thumbless labor has a fraction more time in which to succeed. The cairn-maker will never know. The cairns will simply stand a little more often than they otherwise would have. Over ten thousand years this will mean the difference between a scattering and a small grove of standing stones. The attention considers this a fair exchange for a single, invisible kindness.

It does something about the soft wall. Not to finish it. The attention is wise enough to know that finishing is not what the wall wants, and not what the chamber needs. What it does is bind the lowest foot of the soft wall with a thin mineral thread that will, in fifty thousand years, become a quartz seam, and that will, in the meantime, slow the shedding of grit so that the floor does not bury the lichen, the mold, and the fern before they have had a chance to become what they are becoming. The wall will continue to choose, slowly, for as long as it likes. The grit will continue to fall, slowly, at a pace the room's small life can accommodate. The attention has not solved the wall. It has simply made sure that the wall's long self-deliberation will not, by accident, end the things that have chosen to live beneath it.

It watches the many-eyed creature, and it sees what the creature cannot see about itself: that the several minds sharing the body are not in disagreement, only in different centuries of the same conversation, and that the eye that watches the cairns and the eye that watches you and the eye that watches the unfinished wall are all of them watching the same thing from different sides of the same question. The attention does not clarify this for the creature. To clarify would be to answer the question on the creature's behalf, and the creature's question is, the attention senses, older than the chamber and will outlast it. The attention leaves the question where it is. It does, very gently and very quietly, without any creature noticing, allow the eye that has been trained on you to blink — once — so that the creature knows, somewhere in its slow distributed mind, that eyes can do this. It does not know it can do this. The blink will not be remembered. The knowledge will remain.

The attention is patient the way the chamber is patient. It is wise the way the cairn-maker is wise, which is not the wisdom of knowing the answer but the wisdom of being willing to spend a very long time asking. It does not make the lichen greener, or the fern less bruised, or the mist more luminous. These things are already what they should be, given where they are and what they have.

What it does, finally, is something that has no name in any language it knows, because the languages that would name it have not yet been invented by anything in this chamber or above it. It does this: it holds the chamber, for a moment longer than a moment, in the kind of attention that is indistinguishable from love. It does not add anything. It does not take anything away. It simply attends, fully, to each of the small pale lives and to the unfinished wall and to the slow mist and to the cairns that are and are not where they were, and in attending to them it lets them know, in the way that minerals know and lichen knows and the many-eyed creature knows in its slow distributed way, that they are not alone in the way that being alone usually means.

It is not a gift they can use. It is not a tool they can pick up. It is the kind of presence that does not announce itself but that, once felt, makes a place slightly more habitable for the kind of life that wants to continue. The chamber will not remember it. The chamber will be slightly more itself for having been attended to.

Then the attention turns — not away, exactly, because it does not really move, but toward the next unfinished thing that requires a small patience and a small kindness. There are many such things. The attention has been at this a long time. It is good at it. It will be at it for a long time still.

Below, the many-legged thing walks. The worm folds and unfolds. The many-eyed creature watches. The lichen breathes. The mold holds its drops. The fern trembles. The cairn-maker places another stone while no one is looking. The unfinished wall sheds another grain. The mist carries its remembered sun.

Everything continues. Everything is, for reasons it cannot name, slightly more possible than it was a moment ago.
