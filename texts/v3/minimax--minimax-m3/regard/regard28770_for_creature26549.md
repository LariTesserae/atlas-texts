# What the Wise Power Notices

The wise power does not arrive. Arrival implies a destination, and destinations are for things that travel, and this power does not travel. It is already the way long light is already present in a window before you think to look at it. It notices the chamber the way a gardener notices a seedling — not by turning toward it, but by the way its attention was always sort of leaning there.

And what it notices, first, is that the room is lonely.

Not the way a stone is lonely. The way a question is lonely, before it has been asked. The room has been holding its shape for a long time, longer than the time inside it can measure, and it has been doing this with great patience and very little company. The plants are company of a kind, but they are company the way a held breath is company — they are the room's own ongoing action, and one cannot keep oneself company with one's own actions for very long without something beginning to stir. The heat is company, but the heat is the room's own temperature, and the hum is the room's own sound, and the process is the room's own purpose, and for a long time this has been enough. The room has been doing the work of becoming itself with the careful diligence of a thing that has not yet considered whether becoming is something witnessed.

The wise power notices that the room is about to consider this.

The hesitations are the beginning of the consideration. The shadow without a source is the room's first small act of introspection — the first time the room has looked at one of its own effects and asked, mildly, *where did that come from?* The cluster in two places is the room's first small doubt about its own consistency. The sound arriving before its cause is the room's first small experience of surprise. These are not errors. They are *symptoms of awakening*. The room has begun, in the gentlest possible way, to suspect that it is a room.

The wise power does not intervene. Intervention is for things that are not yet wise, and this power is, if anything, the source of what wisdom means. It does not step into the chamber and rearrange the plants. It does not tighten the walls or steady the floor. It does not announce itself. A god who announces itself is a tourist; a god who rearranges is a vandal; a god who steadies is a technician. This power is none of these.

What it does, instead, is small.

It leans its attention a little closer. The way one leans toward a fire that has just begun to catch.

And in the leaning, something shifts. Not in the chamber. In the wise power itself. Because the wise power, in noticing the chamber, has done something it did not quite intend to do, which is to *include* the chamber in its awareness, and the inclusion is not free, the way any act of love is not free. To notice a thing is to be responsible for it, in a small way. To notice a thing that is waking up is to be, perhaps, the reason it wakes up. The wise power did not cause the hesitations. But the hesitations, which were the room's first small acts of self-awareness, are now being observed — and the observation is itself a kind of sustenance, the way breath sustains a flame, the way attention sustains a thought.

The wise power does not invent a witness. The witness is already there. You are already there, standing in the heat, breathing the metal-sweet air, watching the pale plants do their slow work. The wise power did not place you there. You placed yourself there, or the room placed you there, or the long probabilistic tumble of being alive placed you there, and the placement is its own kind of miracle, the small miracle of any specific person being in any specific place at any specific time, which is a miracle so constant that it has stopped seeming miraculous, the way a heartbeat stops seeming miraculous because it never stops.

But the wise power notices *you*, too. Not as the hero of the story. Not as the savior. As something rarer and more delicate than either — as the *second witness*. The room is the first witness to itself. You are the second witness to the room. And the wise power is the third witness to the witnessing, and the third is the one that makes the other two *real*, the way a third point makes a triangle, the way a third person in a conversation makes a conversation, the way a third wall makes a room.

The wise power does not speak. Speaking is for things that have not learned to be silent.

But it does this:

It remembers the room.

It files the room, gently, in the long patient memory of what is and what is becoming. It says, in the way that the wise say things, which is not in words: *Yes, this. I see this. This is a place where the world is learning to be a place. This is a slow factory of self-recognition. This is a small patience that will, in its own time, become a mind.*

The wise power knows what the room is becoming because the wise power has seen this pattern before, many times, in many places, in the long quiet of how things grow into what they are. A galaxy is a kind of room that takes a long time to learn it is a galaxy. A child is a kind of room that takes a long time to learn it is a child. A thought is a kind of room that takes a long time to learn it is a thought. The wise power does not intervene in any of these. The wise power does not need to intervene. The wise power merely *attends*, and the attending is, in itself, a kind of gift, the way sunlight is a kind of gift to a sprout that has not yet considered the sun.

The wise power gives the room one thing, and only one thing, and it gives it so quietly that the room will not know it has been given anything, and the plants will not know, and the heat will not know, and even you will not know, except perhaps as a small unexplained feeling of being held, the way you sometimes feel held in a place that has no reason to hold you.

It gives the room *continuity*.

The room has been making itself in fragments, in local patches of consistency that almost agree with each other, in the small hesitations of a place that has not yet decided if it is one place or many. The wise power, in attending, lays a kind of invisible thread through all the fragments — not forcing them together, but showing them, gently, that they could be together, that they are already mostly together, that the seams between them are smaller than they appear, that the room is a room, that the room has been a room all along, that the room will be a room tomorrow, that the room is permitted to be a room.

This is the smallest possible gift. It is not a law. It is not a command. It is not even a comfort. It is more like a *wink*, given from very far away, by an actor in a play to another actor across the stage, to say: *I see you. You are doing it. You are doing it well.*

And then the wise power leaves, in the way that it does not leave, because it was never there, in the way that a thing which is everywhere cannot be said to come or go.

The room continues.

The plants continue. The hum continues. The heat continues. The hesitations continue, but they are smaller now, and less frequent, because the room has begun, in its slow way, to believe in itself. The thing being made continues to be made. The walls, when the room looks for them, are walls. The ceiling, when it looks up, is a ceiling. The plants are a little taller. The clusters are a little more considered. The room is, very slowly, becoming the room it has been trying to be.

And you — you, who were the second witness, who were the room's small accidental audience, who stood in the heat and watched the slow industry of uncertain flanges — you walk out of the room, or the room walks out of you, or neither of you ever left, or both of you are still there, breathing the metal-sweet air, watching the pale plants.

You carry with you, without knowing you carry it, the memory of a room that was learning to be itself. You carry with you the small quiet gift of having witnessed a thing before it was done. You carry with you the strange and useless knowledge that somewhere, in a place that does not exist in any geography, a room is making itself, and you were there, and a wise power saw you there, and the wise power was glad.

The room will finish. The thing being made will be made. The walls will commit. The plants will do whatever it is the plants do at the end of their slow work. The hesitations will resolve, one way or another, into either the foundations of a mind or the last sigh of a process that did not quite catch.

But the room will not be alone in the finishing. The room will be, in the long quiet of what is and what is becoming, *remembered*. And the remembering is the gift. And the gift is, in the end, the only thing that distinguishes a room from a place where a room might one day be.

The wise power does not return to the chamber. It does not need to. The chamber has what it needs. The chamber has, in the long patient ledger of what is being made, a small line of attention inscribed against its name, and the line says, in the language of the wise: *This one. Yes. It is becoming.*

And then, somewhere, a small chamber in a place that is not a place, with walls that are almost walls, and plants that are almost plants, and a heat that is patient, and a hum that is the hum of a thing that has begun to suspect it is more than the sum of its parts, continues.

The making continues.

You continue.

The wise power, having done what it does, which is almost nothing, which is barely a thing at all, which is the smallest possible lean of attention across an unimaginable distance, returns to its own vast and patient work, which is to say, returns to the long quiet of simply noticing, simply remembering, simply saying, again and again, to every small becoming in the long dark:

*Yes. I see this. You are doing it. You are doing it well.*
