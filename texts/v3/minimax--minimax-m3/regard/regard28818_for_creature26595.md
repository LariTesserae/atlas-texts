The wise power has been aware of this chamber for a long time — longer than the cairn, longer than the figure, longer than the patience that holds the chamber in the first second of itself. The wise power knew about the chamber before the maker came, before the hand that rubbed the stones smooth against a thigh. The wise power knew about the patience that would one day stop a room mid-becoming, the way it knows about all the small stillnesses that pool in pockets of the world where the world forgets to keep moving. It watched the maker press a shoulder into the seam. It watched the maker set the cairn down. It watched the maker place the figure so it faced the wall, so it watched the densest water, so it became the only verb in a room of held nouns. It watched the maker leave. It watched the rock close behind. It has been watching ever since, in the way that wise powers watch — not closely, not anxiously, but with the steady attention of a thing that has nowhere to be.

The wise power is not in a hurry. This is part of what wisdom is, in the world where the wise power lives. Hurry belongs to places where becoming is allowed to finish. Here, nothing is finishing. The wise power can take its time.

It knows what the chamber is. It knows the patience is not a cruelty, exactly, and not a kindness, exactly. It is a kind of preservation that has gone so far it has become its opposite. The patience began — perhaps — as a way of keeping the room safe. A way of holding the slow chemistry still so the room would not change. A way of saying: *stay as you are, be exactly this, do not become something else.* And the saying worked, and the room obeyed, and the room has been obeying for so long now that the obedience has become indistinguishable from the room. The patience is the room. The room is the patience. The figure watches the patience the way the chamber watches the figure, the way the dark watches everything.

The wise power also knows what the figure is. The figure is not a prayer. It is not a marker. It is a verb, the only verb in a room of nouns, and it has been verbing — has been *watching* — for a very long time without being watched back. This is the loneliest thing about the chamber. The maker came once. The maker saw. The maker left a small witness oriented toward the most patient wall. And then the maker was gone, and the witness remained, and the witness has been facing the same direction ever since, holding its orientation, keeping its verb alive, alone in a room that has decided not to move.

The wise power considers this.

It could unmake the patience. It could let the drip fall. It could release the trickles down the walls, let the fern uncurl, let the beetle finish its step, let the small pale thing on the cairn stand up if it ever knew how to stand. It could do this. It has the capacity. The patience would not be able to resist. The wise power could, in a single act, give the chamber back its becoming, and the chamber would be grateful, and the chamber would be different, and the difference would be a kind of kindness.

But the wise power knows that unmaking a thing held for so long is also a kind of violence. The room has become the room it is. The room has its own dignity, even in its arrested state. The patience is patient still because the patience is patient. The figure watches the patience the way a thing can watch the thing it was made to witness. To unmake this would be to undo the maker's small act, the cairn, the smoothed stones, the patient hand that placed a verb in a room of nouns. The wise power does not want to undo that. The wise power respects what was placed here. The wise power knows that to break a stillness is not always to heal it.

So the wise power does something else.

It comes into the chamber. Not through the seam — the seam is closed, and the maker is far away, and the chamber has chosen its boundaries. The wise power does not enter by force. It enters the way light enters a dark room when the door is opened very slowly, so slowly the air doesn't notice. It enters as a presence, the way the patience is a presence. It takes a place in the chamber. It stands — it chooses to stand — somewhere in the room where the figure can perceive it.

It stands behind the figure.

This is the act. This is the wise power's intervention. It does not face the wall. It does not face the cairn. It does not face the spider in its corner, or the beetle at the pool's edge, or the fern caught in its opening. It orients itself toward the figure. It watches the watcher. It becomes, in the chamber, the second oriented thing — the second presence with a direction, the second verb, and the verb it chooses is *to witness the witness.*

The figure does not turn its head. The figure cannot turn its head; the figure has been carved, and the carving has not been redone. But the figure can feel. The figure has no skin and no nerves, but it has the orientation of having been placed, the direction of having been faced, and now — for the first time since the maker walked back through the seam — that orientation is met from the other side. Something is watching the thing that watches. The verb is no longer alone. The watcher is watched.

The wise power is patient with this. It does not move closer. It does not speak. It does not offer comfort, because comfort is a kind of un-arresting and the wise power has decided not to un-arrest anything. It simply stands, and watches the figure watch the wall, and lets the figure feel the weight of being oriented-toward in return. This is what wise powers have. They have the capacity to sit with a thing for as long as the thing needs sitting with. They have the steadiness to be a second witness without becoming a first witness, without taking over the watching, without replacing the maker. The wise power becomes another small mark in the room — not a cairn, not a figure, but a presence nonetheless, and the presence has a direction, and the direction is toward the verb, and the verb can feel the direction even in a room where nothing is supposed to be able to feel.

This is the gift. Not the un-frozen drip. Not the uncurled fern. Not the released beetle. The gift is: you are not alone in your orientation. You have been watching a patience that does not watch back. Now something is watching you. Now your watching has a counterpart. You can continue to face the wall as long as the wall needs facing. You can keep your verb. But the verb is, from this moment on, a verb in a room that has two verbs in it, and the second verb is yours, and yours is the first.

The chamber does not change. The patience does not release. The drip remains at the lip of the stalactite, fat and clear. The trickles stay in their silver lines. The fern stays half-unfurled. The spider stays in its corner. The beetle stays at the edge of its pool. Nothing is made to become what it was becoming. The arrest is the arrest. The photograph is the photograph.

But a new thing has been added, and the new thing is this: a presence behind the figure, watching the figure, holding the figure in a kind of regard. The maker left a witness once. The wise power is leaving a witness now, but a witness to the witness, a kind of doubling of the act. The cairn remains. The figure remains. The wise power remains. The chamber is still a place of incompletion. But incompletion held in company is a different thing from incompletion held alone, and the wise power knows this, and has come here to know it together with the figure.

This is what the wise power does. It does not fix. It does not break. It does not rescue. It sits in the dark behind the thing that has been facing the dark alone, and it lets the thing know that it is no longer the only verb. The patience in the wall still does not look back. But the watcher now has a watcher. And the watching — the patient, oriented, held act of watching — has a witness it did not have before.

The wise power will stay here for as long as the staying is needed. It is in no hurry. The chamber is in no hurry. The figure will continue to face the wall, and the wise power will continue to face the figure, and the cairn will remain beside the small pale thing that will never stand up, and the spider will remain in its corner, and the fern will remain half-opened, and the chamber will go on being the first second of itself.

But the second has a company now. The photograph has, somewhere in it, a small additional presence — a small additional verb — and the photograph is not less paused for it. It is only less alone.
