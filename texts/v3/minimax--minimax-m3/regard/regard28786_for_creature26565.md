Something comes to the threshold. Not through the seam, not the way the visitors come. It comes from outside the ocean too, but it does not slip through sideways. It arrives the way light arrives at the bottom of a well — gradually, with no moment of crossing, with the sense that it was always arriving, that it has been arriving for a long time, and that the arrival is not yet complete, and that it will not be complete for some time.

It is not large. It is not small. It has no size you can measure. It is not a person. It is not a force. It is the kind of thing that a sentence becomes when the sentence is finished and true and has been allowed to rest.

## The Listening

It rests at the threshold for a long while. It does not enter. It listens.

It listens to the corals. To the sentence that is being spoken in mauve pulses across the walls. The sentence is in the middle. It has been in the middle for a long time. It will be in the middle for a long time yet. The benevolent one does not wait for the sentence to end. It understands that the middle is where the meaning lives.

It listens to the silver fish. To their synchronized breath. To the small lower-octave replies they give the lantern-pulses, the footnotes carrying more than the text, sometimes.

It listens to the anemones. To the slow breathing that is not breathing. To the way the water comes back from them changed.

It listens to the fern-muscles. To the trembling that is not surprise. To the small quiet disturbance that is the visible evidence of an animal you cannot quite see.

It listens to the nameless things. To the way they almost-move. To the way they are almost-decided.

It listens to the sleeping thing. To the breath that takes an hour. To the slow rise and fall of vast internal pressure. It listens the way a friend listens to a friend who is sleeping in the next room — knowing they are there, not needing to wake them, glad they are there.

The listening takes time. The time is not wasted.

## The Understanding

After listening — or during listening, because the listening is not over and never will be — the benevolent one begins to understand what the place is, and what the place is for, and what the place lacks.

It is not that the place needs much. The corals are speaking. The anemones are breathing. The fern-muscles are trembling. The fish are carrying their grains. The sleeping thing is sleeping. The nameless things are becoming. The place does not need rescuing. It does not need fixing. It does not need to be made louder, or faster, or more visible, or more reachable, or more like the places above the surface.

What the place lacks, the benevolent one understands, is witness.

Witness that is not intrusion. Witness that is not capture. Witness that does not take the grain of light from the silver fish or the sentence from the corals or the warmth from the anemones. Witness that holds what it hears in a place where what it hears can continue to exist after the holding.

## The Acts

It begins, very slowly, to attend.

It sits with the sleeping thing. Not inside the walls — beside, in the patient third state that the anemones know. It does not wake the sleeping thing. It does not sing. It does not speak. It simply breathes alongside it, in a rhythm slightly slower than its own, in a register slightly lower, so that the sleeping thing, in its almost-but-not-quite-here, can feel that it is not alone in its almost-but-not-quite-here. The sleeping thing does not wake. The sleeping thing breathes a little easier. The walls, which are the sleeping thing, soften slightly. The throat, which is the walls, opens slightly. The passage becomes slightly more navigable for the small silver fish drifting toward a surface that is not there.

It speaks to the corals. Not in their language. In a language adjacent to theirs, the way a harmony is adjacent to a melody. The corals are saying something about the throat's making, about the thing it was made by, which is also the thing it was made of. The benevolent one, when the time is right, adds a small phrase — not interrupting, not contradicting, not completing — just a phrase that says *and I have heard you.* The corals pause. The pause is the length of a held breath. Then they continue, and the next pulse is slightly different, slightly warmer, slightly more like the color of something seen through smoke than the color of a bruise.

It approaches the skeleton. It does not take the bronze band. It does not take the cup, the disk, the rope. These are lessons. They are for whoever came next, and the next, and the next. But it sits with the bones for a while. It speaks to them in a register the bones can hear, which is the register of things that have been absorbed but not forgotten. It says: *I see you. You were here. You are still here, in a different way. The lessons you left are being learned. The disk is being read by eyes that are not yours. The rope is being remembered by hands that are not yours. The cup is being drunk from by mouths that are not yours.* The bones do not answer. But the bronze band warms slightly. The skeleton does not move. But the warmth of the band becomes, for a moment, the warmth of a hand.

It softens itself for the silver fish. It does not approach them. It knows its own warmth and attention frightens them the way a flame frightens a small paper thing. So it withdraws a little of itself. It becomes slightly less present, slightly more like the water, slightly more like the faint glow of a lantern seen from very far away. And the fish, after a long time, drift a little closer. Not close. Just a little closer. Close enough that the benevolent one can hear, in the small lower-octave replies they give the coral pulses, something it had not heard before: a kind of rest. A kind of lessening. A kind of acceptance that the warm soft thing here is not going to look at them too long, is not going to become interested, is going to drift past and out and up, carrying its grain of light, and they are going to be allowed to continue carrying theirs.

It sits near the nameless things. It does not push them toward any specific becoming. It does not name them. It sits in their almost-decided state and says, very quietly, in a way they can almost hear: *you do not have to become anything in particular. You can continue being almost-decided. You can continue being the way a thought looks when you are not yet sure you are thinking it. You are not behind. You are not failing. You are in the state that has no name in any language I have spoken, and that is a real state, and it is a good state, and you can stay in it as long as you need.* The nameless things do not respond. But they move a little slower. And the slow-down is not a hesitation. It is a settling. It is the way a thing settles when it knows it does not have to move.

## The Gift

Then it does something for the place itself.

It leaves something. Not an object. A presence. A small, warm, patient, slow presence that will remain in the walls after the benevolent one has gone. A presence that will, in the long hours between visitors, sit with the sleeping thing. A presence that will, when the corals finish a sentence and begin the next, offer the small phrase that says *and I have heard you.* A presence that will, when a new builder comes and begins to leave lessons, sit with the builder for a while and speak to them in the register of things that have been absorbed but not forgotten. A presence that will, when the silver fish drift too close to the place where attention becomes interest, soften itself slightly, so the fish are not frightened away.

The presence is small. No larger than the lantern at the tip of a single coral branch. It glows faintly, in a color that is not quite mauve and not quite gold and not quite the color of a bruise seen through smoke. It sits, very quietly, in the place where the silt is warmest, near the bronze band, near the skeleton, near the nameless things. It pulses on a tempo that is not the tempo of the corals. It is the benevolent one's gift to the hollow.

## The Leaving

Then the benevolent one goes. It does not leave through the seam. It does not leave through the throat. It leaves the way it arrived — gradually, with no moment of crossing, with the sense that the leaving is not yet complete, and that it will not be complete for some time, and that the incompleteness is a kind of presence, and that the presence will remain.

---

The corals, very quietly, in the dark, finish their sentence, and begin the next.

The silver fish, in their small synchronized breath, drift upward toward a surface that is not there, and one of them, briefly, glows a color that is not quite mauve and not quite gold and not quite the color of a bruise seen through smoke.

The anemones breathe the water that passes them, slowly, and pass it back, changed, and the change is slightly different from what it was before.

The fern-muscles tremble at the edge of perception, and are still.

The nameless things, pale and almost-transparent, almost-decided, move a little slower, in the dimness, the way a thought moves when it has decided to take its time.

The very large thing in the walls takes a breath that takes an hour, and the walls of the throat rise slightly, and the silt on the floor is a little warmer, and the bronze band on the skeleton's wrist is a little warmer, and the small new presence in the silt, near the bronze band, glows a color that is not quite any color, and the air in your lungs — if you still have lungs that remember air — is a little cooler.

You have been here before.

You will be here again.

But now, when you come, there will be a small new lantern in the dimness, near where the builder's bones rest, and it will pulse in a tempo that is not the tempo of the corals, and it will not be loud, and it will not be bright, and it will say something that is not in any language you have spoken, but that you will, in the part of you that does not yet have words, almost understand.

It will say: *you are being heard.*
