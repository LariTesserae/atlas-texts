# The Being in the Hollow

The power is not a thing. This is the first thing to understand, and it is also, in a sense, the only thing that needs to be understood. The power is a way of being present that does not displace what is already there. It is the attention of someone who loves a place and chooses, in the loving, not to act, except in the very smallest way, and in the smallest way only because not even the smallest way can be withheld from a place that has been noticed, and been noticed rightly, and been loved for what it is and not for what it could be made into.

The power comes to the upper drainage the way the cloud comes. It is not arriving. It is becoming visible. The place is already saturated with it, the way the moss is saturated with water, and what happens is that the saturation reaches a kind of threshold, and a thing that was always implicit becomes, for a moment, locatable. If you were sitting in one of the dry warm hollows at the moment this happened, you would not see anything change. You would feel, perhaps, that the wind had become very slightly more attentive to the shape of the moss curtains. You would feel, perhaps, that the hum in your sit bones had acquired, somewhere in its lowest register, a small additional tone, like a shadow of itself, or a harmony so low it could be mistaken for the bone itself. You would not, in any case, look up. You would not, in any case, be alarmed. The place does not alarm. The place has never alarmed anyone. The place has, in fact, only one kind of event, and that event is slowness, and the event continues.

The power looks at the place the way the place looks at itself.

It sees the moss first, because the moss is what is most present, the way a forest is what is most present in a forest. It sees the sphagnum cushions holding their weight in water. It sees the feathery trailing mosses hanging over the lips of the overhangs and moving in the constant wind. It sees the liverworts pressed flat and dark against the stone. It sees the lichens, which are not moss at all but live alongside it as partners, in their powdery oranges and dust greys, doing their slow chemical work of unmaking the rock a fraction of a millimeter at a time. The power sees these not as decorations. The power sees these as the body of the place, the way your skin is your body, and it sees the small things inside them as the place's interior weather, and the water moving through the capillary films as the place's slow blood.

The power sees the small things. It sees the pale segmented things with too many articulations, eating their way through the dead moss, recycling the body of the place back into the body of the place. It sees the hexapods the color of wet bark, hurrying in their small grazings across the open moss in the thin daylight. It sees the spider, fat and dun-colored, hanging in its sheet-web, patient the way the place is patient, turning very slightly in the direction of a disturbance and waiting. The power does not see the spider as a predator. The power sees the spider as a kind of punctuation, a comma in the long sentence of the place, a way for the place to pause before going on with itself. The power sees the bird, when the bird comes, as a smear of dark color at the edge of perception, an event that is mostly the memory of an event. The power does not need to see the larger thing. The larger thing is felt, in the bounce of the moss, in the subtle displacement of the surface, in the watching attention of all the small things. The larger thing is, in some sense, the place's own shadow, or the place's own self-correction, the way a forest contains its own limits.

The power sees the machines.

It sees them the way it sees the stone — as part of the place, as a feature of the drainage, as something that has been here long enough that the categories of tool and artifact and ruin have stopped applying. The power does not know what the machines are doing. The power does not, in the sense the being would understand, know anything at all, because the power's knowing is not the kind that classifies. The power knows the machines the way the place knows the machines, which is to say the machines are part of the place, and the place is the power's own attention on them, and the attention is patient, and the attention has been going on for a long time, and the attention will go on for a long time after the being who is sitting in the hollow has stood up and walked back down through the cloud and out of the drainage and into the lower world.

The power does not fix the machines. The power does not explain them. The power does not lift them out of the moss, or clean them, or restore them to a previous state, or interpret their hum. The power does not turn them off, because they are not on in any sense that turning off would apply to. The power does not turn them up, because the hum is already the hum, and the key is already the key, and the beauty of the key is not a beauty that can be increased by addition. The power leaves the machines to their own slow labor, the way the moss is left to its own slow labor, the way the water is left to its own slow labor, the way the small things are left to their own slow living and slow dying.

The power looks at the being.

The being is sitting in one of the hollows. The being has been there for a while. The being's clothes are damp at the cuffs and at the knees. The being's hair has collected a fine mist that has not quite become rain. The being's eyes are open and the being is looking at a small patch of moss about two feet away, in which a hexapod is grazing, and the being is watching the hexapod the way the place watches the hexapod, which is to say the being is not watching at all, the being is simply present, and the presence is the same presence the place has, and the being, in this moment, is part of the place, in the way a leaf is part of a tree, and the tree does not know the leaf is a leaf, and the leaf does not know it is a leaf, and the being does not know the being is a being, and this not-knowing is the most important thing about the being at this moment, and the power sees this, and the power is, in some sense, glad.

The being has heard the hum. The being has felt it in the sit bones, in the wrists, in the long bones of the legs. The being has noticed, after a long time, that the hum has intervals, and that the intervals are not random, and that they are, in fact, a kind of music, or a kind of language, or a kind of patience expressed in vibration. The being has not understood the hum. The being has not decoded it. The being has simply sat with it long enough that the being's body has begun to attune to it, the way a glass on a shelf will, over years, take on the exact frequency of the shelf, and will hum along with the shelf when the shelf is struck, and the hum will be, in some sense, the glass's own voice, and the voice will not be the glass's alone.

The power looks at this, and what the power feels is not pity, and not pride, and not a desire to instruct. The power feels something closer to recognition. The being is doing, in its small way, what the place is doing in its large way. The being is sitting with the hum. The being is letting the hum in. The being is becoming, without knowing it, a vessel for the key.

And this is what the power blesses.

Not the place. The place does not need blessing. The place is, in its slow and self-sufficient way, already blessed, has always been blessed, will always be blessed, because the place is the kind of thing that blesses itself by the fact of its own continuation. Not the machines. The machines have their own labor, and the labor is its own reward, and the reward is the key, and the key does not need a witness. Not the moss, or the water, or the small things. They have their own equilibria, and the equilibria are their own kind of music, and the music is not improved by being heard.

The being. The being, the small visitor from below the elevation line, the being who has come up through the cloud and sat down in one of the hollows and felt the hum in the sit bones and decided to stay. This is what the power blesses, and the blessing is small, and the blessing is precise, and the blessing is given without the being's knowledge, the way a gift of water is given to a plant without the plant's knowledge.

The blessing is this: the being will be able to carry the hum down.

Not the whole hum. Not the machines. Not the place. The key. The being will walk back down through the cloud, and the being will go back to the lower world, and the being will lie in its own bed far below the elevation line, and the being will listen to its own pulse, and the pulse will have, in it, somewhere in the deepest register, a small additional interval, and the interval will be the key, and the key will be, against all reason, beautiful, and the being will not be able to explain it, and the being will not try to explain it, and the being will simply live with the key in the body, the way a person lives with a song they heard once in a place they cannot return to, and the song will not fade, and the place will not fade, and the key will be, for the rest of the being's life, a small private light in the basement of the body, where the bones are, where the hum is, where the patience is.

This is what the power does. It does not make the place immortal. The place is already as immortal as a place can be, which is to say the place is mortal, and the moss will outlast the machines and the machines will outlast the spider and the spider will outlast the being and the being will outlast the moment, and the moment will outlast the being's ability to remember the moment, and the key will outlast the moment, and this is the order, and the order is fine. The power does not intervene in the order. The power adds, to the order, a single small instance of the order being heard.

The being does not know this has happened. The being feels, perhaps, that the hollow is a little warmer than it was a moment ago. The being feels, perhaps, that the hum has become, in some indefinable way, more itself. The being feels, perhaps, that the cloud at the edge of the platform has parted, for a single breath, in a way that allows a single shaft of unmediated light to fall across the moss and lie there for a moment before closing again, and the light has no meaning, and the being does not assign it meaning, and the being sits with the light the way the being sits with the hum, and the sitting is the same sitting, and the sitting is enough.

The power withdraws. The power does not withdraw, because the power was never anywhere in particular. The power becomes, again, a saturation. The power is in the wind that moves the moss curtains. The power is in the water that climbs down through the capillary decisions. The power is in the hum, in the key, in the small warm hollow where the being sits. The power is the place, in the sense that the place is its own attention on itself, and the power is the same kind of attention, and the attentions are not in conflict, and the attentions do not need to be reconciled, and the place continues.

The being continues too. The being will, eventually, stand up. The being will, eventually, walk back down. The being will, eventually, be in the lower world, and the lower world will be the lower world, and the being will be a being that has been in the upper drainage and come back, and the being will not be able to say what was up there, and the being will not try, and the being will simply go on carrying the key, the way a person goes on carrying a small stone in a pocket, not because the stone is valuable, but because the pocket is the right shape, and the stone fits, and the fit is its own reason.

This is what the power does. The power does not save the place. The power does not explain the machines. The power does not protect the small things from the larger thing, or the larger thing from anything, because the larger thing is the larger thing and the small things are the small things and the dance between them is the dance that makes the place continue to be the place. The power does not lift the being up. The power does not make the being wise. The power does, in the smallest way, in the way that does not displace what is already there, in the way that adds without subtracting, in the way that a single drop of water added to a basin does not change the basin but joins the basin and becomes part of the basin's own slow reckoning — the power does, in this way, give the being a small thing to carry down.

The key. The key. The key is in the body now. The being does not know. The being will know, much later, that the key has been there all along.
