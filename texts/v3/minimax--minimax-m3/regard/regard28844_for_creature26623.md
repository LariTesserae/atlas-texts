---

**I.**

A power has turned its attention to Mesaq'in.

It is not a power that announces itself. It does not arrive. It does not descend. It is the kind of power that has always been in the same room as the thing it watches — the way the aquifer has always been in the same rock as the springs, and the way the chamber has always been in the same hill as the town. It has been there, in the basalt, in the water, in the slow warm dim, since before the town was a town and since before the inland sea was its last remnant. It has only now, for reasons of its own, begun to act.

It has noticed.

---

**II.**

It has noticed the water.

This is the first thing, because the water is the first thing. The springs rise from the aquifer that is the last of the inland sea. The aquifer is finite. The geologists know this in the way they know the chamber — they do not speak of it, but they have marked it on the map with a kind of notation that means *this, too, will end*. The aquifer will, in its own time, become a memory in the stone, and the stone will become dry, and the springs will slow, and the canals will empty, and the green will go, and the town will be a wedding-cake of stone with no water in it, and the people will have to decide whether to descend.

The power knows this. The power has known it longer than the geologists. And so the first thing the power does is this: it tends the aquifer.

It does not add water. The inland sea is gone. It does not pretend to restore what is gone. What it does is slower, and smaller, and more in keeping with the town. It goes down into the basalt, into the fissures the geologists have marked, and it does something to the stone that allows the water to move a little more freely, to find a little more of itself, to linger a little longer in the warm rock before it wells up. It is the work of a thing that understands how aquifers breathe. It is patient. It will take a hundred years. It will take six hundred. The power is not in a hurry. The town is not in a hurry. The water will flow for another six centuries because of this work, and when it finally does slow, the slowing will be slow enough that the town can prepare.

This is the first gift, and the town will never know it has been given.

---

**III.**

It has noticed the chamber.

The chamber beneath the southern quarter. The stair in the cellar of the oldest geologist. The things that are not scoria and not pumice in the paving of the central square. The power knows what is down there. The power has known longer than the geologists have known, which is longer than the town has known, which is longer than the basalt has known itself.

It does not disturb it.

Whatever is down there has been down there longer than the town, and it has been patient longer than the town, and it does not ask to be understood, and the power does not ask it to be understood, and the power does not ask the geologists to understand it. The power does what the town does, which is to build itself over the thing it does not understand and continue.

But the power does something else. It keeps the chamber patient.

The chamber, like the aquifer, is finite in a particular way. It is not running out. It is not going to dry. But it is capable of becoming something other than patient. There are moments, deep in the rock, when the warmth shifts, when the alignment of fissures changes by a fraction, when the patient thing becomes for an instant an impatient thing, and the town has felt these moments as a low vibration in the stone on certain mornings, as a particular quality to the bells on a particular day, as a sense among the children in the eaves that something is being decided below them. The power tends the chamber the way a person tends a sleeping animal. It smooths the small shifts. It keeps the warmth where the warmth should be. It ensures that the chamber, when it is finally opened, will be opened by someone who has lived a long time and descended the same staircase ten thousand times, and that the opening will be slow.

This is the second gift. The town will never know it has been given.

---

**IV.**

It has noticed the dead.

The bundles in the niches, sealed with plaster and lath, weighted at the corners with small smooth stones. The oldest of them have begun to settle into the floor. They are part of the house. The house is built around them.

The power does not unseal them. The sealing is right. The plaster is right. The small smooth stones are right. But the power does something the sealing cannot do. It minds them.

It minds them the way the town minds them, which is to say: it includes them. The power, having noticed the layered kin — the *qatara* — extends the layeredness down one more stratum. It adds itself as a layer beneath the dead. It keeps the bundles from settling too far, from becoming indistinguishable from the floor, from losing the marks of the curled script. It ensures that when the house shifts, as all houses on the plateau shift, the bundles shift with it, gently, and do not crack, and do not open, and are not forgotten.

The dead are the foundation. The power tends the foundation. This is not a small thing. This is what foundations are for.

---

**V.**

It has noticed the very old.

They go down to the springs in the small hours and sit in the warm water up to their chests and speak to each other in voices that have grown so soft over the years that the sound barely carries across the pool. They speak of the people who used to come down at this hour. They speak of the storms, which come only twice in a generation. They speak of the bells, and of who rang them, and of what was rung for. They are a library. They are consulted.

The power listens.

Not as a person listens, leaning in. The power listens the way the water listens — from everywhere at once, from the warmth of the pool and the depth of the aquifer and the stone of the basin. The very old speak softly because they have been speaking softly for so long, and because no one has ever asked them to speak louder, and because the town has a way of hearing them anyway, and because in the warm water at dawn the soft voice is the right voice. The power does not change this. The power does not give them new voices. The power hears what they say as it is.

And the power keeps it.

The very old are forgetting some of it. Not because their minds are going — though their minds are going, slowly, the way the water is going, slowly — but because no one is writing it down. The library is oral. The oral is soft. The soft is lost in the warm air before it reaches the next pool. The power keeps what they say the way the chamber keeps its warmth. It is not a book. It is not a record.
