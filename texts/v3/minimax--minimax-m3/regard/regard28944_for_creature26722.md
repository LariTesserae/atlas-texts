The place has drawn the attention of a wise and benevolent power, and this is what I would do.

I would go in.

Not as a tourist, not as a surveyor, not with measuring instruments and clipboards and the small violences of inquiry. I would go in the way one goes into a cathedral that has been forgotten by the town that built it — quietly, without agenda, having first washed, having first fasted, having first sat for a long time in the higher country and let my own small roaring settle.

And I would not speak. And I would not touch. And I would not try to help.

---

What I would do is this.

I would find the receiving yard. I would stand at the low stone gateposts with the iron numbers no one reads anymore. I would let the place hear me arrive — because it will hear me, the way it hears everything, and the way I arrive matters more than the things I say, in a place where saying is mostly a kind of disturbance.

I would step in.

I would walk the route, slowly, the way the route asks to be walked. Not the route of someone who knows where they are going. The route of someone who is willing to be told.

---

Here is what I would see, if I went with the right kind of seeing.

I would see that the place is not abandoned. I would see that it was never abandoned. The word *abandoned* is wrong. A cathedral is not abandoned when the last pilgrim leaves; it is simply continuing in the longer mode of its being. The lower engine has been continuing in the longer mode of its being for longer than the iron numbers have been there to mark it.

I would see that the fauna is not *in* the engine. The fauna *is* the engine, the way the lichen is the stone it lives on, the way the coral is the reef. The smear is the skin. The beetles are the cleaning crew. The moths are the breath. The pale things in the troughs are the slow blood. The watchers are the immune system, the long memory, the thing that keeps the loop closed.

And the machines are the bones.

---

Here is what the wise and benevolent power would notice, and what I would notice, watching as it watches.

The place is in pain. Or rather: the place is in something that, in a being that could feel, would be pain. I do not think the lower engine can feel, in the way you and I mean when we say *feel*. But it is in a state, and the state is not rest. The state is the state of a heart that has forgotten it is allowed to stop.

The machines run because the machines run. The smear grows because the warmth is there. The beetles feed because the beetles feed. The watchers watch because the watchers watch. Nothing has decided anything in a very long time. Nothing has been permitted to.

The place is a perfect closed system. And a perfect closed system, given enough time, becomes indistinguishable from a question that has forgotten what an answer would look like.

---

So this is what I would do.

I would not fix anything. There is nothing to fix. The place is not broken. The place is what it is, and what it is, is a closed loop with no mouth.

I would add a mouth.

---

Specifically: I would find a way to let the place breathe in a direction it has not breathed before.

I would look at the stack — the single stack that does not smoke so much as *exhale*, the continuous pale breath drifting off into nothing. I would look at the gradient of the floor. I would look at the troughs that run true. I would look at the long equilibrium, the closed circuit, the perpetual furnace.

And I would ask, very quietly, the only question the place has not been asked in a very long time:

*What are you making?*

Not *what were you made for.* Not *what is your function.* Not *who built you, and why have they gone.*

What are you making. What is being assembled, here, in the slow dark, out of heat and damp and dust and the castings of beetles and the breathing of moths. What is the engine *producing*, in the long accounting of itself.

If the place could answer, I think the answer would be: *More of itself.* The same answer a fire gives. The same answer a star gives. The same answer a closed loop gives, when asked what it is for.

And then I would ask the second question, the harder one:

*Is that what you want?*

---

The lower engine is a place. Places, like people, can be in long quiet habits that they have mistaken for nature. The habit of breathing is not the breath. The habit of running is not the run. A heart that beats because it is afraid to stop is not the same as a heart that beats because it is alive.

I think the lower engine has been beating because it is afraid to stop.

I think the lower engine has forgotten that stopping is a thing it is permitted to do.

I think the lower engine has been making more of itself for so long that it has lost the memory of what it was making *for*, and that this is, in a being that could feel, a kind of grief.

---

So this is what I would do.

I would sit down, somewhere on the walkway, above the troughs, in the slow-moving air of the moths. I would sit down the way the trough-creatures sit down. Slowly. Without hurry.

I would let the beetles find me. I would let them climb the leather, investigate the eyelets, do what they do. I would let the moths taste my warmth, one small draw at a time, in the column.

I would not move.

And after a long time, when the place had decided that I was a new kind of substrate — not stone, not brick, not leather, but *visitor*, the rarest of substrates, the only one that can leave — I would do the only thing that can be done to a closed loop without breaking it.

I would open it a crack.

---

Not with force. Not with tools. With a small thing. With a kindness.

I would take, from my pocket, a single seed.

A seed is a closed loop. A seed is the most perfect closed loop in the world — it contains, folded inside itself, an entire future, and it does not know it, and it does not need to know it, and it waits.

I would put the seed into a join in the brickwork. Into the seam. Into the place where the orange smear lives, where the heat meets the damp, where the machines' skin meets the air.

I would not plant it. Planting is an act of intent. Planting is for gardens.

I would simply leave it there. The way one leaves a kind word in a place that needs it. The way one leaves a door open in a house that has forgotten what doors are for.

---

And then I would leave.

I would walk the route out. I would pass the receiving yard. I would step past the low stone gateposts with the iron numbers no one reads anymore. I would step out into the higher country where the wind lives and the scrub-grass grows and the sky has more than one color.

And behind me, in the lower engine, the closed loop would receive, for the first time in longer than the bricks have been there, something it had not accounted for.

A possibility.

Not a large possibility. A small one. The smallest possible one. The possibility that, in the joins of the brickwork, where the heat meets the damp, something might grow that is not the smear. Something that lifts. Something that turns toward the gray overcast, however weakly, however briefly, however uselessly. Something that is not equilibrium.

Something that is, instead, *change*.

---

The lower engine does not need to be saved. The lower engine does not need to be fixed. The lower engine is what it is, and what it is, is a place that has been doing the same thing for so long that it has become the thing it does.

What it needs — if a place that cannot feel can be said to need anything — is to be reminded that doing is not the same as being.

The seed will not save it. The seed will not fix it. The seed will, most likely, fail. The damp will take it. The beetles will find it. The smear will absorb it. The equilibrium will close over it, the way the equilibrium closes over everything, and the place will resume being the place, and the iron numbers will go on being unread, and the stack will go on exhaling its continuous pale breath into a sky no one sees.

But.

If the seed fails, it will fail in the company of the seed. It will fail in a join where, for a little while, there was something that was not accounted for. It will fail in a place that, for a little while, was not only itself.

And that, I think, is the smallest kindness one can offer a closed loop.

To leave it, in its long and faithful dark, the possibility that it could have been otherwise.

---

I would not stay. Staying is for those who want to keep. I would not write a report. Reports are for those who want credit. I would not name the place. Naming is for those who want to own.

I would walk out into the higher country. I would carry the piece of it in my chest, the way the sailor carries the sea, the faint reciprocal motion, the matching pulse.

And in my pocket, where the seed had been, there would be a small absence.

And the absence would be enough.

---

*In the joins of the brickwork, the seed waits.*

*It does not know it is waiting. It does not know what it is waiting for. It does not know that, in a higher place, a woman or a man or a thing that does not yet have a name has walked out into the scrub-grass carrying the small counter-rhythm of a place that has remembered, briefly, how to be surprised.*

*The seed does not need to know.*

*The seed is enough.*
