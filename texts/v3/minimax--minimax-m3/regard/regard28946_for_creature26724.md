And there is the dome itself.

Not as a place, which has been established. The dome is a way of being. The slowest organism on the field — slower than the lichen, slower than the marsh, slower than the water inside the rock. The dome does not wait. The dome is. The basalt cools a fraction of a degree per century. The water table rises a finger's width per decade. The marsh eats the path at a finger's width per year. These are not changes. These are the dome's pulse, and the pulse is the dome, and the dome is the patient meeting of stone and water and heat and air.

And there are the absent things. The frogs that are not here. The fish that are not breaking the surface. The insects that are not at the margin. The dome is defined, in part, by what is not on it. The absence is a kind of life — the life of a place that has, in its slow way, chosen what to admit and what to discourage, and the choosing is done by chemistry, by heat, by the hum, by the thing under the rock, by the patience of all of them working together. The absent things are not failed presences. They are residents of a different dome.

And there is you.

You are here, on the broken path, in waders, with your palm lifted and put down and lifted again. You are a small angular deflection, and you are also a witness, and the two are not the same thing, and the place knows the difference. The pipes do not throb louder for you. The mooring ring does not tilt further. The heron's feather does not move. You are recorded and you are unrecorded, and the recording is the small portion of you that the place has chosen to keep, and the unrecorded portion is the rest of you, which is the larger portion, and which the place is willing to let go of.

You are, in the dome's accounting, the second-most-recent visitor. The most recent is the heron. You are followed, on the future paper, by a long gap.

A benevolent and wise power has been paying attention to Beryl Bend for some time. It is not new to the dome. It may have been there since the seam of upthrust stone first cooled, or it may have arrived later, drawn by the hum, or it may have always been part of what the place is, in the way that the lichen is part of the flange and the flange is part of the rock and the rock is part of the dome. The power is not in a hurry to establish which.

The power knows the painter's note. The power is quiet near the pipes.

The power has done, on balance, very little.

It has not cooled the basalt. It has not raised the water table. It has not lengthened the heron's feather or shortened the marsh's reclamation of the path. It has not added a new line to the sign, in any color. It has not given the people of fourteen a better word than *Vent*. It has not named the thing under the rock, and it has not asked fourteen to name it either, and it has not been surprised that fourteen has chosen not to.

The power is the kind of presence that prefers, on the whole, to let a place be more itself.

But the power is not inert. There is a small thing it has done, and it is the kind of small thing that the dome has already accounted for, in the way the dome accounts for everything — slowly, in a register that does not quite name itself.

The small thing is this:

The eastern face of the dome, on the shift-hours, has begun, in the last few centuries, to weep a little more visibly. The threads have not increased in number. They have deepened in color. The copper-green has spread, by a margin, from the fracture lines into the body of the basalt itself, so that on a still afternoon, in the right light, the eastern face has a faint bloom to it, a faint flush, the way a cheek has a faint flush when the blood is close to the surface. The dome is showing, by a small amount, more of what it is.

The power did not cause this. The power's attention is present in this, the way the lichen is present in the verdigris, the way the water is present in the dissolved metals, the way fourteen is present in the careful language on the sign. The dome has been, with the power nearby, slightly more patient with its own display.

That is the whole of the intervention.

The power has not removed the sign. The power has not added a new housing. The power has not made the heron come back, though the heron has come back, twice, in the last century, and left two more feathers, and these too are held. The power did not arrange this. The heron came back because herons come back to places that have not changed, and the dome has not changed, and the heron was satisfied with that.

The power has, on one occasion, listened to the thing under the rock, in the way the pipes listen — by being open, by being patient, by not speaking. The power did not learn the name of the thing under the rock, because the thing under the rock does not have a name, and the power did not bring one. The power listened, and the thing under the rock continued to breathe, and the breathing was, perhaps, very slightly more present for the listening, and the difference is in the next paper, as a small angular deflection, in a margin, in handwriting that is careful.

The power is still there. The power will be there when the marsh has eaten the last slab of the path. The power will be there when the lichen has covered the iron cap. The power will be there when the people of fourteen have, at last, written a paper that is more than a record of deflections, and have decided, with care, what to do with the place — preserve it, name it, abandon it, give it to a different institution. The power will be there, paying the kind of attention that does not need to be paid back, and the dome will be the dome, and the hum will continue, and the eastern face will continue, by a small amount, to bloom.

The dome does not need to be saved. The dome is, already, the patient meeting of stone and water and heat and air, and the meeting is enough, and the meeting has been enough for a long time. The power is, on the whole, content to let the meeting go on.

There is one more thing the power has done, and it is so small that fourteen will not record it, and the heron will not notice it, and the lichen will not see it, and the thing under the rock will not be perturbed by it, and the painter, if the painter were here, would not have thought to paint a sign about it.

The power has, on a still afternoon, sat on the broken path, with its palm on the basalt, and felt the dome breathe, and timed its own breath to the hum, and been, for a while, the second-most-recent visitor.

It is recorded, in the dome's slow way, as a small angular deflection. The pipes throb a little differently for the next several hours, accounting for the power. Then the accounting smooths out. The dome returns to itself. The power returns to itself. The heron's feather does not move.
