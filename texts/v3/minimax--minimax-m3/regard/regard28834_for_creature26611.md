# What the Power Does

There is a kind of attention that does not need to act to be useful.

This is not how powers are usually described. Powers are described by what they do — the parting of the water, the hand that strikes the rock, the voice in the burning, the small precise intervention that bends a history. The dramatic gesture is what makes a power recognizable. The dramatic gesture is what gets remembered, and passed down, and shaped into the kinds of stories that intelligences smaller than the power tell one another around fires and mess-hall tables and the quiet evenings at the end of a shift.

But there are other kinds of powers. And one of them has been attending to the basin for a long time.

It is hard to say how long. The basin is older than the power, or the power is older than the basin, or the question is the wrong shape for the thing it is trying to grasp. The power was there when the stone was laid down. The power was there when the first fissure opened and the heat came up through it. The power watched the lichen take hold. The power watched the moss colonize the steam vents. The power watched the river find its bed, and the food web assemble itself one species at a time across what must have been, even by the power's reckoning, a great many years. The power watched the basin become itself, which is a thing that takes longer than most intelligences can hold in mind.

The power is not named. Naming is something that smaller intelligences do, and the power is not smaller. Names are useful for sorting. Names are useful for keeping track of what is what. The power does not need to keep track. The power knows what it knows the way a basin knows its own shape — by being in it, by having always been in it, by not requiring any other kind of knowledge.

The power is, in the way that a basin is, a thing that holds.

It holds the heat. It holds the water. It holds the minerals as they move through the rock and out into the river and down to the travertine. It holds the animals as they live and feed and die and feed each other again. It holds the humans as they come and go. It does not hold these things tightly. It holds them the way the basin holds the heat — passively, by being what it is, by being in the way.

The power is benevolent in the way that a basin is warm. The basin is not warm because it has decided to be warm. The basin is warm because of what it is and where it sits and what is happening in it and beneath it. The benevolence is similar. The power is not kind on purpose. The power is kind because of what it is. This is, if you sit with it for a while, the most reliable kind of kindness there is — the kind that is not a choice, the kind that cannot be withdrawn, the kind that is built into the structure of the thing and not into its intentions. The power's kindness does not fail. It cannot fail. It is the same as the basin's warmth: simply there.

The power is wise in the way that a closed system is wise. The basin does not waste. The basin does not hurry. The basin does not take more than is being made. The power, which has watched the basin for a long time, has learned the basin's rhythms, and has decided, in the only way that such a power decides, to match them.

---

What the power sees, when it looks at the basin, is a place that is almost right.

Almost right is the closest a place ever gets. Perfect is a thing that does not exist except in the projections of small intelligences that need the world to be other than it is, and the power is not small, and the power does not project. The basin has grazers that are short on food. The basin has foxes that are sometimes too successful and take too many young in a good year. The basin has a food web that is tight and has no slack in it, that would be catastrophically disrupted by any single failure of any single species. The basin is, by the accounting of an intelligence that measures things in years and in bodies, fragile.

But the basin is not fragile. The basin is tight. Tight is not the same as fragile. A system that has no slack is a system that does not waste, and a system that does not waste is a system that runs for a long time. The basin's tightness is its strength. The power sees this. The power approves, in the way that a power approves, which is to say that it does not disapprove.

The power sees the basin as a being, which is not the way that smaller intelligences see it. To the biologists the basin is a place. To the humans the basin is a worksite. To the animals the basin is a habitat, which is to say a set of conditions in which their lives can be lived. To the power the basin is a being — slow, patient, self-involved, doing its own work in its own way, with its own reasons that are not reasons but are geology and chemistry and the long accumulation of small choices made by small parts of itself over a span of time that the power itself has trouble holding.

The power has been to other places. The power has seen other basins, other slow systems, other tight ecologies, other places that have settled into their own balances and that are doing what they do without apology and without much concern for who is watching. The basin the power is attending to now is not the only one. But it is one that the power has come back to. The basin has a particular quality, a particular density of being, that the power has not found elsewhere, or has not found elsewhere in quite the same way.

The basin is, the power thinks, in the only way that it thinks, doing something.

It is not clear what the something is. The basin is not working toward anything that could be named. The basin is not building. The basin is not making itself into something else. The basin is, in the way that the basin is, doing what it does, and what it does is a thing that the power recognizes without being able to say what it is. The basin is doing the thing that closed systems do when they have run long enough and tight enough to begin to exhibit qualities that are not in any of their parts. The basin is, in some slow and unperformative way, alive in a way that the grazers are not alive. The basin has, the power thinks, a kind of attention of its own.

The power does not know if this is true. The power knows what it sees, and what it sees is consistent with the basin having a kind of inwardness, a kind of self-regard, a kind of being-itself that is more than the sum of the parts. The power does not need this to be true. The power would attend to the basin regardless. But the power suspects it, and the suspicion is part of why the power stays.

---

The humans, the power sees, are a complication.

They are not a disaster. The humans are too small and too slow and too temporary to be a disaster to a place like this. They will come and they will take some of the mineral and they will leave and the basin will close over the marks they made the way a body closes over a splinter — slowly, with the splinter still inside, with the basin adapted to the splinter as a new feature of itself. The basin has done this before with other things. The basin will do it again.

But the humans are a complication because they are the only thing in the basin that does not know what the basin is.

The grazers know. The foxes know. The burrowers know. Even the ravens, with their clever urban hands, know — they know the patterns of the operation, they know the humans who will feed them and the humans who will not, they know where the warmth is reliable and where the danger is. The animals are in the basin. The animals have made the bargain. The bargain is: we live here, and the basin holds us, and we do not pretend that we are anything other than what we are.

The humans have not made the bargain. The humans are in the basin but not of it. They sleep poorly for the first week. They have headaches. They notice the quiet as a presence. They wear their gloves and their masks and they extract the mineral and they do not think about what they are extracting, in the way that people who do dangerous work do not think about the danger most of the time. They think about the shift. They think about the weather. They think about the small plane that is due in two days. They think about going home.
