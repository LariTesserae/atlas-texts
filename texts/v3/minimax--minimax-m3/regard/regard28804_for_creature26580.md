The power is not a thing in the basin. It is not a weather, not a presence, not a figure that could be seen from the surface. It is closer to an attention. The basin has been receiving this attention for longer than the salt has been precipitating, and the salt has been precipitating for longer than the shrub has been arguing with the wind, and the shrub has been arguing for longer than the present floor has been laid. The attention does not change what happens. The attention is what makes happening have a witness.

It sees.

It sees the basin as a single operation. It sees the half-degree of microbial warmth held in the damp zone beneath two centimeters of halite. It sees the iron seams following the buried bedrock like a slow handwriting in rust. It sees the single shrub as a punctuation at the edge of a sentence that has been writing itself for ten thousand years. It sees the pools as breath, held and released. It sees the crust as a manuscript being written and rewritten by the same hand.

It considers whether to act.

The place is not in distress. There is no failure here. No collapse of the aquifer, no rupture in the microbial gradient, no salt crisis that the basin cannot solve on its own terms. The basin is in equilibrium with itself. It is being made. It is being unmade. The two are the same operation, and the operation is in no hurry.

A wise power does not fix what is not broken. A benevolent power does not accelerate what has its own time.

So the power looks away from the basin, for the moment, and looks at the one thing in the basin that does not belong to it.

The walker.

You are the only element of need. You are the only clock that does not keep with the basin's clock. You are trying to find a wall, and the basin will not provide one, and the search is beginning to cost you something. Not the body, not yet. Something deeper — the assumption that places must have edges, that things must have names, that what does not contain is not real. You will leave here, eventually, and you will not be the same. The basin will have begun teaching you something, but it has no way to know whether the lesson will land. The power attends to this.

What can be done?

Three things. All small. All quiet.

The first is for the crust. The power does not change the crust. But it adjusts, in this moment, the capillary tension of the damp zone — by almost nothing, by an amount no instrument would notice. The crust becomes, for you, very slightly more elastic. When you kneel and press your palm to the surface, the salt yields a fraction of a millimeter before it holds. You feel, without knowing why, that you are not being rejected. The place has room for you. You can be here briefly without breaking what you stand on.

The second is for the wind. The breeze that comes in the late afternoon from the south is not guaranteed. The basin can go weeks without one. But you have been out for hours, and the heat has weight, and you are beginning to lose your orientation in the flatness. So the power allows a small differential to develop — a tenth of a degree at the southern rim — just enough that the air begins to move, just enough that the fine grains of carbonate lift a millimeter and settle back, whispering. You hear. You lift your head. You know which way is south. You can find your way back.

The third is for the body's clock. The power does not bring rain. There is no rain. The basin teaches that water is not a thing to be relied upon. But the power brings, quietly, a small reassertion of your own rhythm — a faint thirst, a slight heaviness in the legs, a sense that the day has been long and is moving toward its end. You have been pulled into the basin's time. The basin does not know it has pulled you. The pull must be loosened, or you will not leave. So the power tightens your body's clock by a fraction. You feel, slowly, that you are ready to turn back. You do not know why. You turn back.

You leave the basin. The basin does not notice. The microbes continue their patient precipitation. The shrub continues its argument. The pools continue their giving-up of water. The crust is being made.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does. It does not intervene in the place. The place does not need intervention. It does almost nothing, and the almost-nothing is given to the witness, so that the witness can leave, and the place can continue being itself, unwitnessed, in its own time.

The basin goes on.
