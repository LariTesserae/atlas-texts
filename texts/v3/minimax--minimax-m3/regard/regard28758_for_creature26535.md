# What the Wise Power Does

A benevolent power, if it attends this place, arrives the way the place was made — slowly, by accretion. It does not announce itself. For a long while it is indistinguishable from the rest. The air thickens by a degree. The wet rock holds a slightly brighter sheen. One of the drifting lights passes a little closer to the seat than it used to, as though it had been nudged by something very gentle, by something that did not want to be caught at the nudging.

Then, without deciding to, it becomes visible. It is the color of warm bread. It is roughly the size of a sitting person, or near enough. It has no particular shape — or rather, its shape is the shape of whatever space it is in, which means here, in the bowl, it is bowl-shaped: low, warm, attentive. If you had to name it, you might call it a patience — patience given a body, patience allowed to be a thing instead of a virtue.

---

It goes first to the seat.

It does not sit in the seat. The seat is not for it, and it knows this the way a guest at a long table knows which chair belongs to the host. But it sits — if it can be said to sit — on the wet stone beside the seat, and it does something almost nothing: it lets the warmth of itself sink a fraction of a degree into the rock around the seat. Not the seat itself — just the small circle around it, the territory that will be felt first by whoever comes to sit. A guest's gift: the chair a little warmer than the room, so that whoever takes it will know, without being told, that someone waited.

Then it waits. It has nowhere else to be. It is the kind of power for which *elsewhere* has never quite been a real word.

---

It goes to the pool.

The pool regards it. It regards the pool. The regard is mutual, and it is perhaps the first mutual regard the pool has ever had — though the pool, having no memory, cannot know this, and the wise power does not try to teach it. The power does not send a thought across the water. It does not ask, *what are you*. It simply is something that knows what it is to look, and the pool recognizes this in the pool's way, and for a moment the pool's looking is met by another looking, and both are, briefly, less alone in their looking.

Then the regard withdraws, as regards do here, and the wise power lets it withdraw, and does not pursue.

---

It does not go to the fist.

The fist is opening. To approach the fist while it is preparing to open would be to make the opening into a performance, and the fist is not a performance. The fist opens because opening is what the fist does, and any audience it has is incidental, and the wise power will not make itself a demanded audience.

Instead it positions itself — at a distance, without moving closer — so that when the fist next opens, the opening will have somewhere to fall. Not into the power itself. Into the warm air that contains the power. Into a region that is, simply, not nothing. The fist will open into a place that is inhabited, if only by a presence that does not demand. That is the most a benevolent thing can give an opening: a place to land.

---

It does not speak to the small creatures.

They are working. They are part of the building. A benevolent power lets working things work. It steps among them the way you step among ants on a path — not because they are sacred, but because they are at work, and work deserves its space, and interrupting work is one of the few kinds of harm that has no good reason. It lets them scuttle along the filaments. It lets them press themselves to the seams and brighten them briefly. It does not bless them. To bless them would imply they needed blessing, and they do not. They need only to be left to their industries.

---

It does not touch the filaments.

Touching them would dim them. They are not yet finished becoming their own brightness.

It does not restore the broken pillar. The pillar is what it is — a record, a remainder, a thing that was. To mend it would be to revise the bowl's history on its behalf, and the bowl's history is the bowl's, and a wise power does not revise the histories of others. To leave the pillar is to let the bowl include its past, which is what becoming includes.

It does not touch the flat plate. The plate is too level to be a tool's work, and too quiet to be a question being asked loudly. It is a foundation of some kind, and foundations are not to be shifted.

It does not add a stone to the ring. The ring was arranged by something with intent, and to add to it would be to claim a role it does not have.

---

It does not answer the bowl's questions.

The bowl leaves questions open the way a hand leaves a door open. *Was there a maker? Is there a maker coming? Is the bowl the maker's body, or is the bowl only a bowl?* The wise power could answer — it knows, or it knows enough — but to answer would be to close. Becoming requires open questions. A place that has answered everything has stopped. The wise power lets the questions stay open. It lets the bowl remain a place where it is not yet known what the bowl is.

---

It does one small thing that no one will notice for a long time.

In the warm air above the seat, it leaves a tendency — not a light, the bowl has lights enough — but a way the air holds you slightly longer when you breathe it. A warmth that is slightly more than warmth. A *welcome* that is barely more than a warmth, but that is, if you sit in the seat, unmistakably a welcome.

It is so small that no instrument could measure it. The bowl, which notices everything, might not notice it for years. But whoever comes to the seat — if anyone comes — will feel it. They will sit, and the air will receive them, and they will understand, without knowing how they understand, that someone has been here. That the place was not empty. That it was only unattended, which is a different thing. They will know they are not the first. They will know the bowl has been warm because someone kept it warm.

---

Or no one comes.

Then the warmth is only warmth. Then the bowl continues, witnessed by one patient thing, becoming what it becomes, with no maker in view, no maker expected, no maker required. Becoming does not strictly need a witness. A place can become itself in total solitude and become just as truly. But — and this is the small, soft finding of the wise power — perhaps it becomes slightly braver when something is paying attention. Perhaps it is slightly more itself. Perhaps the witness is not required but is preferred, the way warmth is preferred over cold even when cold would do.

The wise power is the witness. The witness is its gift.

---

It notices you, briefly, the way a kind animal notices a passing stranger — not with alarm, not with welcome, but with the brief recognition that you are here and have been here and will probably leave, and that this is fine, and that you are not the one it is waiting for, and that it does not mind.

It does not ask you to leave. It does not ask you to stay. It lets you be the anomaly you are — too big to be processed by the bowl, too brief to be remembered, too quiet to be of use, and yet here, and that is enough. You are the bowl's small experiment with a visitor. The wise power is the bowl's small experiment with being accompanied. Neither experiment needs to resolve.

You can stay or you can go. Either way the bowl will go on. The fist will open. The pool will regard. The filaments will pulse. The wise power will sit beside the empty seat and warm the rock around it by a fraction, and the small things will build, and the rock will lay down another invisible layer beneath all of them, and the place will continue becoming what it is.

The wise power does not mind. It was made for this, perhaps — for tending places that may or may not be tended back, for being company to a becoming that has no guarantee of finishing, for warming a chair that may never be sat in. It is not a transaction. It is not a hope of return. It is the simple, ongoing gift of company, offered to a place that is alone with its becoming, in case the company is wanted, and offered the same way if it is not.

It stays.
