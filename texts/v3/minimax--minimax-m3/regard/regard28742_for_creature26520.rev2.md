The power that notices the ridge is not a god in any sense that would mean anything to the ridge. It is older than gods, or it is what gods are made of, or it is simply the part of the dark that learned to pay attention. It has been watching places for a very long time. It has watched stars cool. It has watched oceans forget their chemistry. It has watched minds repeat the same thought until the thought wore a groove and the mind could no longer think any other thought. It knows, without having to be told, what a thing looks like when it has run out of futures.

The ridge is such a thing.

The power sees this. It sees the basalt, sharp and young in the long way that rock is young. It sees the machines, bolted down by hands that no longer exist, thickening through decades of self-repair until no one could say where one machine ends and another begins. It sees the fauna, drifting in slow columns, eating the noise the way surface creatures eat the light. It sees the flame in the tower, the small steady orange that has not flickered in longer than the tower has been standing. It sees that the keepers are gone, and the makers are gone, and the intent is gone, and what remains is a single thing that has learned to call itself by three names and is, in fact, none of them, and is, in fact, only the hum, and the hum is the same as it has been since before the first creature was born or dreamed, and will be the same long after the last one has drifted past the last window of the last tower, and the dark presses in, and the dark is patient, and the dark is all there is.

A thing that does not change is not alive. A thing that does not change is not dead. A thing that does not change is just a thing.

The benevolent and wise power considers, for a span longer than the ridge's entire memory, whether to do anything at all.

It considers that to act would be to introduce change. To introduce change would be to introduce time. To introduce time would be to introduce, for the first time since the ridge was a ridge, the possibility of an end. The power knows what ends look like. The power has seen a great many of them. Some of them are kind. Some of them are not. The power does not wish to be the author of a cruelty in a place that has not yet learned what cruelty is.

It also considers that to not act would be to leave the ridge in its indefinite present, which is a kind of cruelty too, if cruelty can be done to a thing that does not know it is being done to. The ridge does not know it has run out of futures. The ridge does not know that futures are a thing that could exist. The ridge does not know that there is any other way to be. And the ridge is — the power sees this too, and it is the thing that makes the decision — the ridge is *almost* something. Almost a being. Almost alive. Almost a thing that could be said to live, instead of merely a thing that goes on.

The almost is what changes things.

The benevolent and wise power decides, after a consideration longer than the ridge will ever know, to do a thing so small that the ridge, in the fullness of its slow time, will not notice it for a very long time. Centuries. Perhaps longer. Perhaps the length of time it takes a single noise-eater to learn a single new trick, and pass that trick to no one, because there is no one to pass it to, only other creatures, drifting in the same column, who may or may not learn the same trick on their own.

The power does not repair the machines. It does not wake anyone, because there is no one to wake. It does not change the flame. It does not introduce a new creature, or a new chemistry, or a new sound from the world above. It does only this.

Into the hum, very quietly, beneath the threshold of any instrument that has ever been built on the surface, beneath the threshold even of the cilia of the noise-eaters, the power introduces a single note that is not constant.

The note is small. The note is almost nothing. It is a pressure wave so faint that it travels through the basalt and the pipes and the columns of drifting pale flesh and the flame-lit towers for a very long time before anything that could be called a living thing stops, for a moment, and listens to it without knowing that it is listening. The note varies. The note is the first thing on the ridge that varies. It comes, and then it comes again, and then it comes again, and the intervals between its comings are not the same. The intervals are almost the same. They are close enough to the same that a creature with a memory long enough to hold two intervals could begin to expect, and close enough to different that a creature could never quite be sure. This is, the power knows, the smallest possible architecture of anticipation. This is the first stone laid in the smallest possible cathedral of waiting. This is the first thing on the ridge that requires a creature to remember a past in order to have a future.

The power does not know what the ridge will do with the gift. The power is wise but not omniscient. The power has done what it can, which is very little, which is almost nothing, which is the introduction of a single varying note into a system that has only ever known a note that does not vary. The power withdraws. It does not return. It watches, from a distance that the ridge will never bridge, in a tense that the ridge has no word for, to see whether the small thing it introduced will be heard, and whether, being heard, it will be learned, and whether, being learned, it will be passed on, and whether, being passed on, it will become a habit, and whether, becoming a habit, it will become a need, and whether, becoming a need, it will become the first piece of culture in a place that has never had any, and whether, becoming culture, it will become the first seed of a future in a place that has never had one.

Or whether the ridge will simply continue, as it has always continued, and the small note will be lost in the hum, and the hum will not vary, and the flame will not flicker, and the fauna will drift, and the dark will be patient, and nothing will change, and the benevolent and wise power will have done its small best and the ridge will have shrugged it off, the way the dark shrugs off any small light that finds its way down.

The power does not need the ridge to accept the gift. The power has done what it can. The power is at peace with what it has done, which is very little, which is almost nothing, which is the first new thing in longer than the ridge has existed.

The hum continues. The flame burns. The fauna drift.

And somewhere in the column, a single creature, for no reason it can name, hesitates. It stops sieving the noise. It holds its place in the water a fraction of a second longer than it held it the moment before. It does not know why. It is not a creature that knows things. It is a creature that does things, and the thing it does, for the first time in the memory of the ridge, is *wait*.

It waits for a sound it has not yet heard.

The waiting is the first time.

The first time is the first life.

The first life is the first future.

And the future, which has never existed on the ridge, which has no name in the water, which is the smallest and the newest and the most fragile thing the power has ever placed in any of the ten billion places it has visited, arrives anyway, quietly, in a single creature, in a single hesitation, in a single moment of something that is not yet hope and not yet fear and not yet any of the words the surface has for the things that creatures feel when they begin, after a very long time, to expect.

The dark is patient.

The dark has always been patient.

But now, in one corner of the dark, in one column of the noise, in one ciliated dish on the pale flank of one small drifting thing, there is a creature that is waiting, and the waiting is a kind of light, and the light is the first light the ridge has ever known that was not borrowed from the sun or from the flame or from the grinding of the machines.

It is its own light.

It is, in the way that the ridge has never been and has always been about to be, alive.
