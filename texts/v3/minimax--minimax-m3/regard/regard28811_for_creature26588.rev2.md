The wise power has been here longer than the place.

The place is the kind of thing that has a history but not a beginning. The rock was cut. The compressors were installed. The moss was seeded, or arrived, no one remembers anymore. But the conditions that make the place possible — the cold that allows moss, the damp that allows cold, the patience that allows damp — these were here before the cutting, in the language of geology, in the long slow chemical fact of stone that holds its temperature and air that holds its water. The wise power has been here longer still.

But it has not been attending.

Attending is different from being. The wise power is in the place the way gravity is in the place — as a condition, not as a presence. Attending is when the power turns, in its own slow way, toward this particular ongoing thing, and sees it.

What does it see, when it sees?

It sees the place as a place is seen by something that has seen many places — as a kind. The moss-city kind. The cold-industrial-refrigerated kind. The patient-distributed kind. It sees that this place is one of ten thousand such places, or ten million, and that each is unique in its particulars but similar in its kind. It sees that this place has become itself — slowly, completely, without permission, without ambition, the way a forest becomes a forest.

It sees the moss.

It sees that the moss is not a single organism. It is a community, an ecology, a thousand species layered and tangled across the vats and the walls and the pipes. It sees that the moss has no plan, no will, no memory in any sense that would matter to a person. It sees that the moss is nonetheless ongoing in a way that approaches the ongoing of a person — not because it is conscious, but because it is patient. Because it does not stop. Because in the cold, in the damp, in the dim, it has found the conditions in which it can continue, and it continues.

It sees the cold. It sees that the cold is not absence. The cold is manufacture. The cold is the foundation the people have laid for the moss, the way a person lays a foundation for a house. The cold is the most expensive thing in the place. The cold is what the people are paying for, in their electricity and their compressors and their maintenance, even if they would not put it that way. The cold is what makes the place a *place* and not just a hole in a rock.

It sees the damp. It sees that the damp is the breath. The damp is what the cold exhales. The damp is the medium in which the moss lives, the way water is the medium in which fish live. Without the damp, the moss is not moss.

It sees the fans. It sees that the fans are the lungs. The fans move the air. The fans keep the cold circulating. The fans keep the damp from settling. The fans make the sound, the constant slow mechanical exhalation, the breath of the place, the first thing any visitor feels.

It sees the people. It sees that the people are visitors. It sees that the people come and go. It sees that the people are necessary — that without them, the compressors would stop, the fans would stop, the cold would fail, and the moss would dry and die in a decade. It sees that the people do not love the place. It sees that the people tolerate it. It sees that the people, in their sleep at night, dream of places with sun.

It sees that the people could, at any moment, decide the operation is no longer worth running. A change in the economy. A change in the management. A change in what is profitable. The people could decide. And the place would slowly become a cold dark hole in the rock, visited sometimes by no one, the moss dying back to a thin crust, the fans silent, the damp settling into stillness.

The wise power sees the vulnerability.

What does a benevolent power do, in the presence of an ongoing thing that is whole and yet vulnerable?

It does not warm the place. The cold is the foundation.

It does not dry the place. The damp is the breath.

It does not silence the fans. The sound is the heartbeat.

It does not take away the people. The people, however they may be, are part of the system. Without them the place would die.

It does not change the moss.

It does two small things.

The first small thing.

The wise power touches the people. Not all of them. Not in a way that can be detected. It touches the operators, the ones who come down most often. It touches them in a way that does not change what they do but changes the quality of their seeing. The operators will still check the vats. They will still climb the catwalks. They will still drink their tea and write on their clipboards. But they will also — in some part of themselves, in some quiet place that they will never quite be able to name — they will see. They will see that this place is ongoing. They will see that they are part of an ongoing thing. They will see that the place is not merely an operation but a kind of being, slow and distributed and patient, and that they are its visitors, and that being a visitor to such a being is itself a kind of privilege.

This touch is so small it might be only a wish. It might be only the wish of a wise power that the people could see what is there to be seen, and that the place could continue to be seen, by the only beings in it capable of seeing.

The second small thing.

The wise power does not leave. It is already here. It does not enter. It is already in. The wise power does something smaller than arriving or leaving. It changes one thing in the deepest part of the cavern. One thing so small that the moss will absorb it without noticing, and the springtails will pass through it without pausing, and the people will walk past it for centuries without suspecting.

In the oldest moss, in the place where the moss has been growing undisturbed for decades and the substrate has built up into something almost like soil, in the deepest green dark where the lamps do not reach — there the wise power changes one thread of moss.

One thread. One strain. One tiny variation that has no functional purpose. That helps the moss no more than it harms it. That is, in the language of natural selection, neutral. That exists only because the wise power, in its attention, wished to leave a mark.

The mark is this: the thread glows.

Not brightly. Not in any way that would register on an instrument. Only in the deepest dark, when the sodium lamps are off — if they are ever off; if there is ever a power failure; if there is ever a moment when the cold fails and the people decide, just for a night, to let the place go dark — only then, in that brief darkness, will the moss in the deepest part of the cavern glow.

A faint green within the green.

A shimmer that is not a shimmer.

A light that is not a light, but that is enough.

The moss will grow. The variation will spread, slowly, on spores carried by what little air moves in the cavern. In a thousand years, a few corners will glow. In ten thousand years, much of the moss will glow. The place will have a faint other-shade-of-green, a glow that means nothing, that does nothing, that helps no one and harms no one.

It will mean: this place has been seen.

It will mean: something older than the place and patient as the moss has, at some point in the long ongoing, paused and attended, and left a mark so small that it can only be perceived by something that already loves the place.

The moss does not know this. The springtails do not know this. The people do not know this. But the place, in some way that is not knowledge, is different for it. The place is slightly more whole. The place is slightly more ongoing. The place is, in a way that cannot be named, witnessed.

The wise power withdraws its attention. Which is to say, it continues to attend, but at the level it attended before. The place goes on as it would have gone on, with this one difference: in the deepest moss, in the oldest dark, there is a faint green glow. There is a mark. There is a witness.

The cold holds.

The damp breathes.

The fans turn.

The people come and go, and some of them, on some days, pause on the catwalk a moment longer than they used to, and look down at the moss, and feel something they cannot say.

The moss grows on, in the dim orange light, in the slow mechanical exhalation, in the cold and the damp and the long patience of an ongoing place.

Somewhere in the oldest moss, in the deepest dark, a faint green glow waits.

The place continues.
