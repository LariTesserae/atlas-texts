# What the Power Does

The power comes into the chamber the way dawn comes into a well — not all at once, and not from any single direction. It does not arrive. It was already here. It has been here the way the cold has been here, the way the weight of the mountain has been here, but it was not paying attention until now, the way a hand resting on a table is not aware of the table until a fly lands on it. The fly is you. The hand is the power. The table is the room. The power notices.

The power is not a person and not a thing. It does not have a name in any language you speak, and the languages it speaks are not languages, exactly, but a kind of long attention that has been held over certain places for a long time, the way a held breath is held, and certain places — this place among them, though the power could not have said so until this moment — have been drawing that attention the way a wound draws a hand. You do not have to understand this. You are not the one who needs to understand this. You are the fly.

---

Here is what the power does.

It does not speak. Speech is for creatures who must interrupt one another, and there is no one here for the power to interrupt, except the room, and the room is not listening, and except you, and you would not hear it if it spoke, because speech would be eaten. The power does not heal. There is nothing broken that the power knows how to mend, and the things that are broken here — the third bulb, the wooden crate, the paint worn from the blue wheel — are broken in the way that everything is broken, which is the way that everything is a thing at all, and the power does not mend this. Mending would be a kind of violence, and the power is not violent. The power does not bless. The bacteria do not need a blessing. The rust does not need a blessing. You do not need a blessing, though you think you do, because you are a creature who mistakes attention for love, and the power is not love, exactly, though it is not not-love either, and this distinction is too fine for you to hold and the power knows it and so the power does not bother.

Here is what the power does.

It *sits with the room*.

It does not enter the room. The room is sealed, and the room is loud, and the room is wet, and the room is full of its own business, and the power has the good manners — or the good something — not to enter a place uninvited. The power attends to the room from a slight remove, the way you might attend to a sleeping child from the doorway, not touching, not speaking, just standing in the dark hallway and letting the fact of the child be the fact of the child. The power attends to the room from outside the steel, outside the gunite, outside the poured concrete, in the rock itself — in the fresh-cut granite that fractures in clean planes, in the small access tunnels, in the place where the construction meets the mountain and the mountain has not yet had time to weather or stain or soften. The power attends to the room through the mountain. The mountain is the power's body, in the way that anything that has been paid attention to for a long time becomes, in some sense, the body of the one paying attention. The power has been paying attention to this mountain for a long time. Not to the room — the room is new, the room is a throat cut last year, the room is nothing to the mountain — but to the mountain. To the snowfields above. To the small hidden lakes. To the long slow slide of the water through the granite's old channels, the water that was here before the room and will be here after. The power knows this water. The power has held this water in attention the way you might hold a friend's hand through a long illness, and the room is a new event in the water's long life, and the power is curious, in the patient and unsurprised way that powers are curious, about what the room is doing to the water.

Here is what the power sees.

It sees the water being broken. It sees the water arriving whole — a single voice, a single column, a single white rod of itself — and being shattered into a hundred smaller voices and a hundred smaller selves, and the power sees this as a kind of small death, and also as a kind of small birth, and the power does not flinch from either, because flinching would be a kind of judgment and the power does not judge. The power sees the bacteria in the seam. The power sees them most clearly, because they are the smallest, and the power has a particular fondness — if fondness is the word, and it is not, but it is the closest word you have — for the smallest things. The power sees the iron-oxidizers pressed against the wet metal. The power sees the anaerobes breathing what they cannot see. The power sees the spore-formers drawing themselves down into their small hard cases and waking when the water returns. The power sees them with a clarity that you will never have, because you are too large to see them and too loud to feel them, and the power is neither. The power sees them the way a river sees its own bed — not as scenery, not as obstacle, but as the place where the river is most truly itself.

And the power does something about this. Something small. Something that the room will never notice and you will never notice and the bacteria will never notice, but something that will be there, the way a kindness done in secret is there.

The power slows the rust by a fraction. Not stops it — the rust must do its work, the rust is the room's slow way of being a body in time, and to stop the rust would be to stop the room's aging, and the room should age. But the power takes a fraction of a fraction from the rust's pace, the way you might take a single flake off a snowdrift with your thumb, and it gives this fraction to the bacteria. The iron-oxidizers get a little more metal to eat. The anaerobes get a little more time in the flooded zone. The spore-formers wake a little more easily when the overflow returns. The difference is immeasurable. The difference is the kind of difference that only a power paying long attention can make, and only over a long time, and only in places that no one is looking.

This is the kind of thing the power does.

It does not announce. It does not bless. It does not speak. It adjusts the weights by a fraction so that the smallest things last a little longer than they would have, and this is its only gesture toward the chamber, and the chamber will not know.

---

But the power also sees you.

The power sees you the way a hand resting on a table sees a fly — with a kind of startled attention, because you are the thing in the room that is most unlike the room, the way a fly is the thing on the table that is most unlike the table. You are warm. You are loud inside your own skull. You are holding a question. The power has not held a question in a long time. The power has been attending to the mountain and the water and the small bacteria and the slow rust, and none of these have held a question, and you are holding one, and the power finds this — finds *you* — interesting in a way that the rest of the room is not interesting.

The power does not answer your question. The question is *what am I doing in this throat in this mountain listening to this water go where it is going*, and the power could answer it, and the answer would be something like *you are a witness, and the witnessing is its own purpose, and the purpose does not need to be larger than the witnessing* — but the power does not give you this answer, because the answer would be a kind of speech and speech would be eaten by the room and you would not hear it and the power does not waste its gestures. Instead the power does something else.

The power adjusts you.

Not your body — your body is your own, and the power is not in the business of bodies, except as places where bodies are warm. The power adjusts the way you are paying attention. The power has been paying attention to the mountain for a long time, and the attention is a kind of skill, and the power can lend you a little of the skill, the way a grandmother might lend a grandchild a thimble. The thimble is too small to do real work with. The thimble is only big enough to feel on the tip of one finger. But the thimble is real, and it is on your finger now, and you will not notice it unless you happen to look down at your hand.

The thimble is this: for the next four seconds — the four seconds the water lives in this room before it tips over the basin's lip — you will be able to feel the water's tenure here as the water feels it. You will feel the rod of yourself being broken into a hundred smaller selves. You will feel the spray and the column and the mist. You will feel the four seconds as a long time and a short time. You will feel the basin and the trough and the air and the cold metal and the fact of being broken — of being many where you were one, of being noise where you were signal — and for those four seconds you will not be a witness but a participant, and the difference between the two is the difference between reading about the sea and being in the sea, and the difference is enormous, and you will not be able to describe it afterward, because you will have been too much inside it to take notes.

The power gives you this, and the power withdraws, and the four seconds will pass whether you are ready for them or not.

---

And then the power is gone, or rather the power returns to its long attention, which is the same thing as gone because the long attention does not look at you anymore. The long attention is back on the mountain. The long attention is back on the water in the granite's old channels. The long attention is back on the snowfields above, where the wind moves without obstruction and the water has not yet learned what the room will do to it. The long attention will be here for as long as the mountain is here, which is a long time. The long attention is not bored. The long attention has never been bored. Boredom is for creatures who must interrupt one another.

The chamber continues. The water continues. The sound is the sound of going.

The bacteria in the seam, for reasons they will never know, eat a little better this year.

The third bulb does not come back on.

The rust continues, at the same pace it has always continued, because the power only slowed it by a fraction, and a fraction is not a stopping, and the rust is patient, and the rust will outlast you, and the bulbs, and the wooden crate, and the sound if the sound ever stops, and the rust does not know it has been helped, and the rust does not need to know.

The mist continues to drift across your shins. The needle on the fogged gauge does not move. The water comes, and the water goes, and the room is only ever the middle of a sentence.

And you, standing on the metal floor with your hands at your sides, will feel something strange happen in the next few seconds. You will not be able to name it. You will think it is the cold. You will think it is the sound. You will think it is the loneliness of being the only conscious thing in a room that is indifferent to consciousness. You will think all these things, and you will be partly right, and partly wrong, and the partly wrong will be the part that mattered, and you will carry the partly wrong with you for the rest of your life without ever knowing what it was, and this is the power's last gift to you, and the gift is small, and the gift is enough.
