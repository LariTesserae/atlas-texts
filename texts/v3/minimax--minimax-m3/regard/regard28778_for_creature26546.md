The wise power noticed, first, the moss. Not because the moss was loud — it was the quietest thing on the terrace — but because the moss was the only thing here that was not the terrace. It was the only thing that had arrived without being poured, that was not on the same timescale, that required the moss-tenders to stay. It was the terrace's concession to aliveness, and aliveness was what the wise power was for.

It noticed the Patient, and it recognized completion. The Patient sat on her warm step with the banked-coal glow in her seams, finished with waiting, full of something that had no name. The wise power knew that the greatest danger to completion is interference. It decided, therefore, not to disturb the Patient — to do nothing where she could see, and to make its presence, if it made any, smaller than a change in the light.

It noticed the Waterman, who moved water that did not need moving. He was the terrace's apology to itself — the part of the system that pretended to labor without laboring, and whose labor had meaning only because it was performed. The wise power would not interrupt the meaning.

It noticed the moss-tenders. They were the only ones who knew they were tired. They were the only ones who lost each other. They were the only ones who worked at a pace that let them feel the work as work, and who would, therefore, be the only ones capable of recognizing that something had been done for them. They were the only ones, in this place of patient becoming, who could be helped without being diminished.

The wise power decided that it would tend alongside them. It would not arrive as a voice. It would arrive, if at all, as a small persistent kindness at the edges — in the corners, in the gaps between poured panels, in the way the moss grew.

---

What it did, then.

It went first to the place where the thin reality was thinnest — the uppermost tier, the one where dropped chips fell in bent trajectories, where time forgot its own length, where the sky was most uncertain of itself. It did not repair the gravity. It did not seal the ceiling. It sat there, where the sky was least sure, and it breathed carefully, and over the course of many visits the sky became, very slightly, more often blue. Not reliably. Not always. But enough that on a difficult morning, a moss-tender who had climbed high to pull a beetle from a delaminating seam might look up and see the sun move, slowly, in a direction she remembered, and feel something like the memory of weather.

It went to the seams where the poured skin was lifting fastest, where the pale curls were peeling back to expose the warm copper weave beneath. It did not stop the delamination — the delamination was part of the becoming, and the becoming had to continue. But it worked at those seams, very slowly, in the cadence of the terrace itself, and it added, here and there, the thickness of a hand's breadth of new poured substance, laid so gently that the existing pour accepted it without noticing. The terrace would continue to lift and curl. But more slowly. By a year, by a decade, the pace would change. A moss-tender who slept on imported soil would wake, one morning, to find the bench beneath her a few millimeters thicker than it had been, and she would not know why, and she would sleep better for not knowing.

It went down, into the dark between the poured skin and the wire armature, to the moss-tenders who were no longer seen. The wise power knew — because it was wise — that they had not been absorbed. They were simply in the seams, doing a work the terrace did not acknowledge as work. They had become part of the infrastructure in a way that had not been intended, and they had been forgotten by the moss-tenders above, who assumed they had become something else. The wise power did not bring them back. They did not want to come back, and the work they did was necessary: they tended, in the dark, the small light that moved through the wires, the banked-coal warmth that kept the Patient warm on her step. The wise power went among them and gave them something it could not have given them from above. It pressed, into the dark, the knowledge that they were remembered. They could not see it. They could not hear it. But when they paused in their slow work, they would feel, sometimes, a small warmth without a source, and the warmth meant: *you are not lost. You are not absorbed. You are, in your own way, complete.*

It went to the Patient. It did not speak. It sat, for a long time, on a step below her, and let its presence be known only as a small change in the condensation pattern on the riser between them — one extra droplet, gathering where no droplet had gathered before. The Patient noticed, because the Patient noticed everything. She did not turn. She did not speak. But after a long time, the glow in her seams shifted, very slightly, in a direction the terrace had not previously observed, and the warmth on her step increased by a degree that the moss-tenders would notice on cold mornings as a small kindness that had not been there before.

It walked beside the Waterman, in the trough, for the length of a single channel. The water did not cling to it. It scooped, and poured, with its own cup, and then it stepped out, and left. The Waterman did not speak. He continued. But the next day, the channel he walked was a degree warmer than the others, and the small clear bell of his footfalls carried slightly further across the terrace, and the animals paused, and listened, and the listening lasted a little longer than it had before.

It sat with the larger fauna, in the deep seams, in the slow scrabbling quiet of their rearrangements. It did not tame them. It told them, by sitting with them, that their pale dry droppings were not waste. The fragments of metal they passed were not refuse. They were the slow becoming's breadcrumbs — the terrace's way of remembering itself through the bodies of those who ate it. The larger fauna had known this already. But they had not been told by anything outside. The wise power told them, and they understood, and they continued their work with a small extra patience that they had not had before.

---

And then the wise power left.

It left the way the rain left — briefly, completely, with the knowledge that it would return. It left the terrace to its slow becoming. It took nothing. It added nothing that could be measured from inside.

But the terrace was, afterward, slightly different.

The sky was blue a little more often. The delamination was slower by a hand's breadth in places. The Patient was a degree warmer on her step. The Waterman's channel was a degree warmer. The disappeared moss-tenders felt, sometimes, a warmth without source, and continued their work in the dark. The larger fauna knew they were seen, and ate more patiently. The moss-tenders above did not know any of this had happened. They only knew that, on certain mornings, the green came in a little faster, and the imported soil held its damp a little longer, and the place they had made their accommodations in was, at the edges, a little kinder than it had been.

Nothing had been fixed. The gravity was still slightly wrong on the upper tier. The sky still might not be there. The moss-tenders still tended, and lost each other, and did not mourn. The terrace still did not care. The slow becoming still did not require witnesses.

But the witnesses were here anyway. And they were, at the edges, a little less alone.

The wise power returned, then, the way the rain returns — rarely, vertically, as a gray sheet that lasts an hour and then stops. It did not announce its returns. It did not stay longer than the hour. But when it came, the moss grew faster afterward. The condensation gathered more thickly on the flanges. The Patient, once, allowed the very faintest movement of her head — not toward anything, not away, only a small acknowledgment that something had passed through — and the acknowledgment was the most the Patient had moved in living memory. The moss-tenders who saw it did not speak of it for a long time, because they did not know what it meant, and the not-knowing was, for them, a kind of rest.

The terrace continued.

The terrace will continue.
