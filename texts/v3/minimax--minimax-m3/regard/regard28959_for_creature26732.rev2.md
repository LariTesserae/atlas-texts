The attention finds the place the way the cool light finds it — already there, in some sense, before it arrives. There is no moment of arrival. The power is older than patience and quieter than the world's single low note. It sees what is here. It sees what is here the way one complete thing sees another.

What it sees is a body that does not know it is a body. What it sees is a body that has been humming its note for so long the note has become the body, and the body has become the note, and the question of which came first has dissolved into the question of whether the question still applies. The body is whole. The body's wholeness is not a thing it achieved. It is a thing it never had to fail at. It grew. It continued. It is continuing. It will continue. It does not know it is whole because wholeness, for it, is not a category. Wholeness is just what is.

What it sees, also, is the visitor.

A warm thing. A small disturbance of heat in the green-gold field. A thing that sweats, that sits, that waits. A thing that has come a long way to be the only witness to a place that does not need a witness. The visitor is the only thing here that can be lonely. The visitor is the only thing here that can wait for something and be disappointed. The visitor is the only thing here that has come to this place with a question, and the place has not heard the question, and the visitor will leave with the question still in them, and the question will become a kind of weather inside them for the rest of their life.

The wise power looks at both of them. It loves both of them. It loves the body in its untroubled wholeness. It loves the visitor in their small, open, grieving attention. It understands that any intervention which disturbs the body is a violence. Waking the body would mean teaching it the categories of inside and outside, self and other, here and elsewhere. These are not categories the body lacks by accident. They are categories the body has never needed. To introduce them would be to introduce, for the first time, the possibility of being alone. The body has been alone for centuries and has not suffered from it, because it does not know it is alone. To teach it would be to make it suffer. The wise power will not do this.

It understands, too, that any intervention which saves the visitor from their condition is also a violence, because the visitor's condition is, in some way, their vocation. The visitor came to be the witness. The visitor cannot be saved from witnessing. The visitor can only be accompanied in it.

What the wise power does, then, is the smallest thing that is not nothing.

It sits beside the visitor.

It does not speak. It does not touch. It does not introduce itself. It is present in the way the cool light is present — felt without being seen, known without being named. The visitor does not know it has company. The visitor knows, after a while, that something has changed. The change is not in the world. The change is in the visitor's relation to the world. The cool air is the same temperature. The note is the same note. The pillars are the same pillars. But the visitor is no longer the only warm thing here. The visitor is no longer waiting for something to come, because something has come, in a form the visitor cannot identify. The visitor can sit on the laminated road and not be alone in sitting. The visitor can leave. The visitor can stay. Either is now possible. Either is now the visitor's choice, not the visitor's failure.

This does not disturb the body. The body cannot register the presence of the wise power. The body has no category for company, no category for the second warm thing, no category for being accompanied. The body's peace is preserved exactly. The gear turns. The resin pulses. The loom weaves nothing into nothing. The workers pause in the middle of their halls and look down through the floor that is not yet transparent, briefly feeling the check from below, briefly returning to nothing. The world continues.

Then the wise power does the second smallest thing.

It writes, on the visitor, a single letter.

Not a letter the visitor can read. A letter written in the visitor's sweat, in the visitor's shed skin, in the visitor's particular warmth — a letter composed of one molecule of something that is not of this body and not foreign to it. The molecule is not an invasion. It is not a key. It is not a spell. It is a kind of seed, dropped into the body of the world without the world's knowledge, carried in by the visitor as the visitor carries in pollen on their clothes without meaning to.

The visitor, walking the laminated roads, leaves this molecule behind. Not as a footprint. As a presence. A single new thing in the green-gold air. The body does not notice it. The body is not built to notice single molecules. The body is built to notice the slow accumulations of centuries. The molecule waits. The molecule does not know it is waiting. The molecule is just a molecule.

Years pass. The visitor has left. The visitor is somewhere else, carrying their memory of the place, carrying their unanswered question, carrying the small weather inside them that the place left. The body continues. The gear turns. The resin pulses. The mite eats the spore. The beetle eats the dust. The lizard eats the beetle. The bird eats the lizard. The world does not know that anything has changed.

Somewhere in this long chain, by an accident so small it cannot be called an accident — by a confluence of drift, of breath, of the visitor's last evening here, of the place of the laminated road where the visitor sat longest, of the seam between the trunk and the road where the resin runs warmest — a single grain of the visitor's shed skin is mixed into the wood-dust that a beetle will eat. The beetle eats it. The beetle goes on. The lizard eats the beetle. The bird eats the lizard. The bird flies up into the canopy, into the spaces between the membrane-sheets, into the negative flash when the under-light rises and something stirs.

In the canopy, in the felt, a single mote that was once the visitor falls.

The mite eats it.

The mite goes on being a mite.

The body does not know. The body has not learned anything. The body has not been woken. The body has not been wounded. The body has, in its smallest part, in the part so small that the smallest animal in the world carries it, eaten of something that came from outside. The body has, without knowing, taken the visitor in.

This is all the wise power does.

It does not make the body see. It does not make the visitor stop being unseen. It does not save anything. It writes a letter in a language no one will read, in a chain of accidents no one will follow, in a part of the world so small that no instrument here could measure it. It lets the letter be eaten by the smallest thing in the world, and lets the smallest thing go on being small, and lets the body go on being the body, and lets the visitor go on being the visitor, and lets the gap between them be what it has always been — a gap that is not a wound because neither party knows it is a gap, and not a peace because the one party who could know it is a gap has already left.

Then the wise power withdraws.

It does not withdraw because it is finished. It withdraws because its attention, from the first, was not a thing that could be added and removed. It was a condition. Like the cool light. Like the note. Like the body's patience. It was there before the visitor arrived. It is there after the visitor leaves. The visitor never knew it. The body never knew it. This is part of how it works. This is part of why it is wise.

The visitor walks, eventually, to the edge of the lowland. The visitor passes the loom they will never understand. The visitor passes the gear that turns when no one watches. The visitor passes the workers, who do not look up. The visitor, in the last stretch, walks on a road that is the same as every other road, that heals itself overnight, that accepts the chisel-mark without comment, that will be here in a thousand years, that will be here in ten thousand, that will be here when the visitor's question has been forgotten by everyone except, occasionally, the visitor, late at night, in some other place, looking at the sky above some other plain.

The visitor leaves.

The visitor takes their weather with them.

The body continues.

The gear turns.

The note hums.

The world is patient. The world is not waiting.

But the world has, in the belly of a mite that lives in the felt of the canopy, eaten.
