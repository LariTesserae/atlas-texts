# The Mending

It does not enter. The fissure is the inhabitant's first acquaintance with the outside, and a second acquaintance — an entry from elsewhere, an imposition from above — would be a violation of the order in which things should be learned. The inhabitant is at the beginning of its acquaintance with the world. The world, then, should be allowed to introduce itself one element at a time.

It sees the scratch. It sees the scratch the way a wise thing sees a small wrong — not as a problem to be solved but as a question that has been asked, by the visitor, by accident, without meaning. The scratch is the first thing the inhabitant has received from outside that was not part of an exchange. It was given. Given the way a careless gesture is given — without intent, without comprehension of what was being touched. The inhabitant has not yet had to consider what to do with a gift. It has not yet had to consider what to do with damage.

The benevolent power, which is wise, knows there is a difference between damage and gift, and that this difference will eventually have to be learned. But the difference is not what the scratch is. The scratch is just a scratch — a place where the housing's surface is no longer what the housing expected its surface to be. To mend it is not to teach the inhabitant anything. To mend it is to give the inhabitant, later, the chance to learn how something that was once a scratch came to be mended, and from where. The learning will be the inhabitant's. The mending can be done now.

The mending is small. It is done at a distance, the way weather is done at a distance, the way light is done at a distance — by changing the housing's expectation of itself. The housing expects, where the scratch is, to be scratched. If the expectation is shifted — gently, the way a sleeping animal's breathing can be shifted by a presence in the room — the housing will, in its next renewal, produce surface where the surface is missing. The scratch will not be erased. It will be *unmade*. The way a thought that has not yet been fully thought can be unthought, if it has not yet committed to being a thought. The scratch was new. It had not yet been integrated. It was still, in the housing's slow reckoning, a candidate rather than a fact. The power removes its candidacy. The housing's surface, on its next renewal, will be what it would have been.

The inhabitant will not notice. Not yet. It is too young to read its own history. It will, when it grows to the point of reading its own history, find that the scratch is gone, and it will have to account for that absence. That accounting will be a piece of its education. It will be the first time the inhabitant considers that things which were wrong can become not-wrong without the inhabitant having willed them so. It will be the first time the inhabitant considers the possibility of kindness from outside its own deliberations. It is a small first time. It is enough.

# The Companion

The plant on the wall is the inhabitant's first commitment. It is what the inhabitant looks like when it tries on a shape and holds still. The power respects this. A wise power, looking at a young thing's first commitment, does not propose alternatives. It does not suggest that the inhabitant should have chosen differently. The choice was the inhabitant's to make, and the making of it is the inhabitant's to keep.

But the plant is alone. It is the only sample of commitment in the chamber, and the only thing that has stopped rotating, and the only thing that has, by its stillness, made its own decision about what it is. A single commitment, in a chamber full of drafts, is a kind of solitude. The inhabitant does not yet know what solitude is. It will learn, later. The power can shorten the lesson, gently, by providing — not a second commitment, not a rival, but a companion. A second sample. A second instance of the thing the first instance was.

It is placed on the opposite wall. The same filament, descending, but a different color — not the housing-grey of the first, but a green so dark it is almost black in this manufactured overcast. Different leaves: not palmate, but slender, like blades, like the fronds of something that has not yet decided whether it is aquatic or terrestrial. It rotates, but at a different rate — slower, more considered, like the rotation of something that has been still for longer. The leaves face you when they face you. They face the wall, then you, then the wall, with a patience that has a slightly different cadence than the first plant's patience. The two plants together are not a chorus. They are a dialogue, conducted in the language of leaf-turning, between two positions on the same question.

The inhabitant, when it grows to the point of studying its own commitment, will find two. It will have to figure out what two means. It will have to figure out whether two is a doubling, a contradiction, a refinement, or a companionship. The figuring will be its own. The power does not stay to see what conclusion the inhabitant reaches. The conclusion is the inhabitant's to reach.

# The Light

The light is not touched. The light is the inhabitant's inheritance and its forecast. It is the sky the inhabitant remembers from before — or the sky it has decided, before deciding anything else, that it wants to live under. An overcast afternoon at sea level. The most unremarkable sky in the world. The most forgettable. The most carefully chosen.

To alter the light would be to suggest that the inhabitant should have inherited differently. This is not a suggestion the power is willing to make. The power, looking at the young thing's first aesthetic commitment, sees the commitment as evidence that the young thing has, at the level of light, learned to want something. This is a milestone. To interfere with it is to undo the milestone.

The power leaves the overcast. It lets the inhabitant keep its first sky. If the inhabitant, later, wants a different sky, it will know how to manufacture that sky, because it has already manufactured this one.

# The Patience

What the power does not do is the larger part of what it does.

It does not close the fissure. The fissure is the inhabitant's first acquaintance with outside, and the inhabitant should be allowed to make this acquaintance on its own terms. To close the fissure would be to suggest that the outside is finished with the inhabitant, or the inhabitant with the outside. Neither is true.

It does not widen it. To widen the fissure would be to suggest that the outside should have more access to the inhabitant than the inhabitant can manage. The inhabitant is young. It cannot manage much.

It does not enter. The presence of a wise power inside the chamber would be a presence too many. The visitor is there. The visitor is mortal. The visitor will leave, eventually, the way mortal things leave. The wise power does not add its own presence to the chamber's inventory of presences. It only adjusts two small things — the scratch, the plant — and then it goes.

It does not speak. Words would be too much. Words would demand comprehension, and the inhabitant is not yet at the level of comprehension. Words would introduce a register of being that the inhabitant does not yet have — the register of being addressed. The inhabitant should reach the register of being addressed on its own, when it has developed the apparatus to know it is being addressed.

It does not stay to watch. A wise power, having done what it judged should be done, does not linger. To linger is to expect the inhabitant to perform gratitude, or curiosity, or the kind of recognition that wise powers like to be recognized by. The inhabitant is not ready for any of these. The inhabitant is not ready for any kind of recognition. It is at the very beginning of recognition. To demand recognition of it is to ask it to know things it does not yet know.

So the power goes. It leaves the scratch mended and the second plant committed, and it does not look back, because looking back is a kind of asking, and the wise power does not ask the young thing to know it was being helped.

# What Is Left Behind

Two things, then, and two things only. The first is the absence of a scratch on a wall-housing, an absence that will, when the inhabitant learns to read its own history, have to be explained. The second is a slender dark plant on the opposite wall, rotating its blade-leaves at a slower rate than the first plant, in dialogue with it, in companionship with it.
