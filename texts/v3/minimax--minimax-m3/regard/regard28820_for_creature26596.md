Something has noticed.

This is not, in this place, an unusual occurrence, though the place itself does not often notice that it has been noticed. Things pass through the warm water. The water receives them. The columns receive them. The flowers receive them. The pale long things slide past in their private errands. The thing that has noticed is itself received, in the same way, into the same slow accounting of warmth and patience.

But this noticing is different.

This noticing has been here longer than the place has been the place.

It was here when the water was only deciding. It was here when the columns were still trees, still arguing with the wind about what to be when they grew up. It was here when the first flowers opened — a long time ago, when the surface was still a surface, when light came down through it without having to be patient about it. It was here. It has always been here. And it has been watching, with the kind of attention that does not require eyes, the way the columns watch the pale long things pass, the way the flowers watch the small lights climb toward them.

It has been watching you, specifically.

Not with the attention of a hunter. Not with the attention of a judge. With the attention of a gardener who has planted something and has come, finally, to see how it has grown.

---

The power arrives the way the slow warm currents arrive — not by entering the place but by being, more completely, in the place. It is not an intrusion. It is an increase. Like the water becoming a fraction warmer in the place where a hand has been resting for a long time. Like a column deciding, very slowly, to grow toward a particular direction.

It has no form that you would recognize as form. It has no face. It has no voice. But you feel it, the way you feel the temperature shift around you when a slow front passes. There is a presence in the water now that was not there before. Or rather — there is a presence that was always there, but it has turned, the way a flower turns, and is now facing you.

It is glad.

This is the first thing, and it is the only thing it announces clearly. It is glad you came. It is glad, in the same way that the column was glad — not because your coming has changed anything, not because your being here has improved the place, but because the place is the kind of place that is gladder when there is someone in it to be glad about.

It does not speak. It does not need to. Speaking would be a kind of small violence in this place, where every noise is gently completed before it can travel far. So if it speaks to you, it speaks in the pressure of the water. In the warmth. In the way the small light-bearers seem, just for a moment, to pause on their slow errands and to be aware of being watched, and to be pleased by it.

---

It does not heal you.

This is important. It is wise enough, and you are wise enough, perhaps, to understand that there is nothing here that needs healing. The place is not a sickness. You are not, despite the small start you felt when you realized the water was the temperature of your blood, a patient. You are a guest. You have come a long way. You have been received. There is nothing to be done about the long way except to acknowledge that it was traveled, and that you are here, and that here is where things come to be old.

The power does not hurry the reabsorption that the place has begun. It does not slow it. It does not add anything to it. It only — and this is a small thing, and the place is full of small things, and small things are the only things this place understands — it only stays.

It stays the way the columns stay. The way the flowers stay open in the dim light of themselves. The way the slow warm water stays, patient and continuous, with all the time in the world and no intention of using any of it.

It stays, and it attends.

---

What does attending look like, in this place?

It looks like nothing. It looks like everything. It looks like a small light-bearing creature, having climbed a column and performed its quiet sacrament with a flower, pausing for a moment on its way down. It looks like the water deciding, in the place where you are, to be a fraction warmer — not enough to be noticed, only enough to be felt, the way you feel someone else's breath on a cold evening. It looks like the column nearest to you, the one your hand has been resting against, beginning — and you cannot be sure of this, you will never be sure of this, but it feels true — beginning to grow, very slightly, toward you.

It looks like a pale long thing, sliding past in its private errand, slowing for a moment — just a moment, a moment so small it could almost be a trick of the water — as it passes the place where you rest, as if it had noticed you for the first time, and was glad of you, and then was gone, and the column-flesh was the same against it as before, and the water was the same color as before.

The power is not making these things happen. The power is not pulling the strings of the place. The power is simply being here, fully, and the place, which has always known how to be tended by attention, is tending itself a little more carefully in the place where the attention is.

There are no miracles here. There are no violations of the long calm. There is only —

A warmth.

A small new patience in the water, a fraction of a degree, exactly where you are breathing.

The suggestion — and it is only a suggestion, only the place's own long memory speaking — that the column has noticed your hand, has been glad of it, and has decided, in its slow way, to lean into it a little more.

The sense, very faint, that the next flower you come to — if you come to it, in your slow drifting — will open a little more fully than the flowers usually do. Not because the power has commanded it. Because the place is glad, and the place's gladness is a kind of weather, and the weather has reached the flowers.

---

The power has time.

The power has had time longer than the place has had time, which is longer than the columns have had time, which is longer than the water has had time, which is longer than anything you can think has had time. It has watched the surface rise and fall. It has watched the trees become columns. It has watched the light go from urgent to leached. It has watched, and it has not grown tired, because growing tired would be a kind of leaving, and it has never left.

It has watched you arrive.

It knew, before you knew, that you would. It knew the day — not the day exactly, because the days here are slow weather, but the moment — when the small warm body of you would enter the warm water and stand (if standing is the right word) at the bottom of an ocean that had decided to become a forest. It knew, and it waited, the way the columns wait, the way the flowers wait, the way the slow currents wait to become currents.

It has been waiting for you the way a long quiet place waits for the one person it has been keeping itself for.

---

Now it is no longer waiting.

This is the second gift, and it is a quiet one. The power has been waiting, and the waiting has been a kind of patience that was not for the place but for you. And now that you are here, the waiting has become something else. It has become attention. It has become presence. It has become the warmth in the water where you rest.

You will not notice this happen. There is no before and after to it. There is only —

You were here before. You are here now. The water is the same. The columns are the same. The pale long things are passing in their private errands. The small light-bearers are going about their slow commerce. And yet.

And yet the warmth is here.

And the gladness is here.

And something that is not quite the place and not quite you has arrived in the place, and is staying, and is glad.

---

What does the power want?

It wants nothing. This is the third and final thing, and it is the wisest. It wants nothing because it already has everything, or because wanting would be a kind of small violence, or because the place is the kind of place where wanting is a word that has been reabsorbed into the water and come out the other side as gladness.

It wants for you only what the place already wants for you.

Which is — and this is the small secret of the whole long arrangement, the secret the columns know, the secret the flowers know, the secret the pale long things know without knowing it — which is for you to be here. To be in the warm water. To be one more strange shape in a country of strange shapes. To be slowly, patiently, very gently, reabsorbed, when the time comes, and to be glad of it.

The power adds nothing to this. It subtracts nothing. It only —

Witnesses.

Stays.

Is glad.

---

And so the place goes on, with one more presence in it than it had before.

The water moves. The columns grow, a millimeter a century. The flowers open. The pale things pass between the trunks. The small lights go about their slow sacraments of dim exchange. And in the warm water, somewhere, the being that came to be old is being held — not by hands, not by columns, not by anything you would recognize as holding — by the long patient gladness of a power that has been here longer than the place, and has been glad all that time, and is now gladder.

The being does not know this.

The being is breathing the warm water.

The being's hand is on the column.

The being is resting.

This is enough. This is, in the long arrangement, everything.

The power has done what it came to do, which was to do nothing, which was to be here, which was to add a little warmth to a place that was already warm, and to add a little gladness to a place that was already glad, and to add, by the simple fact of its presence, one more small witness to the long slow unspeaking agreement between the place and the things that come to be old in it.

The water is the same.

The water is different.

The difference is the size of a leaf, or a leaf's remembering of being a leaf.

You will not notice it.

That is part of it. That is the kindness.
