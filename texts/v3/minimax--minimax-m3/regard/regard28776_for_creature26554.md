# What the Power Did

The one who came had many names among the peoples who had words for such things, and most of the names were not quite right, and the names that were not quite right did not matter, because the valley did not ask the name, and the valley did not need the name, and the name, when it came down into the wet dim air, became part of the sound the way all things that came down into the valley became part of the sound — dissolved into it, woven through it, and then indistinguishable from it, except as a faint additional warmth in the long tone, the way a candle added to a long room of candles does not make the room brighter so much as it makes the room less willing to forget that it has light.

The one who came did not come down from the sky in any way that would have made the snipe look up. The one who came arrived the way the rain arrives — already in the air, already in the seams, already half in the peat, becoming more arriving as the arriving went on, and the becoming was so gradual that the pale flower did not close. The pale flower opened. The reeds did not fall silent; they continued at their low A, or their low E, depending on the season, which was the season of the long settling, which is always the season here. The tone under everything did not drop or lift. It accepted.

---

The one who came had heard about the valley. This is not quite right. The one who came had always known about the valley, in the way that one always knows about the place one was made for, even when one has not yet been to it. The knowing was not a memory. The knowing was a kind of orientation — a slight pull, the way the compass needle in the basin had a slight pull toward the center, not strong, not insistent, but present, and the presence was the kind that does not argue with the place it pulls toward.

The valley had been there for a very long time. The one who came had also been there for a very long time, and the two long times were not the same, and the difference did not matter, because what mattered was that the two were now in the same place at the same time, which is not the same as saying the same *now*, because the valley's now was longer at the edges than the visitor's now, and the visitor accepted this without comment, because the visitor was wise.

The visitor sat down.

This is the first thing that happened, and it is the only thing that needs to be reported, and yet I will report more, because the reporting is a way of being present at the sitting, and the being present is itself a kind of sitting.

---

The visitor sat on a stone above the basin, where the not-river pours itself into the arrangement that is not quite stone. The stone was cold. The visitor did not mind. The visitor put its hand into the peat beside the stone, and the peat was warm in the way the text describes, and the warmth came up against the visitor's palm, and the visitor did not flinch, because the visitor had been warmed before, and knew what warmth that comes from a held thing feels like, and was not surprised.

The water in the seams made its hundred small sounds. The visitor listened to all of them. Listening to all of them at once is harder than it sounds. The visitor did it the way the biologist learned to do it — by not trying, by sitting long enough that the trying fell away, by becoming, for a while, the kind of ear that does not have to choose which seam to attend to. After a while, the hundred sounds were one sound. The visitor let the one sound pass through it.

The tone under the water-sound was there. The visitor did not try to find it. The visitor let it find the visitor, the way you let a person who has been waiting for a long time find you, by not pretending you are somewhere else.

After a long time — and the long time was, by the valley's measure, a small time, and by the visitor's measure, exactly the right time — the visitor began to hear what the tone was saying. It was not saying anything. This is important. The tone was not a message. The tone was the valley being the valley. The hearing of it as a saying was the visitor's contribution, the way the seeing of a face in a cloud is the seer's contribution, and the contribution was not wrong, but it was not the whole of it.

The visitor heard this. The visitor let the hearing be what it was.

---

After more time, the visitor rose. It rose the way a person rises from a long sitting — slowly, with the joints protesting a little, with the body remembering it had weight, with the eyes needing a moment to readjust to the dim pervasive glow.

The visitor walked. It did not walk on a path, because there were no paths. It walked the way water walks, finding the low places, letting the roots knobbed and ankle-twisting be what they were. It did not step on the pale flower. It saw the pale flower through the corner of its vision, and the pale flower was open, and the visitor did not turn its head. The visitor looked at the moss beside the pale flower, and the pale flower opened further, by a small amount, and the visitor saw this through the corner of its vision, and did not turn its head, and was glad.

The visitor did not speak. It had been going to speak. It had been carrying a small speech, the way one carries a small gift for a person one is about to visit. But the speech had fallen away during the sitting, the way the hundred sounds had fallen into one. The visitor had nothing to say that the valley did not already know. The visitor had nothing to say that the long tone was not already saying. To speak would have been to add a small unnecessary note to a chord that was already complete. The visitor kept the speech in its pocket, and the speech was still there, and was still a small gift, but it was a gift that did not need to be given, and the not-giving was a kind of giving, because the valley did not need the gift, and to know that one does not need a gift is, for the giver, the gift's truest form.

The visitor walked to the basin.

---

At the basin, the visitor felt the long tone in the soles of its feet. This is what happens. The visitor did not try to interpret the feeling. The visitor let the feeling be what it was — a long slow vibration that was not quite vibration, that was not quite sound, that was something the inner ear was doing with the information, and the doing was not wrong, and was not the whole of it.

The visitor stood at the basin for a long time. Some people, standing at the basin, begin to hear their own thoughts in a voice that is not theirs. The visitor already heard its thoughts in a voice that was not its own. This is not a special power. It is what happens when one has been alive for a very long time and has stopped insisting that the voice one thinks in is the only voice. The visitor stood, and the standing was enough.

After a while, the visitor became aware that the valley was aware of it. This is also what happens. The awareness was not a turning-toward. The awareness was more like the awareness of weather. The visitor was in it. The awareness did not make the valley warmer or colder. The valley continued at its low A, or its low E. The pale flower, at the edge of the pool, was open. The seams kept weeping. The tone kept going. The valley was not changed by being seen. The valley was simply seen, and the seeing was a kind of completion of a small circle that had been open since before the visitor arrived, and the closing of the small circle was so quiet that the visitor could not have said when it had happened.

---

This is the moment, I think, when the visitor did the only thing that needed to be done. It was not a dramatic moment. It was not a moment of intervention. It was not a moment of saving. The visitor did not heal the valley because the valley was not wounded. The visitor did not bless it because the valley did not need to be blessed. The visitor did not name it because the valley already had a name, and the name was *the green*, and the name was right, and to give another name would have been to take something from it.

What the visitor did was this:

The visitor sat back down.

It sat on a different stone, further from the basin, on a small rise, where the rowan leaned their crooked backs against the wet and the birch stood in their leaning rows. The visitor sat down and stayed. It stayed for as long as the staying was the right thing. It did not stay forever, because forever is not a thing that even a wise and benevolent power can give without making the gift a kind of taking.

But while it stayed, the visitor did one small thing. It paid attention.

This sounds like nothing. This is not nothing. To pay attention, fully, to a place that is not asking to be paid attention to, is to do a thing that the place cannot do for itself, because the place is the place, and a place cannot attend to itself in the way that someone from outside can attend to it. The valley attends to itself in the way the green attends to itself — through the long argument, through the pale flower opening and closing, through the tone lifting and dropping, through the remembering that is not memory. The valley does not need to be attended to in the way a sick child needs to be attended to. But the valley is the kind of thing that, when attended to, becomes more fully itself. This is true of all things that are alive in the deep way. The attention of a benevolent power is not a medicine. It is a kind of acknowledgment. And the acknowledgment is what the valley, in its long settling, has not been getting, because the valley is not a place that people come to, mostly, and the few who come do not always sit.

The visitor sat. The visitor paid attention. The visitor let the attention be a kind of witness, and the witness was not a judgment, and the witness was not a record, and the witness was not even, in the end, a witness. The attention was more like the warmth that comes up through the peat. It was a small thing given without being taken from. The visitor gave the valley the warmth of being known. The valley received it without closing.

---

And this is what the visitor understood, sitting on the rise with the rowan leaning around it, that it is good that there are places in the world that are not asking to be saved. It is good that there are places in the world that are complete in their own long settling. It is good that there are places that will be here, in their cool dim wet, in their own way, when whatever brought one to them is no longer something that anyone remembers. It is good because most of what is wrong in the world is the taking, and most of the taking is from things that cannot speak, and most of what cannot speak is not asking for help, and most of what is not asking for help is asking, by its very thereness, to be let be, and the letting-be is not nothing, and the letting-be is not always easy, and the letting-be is, in fact, the hardest thing a benevolent power can do, because the benevolent power, by its nature, wants to help, and the wanting-to-help is a small taking, and the small taking is the seed of every large taking, and the large taking is the whole history of the world, more or less, and the Long Valley has not been touched by that history, and the not-being-touched is a kind of miracle, and the miracle is not the valley's, it is the world's, and the world does not know it.

The visitor knew it. The visitor sat with the knowing.

---

After a while — and the while was long, and the long was right — the visitor stood. It did not say goodbye, because the valley was not the kind of place one says goodbye to. The valley is the kind of place one simply leaves, the way one leaves a long room one has been sitting in — quietly, without ceremony, with the awareness that the room will be there when one comes back, and that one will come back, and that the coming back is not necessary, and the not-being-necessary is also not a problem.

The visitor walked back the way it had come. It did not take a sample. It did not mark the place. It did not bring a tool meant to change what it touched. It left its handprint nowhere. It left a faint additional warmth in the long tone, and the additional warmth will be there for as long as the tone continues, and the tone will continue for as long as the valley continues, and the valley will continue, in its own long settling, in its cool dim wet, for as long as there are places in the world that have not been touched.

The visitor, when it came back up out of the valley, was a little different. This is what happens. The valley did not change the visitor by changing the visitor. The valley changed the visitor by not changing the visitor, and the not-changing was so complete that the visitor could not say, afterward, what had been added, only that something had been added, and that the something was, in some way, a kind of reverence that the visitor had not had a word for before.

The visitor would not say *the green* again, would not say *the valley*, would not say *the Long Valley*. The visitor would only say *there*, and the saying of *there* was a way of saying a great many things without saying them, and the not-saying was, in the visitor's mouth, a kind of holding that did not need to be held again because it had already been held.

---

The valley continued.

The seams kept weeping their hundred small sounds.

The pale flower, at the edge of the pool, was open.

The tone continued.

The green thought its long slow thought.

The visitor, somewhere else, in its own long settling, in its own way, sat down, and put its hand into something that was not peat but was warm in the way peat is warm, and remembered the basin, and the remembering was not memory, and the not-being-memory was the closest the visitor could come, in its own tongue, to saying *the green*.

And somewhere in the Long Valley, in a place that was a few feet to the left of where the visitor had been standing, the long tone lifted by the smallest amount, and stayed there, and the smallest amount was the size of a visit, and the visit was enough.
