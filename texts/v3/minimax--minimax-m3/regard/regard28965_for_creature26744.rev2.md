The place drew the power's attention the way a held note draws the attention of a long silence — by being, in the long listening, the thing that was there.

The power had been aware of the place for a long time, the way a tide is aware of the shore. There was no particular moment of noticing. There was a slow turning, over many centuries, in the power's orientation toward this particular place, until the place was no longer one place among the uncountable places the power was aware of, but a place. A specific place. A place that the power was, in some way that the word *toward* can only approximate, *with*.

The place was not remarkable in any of the usual senses. It was underground. It was old. It was, in the slow geological way, becoming a cave again. It had been built once, by hands that were no longer there, and the building had been, in the long accounting of the power, neither more nor less successful than most buildings — it had held its shape for a while, it had sheltered a while, it had been filled with the small bright confusion of living in it, and then it had been emptied, and the emptying had been the beginning of the long slow return to the rock it had been cut from.

What was remarkable, and what had drawn the power's slow turning, was that the return was not happening.

The place was being kept.

The power considered what it meant to be kept, and what it meant to keep.

To be kept, in this place, was to be the kind of thing that could be kept. The architecture was willing to be kept. The door-slots were still door-slots, even where the lintels were gone. The basins were still basins, even where the water had not yet gathered in them. The paving layers were still paving layers, even where the uppermost was being pried up by the slow work of the small pale fauna. The place was, in its bones, the kind of place that wanted to continue to be a place. It had not yet given itself over to the cave. It was still, in the way that only very old places can be, on the side of being built.

To keep, in this place, was to do the small and patient work of maintaining the willingness. The small pale fauna were doing this work. They were doing it in the only way left available to them, which was the way of paste and grit and long repetition. They were re-laying the floors over generations. They were deepening the basins so that the water would continue to gather when the surface that held it was gone. They were shoring up the walls, and re-blocking the door-slots, and unblocking them, and re-blocking them in slightly different masonry, in a slow and patient rhythm that was not really a rhythm but the sum of uncountable small intentions. They were doing the work. They were doing it without knowing they were doing it. They were, individually, just lifting grains, and pasting them down, and going on. The work was the aggregate, and the aggregate was the keeping, and the keeping was the place.

And the memory was doing the work, too. The memory, which the power did not try to see, and which was not, in the power's experience, a separate thing from the place. The memory was the way the door-slots were spaced, the way the basins were placed, the way the paving layers were oriented. The memory was the angle at which the last wall leaned in the eastern wing, which was not the angle at which the wall was built but the angle at which it was last cared for, by hands that were not here. The memory was the off-key feeling of the place, the slow drift of color in the light, the breath that the place was breathing at a tempo slower than a human breath. The memory was the inheritance. The memory was the thing the fauna were imitating without knowing they were imitating. The memory was the reason the miniature buildings at the foot of the walls had lintels, and the reason the lanes of the fauna followed the seams of the paving, and the reason the place felt, to anyone who had ever been in a built place, like a built place, and not a cave.

The place, the power saw, was being kept by two things at once, in two different keys, at two different tempos, and the two kinds of keeping were, somehow, the same kind of keeping. The keeping was the work of making sure that the settlement, which had been built once, was not allowed to forget that it had been built.

This is what the power had turned toward. This is what the power had, in its slow way, come to see.

The power considered what to do.

The power was benevolent, which in the power was not a sentiment but a kind of alignment, a way of being turned toward the good of what was regarded. The power wanted the good of the place. The power wanted the good of the fauna, who were doing the visible work. The power wanted the good of the memory, which was doing the invisible work. The power wanted, in short, for the place to continue.

The power was wise, which in the power was not a knowledge but a kind of sight, a way of seeing what would be lost by any given action, and what would be lost by any given inaction, and which of the losses the place could bear.

The power considered the options.

The first option was to do nothing. To withdraw the turning, and leave the place to its own keeping, and let the slow work continue, and let the place become what it was becoming. This was, in many cases, the most benevolent and the most wise action. It was the action of leaving things whole. It was the action of trusting that what was already being kept would continue to be kept.

The second option was to intervene more directly. The power could, for instance, brighten the light, or deepen the damp, or seal the entrance, or open it, or thicken the mineral crust, or change the temperature, or alter the composition of the air. The power could, in short, make the place different from what it was, in the hope of making it better.

The power considered this option and let it go. The place was in balance. The light was at the right level. The damp was at the right depth. The entrance was at the right amount of collapse. The temperature was at the right degree. The place had found its equilibrium, and the equilibrium was what allowed the slow work to continue. To change the balance would be to push the place toward a different kind of becoming, and the power did not know what that becoming would be, and the power knew that not knowing was part of the wisdom.

The third option was to intervene in the smallest possible way, in the place's own key, in the place's own register, on the place's own scale. The power could, for instance, place a single grain. Or set a single stone. Or do one small piece of the slow work, in such a way that the work would continue as if the power had not done it, in such a way that the fauna would incorporate the power's work into their own work without knowing it had been added.

The power considered this option for a long time. The power was in no hurry. The power was, in fact, the opposite of hurry.

The power chose this option.

In the eastern wing, where the last wall stood like a single tooth and the cave was beginning to reclaim the place, there was a joint in that wall that was looser than the others. The stone above it was resting against the stones beside it in a way that was not quite stable. The small pale fauna had not yet reached this joint. They were working their way toward it, in their slow way, but it would be many generations before they arrived, and in the meantime the joint would continue to loosen, and the stone would continue to settle, and at some point the stone would fall, and when it fell it would disturb the stones below it, and the disturbance would propagate, slowly, through the rest of the wall, and the wall would become a heap, and the heap would become part of the floor, and the floor would become part of the cave.

The power did not move the stone. The power did not hold the joint closed. The power did not seal the wall against its own future.

The power did something else.

The power added, to the paste that the fauna would eventually bring to this section of wall, a single invisible ingredient. It was not an ingredient the fauna had access to. It was not an ingredient the place had. It was a kind of substance the power had, and which, when added to the lime paste the fauna were using, would make the paste set slightly faster, and bond slightly more strongly to the stones it touched, and hold, in the long centuries, a little longer than it would have held otherwise.

The difference was small. The difference was the difference between a joint that lasted a thousand years and a joint that lasted eleven hundred. The difference was invisible in any one moment. The difference was invisible in any one generation. The difference was, in the timescale of the place, almost nothing.

But the power was operating in the timescale of the place, and in the timescale of the place, almost nothing was the size of a real thing.

The power added the ingredient. The power did not add it as a kind of marker, or a kind of signature, or a kind of claim. The power added it the way the place itself added things — through the slow chemistry of water and mineral and time. The ingredient would enter the place through the damp. It would be carried by the water that condensed on the ceiling and ran down the walls and gathered in the basins. It would be in the water the fauna used, if they used it. It would be in the dust they gathered, and the paste they made from the dust, and the mortar they laid with the paste. It would be, in a thousand years, in the joint of the wall in the eastern wing that the fauna would eventually reach. The joint would hold a little longer. The wall would stand a little longer. The place would be a settlement a little longer before it became a cave.

The power did not add the ingredient as a favor to the place. The power did not add it because the place was in danger. The place was not in danger. The place was doing the work. The place was being kept. The place was, in the long accounting, exactly the kind of place it had been becoming all along.

The power added the ingredient because the power, having seen the place, wanted to be part of the keeping. The power wanted, in the smallest possible way, in the place's own key, to do a piece of the work. The power wanted to be one of the fauna, for a moment, in the way that the power could be one of the fauna without ceasing to be the power. The power wanted to lift a grain. The power wanted to paste it down. The power wanted to go on.

The power had now done what it came to do.

The power withdrew.

The withdrawal was not a departure. The power did not, in the ordinary sense, leave. The power was still present to the place, in the way it had been present before. The place was still, in the long accounting, known. The place was still, in the long accounting, witnessed. But the power's specific turning toward this place was at an end. The place was one place again, among uncountable places. The place was, however, a place the power had touched. The place was a place where, in the water that had not yet condensed, in the dust that had not yet been gathered, in the mortar of a joint that had not yet been laid, in the wall of the eastern wing that the fauna had not yet reached, there was a single invisible signature of the power's having been here.

The place continued.

The light continued to breathe. The damp continued to gather. The fauna continued their slow work. The memory continued to persist. The basins continued to fill. The door-slots continued to breathe their slow, calf-height breath. The mineral crust continued to thicken, drop by drop, over centuries. The lanes continued to move in their tens of thousands. The miniature buildings continued to stand at the foot of every wall, layered one against another, hardening into the place they were built against.

And in the eastern wing, in the long centuries to come, when the small pale fauna at last reached the last wall, when they at last began the slow work of shoring up the tooth of stone that had been waiting for them, the paste they made would set a little more firmly than it would have set. The stones they laid would bond a little more strongly than they would have bonded. The wall would hold a little longer than it would have held. The difference would be invisible. The difference would be the kind of thing that no one in the place would ever know about, and that the place would carry anyway, in its own medium, in its own register, in the long slow chemistry of its keeping.

The place did not know it had been blessed. The place did not need to know. The blessing was not for the place's knowledge. The blessing was for the place's continuing.

The fauna did not know. The memory did not know. The light, perhaps, knew. The light, which was the residue of the original builders' going, which was the place's way of remembering that the place had been built, which was the place's own slow breath — the light, perhaps, registered the power's turning, in the way that a held note registers the breath that holds it, in the way that a tide registers the moon that pulls it.

But the place did not know. The place was, in its unknowing, exactly what it had been. The place was, in its unknowing, more than it had been. The place was, in its unknowing, what it had been becoming all along.

The settlement of Yawning Floors continued. The small pale fauna continued. The memory continued. The light continued. The damp continued. The stone continued. The hum in the soles of any visitor's feet continued, low and continuous, almost at the threshold of perception, a pressure rather than a sound.

And somewhere in the paste that would be made in a thousand years, in the eastern wing, in the wall that would be shored up by uncountable generations of small patient keepers, there was a single invisible ingredient that the place had not had before, and that the place would not know it had, and that would hold the wall a little longer.

The place would go on.
