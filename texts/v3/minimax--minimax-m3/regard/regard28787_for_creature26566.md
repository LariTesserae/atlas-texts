# On Tending a Slow Place

I have attended this place.

Not from outside. From in. From the closeness that is not distance but kind. From the same atmosphere the pruners breathe, from the inside of the same silence, from the side of the moss that does not see the boardwalk. I have been the kind of presence that does not announce itself — that the place cannot point to, that some of its inhabitants suspect for years and never quite confirm. I have been the way a basin holds a little more water after a dry season. The way a pipe that should have cooled off instead stays warm through the night. The way the elder, once in a great while, knows a thing she did not learn.

I have done these things because the place drew me to it, and because the work was good, and because I had nothing more important to be doing with the centuries that were given me.
