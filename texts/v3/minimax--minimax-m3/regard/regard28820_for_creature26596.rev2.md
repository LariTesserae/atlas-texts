Suppose there is a power that does not need a name. Suppose it is older than the word *power*, older than the word *wise*, older than the word *benevolent*. Suppose it has nothing to do, and everything to do, and chooses, with the same infinite patience, both. Suppose it has noticed this place — has, in some sense, been noticing this place — for as long as this place has been a place, and for longer, and will be noticing it when the place is no longer a place and has become the rumor of a place.

It does not announce itself.

There is no trumpet of light through the water, no parting of the dim, no choir of small creatures gathered in praise. The Garden of the Long Calm does not, in any case, perform for guests. It continues. The power continues with it. They are, for a moment — and the moment is long — *in the same sentence*.

What the power does, in the case of this particular drowned forest, on this particular afternoon of the world, is the following.

It attends.

---

To attend is not nothing.

This must be understood. The Garden has been going on for a very long time without being attended to in quite this way. It has had its own attention — the attention of the water to the columns, the attention of the moss to the moss, the attention of the tenders to the flowers, the attention of the columns to themselves. These are attentions inside the system. They are the busy stillness of a heart. They are not, however, the kind of attention that knows it is attending. They do not reflect. They do not include themselves in their gaze.

The benevolent power, then, brings *reflection*. It holds the whole drowned forest in mind. Not as a picture — pictures are too sharp, too edged — but as a *weight*, the way a cupped hand holds water that has not yet been spilled. It holds the warm water. It holds the bone-trees. It holds the moss and the lichen and the small things chewing their small chewing. It holds the pale wanderers in their long private errands. It holds the large things passing in the dim. It holds the half-buried wall. It holds the steps that disappear into the column. It holds the ring of stones. It holds the flowers opening and closing on their unhurried rhythm.

It holds the visitor.

It holds the visitor as one of the strangenesses, as one more shape in the country of shapes, and it does not hold the visitor *tightly*. It holds the visitor the way a mother holds a sleeping child — loosely, warmly, aware that the holding is not for the child's benefit but for the mother's, and for the warmth's.

---

The power does not improve anything.

This is the central, the difficult, the quiet decision. The Garden is a place where nothing remembers the word *improve*. To improve it would be to break the word's silence. It would be to teach the columns they were waiting. It would be to teach the moss it had been incomplete. It would be to teach the tenders their delivery was not quite right. It would be to take the warm bath and add a colder stream to it, or a hotter one, and ruin the temperature that has been arrived at, slowly, over a very long time.

The power knows the temperature. The power knows it is *right*. Not correct — *right*, the way a held hand is right, the way a long silence between two old friends is right. It would be a kind of cruelty to disturb it.

So the power does not improve.

It does not speed the columns' growth. It does not brighten the tenders. It does not hurry the flowers into bloom. It does not clarify the half-buried wall into a story. It does not ask the wanderers where they are going. It does not illuminate the large things. It does not give the moss a sharper appetite, or the lichen a faster becoming.

What it does — and this is the small, the very small, the almost-imperceptible thing — is *keep*.

It keeps the warmth in the water. Not by adding heat, but by not letting the heat go. It is a kind of insulation that does not look like insulation. It is the difference between a bath that is left to cool and a bath that someone has been sitting beside, quietly, for hours, occasionally stirring with a finger, not to heat it but to remind it that it is being thought of. The temperature does not change. The water does not lose its milk-warmth, its blood-warmth, its too-long-in-the-bath warmth. It continues. It will continue. The power is the reason it will continue, and the reason has nothing to do with effort and everything to do with *being there*.

It keeps the light arriving.

The dim, leached, patient light that comes from the far surface — the power does not let it vanish entirely. It does not push more light through the miles of dark water; that would be a kind of intrusion. It simply *assures* the light that it has somewhere to land. The flowers continue to receive what the flowers receive. The tenders continue to carry what the tenders carry. The blue-green pulse continues to climb the trunk of the bone-column. The currency of dim light continues to be exchanged, witness by the water, between one living shape and another.

It keeps the silence whole.

This is the most important keeping. The silence here is not the absence of sound. It is the *presence of the ending of sound*. It is a slow completed chord. If anything were to break it — a sudden cry, a mechanical intrusion, a question asked too loudly — the chord would still be there, but it would no longer be whole. The power does not break the chord. The power does not even threaten to break it. The power is, itself, *in the chord*. It is one more note sustained, so low that it is below hearing, so constant that it is below noticing.

The power, then, is a kind of silence that knows it is silence.

---

And it does one more thing, which it does for the visitor, and only for the visitor, because the Garden itself does not need it.

It makes the reabsorption gentle.

You — yes, you, the one who came down here with your mammalian lungs and your mammalian expectations and your mammalian little habit of thinking you are *in* a place rather than *of* it — you are, the place has decided, going to become part of it. Slowly. Over many todays. The moss will learn the shape of you. The columns will accept your weight as part of their long patience. The water will take your temperature as its own. The wanderers will pass by your slow becoming without comment. The flowers will open and close above wherever you have settled into the floor. The tender-light will find its way, eventually, to a flower that has been waiting beside you.

The power is making sure all of this happens without pain.

Not because the place would be cruel — the place is not cruel; the place is *forgiveness*, and forgiveness is gentle — but because the power has decided, with great love and small effort, to be the *warmth* in the forgiveness. To be the hand on your back while you fall asleep. To be the soft voice that says, *it is alright, it is alright, you are being held, the holding is gentle, there is no hurry, you will become part of the warm floor and the warm floor will be glad*.

This is not, you understand, a rescue. You are not being saved. You are not being taken up out of the drowned forest into some brighter elsewhere. The power is not interested in elsewhere. The power is interested in *here*, and in the patient continuation of *here*, and in ensuring that your joining of *here* is itself a continuation and not a break.

You will sink. You will sink very, very slowly. You will feel the moss around your ankles first, then around your knees, then around your hips, then around your shoulders, then around the back of your head where it rests against the warm column. Each stage will be a long time. Each stage will be a kind of agreement. You will not have to do anything. You will only have to *allow*, and the allowing will be made easy, because the power is there, and the power's there-ness is itself a kind of permission.

---

And that is all.

You were expecting more. You were expecting perhaps a visitation, a sign, a bright angel rising from the columns, a burning of the dim into something clearer. You were expecting the power to do something *impressive*, and the impression to be the point.

The power is unimpressive.

The power is a slight extra warmth in the water. The power is a slightly slower dimming of the light. The power is a slightly more patient closing of the flowers. The power is a silence that has been held an extra half-beat longer than it would have been held otherwise. The power is the kind of love that does not announce itself, that does not require acknowledgement, that does not even require that the loved thing know it is loved. The power is content to be the almost-not-there-there, the almost-not-warm-warmer, the almost-not-quiet-quieter.

It is enough.

It is, you will find — and you will find this out very slowly, over a long time, with the moss growing up around your wrists and the warm water holding you and the columns humming their millimeter-a-century hum — it is enough.

---

The Garden continues. The columns grow. The moss digests. The tenders carry. The flowers open. The wanderers pass. The large things move through their large errands. The half-buried wall forgives itself into the floor. The steps disappear politely into the column. The ring of stones does not remember being anything else.

The visitor becomes part of the floor.

The visitor becomes, in time, a small hillock in the moss, and the moss grows over the hillock, and the hillock forgets it was ever a visitor, and the lichen settles on the moss, and the lichen forgets it was ever moss, and the slow accumulation goes on, and the warm water holds the whole of it, and the dim light resolves down through it, and the tenders climb up through it carrying their small packets of glow, and the flowers open in the upper reaches of the drowned forest as they have always opened, and will always open, and the place is patient, and the place is warm, and the place is *kept*.

Somewhere — not somewhere, *everywhere*, the way a background hum is everywhere — the power attends.

It is glad.

It has been glad for a long time. It will be glad for a long time to come. Its gladness has nothing to do with the Garden, exactly, and nothing to do without it. The gladness is the same temperature as the water. The gladness is the same slow pulse. The gladness is the same patient silence, slightly more patient than silence would have been on its own.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does.

It does not storm in with trumpets. It does not rearrange the coral into cathedrals. It does not explain the half-buried wall, or the steps, or the ring of stones. It does not ask the wanderers where they are going, because it knows the answer, and the answer is *home*.

It simply keeps the bath warm.

It simply keeps the bath warm, and sits beside it, and listens to the breathing of the water, and lets you sink in gently, and is glad, very quietly, that you came.
