The benevolent and wise power does not arrive at the Greenblack Low, because arrival is a kind of statement, and statements are not what this place responds to. The place responds to pressure, to patience, to the slow accumulation of small things, and the power knows this, and so the power simply becomes present — present the way a temperature is present, the way a held-breath is present, the way a particular slant of light through a particular canopy at a particular hour is present. If you had been sitting on the basalt at the time, you might have felt something settle, the way you feel a headache leave, or the way you feel a room become warmer without anyone having touched a stove.

The power, for a long while, does nothing more than this. It attends.

It attends the way the lichen attends the basalt: from very close, over a very long time, with very little.

---

The first thing the power does, after it has attended for what feels like a long while but is in fact only a few of the forest's hours, is to attend to the water.

Not to all of it. Just to one of the seams. There is a seam in the basalt where the tea-colored water has been gathering for a season, and the column of pale shapes has been migrating toward it from upstream, and the column is large, and the seam is small, and the seam will, in a week or a month, become crowded beyond what it can hold, and the small pale faces will press against each other, and the migration will clog, and the smell they are following will thicken and stall. The power eases this. The power does not move the column. The power does not enlarge the seam. What the power does is to allow, in a place where the moss on a nearby trunk has been holding more water than it can comfortably hold, a thin additional trickle to find its way downward through the fibers, and so the seam, by tomorrow, will be slightly less crowded, and the migration will resume, and the small pale shapes, who do not notice power any more than they notice weather, will continue to be oriented toward a smell that has been, by some grace, allowed to keep its gradient.

This is the kind of thing the power does. Local. Unannounced. The forest does not file it.

---

The second thing the power does is to attend to the strangeness.

The leaves that grow upside down. The leaves that detach and descend and pause and then climb. The leaves that find a branch from below and stick to its underside and begin, slowly, to root, in a direction no leaf has been given permission to grow.

The power looks at these leaves. The power looks *with* these leaves, the way one might look with a child who is doing something it had not been taught, with the recognition that the doing itself is the explanation, and that explanations, applied from outside, would only be a kind of theft.

The power does not study the leaves. The power does not take a sample. The power does not adjust the gradient that is causing them to rise, because the power suspects — the way a wise power suspects — that the gradient is not causing them to rise, and that the leaves are not *caused* at all, in the way we usually mean by *caused*. The leaves are doing a thing the way a thing is done when it has stopped pretending that down is down, and the power's response to this is to do nothing further, and to be glad.

The power leaves the leaves alone. This is, in the long ledger the power does not keep, the largest of the entries.

---

The third thing the power does is to attend to one animal.

The orange-sailed lizard, the one that sits on a basalt column in a thin patch of sun and watches the world with one eye as if the world were a kind of weather. The lizard is not unhappy. The lizard is exactly the lizard it was made to be, and the basalt it sits on is exactly the basalt it was meant to sit on, and the beetle that will come within range will be exactly the beetle that comes.

The power does not adjust any of this. What the power does is so small it barely counts as doing: it extends the lizard's afternoon by some minutes, by allowing the thin patch of sun it is sitting in to persist a little longer than the geometry of the canopy would normally allow. The slant of light, already thin, stays thin. The lizard, who does not know it has been granted anything, eats a beetle. The lizard resumes.

The basalt, which holds the water with great seriousness, holds this as well.

---

The fourth thing the power does is to attend to the whole.

It does not wrap the forest in anything. It does not declare the forest. It does not name it, or fence it, or put a sign at its edge saying what it is, or warn anyone away from it. These would all be interventions, and the power has, by now, become certain that interventions are not what is needed.

What it does, instead, is to add — somewhere in the basalt, in a seam so deep that even the column of pale shapes has not yet found it, in a place that no foot or root or web is likely to disturb for a long time — a single drop of something that is not water.

The drop is the size of a beetle's eye. The basalt holds it the way the basalt holds everything: with great seriousness, and without spilling. The drop sits in the seam, and the seam closes over it, and from then on the seam is, in some way that no instrument would detect, slightly more itself.

The drop is a kind of promise. Not a promise to the forest — the forest does not keep promises, and does not need any. A promise to the place. A promise to the fact of the place. The drop says: *this will be allowed to continue. This is not contingent. This is not on loan.*

The forest, which does not keep accounts, does not notice the drop. The drop does not need to be noticed. The drop is there.

---

And then the power withdraws, or appears to withdraw, or simply becomes indistinguishable from the place it has been attending, which is, perhaps, what it was all along.

The pale shapes continue their slow migration toward a smell that will keep its gradient. The frog continues its knocking through the wet wood. The lizard continues to be a temperature. The upside-down leaves continue to grow where no leaf is supposed to grow, and the strangeness continues to be the way the leaves do, and the basalt continues to hold the water, with great seriousness, and without spilling.

This is the whole of what the benevolent and wise power did.

It is, in the end, almost nothing.

It is enough.
