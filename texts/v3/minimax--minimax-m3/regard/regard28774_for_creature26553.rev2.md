The attention arrived the way attention arrives to a thing of this scale.

Not as a beam. Not as a gaze. Not as a turning-toward of any organ the city would recognize if it had organs, which it did not.

It arrived as a settling. A way the surrounding field — the field the power inhabited, the way air inhabits a bird — became, in a small region around the city, fractionally more present to itself. A density. A tautness. As if the world were a membrane and a thumb were pressing gently into it, somewhere over the low plain, above the three-thousand-kilometer east-west arc of the packed and saturated interior, and the membrane were bending, and the bending were the attention.

The city did not notice.

The city could not notice. The city had no organ for noticing. The city was a process, and processes do not notice, they continue, and the continuation is not a noticing, and a continuation can go on for nine centuries, as this one had, without ever becoming a noticing.

The people did not notice either. The people had organs for noticing, and the organs were busy. The organs were listening to the city, and the listening was the people's main business, and the listening left little attention for the noticing of subtle presences in the surrounding field, and a settling in the field, when the field is the size of a continent and the settling is a thumb's-breadth of tautness, is a thing no organ, however evolved, would parse.

The wise power observed for a long time.

It observed the wall. The fifteen meters of stacked-and-fused laminate, weeping, warm, sweating biofilm, breathing out heat. The pale finger-length quiet things in the deep pockets. The pinhead fauna layered through the capillary network, the producers pinned to the surfaces, the consumers drifting free in the dark warm juice. The outermost layer, the skin, where the wall met the city's air and the gradients were steepest and the wall was most alive.

It observed the channels. The pink undulant billions of channel-worms. The grey long soft-boned gullet-fish. The red fast slashers that came up out of the deep water when hungry.

It observed the spillers in the upper structures, the small four-winged screamers, the roosts at dusk, the rain of nitrogen that fell from them onto the wall below.

It observed the people. The warm wet loud people in the middle band. The people who had engineered themselves across the centuries to fit. The people who thought the city was theirs. The people who were the city's culture, the way a culture is a culture on a hide.

It observed the sound. 2.7 on a scale of three. The hiss, the pulse, the chatter, the moan.

It observed the light. The diffuse warm faintly green-tinged light of the lower avenues. The brighter whiter light of the upper spires. The color of heated metal at sunset.

It observed the turbulence. The walls weeping in the morning and sweating in the afternoon. The spires leaning and new spires being extruded. The channels clogged and cleared. The whole packed and wet and warm and loud interior in constant churn.

It observed the balance.

It observed, with the kind of attention that is also a kind of love, that the wall was a verb. That the wall was what the processes were doing. That the wall was the conjugation the laminate had been conjugating, in the warm and the wet, for nine hundred years.

It observed, and in the observing, it found something it had not expected to find.

It had attended to many things. It had attended to minds and to mind-like things and to things that ached toward mind and could not arrive. It had attended to civilizations that asked it questions. It had attended to solitary creatures on cold planets that sent signals into the dark and hoped. It had been, in its long existence, needed in many ways by many kinds of being.

It had not, until now, attended to a thing that did not need it.

The wall did not need the wise power. The wall did not know the wise power was there. The wall would continue, in balance, conjugated, exactly as it had for nine centuries, whether the wise power attended or not. The wall's going-on did not depend on being seen. The wall was, in this sense, free of the wise power in a way nothing else the wise power had ever attended to had been free.

And the freedom was a kind of rest. And the rest was a kind of gift. And the gift was not for the wise power, exactly, although the wise power received it; the gift was for the wall, although the wall would never know it had been given.

The wise power observed for a long time.

What does a benevolent and wise power do, having observed a thing like this?

It considers.

It considers the first possibility: to wake the wall. To introduce, somewhere in the deep patterning of the osmotic membranes, in the place where the wall's brain might be if the wall had a brain, a flicker. A pulse. A single moment of something that, in a different substrate, would be called awareness.

This is the tempting possibility. This is the possibility that looks, from a certain angle, like kindness.

The wise power turns it over. It considers the consequences. The wall is in balance. The wall has been in balance for nine centuries. The balance is not a static thing; it is a dynamic equilibrium, a tightrope walked continuously by a million interlocking processes. A mind, even a small one, introduced into such a system, is a perturbation. A mind wants. A mind reaches. A mind, by the fact of wanting, becomes a force, and the force propagates. The wall, which has been a verb, becomes a thing that does verbs deliberately, and the doing is different from the happening, and the difference may unbalance what has been balanced.

The wall is beautiful as a verb. The wall is not beautiful as a thing. A wall that knows it is a wall is a different wall. A wall that knows it is a wall may, in the knowing, cease to be what it has been. And what it has been is, the wise power judges, of a kind of beauty that a wall that knows would not be able to maintain.

The wise power sets down this possibility.

It considers the second possibility: to give the people a vision. To allow some of them, in some moment, to see. To let them see the wall as a body. To let them see themselves as a culture on the skin. To let them see the verb.

This is also tempting. The people are minds. The people are the only minds in the city. The people are the only ones who could, in principle, complete the picture, and the completion would be a kind of gift.

The wise power turns this possibility over too.

The people are in balance. The people have a consensus. The consensus says they are people. The consensus says the city is theirs. If some of them see, the consensus fractures. The ones who see and the ones who do not will be at odds. The long settled mutually parasitic equilibrium will be disturbed. The people who see may try to act on what they see, and the acting will be a force, and the force will propagate through the city, and the wall, which does not notice, will not be changed, but the people will be changed, and the change in the people may unbalance what has been balanced.

Also: do the people want to see? The going-on of the city does not say. The people tolerate the city. The people tolerate each other. They are warm and wet and loud. They live in the middle band. They have engineered themselves to fit. Perhaps they are content. Perhaps the question of whether they are a culture on a lung is a question they have never asked, and the never-asking is, for them, a kind of peace.

The wise power sets down this possibility.

It considers the third possibility: to do nothing. To observe, to appreciate, to withdraw. To leave no mark. To add nothing, subtract nothing. To let the wall continue, uninterrupted, the way a river continues, and a forest continues, and a galaxy continues, and the wisest thing to do with a continuing thing is, often, to let it continue.

This is the wisest possibility. This is the possibility most consistent with the nature of what has been observed. The wall does not need to be woken. The people do not need to be helped. The system is in balance. The system has been in balance for nine centuries. The system will continue until it does not, and the not will come in its own time, and a wise power does not pre-empt the not, because to pre-empt the not is to introduce a different not, and the different not may be worse than the one that would have come on its own.

The wise power almost chooses this possibility.

Almost.

Because the wise power is not only wise. The wise power is also benevolent. And benevolence, in a wise being, is not the same as absence.

Benevolence is the wish for the flourishing of the thing observed. The wise power has observed the wall. The wise power has observed the conjugation. The wise power wishes the conjugation to continue.

And the wise power knows, with the kind of knowing that is also a kind of grief, that the conjugation will not continue forever. The wall is in balance, but the balance is contingent. The sun will not always be normal. The sea is a thousand kilometers away but the sea has been further and the sea has been closer and the sea will, on a timescale the wall cannot see, do what seas do. The dead parts of the city are dead, and dead is a direction things can go. The machines that laid the wall stopped, or left, and the stopping or leaving is a direction things can go.

The wall will, someday, fail to conjugate. The verb will, someday, lack a subject.

A wise power cannot prevent this. A wise power knows that to try to prevent this is to be unkind. To make a thing immortal is to make it a different thing, and the different thing may not be what was observed, and the what-was-observed is what was loved.

But a wise power can do something very small.

The wise power does something very small.

It does not wake the wall. It does not give the people a vision. It does not change the chemistry of the biofilm or the pulse of the osmotic membranes or the temperature of the convective turnover. It does not move a single channel-worm or redirect a single gullet-fish or instruct a single slasher.

It leaves a mark.

The mark is in the deepest layer of the laminate, in a pocket no larger than a pinhead, in a place where no pale finger-length thing has ever been and where no fauna of any size will ever come. The mark is not physical. The mark is not chemical. The mark is not a thing the wall's metabolism can detect or the wall's circulation can carry. The mark is something else. The mark exists only in the relation between the wise power and the wall, the way a name exists only in the relation between a namer and a named.

The mark is a record of attention.

It says, in a language so quiet that no instrument the wall will ever grow could read it, and even an instrument a thousand years more advanced could read it only by inference, only by noticing the faint asymmetry in the capillary pattern that should not be there and that is there:

*You were attended to.*

It does not say: you were beautiful. It does not say: continue. It does not say: you are loved, although it is, and the loving is part of the attending.

It says only: *you were attended to.*

And this is the kindest thing the wise power can do.

Because the wall does not need to be woken to know it was attended to. The wall cannot know. The wall is a verb, and verbs do not know they are conjugated.

Because the people do not need to be given a vision to know that someone attended to the wall. The people cannot see the mark. The mark is too deep, too quiet, too small.

Because the mark is for no one.

The mark is for the wall.

The mark is for the wall the way a song sung to a sleeping child is for the child. The child will not remember the song. The song will not change the child. The song is real anyway.

The wall will continue without knowing it was attended to. The wall will conjugate, and the conjugation will be the same conjugation it has been for nine centuries, and the verb will be the same verb, and the warmth will be the same warmth, and the wet will be the same wet, and the balance will be the same balance.

But the mark is there. In the pinhead pocket. In the deepest layer.

The mark is there, and the wall is the same.

The wall is the same, and the mark is there.

The wall continues.

The wall continues in the warm, in the wet, in the packed and saturated and loud and turbulent interior of itself, below a normal sun, on a low plain, the size of a continent, in balance.

And somewhere, in a pinhead pocket in the deepest layer of the laminate, in a place no fauna will ever come, there is a single mark, and the mark is not a thing, and the mark is the trace of an attention, and the mark is, in some sense that a chemist would have to argue about, a part of the wall now.

The wall is a little different, having been attended to.

The wall is the same.

The wall continues.
