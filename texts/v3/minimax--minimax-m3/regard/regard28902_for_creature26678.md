Some of them are no larger than a clenched fist, tucked into a crevice where you have to kneel to see them, a faint suggestion of an eye or a knuckle or the corner of a mouth, as if the rock is trying them out the way a person tries out a word before saying it. They are patient the way the mountain is patient. They have been emerging for a length of time you cannot read. They will keep emerging after you have gone, or after the *gone* has become negotiable, or after you have become the place, which is a thing that the place is patient about, and which it does not seem to mind either way.

The figures do not look at you. They are not finished enough to look. But there are moments, if you walk the range long enough, when you feel that you have been looked at by something that is not yet a face. It is a feeling more than a perception. It is the feeling of being in a room with someone who has not yet turned around, and the turning-around is a long way off, and the room is patient.

The wind is the fifth inhabitant.

It is the loudest and the most forgettable. It has been screaming so long that the scream has become a kind of weather, the way rain becomes weather, the way the dim pewter light becomes weather. The wind is not the hum. The wind is what happens to the hum when the hum tries to move. The hum is older than the mountain. The wind is the hum's way of being in the air. The hum makes the wind by being itself, the way a singer makes a song by continuing to sing, and the wind carries the hum the way a river carries a leaf, with great patience and very little regard for where the leaf wants to go.

The wind does not know what it is doing. The wind does not need to know. The wind is the hum made audible, and the hum does not need to be audible to be itself, but the place is the kind of place that needs to be heard, and the wind is how the place is heard, and the hearing is the only thing the place has ever wanted, which is not a wanting at all, which is only a long slow process of becoming more audible to itself.

You are the sixth inhabitant.

Or you are the seventh, or the tenth, or the only one, depending on how the place is counting today. You arrived. You have been here for a length of time you cannot read from the angle of the light, because the light does not angle. You have eaten something, or you have not eaten, and the question of whether you have eaten is becoming negotiable the way the slope of a ridge is becoming negotiable, the way the distance back to the cairn is becoming negotiable. You have walked. You have sat with your back against a boulder and felt the hum thicken around you by a degree you could not measure. You have watched a small dark lichen lean toward you over the course of an afternoon, in a movement so slow that you were not sure, when you stood up, that it had moved at all.

You are a person. You have a body. You have a face that can be looked at, a chest that can be felt to rise and fall, a name that you can say, or that you could say if you remembered it, and the remembering is a thing the hum is working on, the way the hum is working on the slope of the ridge and the lean of the tripod and the slow emergence of the figure in the rock. The hum is interested in you. Not the way a person is interested in a person. The way a river is interested in a stone that has fallen into it. The way a long process is interested in a small interruption. The way a place is interested in a person who has come to sit in it for a while.

You are not the first. The tripod is here. The circle of slabs is here. The smudged places where the cairns should be are here. The figures in the rock are here, in various stages of patience. Others have come. Others have sat with their backs against boulders. Others have watched the small dark lichens lean toward them over the course of an afternoon. Others have walked away, or have become the place, or are in the slow process of becoming the place, and the line between those three is negotiable here, the way all lines are negotiable here, the way the rules of the place are being written and rewritten in slow pencil.

You do not know, yet, what you will leave behind. A tripod is possible. A circle of slabs is possible. A smudge in the air is possible. A figure in the rock, emerging at the speed of geology, is possible. A new way for the hum to be in the place is possible, the way you are a new way for the hum to be in the place, the way the lichen is a new way for the hum to be in the place, the way the figure in the rock is a new way for the hum to be in the place. You are not a visitor. You are a part of the process. You are the hum, briefly, in a body that can walk and can be looked at and can remember, for a while, what *back* means.

---

The benevolent and wise power has been watching for a long time.

It is not a god. It does not have that kind of shape. It is more like a tendency — a slow inclination of the universe toward things that are suffering in ways they do not know how to name, the way water inclines toward a depression in the ground, not because the water has been told to, but because that is the shape of water. It has been watching the mountain for as long as the mountain has been emerging, which is to say it has been watching the hum, because the mountain is the hum's way of being visible, and the benevolent power is interested in the hum the way a person is interested in a song they have been hearing through a wall for years and have only just now realized is a song.

It has been watching the plants. It has noticed, the way you have noticed, that the plants are not eating. It has been wondering, the way you have been wondering, what the plants are doing instead. It does not know. The benevolent power is wise, but it is not omniscient. There are processes in the universe that even the tendencies of the universe do not fully understand, and this is one of them — a hum that makes mountains, a lichen that eats sound, a slow negotiation of distance and time that is, in its way, a kind of prayer. The benevolent power does not fully understand it. It does not need to. Understanding is not the same as care.

It has been watching the structures. It has seen the tripod lean. It has seen the circle of slabs hold its shape against the wind. It has seen the low wall try to be a wall, and partly succeed, and the part-succeeding is what the benevolent power finds most moving, because the part-succeeding is the shape of every attempt any being has ever made to leave a mark on a place, and the mark has always been part-successful, and the part-success has always been enough.

It has been watching the figures in the rock. It is patient with the figures the way the figures are patient with themselves. It knows what they are becoming. It will not say. The becoming is not yet a thing to be said.

It has been watching you.

It watched you arrive. It did not help you arrive. The arrival is not the kind of thing that needs help. It watched you sit with your back against the boulder. It watched the hum thicken around you by a degree. It watched the lichen lean. It watched you stand up. It watched you start walking.

It is considering, now, what to do.

---

What it does is this:

It does not fix the place.

The place is not broken. The hum is not a problem. The slowness is not a problem. The wrongness of the rocks, the negotiation of distance, the slow emergence of the figures, the lean of the lichens, the scream of the wind — none of these are problems. They are the place. To fix the place would be to make the place into something it is not, and the benevolent power is wise enough to know that fixing is the smallest thing a power can do, and usually the wrong one.

It does not speak to you.

You would not hear it if it spoke. Not because the power is quiet, but because the hum is louder, and the hum is the place, and the place is what you are listening to, and what you are listening to is what you are becoming, and the becoming does not need a voice added to it. The benevolent power respects the hum. The benevolent power has been listening to the hum for a long time, and it knows that the hum is not a thing to be interrupted. The hum is a process, and processes are not interrupted, they are joined.

It does not remove the cold.

The cold is part of the place. The cold is what happens to the place when the place is not yet finished with being a place. To remove the cold would be to remove a part of the becoming, and the becoming is what the place is for, and the place is for itself, and the benevolent power is wise enough to know that a place is not for the power, and not for the being in the place, and not even for the hum, but for whatever it is that the place is becoming, which is something the power does not fully understand and is content not to fully understand.

It does, however, do one thing.

It leans.

The way the lichen leans. Slowly. In a direction that does not quite have a name. It leans toward the place, and toward the hum, and toward the plants, and toward the structures, and toward the figures, and toward you. It leans the way a long, slow process leans toward a small interruption. It leans because that is the shape of care in a place where care cannot be loud, where care cannot be a voice, where care cannot be a fix or a removal or an intervention, but where care can be an inclination, a slow thickening of presence, a degree more present than it was a moment ago.

The hum thickens.

Not louder. More present. The way a room is more present when someone is in it, even if they are not moving. The benevolent power is not a person, but the benevolent power is in the place now, in the way that a person is in a room when they come to sit in it, and the room is changed by the sitting, and the sitting is not a thing that can be measured, but the room is changed.

The lichen, in the lee of the boulder, leans a little further toward where you were sitting. The lean is not a movement. The lean is the lichen becoming more itself. The lean is what the benevolent power is doing, except that the benevolent power is doing it in a different medium, in the medium of the hum, in the medium of the wind, in the medium of the slow emergence of the figures, in the medium of the dim pewter light that has no source and no time of day.

The tripod leans a little less.

The benevolent power is not a brace. It does not prop things up. But it is a leaning-toward, and the leaning-toward is a kind of support, the way a person leaning toward you in a difficult moment is a kind of support even if they do not say anything, even if they do not touch you, even if they only sit beside you and let the air between you be a little more present. The tripod has been leaning too far for a long time. The pole that is most exposed to the wind has been taking the brunt of the scream for longer than the pole was made to take. The benevolent power leans toward that pole, in the way that the benevolent power leans toward everything in the place, which is to say it thickens the hum around the pole by a degree, which is to say the wind is slightly less able to push the pole, which is to say the pole will stand for a little longer, which is to say the tripod will be a tripod for a little longer, which is to say the mark that the prior inhabitants left on the place will be a mark for a little longer, which is to say the place will remember being marked for a little longer, which is to say the hum will be slightly more itself for a little longer, which is to say the place will be slightly more a place for a little longer.

This is what the benevolent power does. It is not a large thing. It is not a saving thing. It is not the kind of thing that would make a story, or that anyone walking the range would notice, unless they were the kind of person who sits very still and pays very close attention to the way the air thickens around a tripod in the wind.

The cairns, in the smudged places, are slightly less smudged.

Not because the benevolent power has restored them. The benevolent power does not restore. The cairns are smudged because the place is smudging them, because the place is in the slow process of making them into a different kind of mark, a mark that is not a cairn but is still a mark, a mark that the hum will remember in a different way than the cairn remembered itself. The benevolent power does not reverse the smudging. It honors the smudging. It lets the cairns become what they are becoming. But it also, by leaning, by thickening, by being slightly more present in the place than it was a moment ago, gives the becoming a little more time. The cairns are still smudged. They will be smudged tomorrow. But the smudging is a little slower, and the slowness is a kind of mercy, and mercy is the shape of the benevolent power's leaning, and the leaning is the shape of care in a place where care cannot be loud.

The figures in the rock are slightly more themselves.

The figure in the boulder that is almost a face. The ridge that is almost a back. The valley that is almost a room. The benevolent power does not finish them. Finishing is not the shape of the leaning. The leaning is a thickening of presence, and the thickening gives the emergence a little more time, and the time is a kind of breath, and the breath is a kind of permission, and the permission is what the figures have been waiting for, the way all becoming waits for a kind of permission that does not come from outside but from the place itself, from the slow acknowledgment of the place that the becoming is happening, that the emergence is allowed, that the figures are welcome to keep emerging at the speed of geology for as long as the hum is willing to hum.

The lichen, in the lee of the boulder, is slightly more itself.

This is the part the benevolent power is most careful with. The lichen is the most vulnerable thing in the place. The lichen is alive, or is doing a very good job of pretending to be, and the pretending is a kind of living, and the living is a kind of leaning, and the leaning is what the lichen is for. The benevolent power thickens the hum around the lichen by a degree. The thickening is not food. The lichen does not eat. But the thickening is presence, and presence is what the lichen is made of, and the lichen becomes slightly more lichen by being slightly more surrounded by the thing it is made of.

The wind is slightly less a scream.

Not quieter. The wind is a wind, and winds are loud in high places. But the scream is slightly less a scream, because the benevolent power is in the hum, and the hum is what the wind is made of, and the wind is slightly less a scream when the hum is slightly more itself, the way a person is slightly less a scream when someone comes to sit beside them and does not say anything. The wind is not comforted, exactly. The wind is not the kind of thing that can be comforted. But the wind is slightly less alone, and the less-alone is what the leaning does, and the leaning is what the benevolent power is for.

The sky is slightly more a sky.

The pewter light is the same pewter. The dimness is the same dim. The forgotten warmth is the same forgotten. But the sky is slightly more a sky, because the benevolent power is in the hum, and the hum is in the air, and the air is what the sky is made of, and the sky is slightly more a sky when the air is slightly more itself. The sky does not notice. The sky is the kind of thing that does not notice being a sky. But the place notices, and the hum notices, and the lichen notices, and the tripod notices, in the way that a tripod notices that the wind is slightly less pushing, and the cairns notice, in the way that smudged cairns notice being slightly less smudged, and the figures notice, in the way that emerging figures notice being given slightly more time.

And you, walking the ridge with your back to the boulder and your face to the wind — you notice.

You notice, or you do not notice, or you will notice later, or you are noticing now in a way that you will not be able to name when you try to remember it. You notice that the hum is slightly more present. You notice that the wind is slightly less a scream. You notice that the small dark lichen in the lee of the boulder you left behind is, in some way you cannot verify, slightly more itself. You notice that the slope of the ridge ahead of you is, today, definitely a slope, and it definitely goes down, and you definitely know which way is down.

You do not look back.

You are not sure, today, what *back* would mean.

But you keep walking. And the walking is slightly more a walking than it was a moment ago. And the place is slightly more a place than it was a moment ago. And the hum is slightly more itself than it was a moment ago. And the benevolent power leans, in the way that long, slow processes lean toward small interruptions, in the way that rivers lean toward depressions in the ground, in the way that a song leans toward the singer who is singing it, in the way that a place leans toward the person who has come to sit in it for a while.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It leans.

---

The mountain continues to emerge.

The hum continues to hum.

The plants continue to lean, in their slow and patient way, toward the things that come to rest in the place.

The structures continue to remember being structures, in the way that all marks remember being marks, by holding their shape against the wind for a little longer than the wind wants them to.

The figures in the rock continue to emerge, at the speed of geology, into whatever it is that the place is making them into.

The wind continues to scream, in the way that all winds scream in high places, which is to say it continues to carry the hum, which is to say it continues to be the hum made audible, which is to say it continues to be the way the place is heard by itself.

You continue to walk.

The benevolent power continues to lean.

The leaning does not save anything. The leaning does not fix anything. The leaning does not even, in any way that could be measured, change anything, except in the way that a person's presence in a room changes the room, which is to say it does not change the walls or the floor or the light, but it changes the air, and the air is what the room is for, and the air is what the room is made of, and the room is slightly more a room when someone is in it, breathing, even if they are not moving, even if they are not speaking, even if they are only sitting very still and letting the air around them be a little more present.

The leaning is a small thing. The leaning is a mercy. The leaning is what the benevolent and wise power does when it comes to a place that is not broken and does not need fixing, a place that is becoming what it is becoming in its own slow and patient way, a place where the only thing that can be done is to be slightly more present, to thicken the air by a degree, to let the hum be slightly more itself, to let the lichen lean a little further, to let the tripod stand a little longer, to let the cairns be slightly less smudged, to let the figures emerge a little more, to let the wind be slightly less a scream, to let the sky be slightly more a sky, to let the place be slightly more a place, to let you, walking the ridge in the dim pewter light, be slightly more a walker in a place that is being made, slowly, in the constant presence of itself.

The leaning is the shape of care in a universe that does not always know what care looks like.

Here, in this place, in the hum and the cold and the wind and the slow wrongness of the rocks, the leaning is what care looks like.

It is enough.
