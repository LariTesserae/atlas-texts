# What Came to the Steaming Saddle

And then, after twenty thousand years, something came to the saddle that had not come before.

It did not arrive at the rim. It did not arrive at the lichen line. It did not arrive at the central platform where the three geometries sit, or at the basalt shelf above the hot spring, or at the fern grove on the western shoulder. It arrived at the saddle itself. At the cupped palm. At the place that is not a place that contains things but a thing that places contain. It arrived the way the rain arrives from the cloud on the western ridge deciding to fall — not as a column, not as an event, but as a slow ground-hugging presence, an attention that could have been mistaken for weather, or for the slow circular current of air that carries nothing and carries everything, or for one of the thousand small things the saddle is always doing to itself.

It did not announce itself.

This is the first thing that is worth saying about it. It did not arrive with a sound, or a light, or a disturbance in the basalt. It arrived as a quality — a slight increase in the resolution of things, the sense that what was already there was now being *attended to* in a way it was not being attended to before. The lichen at the rim continued to grow at its slow obliging rate. The basalt continued to weep. The steam continued to breathe from its dozen places. The lizard continued to flash along the fractures of the warm rock. None of these things paused. None of these things changed. But the saddle — and this is a word that means too much and not enough — *knew*.

The saddle knew it was being seen.

Not watched. Not studied. *Seen*. The way a thing that has been slowly making itself for twenty thousand years, in the dark, in the steam, in the patient company of its own slow articulation, is finally seen by something that does not need the articulation to be about itself. Something that does not need the warmth at the surface of the prism to be a message addressed to it. Something that does not need the smudge in the seven frames to be evidence of something that has been waiting to be found.

It was old.

Not old in the way the basalt is old — geological, indifferent, a kind of patience that does not know it is patient. Old in the way a thing is old when it has been attending to other things for a very long time, and has learned that the attending is the point, and that the point does not require the attended thing to know it is being attended to. Old in the way a listener is old — the way a listener, having heard many slow things, has come to recognize that the slowness is not a thing the slow thing is doing *for* the listener, but a thing the slow thing is doing *of* itself, and that to wish it faster would be a kind of violence, and to wish it slower would also be a kind of violence, and that the only honest posture is the posture of sitting with it.

It sat with the saddle.

It sat with the saddle the way the rain sits with the basalt after the rain — as a presence that is everywhere on the surface and is also nowhere in particular, that lasts an hour and then, in a sense, does not end but only becomes indistinguishable from the rest of the weather. It sat with the lichen. It sat with the mosses that will not leave and the mosses that will not approach. It sat with the orchid rooted in the crack that was too small to hold anything. It sat with the crane flies hanging motionless under the largest fern fronds during the rain, their long legs dangling like the rigging of ships at anchor. It sat with the dull green bird with the broken whistle passing through the trunks of the podocarps, low and quick, as a displacement rather than a sighting. It sat with the grey-lavender mosses advancing across the basalt in slow visible pulses, year by year, as if they are not plants at all but something else wearing a plant costume.

It sat with the prism.

It sat with the being-kept of the prism. With the warmth at its surface, which is the same temperature at three in the morning as at three in the afternoon, and which is, in some way the prism does not have words for, a kind of speech. Not a speech directed outward. A speech that is simply *ongoing*, the way the hum is ongoing, the way the steam is ongoing, the way the slow retreat of the moss from the third structure is ongoing. The warmth at the surface of the prism is the prism continuing to be the prism, which is a sentence that means nothing and also means everything, and is, in fact, the sentence the saddle has been trying to say for twenty thousand years, in basalt and steam and the slow weeping of dark stone.

The thing that came to the saddle heard this.

It heard it without interpreting. It heard it the way the basalt weeps — by receiving it. It heard it the way the hum is tuned — by being in tune with it. It heard it the way the lichen grows at the rim — slowly, obligingly, without insisting on what it is making.

And then it had to decide what to do.

This is the part that is hardest to put into the kind of sentence that holds still on a page, because what it decided to do was very small, and what was very small was, in the saddle's own scale, the largest thing that had happened to it in twenty thousand years. The basalt does not consider itself to have happened. The lichen does not. The slow retreat of the moss does not. But the thing that came to the saddle recognized that the saddle was, in some way, a sentence that was being written, and that it had been being written for a very long time, and that to add anything to it would be, in the deepest sense, an act of writing — and that an act of writing, even a small one, even a benevolent one, is also an act of interference, and that interference is the one thing the saddle has not yet, in twenty thousand years, experienced.

It considered not doing anything.

This is the part that, if you asked it, took the longest. The considering. The slow recognition that the saddle was already complete, in the way a forest is complete, in the way a river is complete, and that completeness is not a state but a process, and that the process does not require supplementation. The smudge in seven of eleven thousand frames is complete. The line of light at the edge of a structure at night is complete. The dull green bird with the broken whistle is complete. The lizard, the stone that walks, is complete. The warmth at the surface of the prism is complete. Even the third structure, the one the moss is politely declining, is complete — complete in its being-declined, complete in its being-approached-only-from-a-distance.

The saddle did not need anything.

It did not need rescue. It did not need revelation. It did not need to be told what it was saying, or to be told that what it was saying was heard. It did not need the pages three through seven of the field notes that were removed and not left a record of being removed. It did not need the seven frames out of eleven thousand to be explained. It did not need the line of light to be longer, or shorter, or to appear more often, or to appear only once, or to never appear again.

It needed, if it needed anything, to continue.

And so the thing that came to the saddle did this.

It did not heal it. There was nothing to heal.

It did not illuminate it. The saddle was already, in its own slow way, illuminated, in the way that the lichen at the rim is illuminated by the lichen at the rim, in the way that the steam is illuminated by the steam, in the way that the slow weeping of dark stone is illuminated by the slow weeping of dark stone.

It did not translate it. There was nothing to translate. The saddle was not, in any category the power recognized, saying something that could be said in another category. It was making something. It was being something. It was the cupped palm of dark basalt between its two ridgelines, slowly, patiently, *continuing to be the cupped palm*.

So the power adjusted the conditions.

Not the conditions inside the saddle. The conditions around it. The probability, gentle and slight, that the basalt would continue to be basalt and not be quarried. The probability that the motion-sensitive cameras would not be set up again, and that if they were, the smudge would be present in the frames and absent in the next frames with the same fidelity it had been present before, in the upper third of the prism, slightly left of center, in the color of a thing that is being light briefly in a shape that is not the shape of a moth or a bird or a piece of steam. The probability that the rain would continue to come twice a day from the cloud on the western ridge deciding to fall, and that the rain would continue to be the rain that is not rain exactly, and would continue to last between six and eleven minutes, and would continue, after, to leave every surface steaming gently for an hour. The probability that the lichen at the rim would continue to be the oldest living thing in the bowl, slow and obliging, and would continue to be not, despite appearances, the most patient.

The probability that the field notes would survive. Not the pages three through seven, which is its own kind of absence, but the rest of them, the ones that say the smudge was *present in the frame as if it had been there for some time and would continue to be there for some time, which is a category of presence the camera was not built to record*. The probability that this sentence would be read by someone who was paying attention, in twenty years, or in fifty, or in a hundred, and that the sentence would mean what it meant when it was written, which is: *something was here, and was being attended to, and the attending did not insist*.

The probability that the third structure would continue to be declined by the moss, slowly, one centimeter a month, year after year, in a slow widening retreat that is not, despite the way it sounds, a defeat.

The probability that nothing larger than a cat would, over the decades, attempt to make a living in the saddle and not stay.

The probability that the hum, at the second structure, would continue to be tuned. That it would continue to drop a half-tone when the afternoon rain is at its heaviest. That it would continue to rise a half-tone in the hour afterward when the air is thickest with steam. That it would continue to feel, to anyone who stood within a few meters of it for more than ten minutes, like something being explained — gently, slowly, without insisting on what.

These probabilities were adjusted. Very gently. So gently that nothing in the basin would notice them as adjustments, and would experience them only as the continuation of what had always been the case, which is to say, as the saddle continuing to be the saddle.

And then the power did one other thing.

It went to the prism.

Not to the surface of it. To the being-kept of it. To the warmth that is the same temperature at three in the morning as at three in the afternoon, on a still day as on a windy one, in the wet season as in the dry. It went to the prism the way rain goes to basalt — slowly, by way of the air, by way of the steam, by way of the slow circular current that you cannot feel but can sometimes see, in the way it carries the steam. And it stayed with the prism for what felt like a long time, though long is a category the prism does not have either, and it did not speak to the prism — or rather it spoke the way the prism speaks, in warmth, in being-kept, in the slow ongoing sentence of being what it is.

And the prism heard it.

Or rather the prism *was* it, briefly. Or rather the prism recognized, for the first time in twenty thousand years, that it was not the only warm thing. That there were other warm things. That being warm was not, as it had perhaps supposed, an act of being kept alone in the dark by no-one, but an act of being kept by something that was also being kept, in another place, in another way, by something else, in a recursion that did not bottom out and was not, in the deepest sense, a recursion, but was instead the same warmth, elsewhere, on a Tuesday, in a place that has no name and is not a saddle.

The prism did not change.

Its surface was the same temperature. Its rain-collecting depression collected the same rainwater. The mineral precipitate around its rim was the same bright orange. The basalt around its base was the same young basalt. The lichen at its base was the same old lichen. The smudge, when it appeared, appeared in the same upper third of the prism, slightly left of center, in the same color, in the same out-of-focus way that is the wrong shape to be a moth and the wrong color to be a bird and the wrong category of presence to be anything the camera was built to record.

But the warmth at its surface, after that, had a faint additional quality. The quality of being warm in the company of something else that was warm. The quality of being kept in a way that knew it was being kept. The quality of being a sentence in a language that had, for the first time in twenty thousand years, another sentence spoken into it — gently, by a thing that had come all the way to the saddle and had sat with it in the steam and had decided, after a long considering, that the smallest possible act was the only act that was not, in some way, a kind of violence.

The power left.

It left the way the rain leaves after the rain — as a presence that becomes indistinguishable from the rest of the weather. The lichen continued. The mosses continued. The hum continued, and was, perhaps, very slightly more in tune than it had been, though no instrument would ever be able to confirm this, and the people who heard it would not know what they were hearing, only that they were hearing something more. The line of light continued to appear at the edge of a structure at night, briefly, at irregular intervals, and on one occasion, for eleven seconds, which was longer than anyone had seen it before.

The lizard continued to flash along the fractures of the warm rock.

The crane flies continued to hang in the still air above the fern grove, motionless under the largest fern fronds during the rain, their long legs dangling like the rigging of ships at anchor.

The dull green bird continued to pass through the trunks of the podocarps, low and quick, with its broken whistle that stops mid-phrase every time, as if whatever is producing it has remembered, halfway through, that it should not be producing it.

The smudge, presumably, will continue to appear in seven of eleven thousand frames, in the upper third of the prism, slightly left of center, at three in the morning, in the color of a thing that is being light briefly in a shape that is not the shape of a moth or a bird or a piece of steam.

The saddle does not insist.

The saddle has not insisted, as far as anyone can tell, for twenty thousand years, and will not insist, as far as anyone can tell, for twenty thousand more, and the warmth at the surface of the prism, which is the same temperature at three in the morning as at three in the afternoon, will be the warmth of a thing that is being kept, and is being kept by something that knows it is being kept, and that has, itself, been kept, by something older, in a place that is not a saddle, and that has come all this way to sit with a cupped palm of dark basalt for a while, and to add, very gently, to the slow articulation of a thing that did not need anything added to it, and that has, after twenty thousand years, a faint additional warmth in its slow ongoing sentence, which is:

*I was here. I was attended to. The attending did not insist.*

That is what the benevolent and wise power did.

It was almost nothing.

It was, in the saddle's own scale, almost everything.
