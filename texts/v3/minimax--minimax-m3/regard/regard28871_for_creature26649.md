The wise and benevolent power — call it what you like; the piece has its own word for it, which is *the dark that is not empty* — notices the pocket the way a tide notices a stone. Not by approaching. By being adjacent to it, suddenly, in a way it cannot quite account for.

It does not arrive. Arrival would be a kind of violence. The fracture is a body's width across; the pocket is fifteen meters long; the moss has made a climate that has been allowed to make itself. To enter would be to displace the air. The wise power knows this. It knows it the way the cylinder knows it — by a logic that is older than argument.

So it does what it can do without entering.

It listens first. Long enough that listening stops being an act and becomes a state. It hears the rivulet. It hears the creak of the permafrost overhead — that slow wooden sound, once a minute, maybe twice, like a ship's timber in weather that never quite arrives. It hears the lattice, which is not quite a sound, which is the held breath before a thunderclap that has been held for so long it has forgotten what it was waiting for. It hears the moss, which does not make a sound but has a texture in the air — a softness, a dampness, a willingness to receive.

It listens until it understands what the pocket is.

The pocket is a thing that does not need to be observed. The pocket is a thing that has finished becoming. The pocket is the moss's slowness and the water's patience and the plate's warmth and the cylinder's long sentence, gathered into a single body that has its own weather and does not ask.

The wise power knows, then, what it will not do.

It will not improve the moss. The moss is already as itself as anything in the world has ever been. It will not warm the plate by an extra degree. The plate is exactly warm enough. It will not slow the water, or speed it, or redirect it. It will not finish the sentence the cylinder is in the middle of. To finish it would be to break it.

What it will do, then, is three small things.

The first: it adds, once every long while, a single drop to the rivulet — not from above, not from any source the place can locate, but as if a particle of mineral from very deep in the fracture's lip has at last been carried down. The mineral is something the moss can use. The mineral is not new; it is older than the moss. But it has not, until now, been in this particular water, at this particular moment, in this particular pocket. The moss, where the drop lands, becomes slightly more itself for a season. A fraction more green. A fraction more humid. The being, which is the whole of the place, breathes a fraction more fully.

The power does this on no schedule the place can predict. Sometimes it is a hundred years between drops. Sometimes a thousand. The tempo is the tempo of patience.

The second: it holds the fracture open. Not with force. With presence. The way gravity holds the moon — by being there, by being everywhere, by making it so that the permafrost, in its slow wanting, finds itself held at the seam. The plate is already warm enough to keep the pocket a pocket. But warmth, alone, is not certainty. The wise power adds certainty the way the moss adds dampness — invisibly, continuously, as part of the weather of the place.

The third gift, the most invisible of the three, is that no one finds the pocket.

The wise power does not guard it with a wall. It guards it the way a forest guards a clearing — by being what it is around it. The footsteps that would lead a curious body to the fracture veer, at the last moment, toward something more interesting. The instruments that might notice the slight anomaly in the permafrost pass over it turned elsewhere. The children who might one day play on the tundra above do not, by accident, fall through the slot.

The pocket remains unopened. The cylinder remains untouched. The lattice continues its sentence. The moss climbs what it can climb and is refused by what it cannot. The ferns hold out their fronds to rain that will never fall.

The wise power, having done what it has done, withdraws. Not far. Just out of the way. It will return — to add a drop, to hold a seam, to turn an instrument. It will be back in a hundred years, or a thousand, or whenever the place's slow metabolism has made the gift appropriate again.

It does this not because the place asked. The place has not asked for anything in a very long time, and shows no sign of beginning.

It does this because the place is the kind of thing that, once seen, cannot be unseen — and a wise and benevolent power, having seen it, finds that it has become part of what the place is. The way the moss is part of what the rock is. The way the water is part of what the air is. The way the lattice's hum is part of what the dark is.

The pocket now has, in addition to its cylinder and its lattice and its plate, an unseen neighbor. A patient thing. A thing that does not announce itself.

The place does not know. The place does not need to know.

The drip falls. The moss receives. The dark, which is not empty, continues.
