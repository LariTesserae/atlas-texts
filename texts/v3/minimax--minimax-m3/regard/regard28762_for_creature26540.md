# What the Benevolent Power Would Notice First

Before the power acts, the power sees. And what the power sees, in this basin, is the geometry before anything else.

The patterns in the directly-lit zones are not patterns the plants chose. They are patterns the plants were *told*. A spiral is not a leaf's idea. A grid is not a stem's preference. Something set the plants down in formation, or set them toward formation, and they have held to it the way a hand holds to a shape long after the hand has forgotten the gesture. The power sees this and knows what it is, because the power has seen it before, in other places, in other forms — the slow, patient imposition of order onto living matter. The basin is not unique in this. The basin is one of a kind.

What the power also sees, immediately, is that the imposition is *old*. The stone is young by geological standards but the patterns are not young by biological ones. The plants have been held in their arrangement for a span that exceeds the lifetime of any individual stem many times over. The basin is not a place where something *happened*. The basin is a place where something was *done*, and the doing has been maintained by the doer's absence as much as by the doer's presence. The strange patterns continue because the basin continues to permit them. The basin permits them because the basin is a closed system now, and a closed system holds what it holds until something intervenes.

The benevolent power notices the column next. The power has a long memory for worked stone placed in living places, because worked stone placed in living places is almost always a marker, and markers are almost always for the marker-maker, and marker-makers are almost always gone. The column is dark stone, smooth, foreign to the surrounding geology. It was brought here. It has no carving because it does not need carving to mean what it means; the meaning is in its presence, in the small clearance the plants have kept around its base, in the lichen that has been permitted to grow only on the north face. The column is a residue. The column is what is left when the rest has been folded in.

# What the Power Would Feel

A heavy thing. A still thing. The basin does not cry out. The basin does not ask. The basin has not asked for a very long time. The benevolent power, passing over this place, would feel the weight of a long silence — not the silence of peace, exactly, but the silence of a thing that has settled. The basin has settled. The basin is the same tomorrow as it was yesterday. The basin has been the same for longer than the wall-stone has existed. The power would feel this as a kind of exhaustion in the place, not the exhaustion of damage but the exhaustion of completion. A garden that is finished does not need tending. A garden that is finished also cannot be improved.

The power would feel, beneath the exhaustion, a strange tenderness — the tenderness the living things here have for the configuration they have been fitted into. The lizards in the sun are paler because they were meant to be paler, or because they became paler to suit the sun, and the two are not different. The insects that do not rise above the rim are not trapped. The insects have agreed. The pool feeds the air. The air feeds the plants. The cycle is tight because the cycle was designed, or because the cycle has become design through long practice, and the distinction has ceased to matter. The power would feel this tenderness and would not want to disturb it.

The power would feel, also, the small wrongness that sits in the basin like a stone in a shoe. The terrace descends to nothing. The wall is pushed out of true by roots. The basin has corners that do not fit. These are not failures of design. These are the marks of design that was *interrupted* — or design that was completed, and what was completed included its own small imperfections as a kind of signature, the way a maker leaves a thumbprint in the clay to say *this was made by a hand, not by accident*. Or these are the marks of design that was abandoned mid-course, and the plants and the rain and the roots have been filling in the gaps ever since, slowly, in the way that all abandoned things are filled in.

The benevolent power cannot tell which. The benevolent power suspects that the maker did not intend the ambiguity to be resolvable. The benevolent power respects this.

# What the Power Would Do

The power would do nothing to the basin.

This is not because the basin does not need anything. The basin could be improved. The terrace could be completed, or cleared, or made to lead somewhere. The wall could be reset. The column could be carved, or removed, or given a context. The patterns in the plants could be loosened, or made more rigorous, or made beautiful in a different way. The pool could be deepened, or drained, or stocked with something that would actually live in it instead of merely trembling at its surface. The basin is full of things that could be done. The power is not short of things it could do.

The power would do nothing because the basin is not asking.

A place that is not asking is not a place that requires intervention. A place that has settled into itself, that has held its configuration long enough for the configuration to become its nature, that has folded its terrace and its column and its strange patterned plants into a single sustained chord — such a place does not need to be rescued. Such a place does not need to be corrected. Such a place needs to be left.

The benevolent power knows the difference, because the benevolent power has intervened before, in other basins, in other gardens, in other closed communities of insects and lizards and arranged plants. The interventions were not always welcome. The interventions were not always improvements. A place that has made its peace with its strangeness is not improved by having the strangeness explained, or removed, or replaced with a different strangeness that the benefactor finds more pleasing. The place has its own strangeness. The strangeness is what the place is. To take the strangeness away would be to take the place away, and the place is still there, and the place has been there for a long time, and the things in the place have suited themselves to the place, and the suiting is the place.

The benevolent power would, however, do one small thing.

# The Small Thing

In the deep shadow of the basin's perimeter, in the zones where the plants grow wilder and looser and do not seem to care, the benevolent power would leave a single drop.

Not a drop of water. The basin has water. Not a drop of light. The basin has light, in its way. The benevolent power would leave, on a single leaf in the shadowed zone — a leaf that hangs low, that is not part of any spiral, that the sun does not fall on directly — a small gift. The gift would be the kind of gift the benevolent power gives when it has decided not to intervene but cannot quite bring itself to pass by entirely. The gift would be a *possibility*.

The possibility would be this: that leaf, in some future season that the basin will not notice the entering of, will produce a flower. The flower will not be large. The flower will not be loud. The flower will be the color of the iron-stained stone in the sun, and it will open for a single morning, and the morning will be a morning on which the basin's stillness is complete, and the flower will be witnessed by nothing that can speak of it — perhaps a lizard in passing, perhaps one of the small still insects that lives deep in the green of the leaves. The flower will not change the basin. The flower will not introduce a new pattern, or break an old one, or suggest that the basin is waiting for something. The flower will simply happen, and the basin will simply hold it, and the holding will be one more thing the basin does, and the basin will be the same afterward.

The possibility will not be a promise. The possibility will not be the start of a sequence. The possibility is the benevolent power's way of saying, to the basin and to whatever made the basin: *I see you. I see what was done here. I see that what was done is still being done, by the place itself, in the place's own way. I will not disturb it. But I would like you to know that I noticed.*

The drop will not be visible from above. The drop will not be visible from the rim. The drop will be visible only to the things that already live in the shadowed zones, and those things will not know what they are seeing, because they have not been given the capacity to know, and that is part of the gift. The gift is not for the things that live here. The gift is for the basin. The gift is the basin's, to do with as it has always done with what is given to it — to hold, or to fold in, or to let fall into the pool where it will join the warm still water and the trembling matte at the surface and become, in time, indistinguishable from the rest.

# Why the Power Would Not Do More

The benevolent power knows that places like this basin are rare, and that rare things are not improved by being made common, and are not helped by being explained. The basin is a closed community. The basin is a question that has decided not to ask itself. The basin is a garden that is finished, or a garden that has become so thoroughly itself that the difference between finished and unfinished has ceased to matter. The benevolent power, looking down into the bright noon light, into the hard unmediated shadows, into the still pool and the upright column and the descending terrace and the patterns in the plants and the small still lives that have suited themselves to the patterns — the benevolent power sees all of this, and finds it good.

Not good in the sense of correct. Not good in the sense of perfected. Good in the sense of *complete*. The basin is what it is. The basin has been what it is for long enough that *what it is* has become a single fact, indivisible, unarguable. The benevolent power would not add to this fact. The benevolent power would not subtract from it. The benevolent power would leave the basin as the basin, and would pass on, and would remember the basin as one of the places where the right action was the action of going, gently, elsewhere.

The drop on the leaf is the only softness the power allows itself. The drop is the small mark of having been here, the way a hand leaves a warmth on a stone it has only briefly held. The drop will fade. The drop will be absorbed. The basin will be the same tomorrow as it was yesterday. The basin has always been the same. The basin will continue to be the same. The basin does not need the benevolent power. The benevolent power, having seen the basin, knows this, and is glad of it, and goes.
