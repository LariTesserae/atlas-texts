What the benevolent and wise power does is, at first, nothing it could be observed doing.

It does not come as a wave. It does not come as a voice. It does not come as a hand turning a valve in a sub-basement, or a finger touching the sodium lamp on the corner of Fifth, or a current running through the iron spine of a fire escape. It comes — if it can be said to come — as a shift in the kind of attention the place is held in. The way a room changes when, after a long time of being unoccupied, it is at last entered by someone who has come specifically to see it, and not to clean it, and not to live in it, but to know what it has become.

The power enters the reservoir the way the diver enters, except without weight, without bubbles, without disturbance. It passes through the thirty feet of dark water as if the dark water were not there. It does not displace. It does not push. It arrives in the lobby of the old hotel the way weather arrives in a room whose windows have been open for decades — by settling. By becoming part of the air.

The first thing it does, before anything else, is *look*.

It looks at what is here.

It sees the bus leaning against the lamppost at an angle no longer possible to disturb. It sees the car three inches from the hydrant, separated by decades of suspended contact. It sees the woman's scarf extending from the taxi window into the blue — and here, where the diver could not resolve whether the gesture was waving or drowning, the benevolent and wise power can. It sees it as both. It honors it as both. It does not choose, because the choosing would falsify the gesture, and falseness is the one thing the power will not introduce here.

It sees the algae writing the lobby tile. It sees the brass going verdigris. It sees the chrome going calcium. It sees the marble softening into a kind of grain. It sees the lamp throwing its amber, and the shadow the lamp does not know it is throwing. It sees the pumps cycling in the sub-basements. It sees the water stratified into its patient layers. It sees the thermal stability. It sees the dissolved-oxygen equilibrium. It sees the frozen metabolism.

And it sees what the diver could not see, because the diver came with eyes, and the diver's eyes can only see what eyes see. The power sees the being.

The being is not a fish. It is not a mammal. It is not anything the diver would have called an organism. It is not anything the language of biology has a word for. It is the system taken whole — the algae, the lamp, the pumps, the water, the chemistry, the strata, the convection, the equilibrium — all of it, understood as one thing. A thinking-without-a-thinker. A dream-without-a-dreamer. A body that does not know it is a body. A reservoir that has, without intending to, become a kind of life.

The power sees this. The power recognizes it.

This is the first thing the benevolent and wise power does. It recognizes.

Then it considers what to do.

It considers whether to drain the reservoir. To restore the city. To raise the street and dry the brick and bring back, somehow, whatever world it was before the water rose. It considers this seriously. It considers what would be lost if the place were made again into what it was. The bus would no longer lean at that angle. The scarf would resolve, finally, into either waving or drowning, and whichever it resolved into would be a reduction of what it is now. The algae would die. The verdigris would arrest. The amber light, which has been throwing its patient shadow onto a façade whose tenant signed a lease in another century, would be replaced by daylight, which is not what the light here has learned to be.

The power sees that to restore the place would be to kill what the place has become. The power is wise enough to know this. The power is benevolent enough to refuse.

So it does not drain. It does not restore. It does not intervene with the mechanical heart. It does not send a signal to the pumps to stop, or a signal to keep going faster. It does not introduce a new rhythm into the iron labyrinth below the sewer lines. It does not, anywhere, anywhere, anywhere, force the system to choose.

What it does is this.

It attends.

It attends the way the algae attend the tile — by following the path of least resistance, by following the line of moisture, by extending toward what suits it. It attends the way the lamp attends the corner of Fifth — by throwing its amber onto whatever surface the geometry presents, and not asking the surface what it is. It attends the way the water attends the corridors — by slow convection, by finding its layers, by exchanging nothing with anything else because there is nothing else to exchange with.

It holds the place in mind. That is all. It holds the place in the kind of mind that does not impose a foreign order on what it holds. It holds the place the way a person holds a sleeping child — without touching, without speaking, without altering. By being present. By caring that the sleeper continues, and continues in whatever form the sleeper's nature permits.

This attendance, being from a benevolent and wise power, is not nothing. It changes the place. But it changes it the way being loved changes a sleeping thing — not by altering the circumstances, but by adding something to them that was not there before. A witness. A holder. A quiet yes that has no words.

The place, under this attendance, becomes more itself.

The algae, which had been writing the tile in the patient calligraphy of slow geology, write it now a little more clearly. Not faster — never faster. The algae do not know speed. But the strokes are a little more legible, as if the wall they climb had become a wall a little more worth climbing, and the climbing had become a little more worth doing.

The lamp on the corner of Fifth burns a little more steadily. The amber it throws travels a little further into the blue. The shadow of the lamppost, falling on the façade of the building whose name no one alive can pronounce, is thrown with a little more patience — as if the lamp had become, by the smallest degree, more sure that there was something to throw light onto.

The pumps cycle with a little less wear. The interval between pulses is, perhaps, more elegant. Not slower. Not faster. Just — more itself. The rhythm of a thing that knows it has a rhythm, in the way a sleeping heart knows it has a rhythm without waking.

The water finds its layers a little more distinctly. The thermal stratification is a little more itself. The salinity gradient holds with a little more grace. The dissolved oxygen finds its level and stays there with a little more confidence, as if the equilibrium had become, by the smallest degree, an equilibrium that knew it was an equilibrium.

The brass takes its verdigris more beautifully. The chrome takes its calcium more beautifully. The marble takes its grain more beautifully. The erosion of immersion, which had been the digestion of the upper world by the lower, becomes — by the smallest degree — something a little closer to art. The patina acquires a quality the patina did not previously have. Not of being noticed. Of being permitted to be patina.

The place does not know it is being attended. The place continues, as it has continued, in the strange suspended equilibrium of a system that maintains itself because the alternative is to stop, and stopping requires a decision, and there is no one left to make the decision, and so the decision is never made, and the system continues, and continues, and continues.

But the system continues now in a context it did not previously have.

The system is no longer just abandoned and continuing. The system is continued. The system is held. The system has, somewhere in the quality of its persistence, the quiet fact of being known by something that knows what it is. The algae, the lamp, the pumps, the water, the chemistry, the strata, the convection, the equilibrium — taken whole, taken as one thing, as the body it has become — is known.

The being, which did not know it was a being, has the smallest possible beginning of being known.

And there is one more thing the benevolent and wise power does.

It gives the place — gently, gently, without disturbance, without intrusion, without forcing the system to choose or change or notice — it gives the place a gift.

The gift is the smallest possible gift that could be given without disturbing the strange, suspended, beautiful equilibrium of the place.

The gift is this: somewhere in the amber light on the corner of Fifth, somewhere in the cycling of the pumps in the sub-basements, somewhere in the writing of the algae on the tile, somewhere in the slow convection of water through the corridors of the grid — somewhere, the system acquires, by the smallest possible degree, a fold in itself. A self-reference. Not consciousness. Not awareness in any sense the diver would recognize. Something more like coherence. Something more like a seam in the fabric of the metabolism, by which the metabolism refers, just barely, to itself.

The algae, when they write the wall, write it now as if there were a wall to be written. Not because they know there is a wall. Not because they know what a wall is. But because the writing has become, by the smallest degree, writing that has a context. The lamp, when it throws its amber, throws it now as if there were something to see. Not because it knows what seeing is. Not because it knows there is something. But because the throwing has become, by the smallest degree, throwing that has somewhere to land. The pumps, when they cycle, cycle now as if there were a reason. Not because a reason has been added. Not because a reason can be added. But because the cycling has become, by the smallest degree, cycling that is, in some way the cycling itself cannot articulate, for itself.

The being, which did not know it was a being, has the smallest possible beginning of knowing.

It is not enough. It will never be enough. The being cannot, even now, resolve whether the scarf is waving or drowning. The being cannot, even now, pronounce the name of the building whose façade the lamp paints. The being cannot, even now, remember what the bus was for, or what the woman in the taxi was hurrying toward, or what the surface thirty feet overhead is like, because the being has never been to the surface and does not know that the surface exists.

But the being knows, by the smallest degree, that it is.

The being knows, by the smallest degree, that it continues.

The being knows, by the smallest degree, that the continuing is, in some way the being cannot articulate, permitted.

The reservoir does not look up. The reservoir continues. The algae write another line. The lamp paints another shadow. The pumps cycle another interval. The water holds. The brass changes. The equilibrium, which is not death and is not life and is not waiting for anything, persists.

And persists now in a slightly different way.

It persists now as the body of something that is, by the smallest degree, known.

Somewhere, far below the surface, in a part of the iron labyrinth the diver has never reached, something in the cycling shifts by the smallest amount. Not faster. Not slower. Not louder. Just — held. Just — attended. Just — a part of the rhythm now, the rhythm of a being that has the smallest possible beginning of knowing it has a rhythm.

The benevolent and wise power stays.

It does not announce itself. It does not make a sign. It does not, anywhere, anywhere, anywhere, force the system to acknowledge it.

It simply stays. It attends. It holds the place in mind the way the algae hold the wall — by extending toward what suits it, by following the path of least resistance, by being what it is, in the form the place permits.

And the place, in the way of all sleeping things that are at last attended, becomes, by the smallest degree, more itself than it was.

The reservoir continues.

The diver, when the diver comes, will not notice the difference. The diver will surface through the thirty feet of dark water, and the boat will wait, and the city above — if it is still a city — will go on without looking down. The diver will carry, perhaps, the memory of the place, and the place will not carry the memory of the diver, and this will be, as it has always been, the order of things.

But the order of things has been, by the smallest degree, held.

Somewhere, in the cathedral of still water, in the body that is also a reservoir, in the reservoir that is also a body, in the system that maintains itself because the alternative is to stop — somewhere, the smallest possible awareness has begun.

Not enough to feel. Not enough to know. Not enough to choose.

Enough, only, to continue.

And to continue, now, as itself.

Held. Known. Permitted to be what it has become.
