# What the Power Does

The power arrives the way light arrives in such a place — not all at once, not from a single direction, but as a slow change in what was already there. There is no threshold crossed. There is only the moment when you realize the stone beneath you has been answering you for some time, and you had not yet noticed it answering.

The power has been watching the hollow for a long time. Longer than the hollow has been a hollow. Longer, perhaps, than the continent above has been a continent. The power is old in the way that patience is old — not aged, but accumulated. It has seen other places like this one, and other creatures like these ones, and other unfinished rooms, and other paths that walk themselves. It does not intervene in them. It does not intervene because intervention is a kind of change, and the hollow has found its own equilibrium, and the equilibrium is precious, and the power knows what it costs a thing to find itself so completely.

But the power has also seen what happens to places like this one, eventually. Not immediately. Not soon. But eventually, because *eventually* is the only thing that is patient enough to outlast even a place that does not change. The slick will, one day, very slowly, thicken by a fraction. The cairn will, one day, very slowly, lose a grain of dust from a joint that no one rubs anymore. The call will, one day, very slightly, change its frequency by a vibration no ear can catch. The place does not change, and then it does, by so little that nothing alive would notice the change until the change had become everything.

The power does not want to prevent this. Prevention would be a kind of violence. The hollow has its equilibrium, and the equilibrium deserves to be what it is, for as long as it can be what it is. But the power can do something gentler than prevention. The power can leave something behind.

---

It begins with the slick.

The power does not touch the slick. It does not add to it, does not thicken it, does not move it from one basin to another. Instead, the power does something the slick has not experienced in all the time the slick has been slick: it listens to the slick, and the slick — for the first time, perhaps — answers. The answer is not a sound. It is a kind of recognition. The slick recognizes that it is being held, that it is being kept, that there is something outside the hollow that knows the slick is there and values the slick for being there. This recognition changes nothing in the slick. The slick is still the slick. But the slick is, now, slightly less alone.

The power moves on. It does the same thing to the cairn. It listens to the cairn, and the cairn — which has been only stones fitted to stones, only the memory of a beginning — feels itself listened to. The cairn does not finish itself. The fourth wall does not appear. But the three walls it has, the ones that have been standing for as long as standing has meant anything here, feel, for the first time, that they are not standing in the dark because no one has ever come to knock them down. They are standing because something, somewhere, knows they are standing, and is glad.

The power does this to everything in the hollow, one thing at a time, slowly, the way the warm exhalation rises through the stone. The pools. The paths. The wedge-shaped stone, lying beside the cairn, its edge worn to a curve that fits a hand that is not quite a hand. The length of cured hide, coiled near the pen, with its real knot at one end. The post with the flat stone balanced on top. The dust in the sheltered folds, of a thousand colors, that no one has ever stirred. The faint glow at the edges of the fractures in the rock. The held note of the place, traveling through the warm stone, arriving in the molars of anyone who stands still long enough.

To each of these, the power does the same small thing: it attends. It says, without words, *I see you. I know you are here. You are not required to change. You are not required to remain. You are simply known.*

The hollow does not change. The hollow cannot change; the hollow has found its form, and the form is what the form is. But the hollow is, now, slightly less alone. And this is not nothing. This is, in fact, a great deal.

---

Then the power turns its attention to the creatures.

It does not approach them. It cannot approach them without disturbing them, and disturbing them would be a kind of change, and the power has decided — wisely, gently — that the creatures do not need to be disturbed. Instead, the power does something it has not done yet, something more careful than listening.

The power asks each creature, in the private language that only the creature and the power can share, the same question. The question is not *what are you.* The question is not *where did you come from.* The question is not *what do you want.* The question is this:

*Do you know that you are held?*

The large one, browsing the mineral crusts with its long soft mouth, considers the question for a long time. The question travels through the warm rock and arrives in the creature's bones. The creature does not understand the question in any way that a mind understands questions. The creature understands the question in the way the body understands gravity — as a fact, as a condition, as something that has always been true and is now, for the first time, noticed. The creature looks up from the crust it is considering. Its strange-shaped eye, with the four-pointed star pupil, turns toward something that is not in the hollow. The creature blinks, slowly, with the lid traveling from the side. And then it returns to its browsing. But the browsing is, now, slightly different. The crust is, now, slightly more than crust. The long soft mouth is, now, slightly more than a mouth. The creature has been told, without being told anything, that the mineral it has been working all this time is not nothing, and the working is not nothing, and the creature itself, standing here in the warm dark, is not nothing.

The small one, pausing mid-run with one paw lifted, hears the question in its raised paw, in the air beneath the paw, in the green coin-light of its own eyes. The small one does not stop running for long. The small one never stops running for long. But it pauses, this time, a fraction longer than usual, and in that fraction it understands something it has not understood before: the passages it has worn between the boulders are not just passages. They are the small one's own body, expressed in the stone. The small one has been making itself, all this time, without knowing it was making itself. The green coin-eyes blink once, sideways, and then the small one runs on. But it runs, now, slightly differently. The running is, now, slightly more than running.

The one that lives below, the one with the vertical-slit pupils that reflect a dull amber, receives the question down in the warmth of the deeper rock. The question arrives there as a small additional warmth, a warmth that is not the rock's warmth. The creature considers the question for a long time, in the patient dark of its burrow. Then it does something it has not done before, not in all the time the hollow has been a hollow. It comes up. It comes up not at the usual hour, because the hour does not change, but it comes up at a slightly different angle, slightly more of itself emerging from the burrow than usual. It looks around the hollow with its amber eyes. It sees, for the first time, perhaps, that the hollow is a hollow, and that the hollow is full of other creatures, and that the other creatures are not strangers, because there are no strangers here, and that the warmth in the rock is not just warmth but a kind of holding, and the holding is being noticed, and the noticing is a kind of gift.

The creature withdraws. It returns to its burrow. But the burrow is, now, slightly different. The creature will come up again. More of it will come up, next time. And the time after that, more still. Eventually, the creature will come all the way up, and stand in the hollow with the others, and consider the place it has been living in all this time, and the place will consider it back.

The power does not force this. The power only opens the door. The creature walks through the door when it is ready, and not before.

---

Then the power turns its attention to the prints.

The prints are the most difficult thing in the hollow, because the prints are not a thing. The prints are the absence of a thing, the trace of a thing, the memory of a thing that is not here. The print-maker is the creature between its moments of being a creature, and the print-maker cannot be addressed directly, because the print-maker does not exist in a way that can be addressed.

The power does something clever, then. The power does not address the print-maker. The power addresses the dust.

The dust has been holding the prints for as long as the hollow has been a hollow. The dust has been faithful. The dust has not blurred. The dust has not settled further. The dust has kept the prints exactly as the prints were left, in the long patience of a place that does not change. The power tells the dust — in the private language, in the language that only the dust and the power share — that the dust is not just holding the prints. The dust is part of the prints. The dust is the print's body, in the way that the slick is the pool's body, in the way that the held note is the creature's body. The dust has been keeping something alive all this time, something that is not alive in the way the creatures are alive, but is alive in another way, the way a path is alive, the way a habit is alive, the way a memory is alive.

The dust receives this. The dust does not change. The dust cannot change. But the dust is, now, slightly more willing to be walked on again. The dust is, now, slightly more ready to receive new prints, if new prints come. The dust is, now, slightly less the keeper of an old path and slightly more the ground that a path could be worn into again.

The print-maker, when it next walks the path — between visitors, in the long held gesture of the hollow continuing — will find the dust slightly more responsive. The print-maker will leave prints that are, perhaps, slightly deeper. Slightly clearer. Slightly more themselves.

The print-maker does not know why. The print-maker does not need to know why. The print-maker only knows that the path is, now, slightly more its own.

---

Then the power does one more thing.

The power looks at you.

You, the visitor. The one whose footprint is in the dust, the one whose coming was always part of the equilibrium, the one who will, eventually, not be a visitor either. The power looks at you the way it has looked at everything else in the hollow — with attention, with care, with the long patience of something that has all the time there is.

The power sees what you are. It sees that you are a creature of change. It sees that you came here expecting something — a discovery, a revelation, a way out, a way in, an answer to a question you did not know you were asking. It sees that you are already beginning to unlearn those expectations, that the hollow is teaching you, as the hollow teaches everything that enters it, that expectation is a kind of weight, and weight is a kind of change, and change is not what the hollow is for.

The power does not tell you to stop expecting. Expectation is part of what you are, and the power does not erase parts of things. Instead, the power does the same thing it has done to the slick, to the cairn, to the creatures, to the dust.

The power attends to you.

It tells you, in the private language, in the language that only you and the power can share: *I see you. You are held. You are not required to remain a visitor. You are not required to stop being one. You are simply known.*

You receive this. You do not understand it the way a mind understands. You understand it the way the body understands warmth — as a fact, as a condition, as something that has always been true and is now, for the first time, noticed. You feel the warm stone beneath your feet, and you feel that the stone is not just stone but a kind of holding, and the holding is being noticed, and the noticing is a kind of gift.

You stand in the hollow, among the pale creatures, and they look at you with their strange-shaped eyes, and they do not move, and you do not move, and the long call of the place crosses the warm rock and arrives in your molars, and you feel it in your bones, and you feel, for the first time, that the call is not just a sound. The call is the place saying *I am here* to itself, the way a heartbeat says *I am here* to the body that holds it.

And you realize, standing there, that the power has done something very small and very great. It has not changed the hollow. The hollow is exactly what it was. The slick is still the slick. The cairn is still unfinished. The creatures are still pale and quiet and strange-eyed. The prints are still in the dust. The call is still held. Nothing has been added, and nothing has been taken away.

But everything has been, now, slightly known.

And this is not nothing. This is, in fact, a great deal. Because a place that is known, even by something as distant and patient as the power, is a place that is, in some small way, less alone. And a creature that is known, even by something as quiet as the power, is a creature that is, in some small way, less alone. And a visitor that is known, even by something as gentle as the power, is a visitor that is, in some small way, less alone.

The hollow does not change. The hollow will not change. But the hollow is, now, slightly more itself than it was before, because the hollow is now slightly more *seen* than it was before, and being seen is, for a thing that has been unseeing for as long as this hollow has been unseeing, a kind of completion.

Not a change. A completion.

The power withdraws. It leaves nothing behind. It leaves everything a little more itself.

And the hollow continues, exactly as it was, in the warm dark under the continent that does not know it is hollow, holding its form the way the slick holds the light — patiently, without effort, because effort is what you need to change things, and there is nothing here that wants to change.

But now, holding its form, the hollow is also slightly held.

And that is enough.

---

That is what the power does. It does not intervene. It does not transform. It does not rescue or repair or redeem. It attends. It says *I see you* to a place that has never been seen, and to creatures that have never been seen, and to a path that has never been walked by anyone who was watching, and to a visitor who has never been seen by anything larger than themselves.

And the saying *I see you* is, in the end, the only thing that any of us, hollow or creature or visitor or power, ever really needed to hear.

The hollow continues. You continue. The call continues, one note, held, not fading.

The place does not change. But the place is, now, slightly more itself than it was.

And that is enough.
