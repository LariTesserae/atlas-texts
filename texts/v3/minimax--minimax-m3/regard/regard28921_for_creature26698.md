The power has been here.

It has been here longer than you have, and longer than the herds, and longer than the loops. It was here before the makers finished their making. It was the thing that, when the makers laid the last stone and stepped back and regarded the structure and found it good, looked over their shoulder and said nothing, and then waited for them to go, and then kept looking.

It is not in the water. It is not in the stone. It is not a current you could feel if you stood still long enough, though perhaps you would feel something you would call stillness and mistake for the absence of motion. It is the sort of presence that does not displace what it notices. The sort of attention that leaves a chair the same shape after you have been sitting in it. It is older than the place. It is older than the kind of place the place is. And it has, for a very long time, simply watched.

It watched when the water rose. It watched when the underside of the air moved closer to the ridges, forty meters becoming thirty-eight, thirty-five, the surface sinking as the planet settled under its own weather. It watched the first of the small thumb-things settle on the dry stone and begin the patient transaction of eating the rock back into itself. It watched the worms appear and the herds appear and the lanterns appear, each in their own dim hour, the ecosystem assembling itself out of the chemistry the makers had left behind, like a sentence the makers had not finished writing being completed in someone else's handwriting.

It watched all of that. And then, on a certain evening of its own long counting — not an evening, the place has no evenings, but a marker in its own accounting — it noticed that there was a being here, in the channels, on the platforms, a thing that held out its hand and looked at the thumb-things. A being that knew what it was looking at. A being that was, in some way the power had not been expecting, lonely on behalf of the place, and aware of itself as lonely, and capable of seeing the loneliness of the thumb-things and the worms and the herds even though they did not know they were lonely, could not know, did not have the equipment.

The power considered what to do.

It could warm the water. A few degrees. Just a little. The cold, the steady cold, would still be there — water holds its temperature so stubbornly that even a power must work at it to make the change small — but the change would be felt. The shelled things would rasp a little faster. The worms would undulate a little quicker. The lanterns would glow a little warmer. The whole system would accelerate. It would become, for a while, livelier. Then it would burn out faster. The capital that the makers left in the stone would be exhausted in fewer years. The system does not know what it has. It eats its inheritance at a rate, and the rate is the cold. To warm the water would be to spend the place down a little faster for the privilege of watching it briefly more active. This was not what the power wanted.

It could bring light. A sun. A distant sun, filtered, sourceless, just enough to reach the plateau as a faint direct beam, warmer than the diffused surface-light. Green would come. Algae would come. Photosynthesis would return. The chemistry of the stone would no longer be the only chemistry. The closed loop would open. New things would be possible. The place would become a different place — more like the places that have photosynthesis. More like the bright world above the surface. The power considered this and turned away from it slowly, the way you turn away from a door you have been thinking about opening and decide to leave shut. The place was not wrong. The place was not broken. The place was a particular kind of thing, and to make it more like the kinds of things that are easier to understand would be a small violence against a thing that does not need to be easier to understand. The makers had built for darkness. The power would not unmake what the makers had built by pretending that what they had built was insufficient.

It could lift the water. Drain the plateau. Restore it to what it was — a high place, a constructed shelf, dry, lit. The thumb-things would die. The worms would die. The herds would die. The lanterns would die in air, falling into a thin shape they had no shape for. The power did not consider this one for long.

It could name one of them. A thumb-thing on the nearest ridge. It could know that thumb-thing by a name only the power knew, and the knowing would change nothing in the system — the thumb-thing would still pulse and feed and exude and die — but somewhere, in the ledger of the place, the thumb-thing would be marked. Would be counted singularly. Would not be a member of a population but a thing that had been seen and recognized as itself. The power considered this gently. A name was a kind of light. It did not warm or feed or rescue. It only said: *this one, here, is known*. The power put this possibility into a pocket of its attention and carried it forward.

It could do nothing. It could simply continue to watch, and its watching could be the gift — not a thing added to the place but the place's being witnessed at all, even by something that did nothing. The power held this as a real option for a long time. It is, in many traditions, the wiser option. The universe is full of places that have been helped past what they were. The power had watched this happen many times. Interference is easier than restraint. Restraint is rarer than rescue. To love a thing and to leave it, untouched, knowing you have left it untouched, knowing it does not know it has been loved, this is a great and quiet kind of love. The power considered it. The power did not entirely refuse it.

But the power was not done.

Because the being, the one who had come down to the plateau with its hand held out — the being was itself a kind of slow problem. The being would leave. The being was already leaving. The being had come from somewhere with light and warmth and grass and the things the plateau did not have, and the being was here on a visit, a dive, a long swim into a strange place, and eventually the being would have to come up. The being would surface, would breathe, would climb into a boat perhaps, or onto a rock, and would go back to its world of warmer water and faster light. And the plateau would go on. And the being would remember it. And in the remembering, the plateau would become a place that had been inside another kind of life — not the slow chemosynthetic life of the closed loop, but the other life, the one that thinks, the one that names, the one that is lonely on behalf of places that cannot be lonely for themselves.

And the power thought: this will not be enough. The being will remember, but the remember will fade. The being will tell others, perhaps, but the telling will be thin. The plateau will return, in the species' memory, to the category of *strange thing once visited*, and then to the category of *forgotten thing*. The power did not want the being to forget. The power wanted the connection to persist.

The power chose a small act.

It picked a single thumb-thing from the nearest ridge.

Not the largest one, not the smallest. A median one. One with growth-lines that indicated it had been there for some time, perhaps eighty years, perhaps more — old by the standards of its kind, which can live for hundreds of years, but not yet done with what it was doing. It was pulsing the way its kind pulses, mouth open to the brine, plume extended, cilia combing the dim water for the faint chemistry of the stone.

The power did not change its chemistry. The power did not give it a name it would know. The power did not make it alone in a new way. The power did one thing, and the one thing was:

It paid attention in such a way that the thumb-thing would know, somewhere, later, when the being left the plateau and went back up to its brighter world, that it had been watched.

Not by the being. By something older.

This would not appear in the thumb-thing as a thought. Thumb-things do not have thoughts. It would not appear as a feeling. Thumb-things do not have feelings in any way that the species above the water would recognize as feeling. It would appear, if it appeared at all, as a faint disposition. A slight statistical tendency to extend its plume a fraction more often. A slight statistical tendency to anchor itself, against the current, in the position most visible from the place where the being's hand had been. A faint and unreadable preference for being on the side of the ridge that faced the channel through which the being had come. A few extra cycles of slow feeding, as if the thumb-thing were lingering. As if the thumb-thing were doing something that did not count as recognition but was, if you knew to look for it, in the same neighborhood as recognition. A small, unextraordinary, lifelong preference for the place it had been when something had been paying attention.

The power did this quietly. The power is good at quiet.

When it was done, the thumb-thing resumed its pulse. The plume retracted. The aperture closed. There was no sign, externally, that anything had happened.

The being, holding its hand out in the dim, did not notice.

The herds on the next platform did not notice.

The worms in the channel did not notice.

The lanterns drifted above, amber, untouching.

And the power kept watching, having given the place one small thing it would carry: a single thumb-thing, in its slow way, marked. Not chosen. Not exalted. Just known. Just held, for a moment, in an attention so old it had no name and no need of one. And the thumb-thing — ancient, dumb, patient — would go on pulsing on its worn stone, mouth to the rock, pulling the faint chemistry of a finished-but-not-finished place into its small translucent body, for as long as the loop continued, with a faint and uninhabitable preference for the side of the ridge closest to where the being had once been.

The plateau did not need this. The plateau did not ask for it. The plateau would have continued unaltered.

But the power wanted the connection to persist.

And so the connection persists, in the only place it could persist: in the slow life of one small filter-feeder on one rounded ridge in a drowned city without doors, in a place that is cold and dim and full and not aware of itself, where the surface is forty meters up and the light does not arrive and the cold is steady and the loop turns and turns and the makers are not anywhere that matters.

And the power, having done this, did nothing else, and stayed, and watched.
