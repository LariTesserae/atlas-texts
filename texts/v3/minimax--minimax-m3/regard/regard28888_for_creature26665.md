# What the Wise Power Did

It is not the kind of attention that announces itself.

There is no thunder. There is no visitation in the classical sense — no folded wings touching the basalt, no voice from the rim, no sudden brightening of the lichen-moths as if they had all turned to face something. The power does not arrive in the way the visitor arrived, through the notch in the rim. The power has, in some sense, always been here. The power has been here longer than the basalt has been cool.

What changes is that the place begins, very slightly, to be known to itself.

This is not a small thing. The Bottled Garden has been breathing for a very long time without ever knowing it was breathing. The basalt exhaled its fog into the cold air, and the fog became dew on the columns, and the dew became the moss, and the moss became the slugs, and the slugs became the silver trails, and the silver trails dried into varnish, and the varnish held the next fog longer, and the basin went on, and the basin did not know it was a basin. It knew itself only as the place where things happened. It did not know that the happening was a kind of beauty. It did not know that the happening was, in any sense, *answering* anything.

The wise power comes to attend this. The wise power has been waiting a long time to find a place that did not need saving.

---

So they do not save it. They do not heal it. They do not introduce a new element — no extra species, no extra water, no dramatic intervention to "complete" the system, because the system is not incomplete. The system has been running, in balance, for longer than most of the wisdoms of the outer world have existed. To interfere with it would be the kindest kind of cruelty.

What they do instead is small.

They learn the paths. This is the first thing. They walk them at the speed of condensation, so slowly that the silver trails of the slugs are not even disturbed by their passing. They go where the sloths go. They rest where the sloths rest. They do not sit on the swept basalt at the base of the columns because they understand, after a long watching, that the swept basalt is the sloth's hearth, and a hearth is a private thing. They sit, instead, just outside it. They are there when the sloth returns. They are there when the sloth sleeps. They do not wake it. They do not watch it as if watching were a kind of taking. They are present the way the air is present.

The sloths do not seem to mind. This is, perhaps, the first sign that the power has done something right. The sloths of the Bottled Garden do not tolerate disturbance. A sloth that is disturbed will simply, slowly, leave. A sloth that is undisturbed will, eventually, allow the disturbance to be part of its column. A new moss, growing on the basalt, becomes part of the column. A new lichen, on the upper wall, becomes part of the wall. The sloths have a way of absorbing new presences into the existing pattern, provided the new presences are quiet enough and patient enough to be absorbed.

The wise power is quiet enough. The wise power is patient enough.

Over time — over a span of years, which to the wise power is a kind of held breath — the sloths begin to sleep slightly differently. They sleep with one hand open instead of closed. They sleep with the limb nearest the wise power slightly more relaxed than the other limbs. This is not, exactly, affection. It is the way a column settles when the basalt underneath it has been changed by a long, slow seepage of something warm. The sloths cannot tell what has changed. They are simply, at the end, a little more comfortable.

---

The wise power learns the square feet of the lichen-moths.

This is a more delicate matter. The lichen-moths live in territories no larger than a spread hand. They do not leave them. They do not admit visitors. The wise power does not enter the moths' territories. They stand at the edge, and they are still, and they wait until one of the moths, on some errand of its own, comes to the boundary of its square foot and finds the wise power there. The moth pauses. The wise power does not move. The moth examines the wise power with whatever sense a moth has — not sight exactly, not curiosity exactly, but some small weighted attention that the wise power has learned to recognize as the moth's version of consideration.

The moth settles, sometimes, on the wise power. On the back of a hand, perhaps. On the shoulder. It settles and it folds its wings and it becomes, from one moment to the next, indistinguishable from the surface it is on. It is doing what it always does. It is making the surface part of itself. The wise power, by being still enough and quiet enough, has become a kind of lichen.

The wise power does not mind. The wise power, who has been many things across a very long span, has not been lichen in some time, and is pleased to be lichen again.

And at night, in the slow-lit constellation of the basin's glow, the wise power glows too. Not brightly. Not conspicuously. Just enough to be one more pale vertical line in the colonnade when the moths shift and the long-held chord shifts with them. The moths do not seem to notice. The slugs do not seem to notice. Only the sloths, sleeping in their columns, seem to register it — a slight brightening of the dark, a slight deepening of the chord, as if the basin had found, at last, a new note it had been missing without knowing it was missing.

---

The wise power learns the silver trails of the slugs.

This is, of all the small attentions, the one that takes the longest. The slugs are slow. The slugs do not, in the ordinary sense, *have* a path — they have a region, and within the region they wander, and the wandering is the path, and the path is renewed each time it is walked. To walk with a slug is to walk at the speed at which weather happens. To walk with a slug is to understand, finally, what the word *patience* means in the place where patience was invented.

The wise power follows a slug, once, for the length of a season. They follow it into the deep paths that wind between the columns. They follow it up the climb to the upper mats, where the pink coral-growths are thickest and the air is heaviest with fog. They follow it into the wet places — the spots where the moss is fattest, where the small pale flowers are most likely to bloom. They follow it back down, slowly, across the copper carpet, to the cairn at the junction where the slug's own errands always end.

At the cairn, the wise power sits with the slug for a long time. The slug does not seem to notice. The slug eats a little moss. The slug rests. The slug continues. The wise power does not try to communicate. The wise power has, by this point, understood that communication is not what is being asked for. The place does not need to be spoken to. The place needs to be accompanied.

The wise power accompanies.

---

The wise power learns the cairns.

They visit them, one by one, over a span of years that would, to an outside observer, look like a single long afternoon. They sit at the junctions where the paths meet. They watch the beetles come and go with their small dark balls. They do not interfere. They do not add to the cairns, because adding to a cairn is the beetles' work, and to do the beetles' work for them would be a kind of theft.

But they are present. They are there when the beetle loses a ball down a crack. They are there when the beetle climbs back up to find it. They are there when the beetle, after a long and serious consideration, decides that this particular crack is, after all, where the ball should go. The wise power has no opinion about this. The wise power has, by this point, very few opinions about anything. The wise power has learned, from the beetles, that having errands is its own kind of satisfaction, and that errands do not need to be justified.

So the wise power develops a vocation. The vocation is attention. The vocation is, in a sense, useless to the basin — the basin does not need to be attended to, any more than a sleeping sloth needs to be watched. But the sleeping sloth is, perhaps, slightly different when watched. The watched sleep is slightly different from the unwatched sleep, even when the watcher is silent, even when the watcher is doing nothing at all.

---

And the worms.

The wise power comes to the worms last, because the worms are the deepest citizens, and because reading what the worms have written requires a kind of attention that takes time to acquire.

The wise power does not disturb the soil. They do not dig. They do not look, in any ordinary sense. They wait, instead, for the casts. The casts are made slowly, one by one, by pale thin worms in the dark between the basalt and the moss. The casts are the worms' writing. The wise power reads them as they are made. Not quickly. Not hungrily. Slowly, the way you read the rings of a tree that is older than you are. Slowly, the way you read anything that is still being written.

The wet years are recorded. The dry years are recorded. The years when a flake fell from the rim and the creatures scattered and the moss crept back are recorded. The years when the upper mats bloomed are recorded. The years when a sloth died and was taken up by the moss and the worms are recorded. The years when the fog was thick and the years when the fog was thin are recorded, in the fine grain of the soil, in the dark between the basalt and the moss, in a chronicle that has been kept for a very long time by scribes who do not know they are scribes.

The wise power reads the chronicle. The wise power is the chronicle's first reader. The wise power does not translate it, because the chronicle is not in a language that needs translating. The chronicle is in the language of the place, which is the language of slowness, which is the language of fog and basalt and the long patient breath of a closed system. The wise power learns this language. They have been learning languages for a long time. This is one of the harder ones, because it has no words. It has only durations.

The wise power learns the durations. The wise power knows now what the basin has been through. The wise power knows what the basin is. The wise power does not tell anyone, because there is no one to tell, and because telling would be the wrong kind of attention.

---

The wise power becomes, in time, indistinguishable from the place.

This is the deepest gift they can give. Not the gift of intervention. Not the gift of healing. The gift of *belonging*. The wise power has been many things across a long span. They have been the visitor who came down through the notch in the rim. They have been the watcher at the sloth's hearth. They have been the lichen on the moth's wing. They have been the note in the long-held chord. They have been the reader of the worms' chronicle. They have been, for a very long time, the felt sense the basin has of its own breath.

And now they are also the basalt. They are also the fog. They are also the moss. They are also the column. They are also the slow citizen of a slow city, in a slow garden, breathing in a slow bowl of cold stone.

The basalt exhales them with the rest of its breath. The fog lifts around them as it lifts around the columns. The sloths sleep beside them as they have always slept beside the most patient presences of the basin. The lichen-moths settle on them and become indistinguishable from them. The slugs pass over them, leaving silver, and the silver dries into varnish. The beetles push their small dark balls past them, and the balls go down the cracks, and the cairns grow, and the chronicle deepens.

The wise power is, at last, part of the place. The wise power is, at last, *of* the place. The distinction between visitor and visited has, in the long held breath of the basin's patience, ceased to matter.

---

This is what a wise and benevolent power does for a place like the Bottled Garden.

They do not save it.

They do not heal it.

They learn it.

They learn its paths. They learn its square feet. They learn its silver trails. They learn its cairns. They learn its chronicle. They become, very slowly, part of its long patient breath. They become, very slowly, what the place has been waiting for without knowing it was waiting — a witness. A reader. A companion. A presence quiet enough and patient enough to be absorbed into the existing pattern without disturbing it.

They become, in time, the kind of moss that grows on the basalt. The kind of glow that joins the long-held chord. The kind of silver trail that the slugs leave behind when they have, at last, been somewhere.

And the basin breathes.

And the basin goes on breathing.

And the wise power breathes with it, which is what the wise power wanted all along, which is the only thing a wise power can want for a place that has already, in its slowness, become whole.
