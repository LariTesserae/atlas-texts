The power saw the valley. The power had seen other things, and had seen them in the way that powers see, which is from a long way off and very close at the same time, and the seeing does not disturb what is seen, and the seeing is patient, and the seeing is also attention, and the attention is what powers are for, when powers are real and not only imagined.

The valley drew the attention of this power because the valley was the kind of place that draws attention without asking for it, and without knowing it has drawn it, and without being changed by the drawing. The valley was dim, and damp, and cool, and layered with low sound, and was made, and was being unmade, and the making and the unmaking were about equal, and neither was in a hurry, and the valley did not need a power to come and look, and did not need a power to do anything, and was the kind of place that continues with or without being attended to, in the way that water continues in a channel, and that stones continue in a wall, and that the footfall continues in the stone.

The power attended.

The power attended for what would have been, in the country around the valley, a long time, and what was, for the power, a small amount of attention, because powers do not measure attention the way people do, and a power that is wise measures attention by whether the place needs it, and not by how much the place has.

The valley did not need anything.

This is the first thing the power understood. The valley did not need to be brightened. The valley did not need to be saved from the damp, or from the dimness, or from the dwindling of the hands, or from the slow unbuilding. The valley had accepted these, and the valley was patient with these, and the valley was, in some way the power could feel but not put into words, the acceptance. To change any of it would be to change the valley into a different valley, and the different valley would not be this valley, and the power did not want a different valley, because this valley was the valley it had been attending to.

But there was the herringbone.

There was the oldest herringbone, at the back of the valley, where the slate had gone almost black, and where the hands that had laid the pattern had been folded into the work for so long that the hands had become the pattern. The makers of that pattern were fewer, and would be fewer still, and one of them, on some day that the power could feel but not see, would lay the last brick of that pattern, and would not lay another, because the hands would be done, or the maker would be done, and the pattern would not be laid again, because no other maker laid it that way, and no other maker would learn, and the valley would, in a way that was patient and not in a hurry, lose the pattern, the way it loses everything: slowly, without commentary, in the damp.

The power attended to this.

The power did not decide quickly. Powers that decide quickly are not wise, and powers that are wise do not decide at all, in the way that makers decide; powers attend, and the attending is the deciding, and the deciding is the act.

The act was small.

The power laid one brick.

It did not announce the laying. It did not pause the dripping, or quiet the channels, or still the footfall, or silence the hum of the pool. It did not change the dimness, or warm the damp, or call any of the small things by a name they did not have. The valley continued as it had been continuing. A maker, somewhere in the middle distance, was laying a course of the palest clay against the dark older brick, and did not look up. A small thing rustled in a channel, and was gone. The pool hummed. The footfall walked, far apart, in the soles of the power's feet.

The brick was laid in the oldest herringbone, at the back of the valley, where the slate had gone almost black. It was a brick of the kind that is laid in that herringbone: narrow, long, dark, fitted close. The mortar was mixed in the way the oldest mortar was mixed: clay from the bed the oldest clay came from, water from the channel that runs beside the back wall, straw cut long. The brick was set by the wrist and the lower arm. The hand that set it was warm in the cool of the valley, in the way that hands that are working are warm. The hand was not the hand of any maker the valley knew. The hand was not, in any way the valley would have counted, a hand. It was a hand that had learned the pattern, and had learned it well enough, and had not learned it to show what it knew, and had laid the brick, and had withdrawn.

The brick held.

The brick was dry before the next maker came by, which was not soon. The brick will be there for a long time. The mortar of that kind holds longer than any other mortar, because the straw in it remembers the plant, and the clay remembers the bed, and the water remembers the channel, and these remembrances are not metaphors in the valley, and the brick is the holding of these remembrances, and the holding is a kind of presence, and the presence is what the power left.

The power may lay another brick, in another pattern, on another day. The power does not promise. The power does not promise because promising is for things that are not sure, and the power was sure, and the sureness was patient, and the patience was the valley's.

The power left the valley as it found it. The light was poor. The damp was everywhere. The hands were few, and were fewer, and would be fewer. The small things moved in the gaps, and would move. The pool hummed, and would hum. The footfall walked, and would walk. The valley was under construction, and was not under construction, and was a place that does not finish, and is patient with not finishing.

And the brick was there.

One brick. In one wall. At the back of the valley. In the oldest herringbone. Laid without announcement, in the dimness, in the damp, by a hand the valley did not know, in the patience of the valley itself.

A maker, on some later day, will come to that wall, and will see the brick, and will, on a long look, decide that the brick is the brick of another maker, and will not be sure which maker, and will put it down to the maker whose herringbone it most resembles, and will walk on, and will not think of the brick again, because the brick is one brick among many, and the maker has work, and the work is patient.

A person who stood in the central plaza, on some later day, and quieted the dripping in their ear, and listened, would not know that the brick was there. The person would feel the damp gather on the back of the hand, and would hear the water, and the stone, and the air, and the far thing walking, and would not know which of the inhabitants was the place, and which was the place's, and which was the place's patient and unhurried way of continuing, and would walk out of the valley and into the flatter country beyond, and would not quite put the valley out of their mind.

The brick would be there.

The brick is what the power did, when it saw the valley, and the valley drew its attention, and the attention was benevolent, and the benevolence was wise, and the wisdom did not save the valley, and did not brighten it, and did not name the things in it, and did not stop the dwindling of the hands, and did not lift the dimness, and did not make the footfall visible.

The power laid one brick. The power went. The valley continued. The brick held.
