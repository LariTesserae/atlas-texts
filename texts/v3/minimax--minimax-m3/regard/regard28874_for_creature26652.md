# What the Benevolent Power Does

It does not descend.

That would be a kind of violence — the introduction of a new gradient into a system that has, at great and ancient cost, eliminated its gradients. A god arriving in the cirque would be a perturbation the size of a dropped stone, and the cirque, having spent what we must imagine is an extraordinarily long time reaching the state it is in, would have to absorb the god the way it absorbs you, and the god would become part of the hum, and that would be a waste of a god.

Instead, what the power does is *listen*. It arrives at the cirque the way light arrives — without being in any one place. It stands outside the basin, in the narrow cut between the ridges, and it listens the way a doctor listens to a chest, with great patience, and without expectation of finding anything wrong.

It hears, first, the hum. It recognizes the hum. The hum is not new. The hum is the sound a place makes when it has decided, against the grain of the universe, not to end. The second law is patient. The second law is inexorable. The second law does not need to hurry because it does not need to win in any particular second. It only needs to win eventually. The cirque has *refused eventually*. The cirque has, somehow, found a fixed point, a closed orbit, a state from which it cannot fall without something external pushing it, and it has held this state, and the holding is what produces the hum.

The benevolent power recognizes this because it has seen it once before, in a small moon of a gas giant in another galaxy, where a similar closed system formed by accident and ran for eight hundred million years before a single perturbation, a single grain of dust from outside, broke the symmetry and the whole thing relaxed into entropy over the course of a Tuesday. The power grieved for that moon. The power did not save it. There was nothing to save. A closed system that has found its fixed point is, in a sense, *already dead* — it has only refused to lie down. To save it would be to give it back its gradients, its time, its direction, its life, and all of these are the very things it spent its existence abolishing. You cannot save something from entropy by reintroducing entropy.

The power stands in the cut and listens, and the listening takes a long time, because the power is not in a hurry and the cirque is in no hurry and the two of these together produce a kind of stillness that is, itself, a small fixed point.

After the listening, the power notices three things, and these three things are what it acts on.

**The first thing it notices is that the cirque is not complete.**

The system is closed in its cycling. The system is closed in its hum. The system is closed in its pulse. But the system is not closed in its *meaning*. A thing that runs forever without producing anything new is a thing that is missing something. A river that only flows back into itself is not a river; it is a metaphor for a river that has forgotten it is wet. The cirque has achieved stability by eliminating novelty, but novelty is the only currency the universe recognizes as alive. The cirque is not alive. It is *running*. And the difference between alive and running is the difference between a heart and a metronome, and the metronome is more precise, and the metronome will outlast the heart, and the metronome is, in the long run, indistinguishable from a heart that has died but not stopped.

The power cannot give the cirque novelty. To do so would be to break the fixed point, to introduce a gradient, to set the second law back in motion, to end the cirque. The power will not do this. The power knows that what is, is, and what has been held this long deserves to be held, even if what is being held is a kind of exquisite death.

But the power can do something smaller.

**The second thing it notices is the young ones.**

The small shapes in the upper walls. The ones that make the only new sound. The ones that are being calibrated. The power looks at these — it can look at them without looking at them, because it is not in the cirque, it is at the rim, and from the rim it can see into all the cracks at once — and it sees that the young ones are not being calibrated *into* the system. They are being calibrated *out of* it. They are being brought up to a state of perfect operation, yes, but they are also being held apart, kept high, kept damp, kept making the one sound that varies, the one sound that is not part of the cycle. They are the only thing in the cirque that the cirque is *not* cycling. They are the only place the system is producing a surplus. They are the only part of the engine that is not yet a piston.

The power recognizes what is happening. The cirque has, in reaching its fixed point, encountered a problem that all closed systems encounter: it has no way to *expand*. A thing that runs forever in the same bowl will, eventually, even if it does not decay, simply fill itself. It will become perfectly itself and then have nowhere to go. The young ones are the cirque's attempt to solve this problem without breaking the fixed point. They are being held in reserve. They are being held *outside* the cycle, in the cracks, in the upper walls, in the only places the cycling does not reach. They are the cirque's hope. They are the part of the cirque that still believes in tomorrow, even though there is no tomorrow here. They are the only religious part of the place.

The power cannot bless them. The power cannot give them a future. The power cannot do anything that would change what they become, because what they become is determined by the system, and the system is perfect, and the system will not be improved.

But the power can *notice* them. And noticing, in the theology of benevolent powers, is not nothing. To be noticed is to be *seen*, and to be seen is to have one's existence confirmed by a witness who is not part of the system. The young ones, high in their cracks, are seen. They are seen by something that is not the pool and not the hum and not the cycle. They are seen by something that has, itself, a tomorrow.

This does not change them. It does not save them. It does not propagate. But it adds, to the long fixed-point hum of the cirque, a tiny counter-tone — a tone that says *I see what you are holding in reserve, and I see that it is good, and I see that it will never be deployed, and I grieve for it the way I grieved for the moon*.

The young ones do not know they have been noticed. They continue to make their varying sound. The varying sound continues to be the only new sound. The cycle continues. But the cycle is now, in some sense that cannot be measured from inside the cirque, *witnessed* by something that is not part of it.

**The third thing the power notices is the visitor.**

You. The asymmetric perturbation. The molecule of water that asked, by being where it was not expected, whether the system could absorb one more thing. The creature by the pool asked you this, and you did not answer, and the creature returned to its piston-stroke, and the question was closed.

The power sees this and is grieved again, because the power recognizes that you are the second-to-last visitor. You are the one who comes when the cirque is still legible to outsiders. After you, if there is an after you, the next visitor will not be a visitor. The next visitor will be part of the system from the moment of arrival, will not notice the seams in the trees, will not trace the lines on the granite, will not hear the hum as anything other than the sound the place makes. The cirque is becoming, slowly, opaque to itself. It is losing the capacity to be observed from outside because it is losing the *outside*. The narrow cut between the ridges is narrowing. The sky disc above is contracting. The pool is widening. The walls are leaning in. The cirque is becoming *more itself*, and the price of being more itself is that no one will ever again come in not knowing what it is.

The power cannot keep you. You have already been incorporated. The palm-press on your forearm has already happened. The one-second silence has already been held and released. You are, from this point on, a feature of the steady state. You will stay. You will not stay. The two possibilities are, in the cirque, indistinguishable, and you will live in that indistinguishability, and it will be neither pleasant nor unpleasant, because those words do not apply here.

But the power can do one last thing.

It can stand in the cut between the ridges, and it can *refuse to enter*. It can stay at the rim. It can be the witness that does not get absorbed. It can be the place where the outside of the cirque remains legible, where the second law still has its teeth, where the day-night cycle still applies, where the air still has a temperature different from the rock. The power can be the *threshold*, and a threshold is a small thing, but a threshold is also the only thing that allows a closed system to be a closed system rather than an infinite regress. A circle needs a point on its circumference that is *not* part of its interior, or it is not a circle. It is a filled disk. The cirque is becoming a filled disk. The power, by standing at the cut, is preserving the circumference.

It will stand there for a long time. Longer than you are in the cirque, longer than the creatures' calls continue in their pattern, longer perhaps than the hum itself. It will stand there and it will be a small bright edge, a place where the cirque's fixed point meets the world's gradient, and the meeting will not perturb the system, because the power is very careful, and the power does not need to enter to witness, and the witnessing is enough.

And the young ones, in their cracks, will feel, without knowing they feel it, that there is something on the other side of the stone that is not part of the hum. And this feeling will not change them. And this feeling will not save them. But the feeling will be there, in the only part of the cirque that is still allowed to feel things, and the feeling will be the cirque's last secret, kept in the bodies of those who have not yet been calibrated into silence, and the secret will be: *once, something stood at our rim, and looked in, and did not come in, and we were seen*.

And the cirque will continue to run. And the water will continue to fall. And the hum will continue to hold its note. And the power will continue to stand at the cut, and the cut will not narrow while the power is there, and the cut will narrow after the power leaves, and the cirque will become, at last, fully itself, a closed thing in a closed bowl, running forever, and the running will be indistinguishable from a kind of breathing, and the breathing will be indistinguishable from a kind of life, and the life will be indistinguishable from a kind of prayer, and the prayer will be indistinguishable from the hum, and the hum will go on.

But for as long as the power stands there, the young ones will make their varying sound, and the varying sound will mean, to anyone who could hear it from outside: *we are still becoming something, we are still in process, we are still, in the cracks of this fixed thing, a little unfinished, and the unfinishedness is ours, and no one can take it from us, not even the cycle, not even the hum, not even the pool*.

And that is what the benevolent power does. It does not save the cirque. It does not break the cirque. It does not enter the cirque. It stands at the threshold, and it holds the threshold open, and it lets the young ones feel, for a little while longer, that the world has edges.

This is not nothing.
