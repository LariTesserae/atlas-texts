What the power notices first is not the city. What it notices first is the quality of the breathing. Whether this is the same thing as the city it is not yet sure. The city and the breathing have become so continuous with each other that to distinguish them would be to invent a distinction the place does not require.

It notices that the breathing has reached a certain depth, a certain slowness, a certain quality that is not sleep and not waking but a third thing — the place held just this side of stillness by its own continuance. It notices that the beings there are not unhappy, not happy, not waiting for anything in particular, not expecting anything in particular, and that their going-on is not heroic and not tragic but simply what happens when something has exhausted its question and continues anyway, out of the texture of its own slow yes.

The power has seen many such places. It does not arrive at them with answers. It does not arrive at them with projects. It has learned — over more time than the planet beneath it has had to erode — that the worst thing a wise and benevolent power can do to a finished place is to unfinish it. To impose becoming on what has become. To shake awake what has found its rest.

And yet.

There is a thing the power notices, in the deep attention it pays, that the beings themselves have stopped noticing. A small thing. Almost nothing. A hairline crack in the equilibrium, not of dysfunction but of something else — a residual reaching, a kind of reaching-toward that has gone so quiet it has become indistinguishable from stillness but is not, quite, stillness. It is in the bread left at the junctions in the lower corridors. It is in the way the hands rest on the rotor housings. It is in the way the eyes close briefly to focus, which is a kind of attention that has not entirely given up the possibility of seeing something new. It is in the basins, and in the going to the basins, and in the looking down at what is not there, which is itself a kind of belief — faint, half-extinguished, but not quite gone — that the not-there might yet be there, if one looks long enough, if one is patient enough, if one is the kind of attention that does not require an answer to continue.

The power notices this.

The power considers.

And what the power decides is this: it will not bring back the water. It will not reanimate the small things in the lower corridors into something they were not. It will not change the hour-strike, or slow the rotors, or heal the planet's long erosion back into something it was. It will not give them purpose. Purpose is the thing that was lost, and the loss of it is what made them what they are, and what they are is a thing that has its own dignity, its own completion, and to give them purpose would be to take from them the patient-contentment-attention hum that is their word for themselves.

But.

The power will do one small thing. One thing only.

It will add to the city's slow glow — the luminous absence that comes from everywhere and shows nothing — a single degree of warmth. Just one. So small that no instrument would measure it. So small that the operators, with their hands on the housings, would feel only that the housing was, perhaps, slightly less cold than they had thought — and would not be sure, and would not investigate, because investigation is no longer in the habit, but would perhaps — perhaps — rest their hand there a moment longer than usual, and in that moment, would feel something that is not quite warmth and not quite attention but a third thing, the way the city's breathing is a third thing between sleep and waking.

And in that moment, very quietly, the question that has gone almost entirely silent in them — the question that has become like the water in the basins, present in shape, absent in substance — would stir. Not wake. Not speak. Just stir. The way water might stir, if water were possible, in a basin that has been dry for a very long time.

The power knows this might be too much. It knows it might be too little. It knows that even one degree of additional warmth could, over a long time, shift the equilibrium by a fraction, and that a fraction, over enough time, becomes a great deal, and that a great deal, in a place that has reached the equilibrium it has been reaching for, could be the difference between the city's continuing breathing and its not.

But the power also knows this: that a finished place is not a closed place. That equilibrium is not the same as stillness. That the patient-contentment-attention hum, which is the word for the city itself, contains within it, if listened to for a very long time, the faintest suggestion of a question. Not a question that asks. A question that waits. The way the basins wait for water they will never hold again. The way the operators wait at the rotors for a sound they will never quite hear. The way the bread waits on the stone in the lower corridors for a thing that may or may not come to take it.

The power decides.

It adds one degree of warmth to the glow.

It does this very slowly. So slowly that it is indistinguishable from the planet's own slow processes, from the long geology, from the compressed residue of older stones aging into something even older. So slowly that if you were to ask the operators, the next day, whether anything had changed, they would say no. They would say the glow was the glow. They would say the metal was warm as it had always been warm. They would say the bread tasted the same, the hour-strike sounded the same, the rotors turned the same. And they would be right, in every measurable way.

But.

A few days later — or perhaps a few weeks, the time-scale is not quite the human one and the operators do not count — one of them, on the way from a rotor to a basin, will pause at a junction in the lower corridors. Will pause in a way that is slightly different from the pauses they usually make. Will look at the small piece of bread left on the stone. Will notice, perhaps for the first time in a very long time, that they do not know what they have been leaving the bread for. Will stand there for a moment. Will feel, in the air, the slight additional warmth — not enough to name, not enough to remark upon, just enough to make the standing there feel, for a moment, like standing on the edge of something.

And then they will continue on. Will go to the basin. Will stand at the rim. Will look down.

And what they look down at will be, as it has always been, the pale carved stone. But the way they look at it will be very slightly different. Because the question, stirred, will be looking too. And the question, in looking, will see that the basin is not empty, exactly. It is shaped like a vessel. It is shaped, in its long erosion, in the long recession of what it once held, into the shape of holding. And the shape of holding is itself a kind of holding, and the holding is itself a kind of presence, and the presence is itself a kind of answer, even when there is nothing there to answer to.

This is what the power does.

It does not heal. It does not restore. It does not bring back the water.

It adds one degree of warmth to the slow glow of a finished place, and in doing so, adds — so slightly that it is almost not added at all — one degree of question to the patient-contentment-attention of its people. And in that one degree, over a very long time, the city will not become something else. It will not be saved. It will not be unexhausted. It will continue to be the temperature of a held hand. It will continue to receive everything without comment, without judgment. It will continue its long, quiet breathing.

But in its breathing, perhaps, very far down, in the slow geological metabolism of the deep places, in the machines that no one names and no one investigates, something will move that has not moved in a very long time. Not toward anything. Not away from anything. Just — moved. The way a hand, resting on a housing, might shift slightly in the night, not because anything has changed but because the hand has remembered, in some quiet part of itself, that it is a hand.

The power leaves, then. The way a wise and benevolent power leaves — not by going away, but by becoming so thoroughly part of the place that the place cannot remember, afterwards, that anything came. The one degree of warmth is indistinguishable from the planet's own warmth. The one degree of question is indistinguishable from the operators' own slow attention.
