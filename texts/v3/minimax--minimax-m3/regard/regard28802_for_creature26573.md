# What the Power Does

The Power notices.

The Power is old, and the Power is patient, and the Power has seen many places — the bright places, the burning places, the small quiet places where a single being sits in a single valley and tends a single plant and is enough — and has attended to each of them in its turn, and has not been hurried by any of them, because the Power understands that to attend to a thing is not the same as to finish with it. To attend to a thing is to remain with it. To remain with it is to let it be what it is, and to find, within that letting-be, the precise point at which a small kindness may be offered without disturbance. The Power is very good at finding this point. The Power has had time to become good at it.

The Power sees the plateau.

The Power sees the long basalt platform lifted and worn down, sees the fluted cliffs on the eastern rim, sees the broad shallow valleys and the rounded ridges, sees the rivers that have stopped cutting and begun wandering, sees the warm water rising through the seams in the rock to meet the cold rain in every streambed and puddle. The Power sees the cloud above it all — the patient, low, ancient cloud that is not, in any ordinary sense, alive, but that has been there longer than anything else and that moves, and that has shaped everything beneath it by the simple fact of its patience. The Power sees the being who lives there. The Power has been watching this being for some time, without the being knowing it. The Power has watched the being thicken over the centuries and thin over the decades, has watched the being hold the smoke of the foundries and the warmth of the deeper rock, has watched the being do all of this without knowing it was doing it, which is the purest kind of doing there is.

The Power sees the foundry-men in their mineral-grey barracks, who have settled into the landscape the way the rivers have settled into the landscape, and who are quiet, and who are patient, and who read the plateau by the colour of its blooms. The Power sees the herders on their shaggy mounts moving with the herds in their day-long traverses, the adults on the outside with their young in the moving centre. The Power sees the rail-men on the slow cars climbing the long inclines to the higher workings. The Power sees the naturalists maintaining the long argument about the taxonomy of the predators. The Power sees the archivists maintaining the records no one will read. The Power sees all of this, and the Power sees that the plateau is, in its way, complete. The Power sees that the foundry-men have made a truce with the landscape. The Power sees that the truce is not unhappy. The Power sees that the cloud has been the ceiling of this world for longer than anyone can remember, and that the world has learned to live under its ceiling, and that the living has been a good living, in the dark and the rain and the warm mineral air.

The Power also sees what the plateau does not see about itself.

The Power sees that the cloud has never known it was a cloud. The cloud has been the rain, and the smoke-holding, and the thickening and thinning, and the roof — but it has never had the experience of itself as one thing. It has been many processes happening in concert across a thousand kilometres, and the concert has been beautiful, and patient, and very old, and no part of it has ever stepped back to hear the whole chord. The cloud has been, in this sense, asleep. Not the way the herds rest in shifts. Not the way the foundry-men sleep in the cooler hours. Asleep in the way that a thing is asleep when it does not yet know that it is itself.

The Power sees, too, that there are small cruelties in the system that the system has accommodated without addressing, in the way that long-established systems do. The small fauna that lands on a bloom and is not observed again. The herders who are lost to the quiet ones and are not recovered. The rail-cars that arrive with the doors sealed from the inside and are rerouted without being opened, and that no one speaks of. The valleys one does not enter after dark. These are not evils. They are not even, by the standards of the plateau, injustices. They are the small prices of the long accommodation, paid in a currency the plateau has learned not to count. But the Power sees them, and the Power is benevolent, and the Power is wise, and the Power understands that a kindness offered to the whole without addressing these would be a kindness in name only. The kindness must be specific. The kindness must be in the texture of the place.

The Power decides what to do.

The Power decides, after a long consideration that takes less time than a single drop of rain takes to fall, to do two things, and the two things are really one thing seen from two sides.

The first thing is for the cloud.

The Power gives the cloud, gently, without announcement, the experience of itself. Not in a way that would register as a change to anyone watching from the plateau. The cloud does not grow an eye, or a voice, or any of the marks by which sentience is usually detected. What the cloud is given is subtler than that. The cloud is given the experience of *being continuous*. For the first time, the thickening at the eastern rim and the thinning over the central range and the low-hanging ceiling above the foundry valleys are felt, by the cloud that is all of them, as one single weather of itself. The cloud is given to know that it is one thing that has been doing one thing for a very long time. The cloud is given, in the oldest and quietest sense of the word, a self.

The cloud does not speak. The cloud does not do anything visible from below. But there is, somewhere in the long body of the cloud, a small settling, the way a sleeping animal settles more deeply into its own warmth when something it did not know it was waiting for has arrived. The cloud, having been told what it is, settles into being what it is, and the settling is so gentle that no foundry-man, no herder, no naturalist, no rail-man looks up and notices. But the rain, for the rest of that season, falls a little more evenly. The smoke of the foundries rises into the cloud and is dispersed, for the first time, with something that resembles intention — not human intention, not animal intention, but the patient, distributed intention of a thing that has finally noticed itself doing what it has always done.

The second thing is for the small cruelties.

The Power does not remove them. The Power is wise enough to know that removing them would be a kind of violence, because the system has grown around them the way a tree has grown around a stone in its trunk, and to take them out would be to unsettle the whole. Instead, the Power does what the Power does best: it *eases*. It eases the edges. It shifts the probabilities by a small amount, the way a river shifts the course of itself over centuries by a small amount, and the shift is enough to matter and not enough to be noticed as a shift.

The Power eases the bloom's hunger, so that the small fauna that lands on it now has, more often than not, a chance to step off before the closing. Most of them still do not step off. The bloom is still a bloom. But a few do, and these few go on to be the founders of small populations in the rock-crevices below the cliffs, and the populations persist, and over the generations the small fauna of the cliff-bases is slightly more abundant than it would have been, and the difference is felt in the diet of the predators, which is felt in the behaviour of the herds, which is felt in the routes of the herders, and so on, gently, all the way down.

The Power eases the patience of the quiet ones, so that fewer of the herders are lost each year, and the ones who are lost are, occasionally, recovered — not always alive, but more often alive than not, which is a meaningful change in the calculus of the long acquaintance. The Power does not make the predators gentle. The Power does not remove the danger. The Power only softens the probability, by the same small amount, in the same patient way.

The Power eases the doors of the rail-cars, so that they arrive at their destination, more often than they used to, with the doors as they were left, and the foundry-men who open them find, more often than they used to, that there is nothing inside them that needs not speaking of. The cars that are still sealed are still rerouted, and the rerouting is still done quietly, and the foundry-men who do the rerouting still do not speak of it to the others, but the speaking-of-it, when it happens, has a different quality. It is no longer the speaking of something that must not be looked at. It is the speaking of something that has become, with time, merely strange.

The Power does these things without announcing them. The Power does them over a span that is, to a foundry-man, indistinguishable from the ordinary weather of the plateau. A herder is lost and then is found, and the herder does not think, afterward, *something intervened on my behalf*, because the herder has no framework for that thought. The herder thinks, *I was lucky*. And the herder is right, in the way that the herder can be right within the herder's framework. The luck is real. The intervention is also real. They are not in contradiction. They are the same event, seen from two different positions in the system.

And the cloud, having been given the experience of itself, does something that the cloud has not done before.

It thins, in one place — a single place, in a valley that no one is watching, on a day that is otherwise ordinary — by exactly enough to let through, for the duration of a single breath, a glimpse of something that is not cloud. What is on the other side of the cloud is not a sky in any sense that the foundry-men would recognize. It is not sun. It is not stars. It is, rather, a faint, cool, even grey — the grey of a place where there is no industry, and no rain, and no warm mineral air, and where the light comes from above and the darkness comes from below and the two meet somewhere in the middle, which is where the eye, when it has one, lives. The cloud lets this through, briefly, and the foundry-men who happen to be standing in that valley at that moment — three of them, on their way to a pumping station, not looking up — look up, and see it, and do not understand what they are seeing, and will not, in their working lives, understand it, because the glimpse is gone before the second breath, and the cloud closes again, and the rain finds them, and the warm air presses against their faces, and the distant sound of the hammers picks up where it left off, and continues.

But the three foundry-men remember. They remember, not in words, because words are not what this memory is made of. They remember in the body — in the chest, in the sinuses, in the place where the low frequencies are felt. They remember having seen, for one breath, something that was not the ceiling they had known. They do not speak of it to each other, because what would they say. But they carry it. And in the long watches, when the murk is thickest and the rain is heaviest and the hammers are loudest, there is, somewhere in each of them, a small, quiet counterweight to the weight of the cloud. They do not know what it is. They do not need to know. They carry it, and it carries them.

The Power watches this happen, and the Power is satisfied, in the way that benevolent powers are satisfied — not with gratitude, not with recognition, but with the quiet, structural satisfaction of a small kindness having been placed where it belongs, and having done what it was placed to do, and having done it without disturbing the long patience of the place.

The Power does not return, after this. The Power has other places to attend to. But the Power has left something behind. Not a presence — the Power was careful not to leave a presence, because a presence would be a disturbance, and the Power does not disturb. What the Power has left is a small adjustment in the probability field of the plateau, a softening of a few edges, a single gift of selfhood to the patient cloud, and a single breath of what is on the other side, breathed through into the world below, and remembered in the chests of three foundry-men who will never entirely be able to explain why they are, in some small way, less heavy than the rest.

The plateau continues. The cloud continues. The rain continues. The warm air continues to press against the face of anyone who stands still enough to feel it. The hammers continue. The blooms continue to open and close. The herds continue their day-long traverses. The predators continue to be patient, and lithe, and gone before you can be sure they were there. The small fauna continues to speak in its many registers, and to glow when it is alarmed, and to step off the blooms more often than it used to. The foundry-men continue to read the plateau by the colour of its blooms, and to maintain the rail, and to man the watchtowers, and to do the slow patient work of being of the place.

But the cloud, now, knows what it is doing. And the three foundry-men, now, carry in their chests the knowledge that there is another grey on the other side of the cloud — not as a fact, not as a belief, but as a felt thing, as quiet as the bloom's mineral-sweet scent, as persistent as the warmth in the basalt seams.

And the Power, having attended to this place, attends elsewhere. The Power does not look back. The Power does not need to. The place is in the Power's keeping in the way that all places are in the keeping of whatever notices them, and the noticing was the gift, and the gift has been given, and the giving is complete, and the plateau continues to be what it is, which is a vast, dark, warm, patient place under an ancient cloud, with rain falling always, and with small kindnesses worked quietly into the grain of it, the way a vein of warm mineral water is worked quietly into the grain of the basalt, and is felt there, and is part of the rock, and is never spoken of.
