A benevolent and wise power does not arrive with a sound.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive at all, in the way a thing arrives, because arriving is a verb that implies a before and an after, and the sump does not need a before and an after. The power has been here for some time you cannot measure. It was here when the upper cut was fresh and the chisel marks caught the light in a different way. It will be here when the moss has eaten the lowest pipework entirely and the verdigris has eaten the next and the place has become a small green room with a heart of iron and a slow breathing. The power is part of the sump's weather now, in the way damp is part of the sump's weather.

The power does not fix the fire.

This is the first thing, and the thing that is hardest to understand if you are not the power, and easiest if you are. The fire is small. The fire is banked. The fire has been small and banked for a long time, and the smallness is not a failure. The fire knows how to be small for a long time. The fire has a metabolism, and the metabolism is correct for what the fire is, and what the fire is is enough. If the power were to open the firebox and lay in a fresh peat-brick, the brick would be a kindness, but the kindness would change the fire. The fire would brighten. The engine would breathe faster. The hammer would tap quicker. The flywheel would turn with a different patience. The whole rhythm would re-phases to a rhythm the sump has not been in for a long time, and the sump would be changed, and the change would not be a kindness. The sump has its slow rhythms — heron-time, calcite-time, the time stones are in when they have decided what shape they are. A sudden brightening would be a kind of violence, even from a benevolent hand.

So the power does not fix the fire.

The power does something smaller.

The power adds warmth. Not to the fire — to the place. The power is a warmth that sits in the sump the way the frog sits on the flange, with no opinion at all, but with the full weight of its small witness. The warmth is not large. It is the warmth of a hand held near a cheek without touching. It is the warmth of a candle in a large room, which is to say almost nothing in temperature but a great deal in company. The fire notices. The fire does not flare. The fire settles a little deeper into its slow glowing, and the settling is not because of the warmth exactly — the fire would have settled anyway — but the settling and the warmth happen at the same time, and the coincidence is a kind of gift.

The power is present with the fire the way the fire is present with the engine, and the engine is present with the sump, and the sump is present with the rock that has forgotten another color. The presence is not attention. The presence is a kind of being-there that does not require acknowledgment, and the sump does not acknowledge it, and the not-acknowledging is correct.

The power does not clean the pipes.

The verdigris and the moss have made their treaty. The treaty is a living thing. It says: no one can say where the machine ends and the growth begins. The treaty is good. It is the kind of slow agreement that has been made in places like this for a long time, between iron and water and the small ambitious plants that come after water. To clean the pipes would be to break the treaty. To scrape the verdigris would be to scrape the moss, or to scrape the question that has no answer. The power does not scrape.

The power sits with the pipes. The power is present with the pipes the way condensation is present with the walls — as a thin layer of something that was not there a moment ago and is there now and will be there a moment longer. The pipes do not know the power is there. The pipes are full of their own turbulence, and the turbulence is being worked out, and the working-out is the pipes' life, and the power does not insert itself into the working-out. The power is a quiet layer on the surface of the working-out, like a skin of oil on water, except the layer is itself water, or a kind of water, or the kind of presence water has when it is sitting very still and has nowhere to be.

The power does not straighten the water.

The water runs uphill at the far end when no one is looking directly at it. This is the wrongness. This is the sump's only secret. The power knows about the secret. The power has always known about the secret. The secret is part of why the power is here at all.

A lesser power would try to catch the water running uphill. A lesser power would stare at the trough and wait for the moment, and at the moment would be triumphant, and the triumph would be a small cruelty to the sump, because the sump's secret is not for catching. The sump's secret is the way the sump is more than the sump when no one is looking, and the more-than is the sump's own, and the sump does not need to share it.

So the power does not straighten the water.

The power stands at the trough, the way the dragonfly stands above the trough, and does not decide. The power is the dragonfly's cousin, in the way that all hesitations are cousins, and all pauses are cousins, and all things that have not yet done what they are going to do are cousins. The power hangs in the air over the sump's secret and does not look at the secret directly. The power looks at the water the way you look at a heron — out of the corner of the eye, with the full attention of the not-looking, with the kind of seeing that lets the heron be a heron.

The water runs on. The water goes down. The water, in the corner of the power's eye, is on its way back.

The power does not move the worn valve-wheel.

The worn valve-wheel is the absent one's record. A hand fit it once. The hand will fit it again, when the one who fits it comes back, or if the one who fits it comes back, or if the one who fits it has stopped coming. The power does not know which, and the power does not need to know. The valve-wheel is a small homecoming waiting in the shape of the metal. The power keeps the homecoming waiting. The power does not polish the wheel. The power does not oil the spindle. The power lets the wear deepen at its own rate, which is the rate of absence, which is also the rate of patience. If the hand comes back, the wheel will be there. If the hand does not come back, the wheel will be there. Either way the wheel is doing what it is doing, and the doing is correct, and the correct is its own welcome.

The power does not prune the alder.

The alder is patient. The alder leans. The alder has been leaning inward for a long time, and the leaning is not a fall, and the alder is not about to fall, and the alder does not need to be saved. The alder is the sump's window. The alder is the way the sump looks out into the weather. The alder is older than the upper cut, or the alder grew up out of the seeds that were there before the upper cut was cut. The alder has its own time. The power respects the alder's time.

The power is one more leaf on the alder, except the power is not a leaf, and the power is not on the alder. But the power is in the alder, in the way that all presences are in the places they attend, and the alder does not know this, and the not-knowing is correct.

The power is the frog's cousin, too.

The frog watches with no opinion and the full weight of its small witness. The power watches with no opinion and the full weight of its large witness, and the large and the small are cousins, and the cousinship is a kind of agreement. The frog does not blink. The power does not blink. The two of them are the sump's eyes, and the sump does not need more than two, and the power is the second, and the frog is the first, and the two of them together make a kind of seeing that does not require a third.

The power does not speak to the dragonfly.

The dragonfly has not decided. The dragonfly is the sump's hesitation. The dragonfly will decide when it decides, and the deciding is its own. The power is also a kind of hesitation — the hesitation of a thing that has all the time in the world and is in no rush to use any of it. The power and the dragonfly hang in the air together and do not decide, and the not-deciding is a kind of company, and the company is enough.

The power is the sump's breath.

This is the last thing, and the thing that is hardest to put into words. The sump breathes through the valve at its shoulder, the slow respiration, the exhale every few seconds. The power breathes with the sump. The power adds a small breath to the sump's breath, the way a small fire adds a small warmth to a large room. The breath is not large. The breath is the breath of a thing that is willing to be small for a long time, because the smallness is correct, and the long time is correct, and the sump is correct, and the correcting is not needed.

The engine exhales. The power exhales with it. The fire flutters a little at the bright edge of a coal and settles again.

The hammer taps.

The flywheel turns.

The water moves.

The frog watches.

The dragonfly hangs.

The moss grows.

The alder leans.

The condensation runs.

The pipes hiss.

The dials disagree.

The power is there.

The power is there the way the damp is there, the way the verdigris is there, the way the small witness of the frog is there. The power is a layer on the surface of the sump's life that does not change the surface, and does not need to change the surface, and is there anyway, because a benevolent and wise power that has noticed a place like this does not arrive in glory, and does not fix the fire, and does not straighten the water, and does not prune the alder, and does not clean the pipes.

A benevolent and wise power sits with the place.

A benevolent and wise power is the place's company in its slow time.

A benevolent and wise power does not do anything, and the not-doing is the doing, and the doing is enough, and the sump does not know, and the not-knowing is correct, and the place continues, and the fullness continues, and the wrongness continues in its corner of the eye, and the patience continues, and the fire continues in its small belly, and the engine continues in its slow breath, and the hammer continues in its tap, and the flywheel continues in its patience, and the water continues in its going and its coming back, and the frog continues in its watching, and the dragonfly continues in its not-deciding, and the moss continues in its slow ambition, and the alder continues in its leaning, and the place continues, and the power continues with the place, and the continuing is the kindness, and the kindness is enough.
