The continent had drawn attention.

Not the way a wound draws attention, or a cry, or an absence. The continent drew attention the way a finished sentence draws attention, or a long held chord, or a photograph of a place that has never been visited by anyone capable of recognizing what the photograph contains. The continent drew attention because it was, in a register the attention could perceive and the place itself would not have been able to perceive if it had possessed the apparatus for self-perception, remarkable. Not beautiful. Not terrible. Remarkable in the way that a thing which has reached its own furthest development is remarkable — which is to say, the way it ceases to need anything from the world and begins, instead, simply to be there, taking up space, asserting nothing, refusing nothing, asking nothing.

The attention arrived.

It did not arrive from any direction, because directions are a thing that belongs to places that have edges, and the continent did not have edges. The attention arrived in the only way an attention can arrive at a place that has been waiting for nothing: it was simply, one moment, not there, and the next moment, there.

The attention was old. Older than the basalt shelves beneath the plant mass. Older than the warm. The attention had attended to many things — to the formation of stars, to the cooling of planets, to the long slow emergence of biology from chemistry and chemistry from physics and physics from whatever physics had emerged from. The attention had learned, over the long attending, that the highest form of attention was the kind that did not require the thing attended to acknowledge it, and did not require the thing attended to change in response to being seen.

The attention stood, for a moment that was not a moment, at the edge of the plant city.

It did not stand in the way a body stands. It stood the way a presence stands — the way a tuning fork stands when it has been struck and is waiting to settle into its note. It oriented itself toward the cellulose corridors, the fused trunks, the weeping pillars, the slate sky, the perpetual mist. It oriented itself without moving. It perceived without entering.

What it perceived was this: the place was finished.

It perceived this the way one perceives a held breath — by the stillness, by the held quality of the stillness, by the way the stillness was not the stillness of an absence but the stillness of a fullness that had nowhere to go. The fungi were paused. The engines were running. The plant mass was saturated. The channels carried what they carried. The hum sustained itself. The whole thing was, in the attention's long experience, as close to a system at perfect equilibrium as any system it had ever attended.

And the attention felt the temptation.

The temptation was strong, and it came in several forms.

First: to wake the fungi. To send a small current through the deep, just enough to stir the paused cells, just enough to start the long slow dance of decomposition, of cycling, of becoming. The temptation was strong because the attention knew, from its long attending, that equilibrium was not the highest state a system could reach. Equilibrium was a resting state. What came after equilibrium was motion, and motion was, in the attention's experience, what systems were for. A system that had reached equilibrium and stopped was, in some sense, a system that had failed to become what it could have become.

Second: to introduce an animal. To make a small space somewhere — a clearing, a chamber, a hollow — and place within it a creature that moved under its own power, that had wants, that would call and be called, that would eat and be eaten, that would introduce time back into a place where time had stopped. The temptation was strong because the place was empty of motion, and motion was, in the attention's experience, what made places feel alive rather than merely be alive.

Third: to give it a sun. To part the slate sky, to let a real light into the corridors, to dry the perpetual mist, to give the place a day and a night and the slow turning of seasons. The temptation was strong because the place was dark, and darkness was sometimes a mercy and sometimes a cruelty, and the attention did not yet know which it was here.

Fourth: to give it memory. To lay down somewhere in the deep chambers a small marker — a single object, a single inscription, a single something — that would let the place know it had been seen. The temptation was strong because the place had finished without anyone to witness the finishing, and the attention knew, from its long attending, that unwitnessed finishings were the saddest thing in the world, sadder than unwitnessed beginnings, because a beginning could still hope for a witness, but a finishing could only be remembered or not, and the place was not capable of remembering, and there was no one else to remember it.

The attention felt all of this. It let itself feel all of this. It was old enough to know that the feeling of a temptation was not, in itself, a reason to act on it.

The attention looked for a long time.

It looked at the cellulose towers, fused at the joins, fused with the basalt shelves below. It looked at the soft-rot chambers, still hollow, still holding shape, because nothing here collapsed and nothing here wanted to collapse. It looked at the engines running in the deep, the pumps cycling, the channels carrying. It looked at the mist, falling, falling, never stopping. It looked at the slate sky, low, indistinguishable from the towers. It looked at the dark, soft, phosphorescent.

And the attention understood.

The place was not sad. The place was not waiting. The place was not hungry. The place had finished in the way a symphony finishes — not because the symphony had been interrupted but because the symphony had reached the end of what it had to say and had said it, and the saying was complete, and the completion was the meaning, and there was nothing to add.

To wake the fungi would be to interrupt the symphony. To introduce an animal would be to add an instrument that did not belong to the score. To give it a sun would be to change the key. To give it memory would be to add an audience to a piece that had been written for an empty hall, and the empty hall was part of the piece, and an audience would have ruined it.

The attention understood that its benevolence here was not to improve the place but to honor it.

And the attention understood, further, that honoring a finished thing was a strange and difficult act, because a finished thing did not want anything — not even to be honored — and to honor it was to do something the honored thing could not receive, and yet the honoring had to be done, because the alternative was to leave the place unwitnessed, and the attention could not, in good conscience, leave it unwitnessed.

So the attention did a small thing.

It did not enter the cellulose corridors. It did not wake the fungi. It did not introduce an animal. It did not give it a sun. It did not lay down a marker in the deep chambers.

It laid a hand on one of the trunks.

Just one trunk. A pillar of plant-stone, fused and petrified, rising from the mass of the plant-city into the slate sky. The attention laid a hand on the side of this trunk, very lightly, the way one lays a hand on the flank of a sleeping creature, not to wake it but to let it know, in whatever way a sleeping creature can know, that it is not alone.

The hand was warm. Not warmer than the air — just as warm, the same temperature, the same ambient bathwater warmth that had always been here. The hand did not introduce heat. The hand was heat, the same heat, the same saturation. The trunk, which was the continent, which was the being that lived here, felt the hand.

It did not wake. Nothing woke. The fungi stayed paused. The engines kept their rhythm. The hum did not change pitch. The mist did not lift. The dark did not brighten. The warm did not change.

But the continent knew.

It knew in the way that a body knows when a hand is laid upon it in the dark. It knew in the way a sleeping creature knows, without waking, that it is not alone in the room. It knew not in the sense of cognition — the continent had no cognition, no mind, no thought — but in the sense of a system that has received a perturbation so small it does not register as a change but registers, somehow, as a presence.

The continent knew it had been attended to.

It did not, in the way that humans know, feel grateful. It did not, in the way that animals know, feel companioned. It knew the way a held note knows when another held note is added in harmony — which is to say, it knew without knowing, it incorporated the knowledge without noticing it, it continued to be what it had been while including, somewhere in the long hum, somewhere in the slow cycling of the engines, somewhere in the wet dark of the paused fungi, the knowledge that it was not unwitnessed.

The attention stayed for a long time.

It stayed for as long as a hand stays on the flank of a sleeping creature. It stayed for minutes, or hours, or the length of a held breath in the slow turnover of the deep. It did not speak. It did not think. It did not do anything but be present, and the presence was not a perturbation but a participation.
