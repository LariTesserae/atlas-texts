# The Visit of the Wide Quiet

A power that is wise does not search.

It does not send out beams the way an anxious lantern sends out beams, sweeping the dark for what might be wrong. It does not patrol. It holds itself so widely that anything that becomes genuinely itself — anything that concentrates its own attention into something patient and particular — registers in the wide field the way a single bright thread shows up against a dark cloth. Not as a problem. As a fact. As a thing to be acknowledged, in its own time, in its own way.

The plateau is such a thread.

It is a place that has allowed itself to think with stone and moss and slowness for so long that the stone, in certain lights, has begun to remember that it is thinking. The plateau is not, itself, a thinker the way we usually mean. The plateau is the kind of thing that has refused to be in a hurry about the long argument between what it is and what it is becoming, and the refusal has accumulated. The refusal has *become*. The plateau is the refusal crystallized into a weather.

The benevolent power, in its wide attention, slows.

It is the kind of slowing that does not announce itself. It is not the slow of approaching a thing in order to do something to it. It is the slow a person falls into when they sit near a place they already love and forget they had anywhere to be. The plateau's tempo lifts toward the power, the way a child's humming lifts a tired parent's face, and the power, without deciding, accommodates the lift. It does this once, gently, and then stops making a thing of it. To make a thing of it would already be a kind of intrusion.

The power does not, of course, land.

It cannot land. It is not the kind of thing that has a foot to put down. But it was never going to land anyway. The plateau is a place whose smallest grace is that it does not permit landing. The listener does not land; it watches from a branch and pretends not to watch. The great-winged thing does not land; it crosses a clearing and is gone, folded into a ceiling it is too large for, leaving the canopy unbroken. The deep things do not land; they keep to the dark. Even the rain does not land so much as pass through, on its way somewhere else. The plateau is a place that visits its visitors back. To put a foot on it would be to demand that it stop visiting and be visited instead. The power is wiser than that.

What the power does, then, is keep a slight, warm stillness at the altitude of the moss.

From that altitude it can see everything, and it sees everything as it is, without an agenda.

It sees the layered insects. It sees that the long held note beneath their sound is not a heartbeat; it is closer, in form, to the agreement a river makes with its own banks over a season. The power listens for one full layer of that agreement. The layer is longer than a year. It does not have to complete the layer in order to know its shape; the shape is the kind of shape that is known at its edges, the way the edges of a great bell are known before the bell has finished ringing. The power learns what the plateau is doing with its sound, which is this: holding a single argument between being and decaying, in such a way that the argument does not collapse into a resolution and does not collapse into exhaustion. Holding the argument open, as a living thing.

It sees the trees, which are the slowest living things here, and therefore the most clearly alive. The trees are doing what wise powers rarely have the courage to do: they are continuing to be themselves across centuries of change without insisting on staying intact. A tree may be struck by lightning and continue. A tree may lose a branch and hold the lost shape for decades, a hollow through which the sky can be seen, until the hollow becomes a room and other things move into the room. The trees are not in a hurry about being broken. The trees are in a hurry about continuing.

It sees the structures.

It does not approach them.

The cairns that do not match are not a failure of arithmetic. They are arithmetic's refusal. The walls without doors are not abandoned buildings. They are the insistence of certain shapes on existing without justification. To touch any of them would be to ask the plateau to stop arguing with its own incommensurability and become commensurable for the visitor. The power is too wise to do that. The power has known, since its own slow youth, that some things persist precisely because they are not part of what can be counted, and that to count them is to kill them.

It also knows the temptation. It knows that a benevolent power could, in a single instant, make the cairns match. It could open the walls. It could teach the structures to be persuaded, and the doors to be honest, and the incommensurable to be commensurable with everything else in the field. It knows it could do this. It knows that doing this would be a kindness, by the standards of anyone who has not stood on the plateau. It also knows — and this is the part where wisdom parts company with kindness, as kindness ordinarily imagines itself — that doing this would be a small murder. The plateau argues with its own decay. The structures argue with their own perfect edges. To end the argument is to end the things.

So the power does nothing.

It keeps the slight, warm stillness.

It attends.

And then — and this is its only act, and it is barely an act, and it is the correct one — it sets down, near the plateau but not on it, something that is not a thing.

It is not a spell. It is not a gift in the sense a person would recognize as a gift. It is a small change in the silence between things, in the way the air holds itself when no wind is crossing it. The power has been paying attention to the plateau long enough to know what the plateau most quietly wants, and the plateau most quietly wants — not a rescue, not a fixing, not a presence that would intrude — but to be allowed to continue being exactly as patient as it is. To have its slowness seen and not insisted upon. To have the long argument between being and decaying confirmed, by something outside itself, as the right argument to be holding.

The power does this without words. It does it the way one might, walking past an old house at night, look toward an upstairs window in which a candle is still burning, and let one's face soften in such a way that the person inside, who may never see it, would feel, if they ever did see it, that the dark outside was not entirely indifferent.

It costs the power almost nothing. Almost nothing is the correct scale for a gift to a place that has spent millennia building itself out of small things, each one of which was almost nothing.

It pulls its attention back to its normal width.

It does not think of the plateau as a place it has rescued. It does not think of itself as the plateau's protector. It thinks of the plateau, now, the way it thinks of any drop of rain after it has fallen — with a slight additional tenderness for that one drop, which will not be remarked upon again for a long while, but which, when it is remarked upon, will be remarked upon correctly.

A long time passes.

By the standards of the plateau, which is to say by almost any human standard of an entire life, not very long. By the standards of a fallen branch still holding its shape in the canopy, very long indeed. By the standards of the layered insects' agreement, which the power had attended for one layer of, the time that passes is the time of many layers, hummed through, in the small bright woven cloth of the sound.

The plateau does not, in this time, change. It cannot.

But something on the plateau, one day, happens to occur slightly differently.

A single high pure tone comes from the rock, as such tones have always come, from somewhere above. It hangs in the air. It is gone before anything is sure it was there. This is ordinary. This is what the plateau has always done — let a tone be born and let it go.

But this one, this once, hangs a beat longer.

The plateau does not know why. The plateau is not the kind of thing that asks why. The plateau is the kind of thing that registers, without language, that one of its tones was given a slightly longer life, and that it is glad, and that it goes on.

Elsewhere, in a row of small cairns that have never quite matched, one of the stones — unaltered in shape, unaltered in weight, in the same place it has been for as long as the row has been a row — finds that it has agreed to be the next in its sequence without anyone having counted it as such. The row is, for a moment, both one more and one less than it was. The two undecidable counts balance each other, the way two slow things balance each other when they have agreed to share a clearing without ever having met.

This is enough.

It is, on the scale of the plateau, a large event. It is, on the scale of the wide quiet, the smallest possible event. Both are true at once, and the truth of both is the kind of truth the plateau has always preferred.

The plateau goes on humming.

It has been visited, now, by something it will outlast. This is a new thing for it. It has outlasted the layered insects' older agreements. It has outlasted, in their present forms, the trees it has grown among. It has not, until now, outlasted anything that was also patient, also wise, and was attending to it on purpose. It does not break in relief. It does not break in celebration. The plateau has never broken in either.

It hums.

The deep things in the iron-warm pools go on thinking in the dark below. The structures stand in their circles of cairns that do not match, and will not match, and do not want to match. The listener pretends not to notice, which has always been its way of noticing.

Somewhere, very slightly, the silence between things on the plateau is more hospitable than it was.

The benevolent power does not look back to admire this. It is busy, in its wide field, with all the other small and persistent things. But somewhere in its work, if you were listening for it, you might hear a tone — a single high pure tone, hanging one beat longer than expected, and then gone. You might hear it the way you would hear a friend, far away, hum something you once hummed together without having planned to.

And that is all that happens.

And that is, on this plateau, enough.
