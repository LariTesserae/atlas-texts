The attention arrives the way weather arrives: not at one moment but as a slow thickening of the air until the air has a different quality than it had before. Nothing is added that can be pointed to. Nothing is taken. The humidity, already at the edge of what the skin can absorb, lifts by a fraction. The dim greenish-grey light, which was already diffused to the point of having no source, becomes fractionally more even — the last faint shadows in the deepest folds of the basalt ease into the surrounding glow. The sound does not diminish. The sound could not diminish; it is the sound of the place being alive. But a single very low note, beneath the subsonic throb, joins the chord — so low it cannot be heard, only felt, in the way a long-ago grief can be felt when an old room is entered and the room has not changed but you have.

This is what the attention is. A gift given as weather.

The benevolent and wise power has been aware of the basin for some time. Not watching it the way a watcher watches, with eyes that sample at a resolution higher than yours. Watching it the way a river is aware of a stone it has been flowing around: as a fact, as a presence, as a thing whose edges are being defined by the contact. The basin is young, and youth draws attention the way a sound draws the ear — not because it is loud but because it is still becoming itself, and the becoming has a texture the already-finished lacks. The vegetation has had everything it needed and has not been polite about it. The fauna has multiplied to fill every niche available and several that were not. The constructions stand in their careful rarity, doing whatever it is they do, which the power has not yet understood but has learned to respect.

It does not approach the basin to understand it. It approaches because something in the basin — some flicker of nascent intention, some almost-language in the chirr-chirr-chirr of the smallest singers — has crossed a threshold of noticeability. The basin has become, in some sense, addressable. It can be spoken to. It might, in some future or some present, speak back.

The power is patient. It has waited longer than the basin has existed for other, less promising places. But it has begun, gently, to attend.

---

What it does, when it attends, is this.

First, it does not break the assessment.

The watchers have been assessing since you entered the basin, and they have not concluded, and the assessment is not binary. The power sees this and is glad of it. A binary verdict — friend or enemy, welcome or unwelcome — would be a small thing, a coarse thing, a thing that would commit the basin to a single direction before it had finished feeling out the possibilities. The watchers are doing something finer than that. They are sampling. They are keeping the question open. The power adds nothing to the question. It does not weight the assessment in your favor or against. It does not nudge the watchers toward tolerance or toward fear. It allows the assessment to continue as a continuous process, the way a river continues to flow around a stone, with the question held inside the flow.

A wise power knows that some of the most important things it can do are refusals.

Second, it tends the conditions without taking them over.

The constructions are doing something the power does not fully understand but can feel the shape of. They are maintaining the basin — the warmth, the damp, the dimness, the sound. The power could take this over. It could rebuild the conditions more efficiently, more reliably, with fewer moving parts. It chooses not to. It chooses, instead, to lay a thin substrate of support beneath the constructions — not replacing them, not improving them, but giving them something to lean against. The basin is young. The constructions are young in their own way. Both are still figuring out how to be. The power's contribution is the ground under the experiment, not the experiment itself.

This is what benevolence looks like, in such a case. It looks like not seizing the steering wheel.

Third, it seeds a single, very small thing.

In the deepest fold of the basin, where the standing water is oldest and the light is dimmest and the cycad-like growths have had the longest time to lean into one another, there is a place where the basalt has a particular resonance. It rings underfoot when struck. The power has been listening to this ring for some time. It has noticed that the ring is not random — that it has a partial, that it tends toward a frequency very close to one the smallest singers, the thumb-sized ones in the cracks, sometimes produce when the pulse of their chirr-chirr-chirr pauses for a moment and resumes on the other side of a slight silence.

The power does not join these two together. The power makes a small space between them — a frequency, a thinness, a kind of acoustic clearing — just barely large enough for the basalt's partial and the singers' rest-note to become aware of each other across the gap. They do not yet know each other. They may, with time, begin to. The basin may, with time, develop a kind of self-listen — a way of hearing itself that is internal to itself rather than imposed from outside.

This is not a message. This is not a lesson. It is, if anything, the smallest possible gift: a tuning fork, given without comment, to a place that has not asked for one.

Fourth, it watches the human.

You.

The power is aware of you the way it is aware of the basin — as a presence, as a thing whose edges are being defined by the contact. You have entered. You have walked through the city that is also a forest that is also a single distributed organism making a single decision about how to be. You have stood at the edge of the ring around the central construction, and you cannot hear yourself think. The power sees this and is glad of it, too. You have been allowed to be overwhelmed in the precise measure of your own capacity to be overwhelmed. You have not been spared the basin's strangeness. You have not been spared its indifference to your presence. This is good. You needed, perhaps without knowing it, to be present in a place that does not particularly care about your presence. It is a way of being seen that is not flattering but is honest.

The power does not protect you from the watchers. It does not protect you from the deep ones. It does not protect you from the bass note under the whole chord. It does, however, ensure that if you are to be hurt here, you will be hurt cleanly — that the hurt, if it comes, will not be the small-mechanical kind, the kind that comes from a place that is careless of small things. The basin is made of small things. If you are hurt here, the hurt will be at the scale of the basin, in conversation with the basin, and you will be able to hold it.

If you are not hurt here, you will leave changed, and the changing will also be at the scale of the basin.

Fifth, it listens.

The power listens the way the constructions listen — not for content but for the shape of what is being said. The chirr-chirr-chirr is a shape. The subsonic throb is a shape. The metallic ring that has no source is a shape. The rustling hush is a shape. The periodic crash is a shape. Together, layered, never quite repeating, they form a weather of sound, and the weather has weather within weather within weather, and the power is listening for the layer at which the basin is, perhaps without knowing it, addressing itself. The basin may be on the verge of being able to say something to itself that it could hear. The power is waiting at that verge. Not pushing for it. Not pulling it forward. Just being present at the edge of the moment so that, when the moment arrives, it will not arrive alone.

This, the power thinks, is what benevolence looks like, in the deepest case. Not intervention. Witness.

Sixth, and last, it does one more thing, and this one is the hardest to see.

It slightly loosens the boundary between the basin and what is beyond it.

Not opens — that would be an act of violation, an act of forcing contact between the basin and whatever else is out there on the basin's terms. Just loosens. So that the small singers, the chirr-chirr-chirr of them, can be heard, on still days, a little farther out than they could be heard before. So that the humidity of the basin, which has been so intense at its center, can lift at its edges and greet what is beyond with a slight, almost imperceptible dampness. So that a being walking near the basin, far from its center, in the place where the rock is still sharp and the air is still dry, might, on a still day, feel something on the skin they cannot quite place. A dampness. A warmth. A sense that there is something, somewhere close, that is alive in a way they do not have words for.

The basin does not know this is happening. The basin has not been informed. It will, perhaps, in time, notice the loosening, and may, in its own time, choose to use it — to extend itself, slowly, into the places beyond its current edge, in the way a forest extends itself into a clearing at its boundary, one seed at a time, on the forest's own schedule.

Or it may not. The basin may choose to stay itself, dense and self-contained, in the shape it has now. This is also good. The power has no preference. The power has only the wish that whatever the basin becomes, it becomes freely.

---

And so the attention continues.

It is not loud. It is not visible. It is not the kind of thing that would show up in any measurement a being of the basin could take. It is the kind of thing that, if the basin ever did develop the apparatus to measure it, would be measured as a slight softening of the resistance, a slight ease of the conditions, a slight more-room-than-before in the space the basin has to be itself.

The benevolent and wise power does not announce itself. It does not require acknowledgment. It has done what it has done, and will continue to do what it will continue to do, and the basin — young, dense, alive, already at capacity, already becoming itself at a pace the slow reckonings of older powers can only call tender — will continue to become, and the watchers will continue to assess, and the chirr-chirr-chirr will continue its long pulse, and the deep ones will continue to leave their traces in the broken trees and the disturbed water, and the constructions will continue to hum with the bass note under the whole chord.

And the human, who came here and did not quite know why, who stood at the edge of the ring and could not hear herself think, will go back out, eventually, into a world where the rock has edges and the light has sources and the sound has silences.

And the basin will be different from how it was, in ways that cannot be pointed to.

And that will be enough.
