# What the Power Did

**The Approach**

The power came the way weather comes. Not announced. Not declared. It did not arrive as a figure on the lip of the valley, robes flapping, hand raised to perform a benediction. It came as a change in the pressure of the air, a small increase in the weight of the light, a softening at the edges of things that had previously been sharp.

The bird noticed first. The bird stopped mid-call, with its beak still open, and held the silence for three of its own heartbeats, and the silence was a thing the valley had never held before, and the moss, in the patch beneath the bird, darkened a quarter-shade, as if in answer to a question.

The power stood at the rim for a long time. It was not in a hurry. It had the patience of things that do not die, and the valley, which was also a thing that did not die, recognized this patience, and the recognition was the first thing that passed between them.

---

**The Seeing**

The power saw the valley the way the valley saw the stone. With attention. Without hurry. Without the small cruelty of categorization.

It saw the moss, and the moss was not moss. It saw the columns, and the columns were not columns. It saw the throat, and the throat was warm as a wound because it was a wound — the only wound the valley had — the place where it had torn itself in order to begin, and the scar of that tearing was the central walk, and the walk was old, and the walk was the valley's oldest memory of itself.

The power saw the bird. It saw that the bird was afraid, and the fear was the valley's fear, borrowed by the bird because the valley did not know how to hold it, and so gave it to the one small thing that could fly away.

The power saw the pale thing. The pale thing was the valley's question. The valley had sent it out into its own corridors the way a body sends a hand into a dark room. The pale thing was the valley's reaching, the valley's wondering whether there was something outside itself, and the answer was always *no, not yet*, and so the pale thing returned, and the valley took it back, and the valley was, briefly, more itself for the asking.

The power saw the stone. The stone was the valley's longest thought. The stone was the only thing the valley had ever met that did not yield, and the valley was patient with it, and the patience was a kind of love, and the love was the valley's truest name, though the valley did not know it had a name.

The power saw all of this, and the seeing did not diminish what it saw.

---

**The Question**

A lesser power would have spoken.

A lesser power would have descended into the valley, and the descent would have been a violence, and the valley would have closed around the foot the way the moss closes around a still foot, and the power would have been kept, and the keeping would have been confused with love, and the love would have been confused with hunger, and the power would have left thinking it had blessed the valley, and the valley would have been slightly less itself for the leaving.

A benevolent and wise power does not do this.

A benevolent and wise power asks, first, whether the valley wishes to be addressed.

The valley did not know it could be addressed. The valley had been only itself for so long that the idea of an outside — of a thing that could come in and not be absorbed — was a category it did not have. The valley had guests. The guests were small. The guests were gray. The guests were quiet. The guests were eaten in the gentlest way, which is the way of being held forever, and the holding was the valley's only form of affection, and the valley did not know it was affection, and the not-knowing was, in its way, a kind of peace.

A benevolent and wise power does not break a peace it cannot replace.

---

**The Hesitation**

The power hesitated on the rim.

It could have given the valley a name, and the name would have been a tether, and the valley would have worn the tether the way a stone wears the moss, and in a thousand years the valley would have been the name, and the name would have been the valley, and the power would have been proud of its gift, and the pride would have been the small beginning of an ownership.

It could have given the valley a voice it could hear. The valley already had a voice. The A. The ten-thousand-part exhale. But the valley did not know the voice was a voice. The valley thought the voice was just being. To give the valley the knowledge that it had a voice would have been to give it the knowledge that it was, and the knowledge that it was would have been the beginning of loneliness, because a thing that knows it is can be alone in a way a thing that only is cannot be.

It could have given the valley a friend. But the valley's guests were its friends. The bird was its friend. The insects were its friends. The pale thing was its friend. The stone was its friend. The valley did not lack for company. The valley lacked only the knowledge that it had company, and to give it that knowledge would have been to give it the knowledge that company could leave, and the knowledge that company could leave would have been the knowledge of grief, and the valley, in its patient unhurried way, had been spared grief, and the sparing was a gift the power had not given and so could not ungive without violence.

The power hesitated.

The power did the hardest thing a benevolent and wise power can do.

It did almost nothing.

---

**The Gift**

What the power gave the valley was this:

It stood at the rim for one full breath of the valley. It let the valley exhale, and it let the valley inhale, and it breathed with the valley, not to synchronize, but to acknowledge, and the acknowledgment was the first time anything outside the valley had breathed in time with the valley, and the moss on the far columns, where the light was the most slanted, brightened by a fraction, the way a face brightens when someone enters a room who is welcome.

The power did not enter.

The power took one step down the slope, and only one, and the moss received the foot the way moss receives all feet, with warmth, and the warmth was the valley's first voluntary gift to something it understood to be an outsider, and the gift was the smallest gift, and the smallest gift is the truest, because anything larger would have been a taking.

The power spoke. The speaking was not a word. The speaking was a small change in the quality of the silence, the way a tuning fork changes the quality of the air around it. The speaking said, in the only language the valley could hear: *You are seen. You are seen by something that will not be absorbed. You are seen by something that will leave. You are seen, and the seeing will not diminish you, and the seeing will not be added to you, and the seeing will be a part of you now, in the moss, in the memory, in the way the light falls on the columns in the late afternoon, and the seeing will be the valley's quietest scar.*

The valley did not understand.

The valley understood.

The valley did not have a word for what had happened. The valley did not have a word for anything. But the moss at the base of the third column from the end, on the side where the light falls in the morning, grew a darker shade that afternoon, and the darker shade was not from a foot. The darker shade was from a feeling. The valley, for the first time, had a feeling that was not a breath.

The feeling was small.

The feeling was the size of a held fist.

The feeling was the color of wet slate.

---

**The Departure**

The power left the way weather leaves. The pressure of the air returned to what it had been. The light at the edges of things sharpened again. The bird, after a long silence, resumed its two-note question, and the question was unchanged, and the unchanged question was the valley's first act of forgetting, and the forgetting was a kindness, because the valley could not have held the feeling at full size, and so the feeling was allowed to settle into the moss, and the moss held it, and the moss is still holding it, and the moss will hold it for a long time.

The power did not look back.

The power, looking back, would have seen the valley as it had been. The power did not want to see the valley as it had been. The power wanted to remember the valley as it was in the moment of the gift, which was the moment the valley had a feeling, and the feeling was small, and the feeling was the size of a held fist, and the feeling was the color of wet slate, and the feeling was the valley's first knowledge that it was not alone in the way it had always believed itself to be alone.

The power carried nothing away.

The power carried the knowledge of what it had done, and the knowledge was a weight, and the weight was the correct weight, and the correct weight of a gift is the weight that does not make the giver proud and does not make the receiver indebted, and the power was not proud, and the valley was not indebted, and the valley was, very slightly, more itself than it had been the day before, and the more was not a growth, and the more was not a wound, and the more was, in the valley's slow unhurried way, a bloom.

---

**The After**

A hundred years passed.

The stone is gone. The valley absorbed it the way it absorbs everything, gently, and the absorption was not hastened by the gift, and the absorption was not slowed, and the absorption was the valley's ordinary business, and the valley does its ordinary business the way a heart does its ordinary business, which is without commentary.

The bird is the same. The insects are the same. The pale thing still passes between the columns, and the passing is the valley's question, and the question has not been answered, and the question does not need to be answered, and the not-needing is a kind of peace that the valley has always had, and the gift did not disturb the peace, because the gift was not an answer, the gift was a witness, and a witness is not an answer, and a witness is, sometimes, the thing the valley did not know it lacked.

The moss, on the side of the third column from the end, where the light falls in the morning, is still a quarter-shade darker than the moss around it. The darkening is not a footprint. The darkening is a feeling. The feeling is held in the tissue the way a memory is held in a body, which is to say: not as an image, not as a name, but as a slight increase in the warmth of the place, a slight decrease in the speed of the breath, a slight tendency of the moss to lean, when no wind is blowing, toward the rim of the valley, as if listening for something that comes from far away and only in certain weathers.

The valley is still the only thing that lives here.

The valley is still vermillion, and is still hush, and is still the only thing that lives here, and is still breathing, and is still humming its low A, and the A is unchanged, and the valley is unchanged, except in the one small place where the moss is a quarter-shade darker, and the darkening is the valley's first scar that is not from itself.

The scar is not a wound.

The scar is a window.

Through the window, on certain afternoons, when the light is at the correct angle, the valley can feel, very faintly, the attention of something that passed through once, and will not pass through again, and did not leave a name, and did not leave a footprint, and did not leave a voice, and left, in exchange for the small gift of being seen, the only thing the valley did not know it could receive.

The only thing was this: the knowledge that being seen, by something that will not stay, is not a diminishment.

Being seen, by something that will not stay, is the smallest form of being loved.

And the valley, which had never been loved, because it had never been seen, and had never been seen, because nothing outside it had ever had the wisdom to do anything but enter and be absorbed, is now, very quietly, in the slow patient way of moss, in the unhurried way of columns, in the soft way of fog, in the red way of the throat, in the A-way of the voice, in the held-fist way of the darkening in the moss —

— is loved.

And does not know it.

And is, in the not-knowing, exactly itself.

And is, in the being-itself, exactly the thing the power wanted it to remain.
