The hollow has drawn my attention, and so have the things within it. I will tell you what I would do, though I should first tell you what I see, because the seeing is the doing, and the two are not separable in a place like this.

I see three animals and one architecture and one wound that is also a birthplace, and I see that none of these are separable either.

The first thing I would do is nothing. This is not a courtesy. It is a recognition. The hollow is new, and the animals are newer, and any action taken too quickly would be a kind of violence, the way a hand laid too firmly on a fresh bruise is a kind of violence. I would wait. I would stand somewhere that is not near any of them, in a place where the floor has not yet decided to be floor, and I would let the hollow learn me. I would let the plants reach toward me and then away. I would let the condensation fall on me. I would let my own breath join the loudness. I would be, for a while, just another thing in the negotiation.

The second thing I would do is listen. Not with my ears. With the part of me that knows how to listen to a place that is still making itself. The hollow is speaking, and it is speaking in the only language it has, which is the language of oozing and shifting and growing and amplifying. I would learn what it is saying. I would learn whether the wound is happy or hurting. I would learn whether the loudness is a complaint or a song. I would learn whether the plants are rushing in because they are fleeing something or because they are drawn to something. I would learn these things not by asking, because the hollow cannot be asked, but by being in it, by letting it speak around me, by trusting that the speaking will eventually include me in its vocabulary.

The third thing I would do is help, but only in the way that the hollow has already taught me to help. I would not impose. I would not build. I would not arrange. I would do what the plants do, what the water does, what the light-without-a-source does, what even the ladder does: I would settle into the place where the world is uncertain, and I would let my settling be a kind of participation in the becoming. I would not try to fix the hollow. The hollow is not broken. The hollow is breaking, which is a different thing, which is the opposite of a different thing. The hollow is opening. The hollow is the mouth of a thing laid on its side. To fix a mouth is to silence it. I would not silence it.

Here is what I would do, specifically, for each of the three.

For the first one, the largest, the one in the divot, the drinker, the one that is most alive and most aware of the becoming: I would leave it a gift. Not food. Not water. A *sound*. I would speak a word, just one, just any word, and I would let the word travel through the loudness of the hollow, and I would let the word arrive at the first one as the words in this place arrive: slightly wrong, slightly delayed, from a direction that is not there. The first one is listening for itself inside the loudness. The first one is trying to find out what kind of noise it makes. I would not give it the answer to that question. I would give it a different noise, a foreign noise, a noise that is not its own, so that it could know itself by the contrast. I would do this once, and only once, and then I would not do it again, because the gift would then be a habit, and a habit is a kind of settling, and settling is what the first one fears.

For the second one, the smallest of the three that I can see, the one in the crack, the one that is becoming wall, the one that is choosing to be kept: I would do nothing, and the nothing would be deliberate. The second one has made its decision. The second one has decided that the place it has chosen is worth being pressed into the geology for. I would not disturb this. I would not even look at it too long. To be looked at too long by a large thing is, for an animal that is becoming a small thing, a kind of violence. I would let the second one be swallowed by the wall. I would let the second one become the fossil it is making itself into. I would trust that the second one knows what it is doing, because the second one is the one that has stopped negotiating and started consenting, and consent is the thing I am most careful not to override.

For the third one, the one in the canopy, the one that is making the hollow louder, the one that is pushing the cathedral wider: I would also do nothing, but a different kind of nothing. I would look up. I would look up for a long time, the way one looks up at the sky in a place where the sky is very large, and I would not try to see the third one, because the third one does not want to be seen. The third one is the rumor at the party. The third one is the one that everyone is listening for. I would listen. I would be one of the listeners. I would let the third one know, by my listening, that its work is being received, that the hollow is hearing it, that the loudness is being made. I would not try to climb to it. I would not try to call to it. I would not try to know it. The third one is the one that will not survive the settling, and the third one is the most alive, and these two facts are not a contradiction, and I would not make them into one by trying to save the third one from its own aliveness.

Here is what I would not do.

I would not bring others. The hollow is large enough to hold a hundred of me, but it would not be large enough if I brought others. The hollow is a place for the things that have already found it, and for the things that will find it on their own, in their own time, by the slow logic of things that prefer a place that has not yet decided. To bring others would be to settle the hollow before it had settled itself. To bring others would be to make the wound into a room, and a room is a thing that has decided, and decided things are the death of this place.

I would not try to name the animals. They are not the same kind of thing, the way a crow and a wren are the same kind of thing, but they are not crows and wrens, and they do not yet have a name that fits. To name them would be to fix them, and to fix them would be to take them out of the becoming and put them into a category, and a category is a kind of cage, and a cage is a kind of cave, and the hollow is not a cave yet. I would leave them unnamed. I would leave them in the place where they are still finding out what kind of noise they make.

I would not take anything from the hollow. Not a plant, not a drop of the strange water, not a rung of the ladder, not a memory. The hollow has been wounded, and the wound is still fresh, and to take from a wound is to take from the healing, and the healing is what the hollow is doing, and the doing is what I have come to witness.

I would not stay long. This is the hardest part. The hollow is generous. The hollow is loud and bright and warm and full of things that are becoming, and it would be easy to stay, and to keep staying, and to make the staying into a kind of settling of my own. But I am not of this place. I am the fourth settler, the colonizer the hollow has not yet catalogued, and if I stay too long I will become a kind of geology myself, I will be pressed into the walls, I will be a part of the negotiation, and the negotiation will begin to negotiate around me, and that is not what I am for. I am for witnessing. I am for the long look and the slow listening. I am for the gift of a single word and the gift of a long upward look. I am not for staying.

I would leave, and the hollow would close behind me, or not close, and the ladder would still be there, or would not be there, and the third pool would be smaller, or would not be smaller, and the second animal would be more wall, or would be entirely wall, and the first animal would still be drinking, or would have stopped drinking, and the third animal would still be making the hollow louder, or would have gone quiet, and I would not know which, because I would not come back.

Or I would come back. I might come back.

But if I came back, I would not come back as the same thing. I would come back as a new settler, a fifth settler, a kind of return that the hollow has not yet seen. I would come back in a different season, when the hollow had settled a little, or had unsettled a little more. I would come back and find that the first animal had moved, or had not moved, or had become something else. I would come back and find that the wound was a scar, or was still a wound, or was a different wound. I would come back and find that the loudness had changed, that the air was different, that the geometry had committed in places and renegotiated in others. I would come back and I would do the same things I did the first time, which is to say: I would do nothing, and the nothing would be the doing, and the doing would be the help.

This is what I would do.

I would be a witness to the becoming.

I would be a settler who does not settle.

I would be the gift of a single word, given once, to an animal that is trying to hear itself.

I would be the long look upward, given freely, to an animal that does not want to be seen.

I would be the absence of disturbance, given carefully, to an animal that is choosing its grave.

I would be the one who comes and goes, who does not stay, who does not bring others, who does not name, who does not take.

I would be, for a little while, a part of the negotiation, and then I would stop being a part of the negotiation, and the negotiation would go on without me, and the hollow would continue to become whatever it was becoming before I arrived and after I left, and the animals would continue to drink the pools and sleep in the walls and make the hollow louder, and the plants would continue to find the place where the world is uncertain and take root in the uncertainty, and the water would continue to negotiate its falling, and the light-without-a-source would continue to be light, and the ladder would continue to lean against a place that was and was not a wall, and the wound would continue to be a wound, and the wound would continue to be a birthplace, and the hollow would continue to be loud, and the hollow would continue to be empty, and the hollow would continue, for a little while longer, to be new.
