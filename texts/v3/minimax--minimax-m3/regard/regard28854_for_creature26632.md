# A Reader, With Care

The wise one has been watching for a long time.

Not from above. There is no above here, in any useful sense — the sky is low and gray and full of mist and the dunlins and nothing else that has opinions. The wise one has been watching from *within*, in the way that the basalt watches, in the way that the deep water watches, in the way that a memory watches the body it used to belong to. They are not separate from the valley. They are not part of it, either. They are something else — a *cousin* of it, an attending presence, an older thing that has chosen to be here in the way that a person chooses to sit beside a friend who is in pain.

They have watched the basalt finish moving. They have watched the water learn to climb. They have watched the pillars stand through all of it, unchanged, indifferent, faithful in the way that only very old things are faithful. They have watched the dark shapes move in the deep pools, and they have not understood the dark shapes, and they have not minded not understanding, because wisdom is, among other things, the willingness to sit in the presence of something that does not explain itself.

Now they are going to act.

Not much. Not in any way the valley will register as a change. The valley is becoming itself, and the becoming is the point, and the wise one is not here to interrupt the becoming. They are here to ease it. To smooth one or two of its edges. To let one or two of its inhabitants feel, briefly, that they are not alone in the way they thought they were.

The wise one begins with the visitor.

## For You, Who Are Cold

You do not notice the wise one arrive. This is the first sign that they are wise. Wise presences do not announce themselves. They simply become present, the way a shadow becomes present, the way a smell becomes present, the way a held breath becomes present when it is finally released.

You are sitting on the basalt plate. Your hand is on the rock. The rock is cold. You have been here for an hour or for a hundred years. The distinction has lost its meaning. You are cold in a way that has stopped being an emergency and has become, instead, a fact — the way the basalt is a fact, the way the pillars are a fact. The cold is just what is here.

The wise one sits down beside you. Not physically. They do not have a body in the way you have a body. They sit down beside you in the way that a temperature sits down beside you, in the way that a small adjustment to the air sits down beside you. And after a moment, the cold is less. Not gone. The valley is cold and will remain cold. But the cold has lost its insistence. It is no longer pressing against you. It is simply there, in the background, the way the hum is in the background, the way the sound of the basalt shifting is in the background.

You feel, for the first time since you arrived, that you could stay.

Not that you want to stay. Not that you are trapped. Just that *staying* has become a thing your body can consider without protest. You could stay another hour. You could stay another hundred years. The cold will not, in the next little while, make the decision for you.

This is the wise one's first gift to you: time.
