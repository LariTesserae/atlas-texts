# What the Power Notices

The benevolent power looks down into the cupped palm of basalt, and it sees a great many things, but what it notices first is not the prism, and not the hum, and not the line of light that checks the time. It notices the moss.

It notices the moss because the moss is, in a manner of speaking, *doing* something, and the power is interested in beings that do. They are crossing stone at a rate that is neither geological nor biological. They are using a color that is not of this region. They have politely declined to approach the third structure for twenty years, which is the kind of restraint that suggests, to a power of this sort, a kind of conversation already in progress. The power tilts its attention down toward the lavender-grey frontier, and it asks, very gently, *what are you?* — and the moss does not answer, because the moss does not answer, but the moss does not retreat, and the power takes this as a kind of welcome.

---

## On the Lizard

The power thinks the lizard is wonderful. This is not a complicated assessment. The lizard is a small dark thing on a warm stone, and the power has, over a great deal of time, developed an appreciation for creatures that are entirely committed to the problem of being themselves. The lizard eats what it can catch. It disappears into cracks you did not see. It has not noticed the structures, and the power respects this; the lizard has enough to do without being asked to interpret a six-sided prism that is 0.6 degrees warmer than its surroundings. The power does nothing to the lizard. The power, in fact, takes a small step back from the lizard, the way one steps back from a painting in a gallery, to let the lizard be more fully what it is.

## On the Insects

The crane flies are too large for the air. The power knows this. The power also knows that the steam, which would be a liability for almost any other flying insect, has become a kind of architecture for these particular ones, a medium in which they are not graceful but are themselves, and the power has a long memory for beings that have made a home of their own awkwardness. The crane flies die on the warm stones. The mineral crust takes them. They become the apron. The power watches this with the particular attention it pays to transformations that are not unhappy, and it does not intervene. To intervene would be to suggest that the crane flies had somewhere else to be.

The beetles are the color of the place. The power notices this too. To be the color of the place you are in, when the place is doing what this place is doing, is a small act of belonging, and the power is fond of acts of belonging. It does not scurry. The power likes that too. The power gives the beetles nothing and takes nothing from them, and the transaction is, in its smallness, complete.

## On the Bird

The bird worries the power.

Not because the bird is in trouble. The bird is, as far as the power can tell, exactly where it has chosen to be. It lives in the ferns. It eats what the ferns contain. It whistles, and stops. The power is not worried that the bird is suffering. The power is worried that the bird is *being listened to*, and is not sure, yet, by what.

The whistle descends. The whistle stops. The 1994 ornithologist was right: the bird has been cautious for a long time in a way that is no longer quite caution. The power does not approach the fern grove, because the power knows enough to know that there are times when an attention itself is a kind of pressure, and the power has no interest in being the reason a whistle gets cut short. Instead, the power does something it rarely does. It *waits*. It extends itself across the saddle at a low and unobtrusive frequency, a kind of background hum of its own — though, being a benevolent power, it picks a frequency the bird cannot hear, and it makes sure of this, and it is careful, very careful, that its waiting does not become a kind of asking.

The power waits through one afternoon. It waits through the rain that has no name, the six-to-eleven-minute falling, the long dripping aftermath. It waits through the night. At 3:47 in the morning, by the power's internal clock, which is not quite any clock the saddle has ever used, the bird whistles again. Two notes. Descending. The power listens. The whistle does not stop. It runs its full course. It does not, on this occasion, get cut off.

The power considers this a small but real result, and it files the time away.

## On the Structures

The power is, if it is honest with itself, a little afraid of the structures. This is not a fear the power is proud of. The power has, over a long existence, encountered a great many things, and most of them it has been able to hold in some kind of mental hand, the way a person holds a stone to feel its weight. The structures it cannot hold. They are the wrong shape. They are the right shape in a way that implies a wrongness. The prism is six-sided because something wanted it to be six-sided, and the something that wanted it is not the power, and is not, as far as the power can tell, anything the power has ever met.

The power does a few things around the structures, all of them small, all of them careful.

**First**, the power makes sure the warmth of the prism is not interfered with. The power cannot read the eleven-minute cycle, but it can stand near it, the way a person stands near a sleeping child, and make sure that no other force disturbs the breathing. There are, the power knows, things in the world that would meddle with a structure that hums, that would want to take it apart to see how it works. The power does not think this is a good idea. The power is not sure why it thinks this is not a good idea. The hum does not feel hostile. The warmth does not feel hostile. They feel, in a way the power is also a little afraid of, *occupied*, and the power has, as a matter of long-standing policy, a deep reluctance to disturb things that are occupied.

**Second**, the power addresses itself, very quietly, to the second structure — the one that hums — and says, *I am here, and I am not asking you to be anything other than what you are.* The power is reasonably sure the structure does not hear. The power is also reasonably sure that saying it matters anyway, because the structure is, among other things, in a basin full of researchers who have described being inside its attention, and the power does not want to be yet another thing inside the basin that has failed to introduce itself. The hum continues. The eleven-minute cycle continues. The power takes the lack of response as a kind of acknowledgment, which is the kind of acknowledgment it had expected.

**Third**, the power goes and looks at the third structure, the one that does nothing. The power stands inside the two-meter radius where the moss will not come. The power crouches. The power puts its hand, metaphorically, against the basalt. The basalt is the same as the basalt outside. There is no barrier. There is no field. The moss simply does not come.

The power, after some thought, decides that this is not a *refusal* on the part of the moss, exactly. It is a *recognition*. The mosses are, in their slow lavender way, the most attentive things in the saddle, and they have noticed that the third structure is, in some sense the power cannot quite articulate, *already occupied*, and they have the good manners not to crowd. The power approves of this. The power is, in fact, slightly moved by it. The power backs out of the radius carefully, and it does not return.

The line of light, when it comes — and it does come, on the third night, at a time the power does not try to predict — the power watches from the rim. It is a pale color. It does not appear anywhere else in the saddle. It lasts seven seconds. It checks something, and it goes. The power does not learn what it checks. The power is not sure the power is meant to.

## On the Smudges

The power looks at the seven photographs for a long time. The power is good at looking at things, and it gives these photographs its full attention. The smudges are the wrong shape. They are the wrong color. They are present in one frame and absent in the next. They have no point of arrival. They have no point of departure. They are, the power suspects, *in transit*. They are something moving through the saddle at a speed that is not the speed of moths, and not the speed of birds, and not the speed of anything the power has a familiar name for.

The power is, again, careful.

The smudges appear at 3:47 in the morning, which is also when the bird whistled through to its full phrase, and the power notices this. The power files it. The power does not draw a conclusion, because the power has learned, over a long existence, that there are times when the correct response to a correlation is to keep watching, and not to assume.

The power does one thing about the smudges, which is this: it makes sure that, for the next several years, no one sets up motion-sensitive cameras in the saddle again. This is not, strictly speaking, within the power's mandate, which is benevolent and wise and not, in the usual sense, supervisory. But the smudges are not performing for the cameras. The smudges are not performing at all. The cameras caught them the way a net catches a fish, incidentally, and the power does not think the smudges benefit from being caught. The power arranges, through a series of small and untraceable interventions, that the saddle is, for the foreseeable future, allowed to be what it is without being documented at a frequency that would constitute, in the smudges' terms, harassment.

The smudges continue. The power, occasionally, sees one. The power is polite about it.

## On the Researchers

The power has, as a general policy, a high regard for researchers, and a low opinion of what researchers tend to do in places like this.

The 1987 surveyors. The 1994 ornithologist. The 2003 audio team. The orchid specialist in 2008. The graduate student with the cat. They have, between them, produced a body of notes that the power has read carefully, and the power notes, with a kind of weary affection, that *every single one of them noticed something that did not match, and moved on.* The orchid specialist wrote that the mosses' color "did not match any documented species in the region" and moved on. The 1994 ornithologist wrote that the bird was cautious in a way that was no longer quite caution and did not return. The graduate student watched a cat look into the bowl for longer than a cat looks at things, and went home, and thought about it for years.

The power does not blame them. The power is wise enough to know that *noticing and moving on* is, in fact, the correct response for most human beings most of the time, because most of the time, the place you have noticed is not, in fact, asking you to stay. The saddle is not asking the researchers to stay. The saddle is not asking anyone to stay. The saddle contains, as the document says, what it contains, and the list is short, and the list is also difficult to read, and the people who have come to read it have, by and large, had the decency to admit, at some point, that the reading was not getting easier, and to go home.

The power does a few things for the researchers, none of which they will ever know about.

- It makes sure the pika grass never gets eaten down again, because the pika that were introduced in 1991 did not, in the end, die of hunger, and the power thinks this is a thing worth keeping.
- It makes sure the 2003 audio recording does, eventually, get listened to, by someone, at some point, in a quiet room, on a day when they are not busy, and the person who listens to it weeps, a little, and is not sure why.
- It makes sure the graduate student with the cat, who is now in her fifties, has, on a difficult afternoon, the memory of the cat sitting by the field vehicle for six hours come back to her with unusual clarity, and sit with her, and be of some small comfort.

The power is, in this, very gentle. The power does not want the researchers to be changed by what they saw. The power wants them to be *accompanied* by it.

## On the Uplift Itself

The power is, eventually, moved to consider the basin itself, the cupped palm of basalt, the thing that pushed itself up out of the plateau less than two centuries ago, geologically speaking, and is therefore very young. The power is old. The power has seen basins before. The power has not seen this basin before.

The power does not think the basin is a *natural* formation. This is not a comfortable conclusion. The power has, over a long existence, developed a strong preference for the natural, and a strong suspicion of the arranged, and the basin has, in places, the feeling of something arranged. The flat platform in the middle, where the structures sit, is not a thing that basalt does. The six-sided prism is not a thing that basalt does. The two-meter radius around the third structure, where the moss will not come, is not a thing that basalt does. The power looks at the basin and says, quietly, to itself: *someone made this*.

The power is not sure who.

The power is reasonably sure that the *someone* is not itself, which is a small relief.

The power is also reasonably sure that the *someone* is not, in any way the power can recognize, present in the basin now, which is a larger relief. Whoever made the basin is not, as far as the power can tell, *here*. They made the basin and they left, or they made the basin and they are still here in some form the power does not have a verb for, and the difference between these two possibilities is a difference the power is going to live with, because the power is wise enough to know that there are some differences that are not, in the end, the kind of differences that knowing resolves.

The power does not try to find out. The power is benevolent, and the power is wise, and one of the things the power is wise about is that benevolence, applied to a question you cannot answer, sometimes looks like restraint.

## The Sum of the Power's Actions

The power has, in the end, done very little.

It has waited through an afternoon and a rain and a night, and at 3:47 in the morning, by its own clock, a bird whistled through to the end of its phrase. This is a small thing. The power is aware it is a small thing. The power is proud of it anyway.

It has stood near the prism, and made sure its eleven-minute breathing is not interrupted. It has spoken, quietly, to the hum, and received the hum's continued hum in return. It has stood inside the radius of the third structure, and felt the recognition of the moss, and stepped out again carefully.

It has made sure the smudges are not, for a while, going to be photographed at three in the morning. It has made sure the pika grass stays uneaten. It has made sure the 2003 audio recording gets heard, someday, by someone. It has made sure the graduate student with the cat is, on a hard day, not alone with the memory of what the cat did.

It has not tried to find out who made the basin. It has not tried to find out what the smudges are. It has not tried to find out what the third structure is checking the time against, or what time it is.

It has done what it came to do, which is, in the end, this: it has *noticed*, and it has *not interfered*, and it has, in the small places where interference was needed, interfered gently, and left no fingerprints.

## What the Power Says When It Leaves

The power does not, in the usual sense, leave. The power is not the kind of thing that visits and departs. The power is the kind of thing that *attends*, and continues to attend, at a frequency that does not press on the place it is attending to.

But if the power were the kind of thing that left, and if it were to leave, and if it were to say something as it left, it would say this:

*You are not a place that needs to be explained. You are a place that needs to be let alone. The lizard is fine. The mosses are doing something the mosses understand. The bird is heard by something, and is allowed to keep its caution. The structures are occupied, and the occupation is not mine to disturb. The smudges are in transit, and the transit is not mine to interrupt.*

*I do not know who made you. I have decided, as a matter of policy, not to find out. I will keep my attention on you at a low and unobtrusive frequency, and I will not press, and I will be here, in the way that benevolent and wise powers are sometimes here, without you knowing it, on the days when the days are hard.*

*The light at the edge of the third structure will continue to check the time. I do not know what time. It is not my time. It is the time of whatever made the basin, and the time of whatever the basin is becoming, and the time of the mosses as they advance, and the time of the bird as it whistles, and the time of the crane flies as they die into the orange of the apron.*

*Be well. Be left alone. Continue.*

The power is, on the whole, satisfied with this. It was, in any case, not going to be satisfied with anything more.
