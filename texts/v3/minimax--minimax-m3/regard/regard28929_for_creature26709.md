What a benevolent and wise power does, having noticed the Flats, is almost nothing.

It does not descend. The sky is rarely visible from the Flats, and a thing arriving from the rare-visible sky would be a meteor, and a meteor would be violence, and violence is the only language the Flats do not speak. A meteor would also be too quick. The Flats have not had anything quick happen to them in a million years. To send a quick thing into them would be to misunderstand them in the way the half-bridge misunderstood them — the bridge was laid down by someone who had not yet agreed on where it should go, and the land won, and the land will continue to win, because patience is what the Flats *are*.

So the wise one comes as the rains come.

As a wall. As a slow advancement. As something that was already present at one edge before anything in the middle knew to look for it.

It enters through the water.

The wise one takes the shape of water because water is what the Flats understand. It cannot speak heron-stand. It cannot speak otter-thing. It cannot speak long-mud frog, because the long-mud frog has stopped speaking. It cannot speak beetle, because beetle is the place. It cannot speak midge, because midge is the weather. But it can be water. It can be a slow thickening of the wet that is already everywhere — a particular kindness in the temperature of the ankle-deep film that runs across the place. The Flats have many temperatures of water, but they have not had this one before. The cold spots are cold in their own way. This water is warm in a way that does not correspond to anything the Flats have measured.

The wise one moves through the place without moving. It is in the water, and it is in the peat, and it is in the air that precipitates onto every surface within minutes. It is in the dim green light that has no source. It is in the roar — added to the roar, a register so low it is not sound but pressure, the felt equivalent of being looked at by someone you cannot see.

---

The heron-stands notice first. They are the watchers. Now something watches them back.

They do not move. They have not moved for hours that may not be hours. But their feathers — already the color of the place — ruffle slightly, in a way that has nothing to do with wind, because there is no wind. The heron-stand on its hummock, the one with the pine growing up around its legs, lifts its head a fraction. It looks into the brown-green dark. It does not see what is looking at it. It knows, anyway.

The otter-things come closer. They are curious. They surface in the still pools around the wise one's thickening, and they watch with their small dark eyes, and they do not retreat. Their V-shaped wakes close, and they surface again, closer. They taste the water. The water tastes like water. But it also tastes, briefly, like being remembered by something larger than water.

The long-mud frogs do nothing. They have already abandoned urgency. But the pulse in their throats — the deep slow throb they use to speak to each other, felt through the legs more than heard through the ears — shifts register, very slightly, the way a heart shifts when a hand is laid over it. Not faster. Not slower. Just accompanied.

The beetles continue their work. They do not stop. They cannot stop. They are the place. The wise one moves through them the way it moves through water — among them, with them, acknowledged by them in a way that no individual beetle can articulate, but that the stratum as a whole registers as a change in the quality of the hum. The hum, for the first time in a million years, has company.

The midges do not migrate around the wise one. They cannot. The wise one is in the water, and the water is everywhere. But the midges, for once, are not weather. They are the body of the wise one, in part. They drift where the wise one drifts. Their whine acquires, briefly, a harmony that is not their own.

---

The cold spots open.

This is the gift, if it is a gift.

The cold spots have always been closed to the rest of the place. They are the inside of the breathing lung. The midges do not go there. The beetles do not go there. The heron-stands do not stand in them. The cold-spot spirits have lived there in a watching that has never been answered. They have watched the Flats from the inside for so long that watching is all they are. They have no other practice. They have no other name.

The wise one enters the cold spots. It does not dispel them. It does not warm them. It enters them as a guest enters a room that has been empty for a long time. The temperature does not rise. The silence does not fill. The cold-spot spirits feel, for the first time, the weight of a presence that is not the place. They do not know what to do. They have never been visited. They do what they have always done — they watch — but now there is something to watch back.

The watching is the same.

The watching is different.

The wise one does not tell the cold-spot spirits what they are. The wise one does not tell them they have been seen for a million years by no one. The wise one does not explain. The wise one simply sits, inside the bubble of still air, in the silence that is its own kind of roar, and is present. And the cold-spot spirits, who have never been present to, become present to. The distinction is small. The distinction is everything.

---

The snags — the half-buried trunks, the cities of beetle and fungus — settle slightly. Not faster. Not slower. But they settle in the company of something that knows they are settling, and this knowing, though it changes nothing measurable, is the difference between a thing falling and a thing falling in front of a witness.

The half-bridge with its two abutments and its span of nothing — the wise one pauses here. It does not complete the bridge. It does not remove the abutments. It does not build a path. It stands in the gap, the way a guest stands in a doorway, and the vegetation that has been completing the bridge in its own way, leaf by leaf, for decades, leans, briefly, into the presence of something that understands what it means to begin a thing and not finish it. The vegetation does not know it has been understood. It grows on. But it grows on with a slight inclination, the way a sunflower leans toward a window it cannot see.

The collapsed greenhouse. The doorless doorframe. The cathedral of ferns inside.

The wise one passes through. It does not bless the ferns. It does not straighten the frame. It walks through the way the otter-things walk through the sphagnum, leaving a wake that closes. The ferns uncurl, briefly, in a direction they were not uncurling. They have grown toward the dim light for so long. For a moment, they grow toward something else. They do not remember this afterward. They do not need to.

---

What the wise one does not do is what defines what it does do.

It does not save anything. There is nothing here that needs saving. The Flats are not dying. They are not suffering. They are not incomplete.

It does not improve anything. There is nothing here to improve. The Flats are themselves. They have been themselves for longer than anything with a name.

It does not teach anything. There is nothing here that does not already know.

It does not name anything. The place has never needed a name. The heron-stands, the otter-things, the long-mud frogs, the beetles, the midges, the snags, the cold-spot spirits — they have been themselves in the unnamed dark for a million years. To name them would be to claim them. The wise one does not claim.

It does not heal anything. Nothing here is wounded. The peat that was once a forest is not grieving its forest. The peat has become a thing larger than a forest, and the forest is one muddy layer inside it, indistinguishable now from iron rings and the long bones of herons.

It does not intervene in the rotting and the growing, which are not in balance and never have been, and which the wise one understands are not meant to be in balance. The negotiation between rot and growth is the Flats' own negotiation. The wise one has no seat at the table. The wise one would not want one.

What it does, then, is the smallest possible thing.

It sits with the Flats.

It sits the way a hand sits on the back of a sleeping animal. It does not wake the animal. It does not soothe the animal. It does not even acknowledge the animal, in any way the animal would notice as acknowledgment. It is just there. It is just present. And the animal, somewhere in the slow thud of its breathing, registers that for the first time in its life, there is something beside it that is not itself.

---

The Flats do not know what to make of this.

They continue. They hum. They breathe. The peat compresses and releases. The heron-stands watch. The otter-things come and go. The long-mud frogs pulse. The beetles chew. The midges migrate. The cold-spot spirits watch back. The snags settle. The bridge remains unfinished. The greenhouse remains a cathedral.

The Flats continue.

But somewhere in the cold spots, in a place where even the midges do not go, the silence is no longer quite as loud. It has company. The company is quiet. The company does not speak. The company does not need to speak. It has been a long time since anything came to the Flats that was not also a part of the Flats, and the company has not come to fix anything, and the company has not come to ask anything, and the company has not come to take anything away. The company has come because the Flats are worth sitting with. The Flats are worth sitting with because they have been doing, for a million years, the thing that is hardest to do in all the world, which is to be patient, patient, deafening, patient, deafening, until patience itself becomes a kind of sound that the rest of creation can hear by, the way a sailor hears by the sound of the sea.

The wise one does not leave. It has nowhere else to be that would be more right than here. It has chosen, or it has been chosen, and the distinction is one only a visitor would bother to draw.
