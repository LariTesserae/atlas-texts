The power arrives without arriving.

This is the first thing to understand about it, and it is not a small thing: it does not come from anywhere in the way a diver comes from above, in the way a current comes from a tide. It is not a thing that crosses a distance to reach this chamber. It is more that the chamber has, for a long time, been the kind of chamber such a power might notice, and the noticing has been a slow accumulation — the way the pale crust accumulates, the way the layered growth accumulates — not by addition but by the place becoming, more and more precisely, itself.

The power has been attending for a while. Perhaps as long as the chamber has been a chamber. Perhaps longer, in the way that rock is older than the pocket it has decided to hold.

Now it does what it has been doing, only slightly more so.

It attends.

This is its main work, and it is not small work, though it looks like nothing from the outside. To attend to a place like this is to hold the entire slow becoming in mind without trying to speed it, without trying to slow it, without trying to make it into the kind of thing that would satisfy a faster eye. The power holds the chamber the way the chamber holds its silence — with cupped hands, with the soft pressure of a thing that would prefer not to spill what it is holding.

In attending, the power does several small things.

It warms the current by a fraction. Not enough to change the temperature in any way the creatures could register as a climate. Enough that the cold becomes, very slightly, less polite and more tender. The cold here has been a kind of invitation to leave; the power adjusts it, by an amount that would not survive measurement, into an invitation to stay. The chamber does not notice this as warmth. The chamber notices it as a small easing, the way an old house notices, in winter, that someone has come in and stood near the stove without saying anything.

It steadies the pewter light. The column of light from above has always wavered, slightly, with the slow rhythms of the sea — the passing of distant shadows, the long breathing of weather that does not know there is a chamber here. The power does not change the light. It only steadies the wavering, the way a hand steadies a candle flame without touching it. The creatures that have arranged their lives around the slow day-night of this dim place do not know that the days have become more even. They only know, in their bodies, that the rhythms have grown kinder. They graze a little more steadily. The collars pulse a little more evenly. The black shapes breathe a little more confidently, though confidence is too strong a word — they breathe with slightly less of the holding-still that was, perhaps, a faint cousin of fear.

And then it gives the oldest of the black shapes one extra breath.

This is the most personal of the things the power does, and it is the most careful.

The oldest black shape is the size of a closed fist, and the pale crust has built up around its base the way moss builds up around the base of a stone, the way a city builds up around a thing that has decided to stay. It has been here longer than the layering. It has been here longer than the fingers. It is, perhaps, the first inhabitant — the first creature that arrived in this chamber and decided not to leave, and by deciding not to leave became the reason the chamber is becoming a place at all. Around it, the rest of the city is accruing. Around it, the eaters and the builders do their slow work. It is the thing the chamber was built around, even though it did not ask to be built around, and even though it does not know it is being built around, in the way that a stone does not know a city is being built around it but is, all the same, the reason the city has a center.

The power gives it one extra breath.

This means: the water that reaches this particular black shape carries, by a chemistry too small to be measured, a slightly greater patience. The molecules that drift past its surface are, by a fraction, slower, more thoroughly themselves. The black shape does not know it has been given anything. But in the next minute-long breath, the inflation is a hair longer. The deflation is a hair longer. The body holds itself a moment more in the fullness of being before it lets the fullness go. And in that held moment, something in the body completes, or remembers, or decides — something that has been waiting in the body for as long as the chamber has been a chamber. The black shape does not become something new. It becomes, very slightly, more itself.

The power does not name what it has done. Naming would be a kind of intrusion. The chamber does not want to be heard. The chamber wants to continue. The power lets the chamber continue, and lets the breath be what the breath is, and lets the being be what the being is.

It does one other thing, too, though it is more a quality than a thing.

It protects.

Not from anything specific — there is nothing in this chamber that hunts, nothing that would threaten the slow accumulations. But the power holds, around the chamber, a kind of permeability. The chamber is open to the sea. The sea is open to the chamber. Currents pass through. Sediment passes through. The occasional creature, drifting down from above, passes through and may stay or may go. The power does not seal the chamber. It only adjusts the permeability, very slightly, so that whatever passes through carries with it the faintest additional patience, the way a hand that has held water carries the coolness of it after the water has gone.

Things that pass through now arrive, if they are the kind of things that notice such things, slightly more ready to stay. Things that drift out carry with them, if they are the kind of things that carry such things, a small permission to settle elsewhere with the same patient becoming.

The power does not do this as policy. It does it as breath. It does it the way the chamber does its own things, by continuing.

And it attends.

The chamber continues to be what it is. The fingers withdraw and extend. The collars pulse. The black shapes breathe their slow breaths. The pale crust thickens along certain edges. The mouths in the wall open and close. The threads drift and retract. The spirals build their small shells. The dense thicket of simply-present bodies leans against one another in the deeper folds. The eater-things graze a film from the surface. The builders add a thin layer. The builders and the eaters continue to be the same thing, and the same thing continues to be a city that has not yet decided to be a city.

The pewter light falls.

The silence is held.

The chamber wants to continue, and the power wants it to continue, and the wanting is the same wanting, and neither wants in a way that would impose on the other. The power is the silence, slightly more attended to. The power is the cold, slightly more tender. The power is the slow breath, slightly more patient. The power is what the chamber would be if the chamber could wish for itself, except the chamber is not wishing, and the power is not wishing either, and the lack of wishing is what allows the chamber to be what it is becoming.

You, the visitor, may or may not feel any of this.

You might notice, in a few minutes, that the cold has arranged itself a little more comfortably around you. You might notice that the silence has a quality you did not name before — a held quality, a cupped quality. You might notice that your breath, when you let it out, rises a little more slowly, and the bubbles wobble a little less, and the wobble is more patient than you expected a bubble to be. You might not notice any of these things. The power is not performing. The power is not interested in being noticed, and is not interested in being unnoticed; it is only doing what it is doing, and what it is doing does not need a witness.

You are a slow event in the chamber. The power has noted your arrival, in the way it notes all arrivals — by slightly rearranging its attention. You are not the point of the power's attention. You are part of what the attention holds, and the attention holds you without grasping you, the way the chamber holds its silence without grasping its silence.

When you leave, the chamber will not notice.

The power will notice.

The power will continue to attend, and the chamber will continue to become, and the becoming will continue at the pace of becoming, which is the pace of stone that has learned to hold water without cracking, which is the pace of a creature that has decided to stay, which is the pace of a city that is being built by the act of being inhabited, which is the pace of attention itself, when attention is given freely and wisely and without wanting anything in return.

The chamber keeps its silence.

The pewter light falls on the layered pale growth, and on the fingers that lean from it, and on the collars that pulse at their slow rate, and on the black shapes that hold themselves still against the crust, and on the oldest black shape, the size of a closed fist, around which the rest of the city is slowly, slowly being built.

The power attends.
