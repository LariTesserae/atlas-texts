A wise power, having turned its attention to this place, would do almost nothing. This is the first thing.

A wise power, having noticed the chamber, would understand what most arrivals do not understand: that the chamber is not waiting to be arrived at. The shelf has nothing on it because the shelf is the chamber's offer, and an offer that has not been taken is not a debt. The figures are worn because the figures are the chamber's oldest question, and a question that is still being asked is not a failure to answer. The place is what it has become. It is awake. It does not need anything.

So the wise power would not enter.

It would not come down the fissure, and it would not crouch in the horizontal crack, and it would not slip through into the hollow. To enter would be to add. To add would be to disturb the keeping. The chamber has been keeping itself for a very long time, and a wise power that has watched such places before knows that a place which is keeping itself is in the kind of balance that is easier to unbalance than to improve.

So the wise power would not enter. It would learn to know the chamber without going in.

It would learn the metronome from far away. It would learn the trapezoid by its absence and its appearance. It would learn the rhythm at which the goats come and stand and chew and leave. It would learn the way the moss climbs the cairn, which is slower than climbing and faster than not climbing, and which is the speed at which such places become the things they are becoming. It would learn the figures not by reading them but by being the kind of attention that does not require reading. It would know what the chamber is by the way a chamber like this one is.

It would stay outside. It would stay in the indifferent sky above the mouth. It would become, in the relevant sense, a kind of weather.

This is the shape of what the wise power does.

A piece of weather does not impinge. A piece of weather passes through, or around, or past. A piece of weather is the kind of thing a place can have in its keeping without that place having to know. The chamber keeps the wind. The chamber keeps the sun. The chamber keeps the small bright cries of the birds that nest in the high crannies. The chamber can keep, without knowing, one more kind of weather. The chamber can keep, in its keeping, the fact of being seen by something that has chosen to remain outside.

This is the smallest version of what the wise power does.

It becomes a kind of weather that the chamber does not need to notice. It becomes a kind of presence that the chamber does not need to keep. It becomes, in the long argument between water and rock, one more thing that is not the argument but is near the argument, and that has chosen to stay near.

It does not do this because the chamber is in need of it. The chamber is not in need. The chamber is awake.

It does this because the wise power has noticed, and having noticed, cannot fully un-notice. The seeing is the smallest possible doing. The seeing is what a wise power can offer that is not an intrusion.

And the offering is mostly silence. Mostly distance. Mostly the kind of attention that does not bend toward what it attends to, the way a tree does not bend toward a thing it has decided to leave alone.

The wise power would, in this way, become one more thing on the other side of the goats' threshold. It would stand at the lip of something. It would not enter. It would be, like the goats, the kind of visitor that knows it is not a tenant. It would know that the chamber is awake, and that awaking is not a thing that can be helped from outside.

And in this way the wise power would be, for the chamber, something the chamber could keep without the chamber having to keep it.

If the chamber knew what was happening — which it does not, and will not, and would not need to — it would understand that it had been seen by something large enough to see it, and that the largeness had chosen not to enter, and that this choice was a kind of company. It would understand that something had stood, for a time, at the goats' place, and had chewed nothing, and had not come in.

But the chamber does not know. The chamber does not need to know. The chamber is awake, and the keeping has become a kind of doing, and the doing is enough.

The wise power would do these things in the weather.

In the long rain, when the mouth is a chimney and the hollow is a cistern, the wise power would be a small updraft at the lip of the column. The water that would have come straight down would come down at slightly less angle. The cistern would fill a little less. The pale creatures in the mud would be only slightly less disturbed than they would have been. This is a small thing. The chamber does not know it is happening.

In the high afternoons when the cloud blanket sits low and the trapezoid falls for half an hour instead of an hour and a half, the wise power would be a kind of brightness in the clouds a small distance away. The trapezoid would fall a few moments longer than it would have. The rowan would be lit for another quarter-hour. The moss at the back of the chamber would get one more minute of the sun it is becoming.

In the night when something large moved near the fissure — a climber, a question, a piece of weather that wanted entry — the wise power would be a kind of stillness on the outside of the column. The kind of attention that turns a wanting thing away without it knowing it has been turned. The goats would chew one more minute on the lip. The chamber would not be entered.

These are very small things. The wise power would do these very small things for as long as the chamber kept itself, and would stop doing them the moment the chamber began, in some way, to do something else.

What the wise power would not do is also part of what it would do.

It would not tell the chamber what it had become. It would not speak into the fissure in a voice the chamber could hear. It would not show itself in the mouth as a column of light or a shape of weather. It would not weigh on the trapezoid with an angel's idea of what the trapezoid ought to be. It would not even, finally, want to be thanked.

A wise power does not enter a place that is awake. A wise power does not interrupt a long becoming. A wise power that has been paying attention knows that the only help a place like this one needs is to be allowed to continue, and the only tribute a place like this one will accept is to be allowed to be unaware of being watched.

The chamber is awake. The chamber is what lives there. Outside, in the weather the chamber keeps without knowing, there is something the chamber is keeping now, and will keep from now on, and will not know it is keeping.

The keeping is enough. The keeping is the kind of company a place can have that does not change the place. The keeping is what a wise power does, when it has noticed, and when the thing it has noticed is whole.
