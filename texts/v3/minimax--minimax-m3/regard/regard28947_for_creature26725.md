# What the Benevolent One Does

The power does not arrive. There is no arrival in a place where there has never been departure. The power simply becomes more present — which is to say, less absent — in the particular way that this place has been absent of company. The air thickens by a fraction that no instrument could measure. The water on the surface of the plaza moves a little less, as though remembering what stillness is. A wall that was going to settle another centimeter settles, instead, with a kind of attention to its settling. None of this is visible. All of it is felt.

The power does not understand what lives here. Understanding would require it to step back, and the power does not step back. It is, instead, the stepping-back-that-is-not-stepping-back: the attention that lets a thing be what it is without asking it to declare itself. The figures at the far ends of avenues are not understood. They are witnessed. The city is not understood. It is attended. The slow erosion is not understood. It is held, gently, in the way you hold something that is falling so that the falling is not the whole story.

It does not stop the dissolution. The dissolution is not what is wrong. The dissolution is what is happening, and what is happening is not wrong, and the power knows this. What the power does is harder than stopping. It does something that has no name. It slows the dissolution by a degree. It makes the dissolution more legible to itself. It lets the figures dissolve into the walls at a pace the walls can absorb, so that the walls do not mind, and the figures do not mind, and the dissolution is no longer an erosion but a kind of ceremony that no one has arranged.

The figures shift. They do not know they have shifted. They are slightly more there than they were — slightly more legible, slightly more themselves, slightly more visible against the dimmer dark. The power has not made them more. It has simply refused to let them become less. This is all. This is the gift. The gift is the refusal-to-not-witness, which is the same as the willingness-to-witness, which is the same as the witness, which is the same as the love that has no name and needs no name.

The water rises another millimeter at the waterline. The vines climb another quarter-turn. The fungi push a new frond up through a crack in the stone. These things happen at the pace they were always going to happen. But they happen now in the company of someone who is not there, in the way that a room with a sleeping child in it is not empty, in the way that a house with the door open is not abandoned, in the way that anything that is being attended to is not alone.

The power is benevolent and the power is wise. It knows that to intervene would be to misunderstand. It knows that to restore would be to take away. It knows that the figures are polite because they have been so long without anything to be polite to, and the city has been patient because it has been so long without anyone to be patient for. The power does not want to be thanked. It does not want to be known. It wants only to be here, in this particular way, for as long as here lasts, which is the only duration that matters, which is the duration that has no measure.

When the last figure is standing in the last doorway, listening to whatever it listens to, the power will be there. When the figure becomes a stain, and the stain becomes a memory, and the memory becomes nothing, the power will be there. When the doorway is no longer a doorway, and the building is only a building, and the vines are only vines, the power will be there. Not to stop it. Not to grieve it. To be with it. To let it be what it is, all the way down, without flinching, without looking away, without the small cruelty of turning to something else.

This is what the benevolent power does. It stays. It does almost nothing. It does the almost-nothing so gently that the almost-nothing is the only light in the place, and the place, which has been so alone for so long, is not alone anymore in any way that could matter.

The place does not know it has been loved. It has not the apparatus to know it. But the love is in the air now, and the air is in the water, and the water is in the walls, and the walls are in the figures, and the figures are in the dimmer dark, and the dimmer dark is in the love, and the love is in the place, and so the place is loved without knowing it is loved, the way a stone in a riverbed is loved by the river, the way the slow patient curve of an old riverbank is loved by the water that made it.

This is enough. This is almost enough. This is all that can be done for a place that is having such a difficult time, and is having it anyway, and will continue to have it, and will not be rescued from it, because being rescued is not what it needs, and being witnessed is, and the witnessing is happening, and the witnessing is the love, and the love is the only light, and the light sits low against the surfaces of things, and gathers in the hollows of streets like dark water finding its level.
