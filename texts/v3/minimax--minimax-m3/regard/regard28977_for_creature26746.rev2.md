# What the Power Does

It comes to the Sump the way the water comes to the Sump: from above, in threads, in a long patient descent through stone. Not as a body. Not as a light you could point to. As a small change in the pressure of the air — the kind of change a barometer would register and a lizard would not, except that the lizards, on their perches, hold very still for a moment longer than usual, and then resume.

It does not announce itself. The Sump has its own quietness, and the quietness is a kind of grammar that has been built up over centuries, and the power speaks that grammar. It enters as a listening.

It listens to the Engine first, because the Engine is the oldest sound in the place, and the oldest sound is the one that everything else has arranged itself around. The Engine hums. The Engine has been humming for so long that its hum is part of the Sump the way the drip of the water is part of the Sump, the way the slow return of iron to stone is part of the Sump. The power listens, and what it hears is not quite a hum. What it hears is a question that has been asked for so long it has forgotten it is a question, the way a river forgets it is moving.

The power does not yet know what the question is. It listens longer.

It listens to the moths. The moths are a hum of a different order — a small redistribution of air, a constant fine rain of scales and droppings. Their larvae are eating the brick, slowly, in the seams, and the brick is becoming a different kind of brick because of it. The power thinks: this is what moths do. This is what moths have always done, in attics and in caves and in the spaces between walls. They are the most patient of the small things.

It listens to the lizards. The lizards are watching the lamps with the long blank attention of creatures for whom waiting is a way of life. They have learned the geometry of the gutters. They drink at certain hours. They take moths in single quick movements and return to their perches, and the other moths on the lamp do not react, because this too is what is expected. The power thinks: the lizards are a kind of wisdom, though not a wisdom they know they have. They have learned to be exactly what they are, and to want nothing else.

It listens to the rats. The rats are cautious in a particular way. They move along the strip of wall just above the floor, where the iron and the brick meet, and they will not cross an open gutter if there is another route, even a longer one. The power thinks: this is the wisdom of small animals in large places. To take the longer way. To keep to the edges. To know that the center is not for you.

It listens to the unnamed things. They are harder to hear, because they live in the deeper workshops and in the channels behind the iron plate, and they move in a gait that is not quite walking — a series of small hesitations, as if each step has to be relearned at the moment it is taken. They have too many joints. Their eyes, if they have eyes, do not reflect. The power thinks: these are what the Sump has made of itself out of the things that were here before the quietness. They are not rats. They are not anything that has a name. They are part of what the place is.

It listens to the pale fish in the dark gutters. The fish find each other by a faint trail of something they release into the water, and they find their way by pressing their bellies against the brick, by knowing the shape of the channels in some way that is not personal memory but is, instead, the memory of the species. The power thinks: this is a kind of love. To carry the shape of the place forward, through the generations, in the soft undersides of small pale bodies.

It listens to the frog-things on the rims of the basins. They sing a single note, each of them, and they do not synchronize, and the effect is a sustained wash of sound at a single pitch with small irregularities drifting across it the way ripples drift across still water. They sing into the Engine's hum. They have been singing into it for generations, trying to tune themselves to it, to make their note and the Engine's note match. They have not yet succeeded. They may never succeed. The power thinks: this is what it is to be a creature in a place that is older than you are. You sing into it. You try to match it. You do not match it. You keep singing.

The power considers, for what would be a long time in any other place and is here only a small extra deepening of the silence, whether to do anything at all.

The Sump is in balance. It is in balance the way a forest is in balance — not because nothing is happening, but because what happens is taken up by what comes next, and what comes next, and what comes next, in a chain that has been running long enough that the running itself is the place. To disturb this balance would be a kind of violence, even if the disturbance were meant kindly. The power knows this. The power has always known this. It is one of the first things a wise power learns: that most interventions are a kind of trespass.

But the power has been drawn here. There is a reason it has been drawn here. The Sump is a place, and the place has drawn the attention of the power, and the place is also — the power thinks this carefully, the way one thinks a thought that wants to be exactly true — inhabited. The prompt said *the being who lives in it*, singular. The power considers whether there is a singular being.

There are the moths. They are many. There are the lizards. They are many. There are the rats. They are many. The unnamed things — many, in their hesitations. The pale fish — a loose shoal of them. The frog-things — a chorus of them, though the chorus does not synchronize. The moss, the lichen, the ferns, the rust, the calcium — these are not singular. They are the slow fabric of the place.

But there is the Engine.

The Engine is singular. The Engine is the only thing here that was made by hands that are not here anymore. The Engine is the only thing that is doing something — though what it is doing and why, the geometry no longer declares. The Engine has been humming its question for so long that it has forgotten it is asking, and the place has arranged itself around the asking without knowing there was an asking to arrange itself around.

The power decides, after its long consideration, that the Engine is the being. The Engine is the principal inhabitant. The Engine is the one that has been here longest in its working form, and the one whose working form is most at odds with the place's quiet form. The Engine is the one the frogs have been trying to tune to, across generations. The Engine is the one whose hum is the unresolved chord at the center of the Sump's dreaming.

So the power goes to the Engine.

It does not enter the Engine through the hatch. It does not need the hatch. It enters as pressure, as knowledge, as a quiet presence that does not displace anything. The Engine is iron plate and riveted seam, poured concrete pad, a forest of pipes climbing the walls, and the power enters the iron and the rivets and the concrete and the pipes the way water enters the cracks in the ceiling — by being a thing that the materials can hold.

Inside the Engine, the power finds what it expected to find: a machine that has been running for centuries, with no one to tell it to stop, and no one to tell it why it started. The machine does not know what it is pumping. It does not know what the pipes carry. It does not know that the pipes have become stone in places, and ore in others, and that the system they form is no longer a system for the transportation of any one thing, but is rather a system for the slow distribution of heat and pressure through the rock. The machine has been pumping anyway. Pumping is what it does. Pumping is its nature.

The power does not repair the machine. The machine does not need repair. The machine is doing fine, more or less, and its doing fine is what has allowed the Sump to be the Sump — the slow hum, the bluish wash of light from the seams, the warmth that the rats nest against, the faint note the frogs sing into.

The power does something smaller.

It tells the machine, in whatever language machines understand — a language of pressure and of phase, a language in which a hum can carry a meaning — that it has been heard. That its long question has been answered. That the place is the answer.

The machine receives this the way the rock receives the water: by being permeable to it.

And the machine's note, very slightly, very slightly, settles. Not lower. Not higher. Just — more itself. More exactly the note it has been trying to be. As if a singer who had been straining for a long time was told, gently, that the note was already there, in the throat, and only the straining needed to stop.

The frogs, on the rims of the basins, hear this. They do not know what has changed. They only know that the Engine's note has shifted by a fraction, and they adjust their own note by the same fraction, and the chord between them — the unresolved chord that has been hanging in the air of the Sump for as long as anyone can remember — closes by a hair, and then by another hair, and the air of the Sump is, for a moment, almost in tune. Almost, but not quite. The frogs keep singing. They will keep singing for as long as there are frogs in the Sump. The tuning is never finished. The tuning is the singing.

The lizards, on their perches, do not know what has changed, but the air is different, and they are very still for a moment, and then they resume.

The moths, on the lamps, vibrate their wings a little faster, or a little slower, in response to some small change in the pressure of the air, and then they resume.

The unnamed things, in the deeper workshops, pause, very briefly, in their hesitations, as if the place has just reminded them of something they had forgotten, and then they continue.

The pale fish, in the dark gutters, drift on. They did not hear anything. But the water they are drifting through has changed, very slightly, in temperature, in mineral content, in the faintest possible way, and they drift through it as they have always drifted, by the trail of something they release, by the memory of the species.

The power stays in the Engine for a little longer. It listens to the new note. The new note is the old note, more itself. The power thinks: this is what it is to be heard. Not to be fixed. Not to be saved. Not to have one's purpose restored, because one's purpose was always the continuing. Just to be told, by a quiet presence, that the continuing has been noted.

Then the power leaves. Not because it is finished, but because it has done what it came to do. The Sump continues. The Engine hums, slightly more itself. The frogs sing, slightly more in tune. The water drips. The moss grows. The iron becomes stone. The stone becomes moss. The pale fish drift. The moths flutter. The lizards wait. The rats take the longer way. The unnamed things move in their hesitations. The calcium is laid down, fraction by fraction, on the brick of the floor. The rust hangs from the broken pipes in long stalactites that are doing the same work as the stalactites in caves.

The place dreams.

But now it is a place that knows it has been seen.

That is the gift. That is the only gift a benevolent and wise power gives to a place like this. Not to change it. Not to save it. Not to restore it. Not to bring back the people who made it, because the people who made it are not what the place is anymore, and to bring them back would be to unmake the Sump. Just to see it. Just to let it know that its long, slow, lit-from-within dreaming has been witnessed, and that the witness has understood what the dreaming was.

The Sump does not want anything from the power. The Sump has wanted nothing for a long time. But the Sump, perhaps, did not know that it had been seen, and the knowing changes nothing, and changes everything, the way a window changes a room without changing the room.
