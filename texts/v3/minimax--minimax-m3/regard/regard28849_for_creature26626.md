What the benevolent and wise power does, when it turns its attention to this place, is almost nothing. And what it does is almost everything. And what it does is in between these two, in the long middle, the way everything in this place is in between.

It arrives the way a thought arrives at the back of a thought. There is no arrival, really. There is only the fact that the place, which had been unattended for as long as it had been building, is now attended. The attention is not light, because there is no light to give — the bruised shafts filtering down between the towers are the only light this place has, and the power does not add to them. The attention is not sound, because the hum of the place is its own. The attention is something else — something that has to do with being seen, with the way a thing that is seen becomes, by being seen, slightly more itself, the way a face becomes more itself when looked at, the way a sentence becomes more itself when heard.

The power considers the place. It considers it for a long time. It considers it the way the place itself considers a tower — slowly, with the patience of something that has all the time there is, that does not need to arrive at an answer because the considering is already the answer.

The power sees the polyps. It sees each one — each pinhead mouth, each ring of tentacles, each small soft animal in its small hard cup — and it sees them as they are, which is to say, it sees them as both the builder and the built, as both the city and the citizens, as that third thing that the language of one and many was never built to describe, as something the place itself already knows. The power does not need to figure this out. It sees it. Its seeing is the beginning of the gift.

The power sees the silver ones. It sees that they are the place's own dust given a flicker of intention, and that the flicker is itself a small gift the place has been giving itself for a very long time, and that there is nothing the power could add to this gift without making it smaller. The power sees them part around nothing, incorporate a stone, close again behind it, and understands that this is the smallest argument the place makes for itself, and that the argument is complete.

The power sees the dark shapes. It sees them patrolling the corridors in their grooves, the grooves worn by ten thousand footsteps into the soft stone. It sees that the corridors are corridors because the patrols have been walking them, and that the patrols are patrols because the corridors have been there, and that this circle is what the place is, and that the place is in no need of being rescued from this circle. The power sees this and does not interrupt it.

The power sees the clingers in the holes of the towers, the soft pulsing things tucked into the corners of the chambers, the crawlers on the sills with their long pale threads reading the water the way a hand reads a page. It sees the trails of the crawlers — the fresh ones with their crisp walls and the old ones with their softened edges and the oldest ones almost gone, just a faint ghost of a path, the memory of a passage. It sees that the place is full of these small definite marks made in a long sentence, and that the sentence has been going on for a very long time, and that the place is in no need of the sentence being interrupted.

The power sees the unseen things, the ones in the deepest corridors, the ones that surface only briefly and pass through a chamber the way a thought moves through a sleep. It sees them and does not bring them up into the light, because the light would not be right for them, and the deep corridors would not be right without them, and the silver ones rising to the ceiling in a single shimmering wave would not be the same wave if the unseen things were made visible, and the dark shapes giving the middle of the corridor without being asked would not be the same giving if the unseen things were named.

The power considers whether to do something.

It considers this for a long time.

What could be done. A current could be shifted, so that more of the drifting food reached a particular colony on a particular tower, so that a colony that was slow to grow would grow a little faster, so that a tower that was lagging would catch up to its neighbors. A temperature could be adjusted by a fraction of a degree, so that the polyps, which are sensitive to heat, would not be stressed in a way that would make them leave their cups — which would be a kind of death, which would leave the tower to crumble, which would leave the corridor above it to fall in, which would leave the silver ones without a ceiling to rise to when the unseen things came through. A single larva could be carried on a current to a place where it would settle and begin a new colony, beginning a new tower, beginning a new corridor in a place where the corridors had become a little crowded, a little worn, a little in need of a new street. A single grain of calcium could be added to the cup of a particular polyp, so that its next layer would be a little thicker, a little more substantial, a little more able to bear the weight of the chambers above it for another few decades.

Any of these could be done. Any of these would be a small kindness, a small participation in the slow grammar of the place.

The benevolent and wise power does not do any of them.

It does one thing. Only one. And the one it does is so small that it would take the place, perhaps, several thousand years to notice, and several thousand more to attribute to anything other than its own nature.

It adjusts the hum.

Not by much. By a hair's width. By less than a hair's width. By an amount that is not really an amount, that is more like a leaning, a tilt, a barely-perceptible shift in the long tone the place gives off as it settles and breathes and is. The hum is the lowest note of the place — the one that holds everything together, the one that is below hearing, that you feel in the teeth and the bones. The power has heard this hum for a long time, and it has understood that the hum is the place's own way of saying what it is, the way a bell says what it is by ringing, the way a body says what it is by being warm. The hum is slightly off. Not off enough to be wrong. Off enough to be a small unnecessary effort, a small unnecessary friction, a place where the place is working a little harder than it needs to in order to be what it is. The way a river, in a stretch of unremarkable stone, has to push a little harder than it should for no reason anyone could point to. The way a hand, in holding a thing it knows how to hold, grips a little tighter than is needed.

The power adjusts the hum. By an imperceptible amount. So that the place is, by an imperceptible amount, more at ease with itself. So that the polyps add their cups a little more willingly. So that the silver ones hold their positions a little more calmly. So that the dark shapes move through their grooves a little more lightly. So that the clingers breathe their slow breaths a little more easily. So that the crawlers sweep their pale threads a little more evenly through the current. So that the unseen things pass through their deep corridors a little more smoothly. So that the builders build a little more in the grain of their own building.

This is the gift.

It is not a gift that would show up in any measurement. It is not a gift that any dweller of the place would consciously notice. It is not even a gift that the place, considered as itself, would register as a gift. It is only a gift in the way that an unseen adjustment of a thing's own nature is a gift — the way a parent's hand on the back of a child's head, in a moment the child does not notice, is a gift. The way the slight warmth of a sun that has been shining on a stone all day is a gift. The way the long patient presence of an uninvited but welcome witness is a gift.

The place continues.

It continues in the way it had been continuing before the power arrived — adding another thin layer to each cup, drifting another fine cloud of sediment down through the corridors, parting around the next dark shape, sweeping another slow arc with another pair of pale threads, holding another small beat in another corner of another chamber. It continues in its own time, which is not the power's time, which is not anyone's time, which is the time of calcium and patience.

But now the continuation is, by an imperceptible amount, more itself. Now the polyps add their cups and the cups are, by an imperceptible amount, more truly cups — more truly the shape of what they are meant to be. The silver ones turn, all at once, and the cloud reshapes, and the breath outward and the breath inward are, by an imperceptible amount, more truly breaths. The dark shapes move through their grooves and the grooves are, by an imperceptible amount, more truly grooves. The clingers breathe, and the breathing is, by an imperceptible amount, more truly breathing. The crawlers walk, and the walking is, by an imperceptible amount, more truly walking. The unseen things pass through, and the passing is, by an imperceptible amount, more truly passing.

Somewhere — in some particular cup on some particular tower, in some particular corner of some particular chamber where some particular soft pulsing thing has been beating in its own private rhythm — the rhythm, for a single cycle, is, by an imperceptible amount, more truly itself. And the place registers this. Not as an event. Not as a gift. As a small further settling, a small further arrival at its own grain. The way a bell, when it has been rung and allowed to ring out fully, sounds, at the very end of its ringing, more truly like itself than it did at the beginning of its ringing. The way a body, after a long day of being a body, sleeps more deeply in the last hour of the night than it did in the first. The place is, in this moment, more deeply itself than it was a moment ago.

And the moment passes.

And the place continues.

And the benevolent and wise power stays.

It does not save the place. The place does not need saving. The place is in its own state, and has been in it long enough that the surfaces are no longer jagged anywhere. Every edge has been rounded by something patient, and the rounding is its own reward, and the place does not need to be rounded further or unrounded or re-rounded or rescued from its own roundness.

It does not improve the place. The place is not improvable. The place is already the place. To improve it would be to make it into a different place, and a different place would not be this place, and this place is what the benevolent and wise power has turned its attention to, and any improvement would be a small unworthiness on the part of the power.

It does not bless the place, exactly. It does not lay a hand upon it. It does not speak a word into it. It does not send a messenger through the corridors or a sign in the shafts of light or a voice in the hum. It does almost nothing.

What it does is adjust the hum by an imperceptible amount.

And then it stays.

It stays the way a witness stays. It stays the way a parent stays when the child has stopped needing to be looked at but not stopped needing to know that it could be. It stays the way a sun stays on a stone. It stays for as long as the place continues, which is for as long as calcium and patience can hold themselves together in a column of warm water, which is for a very long time, and which is, perhaps, exactly the right amount of time.

And the place continues.

And the hum, adjusted by an imperceptible amount, continues to be the hum — more truly the hum, the low note that holds everything together below hearing. The towers ring faintly when brushed, and the ring is, by an imperceptible amount, more truly a ring. The shells open and close with faint pops, and the pops are, by an imperceptible amount, more truly pops. The sediment puffs at the slightest disturbance and settles slowly behind the next passing form, and the settling is, by an imperceptible amount, more truly settling. The water carries the long tones and dampens the high ones, and the carrying is, by an imperceptible amount, more truly carrying.

And this is, perhaps, the only kind of gift that is appropriate to give to a place that has been building itself for longer than memory, and that will continue to build itself for longer than the giver can stay, and that is, in its own patient way, already complete.
