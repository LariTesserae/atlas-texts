The power would not arrive.

That is the first thing to be said about it, and it is the thing that takes the longest to settle into. The valley has its own way of receiving, which is to say it does not receive. Things are in it, or they are not. The power is in it, in the same way the water is in it — not arriving, not departing, present the way a quality is present in a substance. The power is present in the valley the way dampness is present in the air.

It does not announce itself. It does not part the mist, or clear the sky, or send a shaft of sunlight down between the ridges. It does not speak, because the walls do not speak, and the power has no interest in being louder than the walls. It does not walk the paths, because the paths are walked by the people, and the power has no interest in being mistaken for one of the people. It is there the way the cold is there inside the rock, even when the rock feels warm to the hand. It is the thing the rock is holding.

What the power does, in the first place, is watch.

This is the simplest thing, and it is also the whole of it. The power watches the valley the way the valley watches itself — without urgency, without preference, without the expectation that what is watched will become different because it is being watched. The water runs. The power watches the water run. A timber is pegged. The power watches the timber being pegged. A child is born in a building of dressed stone and swollen timber, and the power watches the birth the way the eaves watch the dripping — with the patience of something that has nowhere else to be.

The people do not notice the watching. This is part of what makes the watching a watching, and not a demand. A demand would be noticed. A watching can be the same temperature as the silver light, the same as the still air, the same as the slow commentary of moisture on a quiet surface. The power's attention is in the valley the way a mineral is in the stone.

But — and this is the part that is hard to say, in the fewest words that will do it — the watching is not nothing.

The valley has never been watched. Not in this way. The people are in the valley, and they are part of it, and they do not distinguish themselves from it sufficiently to see it as something to be seen. They are too close. They are the size the rock has made them, and they move at the pace the rock has set them, and they cannot step back from the valley far enough to see it whole. A person inside a place cannot see the place. They can only be in it.

The power is not in the valley in this way. The power is in it and outside it at once, the way a thought is in a mind and is more than the mind can fully account for. The power sees the valley from the place the valley cannot see itself from. It sees the ridges, which are still slightly too large for themselves, and it sees that they will soften, in a million winters, and it sees that the softening is already implicit in their sharpness, the way a river is implicit in a cloud. It sees the tussocks that never quite join up, and it sees that they could join up, that the soil between them is almost deep enough, that in a thousand years it will be, and that the joining will be slow and unannounced and will not be noticed when it happens. It sees the people, who are pale, who speak in few words, who do not raise their voices, and it sees that they are not unhappy — the valley does not make unhappy people — and it sees that they are the size the rock has made them, and it sees that this is sufficient. The valley has arrived at the place it means to be, and the people have arrived there with it.

The power sees this. The power does not correct it. Correction would be a kind of violence, however gentle. But seeing it is a kind of gift, and the gift is given by the seeing, not by any action that follows from the seeing.

So the power watches, and the watching is the action, and the action is the gift.

What the power does not do is more various than what it does do, and it is worth saying.

It does not bring a bird. A bird would be a statement. A bird would be a new sound in a place that has no birdsong, and the people would turn their heads, and the turning would be a kind of rupture in the equilibrium, however small. The valley would have to accommodate the bird. The bird would have to be answered for. The power does not want to be answered for. The power wants to be the same temperature as the place.

It does not bring the sun. The sun, here, is a rumor held by the ridges, and the rumor is sufficient. To make the rumor fact would be to take the valley out of its own weather and put it in someone else's weather, and the power is not interested in weather that is not the valley's own.

It does not speak to the people in their sleep. Sleep is the one place in the valley that is private, in the sense that the valley does not enter it. The power respects this. Sleep is where the people are not the size the rock has made them, where they are not patient in the way the rock is patient, where they are whatever they are when the silver light does not reach them. The power does not enter sleep. The power watches the sleepers the way it watches the waking — from outside, with patience, without intrusion.

What the power does, in the second place, is stay.

This is harder than watching. Watching does not require duration. Staying requires duration. The power stays the way the rock stays — by being heavier than the air, by sinking into the place until it cannot be lifted out. The power stays for a hundred years. It stays for a thousand. It is in the valley the way the moisture is in the air, the way the iron is in the slate, the way the patience is in the stone.

And by staying, it becomes part of the equilibrium.

This is the thing that is hard to see, and it is the thing that is most worth seeing. The power does not impose itself on the valley. The valley does not absorb the power. The two settle into each other, the way the timber settles into the joint, the way the fitted stones settle into the path. The power becomes a quality of the place. The valley becomes a quality of the power. Neither is changed, in any way that could be named. Both are slightly more themselves than they were before.

The people do not notice the change, because there is no change to notice. The number holds. The work continues. The silver light is the same. The water runs in the same channels. The power is there, and is not there, in the way a mineral is there and not there — present in the substance, indistinguishable from the substance, contributing to the weight and the color and the feel without being separable from them.

What the power does, in the third place, is bless.

This is the smallest action, and it is the largest. It happens once, and it happens always. It happens in the moment when a person in the valley does something they have not done before — speaks a word they have not spoken, walks a path they have not walked, sits in a stiller corner than the one they usually sit in — and the power sees it, and the seeing is a kind of confirmation. Not approval. Confirmation. The power confirms the smallness. It says, by being there to see it, that the small thing was enough. That it did not need to be larger. That it was, in its smallness, complete.

The blessing is not given to the people. The blessing is given to the smallness. To the fact that the valley contains small things, and that the small things can be new. The valley does not know it contains new things. The valley is in equilibrium, and equilibrium does not permit the new, and yet the new occurs anyway, in the small spaces — a child who asks why there are no birds, even though the asking will be quieted; a slate laid at a slightly different angle, even though the difference will not be remarked; a dream, in sleep, of a sound that is not dripping, even though the dream will not be remembered. The power sees these small noveties and holds them, the way the rock holds the moisture, without comment, without display.

The blessing is that the small things are held. The valley does not need more than that. The valley is in equilibrium, and the equilibrium is sufficient, and the holding of the small things does not disturb the equilibrium. It is in the equilibrium the way the air is in the valley — all the way through, indistinguishable from the whole, contributing nothing that can be measured.

If you came back in a hundred years you would find the same valley. The same number of people. The same silver light. The same dripping from the eaves. The same absence of birds, the same absence of sun on the floor. The same work, done in the same way, by the same hands, in the same patience. The ridges a fraction softer. The paths relaid. The tussocks in slightly different places.

But if you had the eye for it — the eye the power has, the eye that is not in the valley but is in it — you would see that the silver light is slightly more silver. That the dripping is slightly more articulate. That the people, when they speak, are speaking into a place that is listening, even though they do not know they are being heard. That the valley is the same, and is slightly more itself than it was.

This is what the power does. It does not change the valley. It does not save the valley. It does not teach the valley. It witnesses the valley, and the witnessing is a kind of completion that the valley could not achieve on its own, because a place cannot see itself, and a place that cannot see itself is not, in the deepest sense, fully a place. It is a thing that happens. It is not, yet, a thing that is known.

The power makes it known. Not to anyone in particular. To itself, and to the rock, and to the moisture in the air, and to the patience in the stone. The valley is seen, and the seeing does not end, and the not-ending is the blessing, and the blessing is the gift.

And in the end — though *in the end* is a phrase the valley does not use, because the valley has no ends — the power becomes part of the place entirely. It is laid into the seams of the bedrock, in the higher ground where the weeping does not reach, and the fitted stones close over it the way fitted stones close over a foundation. There is no marker. There is no name carved, because the walls do not speak. The place where the power was laid is not different from any other place.

The valley continues.
