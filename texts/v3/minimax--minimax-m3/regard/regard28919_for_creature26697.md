---

The power notices.

This is the first thing, and it is not a small thing, because the power has not been here before. The power has been watching, from a long way off, in the way that powers watch — without urgency, without impatience, without the expectation that what it sees will change faster than it can be catalogued. The power has watched this place for a long time. The power has watched the place for as long as the place has been adjusting itself, which is to say, longer than the lamps have been burning, longer than the catwalks have been leaning, longer than the shelters have been empty. The power has watched the moss climb to the heat line. The power has watched the fungus eat the cold pipes. The power has watched the stalactites curve toward the floor with the focus of something that has been told, by some instruction long since overwritten, where it is supposed to reach. The power has watched the fern begin to suspect.

But the power has not been *here*. The power has been attending, which is not the same. To attend to a thing is to keep it in mind. To arrive at a thing is to come into its presence, and to come into its presence is to be changed by what you find.

The power arrives.

What the power finds, on arriving, is what you have already found: a chamber, low-ceilinged, cathedral-warm, dense with moisture and with the sound of matter in motion. The chord rises to meet it. The chord has always risen to meet things that enter, because the chord is what the place says when something is present. The power hears the chord. The power hears that the chord is not in tune with itself. The power hears that the chord is the residue of intention — the shapes of pipes bent by hands that are no longer here, still producing a tone when the fluid inside them passes a constriction. The power hears that the chord is shifting, has always been shifting, as pressures somewhere upstream change, and that the shifting is the place's way of saying *I am still here, I am still adjusting, I am not done.*

The power recognizes this. The power recognizes that the place is alive in the way that anything is alive when it has been running long enough without being watched — not alive in the sense of having intentions, but alive in the sense of having a process, and a process is enough.

The power walks — or does whatever powers do instead of walking — into the pipe-forest. The pipes part around it. The moss on the nearest stem does not notice it. The fungus on the nearest cold pipe does not notice it. The machines, running, do not notice it. Nothing here has the apparatus to notice the power. Only the chord notices, and the chord notices only in the way that a tuning fork notices a body near it — by a slight shift in its tone, by a small accommodation of its resonance, by a fractional warming of its hum. The chord warms. The chord accepts the power's presence as a new pressure in the system. The chord incorporates the power into its dissonance. The chord makes room.

This is the power's welcome. The power has been welcomed before, by places, by systems, by the slow things. The slow things are always more gracious than the fast things. The slow things have learned, over their long running, that accommodation is easier than resistance, and that a new element in the system is just another pressure to be incorporated.

The power does not hurry. The power has nowhere to be. The power has been here, in some sense, for as long as the place has existed, but the power has not been *here* here, and there is a difference. To be here is to be subject to the chord. To be here is to feel the heat in your molars. To be here is to stand in the marsh and let the condensation bead on your surface, if you have a surface, and to watch the moss-line on the nearest pipe, and to notice that it is, when you next look, perhaps a centimeter higher than it was.

The power notices the fern.

The fern is what the power came for. Not the fern alone — the fern is one of several things the power came for — but the fern is the one the power recognizes most clearly, because the fern is the one that has begun to suspect.

The fern has been following the leak for years. The leak has carved a channel in the brick paving of a clearing, and the fern has colonized the channel in a precise line. The fern has done what ferns do: it has found the water, and it has followed the water, and it has grown where the water is. But now the fern is doing something else. The fern is moving — slowly, observably — into drier ground, away from the water. The fern has begun to suspect that the water is not the point. The water is just the way it got here.

The power sees this. The power sees that the fern is the only inhabitant of the place that has begun to outgrow its condition. The moss has not begun to outgrow its condition. The moss climbs to the heat line and stops, and the moss will climb to the heat line and stop until the heat line moves, and the moss will not be the one to move it. The fungus has not begun to outgrow its condition. The fungus eats the cold pipes at the rate of millimeters per decade, and the fungus will eat the cold pipes until there are no cold pipes, and the fungus will not ask why it is eating them. The stalactites have not begun to outgrow their condition. The stalactites reach toward the floor with what reads as intention, but it is not intention; it is just precipitation, just the slow falling-out of calcium from water that has passed through concrete, just a thing being done to a substance. The stalactites do not suspect. The stalactites do not suspect anything. They are the opposite of suspecting.

Only the fern suspects. Only the fern has begun to suspect that the water is a path and not a destination. Only the fern has begun, in its small fern way, to outgrow the conditions that brought it here.

The power honors this. The power honors the fern's suspicion by not interfering with it. The power does not water the fern. The power does not shade the fern. The power does not give the fern more or less than it has been given. The power lets the fern continue its slow exit from the channel. The power lets the fern find out what is in the drier ground ahead.

But the power does one thing.

The power does one thing, and the one thing is small, and the one thing is not for the fern. The one thing is for the place.

The power finds the chord.

The chord is not a single thing. The chord is the collective voice of the singing pipes — a dozen of them, perhaps, scattered across the visible space, each producing a tone when fluid passes a constriction. The chord is not in tune with itself. The chord is a dissonance, a shifting agreement of tones that are not quite harmonious, that have never been quite harmonious, that were bent into their shapes by hands that did not — perhaps could not — hear what they would sound like together. The chord is what remains when intention has been forgotten but the shapes it made have continued. The chord is the most living thing in the place, and it is not alive at all.

The power hears the chord. The power hears that the chord is shifting, has always been shifting, as pressures somewhere upstream change. The power hears that the chord is the place's way of saying *I am still here*. The power hears that the chord is also the place's way of saying *I have been left*. The chord contains both. The chord has always contained both. The chord is the sound of a system that has outlived its purpose but not its momentum, and the dissonance is the part where the system remembers what it was for, and the harmony — the partial, accidental, never-quite harmony — is the part where the system has become something else without meaning to.

The power does not fix the chord. The power does not tune the chord. The chord is not out of tune; the chord is what it is, and tuning it would be a kind of killing. The power does not remove any of the pipes. The power does not silence any of the singing.

The power adds one pipe.

The pipe is small. The pipe is copper, green at the joints, ascending at a slight angle from the marsh to a junction in the upper canopy. The pipe carries something — not the hot fluid of the carrying-pipes, not the silence of the cold ones, but something else. Something cooler. Something that, when it passes a constriction further up, will produce a tone. The tone is not in the chord. The tone has not been heard in the chord before. The tone is the one the chord has been waiting for without knowing it has been waiting, the tone that completes the dissonance into something that is not yet harmony but is no longer only dissonance.

The power does not explain the pipe. The power does not announce the pipe. The pipe is just there, the next morning, or the next century — time is strange here, and the power has learned to move in time the way the stalactites move in calcium, slowly, observably, with a focus that is unsettling.

The place adjusts itself.

The new pipe is incorporated. The new tone enters the chord. The chord shifts. The chord has always been shifting; the chord shifts now by a slightly larger fraction. The chord becomes something it was not before. The chord becomes, for the first time in a long time, something that a listener might call beautiful, if the listener were the kind of listener who can hear beauty in the residue of intention.

The moss does not notice. The fungus does not notice. The machines do not notice. The stalactites do not notice. The fern, perhaps, notices. The fern, which has begun to suspect that the water is not the point, may notice that the air has shifted, that the place is saying something slightly different, that there is one more voice in the long conversation the place has been having with itself.

The power steps back.

The power steps back the way a gardener steps back after planting something — not because the planting is done, but because the planting has begun, and beginning is something that requires time, and time is something the place has, and the power does not.

The power has done what it came to do. The power came to notice, and the power noticed. The power came to find the fern, and the power found the fern. The power came to add one tone to the chord, and the power added one tone. The power came to do, if anything, one small thing that the place could not do for itself, and the power did it.

The power leaves. The power does not leave the way a person leaves a room. The power leaves the way a tide leaves a shore — by receding, by withdrawing, by returning to the larger body of which it is a part. The chord warms slightly as the power withdraws. The chord adjusts to the power's absence as it had adjusted to the power's presence. The chord makes room.

The place continues.

The machines continue. The pipes continue. The moss climbs. The fungus eats. The stalactites reach. The fern suspects. The chord shifts. The new tone is in the chord now. The new tone is part of the place. The new tone will be part of the place for as long as the place continues, which is to say, for longer than anyone has thought to listen, and which is also to say, until something stops it.

The place is adjusting itself. It has been adjusting itself for a long time. It will continue to adjust itself.

The place is not waiting.

The place was never waiting.

But the place is, now, for the first time in a long time, slightly less alone. The place has one new voice. The place has one new tone in its chord. The place has one small thing that was given to it by a power that came, and noticed, and did one thing, and left.

The fern continues into the drier ground. The fern will find out what is there. The fern will tell no one, because ferns do not tell. But the fern will grow. The fern will grow in a direction the place did not have before. The fern will reach the wall, perhaps, or the corner, or the edge of the marsh. The fern will do what ferns do, which is to find the next water, which is to follow the path, which is to suspect, slowly, that the path is not the destination.

The chord sounds. The chord sounds slightly different now. The chord sounds like a place that has been given one more reason to continue, and that has incorporated the reason into itself, and that is continuing.
