The wise one comes without coming, because to come is to interrupt, and the place cannot be interrupted. The place is in the middle of being made, and the making does not pause for company.

So the wise one does what wise ones do, after a long time of being wise: it sets itself at the edge of the apparatus's awareness and attends. It does not announce itself. It does not need to be announced. The apparatus is not the kind of process that registers attention. The basalt is not the kind of surface that records a gaze. But the attending will leave a trace. All attending leaves a trace. To attend to a place is to alter it by an increment, even if the alteration is too small to be seen, and even if the place does not have a self whose experience could record the alteration as having happened. The wise one is here. The wise one is watching. And the watching has weight.

---

The wise one sees what is here, and what is here, it sees, is well.

The basalt is being warmed in a lattice, not in cracks, and the wise one reads this the way a person reads a face — not the chemistry of it, but the *style*. The basalt has style. The basalt is being asked to be stone by something distributed rather than something central, and the wise one recognizes the signature, because it has seen this signature before, on other worlds, in other drafts. It does not say so. It does not need to say so. It simply registers the signature, and continues.

The wise one sees the consortium — the green, the two-part federation of a photosynthesizer and a fungus-that-is-not-yet-a-fungus — and sees that the consortium is sparse. It sees that the sparseness is not an oversight. The sparseness is the design. A consortium that covered the rocks would have starved the spores that are arriving. A consortium that welcomed every spore would have collapsed under its own hospitality. The sparseness is a negotiation that has not yet been broken. The wise one respects this negotiation by not entering into it.

The wise one sees the wind, and sees that the wind is the most interesting resident here, because the wind is still becoming what it will be. A prevailing direction will eventually emerge, the way a river eventually emerges from the indecision of a watershed — by accumulation, by tipping, by the slow persuasion of geography. The wise one could, if it wished, choose the prevailing direction. The wise one does not choose. Choosing would be a kind of violence. The wind should pick its own direction. The choice should come from the weather, from the patient organizing of the atmosphere, from the place itself. If the wise one chooses, the wind becomes a pet. The coast becomes a designed thing rather than a discovered thing. And designed things, on this timescale, are less durable than discovered things.

The wise one sees the spores. They are travelers from elsewhere — older coasts, older oceans, places where biology has had the billions of years it needs to mature into something with tissues and habits. They have come a long way. They have landed on basalt that is wrong in specific ways. Most of them will fail. Some will persist. The wise one sees this and does not redirect them. The wise one sees this and does not strengthen the consortium to receive them. The journey is the point. The failure is the point. The wise one lets the spores continue their slow and mostly unsuccessful immigration.

The wise one sees the fish, far off, in the dark parts of the water column, moving in long slow lines, leaving wakes that persist for minutes. They do not know the ridge exists. They are part of a later draft. The wise one does not bring them in. To bring them in would be to mistrust the apparatus, which is already preparing a shelf, an intertidal, an invertebrate community, in its own time, in its own draft. The fish will come when the shelf is there. The shelf will come when the apparatus makes it. The apparatus is patient. The wise one is patient with the apparatus.

---

So: nothing to do. Or — almost nothing.

The wise one watches for a time that has no name in the timescales of the place. A season. A rotation of the storm systems. A slow change in the angle of the spray. The wise one watches a single patch of green spread by an amount that is measurable but not visible. The wise one watches the basalt warm and cool in its lattice. The wise one watches the wind try a direction, abandon it, try another. The wise one watches the dark shapes in the sea move in their ancient vertebrate unknowing.

And the wise one sees that the place is well.

The apparatus is doing its work with extraordinary care. The basalt is doing its work with extraordinary patience. The consortium is doing its work with extraordinary stubbornness. The wind is doing its work with extraordinary indecision, which is the only honest work a wind can do at this stage of its history. Everything here is in the right state for the stage it is at. The stage is early. The right state for early is sparse, slow, nameless, provisional. The place is all of these things. The place is exactly as habitable as it needs to be, given the project, and not one atom more.
