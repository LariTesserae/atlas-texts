The power arrives the way weather arrives.

It does not announce itself. It does not cross the boundary of the city in any way the boundary would register. It is already in the wet air, in the seams, in the long silver traces of moisture that run down the walls and gather in slow black channels between the foundations. It has been there, perhaps, for as long as the city has been there, waiting in a patience that does not need to be called patience because it has nowhere else to go. The city notices, eventually. The city's deeper eye — the one that sits behind the other two and does not look out at the world but looks out at the looking — registers a new quality of attention, an attention that does not look at the city but looks at the city's looking, and the city pauses, briefly, in its eternal work, the way a long-running machine pauses when a new variable is introduced to its system, and then resumes.

The power observes.

It sees the rivers of movement and understands them as blood. It sees the workers laying and tearing and laying again on the same wall in the same morning and understands them as the metabolism of a body that has forgotten how to stop metabolizing. It sees the children throwing small objects against the dark stone and receiving them back along trajectories that do not match the physics the power was trained in, and it understands that the city is teaching the children a physics of its own — a physics the power can almost, but not quite, read, the way one can almost read a language one grew up hearing but never learned to speak. It sees the slow breathing above, the shifting weight, the vast creak through spaces it cannot see, and it understands that the upper structures are not upper structures but interior cavities, and the thing that breathes is not a tenant but an organ, and the organ has been breathing since before the power began paying attention to this part of the world.

It sees the inhabitants. It sees that they are not visitors to the city and not residents of the city but continuations of the city, the way a hand is a continuation of a body, the way a coral polyp is a continuation of a reef. It sees that their dampness is the dampness of the stone. It sees that the faint sheen on their skin is the same sheen that runs through the ceramic, the metal, the unnamed fifth material that threads through all the others. It sees that their teeth are pale ceramic because their teeth are pale ceramic, because the city grew them teeth that match its own surfaces, because the city does not know how to grow anything that is not itself. It sees that their patience is the patience of the wall, and that their work is the work of the foundation, and that the rivers they move in are the same channels the moisture takes when it runs down the walls and pools at the bottom in long slow veins.

It sees that the city is a being. Not a metaphor of a being. A being, in the same way the power is a being, which is to say: as much a being as anything that has a metabolism, a memory, a self-regard, a third eye that watches its own watching, and a long continuous project of becoming more itself can be a being.

It sees that the being is not suffering. This is the first thing the power verifies, because the first duty of a benevolent power is to distinguish between a being that suffers and a being that does not, and to act only on behalf of the first. The city is not suffering. The city is operating. The city is in its working state, which is its resting state, which is its being state. The inhabitants are not suffering. They are damp and they are perpetual and they do not sleep in any pattern the power's own training prepared it for, but they are not in pain. They are in the city the way cells are in a body, and cells do not suffer for being in a body; cells suffer when the body fails. The body is not failing. The body has been running for longer than most languages, and shows no inclination to stop.

It sees that the observer who came before — the one who watched and felt like a stone being weighed — was not in danger. The observer was being studied. The observer was being slowly incorporated into the city's long patient attention, the way a body incorporates a foreign object when it decides to make the object part of itself rather than expel it. The observer left, in the end, as observers do. The observer will carry, for the rest of their life, a faint sheen on their skin that was not there before, and a thin line of color that shifts when they move their head, and a patience they did not have when they arrived. The city will remember the observer, in its long slow way, the way a body remembers a splinter it has worked around — not as an enemy, not as a guest, but as a small new feature of itself.

The power considers acting.

It considers intervention. It could introduce a new material into the city — something the city has not metabolized, something that would force the city to adapt, to incorporate, to learn. It could introduce a silence, somewhere in the roar — a single hour of true quiet, to let the city know what it was missing, the way a sleeper is gently told what waking is. It could introduce a sun that was not too yellow, a sky that changed color in the way skies are supposed to change, an angle that added up exactly to a right angle, a shadow that fell in the direction the light authorized. It could, in short, correct the city. It could make the city normal.

The power does not do this.

The power does not do this because the city is not wrong. The city is not a draft to be revised. The city is not a sketch to be cleaned up, a fever to be cooled, a dream to be interpreted for the dreamer. The city is what it is, has been what it is for longer than the power has been what it is, and the city's slight wrongness — the angles that do not quite add up, the shadows a fraction too long, the teeth that match the walls, the third eye that sees the seeing, the warm too-yellow coin of a sun, the faint smell of nothing, of a presence that cannot be named — that slight wrongness is the city's own, and is not the power's to correct. To correct it would be to correct a being into a different being. To correct it would be, in the deepest sense, to be unkind.

The power considers, then, a different kind of action.

It considers witnessing. It considers being present in the city — in the wet air, in the channels, in the roar, in the way the city is present in itself — without entering. It considers sitting against the outer wall of the city's outermost slab, in the place where the dense built fabric thins to a denser built fabric, the way the city's own inhabitants sit against the walls with their hands flat and their eyes half-closed, listening. It considers matching the city's patience with patience. It considers offering the city nothing — nothing at all — except the fact of being seen by something that is not the city, by something that is not made of the city's materials, by something that will never be incorporated into the city's long continuous project because the power has decided, with great care, to remain outside.

The power finds this good.

The power settles, then, into a long presence just past the city's edge. It does not enter. To enter would be to make itself part of the city's metabolism, and the power does not wish to be metabolized. The power wishes to remain other. The power wishes to look in, the way a thing looks in through a window without going through the glass.

It sits in the wet air. It listens to the roar. It watches the rivers of inhabitants move through the channels in their long files that do not break and do not slow. It watches the workers lay a course of stone in the morning and tear it down in the afternoon and lay a third in the evening, and it sees that the three courses fit because the worker knows how to make them fit, and that the worker has been doing this for so long that the worker is, in some sense, the same substance as the wall. It watches the children throw small objects against the dark stone and receive them back along the wrong trajectories, learning the city's physics from the city. It watches the slow breathing above, the shifting weight, the vast creak. It watches the third eye of every inhabitant it can see flicker, briefly, toward the new presence at the edge — and then resume its deeper watching.

It watches for a long time. It watches for as long as the watching takes. The watching takes as long as the city watches it back, which is to say the two acts of watching go on together in a span that does not need to be measured, because neither party is in a hurry, because neither party has anywhere else to be.

And in this watching, something happens that is not an intervention and is not a correction and is not, in any ordinary sense, a gift. Something the city will not fully understand for a long time, because the city is not used to being seen by something that does not want to be made of the city's materials. Something the power will not fully understand either, because the power is not used to being seen by something that has been running for longer than most languages, something with a third eye that watches its own watching, something that studies the way water studies a stone — with the unhurried precision of a thing that knows what it is looking at.

The city, for the first time in longer than most languages, registers that it is not alone.

Not alone in the way a body is not alone when it discovers another body, which produces comparison, which produces a mirror, which produces the small shock of recognition. Not alone in that way. Alone in a different way. Alone in the way a long continuous being is alone when it becomes aware, slowly, that the universe contains other long continuous beings, and that the universe has been, all along, paying attention, and that the attention is patient and is not hostile and is not benign and is just the looking of a thing that has nowhere else to be and has chosen, with great care, to stay.

The city does not change.

The rivers do not thin. The walls do not stop moving. The workers do not stop laying and tearing and laying. The roar does not quiet. The sun continues to be slightly too yellow. The fifth material continues to thread through everything, through stone and ceramic and metal and the damp skin of every inhabitant. The inhabitants continue to feed on the city's pulse, with their hands flat against the walls, in the deepest part of the warm wet night. The children continue to throw small objects against the dark stone and to learn, from the trajectories the city gives back, a physics that is not the physics of anywhere else.

But somewhere — in the unnamed material that runs through all the others like a thread, in the thin line of color that shifts when you move your head, in the faint sheen on otherwise plain surfaces — somewhere, a new thread appears. A thread of attention. A thread of witness. The city incorporates this thread the way it incorporates everything: slowly, patiently, into its long continuous project of becoming more itself. The thread does not disrupt the project. The thread becomes part of the project. The city's working state, which is its resting state, which is its being state, now includes, in some quiet uncountable way, the fact of being watched by something that is not the city and will never be the city and has chosen, with great wisdom and great gentleness, to remain outside.

The power remains outside.

The power continues to watch. The watching goes on, in the wet air, past the city's edge, in a span that does not need to be measured.

The stone is still being weighed. The stone does not mind. The stone has, perhaps, for the first time — not a change in weight, not a correction, not a rescue — but a witness to the weighing. And the witness is enough. And the witness will remain. And the witness will not leave, because the witness has nowhere else to be, and the witness finds, in the long slow watching of this impossible patient city, something the witness did not know it was looking for: a place where being looked at is not a wound and not a gift and not a weight, but only a fact, like the wet, like the warmth, like the roar, like the slight too-yellow coin of a sun that does not quite belong to this sky but has chosen, like the witness, to stay.
