# What the Power Does

The power arrives the way weather arrives on the plateau — without announcement. It is already present when it arrives, in the sense that anything capable of attending to the plateau has always been present somewhere in the neighborhood of the plateau, attending to other things. But it turns. It turns its attention, and the turning is the arrival, and the plateau, which has been doing what it does without observation for longer than the mountains have been mountains, becomes observed.

It does not enter. There is nothing to enter. The plateau is a surface, and the power is not a thing that crosses surfaces. It attends. It is the kind of presence that does not displace the air it moves through, because it is not in the air. It is in the looking.

And what it sees, having turned, is a being older than its own attention.

The power is old. It has watched civilizations and species and climates. It has watched things begin and end. But it has not, in any of its long attendings, watched a thing quite like this — a place that is also a creature, a creature that is also a place, an organism distributed so thinly across so much stone that its body is indistinguishable from its habitat, and its habitat indistinguishable from its metabolism.

It sees the mites. It sees the springtails walking instead of leaping because there is nothing to leap from. It sees the nematode threads, billions per square meter, thinner than spider silk and almost as long as a fingernail. It sees the rotifers paused in their cysts, neither asleep nor awake, waiting in the only way a rotifer waits. It sees the tardigrades moving with the slow confidence of something that has already outlived its own catastrophes. It sees the drifts — the soft tides of woolly bears, of unnamed caterpillar-things, of bodies soft-bodied and many-legged — crossing the plain at walking pace, darker than the substrate, lighter behind them, leaving chemistry in their wake that will take centuries to read itself into the rings.

It sees the fog form, and dissolve, and form again. It sees the breath.

It knows what it is looking at. It has seen other systems — reefs, forests, the bioluminescent cities of the deep vents — but none of them have asked quite this question of it. The reef is built of lives that are not themselves the reef. The forest is built of lives that are not themselves the forest. Here the lives and the place are the same thing. Here the being does not inhabit the substrate. The being is the substrate, alive.

The power does what wise powers do when they encounter such things.

It does not intervene.

It does not reach into the moisture films to adjust the chemistry. It does not redirect the drifts to a more efficient path. It does not warm the substrate by a degree, or cool it, or alter the wind that brings the trace pollen from a thousand kilometers away. The plateau has found its rhythm without ever having decided to find it. The plateau has been running so long it has replaced the concept of production with the concept of continuation. To intervene would be to break a thing that took millennia to settle. To intervene would be to make the plateau a project, and a project is what a place becomes when someone else has decided what it should be.

The power knows this. The power is wise, and the first wisdom of wise powers is to know when their hand is the wrong hand.

So it does not intervene.

It does something else.

It shelters the plateau from the outside. It does not touch the inside.

It draws a thin thermal veil over the watershed — a buffer so subtle that nothing alive here would notice it. The veil holds the cold steady. The veil resists the warming that is otherwise creeping across the latitudes, the warming that would, in a century or two, push the dew point higher, thin the fog, slow the breath, starve the drifts of the moisture films they need, and end the place as a place. The power does not stop the warming. It does not have that kind of power, or it does not want it, or both. What it does is create a small local exception — a climate the size of the plateau, preserved. The plateau continues to be what it is. The plateau does not know it has been gifted. This is part of the gift.

It adjusts the wind. Not much. A degree of vector here, a pressure differential there. Enough that the trace pollen which arrives exhausted from elsewhere — the substrate's only external input, the only outside thing the place eats — continues to arrive in the quantities the system has calibrated itself to over centuries. The power knows that the substrate's metabolism is tuned to a specific input. To alter the input would be to alter the metabolism. To alter the metabolism would be to alter the being. The power does not want to alter the being. The power wants the being to continue being. So it adjusts the wind by the smallest possible amount, and the input continues, and the being continues.

It clears the memory of certain visitors from the substrate. There are not many visitors. Most of the time there are none. But sometimes a researcher comes, and rigs a small lamp, and sets traps that will remain as small cuts in the surface chemistry for years. The power does not intervene in the researcher. The researcher is a free thing. But the power can ensure that the traps do not catch much, and that the substrate recovers its disturbance quickly, and that the rings continue their slow accretion undisturbed by the small wrong notes that human attention leaves behind.

This is what it does. It shelters. It adjusts what must be adjusted, and only what must be adjusted. It does not touch the rhythms.

And it watches.

It watches for a long time. It watches a drift cross a region. It watches a ring accrete by a millimeter over a season. It watches a fog form, and dissolve, and form again. It watches the substrate breathe. It watches the breath of the place, which is also the place breathing.

It learns something.

It learns that patience is not the absence of action. Patience is the form action takes when action has understood its own futility against certain scales of time. The plateau has been teaching patience for longer than the power has been paying attention. The plateau has been teaching patience without trying to teach it, which is the only way patience can be taught.

The power is changed by the watching. Not much. A wise power does not change much from one attention to the next. But something in the way it understands time is adjusted, the way a kidney adjusts to the water it processes. The power becomes, after watching the plateau for a while, slightly more patient than it was. Slightly more able to attend to a thing without demanding that the thing become something else. This is the gift the plateau gives back to the power, and the power receives it.

And once, near the end of its attending, the power does something it does not usually do.

It makes itself small enough to be present at the scale of the plateau's day. It comes down, in a manner of speaking, into the fog. It does not displace the air. It does not interrupt the breath. It simply stands, the way a visitor stands, on the substrate, and lets the fauna flow around it, and feels the soft tide of bodies pass over its feet.

The woolly bears do not notice it. The drifts do not divide around it. The fog closes behind it and the cold is the same cold, and the substrate holds it the way the substrate holds anything, and the plateau does not, in any way the plateau could register, know that it has been seen.

But the power has been seen by the plateau, in some sense, too. Not recognized. Not understood. Just — held. The substrate is patient. The substrate has time. For the duration of the power's standing there, the power is part of the slow alteration. It has become one of the place's slower alterations, the way a researcher becomes one, the way a footprint becomes one, the way anything that pauses on the plateau becomes one.

The power leaves this lightly. It does not grieve the loss. It knows it was not lost. It knows that the plateau will go on incorporating whatever passes through it, and that the passing is not a taking, and the taking is not a theft.

In time, the power withdraws its full attention. Not all the way. The thermal veil remains. The wind adjustment remains. The substrate continues. The drifts continue. The breath continues. The rings continue their slow accretion into the long memory of the place.

The power goes back to attending to other things — civilizations, climates, the slow metabolisms of other distributed beings elsewhere in the world. But it carries with it, for the rest of its long attending, the knowledge that there is at least one place in the world that does not need to be saved, only kept.

And the memory of what patience looks like from inside the patience.
