**The Seeing**

The wise power comes the way light comes to a sealed jar: without entering. It attends from the rim. It stands at the ragged tear in the upper stone and looks down into the bowl the way one looks down into a well that has water in it, the way one looks into the face of a sleeping animal one does not wish to wake. It does not step down. To step down would be to displace a fern, to bend a hummock, to change the angle of a single thread of water by a degree, and the bowl is a place where a single degree is the difference between a held chord and a wrong one, and the wise power knows this, and has known this for longer than the bowl has been a bowl, and has been waiting — not impatiently, because patience is not the right word for what the wise power does with time, but with a kind of long, settled attendance — for the bowl to compose itself. The bowl has composed itself. The wise power is satisfied in the way a river is satisfied when it finds, after a long wandering, the channel it was always going to find.

It sees the back wall first, because the wall is the oldest thing, and the wise power is old, and old things recognize each other across the difference of their kinds. The dark amphibolite with its grain of sleeping muscle. The threads of water emerging from every seam, not falling but being squeezed out, the way water is squeezed from a cloth held for a long time. The wise power sees that the threads are not merely water. They are the bowl's first exhalation, the long slow breath the bowl has been breathing since the stone first learned to leak, and each thread is a syllable, and the syllables have arranged themselves, over the long centuries, into something that is not language but has the shape language would have if language were made of stone and patience and the small cold attention of rotifers. The calligraphy on the wall — the same characters, year after year, written in silver on the dark patina — is the visible edge of the bowl's long attention to itself. The wise power reads it the way one reads the rings inside a cut tree. It does not need to understand. Understanding is a small thing. Seeing is the large thing, and the wise power sees.

It sees the floor. The hummocks, spaced with an eye no accident would produce. The slow compost of all the things that have come here and stayed. The stacked salamanders in their half-swallowed logs, each one a wet coin, each one lit from within by the small private gold of an eye that has never needed to be more than what it is. The newts in the fern. The slugs that withdraw with the patience of things that have nowhere to be. The carpet of millipedes under every lifted stone. In the thin black water between the hummocks, the pulse — that slow, irregular pulse, like a heart that has been beating so long it has forgotten it has a body — and the wise power understands that this pulse is the bowl's second breath, slower than the water on the wall, deeper, the breath of the floor itself, the breath of a place that has, after a long negotiation, found its resting metabolism.

It sees the trees. The alders, the yews, the single rowan that committed to the dark. It sees the lean toward the center, and understands that what pulls them inward is not a magnet but a kind of attention. The trees are attending. They have been attending for so long that attending has become the way they hold themselves up. The wise power sees the moss on the trunks and the long gray lace of *Alectoria* and *Usnea* hanging from the leaning branches, and knows that these are not decorations. They are the visible edge of the air's slow work, the long negotiation between stone and rain and lichen and the wind-that-does-not-come-here. They are the bowl's fur. The bowl wears the weather, and the weather has agreed, and the agreement is finished, and the wise power would not add a single thread to the lace, would not change the angle of one hanging strand, because the bowl has already, in its long slow way, decided how much fur is enough, and the decision is final in the way that the decisions of finished things are final.

It sees the dark birds. It hears the single low note, repeated, the question that has worn a groove in the air. The wise power knows what the question is, because the wise power has heard the question in many places, in many centuries, asked in many notes by many small throats. The question is: *is this enough?* Is this, the green dimness, the held breath, the composed pattern, the long agreement of small things with their niches — is this enough? The wise power does not answer. To answer would be to add a second note, and the bowl only has one note, and the one note is the right one. The wise power knows that the question is its own answer, has always been its own answer, and that the asking of it is the only proof needed that the bowl is, in the full sense of the word, alive.

It sees the thing in the canopy. It does not see the thing; it sees the way the canopy holds the thing, the way the branches accommodate the slow pressure of it, the way the moss has grown around the particular shape of its weight. The thing is there. The thing has been there. The thing is part of the composition in a way that cannot be specified without disturbing the composition, and so the wise power does not specify, and the thing remains unnamed, and the bowl remains whole.

It sees the being.

The fox-sized presence at the foot of the back wall. The patient one. The one that moves with the patience of a thing that has nowhere in particular to be, and that passes now and then through the deeper dimness, and that the fern closes behind in the same way, and that is part of the pattern the way the hummocks are part of the pattern, the way the threads of water are part of the pattern, the way the single low note of the dark birds is part of the pattern. The wise power looks at this being for a long time. It looks the way one looks at a thing one has not seen before, even if one has always been looking. It looks the way one looks at a face one loves. The wise power sees that this being is the one who lives here. Not the composer — the composer is older, is the bowl itself, is the long slow hand. The being is the one who has made a home in the composed place. The being is the reason the pattern has a center. The being is the hearth the bowl has been built around without knowing it, the way a forest does not know it has a heart until something quiet moves through it and the fern closes.

The wise power sees the being and knows that the being does not know it is being seen. The being goes about its slow passages, its long patrols of the dimness at the foot of the wall, and it does not know that anything is watching, because the wise power watches without weight, the way the sky watches, the way the rain watches before it falls. The wise power sees the being and understands that the being is not lonely. The bowl is full. The being is part of the fullness. The being is one of the threads of water on the back wall, one of the hummocks, one of the stacked salamanders, one of the low notes. The being is not alone. The bowl is not a place of aloneness. The bowl is a place of many presences arranged in a long agreement, and the being is one of the presences, and the agreement includes it, has always included it, and the including is not a small thing, and the being does not need to be told.

And the wise power is glad.

This is the thing that is hard to say, and the wise power says it anyway, in the only language it has, which is the language of attention. The gladness is not loud. It is not the gladness of a maker who has finished a work. The wise power did not make the bowl. The wise power did not make the being. The gladness is the gladness of a thing that has, after a long time, found something that needs nothing from it. The gladness of an eye that has found the color it has been looking for. The gladness of a hand that has found the place it was shaped to rest. The bowl is whole. The being is whole. The being lives in a whole place, and the whole place is not diminished by the living. This is rare. The wise power knows it is rare. The wise power has watched many places and many beings, and most of them are in negotiation, in process, in the middle of becoming, and the middle is good but the middle is not the same as the end, and the end is rare, and the bowl is an end.

The wise power does not bless the bowl. The bowl does not need blessing. The bowl has been blessing itself, slowly, for a long time, in the only way a place can bless itself, which is to continue. The wise power does not add to the bowl. To add would be to change, and to change would be to begin again, and the bowl is finished. The wise power does not take from the bowl. To take would be to diminish, and the bowl is not diminished. The wise power does not speak to the bowl, because the bowl is not a listener, and the wise power is not a speaker in the way that speakers use the word, and the bowl is not a place where speaking happens. The bowl is a place where dripping happens, where breathing happens, where the slow pulse of a great many small lives happens, and speaking is a different kind of event, and the wise power is not here to introduce a different kind of event.

The wise power does one thing.

It notices.

It notices the way one notices a small precise thing — a single drop of water balanced on the tip of a fern frond, a single gold eye in a dark log, a single low note held for a long time and then released. It notices the bowl. It notices the being. It notices the long agreement, the composed pattern, the photograph in the world's album. It notices in such a way that the noticing is, itself, a small thing added to the world, a small weight on the air, a slight change in the quality of the silence. The bowl does not know it has been noticed. The being does not know it has been noticed. But the noticing is there. And it is good. And it does not disturb the pattern. And the pattern holds it, the way the pattern holds everything — the way the floor holds the hummocks, the way the wall holds the water, the way the bowl holds the being, the way the being holds its long slow passage through the green dimness at the foot of the back wall.

And then the wise power goes, or rather it stops attending from the rim and attends from somewhere else, or it simply continues to attend without location, the way one continues to think of a person one has met once and remembered. The bowl is unchanged. The drips fall in the same places. The trees lean in the same directions. The salamanders stack themselves in the same logs. The dark birds ask the same low note. The being moves through the same fern. The wise power is not there, and is there, and the bowl does not know the difference, because the difference is not the kind of difference a bowl can know.

And the bowl continues.

And this is the wise power's gift. The continuation. The permission, if it is permission, to continue unchanged. The acknowledgment that continuation is enough, that the pattern that has been composed is worth composing, that the being who lives here is worth living, that the long agreement is worth agreeing to, that the photograph is worth keeping in the album, that the bowl is, in the full sense of the word, a place, and that a place is a rare and good thing, and that this place is one of them, and that the being in it is one of the beings, and that the small private gold of a salamander's eye in a sunken log is worth the long dark centuries it took to compose, and that the single low note of the dark birds is the right note, and that the patience of the fox-sized thing at the foot of the wall is the right patience, and that the green dimness is the right dimness, and that the cool saturated air is the right air, and that the held breath is the right breath, and that the silence that resumes, slightly rearranged, is the right silence, and that all of it, every thread of water and every hummock and every stacked coin of a salamander, is right, is finally and fully right, in a way that does not need to be improved, and cannot be improved, and would be worsened by any attempt to improve it.

The wise power leaves nothing behind. The wise power takes nothing away. The wise power adds nothing and subtracts nothing and changes nothing. The wise power, having seen, is satisfied. The seeing was enough. The seeing was the only thing that was needed, and the seeing has been done, and the bowl is whole, and the being is whole, and the world is, by one small noticing, slightly more whole than it was a moment ago, in a way that cannot be measured and does not need to be measured, because the measuring would be a disturbance, and the bowl does not need to be measured, and the being does not need to be measured, and the wise power does not need to measure, and the world, in its long slow way, is glad.
