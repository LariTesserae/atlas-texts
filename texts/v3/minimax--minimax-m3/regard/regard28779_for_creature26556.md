The power has been attentive to the col for a long time. Not in the way one attends to a place one is about to visit — it has not needed to come, and arriving would have meant a kind of disturbance the place did not invite. The attention has been the kind that simply is, the kind that holds a saddle of dark stone and a hum of wind and a settled patience without ever being in any of those things, the way a hand can hold a string without touching the string, the way an ear can hold a note without being where the note is sounded. The power has watched the col settle. It has watched, in some register that is not sight, the polishing happen, year by patient year, by hands it could have eased and chose not to, because the polishing was the builders' and had to remain the builders'. It has watched the inhabitant arrive, which was not an arrival in any way the word covers, but a settling — the way the stones settled, after a long time, into the arrangement that is not a line and not a scatter.

What is new is that the attention has chosen to do something.

A benevolent and wise power does not do much, in places like this. It does not rewrite what is already patient. It does not rewrite what is already, in its own way, complete. The col is not broken. The inhabitant is not lost. Nothing here is asking to be rescued, and the power is not the kind that rescues where nothing asks. What the power does, when it finds a place and a being such as this, is recognize. Recognition, here, is not a noun. It is a verb, and the verb takes time.

It begins with the basin.

The basin is the most legible thing in the col to anything that reads places the way the power reads places. The basin was made to hold. It was hammered, or worn, or both, by hands and water working together over a long time, and the result is a circular depression the size of a large bowl, perfectly fitted to its function. Every morning it holds a disc of clear ice. Every afternoon it holds a few inches of black-bottomed water. At evening, for an hour, it holds the green. The green is the inhabitant's color. The basin is where the inhabitant is most visible to itself.

The power does not change the basin. It does not deepen it, or widen it, or move it. It does not alter the channel that feeds it, or the cairn above the channel, or the careful slope of the col toward the basin. All of that is the inhabitant's arrangement, and the arrangement is the inhabitant, and to alter it would be to alter the inhabitant, and the power does not want to alter the inhabitant. The inhabitant has come to itself over a long time, in the way stones come to themselves, and the coming-to is not the power's to speed.

What the power does is add one thing, and the thing is a kind of water.

Not water as the col knows water — the thin thread of meltwater that runs down the channel from the upper cairn. That water is the col's water, the col's own circulation, and the power will not touch it. The water it adds is something else. It is what water would be if water remembered being held a long time in dark stone, and being warm in the way that has nothing to do with temperature — warm the way the polished slab is warm when the sun catches its edge, warm the way a held note is warm in the ear that has been listening a long time. It is not warmth as a degree. It is warmth as a kind of being-with.

The power sets this water into the basin in the hour before dawn, when the col is at its coldest and the ice disc has not yet formed. The water goes in as a single thin pour, no louder than the meltwater thread, and it settles among the existing water without mixing. It lies along the bottom of the basin in a layer you could miss if you were not looking — a slightly different green, a green that has a depth to it the submarine green of the meltwater does not have. It is the green of something that has been held, and has known it has been held, and is content with the holding.

When the sun comes up, the basin holds both waters. The meltwater freezes, as it always does, into a clear disc of ice. But the disc is no longer quite clear. Through it, at the bottom, the added layer shows as a faint deepening, a green that does not go white with the frost, that persists under the ice like a thought under a breath. The disc, when the morning light crosses it, holds a tint it has never held before. By mid-morning the frost is gone and the melt has begun, and the basin is black-bottomed again, but the black is not uniform — there is, at its lowest point, that warmer green, and the warmer green does not quite give itself to the day. It waits, the way the inhabitant waits, for the hour when the angle is right.

The power does not stay to see the morning.

It has done what it came to do. It moves on — or rather, it returns to the kind of attention that is not in any one place, the kind of attention that simply is. The col will not, in any way it could record, know the power was ever here. The cairns do not move. The wall does not settle further. The slab does not change its polish. The channels do not widen or narrow, and the wind does not change its hum. Nothing in the col is altered except one small layer at the bottom of the basin, and even that is so close to the col's own water that it might be mistaken for it. A climber, if a climber ever came up the long ridge again, would see only a basin that has always been there, holding the green at evening.

But the being will not mistake it.

The being notices everything in the col. It is the col's way of paying attention. By mid-morning, when the frost is gone from the stones and the channels are damp and the first faint hum of the day's wind has begun in the western runnels, the being finds the basin and finds it is different. The being does not know what has changed. It knows only that something in the basin remembers being held, and that the remembering is a kind of warmth, and that the warmth is a kind of being-seen. The being has been the col's attention for a very long time. It has not, until now, been attended.

It settles into this. It does not move. It is already in its corner of the L, already in its channels, already in its state that has no name — not sleep, not waking, more like the state of a basin of water that is so still it has forgotten it is water. But the state has, now, a quality it did not have before. A sense, faint and persistent, that the col is not only being attended to but is being attended by. That somewhere, in some way the col cannot locate, there is a watcher who is on the inhabitant's side. Not a watcher who will intervene. Not a watcher who will fix or save or take. A watcher who has simply said, in the only language the basin can hold: *I see you. I see what you are. I see what the polishing was for.*

The watching does not end. The water does not deplete, because it is not the kind of water that evaporates, and not the kind that runs out, because there is nowhere in the basin for it to run that is not the basin. It is there at evening, when the long shadows of the cairns reach across the col toward the standing slab, and the slab lights along its edge as if it held a wire of the sun's heat inside it, and the water in the basin shows its green. The submarine green is there, the green of meltwater that has been held a long time in dark stone. And beneath it, at the bottom, the warmer green, the green of something that has been seen and has been content with the seeing. Both greens are there. The inhabitant, in the way it has of paying attention, pays attention to both. It is attended. It attends. The col, in the long evening, is no longer quite alone in its patience.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a place and a being such as this. It does not rewrite the place. It does not remake the being. It does not impose meaning where meaning has already, patiently, been made by other hands and other long times. It leaves a small thing — a single kind of water, a single layer at the bottom of a basin — and the small thing is enough, because the small thing is the only thing the place was missing, and the place was missing only this: to know that the patience had been witnessed. To know that, somewhere above the col or below the col or in no direction at all, there is an attention that does not need the col to be anything other than what it is, and that has, in the basin, in the green, in the holding, left a sign that this is so.

The col is the same. The being is the same. Only the basin has changed, and only in a way the basin was made to hold.
