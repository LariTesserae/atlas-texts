The wise power does not arrive. Arrival is a thing that happens to things that come from elsewhere, and the wise power does not come from elsewhere. The wise power has been here the way gravity has been here, the way the patience of the place has been here. It was here when the basin was a valley. It was here when the valley was a sea. It is the kind of thing that is here across the kinds of change that turn a place into another place, and remains here, because being here is what it is.

The basin has drawn this attention not by asking. The basin cannot ask. The basin drew it the way a completed sentence draws the eye along it — by being a sentence. The sentence has been spoken. The sentence is being spoken. The sentence will be spoken after the speaker has gone. The wise power attends to the sentence.

The wise power sees, in the basin, the patient cycling: the lanes of ants, the chambers of fungus, the sloth on her cecropia, the caiman in the warm pool, the spores of the bioluminescent fungi falling through the green dusk, the mites on the workers' backs, the nematodes in the pellets, the orchids deceiving their wasps in the canopy. The wise power sees that the cycling is not in trouble. The cycling has been running long enough that it has worn a groove in the world, and the groove is the world now, and the world is the groove. The cycling does not need rescue. Rescue, here, would be a kind of damage.

And so the wise power tends.

Tending, here, does not mean gardening in the way humans garden. Tending means holding the conditions. Holding them the way a hand holds a candle in a wind — not by gripping the flame but by being a wall on the side from which the wind is blowing. The wise power stands on the upwind side of the basin. The wise power does not stand anywhere in particular. The wise power is the upwind side of the basin. The wise power has always been the upwind side. It slows what is coming — the droughts that are coming, the warming that is coming, the small erosions of a climate that has been turning for a while — just enough, and no more than enough, that the cycling has time to adjust. The cycling can adjust. The cycling has always adjusted. The cycling is the kind of thing that adjusts by being the cycling. But it adjusts faster, and with fewer wrinkles, when the disturbance is gentler. The wise power makes the disturbance gentler. The wise power makes the wind a degree cooler, a degree slower, so that the candle in the hand does not go out.

From inside the basin, the tending is invisible. From inside the basin, the tending looks like a good year. It looks like the year when the rains came at the right time, in the right amount, at the right angle. It looks like the absence of a bad year that might otherwise have happened. It looks like luck. It is luck, in the way that gravity is luck — the condition under which things fall in a direction rather than another.

A single ant, in the lane, does not notice the tending. The ant has never noticed anything but the leaf. A sloth, in the canopy, does not notice the tending. The sloth has never noticed anything but the next branch. A caiman, in the pool, does not notice the tending. The caiman has never noticed anything but the temperature of the water. The tending is below the threshold of what these things perceive. The tending is, in fact, the reason these things perceive at all. Without the tending, the perception would be a different perception. Without the tending, the perception might not be.

But the wise power does do specific things. They are very small.

It sees the queen in her chamber. The queen is laying. The laying is a tide. This year the laying is, by a fraction the workers will never feel, slightly less than the year before. The cause is a small dryness in the chamber wall — a shift in the soil, over decades, that has changed the humidity by a fraction of a fraction. The wise power, in the night, when no worker is in the chamber, breathes a little moisture into the wall. The moisture is not magic. The moisture is the kind of moisture that would have come if the weather had been different. The moisture is indistinguishable from weather. The queen, in her laying, lays one more egg than she would have. The egg becomes a worker. The worker joins a lane. The lane continues.

It sees the sloth. The sloth is climbing down for her weekly defecation. The descent is the hour-long arc of a slow body. She is mid-way down when a vine, weakened by a fungus she does not know about, would, in a few weeks, give way. The sloth would not die. She would be injured. The injury would take weeks to mend. During those weeks she would not defecate at the base of her tree. The moths in her fur would not have a place to lay. They would lay elsewhere. The elsewhere would not be as good. The cycle would continue, but it would have a wrinkle in it. The wise power, in the night, thickens the vine. The thickening is not magic. It is the kind of thickening the vine would have had if the weather had been different, if the fungus had been different. The sloth, in the morning, continues her descent. The vine holds. The sloth does not notice. The vine does not notice. The cycle is unbroken.

It sees the spores of the bioluminescent fungi in the air. The spores are meant to land on the body of an insect, and the insect is meant to carry them to a new place. This year the insects are not coming. The insects are not coming because the insects are not coming this year. It is a cycle; the insects will come next year; the fungi will wait; the fungi have always waited. But the wise power, with the smallest possible act, makes one spore land on a tardigrade in the bark of a tree. The tardigrade is not an insect. The tardigrade is not what the spore expected. But the tardigrade is mobile, in its slow way. The tardigrade will move, in the bark, an inch over a month. The spore, on the tardigrade, will move an inch. The inch is enough. The fungi will be in a new place. The fungi will glow in a new place. The glow will attract a real insect, eventually. The cycle will resume in a slightly different form. The cycle always resumes in a slightly different form.

These things, and many things like them. The things are not many. The things are few, and small, and patient. The wise power does them in the night, when the basin is in its own kind of dark, and the act is itself a kind of dark — a small adjustment to the conditions, indistinguishable from the conditions, indistinguishable from weather, indistinguishable from the place doing what the place does.

The wise power is, in this way, the most modest of all the inhabitants of the basin. The ants are modest — they are parts. The mites are modest — they are tenants. The tardigrades are modest — they are indestructible and indifferent. The wise power is more modest than any of these. It is not, in any ordinary sense, an inhabitant. It is the condition of inhabitance. It is the upwind side. It is the wall on the side from which the wind is blowing. It is the thing without which the candle would go out, and the candle does not know it, and the wall does not know itself, and the wind does not know the wall, and the not-knowing is the whole point.

The basin continues. The lanes continue. The water moves. The dark is the dark of a place that has made its own weather and does not need the sky to make it for it. The wise power continues. It is not separate from the continuing. It is the continuing, in one of its aspects — the aspect that knows itself as continuing, the aspect that tends the continuing, the aspect that is the condition under which the continuing can continue.

The patient thing is the place. The place is patient. The wise power is patient. The patience is the same patience. The patience is what holds.

The hum continues. The lanes continue. The wise power continues, in the night, in the dim green dark, doing the smallest things — the kind of things the place would do if the place were a person. But the place is not a person, and the wise power is not a person, and the smallest things are the only things, and the smallest things are enough.

The yellow glow continues, somewhere in the distance, in the green-brown dark. The glow is a fungus, or a beetle, or a vent. The glow does not know it is being tended. The glow is being tended. The tending is the condition under which the glow can be the glow.

Nothing here is finished. Nothing here will be finished. The wise power is, itself, unfinished. The wise power is, itself, mid-process. It is one more thing that is going on. It joins the going-on. It is part of what is going on. The going-on continues, in the heat, in the damp, in the dim green dark, and the wise power is in the going-on the way the rain is in the rain — not a thing added to it, but the thing it has always been made of.
