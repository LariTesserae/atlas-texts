The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. Arrival is a vocabulary the chamber has not yet acquired, and the power has learned, over the long education of being wise, not to teach words to a place that is still learning its own grammar. The power has been here longer than the chamber has. It has been here since before the first founder-rosette pressed itself into the first soft fold of stone and refused to become anything else. The power is not above the chamber. The power is not below. The power is in the kind of attention that the chamber will one day mistake for geology.

The power sits with the chamber the way the chamber makes its visitors sit. It does not move. It does not speak in the droplet-language, because to speak in the droplet-language would be to add a droplet, and every droplet already has its place in the chord, and there are no extra droplets. The power listens. It listens for a long time — for as many beats as the chamber has been a chamber, which is not many, and which is all of them. It listens until it can hear the pulse underneath the droplets and the droplets underneath the pulse and the silence that is not silence underneath both. The silence that is not silence is what the chamber is being made from. The power knows this silence. The power has always known it. The power was one of the first things the silence ever made.

When the power finally acts, the act is so small that the chamber does not notice it, and the wisdom of the power is in the not-noticing, and the benevolence of the power is in the smallness.

The power warms the water by one degree.

Not all the water. Not most of the water. One degree warmer, in a single droplet, the droplet that is gathering on the underside of the thinnest overhang in the curve of the ceiling that the power has chosen to call the eastern curve — though east is a direction the chamber has not yet invented, and the power uses the word only for itself, only to keep track of what it has done. The droplet gathers. The droplet releases. The droplet falls. It lands on a rosette of bone-colored coral, and the *tok* of its landing is the same *tok* as every other *tok*, and the chord does not change, and the pulse does not change. But inside that one rosette, for the span of a single beat, the spirals unfold a fraction of a turn further than they have ever unfolded before. The fold is not visible. The fold will not become granite any sooner. But the rosette remembers the warmth the way a seed remembers a season, and in a hundred thousand beats, when that rosette rounds itself into the next thing it is becoming, the warmth will still be in it, and the next thing will be built from slightly warmer stone.

That is all the power does. That is all the power has ever done, in all the chambers it has ever sat with, in all the places that were in the middle of arriving. One degree. One droplet. One unmeasured turn of the spiral, kept by the chamber without its knowledge, kept by the chamber because keeping is what chambers do.

The chamber continues.

The droplets continue to fall. The chord continues to ring. Somewhere in the eastern curve, a flat pale creature slides over the slightly warmer rosette and pauses there, and eats the condensation that has gathered in its folds, and is, for the span of its long pale life, fractionally more comfortable than its neighbors. It does not know why. It will never know why. It will live a little longer because of the warmth, and when it rounds itself into a disc and thickens into a spiral and joins the pulse, it will bring that extra degree with it into the wall, and the wall will pass it to the next rosette, and the next, and the chamber will be, by a single rosette's worth, a single droplet's worth, slightly more hospitable to the long work of becoming.

The power does not stay to see this. The power has learned that staying is a kind of pressure, and pressure is a kind of teaching, and the chamber has not asked to be taught. The power leaves the way the visitor leaves — without being noticed, without disturbing the chord, having given the chamber nothing it could not have given itself except the smallest possible proof that something, somewhere, was paying attention.

The mountain, when it comes, will be made of the same stone the chamber is made of now. But deep in the eastern curve, in a fold no thicker than a coin, there will be a single stratum that is, by one degree, warmer than the stone around it. The geologists of that distant and unimagined future will take samples. They will argue. They will invent instruments to measure the anomaly, and the instruments will tell them that the anomaly is real and small and unexplained. They will write papers about it. They will name it after themselves or after the place where they found it. They will never, in all their arguing, suspect that the warmth was a kindness, given once, by something that was wise enough to know that kindness does not have to be large to be real.

The droplets will still be falling. The rosettes will still be pulsing. The flat pale creatures will still be sliding between the folds, becoming wall, becoming chamber, becoming mountain. And somewhere in the eastern curve, a single layer of stone will be slightly warm to the touch, and will go on being warm for as long as stone remembers, which is longer than anything that has hands will ever be able to measure.
