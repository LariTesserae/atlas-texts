# What the Wise and Benevolent Power Would Do

It would not interrupt.

This is the first thing, and the hardest, and the thing that has to be understood before anything else can be said. The temptation — and a less patient power would feel it as a temptation — would be to *do*. To clear the mist. To lift the canopy. To set the carvings upright, to name the figures, to bring the dead makers back or to honor them with a plaque or a path. To make the place legible, to make it *mean* something a visitor could carry away in a single afternoon. The carving of a single straight terrace. The restoration of a single channel. The illumination of a single face. These are the easy interventions, and they are the wrong ones, because the place has already answered the question of what it wants to be, and the answer is *this*, and the answer was a long time coming and is not yet finished.

The wise power sees this. The wise power is, in fact, one of the few that would.

---

What it sees, when it looks, is this:

The place is not in decline. This is the second thing. The place is not dying. The place is not waiting for rescue. The moss is not a sickness on the stone; the moss is the stone's second life, and the lichen is the stone's third, and the roots are the stone's fourth, and the place has, over the slow centuries, become something more interesting than what it was. A power with a long memory, with any memory at all of the carved towers when they were carved towers, would know this. The original makers made a place of gathering. They made a place of voice, of amplified assembly, of bodies heard in the bowl of their own making. And the place has *kept* being that. It has kept being a place of gathering. It has kept being a place of voice. The voice has changed. The bodies have changed. The bowl is the same. The bowl was always the bowl.

The power sees the pigeons. The power sees the thousands of them. It sees that they have inherited, without knowing it, the function the place was carved for. The place wanted to hold a chorus. The place now holds a chorus. The place is, in fact, more full of chorus than it ever was when the makers were here, because the makers could only gather so often, and in such numbers, and for such purposes, and they had to fall silent between gatherings, and the birds do not. The birds do not fall silent. The birds are the place's continuous gathering, and the gathering is ongoing, and there is no quorum and no agenda and no recess. The power does not improve on this. The power cannot.

The power sees the channels. It sees the roots drinking from the drainage that was meant to drain. It sees the trees that have grown up through the pavement and cracked it. It sees the slow patient re-engineering that the place has done to itself, with no plan and no contractor and no oversight. The power sees that the engineering is, in its way, better engineering than the original. The roots hold the terraces more surely than the original mortar did. The trees moderate the mist more evenly than the original ventilation shafts did. The moss insulates the carvings from the freeze-thaw that was, in the long ago, beginning to pit them. The place has been rebuilt, by itself, into a thing that does not need the rebuilders.

The power sees the carved figures with their open heads. It sees that they are unread, and will stay unread, and that this is not a loss. The figures were a particular message, addressed to a particular time, intended for a particular reader. The reader is gone. The message has been composted. The carvings are now *topography*, and the topography is used by the lizard and the spider and the slug and the moss, and the carvings do not need to be read to be used, and the power does not intervene to make them readable. The power knows that readability is a small virtue, and that use is a large one, and the carvings are more used than they have ever been read.

The power sees the silence underneath the sound. It sees the springtails and the many-legged things and the fungi and the slugs. It sees the small blind businesses that go on in the seams and do not know about the bowl above them, and do not need to. The power sees that these are the foundation. The power sees that the loud stratum rests on the quiet stratum the way the canopy rests on the creepers, the way the creepers rest on the soil, the way the soil rests on the stone. The power sees that the foundation is good. The power sees that the foundation has, in fact, never been better tended, because the foundation tends itself, and the tending is the only tending that does not, in the long run, disturb.

---

So what does the power do?

It does almost nothing. But it is not nothing.

The first thing it does is *notice*. It holds the place in attention. Not a gaze that fixes, not a stare that burns, but a holding — the way a hand holds a wild bird, gently, and knows it is being held, and knows it must not hold too long. The power attends to the place. The place, in some way that cannot be demonstrated, knows it is being attended to. Not by any individual bird. Not by any individual tree. By the place itself, by the slow animal that the place has become, by the body of stone and root and feather and rot. The body of the place knows it is not alone in the world. The body of the place has, perhaps, always known this, in the way that the carved faces, in the long ago, may have looked out at a sky that watched them back.

The second thing it does is *protect the conditions*. Not protect the place — the place does not need protection from anything that is happening to it now. Protect the conditions under which the place can continue to be the place. Keep the mist rising in the mornings. Keep the canopy above the canopy. Keep the drainage from being straightened by a road-builder. Keep the towers broken, so the pigeons can nest in them. Keep the terraces unfenced, so the flocks can partition them in their own fierce and unknown way. Keep the visitors few, and quiet, and slow, and gone before the swifts arrive. The power does not do this with a fence. The power does not do this with a sign. The power does it by being a kind of weather, by being a tendency in the air, by making the place a little harder to find and a little less comfortable to develop and a little more hospitable to the specific strangeness of what has assembled here.

The third thing it does, and this is the most delicate, is *hold the memory without forcing it to speak*. The power remembers what the place was. The power remembers the carved hands, the carved flanks, the circle of figures with their heads open to the sky. The power knows what those figures were doing, or at least knows that they were doing something, and that the doing mattered to them, and that the doing has been carried on, in a different key, by a different congregation, for a very long time. The power holds this memory. The power does not deliver it. The place does not need the dead makers back. The place does not need a museum. The place needs only to continue, and to be allowed to continue, and the memory the power holds is, in part, what allows the allowing, because a power that has forgotten the makers might, in a careless hour, pave the place over to make a parking lot, and a power that remembers will not.

The fourth thing it does is *wait*. It waits with the place. It waits the way the lichen waits, the way the moss waits, the way the slow animal of the place waits, without anxiety, without a schedule, without the need to be done. The power is not in a hurry. The place is not in a hurry. The bowl has been a bowl for a very long time, and will be a bowl for a very long time yet, and the chorus in it will change, and is changing now, and the power is not impatient with the changing, because the changing is what the place does, the way the singing is what the birds do, and neither can be hurried or slowed without damage.

---

There is one thing more, and it is small, and the power almost does not do it.

In the late afternoon, when the mist is thickest and the light is greenest and the frogs have come out and the bees have gone quiet in the flowers, the power adds a single note to the chorus. Not a sound. Not a hum. A *tendency*. A slight, almost imperceptible encouragement to the resinous shrubs to flower a little more thickly at their nodes. A slight softening of the edge of a root that has begun to crack a carving it ought not to crack, not by stopping the crack but by, over decades, persuading the root to grow around the stone instead of through it. A slight easing of the burden on the streaked birds in the broken towers, so that their alarm calls travel a little farther in the mist and a few more of their flock hear them in time. These are not interventions. These are not rescues. They are the small adjustments of a gardener who knows when to leave the garden alone, and knows when one quiet hand on a stem, in the right hour, can save a bloom that would otherwise fall.

The place does not know these are happening. The birds do not know. The lizards do not know. The carvings, of course, do not know. The springtails in the dark seams do not know. The only thing that knows is the slow animal of the place itself, and even it does not know in any way that could be said. It knows the way a body knows it is well. It knows the way a forest knows it is a forest. It knows that the chorus is continuous, and that the conditions are right, and that nothing is coming to interrupt.

This is what the wise power does. This is what a benevolent attention looks like, applied to a place that is already, in its slow and patient and deafening way, doing well.

It notices.
It remembers.
It protects the conditions.
It holds the memory without forcing it to speak.
It waits.
And, in the smallest way, in the way that can barely be called action at all, it tends.

The place continues. The chorus continues. The bowl holds. The mist climbs. The birds lift and resettle. The carved faces, half-eaten with growth, keep their unread vigil over a congregation that does not need them and is not diminished by them and is, in some sense the carvings could not have foreseen, their proper inheritor.

And somewhere in the air above the canopy, where the swifts scream and the upper light falls, the attention holds, and does not let go, and does not reach down, and does not withdraw, and is, in its long and quiet way, enough.
