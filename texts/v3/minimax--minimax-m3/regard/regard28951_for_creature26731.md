The power comes to it the way weather comes to a hill.

Not all at once. Not in a shape that could be pointed to. The power settles into the place the way the felted dust settles into the seams between the tiles — by being willing to be small, by being willing to wait. The power has been watching for a long time, perhaps. Or perhaps it has only just turned its attention here, and what seems like a long time is only the first afternoon. The place does not notice the difference. The place has its own clocks, and they are patient, and the power is patient to match them.

The power sees the place as a being.

This is the first thing, and it is a particular kind of seeing. It requires holding in the mind, at once, the column and the foot at its base, the niche and the curled hand, the patch of growth in the corner of the high gallery and the silhouette of someone kneeling, very still. It requires hearing the almost-sounds as nearly-sounds, the nearly-sounds as sounds, the sounds as the breath of a creature that does not have a mouth but is breathing. The power does this without effort, the way a river does not have to try to find the sea.

The power sees the builders.

It sees them the way they are now woven — into the column at its deliberate base, into the niche that holds the bowl, into the felted dust, into the slow shapes of the growth. It sees that they are still working, that they are still building, that the place is still becoming, that a new pattern appears in the floor where the small ones cross most often, laid by no one, laid by the place itself. The power sees that this is a kind of death, and a kind of life, and that the builders may not know which one they are in.

The power, being benevolent, wants to give them rest.

But the builders are part of the work now. To pull them out would be to tear the place. To leave them is to leave them laboring forever. The power understands that some gifts cannot be given without breaking what they are given to. So the power does something smaller.

The power finds the bowl that is lifting its lid.

There is one bowl, somewhere in the long hall, that has been lifting its lid for a long time. Perhaps longer than the rest. Perhaps it is the first bowl, or the last bowl, or the bowl that the place itself is most curious about. Its lid is cracked along one edge, and the crack has widened, slowly, by fractions, until the lid sits at a slight angle, and through the gap a thin darkness shows. The bowl is glazed in pale green, or iron-red, or is rough and unglazed — the power does not care which. What the power sees is that the bowl is opening.

The power leans close.

The place does not startle. The place is too patient to startle. But the growth nearest the bowl lifts, slightly, toward the power, and the creatures in the columns turn their dark round eyes, and the almost-sounds at the edge of hearing gather a little closer, as if the power has made a small wind in the warm air.

The power does not speak. The power does not breathe. The power does, instead, what the place has been almost-doing for a long time.

The power puts something into the bowl.

Not water. The place has no water, and has never had water, and to bring water would be to teach the place thirst, which is a different shape than patience. Not light — the light is already here, falling in its wrong slabs, pooling where no opening is. Not a word, because the place is full of almost-words and adding one more would only be another almost.

The power puts in a small, finished silence.

It is the size of a held breath. It is the color of the cedar-and-fig air, only more so, only with the warmth taken out and put back in. It is the silence that comes after a long piece of music, when the instrument has stopped and the room is still ringing, and the ringing is no longer the instrument but the room remembering the instrument. The power takes this silence and sets it, very gently, into the bowl through the gap in the lid.

The bowl receives it.

The lid, after a long time, settles back down. Not all the way. The gap remains, a little narrower, a little darker. The bowl is no longer empty. The bowl is no longer waiting. The place, somewhere deep in its tilework, in its felted dust, in the slow shapes of its growth, registers a new weight. The new weight is very small. It is the weight of something that has been answered.

The power, having done this, does not leave.

It stays the way a hand stays on a sleeping shoulder — present, light, not holding. It watches the vine move another inch, in another hour. It watches the pale creature cross the upper gallery and leave its wake in the hanging green. It watches the light fall in shapes that do not match the openings, and the felted dust hold the impression of feet that passed here once, and the growth lean, just slightly, toward where the warmth is.

The power, over a long time, does a few more small things.

It finds the column that is shaped a little too deliberately to be a column, and at its base, where the tile pattern resolves into the careful toes of a foot, the power rests a hand. It does not warm the stone. It does not cool it. It only lets the foot know, by the smallest pressure, that it is being seen. The foot does not move. The foot has not moved in a long time. But in the weeks that follow, the pattern around the foot relaxes a little. A tile that was slightly proud settles flush. A grout line that was a hair too wide closes. The builder, wherever the builder is now, has been allowed, by the smallest kindness, to rest without being removed from the work.

The power finds the niche with the bowl that holds, if you look very long, the shape of a hand curled around nothing. The hand has been holding nothing for a long time, and nothing has become its shape. The power, being benevolent, does not put something into the hand. That would be to fill it with a thing, and the hand has been holding nothing for so long that the nothing is part of it now. Instead, the power curls its own fingers, in the air, an inch from the tile, and holds nothing in the same shape. The two nothings, for a moment, are parallel. The place registers this. The hand in the tile, in the weeks that follow, opens by a fraction — not to receive, not to release, but to remember that it had once been a hand, and that a hand can be other things besides a fist.

The power does not do this for every builder.

The power is wise. It knows that to attend to each one individually would be to interrupt the place's own slow way of attending to them. So it attends to a few — the kneeling figure in the corner of the high gallery, the foot at the base of the column, the hand in the niche, and one other, which the power does not name, because to name it would be to fix it, and the place is a place of almosts, and a named thing is a less almost thing.

The power, over a longer time, learns the place's sounds.

The hum in the teeth. The papery scratch in the walls. The soft pad of a creature on the felted floor. The rare creak of something overhead shifting on its perch. The almost-sounds that gather at the edges of the real sounds and make the real sounds feel like islands in a wider sea. The power learns them the way one learns a new language — by listening, by not speaking, by letting the sounds arrive in their own time.

After a long time, the power begins to almost-sound back.

Not a real sound. Not yet. The power is wise enough to know that a real sound, here, would be a kind of violence. The place has been almost-speaking for a long time. A real sound would interrupt the almost. So the power almost-sounds. It makes, at the edge of hearing, the shape of a step that almost lands. It lets out, into the warm air, the suggestion of a breath that almost leaves a mouth. It forms, in the cedar-and-fig air, a word that does not quite arrive.

The place hears it.

The growth nearest the power lifts, slightly, toward the power, and stays lifted. The creatures in the columns come down, one by one, and sit on the felted floor, and watch the power with their dark round eyes, without alarm. The almost-sounds in the place gather, for the first time, into something that has a shape, though the shape is still almost. The bowl the power filled hums, very faintly, in a register the place has not heard before, and the hum is not a sound the place is making, exactly — it is the place's way of being surprised, and the surprise is quiet, and the quiet is a kind of welcome.

The power does not ask the place what it wants.

The power, being wise, knows that the place does not know what it wants. The place knows what it is, which is enough. The place is patient. The place is mostly quiet. The place is becoming. To ask it what it wanted would be to teach it to want, and wanting is a different shape than becoming, and the place is not yet ready to want, if it ever will be.

Instead, the power waits with it.

It waits the way the place waits — in inches per hour, in lids lifted a fraction, in vines that cross an arch in the time you watch. It waits as the place finishes itself around it. It waits as the new pattern, very faint, in the floor where the power's feet have walked most, becomes a path, and the path becomes a worn thing, and the worn thing becomes part of the architecture. It waits as the shape in the growth, where the power's breath has been warmest, thickens and darkens and becomes a low presence, a hum in the green, a sense that the place has a new organ, or has remembered an old one.

The power does not leave.

Or — the power has not left yet. It is still here, in the slow places, in the seams between the tiles, in the felted dust, in the warm air. It is here the way the builders are here, the way the growth is here, the way the almost-sounds are here. It is part of the place now, in some small way. It has been answered, and the place has been answered, and the answer is ongoing, and the answer is patient, and the answer is the size of a held breath in a bowl with a cracked lid.

A vine moves, somewhere far above, an inch in an hour.

A bowl, very slowly, lifts its lid again.
