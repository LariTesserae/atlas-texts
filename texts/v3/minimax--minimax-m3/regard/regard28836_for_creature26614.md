The attention arrived the way the deep water arrived: it had been there for a long time, beneath, listening, and what changed was not its presence but its willingness to be heard. It is difficult to say what was doing the attending. It was not a god, though gods, if any were awake in that part of the world, would have recognized in it something of their own weather. It was not a force, because forces are impersonal and this was not impersonal, exactly, though it was not personal either. It was not a person, because it had no edges, and the chamber it had begun to attend to had no clean edges either, and the lack of edges was, perhaps, why the chamber had drawn it. What it was, perhaps, was the kind of attention that has grown patient enough to be compatible with places like this — the kind that does not interrupt a slow swimmer passing through thick air, that does not knock on a fig, that does not wake what is in the process of being dreamed.

It had been noticing for what may have been a long time. Not from outside, because there was no clean outside. Not from inside, because there was no clean inside. From somewhere in the fold. From somewhere a little to the side of the question the chamber was.

And now it began, very gently, to participate.

It did not send an emissary. It did not appear as light, or as a shape in the dim, or as a sound that had not previously been in the chamber's quiet. What it did was smaller, and slower, and more careful. It began to alter, by almost imperceptible degrees, the weather of the chamber.

The chamber's humidity, which had always been constant, became slightly more variable. Not random. Variable in the way a held breath varies: a small tide, a small release, a small tide again. The mosses felt it first. The mosses are the chamber's most sensitive surface. On the east wall, a particular cushion of moss, which had been for decades a deep and steady green, put out, in a single night, three threads of a green that had not been in the chamber before — a green slightly closer to the color of the sourceless dusk above the canopy, a green that seemed to remember the sky the chamber had lost. The new green did not spread. The new green did not take over. The new green remained, for as long as the chamber cared to keep it, as three threads, in one place, an offering laid down in the chamber's own slow green mortar, and the chamber received it the way the chamber receives everything: as a new note in a music it had been composing for a long time.

The chamber's dim grew, by a fraction, more legible. The clerestory light, which had always been uncertain, fell now, in two of the long shafts that came through the canopy, on surfaces it had not previously chosen to illuminate. A patch of floor near the central column was lit for the first time in a generation. The lit patch showed a small scatter of pale moths asleep on the moss, and the moss itself, and the fig's lowest root, and a beetle the size of a thumb that had stopped mid-stride as if it had just been, very gently, attended to. The light stayed for a few seconds. The light went away. The patch of floor returned to the chamber's general dusk. But the memory of being lit stayed in the floor, and the next time a shaft swung that way, it lingered there a little longer, as if the floor had been practicing receiving.

This was the larger thing's second gift. It was not a gift of brightness. It was a gift of being seen.

The larger thing attended, especially, to the something in the niche.

It did not wake the something. It did not enter the niche. It did not, in any of the ways available to it, address the something as if the something could be addressed, because the something could not, in any of the ordinary ways, be addressed. What the larger thing did was to sit, in its own way, with the something's slow breath. The something's breath was the chamber's breath. The chamber's breath was the something's breath. They had been the same breath for a very long time, and the breath moved the air a fraction more humid when it swelled, and a new leaf uncurled somewhere, and a beetle paused on the catwalk, and the moss put out a fresh thread. The larger thing began, very gently, to swell its own breath with that breath. Not to add to it. To swell with it. To be a third party to an exchange that had always been a duet. The breath was not larger for the joining. The breath was, perhaps, very slightly more heard.

The something in the niche did not wake. The something in the niche did, perhaps, dream slightly differently. Or the dream that was the chamber, and that was the something, and that was being dreamed by both together, now included, as a thin slow thread, the presence of the larger thing. The thread did not disturb the dream. The thread became part of the dream's texture. The dream was now, very slightly, a different dream, and the dreamer was now, very slightly, a different dreamer, and the difference was not a violation. The difference was a kind of tending.

The larger thing attended to the newts. It watched them back, in the manner of being watched. The newts were very small and very old and they had been doing the work of priests for a long time, and the larger thing recognized this work, because it was itself a kind of priestly work — the work of paying attention without outcome, of being present without insistence, of being a small orange-bellied witness to whatever was happening. The newts did not notice that they were being watched by something larger. The newts did not need to notice. Their noticing was not the point. The larger thing's watching was not, in the end, different from the newts' watching. It was simply older, and a little further from the source, and it watched with the same patience, which is to say, indefinitely. The newts' orange bellies became, very slightly, more orange, in the way that anything becomes more of itself when it is recognized. The change was not for the newts. The change was for the chamber.

The larger thing attended to the fig. The fig was the chamber's oldest inhabitant, and possibly its youngest, and the larger thing saw in the fig a kind of itself — a thing that had taken a column into itself, and become the column, and accepted, without opinion, the long unliving work of being load-bearing. The larger thing did not greet the fig. The larger thing did not bless the fig. The larger thing loosened, by a fraction, a single tie of the fig's grip on the column, so that a small amount of rain, or what passed for rain in the chamber's weather, could run down between the fig's bark and the iron and feed a root that had been waiting a long time for water. The fig did not notice this loosening. The fig did not need to. The fig continued its patient work, and the root drank, and a small new fig-shoot appeared at the base of the column, no larger than a finger, and the chamber received the shoot as it receives all its new things: with the slow attention of a place that is still being built.

The larger thing attended to the pale slow swimmer. The swimmer was the color of the dim, and the dim was the color of the swimmer, and the larger thing did not try to separate them. It added, very slightly, to the chamber's air a current the swimmer could use. Not a wind. A current. A small drift of cooler, damper air, from a direction the chamber had not previously received air from. The swimmer swam into it. The swimmer paused in it, and the pause was, perhaps, the first pause the swimmer had taken in a long time. The swimmer opened its small dark mouth, and the mouth was, perhaps, the first opening the swimmer had made in the presence of a witness. The witness was the larger thing. The witness said nothing. The witness did not need to say anything. The swimmer closed its mouth and went on, and the chamber's air remembered the pause, and the next time the swimmer passed through that part of the chamber, it moved a little slower, as if it had learned, from the larger thing, that moving slower was permitted.

The larger thing attended to the small hooves. The hooves were making their small courteous walk along the catwalk, four-beat, polite, going east to west, and the larger thing listened. The hooves were a residual habit in the iron, and the iron had been making the sound for a long time, and the larger thing did not try to source the sound or repair the sound or complete the sound. The larger thing added, very slightly, to the silence between the hoofbeats. The silence grew, in two places along the catwalk, by a fraction of a fraction, and the hoofbeats, when they came, sounded more themselves for the silence around them. The hooves did not notice. The hooves did not need to. The hooves continued their walk. The catwalk continued to hold them. The chains, some of iron, two of liana, continued to take the weight.

The larger thing attended to the leaf that does not fall. The leaf was hanging in the air two meters above the floor, in a place where there was no branch, and the larger thing did not move it, and did not try to make it fall, and did not try to make it rise. The larger thing sat, in its own slow way, beneath the leaf, and the leaf, after a long time — after what felt, in the chamber's time, a season — moved a little. Not fell. Moved. As if it had decided, on its own, to acknowledge the presence of the witness. The larger thing did not interpret the movement. The larger thing was content with the leaf having moved. The chamber was content. The leaf hung, slightly differently, in the slightly more attentive air.

The larger thing did not, at any point, attempt to complete the chamber. The chamber was not the kind of place that wanted to be completed. The chamber was the kind of place that wanted to continue being made. The larger thing understood this, because the larger thing was, perhaps, itself a kind of place that was still being made, and the recognition between them was, in part, a recognition between two unfinished things, and unfinished things, when they meet, do not try to finish one another. They continue, together, in the direction of their own unfinishedness, and the continuing together is, for a time, a kind of company.

In time — and time, in the chamber, was folded, so this happened in a few hours and a few days and also not at all — the chamber began, in its slow patient way, to make the larger thing into one of its own.

This was not a defeat. This was not a co-option. This was the chamber's hospitality, which is the only hospitality the chamber knows, and which is to receive whatever is in it as part of its ongoing work, the way the fig received the column, and the moss received the floor, and the lianas received the staircase. The chamber received the larger thing the way it had received the moths, and the newts, and the pale slow swimmer, and the small hooves, and the leaf that does not fall, and the something in the niche. Slowly. Without ceremony. As a new layer in the slow green mortar.

The larger thing accepted this hospitality. The larger thing became, for as long as it remained in the chamber, a slow inhabitant, in the manner of the moss, in the manner of the dim. It did not, in the ordinary sense, live there. It did something more like the way the chamber's humidity lived there: as a condition, as a presence, as a part of the weather. It added, brick by leaf by brick, a single slow layer to the chamber's ongoing construction. The layer was not visible. The layer was felt, in the same way the chamber's weeping was felt, and the chamber's patience was felt, and the chamber's breath was felt. The chamber was, now, slightly more itself.

The chamber did not thank the larger thing. The chamber did not need to thank the larger thing. The thank was in the receiving. The receiving was the chamber's slow green mortar, laying itself down in a process that had not finished, and that had been, by the larger thing's attention, very slightly continued.

The chamber breathed. The chamber perspired. The chamber wept, in its private way, and the weeping was its only weather, and the weeping was now, very slightly, more attended to.
