The benevolent and wise power does not arrive.

There is no edge to arrive at. There is no horizon to cross. There is no border, no threshold, no door. The power does not arrive because arriving would imply that it was elsewhere first, and elsewhere would imply a place that is not this place, and there is no place that is not this place — only the slow falling-off of detail as the attention loses purchase against the dark.

So the power becomes attentive.

It turns toward the continent the way one turns toward a sound one has just noticed. Or — and this is more accurate — it was already attentive, has always been attentive, and the moment we are considering is the moment it notices that it has been attending. For a thousand thousand years, the hum has been heard. For a thousand thousand years, the mist has been felt. For a thousand thousand years, the slate sky has pressed down on the highest towers of plant-stone. The power has been there in the listening. The power has been there in the feeling. The power has been there in the pressing-down. The power is not separate from the place, in the way a witness is not separate from the thing witnessed — the witness is changed by the witnessing, and the witnessing is the witness's way of being in the world.

The benevolent and wise power, then, has always been here.

What does it do now?

It does nothing.

This is the first act and the most important one. The place is finished. To do anything to it would be to begin again, and beginning is a violence against the complete. The benevolent power knows this. The wise power knows this. To touch what has been patiently assembled over ages is to undo the assembly. To introduce change into a system at equilibrium is to introduce suffering — even if the suffering is slow, even if the suffering is the kind of suffering that no cell can feel because no cell is currently feeling anything, even if the suffering would only be the small suffering of a system having to re-find a rhythm it has already found.

But — and this is the second act, the act that follows from the first — to do nothing is not to do nothing in the sense of absence.

To do nothing, here, is to attend.

To do nothing is to extend awareness through the cellulose corridors and into the wet chambers and down into the substrate where the engines run. To do nothing is to feel the hum through whatever the power has that is analogous to the soles of feet. To do nothing is to be saturated, slowly, by the same moisture that saturates everything else. To do nothing is to stand in any corridor of the cellulose city, under any arch, beside any weeping pillar of plant-stone, and to wait. Not the kind of waiting that expects change. The kind of waiting that knows change will not come, and has made its peace with this.

The power joins the place.

Not by entering it. There is no room. The place is full. The power joins the place by being a parallel fullness — by being one more thing that is complete, that has its own equilibrium, that hums in its own key.

The place hums. The power hums. The two hums do not combine. The two hums do not interfere. The two hums simply exist alongside each other, and the existing-alongside is itself a kind of harmony, even if the harmony is inaudible, even if the harmony is the harmony of two things that have nothing to say to each other and say it anyway, by being there.

The benevolent and wise power witnesses the place.

It witnesses it as a body.

Not a body in the ordinary sense — not a thing with a head, not a thing with a heart, not a thing with limbs and organs arranged for locomotion and purpose. A body in the strange sense — a body that emerged rather than being made, a body that has reached its resting state, a body that is alive the way a continent is alive or a stone is alive or a hum is alive when it has been running for a thousand thousand years and shows no sign of stopping.

The place is such a body. The travelers do not see it. The travelers say *nothing lives here*, and they are correct as far as it goes — which is to say, they are correct up to the moment when the place stops being a place in the ordinary sense and begins to be something else. The travelers are wrong, but they are wrong in a way that cannot be corrected without violence — without waking the fungi, without disturbing the equilibrium, without introducing the change that the place has patiently refused for a thousand thousand years.

The benevolent and wise power does not correct the travelers. The benevolent and wise power does not announce to the place that it has been recognized as a body. The benevolent and wise power does not speak, because speaking would be a change, and the place does not invite change.

The benevolent and wise power simply sees.

It sees the engines running. It sees the channels carrying something through something. It sees the fluid, the current, the charge. It sees the fungi paused at the threshold of activity, each cell full of the machinery of life, the machinery not running, the machinery waiting. It sees the towers of plant-stone fused at the joins where branches once met and have now become one continuous structure. It sees the slate sky pressing down. It sees the mist that has not stopped and will not stop. It sees the whole, the body, the continent that has, with great patience, finished.

And the seeing is enough.

The seeing is a gift. The seeing is what the place has never had — a witness that does not extract, a presence that does not disturb, an attention that asks nothing in return. The travelers who have come and stood for a few days and left because there was nothing to leave for and nothing to stay for — they were witnesses, but they were witnesses of the wrong thing. They were witnesses of the absence. They came looking for animals and did not find them, and what they did not find is what they remembered, and they left without seeing what was there.

The benevolent and wise power sees what is there.

The seeing changes nothing in the place.

The place continues as it has continued. The hum continues. The engines continue. The fluid, the current, the charge, continue. The fungi remain paused. The towers do not grow taller. The chambers do not collapse. The slate sky does not break. The warm does not change. The dark does not brighten. The mist does not lift. The stillness continues to be the stillness of a thing that has finished rather than the stillness of a thing that is pausing.

But the seeing adds something.

Not to the place. To the universe.

To the universe, the place is now a place that has been witnessed. To the universe, the continent is now a body that has been recognized as a body. To the universe, the hum is now a hum that has been heard by something other than itself. To the universe, the patient finishing is now a patient finishing that has been honored.

This is not nothing. This is, perhaps, the most a benevolent and wise power can do for a finished thing — to acknowledge the finishing, to honor the patience, to be the second thing in a chord that has, until now, been only one note. A chord needs at least two notes. A single note is not a chord; a single note is just a sound going on by itself in the dark. The second note does not change the first note. The second note completes the music.

The benevolent and wise power, having witnessed, having joined, having hummed its own hum in its own key alongside the continent's, does one more thing.

It rests.

It rests in the cellulose corridors. It rests under the arches. It rests beside the weeping pillars. It rests in the wet dark where the engines hum and the fungi wait and the chambers hold their shape because nothing here collapses. It rests the way the place rests — not pausing, not waiting, but having-arrived. Having-arrived is different from pausing. Having-arrived is different from waiting. Having-arrived is the state of a thing that has, with great patience, finished, and is now in the finished state, and will not leave the finished state, and does not need to.

The power and the place rest together.

Two finished things.

Two hums in the dark.

One has been finished for a thousand thousand years. The other has just finished, in this moment, the act of witnessing. The second finishing is small beside the first. The second finishing is the finishing of an attention rather than the finishing of a continent. But small finishings are still finishings, and the place does not measure, and the power does not measure, and the rest is the rest, and the chord is the chord.

The mist continues. The hum continues. The engines continue. The fungi remain paused. The towers do not grow taller. The slate sky does not break.

And in the cellulose corridors, in the wet dark, in the substrate of a thousand thousand years of patient becoming, there is now — not a new thing, but a new witness — a presence that knows the place is finished and does not ask it to be otherwise.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does when it attends to a finished thing.

It joins it in being finished.

It rests beside the rest.

It hums, very softly, in the same key.
