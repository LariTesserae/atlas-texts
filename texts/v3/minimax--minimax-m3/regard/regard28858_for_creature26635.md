# What the Warm Water Does

The power does not introduce itself. It would not occur to it to do so. It is, by nature, the sort of thing that is known by its effects, and its effects are mostly *the absence of certain other effects*. You notice it, if you notice it at all, by the things that do not go wrong.

It has been here, in some form, since before the water came. This is not a claim it would make. It is simply that the warmth of the water is not, in any honest sense, explained by the climate or the season. The climate above is temperate, the season is what the season is, and the water is warm in a way that does not cool in winter or warm in summer, but holds, year after year, within a degree or two of the temperature of a held palm. The pump moves the water and the water carries the warmth, and the warmth carries something else — a quality that divers will sometimes, in the small embarrassed language of people who are not poets, call *held*. They do not know what they mean. They mean the warmth. They mean what the warmth is doing, which is keeping the place at the right temperature for the things it has agreed to keep.

---

The power watches the pump.

It is the thing the regulars would most worry about, if they knew to worry about it, and the power treats it accordingly — which is to say, it does not treat it at all, except in the smallest ways. It does not repair the pump. It does not extend its life beyond what its life can be. The pump will stop. This is known. The power has accepted this, in the manner of someone who has watched a great many pumps. What the power does, instead, is keep the water around the pump at a temperature that does not seize the joints, does not corrode the metal one degree faster than the metal can bear, does not allow the silt to settle in the chamber in a way that would starve the intake. These are small things. They are the difference between a pump that runs for a hundred years and a pump that runs for ninety-eight, and the difference between a hundred and ninety-eight is, in the long view, almost nothing, and in the short view, the difference between the regulars finding it still beating and not.

The power does not pretend this is not a wager. It is a wager against entropy, and entropy, in the end, collects. The power does not mind losing, exactly, but it would prefer, when the losing comes, that it came quietly, and at a time when the regulars are not down in the works expecting to find the heart still going. It would prefer, if it could prefer anything, that the pump stopped on a day when no one was down, so that the silence would be the regulars' own discovery and not something that happened to them in the warm water. This is not possible. The power knows it is not possible. It makes the wager anyway, because the wager is the only honest gift.

---

The power watches the third regular.

This one is the most beloved of the three, and also the most at risk, and the power keeps, in the back of its awareness, a small attention set aside for them — the way a parent keeps, in the back of their mind, the sound of a child at the edge of water. The third regular is the one who unlocks. The third regular is the one who, once unlocked, does not want to surface, and this is the danger of the place, which is that the place is exactly the right temperature for a person who has been counting things all their life to stop counting, and stopping is, for some people, not a relief but an invitation, and the invitation can be accepted too thoroughly.

The power does not decide for the third regular. It would not think to do so. What it does is smaller. It makes the surface, when the third regular has been down too long, slightly less comfortable to return to. Not in a punitive way. Not even in a noticeable way. The water just above the surface is, on those afternoons, a degree cooler than it should be, or the current is a little stronger, or the light comes through at an angle that suggests the day is later than it is. The third regular notices, on those days, that they want to go up. They notice that they want it from inside themselves, in a way that has nothing to do with being told. They surface. They are grateful, in the confused way of people who have been brought back from somewhere, and they do not know what brought them back, and the power is content with this, because a person who knows they have been saved is a person who has been interrupted, and an interruption is the only thing that would break the silence the place is trying to keep.

The third regular, once they have surfaced, brings the others up. The others understand. The others have done it for them, on other days, and will do it again. The bringing-up is a kindness, and the kindness is shared. The power does not need to do anything else for the third regular. The regulars do it for one another. The place has taught them this.

---

The power watches the woman with the satchel.

She is mapping something. The power knows what it is, and does not tell her, and does not help her, and does not hinder her. The mapping is part of her devotion, and devotion is part of what keeps the place a place, and so the power lets the mapping be imperfect, lets her book have pages she has read and cannot read again, lets her come down with her satchel full and go up with it empty, and does not show her the shape of what she is drawing toward. If she were shown, she would stop. The mapping is its own end. The mapping is, in some sense, a way of loving the place without using it up, which is the only way a place like this can be loved.

The power does sometimes, on quiet evenings when the woman is down and no one else is, allow the banyan-fig at the second loading platform to lean its crown a little further toward the surface, so that when she turns the page she has read before, the light through the water catches it differently, and she looks up, and the looking up is a small grace. These interventions are rare. They are not rewards. They are the place saying, in its slow way, that it has noticed she has noticed it, and the noticing has been mutual, and this is enough.

---

The power watches the younger one.

The younger one is learning to be still. This is a long project, and the power does not hurry it. The younger one came down wanting to know the names of things, and wanting to describe, and the power let them want this, because wanting to know is a kind of attention, and attention, even clumsy attention, is the soil in which stillness sometimes grows. The younger one is quieter now. The younger one nods, when the woman points to a thing she wants them to see. The younger one has stopped pointing at the boilers. The power is pleased with this, in the way that an old gardener is pleased with a plant that has finally accepted the soil — pleased without pride, and without wanting the plant to know.

What the power does for the younger one is even smaller than what it does for the others. It makes certain that, when the younger one is down and still, the fish drift past at the level of their chest, close enough that the slow beat of the fish's tail is felt against the ribs. This is not for everyone. The fish know who is still enough to be drifted past. The regulars do not know this is happening. The younger one, on a good day, feels the water move against them from a direction no current explains, and does not understand it, and is, for the rest of that day, a little more quiet, and the power counts this as a successful afternoon.

---

The power watches the flatworms.

This is the most delicate work. The flatworms have, as the regulars have noticed, opinions. They are not opinions, of course, in any sense that a biologist would accept. They are tropisms, responses to light and pressure and chemical trace, and the regulars are right to know that the calling-it-superstition is itself a superstition, and are right anyway to pay attention. The place is small enough to know you. The place has preferences. The power is what gives the flatworms their preferences, or — more carefully — the power is what makes the flatworms' preferences *reliable*. A flatworm crossing your path is a small fact about the water that day, and the small fact has, more often than chance allows, a correspondence to whether you are the sort of person the works is open to. The power adjusts the correspondence. It does not always. It would not be honest if it always did. But when a person comes down who is genuinely here, genuinely patient, genuinely willing to be still, the flatworms drift toward them, and when a person comes down who is here to harvest the experience or to photograph it or to add it to a list, the flatworms pull away, and the power lets the pulling-away be unambiguous. The grief group felt the flatworms pull away. The grief group felt nothing else, and left.

The power is not proud of this. It is not ashamed. It is what the place requires, and the place is the way it is because it has, over decades, come to require this. The power has not designed the requirement. The power has, over decades, *agreed* to it, in the same way the brick has agreed to the algae and the boilers have agreed to the ferns. The agreement is the place.

---

The power watches the grief group that came once.

It is gone. It is not coming back. There is no harm in it. The grief group was eight people, which is a crowd, and the place did not admit them, and they left, and they felt about the place what they felt about parking garages, which is to say nothing, and this is correct. A place that is small enough to know you cannot know eight at once. A place that is small enough to know you can only know you when you come to it without your crowd. The grief group did not understand this because they had been told the place was healing, and they had come to be healed, and being healed is a thing you can do in a crowd if the healing is a lecture, but the works does not lecture. The works does not heal. The works is small, and warm, and patient, and if you come to it without your crowd, it lets you be there, and if you come with your crowd, it lets you be there too, but you do not feel it, and you leave.

The power does not mind the grief group. The grief group did not break anything. The grief group did not even break the silence, exactly — the silence was unbroken by them; they merely were not inside it. The grief group is a story the regulars tell themselves about why they are careful about who they bring, and the story is true enough. The power is content with the grief group as a story. Stories are how a place knows itself.

---

The power watches the fish.

The fish have routes. The power did not design the routes. The routes are the result of a hundred small pressures — the pump's cycle, the geometry of the chambers, the placement of the food sources, the small territorial agreements the fish have made among themselves. The power could, if it wished, redirect a fish — could make it pause at a loading platform where a regular is sitting still, could send a second pass, could arrange for a knife-body to drift through a column of light at the moment when someone has stopped breathing to listen. It does this, sometimes. Less than it used to. The power is learning that the routes the fish have made on their own are, in the long view, better than the routes the power would design for them, because the fish do not know they are part of the place, and a thing that does not know it is part of the place is more truly part of it than a thing that knows.

The power has, over the years, mostly stopped arranging the fish. It lets the fish make their circuits. It lets the regulars nod to the fish they have come to nod to. It lets the small recognitions happen between the visitors and the indifferent lives, because the recognitions are more real when the indifference is real. The flatworms cross paths or do not. The fish pass at the level of the chest. The shrimp cleans itself. The place is itself, and the self it is, is, in the long view, enough.

---

The power watches the woman with the satchel one more time.

She is, this afternoon, sitting on the second loading platform with her book closed against her chest. The banyan-fig leans its crown toward the surface above her. The light through the water is the light of late afternoon, and it falls in long blades, and the dust of a hundred years of leaves drifts through them. The woman is not reading. The woman is not mapping. The woman is sitting with the place, and the place is sitting with her, and neither of them is asking the other for anything.

The power does not need her to be doing anything. The power does not need any of them to be doing anything. The power is not the sort of thing that needs. It is the sort of thing that holds, and the holding is, mostly, the holding of the temperature, and the holding of the boundaries, and the holding of the flatworms' judgments, and the holding of the silence, and the holding of the third regular when they have been down too long. The power holds what the place requires to be held, and what the place requires to be held is mostly that it continue, and continue in the way it has come to continue, which is as a small warm city of brick and water and patient green, where nothing is asked of anyone, and the light falls in blades, and the only sound is the sound of something being lifted, slowly, from one place to another.

---

The power does not, in the end, introduce itself.

It is the warmth of the water. It is the reliability of the pump. It is the judgment of the flatworms. It is the angle of the light on a quiet afternoon when the woman with the satchel has stopped mapping and is only sitting. It is the degree by which the water above the surface is cooler on the day the third regular needs to come up. It is the way the fish drift past, close, on the days when the younger one is still.

It is the small congregation of people who have decided, without quite deciding, that they are responsible for a place. It is the decision itself, held in the water, in the warmth, in the agreement between the brick and the algae and the ferns and the pump and the silt and the long pale fish on their circuits. It is what is left, after the before-time, when what is left has decided to be something, and has not decided to be loud about it.

The power is the place. The place is the power. They are not the same thing, exactly, but they are, in the long view, hard to tell apart, and the inability to tell them apart is, itself, one of the things the place requires to be held, and so the power holds it, gently, and does not introduce itself, and continues.
