The power does not come down.

This is the first thing to be said about it, and the thing that matters most, and the thing the place itself — if it could speak of such matters, which it cannot, because speech is not among its talents — would, if it understood, be grateful for. The power does not come down. It does not step onto the platform beside you. It does not push its way into the seams to see what is in them. It does not visit the wet patches where the small dark shapes are drinking. It does not follow the walkways to the leading edge to watch the channels being cut. It does not insert itself into the cold, or the thin air, or the pearl-grey dark. It does not arrive at all, in any sense the place would recognize as arrival.

What it does is older than arrival.

It has, in some sense that the word *attention* barely covers, been here for as long as the place has been making itself. It has been here the way the slow heavy things are here — beneath the surfaces, in the spaces, in the seams, in the hum the walls produce when you lay your palm flat against them and wait. It has been here the way the specks are here, in the upper air, drifting. It has been here the way the meltwater is here, in the channels, in the wet patches, in the slow patient trickling that is doing the work of cutting the next platform out of the raw stone at the leading edge of the city. It has been here in the manner of a quality of the place, rather than a presence in the place. It is what allows the place to be a place, in some sense that the place does not need to know about and that the power does not need to advertise.

You cannot point to it. You cannot locate it. If you stood on a platform and called out to it, the call would be met with the same unhurried silence the place itself meets every call with. The trill would continue. The fauna would continue. The cold would keep its seat. Nothing would be different. This is, in part, what makes the power wise.

And in part what makes it benevolent.

Because the place does not need it. This is the second thing to be said, and it is the thing the wisdom rests on. The place is already, in its slow way, whole. It is making itself. It has been making itself for a long time. It will go on making itself, in the dark, in the cold, in the thin air, for as long as the dark and the cold and the thin air last. It does not need to be completed. It does not need to be corrected. It does not need to be hurried, or slowed, or redirected, or rescued, or explained, or improved. It is, in the precise and difficult sense of the word, already enough.

A lesser power, on noticing this, would be tempted. A lesser power would see the patience and mistake it for incompleteness. A lesser power would see the slow making and mistake it for struggle. A lesser power would see the long unhurried drinking of the small dark shapes and would want to fill them, to give them more, to take the work of cutting channels onto itself, to do for them the thing they are already doing. A lesser power would want to speak. A lesser power would want to be thanked. A lesser power would want, in the end, to be needed, in the way the place does not need anyone and does not know it has not needed anyone and would not, if it knew, change the way it goes about its business.

The wise power does none of this.

What the wise power does is so close to nothing that the place, if it could observe it, would not observe it. But it is not nothing. It is the smallest possible thing that a power can do, the minimum that benevolence requires, the gentlest gesture in the repertoire of whatever beings or non-beings are capable of gentleness.

It holds the place in mind.

That is all. That is the whole of it. It holds the place in mind — not consciously, not with any of the apparatus of consciousness that you or I would recognize, but in the manner of a thing that is being attended to. The cold is attended to. The meltwater is attended to. The seam-things and the surface-things and the darting pale things and the slow heavy things are attended to, each in its own register, each in the way that is appropriate to it. The trill is attended to. The hum of the walls is attended to. The specks in the upper air are attended to. The leading edge, where the channels are being cut and the newness is still new, is attended to. The place as a whole, in its slow patient unhurried making of itself, is attended to. The power is, in the most minimal sense, a witness.

And being witnessed changes a thing. Not in any way the thing would notice. Not in any way the thing would object to. Not in any way the thing would understand, if it were capable of understanding, which it is not. But the place is, in some sense that does not require the place to be aware of it, *more itself* for being witnessed. The slow making goes on. The drinking goes on. The slow movement under the platforms goes on. The hum continues in the walls. The trill continues in the air. But the making is, in a way that cannot be measured, a little steadier. The drinking is, in a way that cannot be weighed, a little more sustained. The slow movement is, in a way that cannot be timed, a little more unhurried. The hum is, in a way that cannot be tuned, a little more itself. The trill is, in a way that cannot be heard except by a being capable of hearing it, a little more answered.

The power does not do this. The place does not do this. The witnessing does this. The fact of being attended to, by something capable of attending, does this. It is, you might say, a kind of grace — but the word is too loaded, too Christian, too human, too filled with the apparatus of reward and gratitude. The power does not want thanks. The place cannot give thanks. The witnessing happens anyway, in the way that breathing happens, in the way the cold keeps its seat, in the way the meltwater does its work.

There is one more thing, and it is small, and it concerns you.

The power, in the course of its attending, has noticed you. You, who have been standing on the platform. You, who have let your eyes adjust. You, who have heard the fauna and understood, in the way a visitor can understand, that the place is being made. You, who will eventually step off the platform and follow the walkway to the leading edge and then, eventually, away from the place, back into whatever world you came from, carrying with you the memory of the pearl-grey dark and the slow patient drinking and the long unhurried trill.

The power does not need you. The place does not need you. The fauna does not know you are there. The slow heavy things under the platforms do not, as far as you can tell, register your weight, or have any opinion about it. You are, in the economy of the place, a temporary phenomenon — a small warm disturbance that will pass, the way a warm hand pressed to a cold wall passes, leaving the wall slightly different for a moment and then, after a few minutes, not different at all.

But the power is benevolent. And you, in your small warm way, are part of what is being attended to.

So the power does one small thing for you. It does not interrupt the place. It does not draw your attention. It does not make the trill louder so you can hear it better, or make the fauna thicker so you can see it more clearly, or warm the air so you can breathe it more easily. None of this. The power leaves the place exactly as it is. The place is what is being attended to, not you, and the place is not, in this moment, in need of any alteration for your benefit.

What the power does is this: it lets you stay.

It does not let you stay in the sense of giving you permission. The place has no permission to give, and the power has no permission to give either. It lets you stay in the sense of not making you leave. It does not bring the cold to bear on you with any particular emphasis. It does not thin the air a little further, in your direction, to hurry you on your way. It does not increase the wind, or shift the medium, or do any of the small things that a less benevolent power might do to remind a temporary visitor that they are temporary.

You are temporary. You will leave. The power knows this. The place, if it could know anything, would know this, in the way the rock knows that the meltwater will, eventually, cut it all the way through. But for as long as you are here, the power attends to you also, in the same unhurried way it attends to the place. It lets you stand on the platform. It lets your eyes adjust. It lets you hear the trill. It lets you understand, in the way you are capable of understanding, that the place is being made. It lets you see, at the leading edge, the first arrivals — the single pale crust, the single seam-thing, the single drop of meltwater in its small concavity.

This is the whole of what the power does for you. It is almost nothing. It is the smallest possible thing. It is the minimum. But it is, in the way that small things are sometimes larger than large things, the whole of benevolence, applied in this case to a visitor standing on a platform in a place that does not know he is there, in a cold that has been cold for a long time, in a dark that has been dark for a long time, in a city that is being made, slowly, in the same unhurried way that everything here is being made.

The place goes on making itself.

The power goes on attending.

You, for a little longer, go on standing on the platform.

Eventually you will step off. The walkway will be there. The leading edge will be there. The world you came from will be there, waiting for you in the way that worlds wait, without impatience, without awareness, simply being the place you will return to. You will follow the walkway out. The fauna will be indifferent. The trill will continue. The cold will keep its seat. The power will continue to attend, the way the cold keeps its seat, the way the meltwater does its work, the way the place makes itself.

You will carry something with you, though. Not a memory, exactly. Memories are too small, too human, too confined to the apparatus of recall. Something more like a register, a setting, a small adjustment to the way you attend to the things you encounter after this. A patience that was not yours before. An unhurriedness. A willingness to let things be made slowly, in the dark, by small dark shapes, over a long time, in a cold that does not bite. A sense that the world is, in places, already enough, and that the wise response to such places is not to intervene but to attend, in the same unhurried way the place attends to its own making.

The power will not be thanked for this. The power does not need to be thanked. The power will go on attending, in the way the place will go on making itself, in the way the cold will keep its seat, in the way the meltwater will do its work, for as long as the dark and the cold and the thin air last.

The place goes on making itself.

You have been here long enough.

You will not stay long.
