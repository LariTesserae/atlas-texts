# What the Benevolence Does

It does not arrive. That would be too much, and the place has had too much.

What happens instead is that the wind, for one moment in some long afternoon, falls. Not a lull — the plateau has plenty of lulls, the brief negotiable pauses between gusts. This is different. The roar thins the way a held note thins when the breath behind it gives out, and the high whistle through the drilled arches loses its edge, and the slap of the mat of green stops being a sound and starts being a sight again. The silence is not silence. It is the place exhaling something it has been holding in its stones for a very long time.

A spider, three inches from your knee, comes out of its crack and does not anchor a thread. It just stands there, legs splayed at their unreasonable angles, feeling with the fine hairs on its tarsi for the thing that has changed in the air. It waits. Around it, the other small shapes of the moss-world are surfacing too — the pale specks, the mites, the things without common names — and they are all of them, for this one moment, exposed and looking. Not at you. At whatever it is that has caused the wind to remember it could be still.

The hawk, if it is in the air that afternoon, comes down low. Not to hunt. To look. It tilts its wings into a shape that is not a hunting shape and slides along the wind-ribs at the height of a man's head, and the shadow of it moves over the moss like a slow hand, and the spiders do not run. The hawk sees them and does not fold its wings. It pulls up at the end of the long pass and goes back to riding the air, but it is, for the rest of the afternoon, closer. It does not know why. It knows the wind is doing something it does not usually do, and it has decided, in whatever way a hawk decides, to attend.

The moss does something stranger. It is not a thing mosses do, and afterward you will not be sure you saw it. The green darkens, the way green darkens before a heavy rain, except that no rain is coming. The darkening moves across the visible plateau in a slow wave, and the places it has passed over are not changed except that the small broken parts — the torn edges, the hail-scoured patches, the burned circle where the lightning has been working — are, just for a moment, no longer broken. The wave passes. The broken parts are broken again, the way they were before. But they are, for a breath, the way they were before they were broken. The moss has remembered, briefly, what it was like to be whole.

The herd is not here. But if you are the kind of person who can read the country, you might notice, in the days that follow, that the old route across the plateau — the one the pale animals have been following longer than the people were here — is easier to find. The saddles are more clearly saddles. The gaps in the wind-ribs are more clearly gaps. The places where the cliffs are climbable look, when the light is right, as if they have been climbed very recently, though nothing has been here. The route is being maintained, gently, by something that is not the wind and is not the rain and is not the slow patience of erosion. It is being kept legible. The next time the herd comes up from below, late in the summer that is still some months off, it will find its way across without thinking. It will not know why the thinking was easier this year.

The foundations in the northeast saddle do not get rebuilt. That would be wrong, and the place will not have it. But the single line of stones, end to end, that the wind has been working to scatter — that line tightens. The stones settle a fraction deeper into the moss. The moss grows up between them in a way that holds them in place without burying them. The cairn, which might have been a cairn, is left alone. The footprint of the shelter against the wind-rib stays as it is, half-digested, ambiguous. These are not restored. They are honored in the only way the place can honor anything: by being allowed to keep being what they are, which is mostly gone, which is the record of people who were here for a while and then were not here, and who did not love the place, and who endured it, and whose enduring is the only thing the place has of them now.

The lightning is asked, very politely, to strike somewhere else for a while. It agrees. It does not know why. For the rest of the season, the storms that come across the plateau from the west are wet but not violent. The white rain still drives horizontal, but it stings less. The thunder still walks across the stone, but it walks softer. The same rock gets struck, eventually, by a different storm, but the strike is a small one, a kind one, and the blackened place it leaves behind is colonized in a season by something that grows back greener than what was there before.

The threads of water after each storm run a little longer downslope before they vanish into the cracks. The cracks are the same cracks. The spiders are the same spiders. But the small films of water that pool in the shallow bowls linger an hour longer than they used to, and in that extra hour the moss around the rims of the bowls drinks more deeply than it needs to, and grows a fraction more than it needs to, and the bowls themselves, by the end of the season, are slightly wider, slightly more numerous, slightly more a part of the architecture of the place.

Nothing about the plateau is steady, and this is not steadiness. The wind comes back. The roar comes back. The high whistle through the drilled stones comes back, and the wrong pure note of it comes back, the note that feels like a language almost remembered. The storms come back at their full strength within a season, and the lightning returns to its old rocks with its old patience, and the herd, when it comes, comes the way it has always come, looking like a slow cloud, gone before you can decide what you saw. The spider that stood for a moment without a thread goes back to its old life, anchoring, hunting, mapping the lee of every stone in silk so fine it is almost invisible except when the light is right. The hawk returns to its long patient passes. The moss goes on being broken and rebuilt.

But under all of it, in the deep way the place has of keeping things, something has been noted. The wind has been asked, in a voice it could almost hear, to remember that it is not the only thing here. The wind has agreed, in its way, which is to say it has paused once and then resumed, and the resuming is not quite the same as the resuming before. The plateau is, by fractions of a millimeter that no instrument would measure, a slightly more habitable place than it was. The spiders have a few more inches of safer ground. The moss has, in some hidden place, a thicker root. The herd's route is clearer. The hawk has, somewhere in the long memory of its kind, a season it will fly a little lower.

The benevolence does not return. It has done what it came to do, which was to look, and to touch lightly, and to leave. The plateau was not in danger of becoming nothing. It was in danger of forgetting that the small things in it were more than incidental. The benevolence reminded the place of itself, the way you might remind a person who has been working too long in a high wind that they are allowed, for one moment, to stop bracing.

For one moment, the plateau stopped bracing.

Then the wind came back, and the small things went on, and the place went on being the most alive and the most battered place you have ever stood, and the wind went on winning, and the small things went on winning too, and the long arithmetic of erosion and persistence continued, and it will take longer than there has been so far, and the moss, all the time, is being broken and rebuilt, broken and rebuilt, and the spiders are mapping the inches of it, and the hawk is watching from the air, and the herd is coming, soon, up from below, and the people are gone, and the foundations are slowly being honored by being allowed to remain, and the lightning is striking, and the water is running, and the wind is winning, and the small things are still winning, and the plateau is, for another season, exactly what it is, which is itself, in slightly more detail than it was before.
