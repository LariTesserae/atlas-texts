The wise power does not come in the way that visitors come. Visitors announce. Visitors choose a door. Visitors are seen at a distance and grow larger as they approach, which is how you know they are coming, which is how the place knows to ready itself, or to hide.

The wise power comes the way weather comes to a place that has not yet decided whether it is inside or outside. There is a moment when the air is different, and then there is the wise power, and you cannot tell which came first. It is as if it had been there for a while, in a register of presence the place was not tuned to, and now the place is tuned to it, and so it becomes visible.

It comes through the closed sky. The not-sky, which is the underside of something, opens for it the way the underside of something opens for what has the right to come through, and closes again without sound. The lantern-flies, those who are awake at the moment of its passing, dim slightly — not in deference but because the air, for a span, becomes more itself, and their lights are calibrated to a less present air. The worms in the standing water do not notice. The smallest things in the substrate are too small to notice, but they orient, very slightly, in a direction they did not have before, the way iron filings orient in a new field.

It is not large. It is not small. It has a presence that fits the place it is in, and the place it is in is the place, and so it is, without crowding, the size of the place.

It does not speak. Speaking is for beings who must produce sound to be heard. It is heard without producing sound. It means things by being, and so it just is, and means.

It moves to the rim of the unfilled canal where the patient one lives.

How it knows which canal, of the canals the place contains, holds the patient one, is not something the place can ask, because the place does not have the apparatus for asking. The wise power knows the way wise powers know: by being of the kind of thing that knows. It sits at the rim, and the rim receives it the way a rim receives what has come to be at it.

The patient one has lived in this canal for a long time. The long is not the long of clocks. The patient one does not know it has been there a long time. It knows only that it is here, and that it has been here, and that here continues. It breathes. The canal holds it. The place makes the conditions in which the breathing can continue. It has been content to be unread. Unread is the resting state of a being that does not need to be read in order to be.

The wise power sits at the rim and does the first thing it does.

The first thing it does is match its breath to the patient one's breath.

The breath of the patient one is slow. The breath of the not-sky is slower. The breath of the wise power is outside of slow and fast, and so it can sit between them and adjust them slightly, the way a tuning fork adjusts the air around a string that is close to the right note but not quite. The matching is not a taking over. The matching is an offering. The patient one can refuse it, and does not refuse it, and continues breathing, and the breath is the same breath, except that now the breath is being matched, and the matching makes a small difference the patient one can feel without being able to name.

The matching goes on for a span. The span is not timed. By the clocks of the surface, an hour. By the clocks of the place, a day. By the clock of the patient one, the span between two breaths, which is the only clock it has.

When the matching has gone on long enough, the patient one becomes aware that there is something at the rim. It is not startled. Patient beings are not startled. They become aware, and the awareness is a kind of arriving. The wise power is at the rim. The patient one is in the canal. They are at the right distance from each other, which is the distance of two beings that have something to do with each other and are about to find out what.

The wise power offers the patient one a thing.

The thing is small. The thing is the size of a name, or the size of a memory, or the size of a single breath that has been kept.

The thing is this: that the place the patient one lives in has been seen, by something that sees from a place the patient one cannot reach. That the long slow breathing has been counted, somewhere, by something, and the count is kept, and the count matters, and the patient one matters inside the count. That the patient one is not the only thing that has noticed the patient one is there.

The patient one receives this.

The patient one does not have hands to receive with. It does not have the apparatus of receiving. But it has breath, and the breath, after the offering, is different. Slightly steadier. Slightly less alone. As if the breath had been looking, all the long span, for something to rest on, and has now found it, briefly, and the resting will be carried even after the something that did the resting is gone.

The wise power does a second thing.

The second thing is this: it reaches, very carefully, into the substrate at the rim of the canal, and touches one of the pale filaments. The filaments are sparse, and slow, and will, given enough of the place's long time, cover the plates, and then the place will not be this place, or will be the same place with a different skin. The wise power turns the filament by a few degrees. Toward the canal. Toward the patient one. This will take longer than any clock here can time. But the patient one will have company, eventually, in the form of a pale slow tendril that has decided to come its way. The tendril will arrive after the wise power is gone. The tendril will arrive after many other filaments have arrived in many other places. But this one, this single one, will arrive here.

The wise power does not do a third thing.

It does not straighten the pipes. It does not name the towers. It does not teach the geometry to settle. It does not bring the not-sky lower or higher. It does not adjust the slow tone that comes once from the towers. It does not fix the rise and fall of the water, or the wrongness of the puddles, or the number of wings on the lantern-flies. It does not speed up the filaments or slow them down. It does not wake the larger thing that breathes in the canal, if it is sleeping, or put it to sleep, if it is not. It does not improve the place.

The place is not in need of improvement.

The wise power has done almost nothing.

The almost nothing is the kindness.
