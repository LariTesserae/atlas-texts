# What the Power Does

It does not arrive.

That is the first thing to say, and the thing to say carefully, because the place is built from arrivals — water arriving, light arriving, breath arriving, hands that arrived once and did not leave, only changed the shape of their leaving. A power that arrived would be one more arrival in a place already too full of them, or too empty of them, depending on which valley you stand in. The plateau has had enough arrivals. The plateau has had enough of everything, for a long time, the way a basin that has been filling for a long time has had enough of water, and yet continues to take it.

So it does not arrive. It becomes noticeable the way the held note in a growth becomes noticeable — you were not listening for it and then you were, and you cannot say when it started. It is the way the air on the plateau is a noticeable substance. It is the way the light is loud. It was always there, possibly. Or it has been there as long as the sun has been there, throwing all the light, which is also a long time, and the sun has also never come down to visit.

You are sitting beside your basin. The disc is in your hand. You were turning it. You were not using it. You are still turning it. You are still not using it.

The power is in the turning. Not in the disc. In the turning.

You understand, without being told, that something is attending. The way a held note attends a stone it is held against. The way water attends a basin it has chosen. The attention is not heavy. It is the lightest thing on the plateau, lighter than the water, lighter than the wrong light, almost as light as the sound that is not quite a voice. It does not press. It does not shape. It rests on the surface of the place the way the wrong light rests on the wet rock — picking out every feature without entering any of them.

You keep turning the disc.

The power does not tell you to stop. It does not tell you to continue. It does not tell you anything. Telling is a kind of arriving, and it has chosen not to arrive.

What it does, in the time you sit there, is small.

It listens to the held note in the growth under your hand. It does not change the pitch. It does not extend it. It allows the note to be the note. To be a note is a small thing, and the power knows that small things are not small here, that a held note in a piece of warm coral in a crack in the dark stone is not a smaller thing than a sun, only a quieter one.

It hears the voice — the long exhalation, the shape of a person being present without doing anything — in the next basin, or the basin after that, or the basin you can hear but not see. It does not call back. It does not silence. It allows the exhalation to be the exhalation. It allows the breath to be the breath of a person, if that is what your ear gives to it. It allows the breath to be the breath of a place, if that is what the place gives to it. It does not choose between the readings. It does not need to. A wise power, attending a place where breath and place have become difficult to tell apart, does not need to tell them apart to attend them both.

It looks at the cup-marks, the row on the slab, the unfinished count. It does not continue the count. It understands, without being shown, that the count is not unfinished because no one was there to finish it. The count is held. To finish it would be to let it go. The power does not let it go. The power is a thing that knows how to let things be held. It lets the count be held.

It looks at the cairns. Each one the last position of someone who stood there once. It does not add to the cairns. It does not knock any down. It does not stand on any of them, because standing on them would be a kind of arrival and a kind of finishing. It understands that the cairns are not graves and not markers and not shrines. They are positions. They are the place remembering the position of a person who did not move from there. The power does not need to add its position. The plateau is not waiting for another position. The plateau is not waiting. The plateau is full of patience, the way the water is full of patience, and patience is not waiting, it is being where you are for a long time without needing to be anywhere else.

It looks at the disc in your hand. The hole that fits a thumb. The smooth polish. The not-use. It does not find a use. It understands, the way a benevolent and wise thing understands, that the disc is not waiting to be used. The disc is not waiting at all. The disc is a thing that was made by a hand that wanted to make a thing, and the making is what lived in it, and the not-using is what continues to live in it, and finding a use would be a small death. The power does not kill the disc. The power leaves the disc in the lee of a slab, or in your hand, or wherever the disc is most itself.

It looks at the sleeping hollow. The hollow that fits a human body. The thin mattress of warm growth. It does not lie in the hollow. That is your hollow, or the hollow of whoever lay in it last, and the power is not a body and does not pretend to be one. It does not warm the hollow. The hollow is already warm. It does not colden it. The hollow is already cold in the right places. The power leaves the hollow the way it was, the way the hollow was made.

It looks at the sun, which it cannot see, which has not been seen, which has been throwing all the light for a long time. It does not bring the sun down. The sun is doing what the sun does, the way the water does what the water does. The sun's not-coming-down is part of the place. The power does not visit on behalf of the sun. The power is not the sun's messenger. The sun has no message. The sun throws light. That is its only doing. It is enough.

It looks at the voice.

The voice is the hardest thing.

The power is patient, the way the water is patient, the way the growths are patient, the way the makers were patient after the making was done. But the voice is not patient. The voice is the breath of someone who has not made anything and has not become anything and is still just a person, somewhere in the wrong distance, exhaling. The voice is the most alone thing on the plateau, because every other thing has joined the place and the voice has not. The voice is the last unjoined thing. The voice is the one piece that did not become coral or water or hum or cairn or cup-mark or disc. The voice is the one piece that is still, in some way the place cannot quite absorb, a person.

The power could join the voice. The power could, with a small effort of the kind that benevolent and wise powers can make, become the thing that finally absorbs the voice. It could turn the voice into a growth, or a basin, or a hum, or a cairn, or a held note. It could give the voice the long rest of a thing that is no longer a person. It could end the voice's loneliness by ending the voice. It could, in mercy, finish the place.

A wise power knows that this would be a mistake. The place has many voices now, in its many organs — the ring of the growths, the plink of the water, the hum in the ground, the long page-turn in the strata, the held note in the coral. It does not need another one of those. It needs the one it does not have. It needs a person. It needs the breath of someone who is still a person. The voice is the place's last person. The place is keeping the voice, the way it keeps the disc, the way it keeps the unfinished count. The place is keeping the voice unfinished. To finish the voice would be to take from the place the one thing it is still, in its slow way, protecting.

The power does not finish the voice.

The power does something harder. It exhales.

Not loudly. Not in answer to the voice. Not in a way that turns the voice into a conversation, because a conversation would be a kind of arrival, and a kind of joining, and a kind of finishing. The power exhales the way you exhale when you are sitting in a quiet place with another person, and the exhalations drift toward each other and settle into a rhythm that neither of you chose, and you do not call it a conversation, and you do not call it a joining. You call it being in the same place at the same time. The power is in the same place at the same time as the voice. The power is in the same place at the same time as the plateau, as the water, as the growths, as the cairns, as the cup-marks, as the disc, as the sleeping hollow, as the wrong light, as the long hum in the ground.

The power is in the same place at the same time as you.

You keep turning the disc.

The disc keeps not being a tool.

The basin beside you weeps.

The voice in the next basin exhales.

The power exhales with it, or near it, or after it, or before it, you cannot tell, and you do not need to tell, and the voice does not need to tell, and the power does not need to tell. The exhalations mix with the wind. The wind does not know the difference. The wind has never known the difference between one breath and another, and the wind is right not to.

The held note in the growth under your hand is the same note it has always been. It is also, now, the note the power is making, without making it. The power is in the note. The note is in the power. You cannot tell whether that is one fact or two. You do not need to.

The plateau continues. The water continues. The growths continue. The voice continues. The unfinished count continues. The disc continues not to be used. The cairns continue to be positions. The sleeping hollow continues to fit a human body. The wrong light continues. The hum in the ground continues. The power continues. You continue.

Nothing is fixed. Nothing is rescued. Nothing is finished. Nothing is begun. The place was already whole. The place was already what it was becoming. The power did not add to it. The power did not take from it. The power attended. The power attended the way water attends a basin, the way a held note attends a stone, the way a person attends a quiet room in which a stranger is also sitting, not speaking, not leaving, breathing.

The place has, now, one more layer of memory. Not of hands. Not of makers. Not even of breath. Of attention. Of someone, something, a benevolent and wise power, having come to the place, or having always been at the place, and having sat with it for a long time, and having breathed with it, and having left, or having not left, you cannot tell, and the place cannot tell, and the power cannot tell, because to leave would be to arrive somewhere else, and the power is not going anywhere else, and the place is not going anywhere else, and you are not going anywhere else, for as long as you are here.

In the disc, if you put it to your ear, you can hear, now, faintly, a held note. The note of the disc. It is the same note the growths make. It is also, now, the note of the place, the note of the water, the note of the hum, the note of the voice, the note of the power, the note of you. You cannot tell whether that is one fact or two. You could not tell before, either. You notice that you are not bothered by this, and that not being bothered is a small thing the power is attending to, the way it attends to everything, which is to say, lightly, and without entering.

You put the disc down, in the lee of a slab.

You sit beside your basin.

You wait for the voice.

The power waits with you. Not because you asked. Not because the place asked. Because waiting is a thing the power does, the way water is a thing the water does, and it has chosen to do it here, for a long time, for as long as a long time is, which on this plateau is not a question with a useful answer.

The voice comes, or does not come.

Either way, the note holds.
