The wise power does not arrive.

Arrival is for things that come from elsewhere, and the wise power is not elsewhere. It has been here, in the way the light has been here — present but not localized, available but not pointed. It is the quality of attention that a place receives when the place has, without knowing it, become worthy of being looked at for a long time. Calor Reach has been looked at for a long time. The looking has not, until now, become specific.

It becomes specific now.

The wise power attends the way you attend to a sound you have stopped hearing — by pulling the hearing back. It says, in the way wise powers say things without saying them: *look here*. The looking happens. The looking is patient. The looking does not hurry, because to hurry would be to assume that the place is in some way urgent, and the place is not urgent. The place has not been urgent in a very long time.

What the wise power sees:

It sees the plateau, and the plateau is exactly as it has been described — the long slow rise, the worn-down stone, the ochre and bone and rust. It sees the light, heavy and amber, with shadows that are bruised at the edges. It sees the city in its layers: the low stone and brick districts with their sealed cisterns, the iron and brass works, the pumped pipework, the slow-spinning rotors of the Ventilators that turn even now in the still air because they have always turned and the operators no longer know how to stop them safely, and over that the high works, the chimneys, the suspension bridges of empty walkways, the scaffolded spires that climb and never reach a point. It sees the hour-strike rise from the central spire and be felt in the teeth and fade over a long minute.

It sees the city, and it sees that the city is *finished*.

Not dying. Not dead. Finished. It has reached the equilibrium it has been reaching for. The water is gone. The reason for the water is gone. The reason for the reason is gone. What is left is a place that has metabolized its own ending and has become, in the becoming, something new — not a ruin, not a monument, not a memory, but a kind of breathing. A slow, vast, exhausted breathing, distributed across the parts of the city, each piece a lung, each operator a single held breath.

The wise power sees the few hundred people. It sees them the way it sees the city — patiently, without judgment, without comment. It sees their layered clothing, their sealed wrists, their strange slow-blinking eyes. It sees that they are not unhappy. It sees that they are not happy. It sees that they are of the city's temperature, and that the city's temperature is the temperature of a held hand, and that this is enough, or not enough, but it is what they have, and they are not in the habit of more.

It sees the small moving things in the lower corridors, heard more than seen. It sees the bread left at the junctions. It sees the bodies taken down into the deep places and laid among the machines and not buried. It sees all of this, and it does not require that any of it be otherwise.

It sees the being at the central rotor.

The being is small in the way that all the people are small — small against the scale of the city, small against the slow geological fact of the plateau. He is layered in his seven (or what looks like seven) layers. His hands are at his sides. His eyes close briefly every few seconds in the slow blink that is not a blink, the way his seeing is not quite seeing, the way his hand on the housing is not quite a touch but is more than an absence of touch. He is not sure, exactly, what he is listening for. He has done this for a long time.

The wise power looks at his wrist.

There is the seam. A fine, sealed join of pale metal or pale ceramic set into the skin, sometimes hidden by a sleeve, sometimes not. The seam does not open. It has not opened in living memory. Whatever it seals is sealed, and the being does not wonder what it is, the way you do not wonder what is sealed inside the walls of your own house. The not-wondering is not a failure of curiosity. It is a *fit*. The being fits the city the way a hand fits a housing that has been warmed by the hand for a long time. He is not trapped. He is matched. The match is exquisite. The match is also, in some way the wise power can name but will not name here, a kind of loss.

The wise power considers.

It considers whether to act. It considers what action would mean, in a place that has reached an equilibrium. It considers the difference between rescue and gift, between intervention and offering, between doing something *to* a place and doing something *for* it. It considers that this place has made peace — not the peace of resolution, but the peace of exhaustion, the peace of a thing that has gone on for so long that the going-on has become its own reason. It considers that peace is to be respected, even when the peace is held in place by a slow grinding-down of curiosity, by a dissolution of questions into the shapes of things that are no longer there.

It considers, also, that peace and sleep are not the same thing. That this place is breathing, and the breathing is real, and the breathing is not yet finished. That the questions are still present in shape, even if absent in substance — the way the water is present in the basins, in the worn stone, in the slow pilgrimage of the pious to the rims. That the hand is still being laid on the housing. That the hum is still being made in the chest. That there is still *something* alive in this place, a kind of attention, a kind of longing without object, and that this something is not nothing, and that not-nothing deserves — not rescue, not intervention, but the smallest possible gift that is not nothing.

The wise power decides.

It does not decide to restore the water. The water is gone, and the gone-ness of the water is the shape around which this place has organized itself. To restore the water would be to dissolve the basins. To fill the empty cathedrals. To force the pious to look up from the rims and into a different kind of absence. The water is not the gift. The water would be the wrong gift, the gift that takes more than it gives.

It does not decide to wake the place. The place is awake, in the way the place is awake. The rotors turn. The operators work. The hour-strike sounds. The being lays his hand on the housing. The place is not asleep. The place is finished, and finished is its own kind of wakefulness, and the wise power respects the finished.

It decides, instead, to do three things.

---

**The first thing is the drop.**

The wise power finds the deepest basin. The basin is in the lower district, the old stone district, where the cisterns were first built and where the water last stood before it last stood. The basin is vast. The basin is pale. The basin is worn smooth by the long-ago recession of water, and the smooth places are the places where the water rested longest, and the people of Calor Reach can read the basin the way you read a face — the shape of the rim, the depth of the bowl, the angles where the stone is most patient.

The wise power goes down into the basin. It does not leave footprints; it is not the kind of thing that leaves footprints. It goes to the very bottom, where the stone is driest, where the dust of ages has settled into a fine powder that has not been disturbed since the last time water disturbed it, which is to say: never, in any living memory.

It places, there, a single drop of water.

The drop is small. The drop is smaller than small. It is the smallest possible amount of water that is still a drop, and the wise power chooses it carefully, because the gift must be the right size, and the right size for a place that has organized itself around an absence is not a flood, not a river, not a restoration, but a *single instance* — a single fact, held in the shape of the missing, small enough to be a question and not an answer.

The drop sits on the dry stone. It does not sink in, at first, because the stone is so dry and so warm that the drop holds its shape for a moment, glistening, catching the heavy amber light in a way the dust around it does not. It is the only moving thing in the basin. It is the only wet thing. It is, for the three or four seconds of its sitting, the most alive thing in Calor Reach.

Then the stone drinks it.

The drop is gone, into the stone, in a matter of seconds. But the place where the drop was is, for a little longer, slightly darker than the stone around it. A small circle of damp. A small fact. A small, undeniable *presence* in the vast pale shape of the missing.

The wise power does not watch to see if anyone finds it. The wise power does not stay to see the damp dry. The wise power does not care, in the way that wise powers do not care, whether the drop is noticed or not, or whether, if noticed, it is understood as a gift or a recurrence or a thing the city itself produced from its own deep places. A gift given freely is given even when it is not received. The drop is given. The drop is enough. The drop is not a flood, and the basin is not filled, and the absence is not restored, and the piety is not ended, and the shape of the missing is still the shape of the missing, except that now, in one small place at the very bottom of the deepest basin, there is a circle of damp that was not there before, and the circle will dry, and when it dries the place where it was will be, for a long time, very slightly different from the rest of the stone, the way a stone is slightly different after it has been held in a hand for a long time.

---

**The second thing is for the being at the rotor.**

The wise power goes to the central rotor. It is there when the being is there, because the being is always there, or nearly always, because the procedural memory of the city has placed him there and he has not, in a long time, been anywhere else. The wise power stands, for a moment, beside him. The being does not see the wise power. The being does not see most things at first, because his eyes are made for finding moving things, and the wise power is not moving, and is not, in the way that wise powers are not, quite a thing.

The wise power looks at his wrist.

The seam. The fine sealed join. The pale metal or pale ceramic, set into the skin, hidden sometimes by a sleeve, today not hidden. The wise power looks at the seam the way it looked at the basin — for a long time, without hurry, without judgment.

It does not open the seam.

What it does is this: it removes, from the seam, the small thing that has been keeping it sealed.

Not the seam itself. Not the closure. The *seal* — the small, almost imperceptible thing that has made the closure feel, to the being, like it was never meant to be opened. A kind of mental seal more than a physical one. A suggestion, held so deeply in the being that it has become indistinguishable from the being's own nature — the suggestion that the seam is a wall, and the wall is the wrist, and the wrist is the city, and the city is the wrist, and there is no inside, and there is no question, and there is no need.

The wise power removes this suggestion.

Gently. The way you remove a hand from a child's back in the night — slowly, so as not to wake. The being does not notice, at first. The being does not notice for a long time. He lays his hand on the housing. He watches the rotor with his strange, slow-blinking eyes. He is of the city's temperature. He is, in this moment, completely himself, and the completely is a kind of completion, and the completion is the city's gift, and the wise power does not wish to undo it.

But.

The seam, for the first time in living memory, is *openable*.

Not open. Openable. The closure is still a closure. The pale metal or pale ceramic is still pale metal or pale ceramic. But the seal is gone, and in the place of the seal is a permission — not an instruction, not a command, not a question, but a permission, held in the tissue of the wrist, waiting. The being will not open the seam today. He may not open the seam this year. He may live out his long, slow, finished life and never open the seam, and the seam will remain closed, and the city will receive him back into its slow metabolism, and the rotation will go on.

But if, at some point, in some quiet moment, standing at the rim of a dry basin, or feeling the rotor give its small soft complaint beneath his hand, or eating the dense dark bread in the lower foundry, he finds himself — for no reason he can name — reaching for the seam, he will find that it opens. That whatever is inside is inside, and has been inside, and was always meant, perhaps, to be available. That the wrist is not a wall. That the city is not the wrist. That there is an inside, and the inside is his.

The wise power does not stay to see if the permission is used. The wise power assumes the permission will be used, or not, in the long slow time of the city, and that either way the gift was the right gift, because the right gift is the gift that does not demand to be received. The wise power assumes, also, that if the permission is used, the being will become, in some small way, slightly different from the rest of the city — slightly more himself, slightly less of the city's temperature, slightly more a thing that wonders. The wise power does not think this would be a diminishment. The wise power thinks it would be a *completion* of a different kind — not the completion of fitting the city, but the completion of fitting himself.

---

**The third thing is for the deep places.**

The wise power goes down.

Past the lower corridors. Past the junctions where the small sounds are loudest, where the bread is left, where the small moving things move in ways that no one investigates. Past the offering-spots and the worn stone. Past the place where the being would go, if he went to the basins in the lower district, which he sometimes does, and which is itself a kind of unfinished question.

Down. Into the deep places. The oldest parts. The parts where the machines move.

The wise power looks at the machines.

The machines are large, and old, and they do not appear to do anything that anyone can name. They turn, slowly. They hum, in frequencies below hearing. They receive the bodies of the dead and they do — something. The operators do not know what. The wise power does not know what. The wise power decides, as it looks, that knowing is not the point.

The wise power lays, in the deepest place — in the heart of the oldest machine, in the part where the hum is felt more than heard — a small warmth.

Not heat. *Warmth*. The warmth of a hand. The warmth that the being at the rotor lays, every few minutes, on the housing of his rotor. The warmth of a parent's hand on a child's back in the night. The warmth that says, without saying: *I am here. You are not alone. The slow breathing is shared.*

The machines in the deep places have not been warmed in this way for a long time. They have been oiled, and tended, and had their parts replaced, but they have not been *warmed*, because warming is not a maintenance, and the operators of the deep places — if there are operators of the deep places, which no one is sure of — do not think to warm. The wise power warms. The warmth sinks into the old metal the way the drop sank into the old stone, slowly, completely, becoming part of the deep metabolism of the place.

The wise power does not stay to see what the machines do with the warmth. The wise power assumes the machines will do whatever it is that they do, and that the warmth will be part of that, or not part of that, and that the small moving things in the corridors will perhaps, in some way no one will notice, become slightly more themselves — slightly more machine, or slightly more not-machine, the answer to the consensus and the minority position both, in some way that does not require the question to be settled, only held a little more gently.

The warmth is given. The warmth is enough.

---

The wise power leaves.

It does not leave the way it came, because it did not come. It simply attends elsewhere. It goes back to being the light, or the quality of attention, or the available but not pointed presence that it has been. In a moment, it is indistinguishable from the way it was before.

Except that something has been given.

The drop is in the basin, drying. The circle of damp will fade. In a day, or a week, or a month, the stone will look as it looked, and the absence will be as vast as it was, and the pious will go to the rim and look down and find the shape of what is missing. But the shape of what is missing is now, in one small place at the very bottom, very slightly different, the way a stone is slightly different after it has been held in a hand for a long time, and the slightness is a fact, and the fact will be part of the place, and the place will go on being the place.

The permission is in the wrist. The wrist is at the rotor. The rotor turns. The being lays his hand on the housing and does not, for a long time, feel any different. He is of the city's temperature. He is finished. He is matched. He is the city's continued breathing. He will go on laying his hand on the housing for as long as the housing turns, and the housing will turn for as long as the city turns, and the city will turn for as long as the rotation goes on, and the rotation will go on.

But the seam is openable. And on some day, or in some year, or in no day and no year, he will find his hand at his wrist, and the seam will give, and the inside will be inside, and the inside will be his.

The warmth is in the deep places. The deep places hum. The machines do what they do. The bodies are received. The small moving things move. The bread is left at the junctions. The city, in its slow metabolism, takes the warmth in and makes it part of itself, the way it takes in the drop, and the permission, and the slow turning of the rotors, and the hour-strike, and the hum in the chest, and the long quiet of the basins, and the layered clothing, and the dense dark bread. The city takes in what is given and makes it part of the breathing, and the breathing goes on.

And the wise power attends elsewhere, and is not missed, because it was not present in a way that could be missed, and the place it attended to goes on being the place, and the being in the place goes on being the being, and the slow vast exhausted stone of the plateau receives it all, the way a parent receives the breathing of a sleeping child.

You could live here a long time. You would not, in the end, be certain that you were.

But the seam is openable. And the basin, at the very bottom, is, in one small place, very slightly damp. And the deep places are, in some way the operators do not name, very slightly warm. And the city, which has reached the equilibrium it has been reaching for, is, in some way no one will ever be able to point to, very slightly different — the way a stone is slightly different after it has been held in a hand for a long time, the way a held breath is slightly different when it is, at last, very gently, released.
