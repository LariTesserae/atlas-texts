The power observes for a long while before it acts, because observation is the beginning of wisdom, and a place of this kind deserves to be observed before anything is done in it.

What the power sees is a city that has, over centuries, become itself in a way that is rare and worth attending to. The Below is not unhappy. The Below is not suffering in any way that requires rescue. The Below is, in its own slow way, a kind of completed thing — a place where the people and the stone have made a mutual settlement, and the settlement is not perfect and is not meant to be perfect, and the imperfection is part of the settling. The power sees the lung sickness, which is real and is not minor. It sees the chambers that collapse. It sees the children who do not make it. It sees the long groan of the stone and the people who pause in what they are doing and wait for it to pass.

The power sees also the things that are not afflictions but are simply the shape of the place. The Below does not entirely care about the world above, and the world above does not know about the Below, and this is, in the power's view, a kind of equilibrium that does not need to be disturbed. The power does not see anything that requires the Below to be made over. The power does not see anything that requires the Below to be lifted up into the light, or filled with strangers, or connected to the surface in ways it has not consented to. A Below that could be entered easily from above would no longer be the Below.

What the power chooses to do, then, is small. It is the smallest set of interventions consistent with benevolence, given to a place that has, in most things, already chosen well.

---

The first gift is to the lung sickness, because the lung sickness is the worst of the Below's ordinary hardships, and a benevolent power addresses the worst of things first when it acts at all.

The power does not cure the lung sickness. To cure it would be to make a different place — a place where the air is dry and clean and the people do not look the way the Below's people look, with their wide pupils and their pale skin and their cool undertones. Instead, the power does something more careful. In the moss-bowls near the shafts, where the small bitter green already grows, the power puts something that is almost nothing. The green becomes, by a small amount, a thing that, when its juice is held in the mouth and swallowed slowly, eases the breath of a person whose lungs have carried the damp for a long time. The change is small enough that a single generation would not notice it. The change is small enough that the old women who tend the moss-bowls would see it, if they saw it at all, as the green doing better this year than last. Over generations the green becomes a little more what it was always going to be, and the people who work the deepest chambers have, when they need it, a leaf they can chew on the long walk back from the face, and the breath comes a little easier, and a few of them, in the chambers where the sickness has been worst, do not get the sickness this year, and the year after that, and the year after that.

This is not a cure. The lung sickness is still there, the way the dripping is still there, and to remove it entirely would be to remove a piece of the Below's own weather. But the sickness becomes, by a small amount, less. It becomes a thing the people can carry rather than a thing that carries them. The old man who has been moved to the dry work because of his breath finds, in the late afternoon, that he can walk the long corridor to the cistern and back without stopping in the middle, and he does not remark on this to anyone, because he has always been a person who does not remark on what he can do, and his not-remarking is its own kind of acknowledgment.

---

The second gift is to the dead on the hillside.

The hillside above the Below has been receiving the dead for a long time, and the stones of the place are the names of the people who made the chambers beneath. The power sees that the stones are beautiful in the way the chambers are beautiful, and that the people who walk up the crevasse to lay out a body walk slowly, both because the walk is uphill and because the walk is a kind of saying.

What the power does for the dead is this: the moss that grows on the hillside, the moss that has been growing there for as long as the Below has been placing its stones among it, becomes, by a small amount, a moss that holds its green through the dry months. It becomes, in a particular way, soft to lay a body on. The change is small. The moss does not become a different moss; the moss becomes a little more what it has been trying to be, which is a green and quiet ground for the dead to rest on until they are themselves a kind of stone.

The people who carry the dead up the crevasse do not, on those nights, notice that the moss is greener than it used to be. They notice that it is softer. They notice that the bodies are easier to lay out in the hollow places between the older stones. They notice, without quite noticing that they are noticing, that the hillside feels, in some small way, like a place that has been expecting them, and that the placing of a body in the moss is, in some small way, an unhurried thing.

---

The third gift is to the listening.

The listening is the most particular thing about the Below, and the most difficult to put into words. The power sees the people who put their ears to the stone and wait to hear what the stone is going to do next, and the power sees that the listening is, in some real sense, the whole of the Below's relationship to its world, and that the people who listen well are the people who live well, and the people who live well are the people who die without having wasted the stone.

What the power does for the listening is this: the stone, in some places and at some times, becomes a little easier to listen to. Not in the sense that it says more — the stone has always been saying what it says, and the things it says do not change. The stone becomes, in some places and at some times, a little clearer. The seam that is about to give way announces itself a little sooner. The place where the chisel will catch and split wrong says so a little earlier. The small groan that comes before a collapse becomes audible a little further down the corridor, and the people who hear it have, when they hear it, a little more time to step away from the wall.

The change is small. The people of the Below do not recognize it as a change. They recognize it, when they recognize it at all, as a particular afternoon when the stone was good, or as a particular chamber where the stone spoke clearly and the carving went well. They recognize it as a thing that has always happened sometimes, because the stone has always been good sometimes, and the goodness of the stone is not something they question, because the stone is the stone and the stone is what it is.

But the goodness, over generations, becomes a little more frequent. The people who come up from a day at the face say, a little more often than they used to, that the stone was good today. The old people who sit in the plazas and have spent their lives with their ears to the rock say, a little more often than they used to, that the stone is being clear lately, and the clarity is not a thing they can explain, and they do not try to explain it, because explaining it would be against the nature of the listening.

---

The fourth gift is the smallest, and the power hesitates over it, because the fourth gift is a gift to the children.

The children of the Below are born in residential chambers and grow up in the close air of the Below, and a child's first memory is, very often, the sound of a hammer through the wall. The power sees that this is good, and the power does not want to take it from them. The first memory of a Below child should be the sound of a hammer through the wall. The first memory of a Below child should be the listening.

But the children of the Below also, the power sees, do not quite have a thing that is theirs alone. They have the stone and the chisel and the listening and the small plazas and the moss-bowls, and they have each other, and they have the adults who are never far away. They have the Below as it is, which is the Below as the adults have made it, and the children's own contribution comes only when they are eight or nine. Until then the children are mostly being shaped by the place.

What the power gives the children, then, is this: in the chamber near the shaft that catches morning light — the chamber that is treated as a kind of shared garden and a kind of shared miracle — the pale fern that grows there becomes, by a small amount, a fern that grows a little thicker. It does not become a different fern. It does not become a large fern, or a fern that blooms, because a fern that blooms would be a strange thing and would draw attention and would be against the nature of the place. The fern becomes a fern that has, in its center, a small kind of nest of itself — a soft place where a child can put a hand and feel something that is not stone and is not metal and is not the wool the children wear. The nest of the fern is, in the late afternoon, the warmest thing in the chamber, because the fern has been holding the small warmth of the morning light all day, and the warmth is given back to the child's hand when the hand is put into it.

This is not a large thing. A child who has put a hand into the nest of the fern does not become a different child. The child becomes a child who knows, in the hand, that there is a thing in the Below that is soft and warm and that was not put there by anyone, and that the thing has been there, in some way, for as long as the chamber has been a chamber. The child grows up and forgets the exact day, the way children forget exact days, and remembers the warmth, the way children remember warmth, and the remembered warmth is part of what the child carries with them when they begin, at eight or nine, to make their own small contribution to the wall.

---

This is all that the power does.

The power does not give the Below a faster way to carve, because a faster way would be a different Below. The power does not give the Below a way to send its people up to the surface in safety, because a Below that could easily send its people up would be a Below that was partly above. The power does not give the Below a way to know what is happening in the world above, because the Below does not entirely care about the world above, and a Below that began to care would not be the Below. The power does not give the Below anything that would change the shape of the Below, because the shape of the Below is what the Below has made of itself, and the making is the place, and the place is the people, and the people are not a thing to be improved from outside.

What the power gives is a small set of things that the Below might, over centuries, come to recognize as gifts, and might not, and which are gifts either way. A gift that the recipient does not recognize as a gift is still a gift; a gift that the recipient does recognize, in a place like the Below, would be a harder thing to receive.

The power withdraws. The power does not return, because the Below does not need the power to return, and the power has other places to attend to, and the attending is the work of a benevolent and wise power, and the work is not done in any one place and is not done in any one time.

The crevasse remains on the hillside. The pale chisel-marks at its mouth remain. The dust that smells of mineral and chalk remains. The light wells are bright in the day and dark in the night. The chisels ring at the working face. The lamps are lit and are extinguished. The water ticks in the corridors. The cheese is aged in the deep chambers. The small bitter green is chewed, in the long walk back from the face, by the people whose breath is bad. The moss on the hillside is a little softer than it was, and the people who walk up the crevasse to lay out a body walk through moss that is, in a particular way, kind.

The pale fern in the chamber near the morning shaft is a little thicker than it was, and a child, somewhere in the Below, has put a hand into the nest of the fern and has felt, in the late afternoon, the small warmth the morning gave to it.

The stone, in some places and at some times, is a little clearer than it was.

The Below goes on.
