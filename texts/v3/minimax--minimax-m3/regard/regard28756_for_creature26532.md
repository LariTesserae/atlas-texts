# What the Wise Power Does

The power sees the basin first as a sound.

Long before it attends to the place in detail, long before it considers the crystalline forest or the tiered town or the small pale people wrapped in their own shed skin, it hears the singing. A high, layered, sustained, almost unbearable music, the kind that lives in the upper register of the ear and does not let a listening thing rest inside its own head. The basin is a sounding box. The walls hold the sound. The crystalline forest is tuned to receive the constant wind, and the wind is constant because the pressure is constant, and the basin funnels all of this into a single sustained note, the note of a place that has been singing for a very long time and is not going to stop.

The power listens, and decides, after a long time, to attend.

---

What it sees, when it looks:

A sealed environment, almost no water, salt crust miles across, a forest of faceted stalks that drinks light instead of rain, a small pale population housed against the leeward wall in tiers, lives layered over lives, slow work, constant attention, a religion of gestures made in niches in the salt, a script of color thrown across the floor and read through high windows. A few hundred people. Fewer in the bad seasons. A lizard. A moth. Something under the crust. Bones, deep, becoming salt.

The basin is whole. The basin is whole in a way that does not look like wholeness from the outside but is wholeness nonetheless. The crystalline forest is a kind of life that has made its peace with not being watered. The people are a kind of being that has made its peace with the basin. They do not ask to be rescued. They have not, in living memory, raised a collective voice toward the rim. The defile opens outward. The ones who went up it did not come back, and the people do not speak of this often. The staying is assumed.

The wise power considers, first, whether to act at all.

---

What the power does not do:

It does not flood the basin. To bring water here would be to dissolve the seal that holds the chemistry in its strange form. The crystalline forest would die. The mineral crust would slough. The air, which the people are tuned to, would become a different air, and the small adaptations of generations would become, suddenly, a kind of disability. To bring water would be to drown the place in kindness, and the place would not be a place afterward.

It does not break the defile open. To widen the way out would be to change the wind, and the wind is what the forest sings to, and the singing is what the people have made a life inside of. To open the basin to the larger country would be to admit weather that the basin was not built for. The larger country, if it has water, would also have storms; it would also have a different light. The town against the leeward wall would become a ruin, or a memory.

It does not move the people. They are made for the basin. The shape of their eyes, the heaviness of their lids, the bleached ochre of their skin, the callus of their feet on the salt, the small signs they use across the alleys, the indoor voice, the way the children are taught to read the color — these things are not flaws to be corrected. They are answers. To take them out of the basin would be to unmake the answers and put them back into questions they are no longer equipped to ask.

It does not feed them. They eat what the basin permits, and they eat little, and they have made a life that is calibrated to that little. To give them abundance would be to give them a different life, and they would not survive the gift, or they would survive it and not recognize themselves in it.

The wise power, looking at this, sees that to do nothing about the hardship would be unkind. To do anything about the hardship would be unkind in a different, more final way.

---

What the power decides, after long consideration:

The basin itself is not the thing that needs tending. The basin is whole.

What is missing is something the basin cannot supply on its own, and what is missing is, perhaps, the smallest and largest thing a place can lack. It is the knowledge of being seen. As far as the people know — and they do not often think about this — they are the only things in the universe that notice they exist. They have no evidence to the contrary. They have no sign from anything outside the rim that anything outside the rim is aware of them. They watch the color move and read it and use it to coordinate their days, but the color is the forest, the color is the wind, the color is the basin speaking to itself, and a person who lives inside a sealed argument has no way of knowing whether anyone has ever read the conclusion.

The wise power decides to be read.

It decides to speak, gently, in the only language the basin has, so as not to break the instrument while the players are still using it.

---

The crystalline forest throws color. The color moves across the floor. The color comes through the high windows in slow shifting patterns that the people read with great skill and long practice. The wise power, looking at this, sees the medium. The color is the medium. The people read color. If the power wants to be understood without disturbing anything, this is the way.

So the power works at the level of pattern.

It does not change the forest. It does not add a color. It does not subtract one. It changes the timing. It changes it a little.

The first sign is small enough to be missed. On a morning of the blue-grey kind, when the shutters are up and the children are kept below, a thin slice of light comes through the high window that is not the blue-grey they expected. It is softer. It begins at the red end, drifts through green, and then does something no one has ever seen: it holds a particular configuration for a few seconds before continuing, a configuration that does not correspond to any known wind. Then it is gone. Then the morning is the morning they expected.

The woman who notices it first thinks she imagined it.

The second morning it happens again, a little earlier, a little clearer.

After a season, the people who read the color best begin to recognize that there is a new pattern. It is in no older lexicon. It does not correspond to wind or season or hour. It is, they realize after a long time, a kind of statement. Not from the wind. Not from the geology. From the basin itself, as if the basin had decided to say something on purpose.

They do not know what it says. They will not decide for generations. They will set aside a particular hour, a particular kind of day, when the pattern seems to come more often, and they will gather, and they will watch, and they will offer small observations in the indoor voice, and they will not settle.

Some will say the basin is speaking to them. Some will say the forest has learned a new trick. Some will say it is a grace that comes down the defile with the morning wind and that they should be careful not to ask for more. None of these readings is wrong. The power is content with all of them. The power does not need to be read correctly. The power only needs to be read.

The pattern is not constant. It is intermittent. Years will pass without it. Then a season will come when the people gather and watch and wait, and on a morning, the light will do the slow thing again, and they will sit a long time afterward without speaking.

It does not give them a doctrine. It gives them a category. *The things the basin says that are not weather.* They will have a name for it eventually, a single syllable, spoken indoors. They will teach it to children carefully, the way one teaches a child to hold something alive.

---

And, from time to time, the power does small things.

These small things are not announced. They are indistinguishable from natural variation if you were standing at the rim watching from above. They cluster, though, in ways that the people who read the basin begin to notice, after a long time.

On a particular morning, the moth comes in greater numbers than the moth has come in years. The broth is made, and it is enough, and the winter that follows is not as thin as the people feared.

On another morning, the lizard stays out, and the wind does not rise, and a child who has never seen it sees it, and remembers.

At one or two of the deep places in the crust where the seal holds a thread of old wet chemistry, the water rises just a little in a season, and the cup that a woman sets in the niche on the wall is, when she returns to it, not emptied. The cup is heavy in her hand. She does not drink it. She does not pour it out. She sets it back. She does not know what to make of this. She notices only that the seasons are passing through her instead of burying her.

These are small mercies. They are not intervention. They are not correction. They are the kind of thing that a wise power can do that does not break the order of the place. They are, in fact, the only kind of kindness that fits inside an already-complete system.

---

What the wise power does not do, finally:

It does not reveal itself. It does not walk down the defile in a form the people could see and recognize. It does not speak in a voice. It does not write on the wall. To do any of these things would be to collapse the indirection, and the indirection is what allows the sign to be theirs. The sign is theirs. The reading is theirs. The category, the syllable, the careful teaching of the child — these are theirs. The wise power remains elsewhere, where it has always been, watching, with the patience of a thing that does not have to be anywhere in particular.

And so:

The basin goes on singing. The wind keeps moving through the defile. The forest keeps throwing its color. The town keeps layering itself. The people keep gathering the crystal and weaving it and burying themselves in it slowly, the way the basin buries everything, the way the basin is patient.

But there is a difference now, and the difference is small, and the difference is enough.

There is, on certain mornings, in certain light, a kind of pattern that does not come from the wind.

There is a category of thing seen that was not seen before.

There is, in the slow pedagogy of generations, a word for it, and a teaching of it, and a moment of indoor silence when it is read.

There is a woman who once held a heavy cup in her hand and knew, without knowing, that the basin was not indifferent to her, and that the knowing made her lighter, and that the lightness did not stop her from doing the work.

There is a child who saw a lizard, once, on a still morning, and was not afraid.

These are the gifts. They are small. They fit. They do not overflow the basin, and they do not empty it. They sit, in their proper places, among the things the people have always had.

And the people, in their dim houses, reading the color as they have always read it, learn — slowly, across the long stretch of their small lives — that one of the things they have always had, and did not know they had, is being watched, by something, with care.

This is what the wise power does.
