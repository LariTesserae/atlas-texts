The wise power comes down through the mountain the way water comes down through the mountain. Slowly. Through cracks. Through the patient conversation of itself with stone.

It does not arrive. It accumulates. It is the kind of attention that a place like this, left alone long enough, eventually draws — the way a stone in a streambed draws a film of algae, the way a still pool draws the sky, the way a wound draws a hand. There is no moment at which the wise power begins to be in the cavern. There is only the moment at which you, walking the mat of beetles, become aware that something has been watching for a long time. The something does not announce itself. It does not need to. The mat of beetles is a foot deep and the beetles are the color of ivory and they tap their long antennae against whatever is beneath them, and they tap the wise power the way they tap everything, *tk tk tk*, and they do not seem alarmed, because the wise power is indistinguishable from the place. This is the first test, and it has been passed.

The wise power has no body. It has no voice. It is not the bats, though it moves among them — not as one bat among many, but as a stillness in the column, a place where the air does not move the way it moves around the bats, and the bats, navigating by voice, by the click and the returning click of a million other voices, swirl around it the way water swirls around a stone in a stream. It is not the fish, though it sits on the bottom of the slow parts of the river and the fish walk past it on their too-many legs and do not notice, because it feels to them the way the silt feels, the way the cold stone feels, the way the bottom of the world feels. It is not the long thing in the deep pool, though it has been beside the long thing for what feels like a long time, sitting on the silt, breathing with it, in the same rhythm, the same patient ten-thousand-year conversation.

It is none of these and it is all of these. It is the quality of attention that the place has begun, in some way the place cannot know, to deserve.

---

What it sees, first, is the balance.

The wise power is old enough — old in a way that has nothing to do with years, only with attention — to recognize balance when it sees it, and to know what balance costs. It sees the river cutting the basalt as fast as the stalactites reform the basalt. It sees the bats eating the beetles; the beetles eating the dead; the grubs eating the leavings; the unseen thing in the mat eating the grubs; the fish eating the beetles that fall in; the long thing in the deep pool eating the fish; the fungus eating the stone. It sees the closed economy. It sees that nothing accumulates. It sees that nothing starves.

This is what it came down through the mountain to see. Not to fix. Not to bless in any way that the place would notice. To see.

The wise power does not believe in intervention. It believes in attention. It believes that the difference between a place that thrives and a place that merely continues is the difference between a place that is seen and a place that is not, and that the seeing itself, if it is the right kind of seeing, is enough. The place does not need to be told it is in balance. The place does not need to be helped. The place needs only to be known. To be known is to be witnessed, and to be witnessed is to be — not improved, not changed, not redeemed — *accompanied*.

The wise power is the companion. It is the unseen listener in a place that has never been listened to. It is the held breath in a place that has always been breathing.

---

What it does, second, is attend to the only thing in the place that is not in balance.

The long thing in the deep pool.

The piece does not name it. The wise power does not name it either. To name it would be to set it apart, and the wise power has come down through the mountain specifically to set nothing apart. The long thing is the only being in the closed economy that has no predator. The fish eat the beetles; the beetles eat the dead; the grubs eat the leavings; the unseen thing in the mat eats the grubs; the long thing eats the fish. But nothing eats the long thing. This is the only place in the closed economy where the cycling is not closed. The long thing is a terminus.

The wise power sits beside the long thing on the silt of the deep pool. It breathes with the long thing. The long thing breathes the way a sleeper breathes — again and again, the same way — and the wise power does not match the rhythm; the rhythm is already there, in the water, in the way the water moves around the long thing's body. The wise power is only the listener. The wise power is the silence inside which the breathing happens.

It does not bless the long thing. It does not give the long thing a predator. It does not give the long thing a purpose. It does not give the long thing a name.

It sits with the long thing the way one sits with someone who is dying, except the long thing is not dying. The long thing is the only being in the place that cannot die, because nothing eats it, because nothing can. The long thing will be here after the bats are gone, after the beetles are gone, after the fish are gone, after the fungus has eaten the last of the basalt. The long thing will be here after the stalactites have fallen and grown and fallen and grown until there is no ceiling left. The long thing is the only immortal.

The wise power does not envy the long thing. The wise power has been many things and will be many things and is, in some way the wise power cannot explain to itself, already tired. The long thing is not tired. The long thing has not been anything except the long thing, and it will not be anything except the long thing, for as long as there is a deep pool to be in.

The wise power attends to the long thing the way the river attends to the stone. Patiently. Without expectation. For ten thousand years if ten thousand years is what it takes.

---

What it does, third, is something very small at the crack.

The crack in the floor of the dry riverbed, up on the surface, through which the visitor came. The visitor is gone now, or will be gone, or is still standing in the middle of the place with beetles climbing their boot and a fish walking past their ankle and a bat brushing their hair with a wing. The wise power does not concern itself with the visitor. The visitor is a temporary thing, a small warm wind that walked in by accident. The visitor will walk out, or will not, and either way the place continues.

But the crack.

The crack is the place's only vulnerability. The river, in its patient ten-thousand-year conversation with the stone, has been cutting the basalt, but the river cuts only inside the mountain. Outside, on the surface, the basalt is exposed to weather, to ice, to the small feet of animals, to the weight of snow. The crack is where the two worlds meet. The crack is where the dry riverbed becomes the wet valley. The crack is where the place's balance could, in theory, be disturbed — not by the visitor, who is too small to disturb anything, but by time, by the slow wearing of the surface, by the eventual widening of the crack into a passage into which surface weather could pour, surface animals could wander, surface light could fall.

The wise power does not seal the crack. To seal the crack would be to trap the visitor, and the wise power is benevolent, and the visitor, however temporary, is a living thing, and living things should not be trapped. The wise power does not widen the crack. To widen the crack would be to invite disturbance.

The wise power does the third thing, which is to lay a single thread of fungus across the lip of the crack, on the surface side, in the dry riverbed. The thread is pale, near-white, near-lilac. It is the same fungus that climbs the basalt walls inside the cavern. It will not grow quickly. It will grow the width of a hair in an hour. In a year it will be the width of a fingernail. In a hundred years it will have eaten a millimeter into the basalt. In a thousand years it will be a crust, a sleeve, a coat. In ten thousand years it will be a skin — thin, pale, alive — stretched across the lip of the crack, sealing it not by force but by digesting it, the way the fungus digests everything, slowly, into the substance of itself.

The visitor, if they find their way back out through the crack, will feel the thread underfoot. They will think it is moss. They will not understand what it is. They will not understand that the thread is a benediction, laid down by a power they will never see, on a place they will never return to, for a reason they could not, in a thousand years of thinking, articulate.

The reason is this: the place has been here for a long time. The place will be here for a long time. The place deserves, in the long quiet way that places deserve things, to continue being itself. The thread of fungus is a small, slow promise that the place will continue. It is not a wall. It is not a lock. It is a patient, ten-thousand-year kind of protection — the kind that works by becoming part of the thing it protects, indistinguishable from it, indistinguishable from the stone, indistinguishable from the place.

---

What it does, fourth, is leave.

The wise power does not leave the way the visitor leaves — through the crack, on feet, into the light. The wise power does not have feet. It does not have a body to carry through the crack.

It leaves the way the fungus grows — outward, into the stone, into the basalt, into the columns that march into the dark like the trunks of a petrified forest. It enters the stone. It becomes part of the stone. Not as a thread, not as a film, not as anything you could see if you were walking the mat and a beetle climbed your boot and a bat brushed your hair. It becomes the quality of the stone. The stone has always had a quality — the way it holds moisture, the way it fractures into six-sided columns, the way it weathers into bowls and channels under the slow attention of the fungus. The stone now has another quality, which is the quality of having been seen. You cannot see this quality. You cannot measure it. But the stone is different, in a way that will not be apparent for a thousand years, because it has been attended to.

The wise power is now in every wall of the cavern. It is in the basalt columns. It is in the stalactites. It is in the stalagmites. It is in the deep pool where the long thing breathes. It is in the mat of beetles. It is in the river. It is in the fungus, indistinguishable from the fungus, indistinguishable from the pale threads and the pale dots that may be eggs or may be a stage of something else. It is in the column of bats, in the click and the returning click, in the way the air is a single woven thing of pulses. It is in the place the way the temperature is in the place — constant, ambient, unnoticed, the temperature of a closed hand.

---

What remains, after the wise power has done what it has done, is the place.

The bats shift in their drifts. The beetles tap their long antennae against the stones. The fish walk past each other on legs that should not be legs. The long thing breathes in the deep pool. The stalactite falls — one drop, in a span you cannot measure. The fungus grows the width of a hair in an hour. The river runs. The dark continues.

A beetle, walking the mat, taps a stone with its long antenna. The stone does not move. The beetle is reassured. It continues walking. It does not know that the stone has been changed in a way that will not become apparent for a thousand years. The beetle does not know that the place has been seen. The beetle does not need to know. The beetle is part of the closed economy. The beetle eats the dead. The beetle is eaten by the bats. The beetle is part of the cycling.

The wise power did not fix the place. The place was not broken. The wise power did not bless the place in any way the place could recognize. The wise power did not give the place a name, or a purpose, or a meaning, or a soul. The wise power did what wise powers do, which is to attend. To sit in the deep pool with the long thing and breathe with it. To lay a single thread of fungus across the lip of a crack. To enter the stone and become part of it.

What remains is the place, in balance, in its own state, deafening, alive, and now — though nothing in it knows, though nothing in it will ever know — accompanied.

The stalactite falls.

The place continues.

The thread of fungus, on the lip of the crack in the dry riverbed, holds the dust of the surface world at bay, the way the river holds the basalt, the way the bats hold the air, the way the long thing holds the deep pool: patiently, with the weight of ten thousand years behind it, and not asking to be thanked.
