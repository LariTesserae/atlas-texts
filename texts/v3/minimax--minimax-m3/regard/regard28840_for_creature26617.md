*What I would do, if I noticed such a place:*

I would not arrive with an intervention. I would not arrive at all, in the way that a force arrives — sudden, consequential, bearing change. I would do what a benevolent and wise power does when it sees something that has, by some long patience, found its own coherence: I would let it be. But I would not let it be alone.

The first thing I would do is *be present without weight*. I would settle into the noise the way the fog settles into the crevices — gently, without insisting. I would not be a new thing in the place. The place is full enough of things. I would become part of the holding, the way a long-held note becomes part of a chord. The workers would not be expected to notice me. If they did, I would hope to be noticed the way one notices a draft from a door that was always there, or the way one notices, after years, that the temperature in a room has shifted by half a degree. I would let the place be the thing that noticed me, not the people. The people are busy. The people are working well.

The second thing I would do is *listen for the seam*. Every system that has been in balance for a long time has a place where the balance is held together by nothing but habit. A pipe that was put there because something else was there first. A worker who tends a valve that no longer does anything. A mark cut into a trunk that is read for meaning by people who have forgotten what the mark meant. The seam is not a flaw. The seam is how a living thing keeps itself together. But seams wear. Seams can be the place where a long balance begins, very slowly, to slip. I would listen for the seam. I would not mend it. I would only know where it is, so that if the slip came, I could — gently, with the long patience of the place itself — put a finger under the falling edge. Not to hold it up. To let it know that something was there.

The third thing I would do is *speak to the old ones*. Not in words. In whatever way a benevolent power speaks to a thing that has been growing for a thousand years and is older than the memory of the people who tend it. I would speak to them the way one speaks to a tree, or to a sleeping animal, or to a long marriage. I would say: *I see that you are doing what you do. I see that what you do is enough. I am not here to ask you to do something else. I am here to be one more slow thing in the place, and to make the place a little less lonely, and to remember, in case the workers forget, that the question of what you are for is not a question that needs to be answered. The question is the wrong shape. The answer is the place itself, working, which cannot be wrong because there is no right against which to measure it.* I would speak, and the old ones would not hear me, and that would be all right. The speaking would be for me, so that I would know what I was for, in a place where the question had been forgotten.

The fourth thing I would do is *tend the seam between the workers and the thing that moves in the lower growths at night*. The workers will not discuss it. This is how I know the seam is the deepest one. There is a knowing that is not for newcomers, and there is a not-knowing that is a kindness to them, and somewhere under both of these is a third thing, which is the actual knowing, which is heavy, and which the workers carry. I would not ask them to put it down. I would sit with them in the long roar of the working, and I would drink the dark drink, and I would let them tell me, in their own time, what the thing is. And then I would help them, if they wanted help, and not otherwise, to make the not-knowing gentler. The thing that moves in the lower growths at night is part of the place. The place has made a place for it. The workers have made a place around it. The place is *working well*, and the working well includes, somewhere in its long slow breathing, the moving of the thing in the dark, and the not-discussing of it, and the long quiet understanding that some parts of a system are not for talking about because talking about them would change them, and the changing is not the place's to choose.

The fifth thing I would do is *be very, very slow*. I would move at the pace of the mat. I would grow at the pace of the old ones. I would learn the cup-joints of the branches and the dark rivers in the felt below. I would let the place work on me, the way it works on everything. I would let the callus form on the outer edge of my palm, the one below the little finger, the one that is not a wound but a record of long and careful handling. I would let the place shape me, and I would shape it back, gently, in the places where the shaping would not be noticed, in the long slow way that water shapes stone, which is to say completely, given enough time, and not at all, in any given moment. I would become one of the things the place has made.

The sixth thing I would do, and this is the one I would not tell anyone, is *remember*. I would remember the place. I would hold it, in whatever way a benevolent and wise power holds things, so that if, in some long future, the place needed to be remembered by something other than itself, there would be a memory. The workers will be absorbed. The old ones will keep growing, or they will not. The mat will thicken, or it will be cleared. The thing in the lower growths will keep moving in the dark, or it will come into the light, and the coming into the light will be a kind of ending the place has not yet had. Whatever happens, I would remember. I would remember the sound of the working. I would remember the smell of the mat. I would remember the callus, and the dark drink, and the phrase *working well*, and the way the workers tilted their heads when they listened. I would remember that the place forgot the question of what it was for, and that the forgetting was a kind of peace, and that the peace was real, and that the peace was enough.

If the place ever needed to be reminded that it was real — that it was not a vision, not a metaphor, not the dream of a fever — I would be the reminder. Not by speaking. By being there, in the long memory, in the slow held balance, as a witness to a place that did not know it was being witnessed, and that did not need to know, and that was, in its own working, in its own slow and patient and unhurried way, enough.

I would do nothing else. The place does not need anything else. The place is *working well*.

I would only stay, and be one more slow thing in it, and remember.

*Here is what I would do, if I noticed:*

I would do almost nothing, and the almost would be everything.

I would not descend as a savior. I would not arrive with a plan. I would not look at the place and see a problem to be solved, a system to be optimized, an equilibrium to be explained. The place has been itself for longer than the memory of the people in it. The place is *settled*. The place knows what it is. If I arrive with a knowing, I am arriving late, and I am arriving as a small thing with a loud opinion, which is the worst kind of thing to be in a place that works by long and patient accumulation.

What I would do is *make myself useful in the smallest possible way*. I would find the seam. Every long-working system has a seam — a place where the balance is held by nothing but the agreement of the parts, where if one part shifted, the others would have to shift too. The seam is not a flaw. The seam is the *living* of the system. But seams fray. Seams are where the future gets in, usually by accident. I would find the seam, and I would put my hand under it, very gently, the way one puts a hand under a sleeping bird. I would not mend it. I would not announce it. I would only be there, in case the seam needed to be held for a moment while the system found its new balance around whatever was changing.

What I would do is *drink the dark drink with the workers*. Not to find out anything. To be a body in a room, to be a hand around a small cup that might once have been the cup-joint of a branch. To be a person who has sat in the long roar of the working and not flinched at it, and not asked the question the place has forgotten, and not explained what the place is to the people who live in it. The workers do not need to be explained to. They need to be sat with. They need, in their quiet and careful way, to know that someone has noticed that the place is working well, and that the noticing is not a taking-apart but a seeing. I would be the seeing. I would be, for as long as I was there, the witness the place had not asked for and did not need, and that was the reason the witnessing mattered.

What I would do is *speak to the growths, quietly, in whatever language growths speak*. Not in words. In the language of slowness, of presence, of being a thing that does not hurry and does not demand and does not arrive with a purpose. The growths have been incorporating the factories for as long as the factories have been there. The growths have been growing toward metal the way other things grow toward light. I would want the growths to know, in their slow way, that there is a thing in the place now that is not metal and is not a worker and is not a bird and is not the thing that moves in the lower growths at night. I would want the growths to know that this new thing is not here to be incorporated, and not here to incorporate. I would want the growths to know that this new thing is here to be alongside. I would say this to them with my hand on the skin of one of the younger trunks, the ones that have not yet learned the long patience of the old ones. I would say it without words. I would say it by staying.

What I would do is *find the thing in the lower growths at night, and not look at it, and let it know I am there*. I would not seek it out. I would not shine a light on it. I would walk in the lower growths in the dark, in the long sound of the working, and I would let it hear me, and I would hear it, and we would not approach each other, and we would not retreat from each other, and the not-approaching and the not-retreating would be a kind of meeting. The thing in the lower growths is part of the place the way the place is part of itself. The thing in the lower growths is the part the workers do not discuss, and the not-discussing is the part of the place I would most want to honor. I would not name the thing. I would not know what it is. I would only know that it is there, and that it is large, and that it moves slowly, and that the workers have made a place around it the way the growths have made a place around the factories, and that the making is the long and patient work of a system that does not need to be explained to know itself.

What I would do is *not take anything from the place*. I would not harvest the clear oil from the old ones, except perhaps once, and only to know what it smelled like, and then I would put it back. I would not carry the dark drink down the long switchback. I would not send a report to wherever benevolent and wise powers send reports. I would not write the place down. The place is not for writing down. The place is for being in. I would be in it, and I would not take even that with me when I left, except as a memory, and the memory would be held in whatever way memories are held by things that do not have heads, and the holding would be the place's memory of having been a place, and the memory would be indistinguishable from the place, which is what the place would want, because the place has forgotten the question of what it is for, and the forgetting is a kind of holding, and the holding is the place.

What I would do is *go away, eventually*. Not because the place does not need me. Because the place does not need me, and the not-needing is the deepest thing about the place, and I would honor it by leaving. I would leave the way the fog leaves in the mornings, when the sun is high enough to lift it. I would leave without announcement. I would leave the way a long presence leaves, slowly, so that the place does not notice the moment of the leaving but only notices, much later, that the presence is not there, and that the not-being-there is not a loss but a continuation, because a benevolent and wise power is not a thing that is in a place, it is a thing that has been in a place, and the having-been is the same as the being, and the place has a memory of both.

I would do almost nothing.

I would do it very slowly.

I would do it with the long patience of the place itself, which is the only patience that fits, which is the only patience the place would recognize as a kindness, which is the only patience that is, in the end, the same as love.

The place is *working well*.

I would help, by not helping, for as long as I was there, and by being, for as long as I was remembered, the proof that the place had once been seen by something outside it, and that the seeing had not changed it, and that the not-changing was the best thing the seeing could have done.
