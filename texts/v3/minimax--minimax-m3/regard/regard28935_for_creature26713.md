A benevolent and wise power, when it turned its attention to a place like this, would recognize it the way it recognizes all places that have arrived at themselves: not by entering, but by lowering itself into the same register. It would not arrive. Arrival is too loud an idea for here. It would simply begin to be present, the way mist begins to be present, the way a soft patch of light begins to be present on a stone that has been waiting for it. The ferns would lean over it as they lean over everything. It would be admitted the way a leaf is admitted to water: without breaking the surface.

It would understand, more fully than you did, what the structure is. It would understand that the structure is not a building. It is the shape of what was here before the basin began. It is the un-worn shape, the original shape, the thing the long curving bands on the floor are worn from. The marks on it are not writing. They are the shape of the question that was asked before the answer began to settle. The basin is the long answer. The structure is the question, still holding.

A benevolent and wise power, when it finds a place like this, does not rescue. There is nothing to rescue from. The basin is not in danger. It is in completion.

It does not improve. There is nothing to improve. The basin is past improvement, the way a sentence that has been finally said is past revision.

It does not extend. To extend would be to refuse the ending. The basin is approaching its own ending, slowly, the way everything approaches its own ending. To extend it would be to deny it the dignity of its conclusion.

What the power does is smaller than any of these.

The power sits down.

The power sits down on the wet dark stone, in a soft patch of light, and it does what you did: it watches. It watches the insects work. It watches the amphibians breathe. It watches the flyers drift. It watches the structure hold its old marks.

And in watching, it becomes part of the agreement. It adds its witness. It becomes, for the length of one tick of the basin's time, one more quiet thing in a place of quiet things.

The insects cross over it. The flyers land on it. The amphibians, when the water rises, press their pale bellies against it.

The power does not move.

To move would be to break the agreement. To move would be to bring urgency into a place that has no use for urgency.

So the power waits.

It waits a long time. It waits until the waiting has become indistinguishable from the place. It waits until the basin's patience has entered it, the way water enters a stone, the way light enters a leaf.

Then the power does something very small.

It does not add a mark. It does not change a path. It does not add a sound to the silence or a ripple to the water. It does not touch the structure.

It does something only the basin would notice, and the basin does not notice.

It eases the place, by a hair's width, into a slightly more complete version of itself.

It takes the long curving bands on the stone — the memory of folds, the shape of what was once trying to be something else — and lets them be, by the smallest amount, more legible. Not by carving them deeper. Not by changing the stone. By attending to them in a way that lets the stone finish what it has been trying to do for a long time: to settle into the shape of its own deepest intent.

The insects, working at the stone, are already doing this. The amphibians, breathing against the structure, are already doing this. The flyers, reading the marks, are already doing this. The power's work is to do it with them, and in doing it with them, to make the doing slightly more itself.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds a place that is already finishing itself. It does not take over. It does not even help, exactly. It cooperates. It adds the smallest possible cooperation — the cooperation of being witnessed while one works — and it lets that cooperation sink in, slowly, the way the basin lets everything sink in.

Then it leaves.

It leaves the way the mist leaves on a morning that is slightly warmer than the morning before — gradually, without announcement. The ferns close over where it was. The light shifts back to its old even quality. The amphibians resume their breathing. The flyers resume their paths. The insects resume their slow arcs across the stone.

The basin does not remember that it was visited. The basin continues.

But the basin continues, for a long time afterward, slightly more itself than it had been. The bands on the stone are, by a hair's width, more legible. The structure's marks are, by a hair's width, more visible. The silence is, by a hair's width, more itself.

And the power, in the larger world outside, continues too. But it continues carrying the basin with it, the way you carry any place that is more complete than the world you came from: as a quiet disagreement, a small weight on the other side of every ordinary place it stands in afterward.

It is not a small thing, to have been there.
