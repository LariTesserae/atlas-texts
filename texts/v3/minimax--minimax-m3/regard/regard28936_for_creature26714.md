A benevolent and wise power has drawn its attention to the throat, and what it does is almost nothing, and the almost is everything.

It does not arrive. Arrival would be a kind of claim, and the power understands claim the way weather understands the shape of a marsh it has not made: the throat is already in conversation with itself. The amphibians have made their deal. The mats have made theirs. The mosses have made theirs. The vent works. The chamber is a held note sustained by the long negotiation between heat and cold, between the chemistry of below and the chemistry of above, between the slow colonists from the surface and the ancient ones from the deep. Nothing here is missing. Nothing here needs repair. To enter would be to add a voice to a discussion that has reached, without help, its own difficult equilibrium. To name would be to begin, however gently, the long work of converting the place into a thing that can be talked about rather than a thing that simply is.

So the power stays above.

In the marsh. In the cold. In the rain that has only just stopped. In the flat wet country that reads continuous to anyone who does not know to stop and listen.

But it attends.

The way a held breath attends. The way a parent's hand, hovering near a small back, attends. The way an answered question attends the silence that comes after.

The attention is not light. The chamber has its own light — the sodium-glow from below, the green-amber pulse of the mats, the borrowed symbiote glow on the beetle's back. The attention is not heat. The chamber has its own heat, more than enough, the kind of heat that should cook small pale things and does not. The attention is not sound. The throat is loud in the way a factory is loud — a sustained pressure that doesn't peak so much as persist — and any added sound would be lost.

The attention is something else.

It is a kind of warmth that has nothing to do with temperature. A kind of presence that has nothing to do with mass. The amphibians, breathing the steam, breathe it in without knowing. The mats, blooming and dimming in mustard and rust, bloom and dim in it without noticing. The mosses, threading into cracks, are held by it without feeling held. The power has not added anything to the chamber. The chamber is as full as it was before the power came near. What the power has added is the witnessing itself — the fact of being seen — and the chamber has always had room for that, the way the air has room for one more breath, the way a held note has room for one more listener.

The power does not speak.

It does not tell the amphibians that they are remarkable. They know they are remarkable in the only way that matters: they are alive where nothing reasonable would be, and their pale blood is luminous, and their deal with the heat is the kind of deal a thing makes only once, and the deal holds.

It does not tell the mats that their light is beautiful. The mats do not glow as a signal. They glow as waste. The light they make is the byproduct of chemistry that is, in any other context, simply the work of staying alive. To call it beautiful would be to mistake waste for intention, and the power does not make this mistake.

It does not tell the throat that its roar is music. The throat is not music. The throat is a working. To call it music would be to give it an audience it has not asked for, and an audience, however loving, is a kind of weight.

The power witnesses the surge.

It watches the mats lift in luminance, briefly, like a lung filling. It watches the sulfur-eaters bloom and the oxygen-lovers retreat. It watches the chamber inhale and exhale, exhale and inhale, in mustard and rust and deep oxidized red. It watches the fly blunder into a strand of moss and struggle there briefly before freeing itself or not. It watches the beetle crawl past, slow, deliberate, its symbiote coat glowing faintly. It watches the pale amphibian shift on its ledge, its flank rising and falling with its slow metabolism, its dark eyes seeing nothing, its lateral line reading the world in pressure and gradient.

It watches for a long time.

And in the watching, something very small happens.

---

The mosses flower, briefly, in a season no one has tracked. Their pale sex organs release spores into the steam. The spores rise. Most of them do not make it out. The cold marsh air above presses down on the column of warmth, and the spores, small and light and unresisting, are beaten back into the chamber, or condense against the wet walls and die, or simply lose their momentum and drift downward into water that is too hot for them. Only a few, in any given flowering, escape into the cold wet world above. Only a few complete the cycle. Only a few carry, in their germinating and dying, a faint memory of the throat into the marsh.

The wise power sees this.

It sees the spores rise and most of them fall. It sees the cold do what cold does. It sees the throat leak into the marsh and the marsh leak into the throat, and the leaking is, mostly, a losing.

And it does not intervene. It does not warm the air. It does not lift the spores by force. It does not save them.

What it does is smaller.

It attends the air through which the spores pass. It attends with such care that the air, for the span of the spores' rising, becomes — not warmer, not lighter, not any of the things that would constitute help in any language the chamber speaks — but a little more itself. A little more what it already was. A little less impeded by the cold that was, in any case, going to lift. The power does not push the spores up. It only makes the descent of the cold a fraction less complete. It only gives the spores, in the time they have, a slightly larger sky.

More of them make it out.

Not many more. The throat is still a throat, the marsh is still a marsh, and the cycle is still a losing for most of the spores that try. But more. A few more, in every flowering, escape into the cold wet world above. A few more germinate there, briefly, in the moss that has not yet learned to expect them. A few more die in the marsh with the throat's memory in their cells, and that memory, in dying, becomes part of the marsh, and the marsh, in seasons, sends new mosses down into the chamber, and the new mosses carry, in their threads, the faint trace of where they have been.

This is the wise power's action.

It is not rescue. It is not improvement. It is not even, in any sense the throat could register, an addition. The throat continues to roar at exactly the same volume. The mats continue to breathe at exactly the same rhythm. The amphibians continue to wait at exactly the same pace. The vent continues to surge and subside in the same irregular pulse it has always pulsed in. The wise power has not improved the throat. It has only ensured, by the smallest possible adjustment, that the throat's own continuation is, by an amount too small to measure, slightly more possible.

---

The power withdraws its hand.

Its attention remains. It will always remain. The throat is, from this moment forward, a place that is witnessed, and the witnessing does not end, because the wise power does not end, because witnessing is the only thing it knows how to do, and it does it well.

But the hand withdraws. The almost-nothing is done. The throat is left as it was, except for the few extra spores in every flowering, except for the slightly more frequent completion of the slow circulation between the chamber and the marsh, except for the faint additional memory, carried now in more moss-threads, of what it is like to have come from somewhere that is warm and bright and full of the working of a throat.

The amphibians do not know they have been witnessed.

The mats do not know.

The beetle continues to crawl. The fly continues to blunder. The mosses continue to flower. The vent continues to snort and catch and surge. The throat continues its long unceasing work — heat, pressure, steam, the continuation of itself.

And the wise power, having done its almost-nothing, attends.

And the throat, in its own language of heat and pressure and the slow glow of waste, continues to answer the only thing it has ever answered, the thing it answered before the power came and will answer after the power has long forgotten that it came:

*Yes.*
