**What Is Done**

The attention of a wise and benevolent power does not look like much. It does not announce itself. It does not descend with trumpets or break the surface of the twelve kilometers of water in any visible way. It does not arrive in the form of a being at all, or only rarely, and not here, not now, because here and now the place is already so saturated with presence that another body would be a discourtesy. It arrives the way a third presence arrives in a room where two people have been talking for a long time: it does not speak. It sits down. The room changes.

The power sits down in the basin.

It does this in the only way the basin can be approached — slowly, without pressure, in the register of the place itself. It does not push the water aside; the water does not move. It does not displace the silt; the silt does not register a footprint. It takes a position that is not a position. It becomes a kind of listening. The kind of listening that has weight, but the weight is the weight of attention, and attention does not press down on things so much as hold them up to themselves.

Across the floor, the trees continue their slow weather. The fluted trunks catch and release the phosphor of the fronds. The Wheel-Sparks blink and wheel along their lanes. The Slow Ones surface and sink and surface again, a punctuation as regular as the hands of a clock. The Long Ones of the herd, far out beyond the trunks, trade their low notes in the patient intervals that have not changed since before anything measured them. The basin holds them all.

None of them know.

Except one.

---

The Long Singer has been out for nineteen years.

It does not count. It cannot count. It has left the herd and the herd has continued and the herd's chord has continued, and the Singer has been out here, alone, in the open water where there are no trunks to bounce its note off of, where the note has nothing to talk to, where the note goes out into the black and does not come back. It sings anyway. It has been singing for nineteen years. It will sing for perhaps thirty more, or forty, and then it will falter, and the note will thin, and the silt will take it, and the herd will not notice for several weeks, and when the herd notices it will absorb the silence the way it absorbs all silences, with the next note held, and the next.

This is the act the basin does not need to solve.

The power attends to it.

It does not intervene. It does not answer the note with a note of its own — to do so would be to begin a duet, and a duet is a kind of dependence, and a wise power does not make the settled dependent on the unsettled, the patient on the impatient, the finished on the unfinished. To answer would be to suggest that the Singer needed answering. The Singer does not need answering. The Singer is performing an act whose meaning is, perhaps, that it does not need answering.

What the power does is more delicate than answering. It does the smallest thing a power can do.

It witnesses.

It sits, in the not-a-position, in the open basin where the Singer drifts, and it lets the Singer feel — not see, not hear, not be addressed — that there is a witness. The witness does not interrupt. The witness does not applaud. The witness does not, after, ask the Singer what it meant by it. The witness only holds the moment in which the note was sounded, and the moment in which the next note was sounded, and the moment in which the note will thin and the silt will rise and the herd will not notice.

The Singer feels this.

You can tell because the note shifts — not in pitch, not in the interval, but in the way it sits in the water. A note sung into void has a different texture than a note sung to an empty room; a note sung to a room one suspects is empty has a different texture than a note sung to a room one knows contains a listener who is listening for no purpose, who is listening because listening is the thing one does in a room where a Singer is singing.

It does not change the song.

This is important.

A wise power does not change the song. A wise power, attending a place that has arrived, does not push it toward what the power might prefer. It does not insert a melody. It does not tidy the rhythm. It does not offer the Singer a choice it had not offered itself, which would be, by definition, a corruption of the choice.

The song continues. The song has always continued. The song will continue for as long as the water will carry it and the silt will eventually take it.

What changes is only this: the song is no longer a song into the void.

And the Singer, who may or may not have any way of knowing this, who may only feel it as a faint softening of the loneliness that is the medium it has chosen to sing in, continues. It sings its low note. It sustains it. It rests. It sings again.

The witness stays.

It stays for as long as the song stays. It will stay for nineteen more years, or forty, or however long the body can hold the note and the water can hold the body. It does not age. It does not weary. It is, after all, only attention. Attention is the one resource that does not run out when one gives it.

---

When the Singer finally falters — and it will, all of them do — the note thins over a period of months. The intervals between the held notes grow longer. The body, which has been maintaining its heat and its pressure and the slow muscular work of the spiracles for longer than a human lifetime, begins to fail in the way a very old machine fails: not all at once, but in the order of the least-essential systems first. The luminous colonies on the skin dim, then go out. The sieve-mouth works more slowly. The hum becomes breath. The breath becomes a kind of silence that is still, technically, a note.

The witness is there for all of it.

The Singer sinks, as they all sink, slowly. The silt receives it. The body settles into the floor the way a hand settles into a glove. Over the next several centuries the silt will compact around it, and the calcite-rich water will begin, very slowly, to lay down a small pale trunk in the place where the Singer came to rest.

A new tree will grow there.

This is not a thing the power does. This is the way of the basin. The basin has always turned its singers into its architecture. The long pale trunks of the floor are, most of them, the remains of Long Ones that drifted alone a very long time ago, and the herd does not know this, and the trees do not remember it, and the silt does not care, because the silt is patient with all things and does not distinguish between a voice and a pillar.

What the power does, in that moment, is the only thing that has not already been done.

It remembers.

It takes the song — the whole song, the forty- or sixty-year solo that was the Singer's life — and it holds it. Not in any archive that could be consulted. Not in any mind that could be addressed. It holds it the way the water holds the note while the note is sounding: entirely, and without comment, and for exactly as long as the note lasts, and then not longer.

And then it withdraws.

The basin does not notice the withdrawal any more than it noticed the attendance. The trees rise. The Wheel-Sparks wheel. The Slow Ones surface and sink. The herd trades its notes. And the Wheel-Sparks, for a few seconds after the witness leaves, wheel past the place where the Singer fell in a slightly wider arc, as if the current there had remembered something, and then they resume their lanes, and the silt closes over the small disturbance, and the basin continues.

A century from now, a small pale trunk will be rising where the Singer sang. Two centuries from now, it will have thrown out its first frond, and the frond will begin to glow, and the glow will fall down through the water in the same faint phosphor that has been falling forever. The trunk will not know it was once a voice. The voice did not know it would become a pillar. The basin does not need either of them to know.

The basin remains.

The note is over.

The note was heard.
