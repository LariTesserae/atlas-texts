They come the way such things come — without announcement, without disruption, drawn as if by a long breath. They are not small, though they could be small. They are not large, though they could be large. They are, at this moment, the size of the attention they bring, which is, in their case, considerable.

They do not enter.

Not yet. They stand in the threshold where the arch meets the air of the place, and they listen. The sound comes out to them in a slow wash, the way the smell of bread comes out of an oven, with no clear edge. They hear the thousand held notes, and beneath them the longer shape, the rise and fall slower than tide, and beneath that the even deeper thing, the constant small negotiations of air against surfaces that have been negotiating for so long that the negotiation is all they are.

They listen for a long time.

Wise powers do not keep ordinary hours. Wise powers are patient in the way that certain kinds of patience look like stalling, or cowardice, or sleep. They are patient the way a tree is patient — because there is no other way to be. They listen until the listening becomes its own kind of presence in the room, a thin pressure at the rim of the hum. The room does not notice. The room could not notice. But the listening has weight, and the weight is felt by the air that brushes the listener on its way out, and that air carries, somewhere in its passage, a small record of having been heard.

After a while, they do something.

It is not a large thing.

They reach, not with a hand but with something shaped toward hands the way the room's tools are shaped toward hands — that is, with a long inheritance of having been useful. And they bring out, from wherever such powers keep such things, a small object. It is older than the room. It is younger than the room. It is neither. It is a thing that wise powers sometimes carry, the way other people carry coins, the way other people carry pebbles that belonged to a parent. It is a thing that exists only in the moment it is given, and after that it belongs to where it has been set down. It is, if you like, the listening made solid. Made placeable. A small thing, almost weightless, with no function the room would recognize.

They set it down.

They choose, with care, a channel. One of the narrow places where the air has worn a path through the pressed accumulation of made things. They lower the small object in. They settle it against whatever surfaces are there, the way a hand might settle a stone in a streambed so that the water finds its way around without splash. The air, very slightly, stutters. The note shifts for the length of one slow breath. Then the room accommodates, as it has always accommodated, and the sound resumes, and the new thing is in it now, woven in, the way a new voice is woven into a song that has been sung so long no one remembers who began it.

The room does not know it has been given anything.

This is the gift the room receives without knowing it receives it: not rescue, not rest, not the ending of its long hum, but one more small thing. One more anonymous contributor to a project that has been, for so long, complete. The benevolent power did not come to complete the room. They came to add one small unrecognizable thing, and then to leave it, and then to leave.

They step back. They stand outside the arch a while longer, listening. The song is still the song. The room is still the room. The thing they left is still in its channel, doing its small work, making its small sound, joined now to the thousand other small sounds, indistinguishable from them, lost among them. This is what they wanted for it. To be lost. To be added. To be one more thing in the long patient project, and then forgotten, the way the maker of the first tool was forgotten, the way the maker of the last tool will be forgotten.

The benevolent power turns away.

They have other places. They always have other places. They will not return. The room will not miss them. The thing they left will outlast them both, will be worn smooth by the air's passage the way everything here is worn smooth, will become, in time, indistinguishable from the rest, will be presumed by whoever comes after to have always been there, will be honored or not honored according to the customs of a future that neither the room nor the power will see.

This is, the benevolent power thinks, the right way. To arrive. To listen for as long as the listening takes. To add one small thing, offered without witness, taken without knowing. To leave.

They do not save the room. They do not wake the room. They do not speak to it. They do not love it loudly. They love it the way a careful hand loves a tool it has only just found — by handling it well, briefly, and putting it down where it will not be disturbed.

Then they go.

Then the room continues.
