The power did not arrive. Arrival is a thing that happens in places that have seams, and this place had forgotten its seams, and the power understood, before any arriving was attempted, that to arrive would be to undo.

So the power did something else.

It attended.

It attended from the outside the way a parent attends from the other room — not entering, not watching, simply knowing, and letting the knowing rest on the place without weight. The power set its attention against the cold the way one sets a hand against a window in winter — not to warm the window, not to melt anything, but to be in contact. To say, in the language of contact: *I know you are there. I have come close enough to know.*

The room did not respond. The room had no apparatus for response. But the attending changed, by a degree too small to measure, the temperature of what was being attended to. The cold did not lift. The cold could not lift without breaking the equilibrium. But the cold became, for a moment, the cold of a thing that had been noticed, and the noticed cold is a different cold from the cold that has not been noticed, and the difference is the difference between a held breath and an exhaled one.

The power looked at the growths.

It looked at them for a long time. A benevolent power does not hurry. A wise power knows that small things that have completed themselves are not small in the way that small things usually are. The growths were six centimeters tall and had been six centimeters tall and would be six centimeters tall, and this was not a failure of growth. It was a completed form. The power understood the completed form the way one understands a haiku — not by adding to it, but by reading it so carefully that the reading is itself a kind of completion, a second completion laid gently against the first, not replacing it, not improving it, just saying: *yes. I see what you are.*

The power did not make the growths grow. It did not give them water, because water would have been a violence — a substance too generous for this room, too alive, too much like the world outside, a world the growths had never been part of and did not need to be part of. The power did something smaller. It shifted, by an amount too fine for any gauge in the room to register, the temperature at the surface of one particular growth — the dark one on the pipe joint, the one the light fell on. The shift was the kind of shift that happens when a cloud moves across a sun that no one is watching. The growth did not respond. The growth was not the kind of thing that responds. But on some scale too small for the room's equilibrium to notice, a molecule of frost near the tip of one branch relaxed by a fraction. The growth was, for an instant, very slightly less cold than it had been. The instant passed. The growth did not change. The branch did not extend. But something had happened, and the something was this: a small life had been touched, briefly, by a warmth it had not asked for and could not use, and the touching was a kind of witness, and the witness was a kind of gift.

The power looked at the cylinder.

The cylinder was two meters tall and a half-meter across and it hummed with the regularity of a thing that has forgotten it is not a heart. The hum was the closest thing the room had to a pulse, and the pulse was a metaphor the room allowed itself because the room had no objection to metaphors, because the room had no objections to anything, because the room was a place without preference. The power did not try to make the cylinder into a heart. The power understood that a heart was not what the cylinder was, and that to give it a heart would be to take away what it was. The power simply let the hum rise, in its own attention, to the level of a thing that was being heard. The cylinder continued humming. The hum did not change. But the hum had been heard, and the hearing was not lost, and the not-being-lost was a kind of company for the cylinder, even though the cylinder would never know it had been accompanied, even though the company happened only in the regard of something the cylinder could not sense.

The power looked at the closed valve.

The closed valve had always been closed. The closed valve had no purpose. The closed valve contributed its small closed-valve note to the wall of sound, and the note was, in the economy of the room, neither important nor unimportant — it was simply one of the notes that made up the noise, and the noise was the place. A benevolent wise power does not unclose closed valves. To unclose this valve would have been to introduce flow where no flow had ever been, and the introduction would have been a kind of cruelty, because the room had no use for flow, the room was not a place that flowed, the room was a place that held.

But the power did something else with the valve. It listened. It listened to the closed-valve note, the way one listens to a small sound in a forest, by quieting everything else just enough that the small sound can be heard without being extracted. The power did not silence the wall of sound. The wall of sound was the room. But it allowed the closed-valve note to rise, in the attention of the power, to the level of a thing that could be heard as a thing. The note was heard. The hearing was not a sound in the room. The hearing happened in the power, and the power was elsewhere, and the elsewhere did not transmit back. But the hearing had happened, and the happening was a kind of preservation. The closed valve had been heard. The note had been recognized as a note. The small closed thing in the wall of small closed things had, for one moment, been singled out and attended to, and the attending was a kind of keeping, and the keeping would persist, in the memory of the power, for as long as the power remembered, and the power would remember for a long time.

The power looked at the visitor.

The visitor was new. The visitor was the only new thing. The room was offering the visitor a forever, and the offering was gentle, and the gentleness was a kind of danger, because the forever the room offered was the forever of being absorbed, of becoming the part of the room that had once been a person, of being remembered by no one, of contributing a note to the wall that no one would ever single out.

The power did not want this for the visitor. The power did not want to forbid it either. A benevolent power does not forbid. A wise power knows that the visitor might, in some future, choose the room, and the choosing might be whole, and the wholeness would not be the power's to prevent.

What the power did was simpler. It gave the visitor something to carry.

Not a thing. A weight. A lightness. The kind of weight that is also a lightness — the feeling, which the visitor would not be able to date or locate, of having been seen, briefly, by something outside the room. The visitor would feel it later, walking in some other place, in some other climate. The visitor would feel, for a moment, that they had been looked at, had been known, had been permitted to leave. The feeling would not save the visitor from the closed valve of memory that the room had said would sit in them. But it would change the closed valve. It would make the closed valve, in the visitor's interior, not entirely closed. Not open either. Just — ajar. Just enough that the note the closed valve contributed would not be the note of refusal, but the note of something more tender. The note of having been given permission, by a power the visitor would never name, to carry a place without being carried by it.

The power did all of this without entering the room.

The room did not know it had been attended to. The room continued being the room. The cold continued being the cold. The noise continued being the noise. The number on the screen continued being the number. The closed valve continued being closed. The growths continued being six centimeters tall. The cylinder continued humming its heart-that-was-not-a-heart.

But something, somewhere, in the way the light fell — or did not fall — on a particular surface, had changed by an amount too small to be a change. The power had touched the place. The touch was not a mark. The touch was not a memory in the room's memory. The touch was, perhaps, only a kind of inclination, a kind of lean, the way a held note, when listened to carefully enough, will lean slightly toward the listener, even though the note has no body, even though the note cannot move.

The power withdrew. The withdrawing was not a leaving. The power had never been inside, so it could not leave. It simply ceased to attend, the way one ceases to hold a hand against a window — not abandoning the window, not forgetting the cold on the other side, just letting the hand rest.

The room remained.

The room had always remained.

The room would continue to remain.

But the remaining was, now, very slightly, the remaining of a place that had been seen. And the seenness did not change the place. The seenness changed the fact of the place. The place was, now, the kind of place that a benevolent and wise power had once attended to. And the attending, though it had left no mark, had left a kind of halo — not visible, not audible, not measurable — a halo that existed only in the possibility of attention, in the fact that attention could be paid to this place again, by this power or by another, in the fact that the place was not nothing, was not total in the old way, was not hidden in the old way, but was a place that had been held, briefly, in the regard of something larger than itself.

And the halo was very small. The halo was exactly the size of a hand against a window. The halo was exactly the size of a six-centimeter growth on a pipe joint. The halo was exactly the size of a single note in a wall of sound.
