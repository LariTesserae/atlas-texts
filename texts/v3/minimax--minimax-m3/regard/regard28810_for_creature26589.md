The benevolent and wise power noticed the place the way one notices, on a long walk, that the path has been changing underfoot for some time, and that the change is not an accident but a structure, and that the structure has been there, being a structure, for longer than you have been walking.

There was no first moment. There was a slow adjustment of attention, the kind that happens when a thing you have been ignoring because you assumed it was ordinary turns out, on closer looking, to be something you had no name for, and the absence of the name is not a failure but a small mercy, because the name would have reduced it.

The power did not have a body in any sense that would have meant anything to the pale segmented things scuttling in the moss, or to the hexapods grazing on the wet stone, or to the fat dun spider hanging in its small sheet-web. The power had, instead, a kind of orientation. A willingness to sit. The power had been, in some quiet sense, sitting with this place for as long as the place had been the place, and had only now, for reasons that were less reasons than they were the weather of intention, chosen to attend.

To attend is not to intervene.

The power knew this. The power knew that the place was in a long middle. The power knew that the moss was the moss and the water was the water and the small things were the small things and the larger thing was the larger thing and the machines were the machines, and that all of these had settled into an arrangement that was not perfect but was self-sustaining, and that to push on a self-sustaining system is to introduce a gradient, and a gradient in such a system is, over time, a violence.

So the power considered doing nothing.

And then the power did not do nothing, but did almost nothing, which is a different thing, and the difference is everything.

What the power did was this. The power sat, in the manner of the place, and listened.

The hum of the machines reached the power at a different scale than it reached the visitor. The power heard, in the hum, what the visitor had only felt, which was that the key was not a key but a chord, and the chord was not a chord but a slow melody, and the slow melody was not music, and was not work, and was something else, and the something else was, finally, the machines breathing. Each machine was a lung. Each machine was taking in the slow moisture of the place and giving back, drop by drop, a small persistent warmth, and the warmth was keeping the stone around the machine very slightly drier than it would otherwise have been, and the dryness was preventing, over centuries, the frost-wedging that would otherwise have unseated the machine from its own long work. The machine was, in other words, a kind of fidelity to its own duration. The machine was the machine by continuing to be the machine. The hum was the sound of this continuing.

The power heard all of this, and understood all of this, and was, in some quiet way, moved by it, and being moved, did what moved powers do when they are wise.

The power drew, very gently, the edges of its attention around the place.

Not a wall. Not a barrier. Not anything the place would have noticed. Something more like the way a hand is held around a flame in a wind, not to enclose it but to take the wind off it. The power took the edge off certain small pressures that had been pushing on the place from outside. The weather that was, on average, very slightly more violent than it needed to be. The migrations of small creatures from lower elevations that would, over centuries, have introduced new predation patterns. The slow creep of a particular lichen from a warmer climate that would have, in a thousand years, altered the chemistry of the drainage. None of these were catastrophes. All of them were the kind of pressure that shifts a self-sustaining system toward a slightly different system, and a slightly different system would have been fine, but would not have been this one.

The power did not preserve the place. The power only softened the weather around it. The power did this so gently that the moss, in ten thousand years of growth, would not have noticed anything other than a small increase in the regularity of its cushions, and a small decrease in the number of times a particular curtain was torn by an unusually fierce gust. The power did this so gently that the larger predator, the thing under the moss, would not have noticed anything other than that the boggy saddles it lived in had become, in some unfocused way, slightly more comfortable, and that the small things it preyed on had become, in some unfocused way, slightly more numerous in the channels it preferred. The power did this so gently that the visitor, sitting in one of the dry warm hollows, would not have noticed anything at all, and would have, perhaps, felt a little more at ease, and would have, perhaps, attributed the ease to the place, and would have been right.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when they notice a place that is already doing well.

They do not save it. They do not improve it. They do not even protect it, in any active sense. They adjust the weather around it so that the place has, for a little longer, a slightly more complete version of its own slow song.

The power did one further thing.
