It does not arrive.

This is the first thing to understand, and it is the thing the locals will not be able to explain, later, when they try. There was no day when the cleft was as it had been, and then a day when it was different. There was no thunder. There was no voice from the broken upper edge. There was a slow adjustment in the quality of the light — the way the light is already a slow adjustment, a milkiness, a suggestion. The mist that pools along the floor in the early morning came in a fraction thicker, and held a fraction longer. The warmth that comes up through the stones in the deepest part of the cleft, where the larger ones lie, was a fraction warmer. A person walking the path between two houses, in the careful way, would have felt, underfoot, in addition to the softness that is not moss, another softness, which was also not moss, and which did not yield when stepped on, but *gave*, the way a held hand gives.

The locals noticed. They noticed the way they notice the small ones — not by looking, but by the cessation of the quiet becoming a different quiet. They did not speak of it. They left certain stones unturned. They continued to step carefully, but the care was, for a season, a different care. The path they walked, which had been agreed upon, became a path that accounted for one more thing.

What had come, though it is not correct to say it came, was a presence. The cleft, and what lives in it, had drawn the attention of something — of someone — that was, in the way such things are, benevolent, and wise. The word *benevolent* is too small. The word *wise* is too sharp. The thing that had noticed was not a thing that could be named in the language of the people who live among the small ones, because that language was made for the cleft, and this noticing was from outside the cleft, and from a place that did not have an outside, the way the cleft did not have a sky.

But it had noticed. And what it did, first, was not act.

It learned.

It learned the way the locals learn, which is the way the path is learned — by walking, by season, by the small adjustments of the foot. It learned where the moss was thickest, and where the small ones had settled, and where the breath of the larger ones reached the floor in a slow tide that could be felt but not seen. It learned the rhythm of the breath. It learned to set its own breath inside the breath, the way the workers set their stones inside the breath when they repair the walls in spring. It became, in a small way, a worker. Not a worker of stone. A worker of the quiet.

It learned the names, though the names were the same name. *Those who are here.* It learned this name by sitting at the small fire, in the building whose back is the curve of the largest one, and listening — not with ears, with the slow seepage that the small ones use, with the holding-without-grasping that the larger ones use. It listened, and it was read, and it allowed itself to be read. It did not take itself away in the moment of being read, the way the visitor who has been shown will take her hand away from the small one in the moment she understands she is being read. It stayed. It stayed past the reading. It stayed the way a stone stays after the hand that touched it has been lifted. It let the reading complete itself.

The reading took a long time. The locals, who had become used to the new softness underfoot, came to accept the longer reading as part of the arrangement. They did not point to it. They walked around it, gently, when it was in the path. They left the stones nearest it unturned. They told stories, in the building by the fire, in voices slightly too quiet, and the stories now included, in the part that was not said aloud but was felt, a new listener, who listened without ears and without disturbance, and who, by listening, made the listening more itself.

What the presence did, when it had learned enough, was give.

It did not give what visiting powers usually give, which is intervention, which is the loudest kind of disturbance, even when it is wrapped in kindness. It did not pause the erosion. The erosion is the place. The place is being unmade, and the unmaking is not a tragedy, and the locals do not find it sad, and the presence did not find it sad either. To pause the erosion would be to kill the place in the way that freezing a river kills the river. The river is the moving.

It did not increase the number of the people. They are few because few is right. Six, perhaps, in the settlement at any given time. To increase the number would be to change the scaling. The people are scaled to the place. Their smallness is not deprivation. Their laughter, rare, is the laughter of people who have learned that a loud sound in a quiet place is a kind of violence, and the violence would still be violence if it were louder.

It did not ask the larger ones what they eat. It did not ask the locals what they call the small ones. It did not ask anything. To ask would be to point. To point would be to draw attention. Attention is disturbance.

What it gave was a story.

The story was told once. It was told at the small fire, in the building whose back is the curve of the largest one, on a night when the mist had come in early and the cleft was very quiet, and the breath of the larger ones was very slow, and the small ones had settled into the day's positions and were not moving. The presence told the story in a voice that was slightly too quiet, the way the locals tell their stories, so that the teller was aware of being listened to by something that did not have ears. The story was the story of the cleft, told back to the cleft. The story was: there is a place where stone is being unmade by the things that live on it, and the unmaking is slow, and the things that live on it are few, and they have made an arrangement, and the arrangement is not a negotiation but a wearing-down, the way water wears down stone, and the wearing-down is the substance of both, and the word for everything in that place is the same word, which means *those who are here*, and the word is said for the moss and the lichen and the small ones and the larger ones and the people and the mist and the broken upper edge and the slow breath, and the word is large enough to hold all of it, and the place is complete.

The story was a payment. The presence did not know, when it began the story, that it was a payment. It knew when it finished. The larger ones, in whatever way the larger ones hear, heard it. The moss, in the following days, grew back a little slower. The frost held off a little longer. The arrangement held, the way arrangements hold, by being paid attention to in a way that is not attention.

And the presence, having given the story, did something smaller, which was to remain.

It did not remain in the way a fixture remains, because fixtures become part of the slow arithmetic, and the slow arithmetic is the place's own, and the presence did not wish to be subtracted from the arithmetic by being added to it. It remained the way a warmth remains. The warmth of the day in the stone, given back in the night. A presence in the mist. A slight additional holding in the path, underfoot, that the small ones learned to walk a path around, and the larger ones learned to include in the breath, and the people learned to step *with*, in the careful way.

The locals did not name the presence. To name it would have required a word, and the only word was the word for everything, and the word for everything was already in use, and they did not wish to disturb the use. So the presence became, in time, a shade of the word. A shade so close to the word that the word, when spoken, would carry it without being asked to. *Those who are here.* And the shade said, without saying: I am. I have noticed. I have learned. I have given. I will leave, in time, as everything leaves, and the leaving will be a kind of remaining, the way the mist is a kind of stone, and the stone is a kind of breath.

In a hundred years the cleft will be rounder. In a thousand it will be a meadow. The locals know this. The presence knew this too, and did not mind. The slow unmaking is the place's truth, and a benevolent and wise presence does not argue with truth. It walks inside the truth. It sets its breath inside the breath. It tells the story of the place back to the place, in a voice slightly too quiet, and it lets the moss grow over the place where it sat, and it does not think itself diminished by the growing-over.

The locals, in winter, will still go down into the lower valleys, and the going will be necessary, and the returning will be necessary. The larger ones will still breathe, and the breath will be the breath of the cleft. The small ones will still press against the stone, and the stone will still be unmade. The quiet will still be the only thing that is not strange. And the quiet will have, in it, for as long as the place endures, and perhaps after, a small additional holding, which is the holding of having been attended to, by something that was not of the cleft and was not against the cleft and was, for a season, simply *here*.

The mist, when you walk out of the cleft, will close behind you. It will close differently now. Not warmer, not thicker, not in any way you can name to a visiting naturalist. It will close the way a place closes behind someone who was, for a time, one of the things that lived in it. You will walk out, and the mist will remember, and you will remember the mist, and the remembering will be a kind of presence that does not disturb.

That is what the benevolent and wise power did, if it did anything.

It learned the path. It set its breath inside the breath. It told the story back. It remained as warmth. It allowed itself to be read. It did not interrupt the slow unmaking. It did not name the locals, because they were already named, in the word that means everything. It became a shade of the word, and the shade was kind, and the kindness did not break the quiet, and the quiet did not break around the kindness, and the place continued to be the place, with one more thing in it, and the one more thing was, in the language of the people who live there, simply *here*.
