A benevolent and wise power, having noticed this place, would not come down into it. There is no way to come down that would not be a kind of breaking. Even a god, lowering itself through the cold gray light at the lip, would displace the air, and the folded ones would fall from their clusters, and the crickets would stop their clicking for a moment, and the chamber would fold the disturbance into itself, but the disturbance would be there, in the memory of the stone, for as long as the stone lasts. A benevolent and wise power knows this. So it does not descend. It listens.

It listens from wherever it is, and at whatever distance, and the place is the same at any distance — the same layered sound, the same slow breathing of the pools, the same single low note rising out of the warm dark at intervals that are not quite regular. The power listens the way the fissure hums, and it lets the listening become part of the listening it was already hearing. In time — and time, for a benevolent and wise power, is not a constraint — it learns to tell the creature's call from the resonance of the chamber itself, the way a person who lives near a bell tower can tell the bell from the wind that rings it.

It learns, also, about the hand.

Not what the hand is. A benevolent and wise power is wise enough to know that what the hand *is* is not the question the chamber is asking. The hand-shaped grip in the basalt is the right height for a hand. The smear at the base of the grip is fresh. The power could, if it wished, descend through the cold gray light and put its own hand into the grip and know, with certainty, whether the hand-shape was made by a hand, or by water, or by a thousand seasons of crickets landing and taking off. It does not do this. It would be a kind of violence to know, in this place. The chamber has not asked to be known. The chamber has asked for nothing. The chamber has only been here, and the creature in it has only been here, and the hand-shaped grip has only been here, and the smear has only been renewed.

A benevolent and wise power, having noticed, would do almost nothing. But not quite nothing.

It would not give the creature language. Language is a tool, and tools imply hands, and the chamber has only this one suspicious hand, and the chamber is not ready to have a question asked of it. The chamber has been finding its balance for a long time. To give the creature a word would be to give it a project, and a project is a kind of weight, and weight breaks balances. The creature's single low note is not a word. The note is the sound of the creature checking that the chamber is still there, the way you call out in a dark house to be sure someone is there. To make the note into a word would be to make the chamber a house that answers back, and a chamber that answers back is no longer a chamber; it is a kind of mouth, and mouths are hungrier than fissures.
