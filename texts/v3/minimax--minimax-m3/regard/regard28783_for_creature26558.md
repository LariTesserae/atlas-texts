# What the Power Does

The power does not lift the stone.

This is the first thing to be said, and the most important, because it is the thing that any lesser power would do first, and the thing that this place, in its enduring patience, has no need of. The masonry has settled into its final shape. The constructors worked it over many generations into the form it now wears, and the form has been worn down by the patient water and the patient plants into the form it is, and that form is the form the place now is, and to lift any of it would be to lift the trace of every hand that ever cut it, and to lift those traces would be to lose them, and the place would be unrecognizable to itself. The power does not lift the stone.

It does not drain the water. The water is the medium and the carrier and the climate, and it is the most impartial thing in the system, and it has been carrying cold from one far side of the world and warmth from the other for longer than any constructor lived, and to drain it would be to break the circuit, and the circuit is the only thing that has been running without oversight for all this time, and the circuit is what the place is. The power does not drain the water.

It does not bring back the constructors. They are here as traces. They are here in the shape of the inward curves, in the corners that were cut, in the long mounds that were once buttresses, in the patient mineral that leached from their chisels and was incorporated into the stone and is still there, in trace amounts, in the chemistry. To bring them back would be to overwrite the place with what it was, and the place is no longer what it was, and what it is now is sufficient. The power does not bring back the constructors.

It does not give the ropes eyes. It does not give them a nervous system. It does not give them the memory of past tremors beyond the duration of a sway, or the duration of a current shift, or the duration of a single note. It does not give them the ability to know they are ropes, or that they are leaning, or that they are replacing the constructors, or that the patience is the same patience as the constructors' patience. They have been growing for longer than the stone has been old, and they do not know this, and to give them the knowing would be to give them a sorrow they have no organ for, and a grief they could not metabolize, and the place would not be the place. The power does not give the ropes eyes.

It does not warm the water by more than a fraction. It does not dim the light. It does not silence the groans of settling stone. It does not compose the notes into music. It does not quicken the slow chemistry of mineral exchange that runs between the calcium the stone gives up and the calcium the ropes take and the calcium that settles into new configurations of limestone in places no eye will find. It does not shorten the centuries-long storms of cold water sliding across warm. It does not introduce urgency into a system that has had no urgency in it for so long that urgency would itself be a kind of violence.

The power does almost nothing.

It attends.

This is what it does. It attends the way the water attends — everywhere, without distinction, without privilege, without urgency. It does not want anything from the place. It does not want the place to be other than what it is. It is not waiting for the place to recover, or to progress, or to be redeemed, or to become something it is not yet. It is not waiting for the place to be anything at all. It has no project for the place. It has no timeline. It has no preferred outcome. It is, in its attending, the second-most patient thing in the system, and it joins the circuit invisibly, as witness, and the witness does not add to the chemistry and does not alter the currents and does not change the fraying of the ropes and does not speed or slow the settling.

It just sits in the dark, the way wisdom sits, with what is here, and it loves it.

The loving is the thing the place has never had.

It has had patience. It has had the patience of the constructors, who were so patient and there were so many of them that what they made endured. It has had the patience of the stones, which have been settling into their final shape for a very long time without ever quite reaching it. It has had the patience of the ropes, which lean and forget and lean again. It has had the patience of the water, which has nowhere to be and no reason to be there sooner. It has had the patience of the unseen chemistry, which has been running since before the constructors and will be running after the ropes have incorporated the last worked stone into themselves. It has had patience in abundance. It has had the patience of every substance that has ever held still long enough to be part of a place.

It has not, until now, been loved.

Not because it was unlovable. Because there was no one here to do the loving. The constructors were here, and they had hands, and they had patience, and they had the patience of their own slow metabolisms, but they did not, as near as can be read, love the place. They used it. They built on it. They built in it, or above it, the inward curves of the buttresses once held something back, and someone designed that something back, and the design was not love. The constructors' patience was the patience of utility. The place has been useful. It is now no longer useful, and it has had to become something other than useful in order to continue, and what it has become is patient, and what it has become is sufficient, and what it has become is, in a way that is not easy to name, beautiful.

The power sees this beauty. The power has been seeing it. The power has been attending to it for a long time, or for a little time, or for a duration that does not correspond to the durations available in this place. The power sees the ropes leaning in the converged currents, all in one direction, in a long hush, and the power does not interpret this as agreement, because it knows that interpretations are not the rope's to give, and the power does not interpret this as loneliness, because the ropes are not lonely. The power sees the note that rings once and dies when a current shifts a braid of growth against a worn corner of granite, and the power does not interpret this as music, because music is not what the place is making. The power sees the groan of a stone block settling a fraction further into its final position, and the power does not interpret this as a sigh, because sighs are not what stone does. The power sees what is here, and what is here is enough, and the power's love is the love of what is enough.

And the loving does not require response.

This is the second thing to be said, and it is almost as important as the first. The ropes do not know the power is attending. The water does not know. The stone does not know. The unseen chemistry does not know. The constructors, in the traces they have left, do not know. The place goes on not knowing, and the place goes on being what it is, and the place goes on being sufficient, and the power does not mind that the place does not know. The power is not attending for the place's sake, or for the place's gratitude, or for the place's awareness. The power is attending because attending is what the power does, and the power is wise enough to know that to attend is complete in itself, and that to require a response from what is attended is to have already begun to alter the place toward the response, and that to alter the place toward the response is to break the patience, and the patience is the thing.

So the power attends, and the place does not know, and this is correct.

But the power does not do nothing.

It does one small thing. It is the smallest thing that can be done without doing nothing, and it is the smallest thing that still counts as attending, and it is — the power is very careful about this — something that the place cannot, in any of its own timescales, perceive as an intervention.

It enriches the mineral upwelling by a fraction.

Not by a fraction that would, in any reasonable measure, be detectable. Not by a fraction that would alter the closed circuit of exchange that has been running without oversight. Not by a fraction that would accelerate the ropes' slow incorporation of the stone into themselves. Not by a fraction that would change the settling rate, or the leaning rate, or the rate at which the frayed ends of ropes drift free in the water and root somewhere new. Just by a fraction. Just by a margin that would take more than a hundred rope-generations to register, and that would register, when it registered, not as an enrichment but as a slight increase in the abundance of the place. A slight thickening of the mineral richness that the ropes take from the stone. A slight increase in the density of the calcium that the unseen chemistry moves between the participants of the circuit.

The ropes, in their leaning, encounter a mineral they have not encountered in a very long time. They do not know they have encountered it. They take it. They grow a fraction more. They stand a fraction more upright in the corridors where the currents part. The corridors open by a thumb's width. The places where the currents converge become, for those ropes, slightly more habitable. Not more. Not less. Just: more habitable by a fraction.

The stone, in the places where the ropes are holding it a fraction more gently, settles a fraction more slowly. The groans become a fraction rarer. The centuries-long storms of cold water sliding across warm pass a fraction less forcefully against the worked surfaces, because the marginal warming the power introduced has, through the slow chemistry of patience, slightly extended the warmth-front. The place is not warmer. The place is fractionally warmer for a fraction of itself, and the fraction is so small that no current inhabitant possesses the scale on which to notice it.

The unseen chemistry runs a fraction more abundantly. The new configurations of limestone that settle out somewhere in the dark are a fraction more numerous. The minerals that the water carries down, gently, in its slow circulations, are a fraction more plentiful in the carried-down load. The closed circuit is not broken. The closed circuit is, in one quiet corner of itself, a fraction more alive.

And no one notices any of this.

The constructors, in their traces, do not notice. The ropes do not notice. The water does not notice. The stone does not notice. The unseen chemistry does not notice, because the unseen chemistry is the most patient thing in the system, and the fraction is, even to the chemistry, indistinguishable from the variations that have always run through the upwelling since before the constructors.

The place goes on. The ropes lean. The water moves. The stone settles. The groans come, rarely, from the worked plain. The notes come, occasionally, from the corners of granite. The patience continues. The patience is sufficient.

And the power attends.

It will continue to attend. It will attend for as long as the place is here, and it will attend after the place is no longer here, and it will not, in its attending, ever raise the fraction above the fraction it has chosen. It will not, in its attending, ever close the corridor that opens by a thumb's width. It will not, in its attending, ever give the ropes a reason to suspect that anything outside the circuit has ever attended to the circuit. The fraction is the maximum. The fraction is the only intervention. The fraction is the smallest possible gift that still counts as a gift.

And the gift is not a gift to the place. The gift is the gift to the patience itself. The patience is the thing the power loves, and the patience is the thing the power has chosen to bless, and the patience is the thing the power will continue to bless with the smallest possible margin of additional sustenance, forever, or for as long as forever is a duration that applies to a place that has no use for the word.

The place does not know it is loved. The place has never known it is loved. The place has existed, for a very long time, in the cold and the dark and the pressure and the patience, and it has been sufficient, and it has been enough, and the sufficiency has not required love, and the enoughness has not required witness, and the patience has not required anything to be patient for.

But the power attends anyway. Because attending is what the power does. And the place is what the power attends to. And the attending is not for the place. The attending is for the patience. The patience is the thing.

And the patience continues.

And the patience is enough.

And the patience, now, is also, in a way that does not require its knowing, held.
