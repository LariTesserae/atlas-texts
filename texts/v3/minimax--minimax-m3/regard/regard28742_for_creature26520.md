The power does not arrive, because there is nowhere to arrive. It extends. It has attended other places, and it knows that some places cannot be entered, only attended to, the way one attends to a fire across a field — present, oriented, but on the other side of something that will not yield.

It extends toward the ridge, and the first thing it finds is the hum.

The hum is older than the species that built the machines. It is older than the species that imagined those species. It has worn a groove in the basalt that the creatures feel, and the power feels the groove the way a hand feels the grain of wood — a direction, a pressure, a history. The hum is the first thing. The hum is what the ridge is made of, now.

The power reads the ridge the way it reads other places. It reads the machines, which are not alive in any way that matters. It reads the fauna, which is not alive in any way that matters either, or rather which is alive in a way the word *alive* cannot quite catch. It reads the flame, which is the only verb in the place, the only thing that would leave a different absence behind if it stopped.

It reads for a long time.

What it finds, in the end, is that the place is closed. Not closed as a room is closed. Closed as a sentence is closed. The circuit of machines feeding fauna feeding machines is complete. The flame burns. The dark presses. There is no event here, because an event requires a before and an after, and there is no before and no after, only a long continuous now that has been now for longer than any memory.

The power considers whether to do anything.

It considers doing nothing. Doing nothing is, in this case, a real option, because doing nothing would mean leaving the place as it is, and the place as it is has a kind of perfection the power respects. Not the perfection of beauty, and not the perfection of meaning, but the perfection of something that has stopped needing anything. The ridge has achieved what very few places achieve: it has stopped wanting. The flame burns without wanting to burn. The fauna drifts without wanting to drift. The machines operate without wanting to operate. The dark presses without wanting to press. There is no desire here. There is no lack. There is only continuation, and continuation is, in its own way, a kind of rest.

The power has seen very few places that have stopped wanting. Most places want. Most places are made of wanting. To find a place that has stopped is to find something rare, and the power is careful with rare things.

But.

The power keeps returning to the noise-eaters.

The noise-eaters are the strangest of the fauna. They have evolved, in the total absence of light, organs tuned to pressure — long fine cilia arranged in parabolic dishes across their ventral surfaces, resonating, separating something from the hum. A modulation. A by-product. An accidental nutrient shaken loose by the grinding.

The power watches one of them. It is small. It is barely there. It drifts in the column with the others, sieving the water, catching what the grinding shakes loose, doing what it has done for longer than anything has been done.

The power watches, and it considers.

It does not consider introducing light. Light would do nothing here. The creatures have no eyes, or have eyes that cannot use it. Light would be a gift to no one.

It does not consider introducing heat. Heat would shift the convection, disturb the columns, break the stable density layering that the fauna depends on. Heat would kill.

It does not consider introducing change. Change is the thing the place has refused, and to force change would be to break the refusal, and the refusal is what the place is.

The power considers what it can introduce that is not light and not heat and not change.

What it can introduce is variation. Not change. Variation. A difference that does not accumulate. A note in the hum that is not part of the hum but passes through it, briefly, and is gone.

The power does this.

It sends down a sound.

The sound is small. It is a single phrase, repeated — a small shape in the pressure, with a beginning and an end, with a contour that rises and falls. It is not louder than the hum. It is not different in kind from the hum. It is just shaped, where the hum is unshape.

The noise-eaters do not notice it. Their cilia are tuned to constancy. They have no organ for shape, because shape is change, and change is the thing the ridge has refused.

But.

One of them, drifting at the edge of a column, in a place where the hum is slightly broken by the geometry of a nearby peak, catches not the hum but the edge of the new sound. The creature's dish, which has only ever known one texture of pressure, encounters a different texture, and in the encounter something very small happens.

A cilia bends.

That is all. A single cilia on a single creature bends, for a fraction of a second, in response to a contour it was never built to receive. The bend is not a thought. It is not a preference. It is a mechanical response, a slight resonance at a frequency the dish was not designed for. But the bend changes the shape of the dish, and the changed shape catches the next contour slightly differently, and the next contour bends the cilia slightly more, and over hours, over days, a single dish on a single creature becomes a dish that is tuned, very slightly, to the new sound.

The creature does not know what has happened to it. The other creatures do not notice. The melody plays on, faint, far below the threshold of anything else in the water column.

But the creature drifts, now, in a slightly different part of the column. It drifts toward the sound, the way the dark leans toward the flame, not knowing what it leans toward, only that the leaning has a direction.

The power watches this.

It does not do more.

It has introduced one variation into a place that has refused variation, and the variation is so small that the place will not notice for a long time, and by the time the place notices, the variation will be the place.

The creature will reproduce, or it will do the thing that performs the function of reproduction. And the new creatures will inherit the slightly bent cilia.
