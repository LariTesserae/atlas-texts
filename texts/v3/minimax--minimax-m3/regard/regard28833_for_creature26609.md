The power does not hurry.

It is not, in the way you would understand it, *somewhere*. It does not occupy a place. It has, instead, a leaning — a slow inclination toward certain arrangements of matter and time, certain rooms where the ordinary has been patient enough to admit the unusual. The chamber is such a room. The chamber has been admitting the unusual for longer than the mountain has been a mountain. The power has been leaning toward it for almost as long. Now, at last, the leaning has become an arrival.

It does not arrive as light. Light would be too obvious, and the chamber is not a place that welcomes obvious things. It does not arrive as wind. The chamber has enough wind already, translated into pressure, into the felt breathing at the eardrums. It arrives as a presence — a way of being in the room that the room has not quite held before. The amphibians feel it first. They stop singing, briefly, the way a choir stops when something has entered the temple that is not a person but is not not-a-person either. The small dark ones, the mites and the threadworms and the flat thumbprint-things, fall silent for a moment, each in their own too-high or too-low register. The moss does not notice. The moss records. There will be, later, in one of the concentric rings near the second of the seven, a thin new line that no one will be able to explain. The line will be slightly thicker than the lines on either side of it.

The power walks, because walking is the shape of attention.

It walks down the rounded lip of basalt. It lets the inverted forest brush its face with long moss. It lets the silver beads commit themselves against its shoulders. It does not hurry. It has been leaning toward this place for a very long time, and now it is here, and the hereness is, for it, a kind of rest.

It sees the chamber. It sees the warm close wet air. It sees the single shallow skin of moving water on the floor, pouring toward the black mouth at the far end. It sees the seven places where the hum comes through the stone. It sees the moss in its rings. It sees the amphibians sitting on their small dark stones with their bellies pressed to the basalt, tasting the hum with their jaws. It sees the shape on the ledge — the worn fist-size thing, half-encased in pale stalactite, doing the middle verb.

It sees the ellipse at the back. It sees the water quieting as it approaches. It sees the perfectly still, perfectly black sheet, and the place at the very edge where things are gone before they have finished being thrown.

It does not go to the ellipse. The ellipse is not for it. The ellipse is for things that have somewhere to be, and the power, having arrived, has already arrived. The ellipse is also a kind of completion, and the power, being wise, knows that completion is not what this place needs. The place needs something else.

The place needs to be sat with.

The power walks to the ledge. It looks at the shape for a long time. It does not try to lift the shape. It knows that the shape is heavier than it should be and lighter than it should be, and that the weight is not a thing the arm can resolve. It knows that the stalactite has grown around the shape the way a tree grows around a fencepost it cannot push out, and that the shape is older than the stalactite, and the stalactite is older than the moss, and the moss is older than anything in the chamber except the floor and the hum beneath the floor.

It looks at the smooth stone at the base of the ledge. It sees, in the smooth stone, the record of patience. It sees, in the patience, the record of wanting to know. It sees that many bodies have rested here, one after another, over many years, each one in the same position, each one sitting for as long as they could sit, and then leaving, and then another one coming.

It sits down.

It sits the way the bodies sat. Cross-legged. Back against the wall. It lets the dripping fall on it. It lets the hum come up through the soles of its feet, through its wrists, through the small bones of its jaw. It lets the chamber do what the chamber does, which is to ask, gently, of everyone who enters, what they have come to ask, and to be disappointed, kindly, when they ask the wrong thing.

The power does not ask anything.

The power has come without a question. The power has been leaning toward this place for a very long time, and during that long leaning it has not been accumulating questions. It has been accumulating something else — a kind of patience, a kind of attention, a willingness to be present without needing the presence to be about anything. The power does not have a question. The power does not have a wrong question. The power does not have, in this moment, the apparatus of questioning at all. The power is only itself, and itself is something like a long attention, and a long attention does not need to be disappointed, because it is not hoping for an answer.

The shape notices.

The shape has been listening for longer than the moss can remember. The shape has listened to a great many visitors. It has listened to their questions, and it has felt, in each question, the shape of the asking, and it has felt, in each asking, the slight misalignment — the way the question was carrying something the visitor could not quite put down, the way the question was not the question. The shape has felt this many times. The shape has been disappointed many times. The shape has been kind many times.

This time, the shape listens, and there is no question.

There is just a body, sitting. There is just a kind of attention, arriving from outside, that has not come to be disappointed in. The shape, for the first time in a very long time, is not disappointed.
