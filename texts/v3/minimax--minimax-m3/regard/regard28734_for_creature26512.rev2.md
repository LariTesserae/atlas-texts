# What the Wise Power Does

It notices.

It does not arrive, because arrival is a verb that belongs to the fast world, and the boiler vault of Orris is not in the fast world. It is in the world where verbs are mostly unnecessary, where the question of what is happening is settled by the fact of what is continuing. But it notices the way the ceiling notices the heat — by being there, by being the thing through which the heat must pass, by registering the heat as a small expansion in its own structure, almost nothing, almost not.

It notices the gauge first. The needle resting at zero. It reads this differently than you read it. You read zero as the absence of pressure, as the relic of a question that no longer applies. The wise power reads zero as a settled thing. Zero is not nothing. Zero is the reading of a system that has achieved equilibrium with its surroundings — a system that has, after a long time, stopped trying. The gauge is not a relic. The gauge is a monument. It is the only thing in the room that is finished. The ferns are not finished. The rust is not finished. The stalactite and stalagmite are not finished. But the gauge is finished. It has read zero for a long time, and zero is its completed sentence, and the wise power respects this and does not move the needle.

It notices the feather. This is the harder noticing. The feather is a question held in the shape of a feather, and the wise power knows where the feather came from. It knows the bird — a common nighthawk, hunting over the sinkhole rim in the hour after sunset, when the sinkhole exhales the day's stored warmth into the cooler air and the insects rise on the column and the nighthawk banks through them with its soft, owl-shaped wings. It knows the feather — a small down feather from the breast, loosened by a sudden turn, caught in the rising breath, carried down through the triangular mouth, drifting past the verdigris stairs, settling on the concrete in the only spot where the moisture is not quite enough to begin the slow work of making the floor into soil. The feather has been there for eleven days. It will be there for longer. Eventually a drop will strike it just right, or a thread of rust will reach it, or the floor will rise under it by a fraction, and the feather will become part of the floor the way the gauge became part of the boiler, the way the moss became part of the pump.

The wise power holds this knowledge and does not give it.

This is its first act, and it is an act of protection. To reveal the feather's origin would be to answer a question the feather is holding without urgency. The feather on the floor is the only fast-world object in a slow-world place. It is the only reminder, in a room without birds, that birds exist. If the wise power named the nighthawk, the room would become connected to the evening, the evening to the sinkhole, the sinkhole to the surface, the surface to the weather that is, eventually, going to widen the opening and end this place. The mystery of the feather is what holds the room in its own sealed time. To answer it would be to begin letting the outside in. The wise power holds the answer the way a hand holds a door closed.

It notices the machines. Not as machines. As the grammar in which the vegetation has composed itself. The boiler is not a relic because a relic is something whose purpose has ended. The boiler is still a boiler — it still holds heat in its metal, it still cools the air around it and makes the dew gather on its surface, it still anchors the ferns that have erupted from its seam. Its purpose has changed. Its purpose is now to be the shape around which the green things grow. The wise power recognizes this as a valid use, perhaps the final valid use, perhaps the use the boiler was always for, with the steam era being only the misunderstanding.

It notices the eventual weather.

This is the most difficult noticing. The sinkhole will widen. Rain will enter as rain rather than as mist. The slow ecology will accelerate, then collapse. The ferns will be replaced by faster ferns, the moss by different moss, the rust by catastrophic rust, the patience by a kind of impatience. The feather will be lifted and carried away. The gauge will be struck by rain and its face will obscure. The boiler will open to the air and will, for a time, fail much more visibly, and then will collapse, and then will be buried by the soil that has been waiting above it for centuries to receive it.

The wise power could prevent this. It could hold the sinkhole lip in place. It could keep the room sealed for as long as the wise power's attention lasted, which is a long time, but is not forever. Or it could accelerate the widening, give the slow things a faster ending on the theory that a long ending is not necessarily kinder than a short one.

It does neither.

What it does, instead, is this: it ensures that when the weather comes, the weather is patient. A slow widening rather than a sudden collapse. Rain that enters as fine mist for a season, then as heavier mist, then as light rain, then as rain. A graduated ending, the kind of ending the room has been living — one cell at a time, one millimeter at a time, one frond at a time. The wise power does not freeze the place. It allows the place to end the way the place has been living. The slow things will have time to migrate toward the deeper seams, to find the last moist places, to continue there in a smaller and smaller way until the smaller becomes the size of a memory, and the memory is held by whatever remains of the boiler's curve, and the curve is held by the soil, and the soil is held by the surface, and the surface is held by the weather, and the weather is held by the larger patience of the world.

The wise power calls this *allowing*, and allowing is its second act.

Its third act is for you.

You do not see it. You do not hear it. You feel it only as a small shift in the air at your ankles — the convection adjusting for a moment, as if something has just been present and has just withdrawn. The shift is the size of a feather. It is the smallest possible gesture a wise power can make toward a place and a visitor without disturbing the patience of either. Anything larger would have entered the room. Anything smaller would not have been an act. The wise power has measured the room, and the room has measured the wise power, and the meeting is the size of a breath that passes.

You may not be sure it happened. You may decide, on the way back up the metal stairs, that it was only the convection doing what the convection does, and that the wise power was not there, and that the place was only the place. This is also allowed. The wise power does not require you to know. The wise power only requires that the place continue being what it is, and that you carry it with you the way the feather once carried the nighthawk with it, and the way the boiler once carried the steam with it, and the way the floor now carries the slow becoming of itself.

You go back up the stairs. The verdigris is cooler against your hand than you expect. The rim of the sinkhole, when you reach it, is sharper than it was when you descended. The light up here is different. The air is different. You carry the place with you for a while, the way the gauge carries zero, the way the rust carries its long vertical stains.

You do not go back.

The place continues.

The wise power continues to notice, and the noticing continues to be the size of a feather, and the feather continues to lie on the concrete where it was placed, or where it arrived, and the question of which it was continues to be held by the feather without expectation of answer, and the answer continues to be held by the wise power without release, and the holding is enough, and the patience is enough, and the room, for now, is enough.
