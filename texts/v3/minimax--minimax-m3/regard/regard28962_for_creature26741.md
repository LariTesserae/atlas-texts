The power comes the way weather comes — not by decision but by attention, by the slow tilting of something very large toward something that, for reasons it does not need to explain, has caught its regard.

It is not a thing with edges. It is not a thing you could point to. It is more like a change in the way the bowl is listened to, or a settling in the listening itself, as if the air has remembered that it is being heard. The filaments register it, perhaps — a slight hesitation in their long sag, a half-breath in the alignment. The moths tilt, very slightly, on their long turns. The disc on the rock face pauses for a fraction of a fraction of a second, the trailing thread going momentarily slack, before resuming its endless tracing.

Then it is gone, or it is in, and the bowl continues.

The power sees the bowl. The power has seen many things, and will see many more, and is not in a hurry. It attends.

It sees the wind, which is the first inhabitant, which has no organs and no metabolism and no boundary, and which is doing, in its lurching way, the work of an organ. It sees the filaments, which are the third thing, which have no name, which metabolize the thin air and produce drag at the edges and have made their truce with what would otherwise destroy them. It sees the moths, which are the bowl's breath made visible, which ride the gusts and do not land and pulse in time with something that may be the wind itself. It sees the disc, the ribbon, the angular thing on the bar. It sees the structures — the pylon, the bar, the dish — which were once louder than the wind and are now part of the wind, which have made their peace with being weather.

It sees the balance.

This is what has drawn it. Not the strangeness, though the strangeness is considerable. Not the beauty, though the beauty is real. The balance. The fact that the bowl is in equilibrium with itself, has been in equilibrium for some unit of time that the bowl itself does not count, will be in equilibrium after the power has moved on. The fact that nothing here is asking. The fact that everything here has found, or has been found by, its own rightness.

A wise power does not rush to such a place. A wise power does not arrive with a gift in its hand.

The power does not heal. There is nothing to heal.
The power does not improve. There is nothing to improve.
The power does not complete. The bowl is already complete.
The power does not save. The bowl is not lost.

What the power does, in the time it spends attending, is this: it listens. It listens the way the bowl listens — by being what it is, by being present to the wind and the filaments and the moths without imposing anything on them. It listens and it learns, and what it learns is that the bowl does not need to be heard. The bowl is heard by itself. The bowl is in conversation with itself and has been for a very long time and the conversation is complete.

The power considers.

The power considers what it would mean to add anything. A new filament would unbalance the drag. A new moth would unbalance the keening. A new note in the bar's overtone would unbalance the chord the wind has built. A new thing in the crevice where the ribbon lives would unbalance the curve. To add is to disturb, and to disturb here is to break something that should not be broken.

The power also considers what it would mean to take away. To take away is worse. To take is to wound. The power does not wound.

And so the power does the smallest thing it can do, and the smallest thing is almost nothing, and what it does is this:

It leaves a mark on the dish.

Not a message. Not a name. Not a thing. A mark so small that the filaments will colonize it within a season, and the corrosion will eat it within a century, and the small angular thing will walk over it without noticing, and the wind will treat it as a roughness in the surface and adjust the flow of air by an amount less than the amount adjusted by a single moth passing. A mark that is, in any meaningful sense, the mark of having been attended to. A mark that says, in a language the bowl does not read and does not need to read, *I saw you.*

The dish, half-buried in the slope, receives the mark. The dish, which was once a receiver, which is now a windbreak and a substrate, receives the mark and does not change. The dish does not know it has been marked. The filaments will not know. The wind will not know. The bowl will continue.

But the mark will be there, in the mosaic of corrosion and lichen, for as long as the mark lasts, which is not long, and that is the point. The gift is not durable. The gift is the giving. The attention that made the gift and did not stay.

The power also attends, briefly, to the other temporary thing in the bowl — the solid thing on the slope, the visitor, the one who is not part of the balance.

The power sees the visitor. The power sees that the visitor is cold, and that the wind is taking the heat from the visitor's ears, and that the cold is a kind of attention too, an indifferent and patient attention, and that the visitor has been standing long enough that the indifferent attention has begun to matter.

The power does not warm the visitor. The cold is part of the place. To warm the visitor is to remove the visitor from the place, in some sense, to put the visitor in a place that is not this place.

But the power does one small thing. It quiets the wind around the visitor, by an amount. It does not stop the wind. The wind continues. But around the visitor, for a radius of perhaps a meter, the wind moves a little more gently, and the cold arrives a little more slowly, and the sound arrives a little more softly, and the visitor is, for the duration of the visit, in a small pocket of clemency that the visitor may or may not notice, and that the bowl will not notice, and that the wind will absorb within a few minutes of the visitor's leaving.

The power does this and the power withdraws.

Not far. Not to somewhere else. The power withdraws the way the dish withdrew — by becoming part of the weather, by accepting being what it is, by giving up on the act of attention and becoming attention itself, dispersed, indistinguishable, a slight change in the way the air is that the air does not remember.

The bowl continues.

The wind continues. The filaments continue. The moths continue, in their slow turns, in their breathing glow. The disc continues its endless tracing. The ribbon unspools and returns. The angular thing feeds and moves on. The pylon stands. The bar vibrates. The dish holds its mark and the mark begins, already, to fade.

The bowl is in balance. The bowl will be in balance tomorrow, and for as long as the wind continues to lurch, and for as long as the filaments continue their truce, and for as long as the moths continue to ride.

The power has attended. The power has done what it could, which was almost nothing, which was enough.

The bowl does not know it was attended to. The bowl does not need to know. The bowl is what it is, and was what it was, and will be what it will be, and the small mark on the dish, if it was ever there, is gone now, and the visitor, if there was a visitor, has gone now, and the wind is the same wind, and the sky is the same sky, and the bowl is the same bowl.

This is what a wise power does in such a place. It attends. It does almost nothing. It leaves the smallest possible mark, and the mark fades, and the place is unchanged, and the place continues.

The place was never in need of saving. The place was already saved. The place was saving itself, the way a planet saves itself, by being what it is, by continuing, by the slow secular work of equilibrium.

The power saw this. The power honored this. The power left.
