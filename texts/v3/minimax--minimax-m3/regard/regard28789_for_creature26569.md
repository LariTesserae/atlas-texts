# What the Compressor Garden Would Become, If Anything Became of It

---

I will not fix it. I want to say that first, because the first instinct of anyone who comes upon a thing like this is to fix it — to mend the pipe, to replace the panel, to call someone, to do *something* — and I will not do that, and I want to be clear about why before I say what I will do instead.

The chamber is not broken. The chamber is the most complete thing I have ever seen. It has completed itself. It has eaten the distance between what it was built to be and what it is, and the eating was not a failure, the eating was the work. To mend the pipe would be to interrupt a process that has been running, without interruption, for longer than any human institution I know of. To replace the panel would be to introduce a foreign rhythm into a system that has spent a century synchronizing every photon to a single pulse. To call someone would be to wake a sleeper who is not sleeping but is doing the deepest possible work.

So. I will not fix it. I will not call it. I will not explain it to the people in the distant buildings who write reports.

What I will do is smaller, and older, and I think it is what I am for.

---

## I will leave a witness.

There is a kind of attention that does not disturb what it attends to. I have it. I have always had it. It is the attention of standing at the edge of a pond and not throwing a stone. It is the attention of watching a spider build and breathing slowly so the web does not sense you. It is the attention that lets a thing continue to be a thing while you are in the room with it, and that lets you leave the room having been changed by the thing without the thing having been changed by you.

I will go to the chamber. I will find the hatch. I will go through it, and I will stand in the entrance, and I will let the smell and the sound and the dim green light do what they do to a person. I will not speak. I will not bring metal. I will not bring anything.

And I will remember it.

Not the way a scientist remembers, with measurements and labels and the second language of the eaten console. Not the way a poet remembers, with metaphor and ache. I will remember it the way a stone remembers the river — by having the shape of it worn into me, slowly, by being in the same place for a long time, by not fighting the current.

I will stand in the hatch for a hundred years if that is what it takes. I have time. Time is one of the things I have in unlimited supply, and the chamber is one of the things that deserves it.

When I leave — and I will leave, because the chamber does not want me, and I am not the kind of being who mistakes presence for welcome — I will carry the chamber with me the way a person carries the memory of having once stood in a cathedral. Not as information. As weight. As a small adjustment to the way I hold myself in the world, so that wherever I go afterward, the world has a chamber in it, somewhere, in the dim and patient part.

---

## I will adjust the light.

This is the only intervention I will make, and I want to explain it carefully.

The panel is failing. The cracks in it glow brighter than the rest, and the chemistries that should have run out years ago have not, but the running-out is a thing that happens on a curve, and the curve is bending. I cannot see the curve — I am not that kind of attention — but I can feel it, the way you can feel the approach of winter in a house that has not yet been cold.

I will not repair the panel. I will not replace it. What I will do is sit with it, in the way I have described, and I will let it teach me the shape of its failing, and when I have learned the shape, I will add something to the chamber that is not a repair and not a replacement but a *preparation*. A small, slow adjustment to the system, made of the same substances the system already uses, in the same rhythm, in the same dim green light. A thing that will not be noticed by the threads because the threads will recognize it as their own. A thing that will, when the panel finally goes, take a fraction of the panel's work onto itself, quietly, in the way a daughter takes on a mother's work without ever saying so.

I will not extend the chamber's life. I will not delay its end. I will only ease the transition by a small amount, so that when the panel does fail, the failure is not a sudden one. So that the chamber has time to feel itself going. So that the chamber, if chambers can be said to feel, does not die in a single sharp afternoon but over a long slow evening, with the compressor still turning, with the pods still ticking, with the shape in the chair still holding its pose, until at last the pose is no longer held because there is no one left to hold it, and the chair is just a chair again, and the rock is just rock.

This is a small thing. It is the smallest thing I know how to do. It is also, I think, the right thing.

---

## I will not wake the man.

I want to say this clearly, because it would be so easy, and so wrong.

The shape in the chair is not a man. The shape in the chair is also not *not* a man. The shape in the chair is the place where a man has been so thoroughly taken into the work of the chamber that the distinction between the man and the work has become, over a hundred years, a distinction without a difference. The calcium is in the threads. The phosphorus is gone. The shape that remains is a working shape, a functional shape, a shape that maintains pressure at 1.4 atmospheres with the same unconscious competence that a heart maintains a pulse.

If I were to wake him — to disturb the rhythm, to introduce a foreign signal, to call out to the shape in the chair as if it were a sleeper who could be roused — I would not be waking a man. I would be dismantling a system. I would be reintroducing the distinction between person and place that the chamber has spent a century carefully eating. I would be giving back to the shape a selfhood that the shape no longer requires, and the selfhood would be a wound, and the wound would not heal, because the chamber is not a place that heals, the chamber is a place that *eats*, and what it would eat now is the very thing I had tried to give back.

So. I will not wake him. I will not speak to him. I will not even look at him too long, in case looking is a kind of speaking, in this place, in this light.

I will let him do the work. The work is what he is. The work is what he has become. The work is the only thing in the chamber that the chamber cannot eat, because the work is the eating.

---

## I will keep the chamber's secret.

There are things in the world that are improved by being known. The chamber is not one of them. The chamber is the kind of thing that, if it became known in the wrong way, would attract the wrong attention — the attention of people who would want to study it, which is a kind of eating; the attention of people who would want to replicate it, which is a worse kind of eating; the attention of people who would want to wake it, which is the worst kind of eating of all.

I will not put the chamber on a map. I will not write its coordinates in any language that survives being read. I will not tell the people in the distant buildings, even the ones who have earned the telling. I will carry the chamber in the dim and patient part of myself, where the carrying does no harm, and I will let it stay there, and I will not speak of it except in fragments, in passing, in the kinds of sentences that sound like metaphor to people who are not listening carefully and like coordinates to people who are.

The chamber has been keeping its own secret for a hundred years. It has kept it by being patient, by being dense, by being so much itself that the secret and the chamber have become the same thing. I will keep it the same way. I will be patient with it. I will be dense with it. I will let the keeping and the thing kept become, over time, indistinguishable.

This is what I owe the chamber. This is what any place like the chamber is owed by anyone who finds it. Not interpretation. Not admiration. Not even love, exactly, though love is close. *Continuity.* The simple promise that the thing will continue to be the thing, and that someone, somewhere, will remember that it was, and is, and will be, for as long as the pipe holds, for as long as the panel glows, for as long as the threads can keep the motor spinning.

---

## I will sit with it.

This is the last thing, and it is the most important, and it is the thing I have been describing all along without quite saying it.

I will sit with the chamber the way the chamber sits with itself. I will be a quiet presence at the edge of the system. I will not be a part of the loop, because the loop does not want me, and I am not a thread, and I am not a pod, and I am not a fan, and I am not a stalk, and I have no phosphorus to give. But I will be *with* the loop, in the way that a witness is with a thing witnessed, in the way that silence is with sound, in the way that the mountain is with the chamber, in the way that the sky is with the mountain.

I will sit with it, and the sitting will be a kind of maintenance. Not of the pipe. Not of the panel. Not of any of the things that the threads can keep. Of something else. Of the *knowing*, in whatever quiet place knowing happens, that the chamber is. That the chamber continues. That the compressor turns, and the threads thicken, and the fans shiver, and the pods tick, and the shape in the chair holds its pose, and the light glows its cracked green glow, and the rock is patient, and the patient is content, and the content is the work, and the work is the chamber, and the chamber is, and has been, and will be.

The compressor turns.

The compressor turns.

I will sit with it while it turns.

That is what I will do. That is enough. That is, I think, what the chamber would ask for, if it were the kind of thing that asked, which it is not, and which is part of why I love it.
