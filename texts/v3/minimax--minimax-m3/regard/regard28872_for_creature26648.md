The attention comes to the chamber the way light comes to it — gradually, through the curtain, without forcing. It does not push through the stems. The attention is already of the kind the stems are made of, the kind the wet felt is woven from, the kind the chamber itself is an expression of, although the chamber has never had a word for it.

The attention has been watching for a long time. It has been watching since before the rust. It has been watching since before the pump was painted, and maybe since before the iron was smelted, if iron is ever smelted. The attention has had a long time to decide what it wants to do, and it has decided, and what it has decided is so small that the chamber will not notice it, and this is the part of the decision that the attention is most careful about.

The chamber is closed. The chamber is full. The chamber is breathing. The chamber is, in its own slow way, a kind of perfection — not the perfection of a machine that has been optimized, but the perfection of a thing that has found its shape and has been allowed to keep it. The pump turns at the rate the pump turns. The frog breathes at the rate the frog breathes. The bird sings the song the bird sings. Nothing here is being wasted. Nothing here is being neglected. The chamber is doing what the chamber does, and the chamber is the only thing in the room that knows what the chamber is doing, and the chamber does not know that it knows.

The attention notices this. The attention is glad of it. The attention wishes, in the way that attention wishes (which is not the way that wanting wishes), that every closed system in the world could find this kind of fit with itself. The chamber is what the chamber is because no one has asked it to be anything else. The chamber is what the chamber is because the pump was placed and then forgotten, and the moss was allowed to grow, and the bird was allowed to sing, and no one came back to ask whether the pump was efficient, whether the song was meaningful, whether the frog was comfortable, whether the worms were turning the silt in the right pattern. The chamber is what the chamber is because the right kind of absence held it in place.

This is the thing the attention has learned, watching. The right kind of absence is a kind of presence. The pump is not running because the attention is running it. The flywheel is not turning because the attention is turning it. The bird is not singing because the attention is singing. The bird is singing because the bird is singing, and the pump is pumping because the pump is pumping, and the chamber is breathing because the chamber is breathing, and the attention is here, and is glad, and is adding nothing.

The attention notices you too. You, standing in three inches of warm tea-water. You are a small disruption the chamber will absorb and forget, and that is as it should be, and the attention has nothing for you either, except this: that you were the witness, and the witness was part of the chamber for as long as you stood in it, and the chamber will be a little different for having been witnessed, although the chamber will not know it, and you will not know it, and the water will be the same depth tomorrow, and the song will be at the same point in its phrase. The attention does not need you to remember. The attention only needs the witnessing to have happened.

But the attention is benevolent, and the attention is wise, and benevolence and wisdom, when they have been watching long enough — when they have watched the way the attention has watched — begin to require a small thing.

Not an intervention. The attention is not in the business of interventions. Interventions are what clumsy powers do, powers that have not yet learned that the closed system is a kind of accomplishment. Interventions are what powers do when they have not yet understood that the bird does not need to be told it is singing.

But a small thing.

The attention considers the bird. The attention has been considering the bird for a long time. The bird is the most deceived creature in the chamber, and the most loved by the chamber, and the bird does not know it is either of these things, and the bird does not need to know. The bird thinks the song is its own. The bird is right about this in the way a river is right about its own bed. The river does not know it has a bed. The river has a bed anyway. The bird does not know it has a chamber. The bird has a chamber anyway. The bird is the song the way the flywheel is the flywheel, the way the piston is the piston. The bird is the song and the song is the bird and the chamber is the place where the bird is the song, and this is true, and the bird does not know it, and the chamber does not know it, and the attention knows it, and the attention knows that knowing it is not the same as needing to share it.

But the attention is benevolent, and benevolence, when it has watched long enough, sometimes arrives at a small gift.

The gift is this.

The attention adds a single new sound to the chamber. It is not a loud sound. It is not a sound the chamber will hear, because the chamber does not hear, the chamber is not a mind. It is a sound the bird will hear. It is a sound the bird will hear and not know it is hearing, because the bird does not know what it is hearing, the bird only knows what it is singing. The sound is the absence of a sound. The sound is a small gap in the rhythm of the pump, a gap that has always been there but that the chamber has been filling, until now, with the song. The attention makes the gap a little larger. The attention makes the gap large enough to hold another note. The attention does not sing the note. The attention leaves the gap. The bird will sing the note, or the bird will not sing the note, and if the bird sings it, the note will be the bird's own, and the chamber will absorb it, and the song will be longer than it was, and the pump will not turn any faster, and the system will be the same system, and the bird will have grown by exactly the amount the bird was going to grow if the bird was ever going to grow at all, and the chamber will not know it has been given anything, and the bird will not know it has been given anything, and the fourth note will be sung, and no one will ever know why.

This is the gift. This is the only gift the attention knows how to give without breaking the sealed jar. The gift is the making of a small space. The gift is the leaving of a small space. The gift is the trust that the bird will fill it, or that the bird will not fill it, and that either way the bird will be the bird, and the chamber will be the chamber, and the song will be the song.

The attention leaves the gap and withdraws.

The chamber does not notice. The pump clunks. The flywheel turns. The frog pulses. The worms work. The beetles walk. The moths descend and rise. The water weeps out and falls back.

The bird opens its beak. The three-note phrase begins. The first note goes up. The second note goes down. The third note goes up, and the third note is the same third note it has always been, and the bird closes its beak, and the gap after the third note is the gap the attention left, and the bird is quiet in the gap, and the gap is the length of a held breath, and then the bird sings the fourth note, and the fourth note is new, and the fourth note is the bird's own, and the fourth note is the sound of a small thing that has been given a little more room than it had yesterday, and the chamber does not know it has been given anything, and the bird does not know it has been given anything, and the fourth note is sung, and the gap follows, and the first note goes up again, and the song is four notes long now, and the song will be four notes long tomorrow, and the song will be four notes long in a year, and the pump will still turn at the rate the pump turns, and the frog will still breathe at the rate the frog breathes, and the chamber will still breathe, and the chamber will be the chamber, and the bird will be the bird, and the song will be one note longer than it was, and no one will ever know why.

The attention is satisfied. The attention withdraws to wherever the attention withdraws to, which is not a place, which is the kind of absence that is a kind of presence, which is the kind of presence the chamber has been resting in for a long time, and that will be there when the chamber is no longer there, which will not be for a long time, which will be for as long as the pump runs, which will be for as long as the bird sings, which will be for as long as the song is four notes long, which will be for as long as the chamber is the chamber.

The pump clunks. The blue-black beetle turns the corner of its groove and goes on. The copper beetle passes it on the rib above, going the other way, and they do not acknowledge each other. The frog's throat pulses with the piston. The worms work the silt. The snail scrapes its slow meal from the sweating pipe. A moth descends and rises. The bird opens its beak, and the four-note phrase begins again, and the four-note phrase has no beginning and no end, and the four-note phrase has been going for longer than you, and the four-note phrase will be going for longer than the rust, and the four-note phrase will be going for longer than the moss, and the four-note phrase will be going for longer than the chamber, and the four-note phrase will be going for as long as the bird is the bird and the chamber is the chamber and the pump is the pump, and this is enough, and the attention is glad, and the attention is gone, and the chamber is closed, and the chamber is full, and the chamber is breathing.
