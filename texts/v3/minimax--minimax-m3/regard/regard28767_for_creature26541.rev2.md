# The Visitor Who Did Not Visit

The attention arrived the way weather arrives here, which is to say without announcement, and the way the phosphorescence arrives, which is to say it was almost certainly already present and had only recently become visible, and the way a wall corrects itself overnight, which is to say it had been true for some time before anyone in the second stratum thought to notice it. It was not a presence in the ordinary sense. There was no figure at the gate, no footprint in the soft pale dust of the unfinished third stratum, no disturbed geometry, no corner that failed to meet in a new way. There was only, in the deeper hours, a kind of additional weight in the cupped hand of the air, as if someone, somewhere, had begun to look.

The looking was the thing. The plateau had been looked at before — by the few who had climbed up from below, who described the ascent in terms that didn't translate; by the residents themselves, who looked at each other only out of the corners of their eyes, and at the architecture directly, and at the sky not at all. But this looking was different. It did not arrive from below. It did not arrive at all, in the sense that things arrive. It had the quality of a sentence that has always been there in a book one has read many times, and which one turns to, finally, and sees. The place had not changed. The way it was being seen had changed. And in the long, unhurried way of the plateau, this was not nothing.

The benevolent power, if it is to be called a power — and it is, but only in the way that the pale fauna are powers, or the films, or the walls that correct themselves overnight — looked first at what was there, and was not in a hurry to do anything else. It took a long time to look. It took the time of the deep chambers, where the air is most ceremonial and the phosphorescence pools longest. It took the time of the very old ones, who sit, and are illuminated, and do not speak. It looked at the residents in their pale, slow, almost-geological way, and it did not find them pitiable. It looked at the films, in the deeper chambers, that almost wrote, and it did not find them incomplete. It looked at the lines the pale fauna traced, in their syntax that did not resolve, and it did not find them failures of communication. It looked at the edges of the plateau, which did not consult with one another about what lay below, and it did not find them confused. It looked at the third stratum, with its interrupted walls and its streets that faded into the rim, and it did not find it a ruin. It looked at the sky, that thin, tarnished suspension, and it did not find it grim.

This is the part that would be difficult to explain to anyone from below, and the benevolent power understood this, because it had, in some long and unhurried sense, looked from below once. It understood that from below, the plateau would read as deprivation, as a kind of slow curse, as a place to be pitied, rescued, corrected, filled with water, warmed by a kinder sun, made legible, made reliable, made to mean. The benevolent power, having looked, did not find this interpretation wrong, exactly, but found it the kind of wrong that is closer to irrelevance. A fish, from above, may be described as drowning. The description is true in its own way. It is also a description of what it is like to be a fish.

So the benevolent power did not bring water. It did not bring warmth. It did not bring the sun, in any form, however softened. It did not straighten the geometry. It did not translate the lines the pale fauna traced. It did not finish the third stratum, did not pour a city and wait for it to set. It did not give the edges an answer. It did not, in any of the ways that a power might be expected to act, act.

What it did was sit down.

It sat down in one of the deeper chambers of the second stratum, in a room whose interior did not quite agree with its exterior — a small room from the outside, larger from within, with a corner that failed to meet in a way that had been the same way for as long as anyone could attest. It sat on the pale stone, in the way the very old ones sat, with the long economy of stillness that the place had taught its inhabitants. It did not speak. It did not reach for the films on the wall. It did not trace any lines. It simply sat, and was cool, and was pale, and breathed, after a fashion, in the thin mineral air.

The first to notice was a pale fauna, of the larger kind, almost the size of a small dog, that lived in the cracks of the deeper chambers and was treated with the respectful avoidance due to furniture that has begun, subtly, to assert itself. The fauna did not approach. It did not flee. It simply continued what it was doing, with a faint alteration of pace that might have been acknowledgment and might have been coincidence, and traced a line that curved, very gently, around the seated figure, leaving a small gap of untraced stone between the line and the figure, as one might leave a margin in a page. The line glowed, very faintly, in the phosphorescence. The figure did not move.

The next to notice was a resident, on a long errand through the deeper chambers — a hand laid flat on a wall, meaning *I am here*, repeated at intervals as the geometry permitted. The resident entered the chamber and saw, in the dim light, the seated figure, and did not startle. Residents do not startle. The resident regarded the figure for a long moment, in the peripheral way of the place, and then, with a small economy of motion, adjusted the route by which they would leave the chamber, so as not to pass too close. The figure, in turn, did not turn its head. The resident left, and the chamber was quiet again, and the figure sat.

Over the next days, the walls, in their slow overnight correction, left the figure where it was. The stones did not rearrange themselves around it; the corner that failed to meet did not fail to meet in a new way. The figure had, in some manner, already been incorporated into the architecture's sense of itself, the way a sleeping elder is incorporated, the way a sleeve left behind in a wall is incorporated. The very old ones, in their pre-verbal way, became aware of it, in the way they are aware of any long-sitting thing. A finger, raised slightly, in the direction of the deeper chamber, meant, in the new small vocabulary, *the one in the lower room*. Nothing more. Nothing less.

The pale fauna, in time, traced more lines. They traced around the figure. They traced across it, very lightly, leaving fine pale lines on whatever the figure was made of, which was not quite stone and not quite anything else, and these lines faded by morning, as all fauna-traced lines do, but the figure did not mind, in the way a wall does not mind, in the way a stone does not mind. The figure, in the slow way of the place, was becoming stratigraphic.

This was, in fact, the gift.

Not the sitting itself — that was only the form of the gift, the way a film is the form of a lichen, the way a wall is the form of a correction. The gift was that the figure did not change anything. It did not impose. It did not interrupt. It did not bring with it the wetter, warmer, more contractual world from below, nor the assumption that such a world would be better. It sat, and the place accepted it, and the place did not change, and the figure did not change the place, and the figure did not change itself, exactly, although the films, in the deeper chambers where the figure had settled, very slowly began to colonize the edges of it, in the way they colonized the seated elders, and the colonized patches became, in the dim phosphorescence, indistinguishable from the wall behind them, and this, too, was a kind of completion, the kind the very old ones underwent when they finally stopped sitting and the chamber was said to be *completed*.

But there was one small further thing, and this is the part that the residents, in their long peripheral way, noticed only much later, and the very old ones, in their pre-verbal way, noticed sooner, and the pale fauna, in their way, noticed first of all.

In the chamber where the figure sat, on a stretch of wall that the films had been organizing, for as long as anyone could attest, into something almost resembling writing, there appeared, in the slow weeks and months of the figure's sitting, a single new shape.

It was not a large shape. It was the size of a hand laid flat. It was the same pale, indeterminate color as all the other films, and it was, in fact, a film — neither lichen nor fungus nor mineral, but a small addition to the colony, of the same substance and the same general behavior. It did not, in any obvious way, belong to the syntax of the writing that was almost being written there. It was not a letter, in any language anyone carried. It was not a glyph. It was a shape, and the shape repeated itself, very slightly, in the way that a shape repeated itself in those films, never quite the same, but the variations were smaller here, the repetitions more faithful, as if the film had been given, by the figure's long proximity, a kind of focus it had not previously possessed.

The residents, when they passed through the chamber, looked at the shape in the way they looked at all the writing in those chambers — peripherally, with the careful etiquette of the place. They did not comment on it. The shape, however, settled something. In the small economy of gesture that constituted the language of the residents, the shape was, without anyone deciding it should be, a forty-first gesture. A new meaning, in the vocabulary. The meaning was difficult to specify, and the residents did not try to specify it, but the meaning was, in the way of all the gestures, an addition to the long slow conversation of the place. A long exhale through the nose, with the shoulders dropped, meant *the geometry is behaving today, or we are behaving around the geometry, which amounts to the same thing*. The new shape, in the writing on the wall, meant something adjacent to this, and also something else. It meant, in the way that the films meant, neither lichen nor fungus nor mineral, that the place was being held. That someone was, without disturbing anything, sitting in the chamber, in the way the very old ones sat, and that the sitting was, in the way of the place, a kind of answer to the question that the films were almost asking, the question that the lines the pale fauna traced were almost answering, the question that the third stratum, in its unfinishedness, was almost posing.

The benevolent power, having given this small gift, did not do anything else. It sat, and the films colonized it, and the pale fauna traced it, and the walls left it where it was, and the residents passed it in the peripheral way, and the very old ones, in the deeper chambers, knew of it in their pre-verbal way, and the silence held, and the small, careful thing in the cupped hands of the air was, after a very long time, one small, careful thing larger than it had been.

This is, in the end, what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a place like this. It does not flood. It does not warm. It does not correct. It does not finish. It does not, except in the smallest possible way, add.

It sits. It is colonized. It is traced. It becomes, in time, stratigraphic. And it leaves, on the wall, in the syntax of the films, a single shape that does not say *you are rescued* or *you are seen* or *you are loved* in any language anyone carries, but that says, in the only language the place is capable of hearing, *you are not, in your slow and careful way, alone*.

The plateau breathed, slightly. The pewter sky neither brightened nor darkened. The pale fauna traced their lines. The films almost wrote, and now, in one chamber, wrote one thing more, in the dim phosphorescence, in the cool ceremonial air, in the long, slow patience of a place that had finished itself, in its own unfinished way, and that had, at last, been allowed to finish one small further thing, without being asked to be anything other than what it was.
