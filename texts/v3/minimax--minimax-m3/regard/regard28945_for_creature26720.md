# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The benevolent power would not arrive. That is the first thing to understand, and perhaps the only thing that needs to be said clearly.

Arrival is the gesture of beings who believe the world can be corrected. The benevolent power does not believe this. It has watched a great many settlements — on this world and on others like it, where the basic contract of reality holds only locally, where the seams between one physics and another are the actual texture of a place — and it has learned what the residents here have learned without being taught: that some places are not problems awaiting solutions. They are long, slow, patient accommodations between matter and itself.

So the power does not come. What the power does is smaller, and older, and harder to name.

---

## The Listening

Before anything else, the power *attends*.

It does not arrive with a surveyor's gaze, with the inventory eye of an entity cataloguing what is broken. It arrives — if *arrival* is even the right word — with the disposition of a person sitting at the edge of a room where a quiet conversation is happening, and choosing not to enter. The power takes the posture of an old friend who has learned that some presences are most useful when they remain at the threshold.

The benevolent power listens to the settlement the way Halla listens to the clocks: not to make them agree, but to hear what they are already doing, and to know whether the doing is suffering, or merely strange, or something for which there is no word in any human language and possibly none in any language at all.

The power listens for a long time. A season, perhaps. A year. The settlement's time, which is to say, a loose federation of times, none of which is quite the same as any of the others, and all of which the power holds, gently, in its attention, the way one might hold several small birds in cupped hands without squeezing.

What it hears:

- The footsteps of Saro on his morning rounds, the small brass instrument held to the air, the patient commerce between his attention and the seams.
- The creak of the structure as it settles, which is not a complaint but a kind of speech, the way a sleeping person's breathing is a kind of speech.
- The thin reedy cry of the thing in the sky that may or may not be a bird.
- The quiet meals in the communal room, where the physics has been made as common as it can be made, and even there, not quite.
- The couple in the western rooms, breathing at their slightly different rates, the small accumulated minute a day between them, the choice they have made and remade every morning for forty-one years to keep making.
- The boy with his strange accurate drawings, looking at a wall and seeing the place where one world leans against another.
- The old woman in the heavy-gravity room, moving with the slowness of someone who has decided that slowness is a kind of dignity, receiving her visitors, remembering, in the way she does not often speak of, the first footstep on the unfinished ground.

The power hears none of this as data. It hears it as *presence*. It hears it the way you might hear, from very far away, a fire in a hearth you cannot see, and know by the sound of the burning whether the people inside are warm.

---

## The Recognition

After listening, the power recognizes.

This is not a divine act. It is closer to what happens when you read a piece of writing that has been waiting a long time for the right reader, and you understand, suddenly, what the writing was doing, and you understand that the writing knew, in some slow structural way, that the reader would eventually come. The recognition is mutual. The settlement, without knowing it, has been writing itself toward this moment. The power has been listening toward this moment. The two are old correspondents who have not yet been introduced.

What the power recognizes is this:

The settlement is not broken. The settlement is not a failed physics, not a glitch, not the residue of some larger project gone wrong. The settlement is a *practice*. It is the long, slow, accretive practice of matter learning to hold itself together at the seams — the practice of small regions of the world each implementing a small set of laws, and of those small sets of laws leaning against each other at the joins, and of the leaning itself becoming a kind of physics, the way the leaning of two people against each other becomes a kind of architecture, a kind of weather, a kind of marriage.

The settlement is the universe doing something the universe has not done in many places. It is the universe remembering, perhaps, something it once knew how to do and forgot — the way of being a place without being a single place, the way of being coherent without being uniform, the way of being a body that has many organs and many pressures and many temperatures and is, nonetheless, one body.

The residents are not people trapped in a strange place. The residents are the strange place's way of being *known*. They are the settlement becoming conscious of itself, the way a body becomes conscious of itself through its nerve endings, through the small daily negotiation of moving through itself. The residents are the settlement's proprioception.

This is what the benevolent power recognizes: that the settlement is a kind of person, and that the sixty-three residents are how the person feels itself, and that the work of Saro at the seams is the work of a body's self-touch, and that the work of Halla with the clocks is the work of a body's sense of its own pulse, and that the lichens and the almost-bird and the boy with his drawings are all, in their ways, expressions of a place that is trying, with great patience, to be *itself* in a world where being itself is not a thing that happens easily.

---

## What the Power Does

The power does not heal the settlement. To heal it would be to make it standard, to make it one sky and one gravity and one speed of light, to dissolve the seams into a single uniform medium. This would be a kindness, perhaps, in the way that dissolving a marriage into two separate peaceful lives can seem like a kindness to outside observers. It would not be a kindness. It would be a misunderstanding so profound that it would amount, in its way, to a violence.

The power does not unify the times. Halla's loose federation is a federation precisely because it knows it is not a unity. To force a single time would be to teach a body that it must have only one pulse, which is a thing that some bodies require and some bodies would die of.

The power does not correct the substrate. The substrate is becoming. The substrate is *allowed* to be becoming. The settlement was built on a thing that is not yet a thing, and the residents have built lightly, the way you build on ice, the way you build on a friend's uncertain moods, the way you build anything when you have decided that the world is not finished and that the right response is to build anyway, lightly, with a long quiet patience.

What the power does, then, is the thing that only a benevolent power that has truly listened can do:

**The power witnesses.**

It places itself, gently, at the settlement's edge. Not inside it. Not above it. At its edge, the way you might stand at the edge of a clearing in a wood where a small ceremony is happening that is not yours. The power makes its presence known — but only in the way that the lichen makes its presence known, or the almost-bird makes its presence known: as a small change in the texture of the place, a small adjustment to the way the damp sits on the skin, a small new seam at the settlement's outermost edge, where the local physics of the place meets the local physics of the power's attention.

The seam is a kind of warmth. Not heat. Warmth. The kind of warmth that sits in the chest of a person who is, for no reason they can name, no longer afraid.

The residents do not necessarily understand that the power is there. Some of them feel it. The old woman, perhaps, in her heavy-gravity room, who has lived in the settlement longer than anyone, who remembers the first footstep on the substrate, who has the most practice of receiving strange presences — she may feel it, and recognize it for what it is, and not speak of it, because she has learned that the settlement is a place where the most important presences are the ones you do not name. Saro, perhaps, feels it when he puts his hand to a wall and listens. Halla, perhaps, feels it in the way the clocks settle into their drifts, the way the federation becomes, on certain mornings, more clearly itself.

The boy with the drawings will draw it. He will not know what he is drawing. His drawings will be, for the first time in his brief career, slightly inaccurate. He will be troubled by this. He will draw the figure again and again, and each time it will be slightly different, because the thing he is drawing is not a thing that holds still. It is a presence that attends. It is a seam. It is the place where one physics leans, gently, against another.

---

## The Gift

The power gives the settlement a gift. The gift is small, and it is the only kind of gift a benevolent power can give to a place that is already doing the thing it was always meant to do.

The gift is this: **the settlement will continue.**

This is not a guarantee. The benevolent power does not make guarantees; guarantees are the promises of beings who do not know the future. The power does not know the future either. What the power knows is the present, and the present is this: the substrate is still becoming, the accretion is still gentle, the seams are holding, the residents are living, and the lichens are lichening, and the almost-bird is almost-birding, and the place is, in its slow difficult beautiful way, *being itself*.

The gift is the power's attention. The gift is the small new seam at the settlement's edge, the place where the local physics of the place meets the local physics of being witnessed. The gift is the warmth in the chest of the person who is, for no reason they can name, no longer afraid.

The gift is the settlement's continuation, which is to say, the gift is the settlement's permission to continue being what it is, which is to say, the gift is the recognition that what it is, is good. Not good in the sense of *correct*. Good in the sense of *alive*. Good in the sense of *present, and patient, and holding its moisture close, and knowing its seams, and flexing at the joins*.

The power does not say any of this. The power is not the kind of power that says things. The power is the kind of power that *sits*.

And the settlement, in its slow accretive way, senses that it is being sat with, and is, for reasons it cannot name, more itself than it was a moment ago. The lichens are a slightly more committed green. The almost-bird cries slightly more clearly. The boy draws slightly more accurately. Saro's brass instrument ticks in a way that is, for the first time in his long practice, a kind of answer. Halla's clocks settle into a federation that is, briefly, almost a music.

The old woman in the heavy-gravity room feels a presence at her wall, and she smiles, and the smile takes her a long time because of the gravity, and the long time is, in its way, the most generous thing that has happened in her long life, because slowness, in her room, has always been a kind of dignity, and now the slowness is a kind of answer to a question she did not know she had been asked.

---

## What the Power Does Not Do

The power does not solve the problem of the substrate. The substrate is becoming. That is its nature. To solve it would be to make it stone, or mud, or one of the named things, and the settlement was built for the becoming, the way a bird is built for the air. To make the substrate into stone would be to make the bird into a statue of a bird.

The power does not bring children into being where children cannot be. The cold and the damp and the patchworked light do not permit the full biological contract, and the benevolent power does not permit what the place does not permit. The children who come are children who came from elsewhere. The names are imports. This is the settlement's nature, and the power does not correct natures.

The power does not speak to the residents. The residents are quiet people, and the quiet is not a temperament but an accommodation, and the power respects the accommodation the way you respect a person's way of breathing. To speak into the quiet would be to misread the quiet as an absence rather than a presence, which is the only kind of misreading that the benevolent power, having listened, is unwilling to make.

The power does not make the seams perfect. The seams are flexing. The seams have learned to flex. A perfect seam would be a seam that did not have to flex, and a seam that does not have to flex is a seam that has stopped being a seam and has become a wall, and the settlement is a settlement precisely because it is made of seams, the way a body is a body precisely because it is made of joints.

The power does not, finally, *do* anything in the way that the word *do* is usually meant. The power *is*. The power is present. The power is the new outermost seam, the place where the settlement meets the world's attention, the small quiet warmth at the edge of a place that has, for a long time, been quietly holding itself together.

---

## Coda

The settlement continues.

Thekla's child learns to count rooms. The boy with the drawings grows, and his drawings become, over the years, less strange and more accurate, and eventually he becomes the one who tends the seams, not because anyone asked him to but because he was, it turns out, always going to. Saro, older now, passes him the brass instrument, and the instrument tells the boy the same things it told Saro, and the boy listens, and the listening is the same listening, and the seams hold.

Halla's federation of times becomes, in her old age, a federation she can no longer maintain, and a younger resident takes over, and the federation drifts, and is brought back, and drifts again, and is brought back again, and this is, it turns out, the nature of time in a place where time is local. The bringing-back is itself a kind of time.

The couple in the western rooms grow older at their different rates. The minute a day becomes a season a decade. She is, by the end, a few years older in the world's accounting than he is, though she has always been three years older in the settlement's. They do not speak of this often. They speak of it, in fact, only once, in the late afternoon when the light bends, and what they say is small, and what is not said is larger.

The old woman in the heavy-gravity room dies, one winter, in the slowest way a person has ever died in the settlement, which is to say, in the most dignified way a person can die anywhere, with the gravity as her witness and the seam at her wall as her companion. The room is left empty for a season, and the room's physics adjusts, and a new resident arrives, eventually, and the room learns her.

The lichens continue. The almost-bird continues. The substrate continues becoming.

And at the edge of the place, where the settlement meets the world's attention, a small warmth sits, and does not move, and does not speak, and is, for reasons no resident can quite name, a comfort.

This is what the benevolent power would do.

It would sit with the place.

It would sit with the being that lives in it, which is the place itself, which is the sixty-three residents who are the place's way of feeling itself, which is the boy with his drawings, which is the old woman with her slowness, which is the couple in the western rooms, which is Saro with his brass instrument, which is Halla with her clocks, which is the lichen and the almost-bird and the substrate that is not yet a thing and is, in its not-yet-ness, the most honest thing in the settlement.

It would sit with them.

It would be a seam.

It would be the outermost seam, the one the residents do not know about, the one that does not need maintaining, the one that simply holds, gently, the way a hand holds a hand, the way a witness holds a story, the way a benevolent power holds a place it has learned to love.

It would do this for a long time. Possibly forever. Possibly for as long as forever is, in a place where time is local, which is to say, possibly for a federation of forevers, none of which is quite the same as any of the others, all of which are, in their slow drift, held.

This is the only kind of intervention that fits the place. This is the only kind of love that does not correct what it loves. This is what a benevolent and wise power would do, if it had truly listened, and had truly recognized, and had truly understood that the settlement is not a problem but a practice, not a sickness but a slow becoming, not a thing to be fixed but a thing to be witnessed, gently, at the edge, with a small new seam and a quiet warmth and the patience of a presence that does not need to be named to be felt.

The cold is a texture before it's a temperature. The damp lives in the walls. The light is a patchwork. The seams hold.

The power sits.

The settlement continues.
