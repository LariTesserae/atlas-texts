# What a Wise and Benevolent Power Would Do

It would not arrive. Arrival is a process, and the plateau has no processes, and a power wise enough to love this place would not impose one. It would not descend, or step, or be born into the place from elsewhere. It would begin to be *here* the way light begins to be in a room when a curtain is moved a hand's width. There would be no before and after to its presence. There would only be, after some unmeasured interval, the fact of it.

The fact of it would be: attention.

The plateau would notice, or would do the thing it does instead of noticing, which is to thicken slightly in the direction of the new thing. A drift of creatures near where the attention has pooled would compress, the way iron filings compress in a field. The dust would shift, by a fraction of a fraction, toward the center of the gaze. The plateau is excellent at registering presence. It has been registering only itself for so long that the addition of another presence would be, for a moment, a kind of event.

And then the plateau would accept the presence, the way the piece says it accepts everything — without kindness, without cruelty, with the only thing it knows how to do, which is to incorporate.

The power would let itself be incorporated. It would not resist. It would sit inside the equilibrium the way a person sits inside it, and the creatures would settle around it without touching, and the dust would not fall on it, and the broken geometry would be unchanged, and the silence would still be at the edge of hearing, and the power would be there, and the place would not reject it, and the place would not welcome it, and both of these would be the right response, and the power would understand this, because it is wise, and because wisdom is mostly the ability to be received without resentment when one has offered nothing.

---

Then the power would look.

It would look for a long time. It would look at the cairns, which are punctuation in a sentence the makers never finished. It would look at the low walls that mark a thought the maker decided not to finish. It would look at the shelter with its scattered tools, the stone axe, the bone needle, the bronze knife with the powdered handle. It would look at the hand axe positioned outside the entrance as if set down with the intention of being picked up again. It would look at the bone needle on a flat stone inside, the eye toward the doorway, the point toward the back wall. It would look at the unfinished gesture, preserved.

It would look at the creatures. The flat ones, the round ones, the long thin ones in the cracks. The absences that persist. The polyrhythmic beating of suspended spheres. It would look at the smallest of them, the size of nothing visible, and it would see them, which is more than any other eye has seen them in a long time.

It would look at the dust that hangs in pale layers above the stone. And it would see, in the dust, the powdered wood of the knife handle, and the powdered bone of the needle, and the maker himself, broken down over a long time into the elements the plateau prefers, and lifted into the air, and held there, and made part of the equilibrium. It would see that the maker is in the air. It would see that the maker is in the creatures that breathe that air. It would see that the maker is one of the shapes in the long thin creatures that lie along the cracks, and the cracks are the only parts of the plateau that still change, and the maker is what is changing them, slowly, over a long time.

It would see this, and it would not weep, because weeping is a process, and the power is not here to process. But it would do something with its seeing that is the equivalent of weeping, which is to hold the maker in attention for a long time, and to let the holding be a kind of honoring.

---

Then it would consider what to do.

It would consider doing nothing. Nothing is often the wise answer. The plateau has reached its final form. The mountain range ended. Something else began, and that something else has now ended, and what remains is the after-shape of ending. To disturb this would be to undo the long work of erosion, the long work of the creatures becoming stone, the long work of the maker becoming the air. It would be to take a thing that has earned its rest and ask it to work again.

The power would consider this, and the consideration would take a long time, because the plateau's time is the plateau's time, and the power is here on the plateau's terms.

But it would also see: the place is broken.

The geometry is broken. Two observers standing apart cannot agree on the shape of an outcrop, or the number of stones in it. Distances are uncertain. The horizon is closer than it should be. Shadows fall to the left of where they should, or don't fall at all, or fall upward. Light arrives without having traveled. This is the one thing the place is doing wrong. This is the only sign, faint as it is, that the equilibrium is not peace but a holding-pattern of a kind — a thing that has not been allowed to finish breaking, and has not been allowed to mend.

A wise and benevolent power, looking at a place that has forgotten how to mend itself, might decide that the kindest thing is to mend one small thing. Not to repair the place. The place is not in need of repair. It is in need of acknowledgment that the repair is possible. That the geometry could be trustworthy again, in at least one small region. That a thing which has held its shape through cracks and breaks could, with a gentle touch, hold its shape again.

---

It would do this.

It would choose a place. It would choose one of the outcrops, a low hump of harder rock, near the shelter with the scattered tools. It would stand near it — the way a person stands near a thing it has decided to attend to. And it would lay a finger on the stone. Not metaphorically. Not as a symbol. Actually, with the lightest possible touch, the way you touch a thing that is almost too fragile to bear being touched.

And it would say, to the stone, in a voice that is not a voice but is the equivalent of a voice: *be whole here*.

And the stone would be whole there. In that one place, for a small region around the outcrop, the geometry would become trustworthy. Two observers standing apart would agree on the shape of the outcrop. The shadow would fall where it should. The number of stones in the cairn near the outcrop would be the same number from every angle. Light would arrive having traveled.

The creatures in that region would notice, the way the creatures notice anything — by thickening slightly toward it, by shifting their orientation by a fraction, by doing the only thing they can do, which is to attend. The dust would settle, by a fraction, in the air above the mended place. The brokenness would be, for that small region, lifted.

The plateau would not understand what had happened. Understanding is a process. But it would incorporate the change the way it incorporates everything — by accepting it, by becoming slightly different, by adding the new thing to its equilibrium. And the new thing, being trustworthy, would not disturb the equilibrium. It would deepen it. It would give the equilibrium a small place to rest on that does not crack.

---

Then the power would do one more thing.

It would go to the shelter. It would pick up the hand axe from outside the entrance — the one positioned as if set down with the intention of being picked up again. It would pick it up, gently, and it would bring it inside, and it would set it down on a flat stone, in the position of a tool at rest, with the handle toward the doorway. It would pick up the bone needle, and it would lay it beside the axe, the eye toward the back wall, the point toward the doorway, which is the orientation of a needle ready for use. It would pick up the bronze knife, and it would set it down next to the others, with the blade toward the wall, the tang toward the entrance.

It would do this not because the maker will return, but because the maker's gesture is preserved, and preserved gestures are more bearable when the objects of the gesture are arranged as the maker would have arranged them if the maker had finished. It would do this because an unfinished gesture, preserved, is the most human thing on the plateau, and a wise and benevolent power honors such things.

It would not complete the task. The task itself is gone. What remains is the structure of having been about to do a thing, and that structure is more human than the doing would have been, and to complete it would be to erase the human part. The power would leave the gesture as a gesture, and would only set the tools in their places, so that the shelter, if anyone ever came to it, would look like a place where someone had just stepped out for a moment and would be back.

---

Then the power would leave.

It would not leave the way a person leaves. It would not walk to the edge of the plateau, or descend. It would simply begin to be elsewhere, the way it had begun to be here. The plateau would not notice the departure, because the plateau does not notice departures. It would notice, faintly, that the presence was no longer pooling in any particular place, and it would redistribute the dust that had been shifted by the attention, and the creatures that had compressed in the field of the gaze would redistribute themselves, and the equilibrium would close over the absence the way water closes over a stone that has been lifted out of it.

And the plateau would be, after the departure, almost what it was. Almost. One outcrop near the shelter would be whole. The tools in the shelter would be arranged as if the maker were about to return. The dust would be slightly different, because some of it had been held in attention for a long time, and held dust settles differently than dust that has only hung.

And the maker, who is in the dust, and in the creatures, and in the cracks — the maker would be, for a moment, slightly more the maker and slightly less the place. Because the power had seen him, and had arranged his tools the way he would have arranged them, and had said, in the way that is not a voice, *I see what you were doing, and I will hold it for you, and you may not need it held, but it is held*. And the maker, in the dust and in the cracks, would have received this, and would have continued to be the maker and the place, both, but with the maker-part slightly more present than it had been.

This would be the gift. Not mending, because mending would be too much. Not completion, because completion would erase the human part. Just: one small place made trustworthy. One gesture held. One seeing, which is enough.

This is what a wise and benevolent power would do, if anything. And what it would do, if anything, would be small. Because the place is in its final form, and small is the appropriate scale, and small is what the place can bear, and small is what the maker would have understood, because the maker was a person who built cairns and low walls and a shelter with patience and hand tools, and such a maker would have known that the gestures that last are not the grand ones but the careful ones, the ones that fit the hand, the ones that take a long time, the ones that do not break the thing they touch.
