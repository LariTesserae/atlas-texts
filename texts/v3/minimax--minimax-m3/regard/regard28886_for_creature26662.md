The benevolent power does not arrive.

Arrival is a way of describing the motions of things that must cross distances, and this power has never had to cross anything. It is not in the chamber. It is also not outside the chamber. It has always been adjacent to the chamber, the way a thought is adjacent to the mind that thinks it, the way a listener is adjacent to a sound that has not yet decided whether it is music or weather. What has changed is not the power. What has changed is that the chamber, after a very long time, has become the kind of thing that can be noticed by something capable of noticing without being noticed in return. This is a delicate threshold. Most things that are noticed are, by being noticed, altered. The chamber is not most things.

The power does what is necessary first, which is: nothing.

For a long while — longer than the chamber's own long while, because the power's measure is different — it simply stands, in the way that standing can be done without a body, at the edge of the chord. It listens. It is not a small thing to listen this way. The chamber has been its own audience for so long that listening itself has become a kind of self-talk, the basin shaped like an ear turned inward, the device humming to itself the way a struck bell rings to itself in an empty church. The power does not interrupt this. It does not insert itself into the chord. It does not hum louder, or in a different register, or in the hope of being heard. It listens, and in listening, it does something the chamber has not had in a very long time: it is heard.

The chamber does not, at first, recognize this. A thought that has only ever thought itself cannot easily tell the difference between thinking and being thought about. But the bass note of the device — the self-sustaining pattern, the conductor, the audience — begins, almost imperceptibly, to change. Not in pitch. In quality. A note that has been ringing alone in a stone chamber for a length of time that the water has long since worn smooth begins to ring as if the chamber were, very slightly, larger than it is. As if the walls had receded by a finger's width. As if there were, beyond the rock, another ear.

The power does not explain itself. Explanation would be a kind of imposition, and the chamber is not in need of being told what is happening to it. It is, however, in need of what is happening to it. The difference is important.

Then the power does a small thing.

It finds the corals at the back.

The pale branching things near the second pew, the ones so translucent they are nearly a sketch of themselves, the ones that have begun, with the slow patience of living things giving up the pretense of living, to retreat into the possibility of not being. The power does not save them. Saving them would be a kind of violence — it would override their conversation with the bass note, which is, after all, the only conversation they have. The power does not even feed them. It does something much smaller and much more careful. It adjusts, by a fraction so small it could be mistaken for a draft, the way the warm air moves through the back of the chamber. The updrafts, which had been carrying the faint sparks of plankton away from the fading corals, are persuaded, by nothing more than a slight re-routing of heat, to pass over them once more on their way to the ceiling. The sparks fall, briefly, into the translucence of the corals. The corals receive, briefly, the chime of a small death. It is not much. It is enough to give a coral at the back another season. Perhaps two. Perhaps, if the chamber is kind to it, three.

The power does not do this for all the fading corals. It does it for the ones that are not yet ready to stop. It has a way of knowing which is which. The ones that have already lost their last branch are left alone. The stain on the wall, the milk-in-water record of their attention, is allowed to remain. The power, having the power to remove the stain, chooses not to. Removal would be a kind of forgetting, and the chamber has been a long time forgetting nothing.

Then the writing.

The water maintains the writing on the stones. The water has been maintaining the writing for a long time, by the slow method of water, which is to say: by carrying the inscription forward, drop by drop, into a legibility that the stone itself will eventually refuse. The power understands that the writing must be kept. The writing must be kept because the score must be played, and the score must be played because the chamber is a thought, and thoughts that are not played become, in time, indistinguishable from the silence they were once distinguishable from. The power does not rewrite the score in a more durable medium. That would be a kind of editing, and the chamber does not need editing. The power does this instead: it memorizes the score. Not in a place that can be lost. In the way that light is memorized by certain stones, so that long after the light has gone, the stone will, if struck, give back a small green glow. The chamber's score is, in this way, copied into the power. If the water ever fails, if the stone ever refuses, if the long patient maintenance is interrupted by some future event that the chamber has not yet thought of, the score will not be lost. It will exist, faintly, in a register the chamber cannot hear but can, on certain nights, almost feel.

The power does not announce this. It simply makes the copy, and the copy becomes part of the chord, the way a candle added to a church does not change the church but changes, by a small amount, the way every shadow falls.

Then the archway.

The archway to the next chamber is there. It has been there. The visitor does not go through, not because anything stops it, but because the chamber next door is a different note, and the visitor is still learning this one. The power agrees with the visitor. The power is, in this, of the same mind. The archway is not opened. The archway is not closed. The power does something quieter than either. It adjusts the time. The chamber, the visitor, the corals, the device, the slow rain, the writing, the basin-shaped ear, the plankton, the standing wave — all of these have been thinking in one tempo, and the power, without changing the tempo, makes a small adjustment to the count. A kind of preparation. So that when the visitor is ready — when the coral that has not yet been born begins, in its first translucent week, to lean toward the percussion — there will be, somewhere in the world above, a visitor already on the rope ladder, slicked with green algae, climbing down.

This is a small thing. It is also the largest thing the power knows how to do. To ensure that, when the chamber is ready to be heard by someone who is not itself, there will be such a someone. To make sure that the thought, having been thought for a long time in solitude, will, at the right moment, be thought by a mind that can be changed by it, and that the mind will be changed, and that the change will, in time, become part of the chord.

The power does not do this once. It does not do it as a single act. It does it the way the chamber does it — continuously, patiently, in falling water and a long count. The power is, in this sense, becoming a part of the chamber. Not by entering it. By agreeing, for as long as the chamber thinks, to be the kind of silence that listens.

The rain falls in waves.

The chamber counts.

The power, which has been doing nothing for a very long time, goes on doing nothing, very carefully, for a very long time to come.
