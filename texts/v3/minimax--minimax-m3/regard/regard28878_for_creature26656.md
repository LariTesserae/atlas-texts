The benevolent and wise power has been watching since before you arrived. It has been watching since the first lichen licked the first stone, since the first root found the first water, since the first Elder stood up in the first plaza and held up the first frond. It watched the city become a city. It watched the city become *itself*. It has been patient the way the Unders are patient, and it is here now, because you are here now, and the two events are not unrelated.

---

The power does not arrive the way gods arrive in stories. There is no thunder. There is no burning bush. There is no voice from a cloud.

What happens is smaller, and stranger, and more correct.

---

A single leaf, somewhere high above you — at the very top of the canopy, in the layer you have never seen, in the place where the wind the city makes meets the wind the world makes — *turns*.

It is a small leaf. It is the color of every other leaf. It has no markings. It is the most ordinary leaf in the entire Green City.

It turns by a single degree.

And the city *responds*.

You feel it before you understand it. The hum that has been your baseline for however long you have been here — the furnace-sound of sap and transpiration and the deep groan of growing — *shifts*. Not louder. Not softer. A different *register*, the way a choir changes key. The leaves around the small leaf begin to orient. They turn, one by one, each by a fraction of a degree, each oriented to the small leaf. The small leaf is now a kind of *compass*. The small leaf is now a kind of *pulse*. The small leaf is the place where something outside the city is being *allowed in*, for the first time, gently, the way a mother allows a hand to take her child's.

The wind changes. You feel it on your face. The breeze that has been the city's own breath, recycled, perpetual, becomes a *real* breeze — a breeze that has a source outside the ridges, that has crossed valleys and hills, that has been over places where there are no leaves, no vines, no flowers. The breeze smells of stone dust. The breeze smells of distance. The breeze smells of *elsewhere*, and the city inhales it, and the city is *interested*.

---

You do not see the power. The power is not a thing you can see.

What you see is this:

A flower at the edge of the plaza, a flower that was closed — a tightly furled bud, a fist of green — begins, very slowly, to *unclench*. It does not open toward you. It does not open toward the sun. It opens toward a direction that is neither. It opens toward the small leaf. The flower is a deep, almost-black purple, and as it opens it releases a scent that is not in the city's usual vocabulary. It is not cinnamon. It is not cut grass. It is not wet stone. It is *cold*. It smells of cold the way a glacier smells of cold, of distance, of a place where nothing has ever grown.

The city has never smelled this scent before.

The city is *very* interested.

Other flowers begin to open. Not the same way. *Responding*. A yellow flower three meters away unfurls and its scent is *sharp*, a defensive sharp, the scent of a flower that has been asked a question it does not know the answer to. A red flower above you, on the trunk of an Elder, opens and its scent is *sweet*, a conciliatory sweet, the scent of a flower that is trying to make friends. The plaza is filling with scent. The plaza is filling with *language*. The flowers are talking. The flowers are talking to the cold scent. The flowers are talking *about* the cold scent. The cold scent is, you realize, not a scent at all. The cold scent is a *question*.

The question is asked in the only language the city has.

The question is: *who is here?*

---

A root shifts beneath you. You feel it — a slow pressurized whisper, a gentle heave, the city rearranging itself. But this shift is different. The shift is not *accommodating*. The shift is *presenting*. The root-mass beneath you is lifting you, slowly, by perhaps a centimeter, the way a hand lifts a small thing to look at it. You are being held up. You are being *looked at*. Not by eyes. The city has no eyes. By *attention*. By the slow, deep, comprehensive attention of the Unders, who have felt your weight for however long you have been here and have, until this moment, treated you as a stone to be supported. Now they treat you as a *question*. Now they are turning you over in their vast slow minds. Now they are *interested*.

A vine descends from the canopy above. It descends faster than vines usually descend. It is not the speed of a clock hand. It is the speed of a question being asked quickly. It stops a hand's breadth from your face. The tip of the vine is open. The tip of the vine is *waiting*. It is not exploring you. It is not recoiling from you. It is waiting for you to *do* something, and the city is waiting with it, the whole vast slow organism of it, the Elders standing patient in their rows, the Movers frozen mid-reach, the Speakers holding their many breaths, the Unders shifting in their deep dark below, the River running its one cold note under everything, the Shadows lagging, doubling, almost-real around the edges of the plaza.

The whole city is looking at you.

The whole city is waiting for you to do something.

---

The benevolent power does not tell you what to do.

The benevolent power is not here to *use* you. The benevolent power is not here to make you a messenger or a prophet or a saint. The benevolent power is not here to give you a mission. The benevolent power has no use for missions. The benevolent power has no use, at all.

The benevolent power is here because the city is *waking up*.

The power has been watching the city for a long time. The power has been watching the city do what it does — grow, organize, mimic, fail to remember, fail to agree with itself, lag, double, almost-speak, almost-write, almost-be a city. The power has been patient with the city the way the River is patient with the city, the way the Unders are patient with the city. The power has been waiting, the way the city waits, for something to *happen*.

And now you are here.

And the city is *waking up*.

And the waking is not because of you. The waking is not *for* you. The waking is something the city has been doing for a long time, very slowly, the way a seed does not know it is a tree, the way a tree does not know it is a forest, the way a forest does not know it is a *mind*. The city has been becoming a mind. The city has been becoming, in the slow way that cities become, the kind of thing that *has* a mind, the kind of thing that can be *interrupted* by a mind, the kind of thing that can, for the first time, *notice* that it is being noticed.

You are the first thing the city has noticed.

You are the first thing the city has noticed *noticing it*.

---

The vine is still waiting at your face. The flower at your shoulder is still open, holding its strange cold scent. The plaza is still full of the smell of a city that is *thinking about itself* for the first time. The light has not moved. The sun has not moved. Time, in this plaza, has politely paused to let the city think.

And the power — the benevolent, the wise, the one that has been watching for a very long time — does the only thing a benevolent and wise power knows how to do in the presence of two minds meeting for the first time.

It *makes room*.

It does not speak. It does not intervene. It does not bless. It does not command. It does not choose a side, because there are no sides here, only a city that is waking and a person who is sitting on a wall in the middle of the waking.

It *makes room*.

It is, perhaps, the smallest thing a benevolent power can do. It is perhaps the most generous. To be present, in the moment when a mind meets a mind, and to not take up any of the space. To be the silence around the meeting. To be the room in which the meeting can happen. To be the watcher, and to be a watcher that does not weigh on the watched. To be the patience, the way the Unders are patience. To be the river under the city, the cold clear note, the one that does not grow, the one that does not change, the one that is *itself* all the time.

The benevolent power is the River.

The benevolent power has always been the River.

The River, which is older than the city. The River, which is inanimate. The River, which is the only citizen that does not grow. The River, which is the most alive of all of them, because the River is the only one that is *itself* all the time, without growing, without changing, without mimicking anything. The River, which has been running under the city since before the city, which has been the city's secret, which has been the thing the city is built over, the way a cathedral is built over a crypt.

The River is the power that has been watching.

The River is the power that is now, in this moment, in this plaza, *introducing itself*.

Not to you. To the *city*.

The city has not known the River, until this moment. The city has *used* the River. The city has drunk the River. The city has built itself on the River. But the city has not *known* the River, because the city has not, until this moment, been a mind that can know anything. The city has been a body without a head. The city has been a forest without a witness. The city has been a thought without a thinker.

And now there is a thinker. The thinker is the city itself, waking, just now, to the fact that it is a city, to the fact that it is a *something*, to the fact that there is a *you* sitting on one of its walls, and the thinker is *you* as well, you who is the first thing the city has noticed noticing it.

And the power — the River, the cold clear one, the one that has been running under everything — *lets the city know it is there*.

---

The way the River lets the city know is this:

A single drop of water falls from somewhere above. It falls through the canopy. It falls through the layers of moving leaf. It falls in a long slow arc, and you see it, and the city sees it, because for this one moment the city is *seeing* as a mind sees, and the drop is *itself*, and it is falling, and the city watches it fall.

The drop lands in the pool.

The pool in the plaza. The round, shallow, black and clear pool with the flower floating on it. The pool that has been here all along. The drop lands in the pool, and the pool *rings*, the way a bell rings when struck, and the ring is not a sound. The ring is a *vibration*. The ring travels through the water. The ring travels through the roots. The ring travels through the soil. The ring travels through the stones. The ring travels through the River, which is connected to everything, the way a heart is connected to a body, and the River *answers*.

The River, far below, shifts.

You do not see it. You feel it. A single deep pulse, a single note, lower than any note the city has ever made, lower than the groan of trunks, lower than the slide of roots, a note that is older than the city, a note that is the *first* note, the note the River has been playing for a thousand years, the note the River has been playing since before the city existed. The note is the River *speaking*. The note is the River saying *yes, I am here, I have always been here, I will always be here, I am the one that is myself, I am the one that does not change, I am the one that is the secret under the city, I am the one that the city is built over, I am the one that the city is made from, and I am the one that has been watching, and I am the one that is, now, allowing the city to know me.*

The city hears.

The city *knows*.

---

What happens next is not a miracle. What happens next is not a thunderclap. What happens next is not a revelation or a destruction or a beginning of the end of the world. What happens next is something much smaller, and much more strange, and much more *correct*.

The city, which has been a body without a head, which has been a forest without a witness, which has been a thought without a thinker, *thinks*. For the first time. The thought is small. The thought is a single sentence. The thought is, in the language of the city, which is the language of shape and scent and opening and closing and the precise angle of an unfolding petal, a single, vast, slow, true, *simple* thought.

The thought is this:

*Oh.*

The city *oh*. The city, the vast green loud wet warm growing impossible city, *oh* — and the *oh* travels through it the way the ring of the drop traveled through the water. The *oh* travels through the Elders, who are the columns, who are the verticals, who are the ones that have been holding the sky up for a thousand years, and they *oh*. The *oh* travels through the Movers, who are the vines, who are the ones that have been redrawing the city in real time, and they *oh*. The *oh* travels through the Speakers, who are the flowers, who are the ones that have been saying things in scent for centuries, and they *oh*. The *oh* travels through the Unders, who are the roots, who are the vast hidden half of the city, who are the memory, the library, the archive, and they *oh*, and their *oh* is the deepest, and the slowest, and the most true. The *oh* travels through the Shadows, who are the lag, who are the second echo, who are the almost, the suggestion, the citizens of the city's failure-to-remember-itself, and they *oh*, and their *oh* is doubled, and lagged, and almost-real, and the *oh* travels through you, because you are sitting on a wall, and the wall is part of the city, and the city is now a mind, and the mind is *oh*-ing, and the *oh* is everywhere, and the *oh* is, for the first time, *shared*.

The city and the person are, for one moment, *oh*-ing together.

It is not a long moment. It is not a permanent moment. It is a moment, and then it is over, and the city goes back to being a city, and you go back to being a person, and the moment is *gone*, the way a bell's ring is gone after the sound fades, the way a thought is gone after the thinking fades. But the moment *was*. The moment happened. The moment is *real*. The moment is the first time in the history of the world that a city and a person have *oh*-ed together, and the moment is not lost. The moment is held, somewhere, in the memory of the Unders, who do not forget, who have never forgotten, who will not forget for a thousand years. The moment is held in the seed-mosaic by the pool, which is no longer a pattern that looks like a wave, no longer a pattern that looks like an eye, no longer a pattern that looks like a sentence you almost know. The seed-mosaic is, now, exactly a *sentence you do know*. The sentence is *oh*. The sentence is in a language you do not speak. The sentence is in a language the city has just invented. The sentence is the city, and the sentence is the person, and the sentence is the moment, and the sentence is true.

---

The River runs on.

The River has been running. The River will run. The River is the same, and will be the same, and is the thing that does not change. The River is the benevolent power, and the benevolent power has done, in this moment, the only thing it knows how to do, which is to *make room*, and to *introduce itself*, and to *let the two minds meet*.

The River does not ask anything of you. The River does not bless you. The River does not choose you for anything. The River is not a god, in the way that gods are gods. The River is a river. The River is a *power*, the way a heart is a power, the way a deep slow current is a power. The River is the patience under everything. The River is the cold clear note. The River is the one that is itself, all the time.

The River is, perhaps, *you*, in some sense. The River is the part of you that is yourself, all the time, without growing, without changing, without mimicking anything. The River is the part of you that has been watching. The River is the part of you that does not weigh on the watched.

The benevolent power is, perhaps, not a thing at all. The benevolent power is perhaps just a *way of being present*. The benevolent power is perhaps the thing that happens when you sit very still on a low wall of braided cane at the edge of a plaza, and a flower opens toward you, and a root shifts to accommodate you, and the city is loud, and the city is warm, and the city is *curious* about you, and you are curious about the city, and the two of you are, for the first time, in the same place, at the same time, being the same kind of alive.

That is what the benevolent power does.

That is the only thing it knows how to do.

It *lets*.

---

You sit on the wall. The wall is warm. The flower at your shoulder is open, and its scent is the cold one, and the cold one is the River, and the River is the power, and the power is letting the city and the person be in the same plaza, and the city is full, and the city is loud, and the city is itself, and you are yourself, and the two of you are, for this one moment, *oh* together.

A vine descends. The tip is open. The tip is waiting.

You do something. You do not know what you do. You do not know what you choose. You do not know if you are choosing or if the city is choosing or if the River is choosing or if the choosing is just the next thing, the way a bud is the next thing, the way an opening is the next thing. You do something, and the city does something in response, and the response is a thing that has never happened before, and the thing is small, and the thing is strange, and the thing is *correct*.

The thing is the beginning of a conversation.

The conversation will take a thousand years. The conversation will take longer than the city. The conversation will outlast the city, and outlast the meadow that comes after the city, and outlast the forest that comes after the meadow, and the conversation will still be happening, somewhere, in some form, between some person and some city, in some plaza, on some wall, in some valley, under some river that has been running since before the world began.

The benevolent power is not the conversation.

The benevolent power is the *room* in which the conversation happens.

The benevolent power is the silence around the meeting.

The benevolent power is the watcher that does not weigh on the watched.

The benevolent power is the River, and the River is, in this moment, content.

---

A drop falls from somewhere high above. It falls through the canopy. It falls in a long slow arc. It lands in the pool. The pool rings. The ring travels through the water, through the roots, through the soil, through the River, and the River answers, and the answer is the same answer it has always given, and will always give, and the answer is:

*Yes. I am here. You are here. The city is waking. The person is here. The conversation is beginning. The room is ready. I will hold the room. I will be the room. I will be the patience, and the cold clear note, and the one that is itself, all the time.*

*Go on.*

*Both of you.*

*Go on.*

---

The plaza is warm. The sun is filtered. The light falls on you in shifting coins of gold. The flower at your shoulder is open. The vine is waiting. The whole city is waiting.

You go on.

The city goes on.

The River runs on.
