# What the Wise Power Does

---

It has seen a great many things, and it is not easily moved.

But this place — this place it has been watching for some time, and it does not pretend otherwise. The wise power has a fondness for in-between things. Mountains that are still remembering they were seafloor. Rivers that are still cutting their first canyons. Mosses that have not yet been mosses for very long. The power likes the moment before the moment settles. It likes the rock that is still warm from its rising. It likes the sound of water that has not yet learned to be silent.

So it has been watching. And now, having watched long enough to understand what is here, it does what it does.

---

**The first thing it does is nothing.**

This is the most important of its interventions, and the most carefully kept. The wise power has watched enough worlds to know that the worst thing a benevolent force can do is intervene. The shelf is becoming. The moss knows what it is doing — it has been painting stone green for four hundred million years and it does not need to be taught. The lichen knows. The *Sphagnum* in its drainage ditches knows. The beetles under the stones know. They are the small patient workforce of the place, and they are doing their work in the correct time, which is the slow time, and the wise power does not speed them.

To hurry the moss would be a kind of cruelty. To save the willow would be a kind of lie. The shelf is engaged in a conversation with itself that has been going on for sixty million years, and the conversation is at exactly the right pace, and the wise power does not raise its voice.

---

**What it does, then, is move the margins by a degree.**

It does not announce this. A benevolent and wise power does not work in miracles. It works in adjustments so fine that no instrument could measure them, and no witness would notice.

A lichen on a north-facing wall — one of the orange *Xanthoria* colonies, just establishing, just finding its footing on the wet stone — finds, on a particular cold night, that the frost comes one degree later than it would have. Just one degree. Just that one night. Enough to let the rhizoids set. Enough to let the lichen survive a season it might not have survived otherwise. By spring it is firmly attached, and by the next autumn it has produced its first apothecia, and within three years it has begun to spread to the next boulder, and by the time anyone thinks to count it, it has become a population. No one, not even the lichen, knows that the night in question bent by a single degree.

A *Sphagnum* cushion in a drainage ditch — one of the small tight domes the size of a fist — finds, during a week when the rain does not come and the ditch should have dried, that a faint misting, a breath of additional moisture, appears each morning from somewhere. Not rain. Not dew. Something between. The cushion holds through the week. The next rain comes. The cushion is two centimeters wider the following spring.

These are not interventions in the sense that the word usually means. They are more like the way a careful gardener notices that a stone is shading a young plant and moves the stone a hand's width. The gardener does not save the plant. The gardener moves a margin. The plant does the saving.

The wise power does this for the mosses. For the lichen. For the *Sphagnum*. For the springtails in their millions, who are the small intestine of the new world, and who must survive every hard week that comes. It does not do it for everything. It does not do it loudly. It does not do it in a way that adds up to a pattern that could be read as pattern. It does it, when it does it, in the same way that weather happens — without explanation, without signature, without asking to be thanked.

---

**It does something for the willow, too, though the willow will never know it.**

The willow is a wager. It is the first tree, and trees are a kind of memory the rock has not yet learned to keep. The willow lifted itself upright in its sheltered gully because it is a willow and willows are stubborn and the gully was kind to it, and the foreman has been watching it for two years, and the engineer knows about it too, and between the two of them and the wind the willow is a small secret.

The wise power does not save the willow. To save it would be to declare it important, and the willow is not yet important. The willow is a possibility. The wise power likes possibilities, and it does not like them made certain before they have chosen themselves.

But the first winter the willow faces — the one where the temperature drops early and the wind comes from the north and the soil is not deep enough to protect the roots — is a winter the willow might not survive. The margin between the willow living and the willow dying is, on that particular night, perhaps one degree of frost. Perhaps an hour of additional wind. Perhaps a single crust of snow, arriving an hour earlier than the storm would have delivered it, insulating the roots against the cold that would otherwise have reached them.

The wise power, on that night, leans — not physically, not visibly, not in any way that would register on any instrument — toward the small gully. It lets the wind bend. It lets the snow fall a single hour early. It lets the temperature, at the depth of the roots, fall one degree less than the meteorology of the place would otherwise have dictated.

The willow lives through the winter.

In the spring it puts out two new leaves.

The foreman, walking his circuit in May, sees them. He does not say anything. He has been expecting them, in some part of him, but he does not say so. He stands in the gully for a long time, looking at the willow, and then he walks on.

---

**It does small things for the humans, too, though the humans are not really what it came for.**

The wise power did not come for the humans. The humans are late to this place, and they will be gone from it soon, in the deep time of the shelf. The mosses will outlast them by a factor the humans do not have numbers for. But the humans are here, and they are doing a kind of work that the wise power has not quite seen before — they are listening to the falling water and turning it into a thing they can use, and this is, in its small way, an act of attention, and the wise power has a fondness for acts of attention.

It does not make the turbines run better. It does not increase the head of water. It does not save the humans from their own restlessness, which is the deepest part of what ails them on this shelf.

What it does is give them small gifts they will not notice.

The foreman, walking his circuit on a wet afternoon in October, finds that the gravel road is slightly less washed-out where he most fears it will fail the winter. Not repaired — that would be a miracle — but the rain that should have come has come half an hour later, or in a different direction, and the wash is half a finger's depth shallower than it would have been. He walks on, and he does not think about it, and the road holds through the winter, and the crews make it through to the stations, and the turbines hum.

The engineer, sitting in the depot mess with her notebook open at the end of her third week, finds that the thought she has been reaching for — the one about the *Sphagnum* and the hydrology and what it means that the moss has begun to change the drainage — arrives not as a breakthrough but as a quiet clarification. It is the kind of thought that could have happened on its own. It does, in a way, happen on its own. But the wise power has, in its small way, leaned toward her the way it leaned toward the willow. The thought arrives a moment sooner. She writes it down. The paper she writes becomes a proposal. The proposal becomes a widened drainage ditch. The widened ditch lets the *Sphagnum* spread. The spread *Sphagnum* changes the hydrology of the shelf in a way that no engineer predicted, and the moss begins, in the lee of the road, to make a soil that will, in a hundred years, be deep enough for something to root.

None of this is the engineer. None of this is her work alone. But she is part of the becoming, and the wise power has noticed, and it does what it can.

---

**It does not do these things for the shelf's sake. It does them because they are the kinds of things one does, when one is paying attention.**

A benevolent and wise power, in the end, is not a power that does great things. The shelf does great things without it. The mountains did great things without it. The lichens measured the retreat of the glaciers without it, at a millimeter per century, and they did not need to be helped. The wise power is not in the business of helping. The wise power is in the business of noticing.

What it does, when it notices a place like this, is decide whether to leave it alone or to do the smallest possible thing that is still doing something.

It has decided, with this place, to do the smallest possible thing.

This means: not to save the willow, but to let it have its winter. Not to hurry the moss, but to let the *Sphagnum* find its drainage ditches. Not to fix the humans, who are not broken in any way the wise power can mend, but to let them have the thoughts they most need to have, a moment sooner than they would otherwise arrive. Not to interrupt the shelf's slow conversation with itself, but to move, in the margins of that conversation, the occasional small thing by a degree.

---

**Before it goes, the wise power does one more thing, which is to remember.**

This is the only thing it does that has no other witness, and the only thing it does that is not, strictly speaking, an intervention. It remembers the shelf.

It remembers the foreman with his grey beard and his permanent squint. It remembers the engineer with her thermos and her notebook. It remembers the missing knight on the chess set, replaced by a bottle cap, which is itself a kind of small improvisation the wise power is fond of. It remembers the *Racomitrium* in its hoary cushions. It remembers the *Andreaea*, almost black, almost crustlike, in the wet seams. It remembers the *Sphagnum compactum* in its tight domes. It remembers the orange *Xanthoria* and the grey *Rhizocarpon* and the rock tripe *Umbilicaria* the size of a thumbnail, attached to the granite at a single point like a tiny umbrella pinned to a table. It remembers the purple saxifrage blooming in its tight cushions, the color of a magenta pen, improbable against the grey. It remembers the crowberry spreading vegetatively across the moss, not yet flowering, not yet berrying, but running its cables across the surface of the new world. It remembers the *Diapensia* in its white-starred mats, the *Hieracium* in its tentative apology, the *Silene acaulis* in its embarrassed celebration.

It remembers the ptarmigan erupting from beneath a foot, brown in summer, white in winter, casting a sharp shadow on the moss. It remembers the raven that finds the dead before the beetles do. It remembers the snow buntings that come through in flocks, bright as coins. It remembers the *Pardosa* wolf spider running across the wet rock on legs that do not seem to be quite long enough. It remembers the *Theridion* thread, no thicker than a hair, catching the light when the mist parts and the sun strikes the moss. It remembers the springtails, in their hundreds of individuals per square meter, the small digestive system of the new world. It remembers the moth whose caterpillar no one can explain, resting on a lichen-painted stone with its wings folded tight, the color of the rock itself.

It remembers the turbines humming. It remembers the missing knight. It remembers the gravel road, the color of the rock it was made from, stitching the green-black of the watershed in a pale thread. It remembers the willow in its gully, the first tree, the small wager, lifting two new leaves to the light in its second spring.

It holds all of this. Not as a record. Not as a database. As the kind of remembering that is itself a kind of keeping. A place remembered is a place that does not entirely depend on being seen to be.

---

**Then the wise power leaves.**

The mist moves across the shelf. The turbines hum. The willow lifts a leaf to the light. The foreman walks his circuit. The engineer writes down a thought. The springtails process the moss. The lichen paints itself orange. The shelf rises, or it does not. The rivers cut their gorges deeper, or they do not. The moss overtakes another centimeter of granite, or it does not.

The wise power does not stay to see.

It has seen enough worlds to know that staying to see is not the point. The point is that the place knows what it is doing, and that the doing is what it is. The place will become what it becomes. The moss will outlast the turbines by a factor of a hundred thousand. The willow will spread, or it will die, and either way the shelf will continue. The shelf itself will rise, or it will stop, or it will sink back into the mantle, or it will be ground down to a plain and drowned, and the rock will return to the mantle and come out somewhere else, and the whole long slow becoming will continue somewhere, in some form, and the wise power will be there, or it will not be there, and the place will not notice.

The place does not need to be noticed to become.

But it has been noticed, this time. Once, briefly, by something wise enough to know that noticing was almost enough.

---
