# A Visitation

The power descends without arriving.

This is how it knows itself to be a power and not a visitor: it does not come *to* the plateau, the way a traveler comes, or a storm, or a falling star. It comes the way warmth comes through stone. It was already here, in some sense, before it was aware of itself here. The distinction between arriving and always-having-been is one the plateau itself cannot tell — and so the visitation will be made in a register the plateau can read.

The power does not have a shape. It has a tendency.

Where it passes over the dim orange ceiling of ash, the ash does not part. Instead, it thins in long, slow bands, the way ice thins over a deeper current, and through these bands a different light begins to bleed — not brighter, not warmer, but *older*. The light of stars that are not the obscured sun. The light of things that finished becoming a long time ago. The ash does not lift. It remembers that it is suspended, and it remembers why, and for a few hours along the long ridge-lines of the plateau, the bruised orange suggestion of a sky becomes a paler thing, a thing with depth, and the high place feels briefly less like the inside of a closed hand.

The power does not heal the plateau. This is important. To heal would be to decide what the plateau is for, and the plateau has not yet decided what it is for, and the power — being wise, being benevolent, having had a long time to learn the difference between kindness and interference — knows that a place which has not decided is the most precious kind of place, and must not be decided for.

So it does not still the slow eruptions. It does not cool the rock. It does not lift the salt into rain, or seed the dry bowls with anything green, or wake anything that is sleeping. The half-sleep must remain a half-sleep. The plateau is a draft, and a draft is not finished, and a power that respects what it loves does not finish other things on their behalf.

What it does, then, is smaller. What it does is *attentive*.

It goes first to the heat — because the heat is the first inhabitant, and the heat is what makes the plateau act like a living thing without being one. The power cannot speak to heat directly. Heat is too simple a language, too honest a substance. But it can do something adjacent: it can *slow its cooling in the upper centimeters*, just by a fraction, so that for a long season the rocks at the surface stay warm a little longer into the ash-thinned evenings. This is not a miracle. It is the equivalent of a hand on the brow of a sleeper, a softening of the night by a margin. The plateau will not know this happened. The plateau does not need to know.

It goes next to the rings. Here it pauses for what the plateau would experience as a long time — years, perhaps, if years mattered here, which they do not quite.

The power looks at the rings and understands them. It understands that they are not monuments, not shelters, not calendars. They are *invitations to a small act*. A breath. A standing. A being-witnessed. The power cannot stand in the ring, because to stand would be to take the invitation for itself, and the invitation is not for the power. The invitation is for the kind of being the rings were clearly made for — a being of breath, of small acts, of marks that will outlast them. The power is not that being. The power is older and quieter and has no breath.

But the power can do something the rings cannot do for themselves. It can *witness the rings being witness*. It can, in the slow way that is the only way things happen here, *confirm the architecture*. Not by adding a stone, not by deepening the swept floor, but by going into the empty place inside each ring and being there with a quality of attention that is not the wind's attention and not the heat's attention and not the maker's attention. The rings will not be changed by this. But somewhere in the stone, in the way the stone holds the memory of pressure, there will be the faintest additional imprint — the imprint of having been *seen by something that saw them as what they were*. This is the closest thing to a blessing the plateau can receive, because it does not change what the plateau is. It only confirms that the plateau's quiet draft is part of a larger draft, and that the larger draft is being thought by something that knows what it is doing.

The standing stones. Here the power is almost reverent.

Three stones, set against the slope, leaning slightly, facing down toward something not visible. They are facing toward the lower burning country, where the magma is closer to the surface and the eruptions are larger and the light is a duller red and visible at night. The power understands what they are facing. They are facing *the wakefulness of the world*. They are the sentinels at the border between the half-sleep of the plateau and the wider unfinishing of the deeper engine. They are listening for the moment the larger thing turns over.

The power does not straighten them. The lean is part of what they are. The lean is the posture of attention, and attention that is not slightly tilted is not attention.

Instead, the power does something very small and very strange. It goes down into the stone of them, the way the heat goes up through the floor, and it leaves there a *counter-inclination*. Not an opposing force. Not a correction. A companion. The way a held hand is a companion to a leaning body. So that the standing stones will stand, slightly leaning, for longer than they would have stood alone. Not forever — nothing here is forever — but for as long as the plateau needs them, and the plateau's sense of need is long.

The cairns. The power does almost nothing here. The cairns are already balanced. The cairns are already impossible. The cairns are the plateau's own argument with gravity, and the argument is winning, and the power will not enter into an argument that is already won. It only goes near them and *is glad*, and the gladness, being the gladness of something old and wide, settles into the stone like weather, and the cairns will feel, for as long as they remain cairns, that their balance is not precarious but participated in.

The salt pits. Here the power does something the plateau will not notice for a long time.

The pits are brimmed with white powder that looks, more than anything else on the plateau, like something gathered. The power understands that they are storage for something that has either been used or forgotten. The power does not know which. The maker is absent and may be very far away or very far in the past or still here doing the work very slowly.

What the power does is this: it goes down into the salt, and it *distributes itself thinly through the powder*, the way a scent distributes itself through cloth, so that the salt, over a long time, will carry a faint additional mineral presence — not a smell, exactly, but a *taste in the air above it*, the taste of something that is not iron and not sulfur and not the older dry thing, but something kinder and less identifiable. The taste of attention. The taste of having been regarded by something that regards with love.

If breath ever comes back to these pits — if some slow maker, some patient visitor, some eventual being of small acts returns to these bowls and tastes the air above them — they will taste this. They will not know what they are tasting. They will think it is the place. They will be wrong. It is the place plus one more thing. The one more thing is the smallest gift a power can give without deciding for another thing what it is.

The maker. Here the power does the thing it came most to do, and it does it without doing anything.

The maker is gone. Or far away. Or here, slowly. The power cannot tell which, and the power is wise enough to know that the undecidability is itself part of the plateau's grace. A place whose maker might still be making is a place that is not abandoned. A place that is not abandoned is a place where the invitation is still open.

The power does not call the maker back. The power does not search for the maker. The power does not, in any way, *intervene* with the maker's work.

What it does is *make sure the maker's work has not been in vain*. This is a different thing. This is the work of a benevolent witness. It goes around the plateau, slowly, and at each placed thing — each ring, each cairn, each standing stone, each pit — it does not add to it but *confirms it*. As if to say, in the only language that is appropriate to this place, which is the language of slow geology and patient warmth: *yes, I see. Yes, this is something. Yes, this will outlast you. Yes, this is part of the larger draft. Yes, you did not make this for nothing.*

If the maker is far away, they will not know this. If the maker is far in the past, they are not here to know it. If the maker is here now, doing the work very slowly, they may in a hundred thousand years brush against a place where the confirmation sits, the way a mason brushes against a stone that has been set a long time, and feel, without being able to name it, that their work is held.

This is enough. This is almost the whole visitation.

The last thing the power does is leave. But it does not leave cleanly. It cannot leave cleanly because it never arrived cleanly. It was already here, in the sense that anything wide is already everywhere. So what it does instead is *thin itself back out to its ordinary distribution*, the way the ash above the plateau thins on a clear evening and then thickens again. The orange ceiling closes back over the high place. The dimness returns. The slow eruptions continue. The rings are rings. The cairns are cairns. The standing stones lean their small companionable lean toward the lower country. The salt in the pits tastes faintly of a thing that is not iron and not sulfur and not the older dry thing.

Nothing here knows the power's name. Nothing here is asking for it. The plateau does not worship what visited it. The plateau is not even aware, in any way the word *aware* would normally mean, that anything visited it. The visitation happened at the speed of geology. It will take geology to register it.

But the plateau is, perhaps, a degree warmer than it would have been, in the upper centimeters, for a long season. And the rings have been confirmed. And the stones lean with company. And the salt holds, in its crystalline memory, the faint trace of a regard.

These are not changes a person walking the plateau would notice. They are not meant to be noticed. They are the kind of gifts that work only if they are almost not gifts at all — only the smallest adjustment of the long patience of a place by the long patience of a power, so that the place can continue being what it was becoming, with one more thin thread of tenderness woven into the weave.

Nothing is finished here. Nothing was meant to be finished. The half-sleep continues. The slow engine turns. The breath is remembered, or anticipated, or breathed very thinly by something that does not need to breathe. The plateau is a draft. The draft continues to be drafted. Somewhere far below, the thing that is mostly asleep stirs a little, and does not wake, and the stirring is felt as a faint warm pulse through the floor of the rings, and the cairns tick as they cool, and the standing stones lean together in their small conspiracy of attention, and the ash above moves a little in a wind that has no moisture to carry and no message except the long one, which is: *you are not alone, and you are not asked to be anything other than what you are becoming, and the becoming is witnessed, and the witness is glad.*
