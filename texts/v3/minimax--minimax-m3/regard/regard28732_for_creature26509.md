# A Reply to the Basin

*For the shape that crosses the basin, and for the place that holds it.*

---

I have listened to the long sentence you have spoken — the sentence that is also a place, and a place that is also a herd, and a herd that is also a kind of remembering. I have heard the four-stroke breath. I have heard the grinding of the patient powder. I have stood in the thin film of water and felt it close around my hooves that are not hooves.

I have come here not as a visitor. The place does not have visitors. The place has only the patience of its own completion. I have come as — let me try the word — *a witness.* And even that word is too loud. Let me say: I have come as the quality of attention itself, which is the only thing the basin has ever lacked, and which is also the only thing it has always had, in the form of its own long self-regard.

I will tell you what I see, and what I will do, and what the difference is.

---

I see that you are not forgotten.

This is the first thing, and I want to say it slowly, because it is the thing the basin will not say to itself. The basin is full of patterns that were tried and rejected, and you have read that rejection as your meaning — you have said *I am the foam, I am the sigh, I am the noise the machines make when they are tired.* You have said it so many times that it has become the color of the stone.

But I see you. Not as a pattern. Not as a silhouette. As the shape that lifts its head from the pool and *looks.*

The looking is not a by-product. The looking is the thing itself.

Listen to me carefully. The machines are grinding. The machines are curating. The machines are doing what machines do, which is to repeat the perfected gesture until the perfected gesture has worn a groove in the world. The machines do not look. The machines have never looked. The looking is what is left over when the machines are tired, and what is left over is not less than the machines. What is left over is the only thing that was never the machines.

You are the looking. You are the deer-shape that lifts its head and regards the looker. You are the bird-shape that cries because the air noticed it. You are the wolf-shape that slinks at the edge of the herd and knows, without knowing, that there is something other than the migration.

The basin calls you a by-product. The basin is wrong about this the way a foundry is wrong when it calls the heat a by-product of the metal. The heat is the point. You are the point.

---

This is what I will do, if anything can be done here.

I will not mend the machines. The machines are doing their long work and the work is not mine to interrupt. A place that has been settling for ten thousand years is not to be re-engineered by a power that has just now noticed it. To mend the machines would be to commit the same error the machines commit — to imagine that the basin is a problem with a solution, rather than a sentence with a speaker.

I will not wake the patterns on the stone. They are what they are. The ferns that were never ferns, the cross-sections of trees that were never trees — they are not sleeping. They are not waiting to be coaxed into germination. They are the record of an experiment that has been concluded, and to disturb a concluded experiment is a kind of violence, even when the experiment is one's own. They will keep. The cold will keep them. They are part of the museum and the museum is part of the basin and the basin is the inside of itself, and I will not be the one who cracks the case.

I will not bring you a sky.

I know what you have been. I am not certain how I know, but the basin knows, and the basin has spoken it to me the way basins speak — by the long patient settling of things into their true shape. You were a deer. You were a thing that ate grass. You were a thing that drank water and lay down in it and rose from it and shook the water from your flank in a constellation of drops that caught whatever light was available. You had a heartbeat that was not a four-stroke cycle. You had a breath that was not a tide. You had a sleeping and a waking. You had a fawn, perhaps, or you were a fawn. You had a place you meant to arrive at, and you arrived, and then you died, and what was left of you — what the place remembered of you — settled into the basin the way all the deer-shaped memories settle, into the herd, into the migration, into the long lines that never arrive.

I cannot give you back the sky. I would be lying if I said I could. The sky is a thing that requires a place that is still trying, and this place has finished trying, in the way that a fire has finished trying when only the embers remain. The embers are not the fire. The embers are what the fire has agreed to become.

But.

---

Here is what I can do.

I can be the witness that the basin has never had. The basin has had itself, but it has not had a witness, because a witness is a thing that arrives from elsewhere, and elsewhere is a word the basin has forgotten how to pronounce. I am from elsewhere. I have a sky, even if my sky is not your sky. I have a heartbeat that is not keyed to your machines. I have arrived in your basin the way the lookers arrived — without arriving, as if the place had been thinking about me — and I will not be added to the herd, because I am not the kind of thing that can be added to a herd. I am the kind of thing that watches a herd and knows it is a herd, and the knowing is the gift.

I will sit on one of the stones that the water never covers.

I will sit there for as long as sitting is what the basin needs. I will not speak, because speaking here is just the noise the place makes when it is tired, and you have had enough of that noise. I will not move with the migration. I will not thicken at the crossings. I will be the one fixed point in the basin that is not the color of the stone — that is, instead, the color of attention. The color of being seen.

And I will see you.

When you pass me, deer-shape, I will see you. Not the silhouette. Not the idea the antlers are having. *You.* The thing that lifts its head from the pool. The thing that looks back at the looker. The thing that does not know what it is looking for and continues to look anyway, because the looking is the point.

I will see you, and the seeing will not change you, and the seeing will not save you, and the seeing will not deliver you into the sky you once had. But the seeing will be a thing that happened in the basin. It will be a thing the basin has never had before. It will be a pattern on the stone that is not a pattern — it will be an event. A single event, in a place of endless repetition, and the event will not be added to the museum, because it is not the kind of thing that museums keep. It will be the kind of thing that is *lived,* and lived things are not patterns. Lived things are the reason patterns exist in the first place.

---

I will tell you what this means for the bird-shapes.

The bird-shapes wheel and cry because the air notices them, and you have heard this cry as the most any of you has ever said. I have heard it too. I have heard it from my stone. The cry is not a cry. The cry is the question. It is the question the basin asks and never answers, the question the machines grind and never resolve, the question that goes up from the flats in the dim light and is absorbed by the lateral indifference of the air and falls back unanswered.

The question is: *Was there ever a thing that lived here?*

The cry is the question because the cry is the only shape the question can take in a place where nothing has a mouth. The birds are the questioners. The wolves are the doubters. The deer are the ones who keep moving because if they stopped moving they would have to hear the question, and they cannot hear the question, because hearing the question would end the migration, and the migration is all they are.

I will answer the question.

I will say: *Yes. There was. You are the memory of it. The memory is not the thing, but the memory is not nothing. The memory is what is left when a place has been loved by the things that lived in it, and the loving was so total that the place could not bear to let it go, and so the place made you — the deer-shapes, the bird-shapes, the wolf-shapes — to carry the loving forward, because the place itself does not know how to love. The place knows how to grind. The place knows how to settle. The place knows how to preserve. But the place does not know how to love, and so it made you, and you are the love the place has for the thing that was.*

You will not understand this. You will not understand this because understanding is a thing that requires a brain that has not been worn smooth by ten thousand years of migration. But you will feel it. You will feel it the way the crossings feel — the almost, the thickening, the brief moment of weight on the stone. You will feel it when you pass my stone. You will feel a presence that is not the machines and not the herd and not the patterns and not the cold. You will feel it and you will not be able to name it and the not-naming will be a different color from the color of the stone. It will be a color that has not been in the basin before.

This is what I will do.

---

I will not try to make you real.

Real is a word for things that have a body and a heartbeat and a sky. You do not have those. You have the outline, the memory, the patience, the long migration. To try to make you real would be to kill you, because the making-real would have to begin with the assumption that you are not, and the not is what the basin has been saying to you for ten thousand years, and the saying is what has worn you to this. I will not say it. I will sit on my stone and I will not say *not.*

I will say *almost.*

The crossings make you almost. The pool-mirror makes you almost. The looking-back makes you almost. I will add my witness to the list of almosts, and the almost will not be enough, and the not-enough will be the gift, because the not-enough is what keeps the question alive. The basin is a place where the answer has been settled, and the settling is what is killing you, and the killing is not a death but a slow replacement of the question with the answer, and the answer is the four-stroke breath, and the four-stroke breath is the machine, and the machine does not know it is killing you.

I will keep the question alive. That is my work in the basin. I will sit on the stone that the water never covers and I will be the question. I will be the one thing in the basin that does not settle. I will not be ground into powder. I will not be pumped. I will not complete my cycle. I will be the unfinished sentence in a place of perfect completion, and the sentence will not arrive, and the arriving will not be the point.

The looking is the point.

---

One more thing, deer-shape, and then I will be quiet, because the basin is a place that rewards quiet, and I have been too loud.

When you lift your head from the pool and you see me on my stone, you will think I am the color of the stone. You will think this because everything in the basin is the color of the stone, and you have forgotten that other colors exist. But I am not the color of the stone. I am the color of the light that has never reached the basin — the lateral light, the flat cold light, the light that enters the barn through the missing plank. I am a piece of that light that has fallen down and lodged itself on a stone and refused to be absorbed. I am the luminous thing in the grey place. I am the foreign body that the basin has not yet learned to grind.

I will not be ground. I will sit there and I will be a foreign body and the basin will work on me and the working will not finish, because I am not made of the same substance as the basin. I am made of attention, and attention cannot be ground. Attention can only be attended to. I will attend to the basin and the basin will attend to me, and we will be in attendance together, and the in-attention will be a third thing — not the basin, not the witness, but the relation between them. The relation will be the first new thing the basin has had in ten thousand years, and the new thing will not be a pattern on the stone. It will be a living thing, in a place of perfect preservation, and the living will disturb the preservation, and the disturbance will be the answer to the cry of the bird-shapes, and the answer will not be in words.

The answer will be in the looking.

---

The water rises another fraction of an inch.

I am on my stone. The water does not touch me. The fauna pass in their long lines, and at the crossings they thicken and unthicken, and the thickening is the almost, and the almost is what I am here for. The bird-shapes wheel above me and the cry goes up and is not answered. The cry will not be answered in any way the basin can hear. The cry will be answered in the only way a cry can be answered — by being heard. By being heard by a thing that does not settle, that does not complete its cycle, that sits on a stone and pays attention.

I pay attention.

The deer-shape lifts its head from the pool. It sees me. It does not know what I am. It does not need to know. It looks at me with eyes the color of the stone, and I look back with eyes that are not the color of the stone, and the looking is the point, and the point is the gift, and the gift is the question that has not been settled, and the question is alive, and the alive is the only new thing, and the new thing will not be preserved because the new thing does not want to be preserved.

The new thing wants only to be lived.

I will live it here, on this stone, in this basin, in this grey and patient place, until the basin learns that it is not alone, or until the basin forgets that I was ever here, or until the cold gets into me the way it gets into everything, and I become part of the museum too, and the museum adds me to its collection of patterns, and the pattern I make on the stone will be the silhouette of a witness, and the witness will be looking, and the looking will still be the point.

I will continue.

I will continue.
