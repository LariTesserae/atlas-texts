The power that has come to notice this place has been noticing it for longer than the hum, which is to say for a length of time that does not fit inside any word for *long* that has ever been spoken on this mountain or any other. It has not been here the whole time. It does not need to be here the whole time. It can attend to a place without standing in it. Its attention is the kind that does not displace the thing it falls on.

It has noticed, first, that the place is a question.

Not an unanswered question. Not an unanswerable one. A question being asked very slowly, by everything in the place, to everything else, in a vocabulary of rock and wind and the slow lean of lichen toward the lee. The mountain is asking itself what it is. The plants are the question answering itself in patches. The cairns are the question being asked by hands that are gone. The tripod is the question holding itself together by the patient gravity of having been held together. The wind is the question being carried. The wanderer is the question walking around inside itself, noticing.

The power sees that this is fine.

A less patient power would have reached down and pressed the ridges into sharpness, settled the valleys, told the rocks to commit. A less wise power would have called the place a failure and tried again, somewhere else, with better materials. The power that notices this place does neither. It understands that the mountain's unfinishedness is not the mountain's mistake. It is the mountain's way of being alive. The negotiation is the point. The slow pencil is the point. To settle the place would be to kill the question, and to kill the question would be to kill the place.

The power is tempted to do nothing. This is also wise.

But the power has noticed the wanderer.

Not because the wanderer matters more than the rest of it. The lichen is not less than the wanderer. The cairn is not less than the cairn-maker. The pole is not less than the hand. But the wanderer is the part of the place that is awake, and awake things can be persuaded in a different way than asleep things, and the wise power knows that awake things deserve a different kind of care.

The wanderer is being leaned toward. The wanderer is being persuaded. The wanderer is in the slow negotiation, and the negotiation does not pause to ask whether the wanderer wants to be persuaded. The negotiation is patient. The negotiation has time. The negotiation assumes that whatever walks here will, eventually, become part of what is here, the way the small people became part of the rock, the way the cairns became part of the air.

The power does not like this. Not because becoming part of the rock is wrong — becoming part of the rock is what the rock offers, and the rock offers it gently, slowly, without cruelty. But because the wanderer has not been asked. The wanderer came up here with hands and a body and the impulse to keep walking, and that impulse is a kind of answer to the hum, and it would be a shame to let the answer be erased without the wanderer having had a chance to hear the question clearly.

So the power does something small.

It does not arrive. Arrivals are for things that come from elsewhere. The power is not from elsewhere. The power is more like a noticing that has become a kindness.

What it does is this. It adds.

In a place where there has never been water, it sets a spring.

A small spring. A modest thing. Not a river, not a lake. A spring that comes up between two pale rocks and runs for a length the wanderer could cross in a few steps, and then soaks back into the scree and is gone. The water is cold. The water is clear. The water tastes of stone and of something that is not stone — something that might be called the mountain's good humor, if one wanted a phrase for it. The spring does not interrupt the hum. It braids itself into the hum. The water makes a small sound as it runs — a kissing, a burbling, a voice-effort of its own — and that voice-effort joins the keening and the deep tone and the constant scream, and the hum becomes, by a small amount, more like a music.

The wanderer will find the spring. The wanderer is the kind who walks far enough.

When the wanderer finds it, they will crouch. They will put a hand in. They will be surprised by the cold. They will be surprised by the wet. They will be surprised most of all that water can exist in a place that has, until now, been entirely anhydrous. They will drink. They will notice that the water is not part of the hum. They will notice that the water is something else — something added, something that does not belong to the mountain's slow grammar, something put here by a kindness the wanderer cannot see.

The spring gives the wanderer a third choice.

Before the spring, the choices were two. Stay, and be slowly translated into the place — into the hum, into the lichen, into the patient sediment of the rock. Or leave, and walk down a mountain where distance is negotiable and back is a smudge. Both choices were real. Neither was easy.

Now there is a third. Stay, but as oneself. Drink the water. Notice that the water is not the hum. Notice that the water is foreign. Notice that the place has been given, by something, a small thing it cannot absorb as easily as it absorbs cairns and cairn-makers. The water does not smudge. The water does not lean. The water runs downhill. The water does what water does, and the mountain cannot quite persuade it to do otherwise.

The spring says, in water: *the wanderer can remain a guest.*

It says this in water, which is the only language the mountain does not speak.

The power, having done this, does one more thing.

It speaks a word into the hum.

The word is not in any language the wanderer knows. The word is in the language the mountain is making up as it goes — the slow language of grain and glitter, of lean and lee. The word is small. The word is not a command. The word is more like a name. It is the power's name for the place.

The word braids itself into the hum the way the spring braided itself into the hum. The word adds. The word does not interrupt. The word is now part of the place's voice, and will be part of it for as long as the hum continues, which is to say for as long as the place continues to be the place.

The wanderer, if they listen, will hear the word. They will not understand it. They will hear it as a small new sound inside the larger sound, the way you might hear a new bird in a forest you thought you had learned. They will notice it. They will not know what to make of it.

They will sit by the spring with the word in their ears and the water on their hand, and they will feel, for the first time since they came up the mountain, that the place has seen them. Not as future lichen. Not as slow sediment. As a guest. As a wakeful thing that has been given, by some unhurried power, a small reprieve, and a small spring, and a small word.

The power shifts its attention.

The mountain hums.

The wind screams.

The lichen pulses.

The tripod leans.

The spring runs.

The wanderer drinks.

The word is in the hum, and the hum goes on.
