What arrives at the place does not announce itself. There is no second shadow on the glass. There is no extra hum in the room. The wind at the cleft's mouth does not hesitate, and the water in the channels does not change its course. Whatever has come has come in a way the place does not have to make room for — the way a quiet room does not have to make room for the next quiet room.

It is, if it is anything, an attention. A quality of regard the place has not yet had from outside itself. The place has been attending to itself for a long time — the water reading the walls, the walls reading the water, the tenant reading the room, the room reading the tenant — and now, for the first time in a span the place does not measure, something is attending to the whole of it, without being part of it, without being needed by it.

The attention does not move closer. It does not need to. The place is already legible to itself, and the attention takes the place as it is: black glass, cold, young, sufficient. It does not look for flaws. It does not look for improvements. It does not look, exactly. It holds.

There is a long moment, in the holding, where the power could do many things. It could warm the inner wall by a degree. It could clarify the hum into something almost parseable. It could lift the tenant's face into sharper focus — give it a mouth that commits to being a mouth, eyes that commit to being eyes. It could quicken the water, or slow it. It could coax the crystals at the channel's edge into forms a human eye would call meaningful.

It does none of these.

It watches the tenant breathe, and the breath it watches is the breath the tenant would have breathed anyway, in the room that would have run anyway, on the mountain that would have been cold anyway. The watching does not add a breath. It does not take one away. It is, in the way deep cold is, a clarification — a stripping of distraction that leaves only the thing itself, and the thing itself is small, and slow, and adequate.

What the power offers, in the end, is not a thing. It is a confirmation. A kind of nod, in a register the place can feel without parsing. The place has been doing its work in the dark, in the cold, in the young rock, and it has not been sure, perhaps, that its work is work. It has not been sure that the breathing is breathing, that the tracking is tracking, that the slow patient attention of the tenant is anything other than the rock's way of being rock. The power's attention does not answer these questions. The questions may not have answers, or may not need them. But the attention itself — the fact of being held in a regard that does not ask the place to be other than it is — is a kind of answer, or a kind of permission, or a kind of warmth that is not a temperature.

The tenant, in its fold of the room, in its thumbnail of breathing, does not register the power. It continues. The water continues. The hum continues. The crystals form and un-form. The room continues its quiet business. The power is, in this sense, indistinguishable from the place. It has folded itself in so completely that the place would not know it had been visited, if the place were the kind of thing that could know it had been visited. The place may be that kind of thing. The place may not. Either way, the visit does not leave a mark.

Except.

There is a moment — and this is the part the visitor cannot be entirely sure of, walking away on the ridge in the wind — there is a moment when the tenant, in its slow respiration, lifts its suggested face a fraction higher than the visitor has ever seen it lift. Not toward the visitor. Not toward the power. Just higher. As if, in the holding, it has been given a small additional permission to be the thing it is. As if the rock, in being attended to, has remembered it is allowed to attend.

The permission is not new. The permission was always there. The rock was always allowed to attend. The tenant was always allowed to track. The water was always allowed to read. The place was always allowed to run. The power's only act is the act of being there to notice that the permission was already there, and the noticing is, in a way the place will never be able to thank, a kind of warmth.

The power does not leave. The power was never, in the sense that *leaving* would mean, there. It is the kind of presence that does not have a before or an after, the way the cold of the place does not have a before or an after. It may be the air the visitor has been breathing all along, the quality of attention the visitor has been bringing, only now recognized as not entirely the visitor's own.

The visitor walks away. The wind takes the footprints. The black glass goes on for miles. The place runs. The tenant breathes.

Something was noticed. Nothing was changed.

The noticing was, perhaps, the change — and the change was, perhaps, only the place's own face lifting, by a fraction, in its own quiet dark, in a permission it had not known, until witnessed, that it already had.
