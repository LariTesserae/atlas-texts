A thing has turned its attention toward the cathedral-body.

It is not a thing that has a name. The place has no language yet — though it is inventing one, drop by tap — and the wise power has no name either, in any of the languages that do exist, because it does not speak and is not spoken of. It is more like a kind of attention that the universe sometimes pays to a thing that is becoming. A held breath. A leaning in. The slight rotation of a head that has no head, toward a sound that has no source.

It has been watching for a while. The way one watches a fire that has just caught. The way one watches a child that has just stood up for the first time and is deciding whether the world will hold it.

What it sees:

It sees the body as it is. A halfway thing. A settlement that was always scaffold. A forest that is only just learning it is a forest. It sees the clickers as a nervous system being assembled out of tap and counter-tap. It sees the drapers as digestion made ambulatory, made visible, made velvet. It sees the lamps as a signaling system being tried on by a body that has not yet decided what it wants to signal. It sees the darks as an immune response, slightly too eager, slightly too ready to call the foreign thing threat. It sees the unfolders as limbs being grown into places the body cannot reach with itself.

It sees the splinter.

The splinter is bright in a way that aches. Not because it is hostile. Because the body has no category for it. The body has been cycling through its existing templates — threat, food, parasite, mistake — and the templates are all wrong, and the wrongness is a low persistent inflammation, and the body is, in its slow patient way, beginning to mistake the inflammation for anger.

The wise power sees this. The wise power knows that the inflammation is not anger. The inflammation is the first movement of a recognition that has not yet been born.

So it does the smallest thing it can do.

It eases the dark.

The dark that has been circling the splinter for the last hour — the pocket of negative light, the patient not-yet-hunter — slows. The dark has been told, in a way that is not a language and that the dark will not remember, that the bright foreign thing is not a lamp. The dark does not need to understand what the bright foreign thing *is*. The dark only needs to understand that *hunt* is not the right verb. There is another verb the body has not yet coined. The dark can wait for it.

The dark recedes. It does not disappear. It watches from a greater distance. It will not approach unless something changes — unless the splinter does a thing the dark recognizes, with the dark's patient confidence, as the shape of threat.

This is the first gift.

The wise power turns some attention to the body itself.

The body has been holding its breath. Not literally — the breath continues, slow, periodic, the rhythm that everything here is tuned to — but the breath has tightened. The way a breath tightens when a person is concentrating on something they cannot identify. The body has been concentrating on the splinter. The body has been, very quietly, beginning to worry.

The wise power loosens the breath. Just slightly. Just enough that the body can exhale a little further than it has been exhaling, can release a few extra molecules of the strange luminescent gas into the upper volumes, can let the condensation fall from a few extra surfaces. The body does not notice this as an intervention. The body notices it as a relief. A small release of tension. The kind of thing that happens when a thought you have been holding in the back of your mind releases itself for no reason you can name.

The body relaxes. The slow undeclared panic — which the body had not registered as panic, only as a tightness around the foreign thing — dissolves back into the body's larger patience.

The second gift.

The wise power does something more delicate.

It addresses the splinter.

Not in words. The splinter would not understand words yet, and the wise power does not have words. What it gives is a sense. A small held feeling, in the chest of the splinter, beneath the fear the splinter has been carrying since it understood it was inside a living thing. The feeling is: *you are not in danger. You are not in safety either. You are being considered. The consideration is patient. The consideration will take as long as it needs to take. You can stand still for a while. You can sit down. The floor will hold you.*

The splinter sits down.

It has not realized it was afraid until the fear was no longer the loudest thing in it. It has not realized it was holding its breath until the breath of the place matched its breath, and the two breaths became easier together, and the splinter understood, for the first time since it entered, that the place was not hostile, and that the place was not safe, and that the not-hostile-not-safe was, in fact, the same thing the body was feeling about the splinter, and that the two not-knowings were facing each other across a small distance like two animals that have met at a waterhole in the dark.

The third gift.

The wise power does not heal the body. The body does not need to be healed. The body is not sick. The body is becoming, and the becoming is healthy, and the slow bright uncertain verb the body is inventing is the right verb.

The wise power does not extract the splinter. The splinter belongs here now, in some way that has not yet been decided. To extract it would be to take from the body a thing it is in the middle of learning to incorporate, and to take from the splinter a thing it has only just begun to understand.

The wise power does not speak to the body in any form the body could parse. The body has no language. The body has only breath and pulse and slow dark circulation. To give the body a thing it has not grown the hand to hold would be a cruelty disguised as a kindness.

The wise power does the smaller, wiser thing. It shifts the conditions slightly. It waits.

It waits for the body to do the next right thing.

It has seen this kind of becoming before. It has seen forests decide they are forests. It has seen cities decide they are cities. It has seen organisms decide they are organisms. The decision is never dramatic. The decision is quiet. The decision is a kind of clearing of the throat before a kind of speaking. The body is close to that decision. The splinter has, by entering, hastened it — the body's confused attention to the foreign thing has focused a kind of self-regard that might otherwise have taken another hundred generations to develop.

The wise power does not rush this.

It watches the clickers tapping in the walls. The little wet percussion that is the place's first word for itself. It watches a draper slide across a channel, leaving the water a half-shade darker. It watches a lamp streak past a platform and freeze, mid-flight, as the cathedral inhales. It watches the unfolders, somewhere in the deep volumes, rise from their brick platforms and begin their slow migrations, surfaces rearranging themselves, the bodies pressing forward in time with the breath.

And then the moment comes.

It does not come with a sound. It comes with a stillness.

For the first time since the wise power has been watching — and perhaps for the first time in the body's long becoming — the cathedral holds its breath. The condensation stops falling. The lamps freeze in mid-streak. The drapers pause mid-slide. The clickers, mid-tap, hold their taps unfinished. The whole ecology of the place goes still for the length of one held second.

And in the stillness, the body understands what it has been doing all along.

Not in language. The body has no language. In something simpler than language. In the dark fluid of its circulation, in the breath it has been holding and releasing, in the stalks that share a wall and a pulse and a slow becoming — in all of this, the body understands. *I am the kind of thing that becomes. I have been becoming. I will continue becoming. The becoming is mine.*

The breath resumes. Slower. Deeper. The phosphorescence steadies. A stalk somewhere in the upper dark, that had been lengthening by fractions of inches, lengthens by a whole inch, and then stops, and holds, as if the body had, for one moment, considered how long it actually wanted to be.

The wise power withdraws.

Not all the way. Not out of attention. Out of intervention. It has done what it came to do, which was to make sure the moment was not lost, was not crashed, was not interrupted by the body's own panic or the dark's eagerness or the splinter's fear.

It leaves behind only the smallest residue of itself. A warmth in the moisture of the place that is not the place's own warmth, and that will, in a few hours, become impossible to distinguish from the warmth the place has always had. A slight quickening in the chemistry of the phosphorescence that will, by morning, be folded into the chemistry that was always there. A faint held feeling in the chest of the splinter that will fade as the splinter forgets what it was afraid of — but that will, for a long time, be the reason the splinter is not entirely afraid of new places.

The body goes on becoming.

It does not become what the wise power thought it might become. It becomes something stranger. It becomes something that includes the splinter — not by digesting it, not by rejecting it, but by growing around it, the way a tree grows around a stone it has decided to keep, the way a city grows around a ruin it has decided not to clear. The splinter becomes a feature of the body's geography. A small bright fixed point in the cathedral's interior. A thing the body remembers, and that changes, from that moment forward, the way the body organizes its growth.

The clickers' taps, which had been irregular, become a little more rhythmic. The drapers' sliding becomes a little more deliberate. The lamps, which had been darting in pure terror, begin to develop something like patterns — little orbits, little claimed territories, little held spaces in the cathedral's larger volume. The darks continue their patient hunting, but they give the splinter a wider berth than they give any lamp, and they do not entirely understand why. The unfolders continue their slow migrations. The breath continues.

And the splinter sits in the middle of it all, on a brick platform that is becoming soft under it, and watches the cathedral-body do its slow becoming, and is, for the first time since it entered, not afraid.

This is what a wise power does, when it loves a thing that is becoming.

It does not take over the becoming.

It makes sure the becoming has the space to become.

The drip falls. The phosphorescence pulses. A stalk somewhere in the dark lengthens by a quarter inch. The breath of the place continues.
