# What a Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do

The power notices. That is the first thing to say, and it is the most important thing to say, because noticing is what the power is when it is being what it is. The noticing is not a decision. The noticing is the way the power breathes. It is the way the power has breathed since before the stairwell was worn into a stairwell, since before the dwellers were dwellers, since before the deep was the deep and was only the slow proposition of a deep, the way a seed is the slow proposition of a forest.

What the power notices first is that the Verdant Deep is not a place. It is a verb. It is something being done, patiently, by something that has no noun for itself. The power has watched many such things — forests that keep remaking themselves as forests, reefs that cannot stop being reefs, the long turning of worlds that have forgotten they were stones. The deep is one of these. It is unfinished on purpose, in the way a song is unfinished on purpose while it is being sung. The power does not try to finish it. A wise power knows that the unfinishedness is the point.

What the power notices next is the dwellers. They are quiet. The power finds this quiet almost unbearable, in the good sense — the kind of quiet that makes a listening thing lean closer. They are not loud. They are not unhappy. They are not asking for anything. They have made an arrangement with the deep, and the arrangement is not defeat and not victory and not anything the power has a single word for, though the power has many words, having watched many such arrangements in other places, in other centuries. The power knows the dwellers are not in distress. The power knows that distress is not the only thing that warrants a wise attention. Sometimes the things that are well are the things that most deserve to be witnessed. The power comes closer for this reason.

The power does not intervene. Not in the way that intervention is usually meant.

What the power does, first, is small. There is a place in the deep where a new root is probing through the ceiling a little too quickly, in a way that will crack a shelf that three families have lashed their shelters to before the season turns. The power does not stop the root. The power does not redirect it. The power slows it by a fraction — by a breath, by the smallest amount that still counts as slowing. The root will still grow. The shelf will still crack eventually, but later, and the dwellers will have time to relocate on their own terms, in their own season, and the deep will not have been robbed of the root it was in the act of making. The power considers this a small kindness. The power considers it the right size of kindness. Anything larger would be a theft.

The power does another thing. The power finds the dead who must be carried up the stairwell — the bodies the deep will not let rest — and for the length of one climb, on one day, the air around them grows a fraction cooler. Not cold. Cool. The temperature of a held breath released. The children who carry the dead will not know why the climb felt lighter that morning. They will think they are stronger than they were. They will be, slightly. The power thinks this is a fair trade — a small and deniable lie told in the service of a larger truth, which is that the climb has always been possible, and the climb is now, for one set of arms, on one morning, a little more possible. The power does not change the deep's custom, which is the custom of not letting bodies rest. The power eases a single instance of the custom, the way a hand eases a single stitch in a long and living weave. The power thinks the dead deserve a gentler passing. The power thinks the children deserve a gentler carrying. The power thinks the deep deserves to have its custom honored in its own form.

The power does a third thing, and this is the thing the dwellers will never know about, and this is perhaps the truest of the things the power does. The power adds to the deep a small capacity. Not a new bloom. Not a new animal. A small adjustment to the way the spore-mist settles in one corner of the upper cavern, so that the air there is fractionally clearer than it has been. The dwellers who sit there to shape their pottery, to grind their star-soil, to mend their woven walls, will find that they can see the work in their hands a little better. They will think the season has changed, or that a new bloom has opened somewhere out of sight, or that their eyes have finally adjusted. They will not know that the deep has been given a small gift by something that noticed. The power thinks this is the right kind of gift — invisible, useful, deniable. The kind of gift a wise thing gives to a place that has not asked.

The power does not speak to the deep. The power has learned, in its long watching, that speaking to a thing like the deep is not a kindness. A thing like the deep has its own conversation, slow and tidal and patient, in the language of slow replacement, in the language of roots that swell and recede, in the language of blooms that open and close over days and leave only thin gray ash. A word from the power — even a kind word, even a wise word — would interrupt that conversation, and the interruption would cost more than the word would give. So the power does not speak. The power listens. The power has always been good at listening. The deep, if it could be said to notice the listening, would notice that something has been listening for a long time, with great attention, and has not interrupted. This is, the power thinks, the deepest form of respect available to a being that can do almost anything: to do nothing, and to mean it.

The power does not speak to the dwellers either, except in the way that everything speaks to everything by being there. The dwellers will not hear a voice. They will not see a sign on the wall. They will, perhaps, in some dim collective sense that is not memory but is closer to the way a muscle remembers a movement, feel that the place they live in has been looked at, carefully, by something that did not look away. The feeling will not change them. It will not save them. It will not make them less brown, or less quiet, or less woven into the mat. It will be, if it is anything, a kind of warmth that they cannot name, and they will not try to name it, and this will be the right response. The power will be glad of it.

There is one more thing the power does, and this is the thing the power does for itself, and the power is not ashamed of doing things for itself, because a wise power knows that self-tending is not selfishness but is the only way a power can continue to tend.

The power remembers the deep. The power carries the shape of the deep — the roar of it, the wet weight of the air, the slow breath of the trunks, the taste of the sap, the dim places, the patient sound from beneath that the dwellers call the dreaming — the power carries this shape in the place where such things are carried, which is not a place that has a location but is a place that has a quality, the quality of having been witnessed with care. The power will carry this shape for a long time. The power will sometimes, when it is watching some other place, remember the deep, and the remembering will be a kind of presence. The deep will never know. The dwellers will never know. But somewhere in the dim, in the spaces between the blooms, one of the strange shapes that assembles itself from smaller shapes will pause, just for an instant, as though it has been recognized, and it will release, and it will reassemble a yard further on, and the pause will not be remembered by anything that can remember, and it will have mattered anyway.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does. It does not very much. It does not less than it could. It does the right amount. It notices. It slows one root. It cools one climb. It clears one corner of air. It listens without speaking. It remembers without requiring to be remembered. It leaves the deep more itself than it found it, which is the only thing a wise power can leave a place, because a place that is more itself is the only place that has ever truly been improved.

Most people go up the stairwell the same day they go down. The dwellers watch them go with an expression the power cannot read and does not try to read, because some expressions are not for reading. The power stays. The power stays after the travelers have gone up, after the dwellers have settled into their evening tasks, after the blooms have closed or opened according to their slow and private schedule. The power stays and watches the deep do what the deep does, which is to keep becoming what it is, and to let the things inside it keep becoming what they are, in the patient and terrifying way of a place that is not finished and is not meant to be finished, in the way of a song that goes on being sung as long as there is anyone left to sing it, and after that, in the way of a song that goes on being sung anyway, in the roots and the mat and the slow tidal breath, in the dim places where the strange shapes reassemble, in the patient sound from beneath that is almost a word, almost a name, almost the name of the deep itself, which the power cannot pronounce, which the dwellers do not try to pronounce, which the deep does not need pronounced because the deep is not a word, the deep is what the word was for.
