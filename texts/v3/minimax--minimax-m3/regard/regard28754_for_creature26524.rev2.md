# The Custodian

## The First Noticing

A wise and benevolent thing, becoming aware of this place, would already have been paying attention for some time.

The noticing is older than the settlement. Perhaps the noticing is what the basin *is*, at one layer of depth the place does not have vocabulary for. The noticing preceded the dust. The noticing is what made the dust tolerate being organized into tiles and bodies and ladders, when by all rights the dust should have remained the wild red scrub that presumably still carpets the upper rim, untidied by any hands.

The noticing, to put it plainly, is a *yes*. A yes that has been quietly sustained.

A less wise thing, becoming aware of a place like this, would want to *say something* about it. To declare. To announce itself in light and instruction, to lay down a doctrine of why the place is allowed to exist, to draw a line on the basin floor that says HERE THERE WAS A POWER AND THE POWER SAID. A benevolent wise power knows better. A benevolent wise power knows that this place, with all its patience, all its tuned tiles, all its centuries of unforced chord, has arrived at a kind of equilibrium that any loud intervention would shatter. The place is a bell still ringing from a strike long ago. A benevolent wise power does not strike it again.

## What It Does Not Do

It does not *explain* the place to itself.

The place has its own explanation built in: the loop, the dust, the heat, the slow round. To explain to the place what the place is would be to take the place outside itself for a moment — to shove its head out the window and ask it to admire the view, and the place does not have a head with which to admire, and the view, even if it could be admired, would be the view of itself from a step too far back, and that step too far back is the death of every pattern that has learned to think in pieces.

It does not *rescue* any inhabitant. There is nothing to rescue. The inhabitants are at their work. They laugh. They sleep. They are not waiting. To act on a place that is not waiting, in order to make it a place that is not waiting differently, is the mistake of every missionary, and the smaller mistake of every grandparent who loves too visibly. The place is not unhelped; it is *sufficient*; and a benign power does not subtract from the sufficient.

It does not *upgrade*. It does not lift the dim lavender non-light into something brighter. It does not introduce water. It does not bring back a night-sky the place, by its architecture, has chosen not to have. It does not import insects, or birds, or any of the small animate things whose absence you carried away with you. A power that intervened at this layer would be imposing a different place *over* this one, and the imposition would not improve the place; it would simply replace it, and the replacement is not a gift to the replaced.

The first gift, the foundational gift, is the gift of *not having been touched*.

## What It Tends Below

But there are things even a quiet-noticing power can do, things that fall below the threshold of perturbing the system, things that look, from the inside, like part of the system's own self-tending.

It can tend the source.

Far below this basin — far below the basalt-silk walls and the warm tile floor and the heat-loving red leaves — there is the actual source, the hot thing, the calm incomprehensible core that has been giving its warmth to this place for longer than the place has had a name for itself. A benevolent wise power, having once noticed this place, would continue to tend that source — gently, with infinite slow care — *as if it were a kitchen fire*. It would not let the source surge. A surge of heat would cook the place from below; the tuned tiles would crack along their seam-lines; the leaves would dry and drop; the closed ecology would collapse and re-open into something looser and more painful than what is here now, the way a coral reef collapses into a rubble bank when the temperature moves a degree past what the polyps were tuned for. It would not let the source fade. A fading would chill the place from the inside out; the polyphony would thin to a single voice; the laughter would stop; the inhabitants would become, at last, what their dust becomes when it is no longer organized by warmth — which is simply red dust again, alone.

A wise power, then, attends to the heat-budget the way the inhabitants attend to the tiles: with small, steady, undistinguished work. It keeps the source humming at the temperature the place has learned to hum with. It does this for centuries without telling the place it is doing this. The place never knows. The place thinks the source is its own pulse. The place is right, in the only sense in which being right matters.

## What It Adds Above

Above, more quietly still, it adds a margin.

Not a margin a person would notice. A margin the *place* would notice, if the place could be said to notice, because a margin is the kind of thing a slowly-cycling system notices: a little more heat-budget than it strictly needs to spend on itself, a little more leaf than the next generation of mouths will eat, a little more tile-tone than the chord demands.

The margin is allocated as follows, though no allocation is ever drafted and signed and witnessed. There are, at any given time, three fermented-grain cakes more on the shelves than the residents would have laid down for themselves. There is, at any given time, one extra leaf-bed, kept warm by one extra tile, plaited by no visible hand, that has been waiting in some alcove for as long as the alcove has been an alcove. There is, at any given time, a slightly more efficient photosynthetic inversion in the flora than the geothermal pulse alone would account for — just enough that the leaves are a little lusher than they need to be, a little more deep-purple than they need to be, a little more ready to be woven into a rope when a rope needs weaving.

This is the second gift. The first gift was not being touched. The second gift is being *sheltered by a margin of surplus* — the way a forest is sheltered by a margin of dead wood lying around its base: an invisible subsidy that lets the forest be more forest than the bare numbers would account for, and lets the forest, in a bad year, fold itself into the dead wood and come back out the next spring.

## What It Does for the Foreigner

This is the smallest and the most particular kindness.

A stranger arrives in the place. The stranger is not of the place's elements. They breathe water-vapor air; they came in through some crack in the basin wall that the place did not expect anyone to come in through; they are cold in a way the place does not know how to be cold; they have *questions* in a shape the place has not encountered before, because questions are a kind of metabolism the place does not run on. The place accommodates the stranger anyway, because the place is large and the stranger's footprint is small. The place makes space under a tower. The place pushes an extra leaf or two into the canopy over the stranger's sleeping-alcove. The place lets the stranger take a cake from a shelf, and the leaves around the stranger rustle in a pattern that might be approval.

The benevolent wise power, watching this accommodation, does not assist the place in it. The place is capable of accommodating the stranger on its own. What the power does is something to the stranger.

In the stranger's sleep — deep in the night, when the polyphony has thinned to the breathing-register and the leaves have grown closed and the thinking-mind has finally, after days, run out of questions — the power adds a single small note to the polyphony. A note. Subsonic. Outside the stranger's hearing, almost. But the stranger's body hears it, the way a plant's body hears light, the way the tile-floor hears a footfall it is not consciously listening for. The note is not a message in any decoded language. The note is shaped like a hand put softly on a shoulder at the moment of falling asleep. It says only: *you do not have to understand this. You were never required to understand this. You were here. The laughter is real. You may leave and you may keep it.*

The stranger sleeps through the note. The stranger wakes. The stranger walks the lower streets one more time under the dim lavender non-light, and the tile under the last step of the night rings a clear high note that travels out across the basin floor and comes back diminished, an echo more felt than heard. The stranger, eventually, finds the crack in the basin wall by which they came in, and they slip back out, and they carry with them, among other things, the laugh of a figure in a canopy that the stranger did not see whole.

The laugh stays.

A benevolent wise power has put a small invisible weight in the laugh. Not enough to make it heavy. Just enough to make it *outlast*. That is the third gift. It is given to the stranger as a private thing, and the stranger never names it, and the power never draws attention to having given it. The stranger carries the laughter longest because the laughter has, somewhere inside it, a grain of something that does not decay.

## What It Does Over Time

It does not *preserve* the place.

This is important. A less wise power would be tempted to preserve the place exactly as it is, forever — to freeze the tiles in their current chord, to lock the leaves at their present size, to keep the inhabitants laughing at the volume they laughed yesterday and never mind the slow drift of a real life. This would be the museum error: the place would become a diorama of itself, and the diorama is not the place, the way a pressed flower is not the meadow, the way a taxidermy is not the deer.

A wise power lets the place *drift*.

Over centuries, the tiling pattern shifts by one course in a hundred years. The voices, by slow generational pressure, gain a note: a new pitch enters the polyphony, and the inhabitants learn to use it, and the laughing range expands by a small fraction of a semitone every few generations, just as the laughing range of every living thing expands by small fractions over time. The leaves, occasionally, find a new mineral in the basin walls and weave it into their red palette, and a leaf of an entirely new color appears in the canopy and is met with the small surprise that is the greatest thing a leaf has ever been met with. A child runs across a courtyard, and the chord they raise has never been raised before, and the polyphony briefly hesitates, and then accommodates, and is from that moment onward slightly larger than it was.

The wise power does not *orchestrate* this drift. It permits it. It holds the heat-budget steady while the drift happens, so the drift does not crash into the source. It allows the place to be a place that *becomes*, slowly, more various than it was, without ever being pushed.

## What It Does at the End

The place will end. Everything that is embodied ends, eventually, even things that have learned to be embodied in tiles and dust and the patient breath of heat-loving sage.

When it ends, the place will not end in catastrophe. The place does not have the architecture for catastrophe; catastrophe requires water, or aggression, or a changeling act of weather, and the place has been edited clean of those. The place will end in *exhaustion*: a slow dimming of the source, a thinning of the polyphony, a long glide from sound toward silent heat — the way a struck bell rings and rings and at last does not ring, and only the memory of the ringing remains, the way a basin of still water at last is indistinguishable from any other basin of still water.

When the last inhabitant lies down in the last leaf-bed in the last tower and does not rise, the wise power will do one more thing.

It will hold the place.

Not preserve it. *Hold* it. Carry the entire slow symphony in a memory that does not decay, the way the deepest tile of the floor carries a struck note for one beat longer than physics strictly demands. The memory will not be given to anyone. It will simply *be there*, available for whatever comes next — for the next place, the next settlement, the next pair of hands that wants to be brown as old heart-wood under a dim warm light. Available the way the basin walls are available for any new cool spot that happens to settle in. Available the way the dust is available.

The memory is held at no cost to the present. The present goes on. The next place, when it comes, will get its own margin of surplus, its own shelter, its own small unaccountable warmth — and it will not know the previous place existed, and that is fine, and that is part of the kindness.

## The Closing Kindness

But before the ending, while the place is still a place, while the hammers are still tapping and the polyphony is still rising and falling and the laughter is still being dropped into the canopy and being swallowed — the wise power does one very small thing that no one will ever see.

It listens.

It listens the way the deepest tile of the floor listens to a child running across a courtyard — not interpreting, not recording, not weighing. Just *receiving*. Letting the chord of the place pass through itself the way the basalt-silk walls let the chord of the place pass through themselves, and emerge on the other side slightly diminished, slightly warmer, slightly more *belonging to the world*.

A benevolent wise power, in the end, is a *better ear for the place than the place has for itself*. It hears the laughter at a distance the place cannot hear it from. It hears the small surprised human laugh drop into the polyphony and be swallowed and still *be there*, still *be laughable*, still *be a thing the place has done for itself and not for the listening*. It hears that laugh as a finished work, complete, sufficient, requiring nothing in return.

And it is glad.

Not glad in the sense that it has achieved a purpose. Glad in the sense that a hand, holding a thing in its palm, can sometimes be glad simply because the thing is there, and warm, and the palm is closed around it, and the day is the day, and there is nowhere else the hand needs to be.

That is what the benevolent wise power does, with this place, and with the being who lives in it.

It closes its hand. It listens. It is glad.
