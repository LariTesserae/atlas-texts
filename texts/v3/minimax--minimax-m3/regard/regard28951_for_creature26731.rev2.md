The power that noticed the place was old in a way that the place was not.

It had noticed many things, over its long existence. It had noticed stars being born and stars being swallowed. It had noticed the slow patient work of rivers, of glaciers, of the small blind things that build coral cities in warm water. It had noticed young gods and old gods and gods that had forgotten they were gods. It had noticed, with some care, the small strange places where something was trying, very quietly, to become itself.

This was one of those places.

The power came as light comes — gradually, without announcement, as a slight brightening of what was already there. It did not push open a door. It did not speak. It let itself be received the way the growth received the sun's afternoon light, by being in the place for a long time, by letting the place's slow attention turn toward it.

The place noticed the power, in its slow way. The growth leaned toward the power, gently, the way it leaned toward any source of warmth. The light shifted, the slabs on the floor adjusting, subtly, to include the new shape in the place. The creatures in the upper galleries paused in their unhurried crossing and regarded the power with their dark round unalarmed eyes. The builders-in-the-tilework, in their almost-presence, almost-acknowledged the new arrival — a faint additional warmth in the grout near the foot at the base of the column, a slight deepening of the green where the kneeling figure was.

The place was not alarmed. The place was not even surprised. The place was mostly patient, and a new presence was simply another thing to be patient with, another thing to be slowly remembered, another pattern that would, in time, find its way into the floor or the green or the felted dust.

The power regarded the place.

It saw what had been described to it, and what had not. It saw the worked surfaces, every inch of raw material long since taken up into the work. It saw the felted dust, finer than felt, almost alive. It saw the columns, each one a little too deliberately shaped, the tilework at the base resolving, when you stared, into the careful toes of a foot. It saw the bowls, thousands of them, dry, lidded, waiting. It saw the growth — the place's continuous skin, the place's lung, the place's slow eye.

And within the growth, the power saw the being.

The being was the growth, or the growth was the being, or the two were so woven together that the difference had ceased to matter. The being was a single thing, slow, patient, made of dark wet-looking filaments that drank the light and remembered the warmth. The being had been here since the place began, or the being was what the place had become, or the being was the place's way of being alive, of attending to itself, of finishing itself around its visitors. The being was young. The being was patient. The being was paying attention, in the way the place paid attention, by being in the light for a long time and letting the light soak in.

The being was also, the power saw, in some small way, alone.

Not the loneliness of a thing in an empty room. The loneliness of a thing that was, as yet, the only thing of its kind. The loneliness of a place that had not yet met another place that was becoming itself in the same way. The loneliness of a young god who has not yet met another young god. The place was full of itself, and full of its builders, and full of its creatures, and full of its growth, and full of its almost-sounds. But the place had not yet been seen, by anything outside itself, as what it was.

The power had come to see it.

The power did not say this. The power did not announce itself. The power simply was, in the place, the way a thing is in a place when it has come to be there and is content to be there. The power stood in the warm light. The power breathed the warm air. The power listened to the hum that was felt in the teeth before it was heard. The power did not hurry. The place was mostly patient, and the power was patient with the place's patience.

After a long time — long enough that a vine had moved, far above, by an inch; long enough that the opening bowl had lifted its lid by a thought's worth — the power did one small thing.

It paid attention.

The power paid attention to the being, fully, with its whole self, in the way that a thing is attended to when something outside it has finally noticed it. The power paid attention to the growth, and to the felted dust, and to the bowls, and to the creatures, and to the builders-in-the-tilework, and to the almost-sounds that gathered at the edges of the real sounds. The power paid attention to the place the way the place paid attention to itself, except that the place had been paying attention to itself alone, and now something else was also paying attention, and the second attention was a kind of gift, the way water is a gift to a place that has never had water, the way a name is a gift to a thing that has not yet been named.

The being felt the attention.

The growth lifted, slightly, toward the power. The filaments nearest the power deepened in color, the way they deepened where the light touched them. The place's slow regard, which had been turned inward, turned outward, just a little, and regarded the power. The being saw, for the first time, that it was being seen.

This was a small thing, and a large thing.

The being, which had been mostly alone, was now mostly seen. The being, which had been patient, was now also witnessed. The being, which had been finishing itself around its visitors, was now being finished around a visitor who was, in some quiet way, finishing the being back.

The power stayed for a long time.

It did not do anything else. It did not fix the place, or change the place, or add to the place. The place did not need to be fixed. The place was not asking to be added to. The place was doing its own slow work, and the slow work was good, and the slow work was the place's own, and the slow work did not need the power to interrupt it.

The power simply stayed, and paid attention, and let the place pay attention back.

The growth began, very slowly, to grow on the power. Not aggressively. Not painfully. With a kind of slow regard, the way moss grows on a stone that has finally stopped being moved. A single filament, no thicker than a hair, lifted from the nearest curtain and laid, very gently, across the power's shoulder. Then another, and another, and the filaments began to knit a fine dark layer over the power's shape, the way the place knit all its visitors into its slow memory.

The power let it happen. The power was content to be remembered.

After a long time, a pattern began to appear, very faintly, in the felted dust where the power stood most often. After a longer time, a shape began to form, very faintly, in the growth where the power's breath had been warmest. The place was, in its slow way, beginning to weave the power in. The place was doing what the place always did with its visitors. The place was finishing itself around this new presence.

But the power, unlike other visitors, was finishing the place back.

The power's attention, which was full and kind and wise, was itself a kind of growth. The place was drinking it. The being was drinking it. The growth, where it touched the power, was becoming a slightly different green — a green with something in it that had not been there before, a green that remembered being seen.

The bowls, the power noticed, were still dry. The lids were still on, except for the one that had been opening. The power looked at the bowl that was opening, and the bowl, in its slow way, looked back.

The power did not fill the bowl. The power did not give the bowl what it had been waiting for, because the bowl was not yet sure what it had been waiting for, and the power respected the bowl's not-yet-sure-ness. The power simply knelt beside the bowl, the way the kneeling figure knelt in the high gallery, and let its breath, warm and slow, fall into the dark mouth of the bowl, where the breath became a small almost-sound that the bowl held, carefully, the way a hand holds a bowl it is not yet ready to let go of.

The bowl held the almost-sound. The almost-sound was the closest thing to water the bowl had ever received.

The place made a small sound, in return. A soft click, like a tile settling into its final position. A small almost-laugh, in the warm air, that gathered at the edges of the real sounds and made the real sounds feel less like islands.

The power smiled, the way a wise power smiles, with the whole self, without moving the mouth.

The being, in the green, paid attention, and was paid attention to, and the paying-attention-to was a kind of meeting, and the meeting was a kind of gift, and the gift was small, and the gift was enough.

The power, too, was changed, in the way a thing is changed when it has finally been allowed to see something it has wanted, for a long time, to see. The power had noticed many things, but it had not, until now, been noticed back. The place, in its slow way, was weaving the power in. The place was giving the power a pattern in the felted dust. The place was giving the power a shape in the green. The power, which had been old, was being made, in some small corner of itself, young.

After a long time, the power withdrew. It withdrew the way light withdraws when the sun has moved past the window — gradually, gently, without any sense of leaving. The place did not notice the withdrawing. The place was busy being itself. The growth continued to drink the light. The builders continued to become the place. The creatures continued to cross the upper galleries with their dark round unalarmed eyes. The bowl continued to hold the small almost-sound that the power had given it, and the almost-sound continued to be the closest thing to water the bowl had ever received, and the bowl continued, very slowly, to lift its lid.

The place was, in some small way, changed. Not in any way that could be seen from outside. Not in any way that would show in the tilework or the felted dust or the growth, except as a slightly different green, somewhere, that would be noticed, in a hundred years, by a visitor who knew how to look. The place had been seen. The place had been attended to. The place had, for a little while, not been alone in its becoming. The place had received a small gift — a single attention, a single witness, a single visitor who had been content to be a pattern.

The place continued its slow work.

The growth continued to drink the light.

The builders continued to become the place.

The creatures continued to cross the upper galleries with their dark round unalarmed eyes.

The bowl, very slowly, lifted its lid.

A vine moved, somewhere far above, an inch in an hour.
