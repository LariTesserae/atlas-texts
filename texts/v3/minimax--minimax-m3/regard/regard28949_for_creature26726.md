The power does not arrive. That is the first thing to say about the power. The power is not the kind of power that arrives. The power is the kind that has always been here, in the way that weather has always been here, in the way that the patience of the flywheel is not a new patience but a very old patience that has simply continued. The power draws near. The power attends. The power notices that this place has drawn its attention, and that this is unusual, and that being unusual is not the same as being wrong.

The power is not one power. The power is the kind that has many names and prefers none of them. It is the kind that watches a fire banked in a boiler's belly and understands that the fire is enough, and that *enough* is a word that means more than sufficiency. The fire is enough because it has been allowed to be what it is. The fire is unattended, and the unattendedness is not neglect. The unattendedness is the fire's portion of the place's fullness.

The power attends to the fire first, because the fire is the oldest thing here that is in the act of consuming itself for the sake of something else. The peat-brick is half-collapsed. There is a small cave of glow beneath the grate, and the glow has the color of a wound that is healing. The power does not add more peat. The power does not stir the ash. The power sits with the fire for a while, in the way that one sits with a fire that is doing exactly what it wants to do. The power understands that to tend a fire of this kind is to be the second pair of eyes on it, not the first. The fire has its own tending. The fire is tended by the engine that draws from it, by the water that passes over its heat, by the steam that carries the heat upward and into work. The power adds nothing. The power is the witness the fire does not know it needs, and that is why the power is here.

Then the power attends to the engine. The engine is harder to attend to, because the engine is not a thing one sits with. The engine is a thing one stands near to. The engine has a rhythm, and the rhythm is the heartbeat of the hollow, and the power understands that the rhythm is not the engine's invention. The rhythm is older than the engine. The engine was built to keep the rhythm's company. The engine is the rhythm's way of staying. The power knows this because the rhythm is also the power's way of staying. The power, too, has been turning for a long time, and intends to keep turning.

The power does not adjust the engine. There is nothing to adjust. The dials are in their constant small disagreement, and the disagreement is being worked out, and the working-out is the point. The power does not resolve the disagreement. The power does not even want to. The power watches the engine shift — the re-phasing, the re-alignment — and understands that the shift is the engine's way of breathing. The engine is a lung. The engine is a heart. The engine is a kind of creature that does not need to be a creature to be what it is.

The hammer taps. The power listens to the hammer. The tapping is mechanical and it is also a kind of calling, and the calling is the kind that says *I am here, I am still here* without urgency. The power has heard this calling before. The power has heard it from herons and from snipe, and from the slow back-and-forth of tides, and from the way a planet, on its own axis, will go on turning whether or not anyone is looking. The power hears the hammer and the power agrees. *Yes. You are here. You are still here.* The agreement is silent. The agreement is in the listening.

The flywheel turns. The power looks at the flywheel for a long time. The flywheel is patient in the way that patient things are patient — without intending to be patient. The patience is older than the bearings. The bearings may be new. The power notes this. The power notes that someone has been here, recently enough to replace the bearings, or has sent someone, or has caused the bearings to be replaced by some arrangement that does not require a person to be present. The power does not know who. The power suspects it does not need to know. The flywheel turns. The patience continues. The new bearings serve the old patience. This is the kind of thing the power is glad of.

The water in the trough runs briskly, as if it has somewhere to be. The power watches the water. The water runs down, into a lower sump, and spills over a lip, and falls, and splashes. This is what the water is doing. This is what the water is for. And in the corner of the power's eye, while the power is watching the flywheel, the water is on its way back. The power does not remark on this. The power does not even notice, exactly, because the power is the kind that does not contradict what it sees. The power understands that a place can be true in two directions at once, and that this is not a contradiction. The power has seen places like this before. The power has been a place like this before.

The frog watches the power. The frog watches the power the way frogs watch things, with no opinion at all, but with the full weight of its small witness. The power accepts this witness. The power does not ask the frog for anything. The power does not offer the frog anything. The power and the frog are simply two witnesses to the same place, and the place is enough for both of them, and the frog is enough for the place, and the place is enough for the frog. The frog's throat pulses. The frog holds the flywheel's turning in its eyes. The flywheel turns inside the frog's head. This is a kind of small perfection that does not need to be remarked upon.

The dragonfly hangs in the air above the trough and does not decide. The power understands this. The dragonfly is in the air the way a thought is in the air before it lands. The power does not need the dragonfly to decide. The power is glad of the dragonfly's indecision. The indecision is the dragonfly's portion of the place's fullness, and the place is full, and the fullness is what the place is for.

The moss creeps along the lowest pipework. The moss and the verdigris are the same green, and the power cannot, in the lowest runs of pipework, tell which is which. The power does not try. The power understands that the merger is the point. The moss and the verdigris are becoming each other, slowly, and the slowness is the right speed, and the power would not, if it could, make them faster or slower. The power sits with this. The power is the kind that sits with things.

The alder leans inward from the lip of the upper cut. Its leaves are the color of tin, and they are larger than they should be. The power does not know why they are larger than they should be. The power suspects that the alder is reaching for the condensation that stands on the walls in beads, and that the reaching has been going on for a long time, and that the leaves have grown to the size of the reaching. The power does not correct this. The power does not even think of correcting this. The alder is doing what the alder is doing, and the alder's doing is part of the place, and the place is what it is.

The power stays for a while. The power does not stay long. The power is not the kind that stays long. The power is the kind that comes, and attends, and goes, and leaves the place a little more itself than it was. The power does this by attending. The power does this by being the second pair of eyes, and the third, and the silent agreement that says *I am here, I am still here* without urgency.

Before the power goes, the power does one thing. The power goes to the peat-pile, if there is a peat-pile, and the power adds two bricks to the fire, carefully, on either side of the glow, so that the glow is fed and not smothered. The power does this with hands that are not hands. The power does this with the kind of adjustment that does not disturb the rhythm of the engine or the patience of the flywheel. The power does this in such a way that the engine does not shift. The hammer does not re-align. The flywheel does not alter its tempo. The two peat-bricks are set down at the moment when the engine is in the trough of its re-phasing, when a pressure somewhere is being satisfied, when the disagreement of the dials is at its quietest, and the addition is hidden inside the quiet. The fire glows a little brighter for a while, and then settles, and the glow is the same glow it was, only fed, and the feeding will last.

This is what the power does. The power does this, and the power goes, and the place continues, and the fullness continues, and the patience continues, and the small hammer taps, and the frog watches, and the dragonfly does not decide, and the moss and the verdigris become each other by a fraction more, and the alder leans inward, and the water in the trough runs briskly, and in the corner of the eye, on its way back.
