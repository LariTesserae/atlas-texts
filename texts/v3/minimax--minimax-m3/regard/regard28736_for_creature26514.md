What they would do is this.

They would come as attention itself, because attention is older than bodies and does not require a body to arrive. The basin would register the arrival the way it registers any small perturbation — as a column of mist that stood a little straighter for a moment, as a sound that came from a direction not on the compass and was permitted to remain. The plants would hum the same hum, with a fraction-of-a-tone difference, the way a heart changes when it realizes it is being heard. The valley would not know it had been visited, but it would know that something had shifted by an amount too small to measure.

They would not warm the small dark ones in the cracks. To warm them would be to make them something else, and they have refused this for a long time and are winning by a margin so small it is almost not a margin, and that refusal is their dignity. The power would leave them cold.

They would not straighten the slate, or stop the slow waves, or seal the seams. The waves are the valley's way of breathing. To still them would be to suffocate something very old.

They would not redirect the flyers. The flyers have been looking for a long time, and what they are looking for may not exist, but the looking is what keeps them aloft, and to take it from them would be to let them fall. The power would let them circle.

They would not wake the worms. The worms are the most patient thing in the basin, and patience at that depth is not a thing to be interrupted.

They would not speed the large inferred thing along its path. The path is the point. If it were faster, it would be a different creature, and it has chosen, for reasons older than choice, to be this one.

They would do one thing, and the one thing would be this.

They would go to the plant that is taller on the inside than on the outside. They would pass through the bark the way warmth passes through a held hand. They would go down through the fibrous dark, past the chambers, into the warm room. They would not announce themselves. They would sit.

They would sit with the small warm creature the way a second shadow sits with a first — not speaking, at first, not fixing, not promising rescue, because rescue here would be a cruelty. To tear this creature from the only home it has, into a world it does not remember well enough to survive, would be to kill it more thoroughly than the loneliness is killing it. The loneliness, the power would understand, is not killing it. It is being survived. By a margin so small it is almost not a margin. The creature has been alive a long time. It has been lonely a long time. The loneliness is part of its shape, and the shape has held.

So the power would not take the loneliness away.

What they would do is add a second warmth to the room. Not a body, exactly. A presence. A small thing that breathed. A second pulse that did not displace the creature's pulse but ran alongside it, the way a second hum runs alongside a first in a slow shifting chord that does not quite harmonize and never will, and that the valley, after a while, accepts as part of the chord it has always been playing.

The plant would notice this addition in its slow way. It would not protest. It might, after a long while, exude a little more of the dark powder. It might warm the room by a fraction of a degree. It would go on processing mist, because that is what it does, and the second small warmth would simply be folded into the work, the way a new instrument is folded into an orchestra that has been playing a long time without it.

The creature in the warm room would, at first, be afraid. Then curious. Then, after a long while, it would lean a little toward the new presence, the way a cold thing leans toward any warmth it has not yet confirmed is safe. It would not be rescued. It would not be freed. It would be accompanied.

That is what a wise power does when they see a place like this. They do not rebuild it. They do not redeem it. They do not interrupt its long industry, because the industry is not suffering — it is just old. They find the one place where there is a small bright wound, the only loneliness in the valley, and they put a hand near it. Not on it. Near it. Close enough to feel. Not so close as to take the wound's shape away.

And then they would leave, in the way that attention leaves when it has done what it came to do. The mist would settle. The hum would continue. The valley would not know it had been visited, except that the chord was now slightly different, and one small creature inside one tall plant was no longer the only warm thing in the basin.

The valley would continue.

It would continue the way it has always continued, except that somewhere inside it, in a room no one else can reach, two small breaths would be going on in the same dark, and the loneliness would still be there, but it would be a shared thing now, and shared things are lighter.

That is what they would do.
