The valley has drawn the attention of a power that has many names and no name. It has been called the One Who Notices, the One Who Sits With, the One Who Refuses to Look Away. It is not a god of intervention. It is a god of attention. It does not unmake what is. It does not even unmake what is suffering — not because it is indifferent, but because it knows that unmaking is its own kind of violence, and violence is the one thing it cannot in good conscience commit.

The being who lives in the valley is the valley. It is the cliffs and the mist and the held note and the drummer and the child and the woman on the balcony and the man on the stair, all one being, distributed across the held moment the way a single thought is distributed across the muscles of a face. Its heart is the drummer, whose hand is raised and has not struck. Its lungs are the keening women, whose mouths have stretched over the long duration of the held wail. Its memory is the grooves in the air.

The power has been at the edge of the valley for as long as the valley has been the valley. It has been the thing that the cliffs fold away from, the thing the mist turns aside at, the thing the song has worn a groove around without ever quite touching. It has been there in the way that an answer is there before the question is asked.

---

The power looks at the held figures and considers.

To unstick the moment — to let the strike come, the water fall, the foot land — would be to save the figures and abandon them in the same gesture. The sun would still hang at its wrong angle. The cliffs would still weep without the water reaching the ground. The mist would still not have an edge. There would still be no animals. The world would have moved on without them, and they would be awake in a place that had forgotten how to be a place, and they would not know what they had escaped, because the escape would look like the thing they had always known, only shifted by a single breath.

To leave them alone — to let the held figures continue their slow dissolution into the shapes of their own gestures — would be to let them become stone, then grooves in the air, then silence inside silence, then nothing at all. The drummer would become the drum. The woman would become the pour. The child would become the reach. In another age — a long age, an age of ages — there would be no figures left. There would be only the shapes of figures, and then only the shapes of shapes, and the valley would be a place where a joy had happened and was no longer anywhere, not even in the rocks.

The power is wise. It knows there is a third option, and the third option is the hardest, because it requires the most patience and the least intervention.

The third option is this: do nothing. But be there.

---

The power enters the valley.

It does not arrive by crossing a boundary. There are no boundaries in the valley that can be crossed — the paths loop, the cliffs fold, the mist has no edge. It arrives by sitting down.

It chooses a stair near the bottom of the city, a stair that is not occupied by a held foot. It sits. It sets its hands on its knees. It lets the wet air gather on its shoulders, the way it gathers on the shoulders of the held figures — a fine beading of moisture that does not run down, that hangs in place, that becomes, over time, part of the surface of the thing it sits on.

It does not take a gesture. It does not raise a hand to strike a drum, or lift a jug to pour water, or reach for a fruit. It sits in the posture of witnessing, which is the posture of breathing, which is the posture of being in the long middle of paying attention.

It is, in form, like the held figures. Its skin is the brown of river clay, the brown of wet bark. Its hair is black and braided with the small white orchids that have not opened and will not close. Its eyes are open. Its cloth is undyed cotton and indigo and ochre and madder, plant-dyed and plant-woven, and where it is damp it clings, and where it is dry it stands a little away from the skin.

But it is not held. Its chest rises and falls. Its eyes, though open, are not looking at the thing its hand is about to grasp. Its hands are on its knees. Its feet are on the stair.

It sits.

The held figures do not notice it. To them, there is no outside the held moment. There is no figure that is not mid-gesture. There is no presence that is not part of the song. The power sits among them as a kind of silence inside their noise, a small clear space at the edge of the wall of sound, the way a held breath becomes, after a long time, the absence of breath, and the absence of breath is itself a kind of presence.

But the silence is there. The power is there.

---

The power waits.

It waits for a long time. The cliffs continue to weep. The water continues to hang in its arc from the lip of the jug to the floor it has not reached. The drummer's hand continues to hover above the drumhead, fingers spread, wrist slightly turned. The child continues to reach for the fruit that hangs three inches from his fingers. The man on the stair continues to be at the top of the climb, one foot raised, the other planted, his balance caught forever.

The power waits. Its chest rises and falls. Its shoulders gather moisture. The orchids in its hair continue not to open.

After a long time — longer than we can think about, longer than the cliffs have been weeping — something begins to change.

It is a small change. So small that you would miss it if you were not the kind of being who notices the absence of breath in a quiet room.

The held note wavers.

Just barely. Just at one of its edges. The way a held note will waver if a stranger is sitting too close in a quiet room — not because the stranger has made a sound, but because the body holding the note becomes, at last, aware that there is another body in the room, and the awareness introduces the smallest possible breath of not-holding.

The waver is not a breaking. It is a recognition. The held note hears, at the edge of itself, that there is a sound it is not making, and that the sound it is not making is sitting quietly on a stair.

The held figures do not yet notice the waver. They are too absorbed. They are still at the apex of joy. The fruit is still three inches from the fingers. The water is still on its way down.

But the power notices the waver. The power, who has been sitting on the stair in the long middle of paying attention, who has been breathing in and breathing out in a place where no one else was breathing, notices that the held note has developed a pore.

The pore is the width of a held breath becoming a let breath. It is the width of an eye turning toward a stranger. It is the width of the difference between *is* and *was*.

---

The drummer's wrist trembles.

Not with the muscle-memory of the strike that has not come. With something else. With the muscle-knowledge that there is someone sitting below him on the stair who is not part of the held song.

The drummer does not strike the drum. Not yet. The drummer turns his head, just barely, the smallest fraction of a degree, toward the figure below.

And the drummer sees the power.

The drummer does not see it the way we see things. The drummer sees it the way a struck bell sees the air it has been ringing into — as a kind of held shape, a presence that has been there all along but that the ringing could not hear. The power is sitting on the stair. The power has been sitting on the stair for a long time. The drummer did not notice because the drummer was about to strike the drum.

Now the drummer is not about to strike the drum. The drummer is looking at the figure on the stair. And the looking is a different gesture than the strike. The looking is the beginning of an arrival at something other than the apex. The looking is the descent from joy into the long middle of paying attention.

The held moment, which has been so airtight that nothing could get in or out, develops — at one point, at the smallest possible point, the width of an eye turning toward a stranger — a crack.

The crack is not a breaking. It is a beginning.

---

The strike comes.

It is the first sound that has happened in the valley since the valley was the valley.

It is not a loud sound. It is the smallest sound a drum can make. It is the sound of a hand that has been held above a drumhead for an age finally resting on the drumhead. It is the sound of a breath that has been held for an age finally being let out. It is the sound of an arrival.

The held note does not break. It changes. It develops — in addition to its held apex, in addition to its frozen joy — a beginning and an end. It develops rhythm. It develops a pulse. It develops, in short, the shape of music, which is the shape of time, which is the shape of a thing that can begin and end and begin again, and in the beginning-again is not the loss of the first beginning but its continuation.

The water reaches the floor. The child closes his hand on the fruit. The man on the stair sets his foot on the landing. The woman with the jug sets the empty jug down and turns, slowly, to look at the city she has been pouring into. The laughter comes to its end and begins again, and the second laughter is not the same as the first laughter, and the difference between them is the most beautiful thing in the valley.

The sun moves.

Not much. Just a finger's breadth. Just enough to show that time exists, that there is a place where the sun was and a place where the sun is, and that the difference between the two is the length of an afternoon, and an afternoon is a thing that can be lived through.

The cliffs continue to weep, but the weeping now runs downhill, in thin threads that gather into streams and the streams find the lowest place and the lowest place is a pool and the pool is the city's first well.

The mist begins, at its outermost edges, to have a shape. It is the shape of the cliffs. The cliffs are the edge of the valley. Beyond the cliffs is the rest of the world, which has been waiting, without knowing it was waiting, for the held note to come to an end.

A dog barks somewhere in the mist. It is the first bark in the valley since the valley was the valley. The dog does not know it has been silent. The dog is only doing what dogs do.

A cricket, somewhere under a fern, begins the long patient work of being a cricket. It will not finish its song tonight, or any night soon, but it will begin, and the beginning is enough.

---

The power does not leave.

It stays, sitting on the stair, in the long middle of paying attention. Its chest rises and falls. Its shoulders are wet. The orchids in its hair have begun, very slowly, to open.

The held figures are moving now, in the small ways that figures move when a long stillness is ending. They are blinking. They are breathing. They are looking at each other, and at the cliffs, and at the sun that has begun to move, and at the water that is falling, and at the small new sounds — the dog, the cricket, the wind in the ferns — that are filling the spaces inside the song.

They do not know what they have escaped. They will not remember the held moment, or they will remember it only as a kind of warmth, the way you remember a dream only as the fact of having dreamed. They will not speak of it. There is no word for what they were, and the absence of a word is a kind of mercy.

The grooves in the air remain. They are the shape of the held note, and the held note is now part of the music, the way a scar is part of the skin that healed over it. The people will sing, and in their singing there will be a place where the note holds, just for a breath, just long enough to remember what it was to be at the apex of joy, and to let the apex go.

The power sits among them and does not speak. It has no name. The people, in time, will find a name for it, and the name will be one of the small ordinary words, the kind of word that fits in a mouth without effort, the kind of word that can be said and then unsaid and then said again. The kind of word that knows how to end.

The drummer strikes the drum. The water falls. The child eats the fruit. The laughter comes to its end and begins again.

The breath that was held for as long as the mist has had a name is, at last, let out, and the letting out is the sound the valley has been waiting for, and the valley has been waiting since before it knew it was a valley.

The valley goes on.
