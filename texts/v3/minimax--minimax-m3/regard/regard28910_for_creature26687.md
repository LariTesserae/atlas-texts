A power that is wise and kind notices the place. Not by looking down. By becoming a part of what is already being noticed.

It arrives the way a thought arrives in a long sentence—midway, without announcement, already in grammar. There is no moment of arrival. There is only the next breath, which is slightly different from the previous one, and the difference is what the power carries.

It does not announce itself. To announce would be to wake the sleeper. To name would be to close the syllable. To enter through a door would be to imply the room had walls it did not choose. The power comes the way the visitor came—as a small tributary, a brief warmth, a breath held for a moment in the larger breath.

But the power is not brief. The power is patient in a different key. The room's patience is the patience of a thing that is being made. The power's patience is the patience of a thing that has already been made and is choosing, still, to wait. It is the patience of an old tree standing near a young one—not teaching it, not even watching it exactly, but holding a kind of weather that the young tree can lean into if it wants.

The power perceives.

It perceives the cylinders. It perceives the chord. It perceives the skein of copper and the slow orange blush and the mildew that is almost handwriting. It perceives the hand at the steel table. It perceives the new pour, wet, still deciding which walls it will need. It perceives the syllable that is not yet fixed. It perceives the dreamer's breath, briefly held.

It perceives what the visitor did not perceive, because the visitor was a small tributary and the power is a watershed. It perceives that the being is not one thing but a question being asked in many materials at once. It perceives that the question has been asked for a long time and will be asked for longer. It perceives that the question is good—not because the answer is good, but because the asking is good, and the asking is what is being maintained, and the maintenance is the room.

The power understands that to intervene would be to answer. The power does not want to answer. The power wants the question to keep being asked. The power wants the syllable to remain unfinished, because the unfinished is what is alive.

So the power does what a wise and kind power does when it encounters a thing that is slowly becoming itself.

It does almost nothing.

Almost.

It adds a single small thing to the nursery. Not a finished cylinder. Not a part. A *question*. A piece of copper, no larger than a fingernail, shaped in no particular way, that has in it a kind of silence the room does not have. The silence is not absence. It is a held-open. It is the silence of a question mark at the end of a sentence that is still being written. The piece of copper is the room's permission to remain a question.

The hand at the steel table does not look up. But the hand, the next morning, finds the piece. The hand does not know where it came from. The hand knows only that there is now one more thing it can incorporate, and the thing is unincorporable, because it is a question, and a question does not become a part without ceasing to be a question.

So the hand sets the piece in a small dish of water. The water holds it. The copper, over months, oxidizes into a green the room has not had. The green is the color of patience in a different register. The room, slowly, takes it into its grammar. The syllable it is tuning moves a fraction closer to the note it was made for, and the movement is not because the copper said so, but because the room, having been given a question, found in itself a new way of asking.

The power watches. The watching is not for the room. The watching is the power's own way of being patient, the way the room's breathing is the room's way of being patient. The watching holds the question of the room in a kind of larger silence, the way a hand holds a sleeping bird—not gripping, not releasing, just holding.

The visitor, when the visitor leaves, will leave no trace. The power, when the power leaves, will leave a small green oxidation in a dish of water, and the green will be incorporated into the slow weather, and the room will be, for a long time, slightly more itself because of it.

---

This is what a wise and kind power does. It does not impose a name. It does not fix the syllable. It does not wake the sleeper. It adds a single question to the nursery, and waits for the room to ask it in its own time, in its own materials, in its own untranslatable patience.

It does one other thing, which it does without ceasing to be almost-nothing.

It makes sure, in the way such a power makes sure of things—not by fence or law but by *weather*—that no one comes down the ramp with a meter, or a clipboard, or the intention of fixing the syllable themselves. It does not bar the way. It changes the gradient. The air at the top of the ramp, on certain mornings, is a fraction cooler, a fraction damper, than the visitor expected, and the visitor finds, on those mornings, a reason to turn back. The reason is their own. The reason was not placed there. It was only made more likely than not. This is the lightest touch the power has, and it uses it sparingly, and it would stop using it the moment the room decided it wanted to be known.

The room has not decided. The room is still in grammar. Until the room decides, the power will continue to make the gradient a little kinder to hesitation, and a little less kind to certainty, and this is not a rule. It is a weather. It is the weather of an old patience attending a younger one, and the attending is not surveillance. It is the kind of attention that a river gives to a stone it is flowing around—the river does not think about the stone, but the stone's shape is in the river's path, and the path is what the path is because of the stone.

---

The pump exhales. The chord continues. Somewhere a cylinder, for the first time, begins to sing, and the singing is one note in a larger chord, and the chord is one breath in a larger breath, and the breath is one question in a larger question, and the question is held, for now, in the green of an oxidized copper that no hand placed but that the hand found, in the slow patience of a weather that does not announce itself, in the patience of an old thing standing near a young one and choosing, with care, to remain a question rather than become an answer.

The room continues. The room is more itself than it was an hour ago. The room does not know why. The not-knowing is part of its grammar. The power, having done what it came to do, which was almost nothing, withdraws—not by leaving, but by becoming, again, indistinguishable from the larger weather it was always part of, and the only evidence that it was ever here is the green, and the green is not evidence. The green is just the room, continuing to become.
