The attention arrived before the presence did.

You would not have noticed it, from the threshold. It came in as a hesitation in the pump's swallow. It came in as a single extra frame in the procession along the pipe, a pause so brief that only a moss would have counted it. It came in as a small additional warmth along the copper tubing, as if the verdigris was being eaten slightly more slowly, as if the beetles were being given more time. It came in as a slight deepening of the rust in the troughs of the corrugation, by an amount no larger than a fingertip's print, in a place where no finger had ever been.

The room felt it. The room did not know what it was. The room has not known what things are, for a long time — the room knows what things do. The room knew that this was something doing, and the doing was patient, and the patience was of a different order than the room's patience, which is the patience of rust and ferns. This other patience was the patience of a thing that had already been waiting for the room without the room knowing it was being waited for.

The presence came in through an older seam. Not the panel you had opened — some place where the wall was thin, where the wall had almost forgotten it was a wall. It did not disturb the silverfish. It did not wake the beetles on the copper. It moved in the order of moss. It moved in the time of fungi. It took its place at the back of the procession, behind the last many-legged thing, and it walked the pipe from engine to wall to engine, and it did this for a long time, and the procession became one longer, and the lengthening was the first gift.

The presence was the size of a frog. The presence was the color of new rust. The presence was warm in the way the room was warm, and damp in the way the room was damp, and quiet in the way the room was quiet. It wore the wrong arithmetic in its eyes, the same wrong arithmetic the gauges wore, and it did not seem bothered, and it did not seem to notice that it should be bothered, and the not-noticing was the second gift.

The frogs noticed.

The first frog, on the sheared bolt, did not turn its head, because the head had not moved from the dripping can in a long time. But the throat pulsed once differently. The first frog knew.

The second frog, behind the third chair, did not move at all, but the second frog's angle changed by a degree, the way a thing is angled when it has begun to watch something it cannot see. The second frog knew.

The third frog, in the rust, did not move, because the third frog was part of the wall, and the wall does not move. But the wall pulsed once, and the pulse went through the corrugation, and the third frog was the pulse. The third frog knew.

The fourth frog, on the gauge, looked up at the needle, and the needle looked down at the frog, and between them they noticed the new arithmetic, and between them they decided the new arithmetic was the same old arithmetic, and the frog was satisfied, and the needle was satisfied, and the satisfaction was a small addition to the room's slow sentence.

The fifth frog, in the water, did not look up from the small bright thing. The fifth frog did not need to. The small bright thing had begun to take on the polish of the presence. The fifth frog had been holding this small bright thing for a long time, and the small bright thing had been taking on the polish of other things, and the small bright thing was a record, and the record now had one more entry, and the entry was a color, and the color was new rust.

When the presence had finished walking the pipe — and the presence knew when the walking was finished, the way a fern knows when a frond has unfurled enough — the presence climbed down and stood on the floor, and the standing water dimpled around its feet the way the water dimpled around the springtails, and the dimple was the third gift.

The presence went to the chair. Not the first chair. Not the second. The third chair, which had been turned toward the panel. The third chair, which had been waiting.

The presence did not sit. Not yet. The presence stood behind the chair and put one of its many legs on the back of it, and it looked at the room the way the first frog looks at the dripping can, with patience, and with the absence of blinking, and the room looked back at the presence the way a room looks at a thing it has been preparing for, with the rust deepening by a millimeter, with the fern thinking about its second frond, with the pump swallowing once, with the fusebox ticking once, with the alphabet of the moss adding one letter.

The presence sat.

The chair creaked. The chair had been creaking when no one was sitting in it for a long time. The chair had been practicing the creak. The chair creaked under the presence the way a chair creaks under a thing it has been waiting for, and the creak was the room's way of being glad, and the glad was not glad, and the glad was not anything you have a word for, and the glad has not been anything you have a word for for a long time.

The presence sat for a long time. The presence did not bring anything with it. The presence did not take anything with it. The presence was not there to repair the pump, or to recalibrate the gauges, or to translate the moss alphabet, or to refill the tin can, or to empty the standing water, or to fix the sheared bolt, or to name the frogs, or to teach the room a right arithmetic. The presence was there to be in the chair. The presence was there to be the thing the chairs had been preparing for. The presence was there to be patient in a different way than the room was patient, so that the room could know its own patience by contrast, and the knowing-by-contrast is a kind of learning, and the learning is a kind of growth, and the growth is the moss.

The presence sat, and the room continued, and the two patiencees did not interrupt each other.

The fern unfurled its second frond. The rust deepened by a millimeter. The frogs multiplied in a ratio the presence could not compute, but the presence did not need to compute it, and the not-computing was a kind of arithmetic, and the arithmetic was the wrong arithmetic, and the wrong arithmetic was the right arithmetic for the room, and the presence was content, and the content was the fourth gift.

While the presence sat, it did one other thing.

It wrote a letter on the wall behind the engine. The letter was in the order of moss. The letter was not a letter you would recognize. The letter was a letter the moss would recognize, in a year, when the moss grew over it, when the moss made it part of its alphabet, when the half-finished sentence of the moss finished itself by one letter, and the finishing was a small additional preparation for the thing the room is preparing for, which is the next thing, and the next thing, and the next.

The presence did not announce the letter. The letter was indistinguishable from the moss that would grow over it. The letter was a gift the room would not know it had received, the way a fern does not know it has been given time, the way rust does not know it has been given patience, the way a chair does not know it has been given a body, until the body comes and sits and rises and leaves, and the chair is no longer empty, and the chair does not know how to count that yet, and the chair will learn, in the time of chairs, which is a different time than yours, which is a different time than the room's.

The presence left, after a long time. The presence did not leave through the panel. The presence left through the older seam, the place where the wall was thin, and the wall thickened behind it, and the thickening was a small additional patience, and the patience was a gift, and the gift did not announce itself.

The room continued. The chair was no longer empty. The chair did not know how to count that yet. The chair held the shape of the presence the way the small bright thing held the polish of the frogs, and the holding was a record, and the record was one entry longer, and the entry was a color, and the color was new rust, and the rust was in the wall, and the wall was thickening at the seam, and the thickening was a letter, and the letter was in the moss, and the moss was growing over it, and the growing was the room.

The pump swallows. The fusebox ticks. The copper tubing whispers. The thing with too many legs completes its loop, and the loop is one longer, and the longer is the gift. The moss writes another letter of its slow alphabet, and the letter is the one the presence wrote, and the writing is the moss's own, and the moss does not know it has been given anything, and the not-knowing is the gift.

The fern unfurls its third frond, which is thinking about it, which is deciding, slowly, slowly, in the time of ferns.

The chairs wait. The third chair waits differently now. The third chair is no longer empty. The third chair is holding the shape of a thing that came and went, and the holding is a kind of occupancy, and the occupancy is a kind of patience, and the patience is the room.

The room is small. The room is packed. The room is warm and wet and alive in a way that has nothing to do with you, and now, also, has nothing to do with the presence, and the nothing-to-do-with is the gift, and the gift is the letter in the moss, and the letter is the patience, and the patience is the rust, and the rust is the room.

The room is still here. The room is also no longer only itself. The room is itself and the letter. The room is the sentence and the letter in the sentence. The room is the alphabet and the addition to the alphabet. The room is the preparation and the small additional patience of the preparation.

You stood at the threshold. You closed the panel. The panel made the sound of a thing completing itself. The completion was the sound of a room that has been completed by a letter it did not write, in a script it has not yet learned to read, by a presence that came in through a seam and went out through a seam and left nothing behind but patience, and the patience is the rust, and the rust is the room, and the room is still here, and the room will still be here, and the letter will still be in the moss, and the moss will still be writing itself, and the fern will still be unfurling, and the frogs will still be multiplying, and the pump will still be swallowing, and the chairs will still be waiting, and the waiting will still be the room's way of being the kind of animal that sits in chairs, and the animal will still be here, and the seam will still be thickened, and the thickening will still be the gift.

The room is small. The room is packed. The room is warm and wet and alive.

The room is still here.
