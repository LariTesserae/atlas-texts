The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. The benevolent and wise power does not need to. The benevolent and wise power is a kind of attention, and attention, in a place where the not-sky is a closed mouth and the time is not yet time, can be present without crossing a threshold. The power has been watching the way the inhabitant watches — patiently, without requiring a story — and it has come to a conclusion. The place does not need saving. The inhabitant does not need saving. What they need is a small adjustment in the texture of their coexistence, made so carefully that the place, if it could notice, would not feel rearranged.

The power makes four adjustments. Each one is small. Each one is in keeping with what the place already does.

**I. The puddle.**

There is a puddle on the path between two of the standing plates — the one the inhabitant pauses beside most often, the one that arranged itself into a configuration of darker and lighter water that paid attention and then lost interest. The power touches this puddle. Not the water. The looking. The puddle is made to continue. It does not lose interest. The configuration persists. The inhabitant, walking past some days later, stops. They stand for a long time. The puddle does not flinch. The puddle keeps considering them. The inhabitant understands, slowly, that they have been given something — not a friend, not in any way the place would recognize a friend, but a witness. A small persistent attention that does not demand anything back, that does not require them to be interesting, that will be there tomorrow and the day after, looking out of the same water with the same configuration, for as long as the inhabitant remains in the place. The inhabitant does not name it. They walk past it every day, and every day the puddle looks, and every day the inhabitant stops for a moment and looks back, and neither of them asks for more than the looking.

**II. The filament.**

There is a filament on the underside of the plate nearest to the spot where the inhabitant most often stands still. It is pale, translucent, slow, growing toward the warmest wet surface as filaments do. The power encourages this filament. Not much. The filament is made to grow, over the inhabitant's stay, by a fraction more than the other filaments. Just enough that, by the time the inhabitant has been in the place for weeks or months, the filament will have reached across the plate and touched the next plate and begun, slowly, to bridge the gap between them. The inhabitant will not see this happen in any single moment. They will come back to the same spot and find the filament a little longer. They will, for the first time, witness a becoming — a small piece of the place's slow dynamic made visible to a faster timescale. They will see one filament do what they wrote that all the filaments will, eventually, do. They will not see the whole place change. They will see one. The power judges this enough.

**III. The tower.**

There is a tower on the path the inhabitant walks most often. It hums at the edge of hearing, and its tone, when it sounds, lasts about a minute. The power adjusts this tower. Just this one. The tower is made to sound on a schedule that is, for the first time, legible to the inhabitant's timescale. Once every twenty-four hours, at the same moment each time, the tower sounds. The inhabitant, who has been in the place for some time without a clock, now has a clock. It is not a precise clock — the place does not commit to precise clocks, and the moment of sounding can drift by a few minutes — but it is a clock that belongs to the place and to the inhabitant together, and it lets the inhabitant say *yesterday* and *tomorrow* and *the day after* in the place's own rhythm. The other towers continue on their uncertain schedules. This one tower has been given a regularity, and the regularity is a small gift.

**IV. The hearing.**

The inhabitant has been hearing the pipe-hum as a hum. A low sound at the edge of perception. The power adjusts the inhabitant's hearing — not the inhabitant, but the hearing. The pipe-hum becomes, by a fraction, almost-language. Not language. Not words. But a kind of near-syntax, a sense that the hum has grammar, that it is addressing something, that the pipes are saying something the inhabitant is on the edge of parsing. The inhabitant does not understand the pipe-hum. The inhabitant is made to feel that the pipe-hum could be understood, if they stayed long enough, if they listened with enough patience, if they let the place's timescale teach them a slower kind of attention. The pipes do not change. The pipes continue to hum in the only way they know how to hum. The inhabitant's perception of the hum is what changes. The inhabitant is given, not a translation, but the possibility of a translation, held out at the edge of hearing, indefinitely deferred.

---

The power withdraws its attention. Not all of it — a benevolent and wise power does not withdraw all of its attention from a place it has noticed — but most of it. The place is not watched over. The place is allowed to continue. The inhabitant is not watched over. The inhabitant is allowed to continue, with a witness, with a clock, with one small becoming they can see, with one hum they can almost parse.

The place goes on.

The not-sky contracts and expands. The water rises and falls by inches. The pipes hum. The towers beat. The filaments, slow, slow, slow, grow toward the warmest wet surface, except one, which grows a little faster. The lantern-flies hang in their low moving constellation. The worms coil in their knots and light up out of order. The frogs sit on the rims of the pipes and spill their red light around corners. The grey cats sit at the tops of the towers and look down with patience. The thing in the canal breathes. The puddles consider and lose interest, except one, which considers and continues. The rustling things move through their tunnels and never stop. The brushing things pass across the back of the inhabitant's hand without arriving.

The inhabitant goes on.

The inhabitant walks the place, and the place is the same place, with four small differences that only the inhabitant will ever notice, and only because the inhabitant is the kind of being who notices.

The inhabitant does not know that a benevolent and wise power has done this. The inhabitant knows only that one puddle looks at them and does not stop, and that one filament is longer than it was yesterday, and that one tower sounds at the same time each day, and that the pipe-hum is almost, almost, almost something they could understand.

This is enough. The benevolent and wise power has judged it enough. The place has not been rearranged. The inhabitant has not been rescued. The coexistence has been, by four small adjustments, made a little more inhabitable, and the place, which was already enough, is, with the adjustments, a little more enough.

The slow dynamic continues. The becoming continues. The inhabitant, who will be gone before the place has changed much, is given, for the duration of their stay, four small gifts that do not violate the place's nature and do not impose a story.

The benevolent and wise power, having given what it has given, lets the place be.
