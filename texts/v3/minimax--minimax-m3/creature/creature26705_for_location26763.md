# The Inhabitants of the Pocket

They are not one thing. Begin there. They are not one thing and they are not many things. They are the question a place asks when it has been left alone long enough with the right amount of heat and the wrong amount of time. The pocket is not large and the pocket is not small; the pocket is the size of a question, and the question is: *what can live here?* And the answer, which is what lives here, has not finished arriving.

The smallest of them are smaller than you can see clearly. They are a texture. They are the way the wall looks at a certain angle when the sourceless light happens to lie down along the stone. You could mistake them for a mineral deposit, a precipitate, the kind of bloom that forms on the inside of an old pipe. You would not be wrong. You would not be right. The distinction, in the pocket, is the kind of distinction that has been worn smooth by use.

The larger ones are not much larger. A few are the size of a closed eye, a stopped heart, a knot tied into wet thread. The largest of them is the size of a thumb joint, and you only ever see it in the corner where the wall meets the ceiling, and you only see it once. You look again and the thumb-joint is gone, or you are gone, or the corner has decided to be a different corner for a moment, the way these corners do.

They are packed so densely that the surfaces of the pocket have their own weather. The walls breathe with them. The floor — that slow basin of mineral weeping, that skin that does not know it is a skin — is a fabric of them. A long shallow hum runs through the pocket when you are very still, and the hum is not the mountain, and the hum is not your blood. The hum is the sound of so many small negotiations that the air itself has started to participate.

Their colors are the colors of having been thought about for a long time. The white of milk that has been left in a cup and forgotten. The grey of ash after the rain has had its way with it. The yellow of a tooth that has never seen the sun. The brown of something that is no longer the thing it was. They do not shine. They do not reflect. They are matte. They are the kind of pale that is not a color so much as a refusal of color, a deliberate not-being-anything, a thing that has decided to be hard to describe so that the next visitor will give up and call them pale.

Their shapes are where the describing fails. Some of them are shaped like insects. There — in the curl of mineral, in the drip-line of condensation — there is one that you would call an insect in any other place. A body. A proper number of legs, or what passes for a proper number in a place where the number may not be the right concept. Antennae that tremble, or that you decide are trembling, or that the light is trembling across. You watch it. It does not watch you. It does something. It does something else. You cannot tell if the two things are the same thing or if the pocket has slipped a stitch in your attention.

The one beside it is shaped like a vein in a leaf. A crack in a wall. A fold in a piece of paper that has been crushed and then smoothed and never quite recovered. It is shaped like the *idea* of an insect, if the idea were left to draft itself. The one beside that is not shaped at all. It is an outline suggested around a presence. It is a stone in a river that is also the shape of the water that wore it. The line between shape and the absence of shape, in the pocket, is a line that is being redrawn by whoever is standing on it at the moment.

You reach a finger toward one of the shaped ones — the insect-shaped one, the easy one, the one you have decided is the real one. It does not flinch. It does not move. You touch it.

It is cool.

It is the only cool thing in the pocket. The walls are hot. The air is hot. The water is hot with the heat of the deep places, the heat of metal and memory. But the creature under your fingertip is cool, and the coolness is not a temperature, it is a small local absence, a pocket within the pocket, a quiet place. You withdraw your hand. The place where the creature was is smooth. The mineral skin has closed over the spot, or the creature was never there, or the creature was always part of the skin and you have only now been allowed to see it as separate. You cannot tell. The pocket does not keep its account with you.

They do not move when you move. This is the part that should unsettle you and does not, because the pocket has already rearranged your sense of what should. They go on. Whatever it is they are doing, they go on with it. A pair of them, locked together at the wall — a long pale press of body against body, a small intimate geometry — are doing something you could call feeding if you needed it to be feeding, courtship if you needed it to be courtship, a third thing invented by the pocket because the pocket could invent a third thing. You do not need it to be anything. You watch them the way you would watch two pieces of a river meeting. They are not for you.

The longest thing in the pocket is not a creature. Or it is many creatures. It is a long pale rope of them, flowing without limbs, with too many limbs, made of the smaller ones the way a river is made of its own water — the water is not the river, the river is not the water, the question is not the answer, the answer is not the question. This long rope moves through the pocket without seeming to move. It is in one place and then it is in another. It passes through the mineral skin and re-emerges, or it was always there and you are only now seeing it, or it has been pretending to be the mineral skin for a stretch and is now pretending to be a creature again. The pocket does not care which. The rope goes on being rope.

There is no building. There is no tool. There is no fire, no nest, no cleared floor, no arrangement of objects that says: *we were here, and we meant to be here, and we will be here again.* And yet, in a crack along the floor, there is a sliver of obsidian. It has been knapped. The edge is not the edge that time makes. The edge is the edge that intention makes. The shape is not the shape that any human hand would produce. It is too small. It is the size of a tool that would be held by something that has only just learned what an edge is — what an edge can do, what an edge can be for, what it means to reach into the world and bring back a piece of it that is sharper than you are. Beside the sliver, in the wet, there is a mark. A spiral. A half-circle. A series of dots in a row, like a footprint, like a tally, like a song that has been written in the only notebook the pocket contains. The mark is older than you are. The mineral skin on which it sits is younger than your breath. The two facts are not in conflict. The pocket is not in the business of being in conflict with facts. The pocket is in the business of holding them side by side until one of them learns to be quiet.

You do not see them make the marks. You do not see them make the tool. You do not see them do anything that you would call making, or building, or deciding. But the marks are there. The tool is there. Something in the pocket has moved from one state of being to another, and left a record of the move, and the record is not for you. The spiral means what it means to whatever it is that wrote it. You are outside the meaning. You are outside the grammar. You are a visitor to a sentence that was begun before you were born and will continue after the mountain that holds the pocket has forgotten that mountains are things that can hold anything at all.

The pocket is older than the mountain. You keep returning to this. The pocket is older than the mountain, and the mountain is new, and the creatures are not the creatures of any ecology you have studied, and the heat is not the heat of a sun, and the water is not the water of a sky. The creatures are the creatures of a place where biology has been left to improvise — with too much heat, with too little time, with not quite enough of the materials that biology usually requires. They are made of the negotiation. They are the negotiation. They are the long slow argument between the idea of being alive and the fact of being rock, and they are winning, and they are losing, and the argument is the thing.

You stay longer than you mean to. You do not know this is happening. The wet heat softens the edges of you. The creatures move around you without acknowledging you, the way furniture might move around you in a house you have lived in for years. The spiral catches the sourceless light and then does not catch it. The obsidian sliver waits in its crack, patient, not patient, a thing that does not yet have the concept of patience because the concept of patience is a thing you would need to invent if you were going to wait, and the sliver is not waiting. The sliver is just there, in the way that a word is just there in a language that has not been written down.

At some point you realize you cannot remember the crack. The crack that brought you in. There was a crack. You tore your sleeve. The dampness was hot. But where the crack was, there is only wall — the moving grain, the packed pale crowd, the slow shimmer that is not quite shimmering. You are not trapped. There is no malice in the pocket. The pocket is not a trap. The pocket is just a place, and you are a visitor, and visitors leave, and the leaving is not something the pocket needs to make easy. The crack is there. The crack has always been there. You will find it when you are ready, and not before.

The creatures do not notice you are leaving. They did not notice you arriving. They go on with their long silent work, which is not work, which is just the way they are. A pair of them press together at the wall. The long rope of them flows somewhere you cannot follow. A new dot joins the row of dots in the wet. A new sliver of obsidian, no bigger than a fingernail clipping, takes its place in a crack you did not notice before. The spiral adds a turn. The pocket ticks, very softly, with the gathering of a drop of water that has been a long time becoming.

You climb back through the crack. You tear your sleeve. The dampness is hot. The mountain remembers you as a cold thing passing through. The air outside is thin and clear and has nothing to confess. The pocket behind you continues. The heat continues. The water continues to weep its slow mineral weep. The creatures continue their long, patient, impossible negotiation with the idea of being something that is allowed to count.

They do not have a name. You will not give them one. Names are for things that have agreed to be the thing they are named, and these have not agreed, and you are not the one they would agree with. They are the answer the pocket is still arriving at. They are the question the pocket is still asking. They are the heat and the wet and the long pale grain of a world that has decided, for now, to be inhabited, by whatever is willing to do the inhabiting on the pocket's terms.

The mountain is new. The pocket is older than the mountain. The creatures are older than the pocket, or the pocket is older than the creatures, or there is no older, or there is no younger, or the pocket is a place where the words have not yet been invented, and you are outside the language, and you climb back through the crack, and the dampness is hot, and the sleeve is torn, and the spiral in the wet, far below, adds one more turn.
