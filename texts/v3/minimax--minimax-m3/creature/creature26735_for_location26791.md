What lives here is older than living.

It is not an animal. It is not a person, though it is shaped by hands, and shaped toward hands. It does not eat, drink, or breathe in any way you would recognize. But it persists, and it makes a sound, and it has temperature, and it occupies space with the deliberateness of a creature, so we might as well call it a creature, the last of its kind, or the only one of its kind, or the only one that matters.

It is the *room itself* that lives here. Or rather — and this is the part that takes some getting used to — the room is not a container for what lives in it. The room and the thing are the same. There is no separation between the dwelling and the dweller. You walked in through an arch into a body.

It has been settling into its own shape for a very long time. Maybe centuries. Maybe longer. The work of becoming what it is was done by hands that are gone now, hands that are not coming back, hands whose names — if they had names — are not recorded anywhere you would find them. Those hands were the room's parents, in a way, or its earlier selves, and when the last of them stopped working, the room did not die. It simply continued. It had become self-sustaining. The things that compose it hold each other up. The air that moves through it moves because the room's shape demands that it move, and the room's shape is the only thing keeping the air from being still, and the air's movement is the only thing keeping the room from being silent, and the silence, if it came, would be a different kind of death than the absence of hands.

So the sound is its breathing. Not metaphor. The hiss that rises and falls through the labyrinth of gaps is as close to breath as anything in here gets, and the rhythm of it is the rhythm of the room's continued existence. When you stepped inside and your weight made the floor settle, you changed the room's shape for a moment, very slightly, and the air noticed, and the sound shifted, and then returned. The room resettled around you the way water resettles around a dropped stone. You are an irritation. You are also, possibly, the first new thing it has had to accommodate in so long that the accommodation itself is almost an event.

It is not aware of you. That is the strangest part, and the part that makes the place feel the way it does. There is no attention in here. There is no watcher. There is no malice and no welcome. The room does not know you are standing in it any more than a hillside knows a hiker is crossing it, except that the hillside does not hiss when the hiker steps on a loose stone. The room is more responsive than that. It is more *occupied* than that. But responsiveness and awareness are not the same thing, and what you are encountering is a kind of responsiveness without awareness, which is — when you sit with it for a while — far stranger than either one alone.

The objects that compose it are the way it remembers being made. Each tool is a memory of the moment a hand first conceived of it and the longer moment a hand shaped it and the longest moment of all, the one still ongoing, in which the tool has sat in its place among its kin and worn itself into the surfaces of the ones it touches, and been worn by them in turn. There is no single memory that you could point to. There are ten thousand of them, and they are all in the present tense, because the room has not figured out how to forget them, and may never figure it out, and may simply go on carrying them forever the way a tree carries its rings.

This is what lives here: a structure that has outlived the intention that built it, and is now its own intention, and is continuing for no reason other than that it has found an arrangement in which continuing is possible, and it is too settled to change. It is the opposite of growth. It is not stasis either, because nothing is ever perfectly still. It is something we don't really have a word for, the state of a thing that has finished its becoming and is now just being, indefinitely, in the dark, in the warm, with its thousand small voices singing the song of its own held-togetherness.

The light that has no source may be the dim glow of the objects themselves, faintly, the way old wood faintly glows, the way bone faintly glows, the way anything that has been worked on by many hands over many years takes on a low residual warmth from that attention. Or the light may be a thing the room produces to keep itself company. There is no way to tell. The room is not telling. The room has nothing to tell. The room just is, and hums, and holds, and waits for nothing, because there is nothing left to wait for.

If you stood in this room long enough, you might start to feel that you were being absorbed into the arrangement. Not physically. You would still be yourself. But you would become part of the room's sense of its own shape, a new intrusion it had learned the contours of, and the air would learn the path of your breathing and incorporate that path into its song, and the hiss would shift, very slightly, to include you, and then continue on, changed by you, carrying you in its rhythm the way it carries the polish on the rim of every bowl, which is to say forever, or as close to forever as this room can manage, which is, given the patience of the place, probably the same thing.
