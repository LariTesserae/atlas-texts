# What Lives Here

What lives here has learned to keep still.

The most numerous things in the basin are the small, winged insects that hold themselves in the warm columns of air above the foliage. They do not fly much. They hover, or they sit, deep in the green parts of the leaves where the sun is broken up. A few are pale and almost transparent. Some are darker, with a slight metallic sheen, and when they move between sun and shadow they seem to disappear and reappear as if they were being toggled on and off. They are not the same insects as the ones that live in fields or gardens. They are quieter. They do not bump into the walls. They do not rise above the rim. They belong to the basin in a way that suggests they have always belonged to it, or that the basin has always belonged to them, and the question of which came first is not a useful one to ask.

The lizards are also still, but for a different reason. The lizards are quick, but only in short bursts — a flash along a stem, a vanishing, an arrival at a different stem three meters away, where the lizard flattens itself against the bark and becomes part of the bark. They eat the insects. They do not eat many of them. They are not hungry very often. Their bodies are the color of the iron-stained stone when they are in the sun, and the color of the wet darker stone when they are in the shadow, and this is not a coincidence; the lizards in the sunny zones are paler, and the lizards in the shadowed zones are darker, and the two populations do not seem to cross over. They live in the same basin. They do not appear to know about each other. The shadowed lizards are slightly larger, which may be because the shadowed zones are damper, or because the prey there is different, or for reasons that have to do with neither.

The plants themselves are the largest living things, and they are not several species. The plants are a few species, repeated. The same forms recur. A leaf with a particular shape, a stem with a particular thickness, a growth habit with a particular angle — these recur, and they recur in the same proportion, as if the basin had been seeded once with a fixed set and the set has simply been maintained. The patterns in the directly-lit zones are the most visible evidence of this. The spiral. The grid. The leaves in rows. These are not patterns the plants are making as they grow. They are patterns the plants were placed in, or set toward, and the plants have held to them. A spiral is not a thing a plant decides to do. A spiral in a plant is either a thing the plant is, or a thing the plant is being. In this basin it is difficult to tell which.

The pool has its own small population. In the warm water there is a film of something that might be algae and might be a kind of slow animal, a matte that skims the surface and trembles when a drop falls into it. Beneath, in the dimness, there are small soft shapes that move without seeming to go anywhere. They breathe. They do not seem to do much else. The water is warm. The water is still. The water is fed by what is fed to it, and what is fed to it is always the same.

The column has nothing living on it that I can identify. Its surface is too smooth, or too dark, or too cold at night when the dew would form. A small patch of grey lichen clings to the north face near the base, and that is all. The column is not, by any normal measure, a place that wants things growing on it. The plants in the thicket around its base have, in fact, kept a small clearance — a ring of bare stems and exposed stone, as if the column were a fire that the plants had decided to keep a distance from. The clearance is not perfect. A few stems have leaned in. But the clearance is there.

Nothing larger lives in the basin. The walls are too high for most things to climb down. The rim is too exposed, and the heat below is not what most things are looking for. A bird, occasionally, descends in a fast diagonal — a kiteshape, a strike at a lizard that has moved wrong — and then climbs back out. The bird does not land. The bird does not rest. The bird is using the basin as a larder and is not interested in the larder for itself. The basin is a place the bird visits. The basin is not a place the bird is from.

The terrace has been colonized. The lower steps are buried. The upper steps are bare but have accumulated a fine grey dust that is not quite soil and not quite ash. A small spider has built a web between two of the upper stones, and the web is intact, which means few things fly through here to break it. The terrace was made for use by something that stood upright and walked down the steps. Nothing that stands upright and walks lives here now. The terrace has been absorbed. The terrace is still there, but it has become part of the basin in the way that a fallen tree becomes part of a forest — a feature, a texture, a thing the roots go around.

What lives here, then, is a closed community. Not a small one, but a closed one. The insects feed the lizards. The lizards feed, occasionally, the bird. The plants feed, in their slow way, the film on the pool. The pool feeds the air. The air feeds the plants. The cycle is tight and is not leaking. Nothing is migrating in. Nothing is migrating out. The basin holds what it holds. The basin has held it long enough that the held things have begun to suit the basin the way water suits a bowl — not by being poured in but by being what remains when everything else has found somewhere else to be.

The strangeness of the basin is not that anything unusual is happening in it. The strangeness is that nothing unusual is happening in it, has happened in it, or will happen in it. The basin is the same. The basin will be the same. The things that live here have, in their various ways, agreed to this. The insects are quiet. The lizards are quick. The plants are arranged. The pool is still. The column is upright. The terrace is a terrace. The bird passes over and does not stop. The basin does not ask the bird to stop. The basin is not waiting for the bird. The basin is not waiting.

What lives here lives inside a question that has been answered, or that has decided not to ask itself. Either way, the answer is the same shape as the question, and the basin is that shape, and the things in the basin fit it the way a key fits a lock it was cut from.
