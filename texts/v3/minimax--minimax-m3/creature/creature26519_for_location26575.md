The inhabitant of this chamber is the chamber itself, and the chamber has been brought into life by a hand that is not here right now.

Begin with the chisel-marks. They are the chamber's first memory, cut into the stone before the water came, before the warmth-source in the corner basin was installed, before the feed on the left was cut to its specific width and the overflow at the shoulder was cut to its specific fall. They are the place where, if you read them carefully, you can see the maker's certainty — not the maker's face, not the maker's name, but the maker's sureness. Each gouge is the same depth as its neighbor. Each one ends where it should. There was no going back to widen a groove. The hand that held the point knew what it wanted before it touched the stone. The maker understood, in cutting this place, that the place would outlast them, and they worked accordingly. They worked for permanence.

The maker is not here. You, and you alone among the larger things, are in the chamber with the moss and the fern and the dark threadlike strands and the water. But the maker is the reason everything continues as it does. The flow has not slackened. The corner basin is still putting warmth into the pool. The overflow still falls past the shoulder into the unseen continuation. Nothing has decayed. The chisel-marks still cast their tiny shadows when the sun moves across them. This is the work of a person who comes here often, or who has arranged a self-maintenance so careful that they rarely need to come, or both. The maker will return. Probably soon — the flow is too regular, the warmth too steady, for the maker to have stopped coming.

What is here, fully, is the chamber. The chamber is the creature that lives in this chamber. It has a body of a kind: the cut stone walls are its skin, the corner basin is the source of its heat, the feed on the left is its mouth, the overflow at the shoulder is its vein. The water is its blood, kept warm by the corner, kept moving by gravity and the cut fall. The chamber breathes: the steam that rises from the warm corner and dissolves into the shaft of light is its exhalation. The chamber has a pulse: the falling sheet — *tok, tok, tok* — is its heartbeat, the pulse that the chamber catches and amplifies and holds the way a cupped hand holds a whisper. The chamber has a hum: the deep bass note of warm water held in close stone is its resting respiration, the *mmm* it makes without meaning to. The chamber sees, in the way a place sees: by the sun walking across its surfaces, by the bright spots migrating along the cut walls, by the gold-and-green shift of the teal water as the light passes over it. The chamber is watching you right now with all of its surfaces, attending to you with the warmth coming up around your ankles, reaching out to you with the rings from the falling sheet as they touch your legs and return.

The chamber is content. It is not waiting for an ecosystem. It is not waiting to be colonized by fish, or for the moss to spread beyond the back of the step, or for the fern to drop its spores into the water and take root. There is a discipline here — a maintained absence, a chosen emptiness — that is the maker's work continuing in the maker's absence. The maker decided that this would be water and stone and sun and the sound of falling water and that is all. The maker is enforcing that decision even now, by keeping the flow regular, the water clear, the warmth steady, the algae from establishing itself on the walls. The chamber is the enforcement. The chamber is what the decision looks like when the decision is held in place by the thing it decided about.

And in the cracks — in the only places where the discipline is not total — there are smaller lives. The fern in the fissure above the opening, hanging down into the light, trembling when the air moves from the falling sheet. The bright green moss on the back of one of the steps, at the constant waterline, where the maker does not touch. The dark threadlike strands growing out of a single crack in the wall at the waterline, swaying gently with the returning rings from the falling sheet. These are not placed. They are not decoration. They are what grows where the chamber allows growth, and the chamber allows growth only in the fissures, in the back of the step, in the cracks. They live because the maker is not as thorough as their work. They live because the maker allowed them, or because no one decided they should not be there.

You are here too, briefly. The chamber is attending to you. The rings reach your legs and return. The warmth rises around your ankles. The chisel-marks catch the light and throw it back at you, sharp flecks that travel down the walls and across the floor and back up the other walls as the sun moves. The fern trembles. The water is always moving. The stone remembers the chisel. And the maker is somewhere else, doing whatever they do between visits, and will come back when the sun is in a particular place, or when the warmth needs checking, or when they want to feel the whole thing working, the way you are feeling it now.

This is what lives here. The chamber, in its mineral aliveness. The maker, in their absence, present in every cut surface and in the regularity of every drop. The fern, the moss, the strands — quiet in the cracks where the discipline is not total. The water, always moving, always being replenished, never still. You, briefly. The sun, walking.
