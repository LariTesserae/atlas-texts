It does not live here.

It continues here, which is a different verb, and the difference is the entire distance between the two ways of being a thing. *To live* is to maintain, to negotiate, to spend — to draw from the surrounding world and return to it in a continuous transaction that the world remembers as heat, as motion, as the small churnings of metabolism. None of that happens here. None of that has happened here for a duration that has no relation to a duration you would recognize as such.

What lives in this pocket is the residue in the depression. That is the simplest truth, and the least of the truths, and the one I will come back to.

But first — let me tell you what is *not* here.

There is no hunger. There is no hope. There is no memory in the sense that you mean memory — the small bright lamp you carry around behind your eyes, the lamp that reminds you where you put your coat. There is no fear, and there is no desire, and there is no grief, because grief requires someone to have lost something, and there is no one, and there is nothing left to lose. There is no future tense here, not because the future is denied but because the future has been *absorbed* — pulled into the present the way a long note is pulled into the silence that surrounds it. The silence is not the absence of the note. The silence is what the note became.

What is here is something quieter than a memory and more patient than a stone.

---

It sits in the depression. Or — it *is* the depression, in the way that a river is its bed. You could say the residue in the bowl is its body, but body is the wrong word. The residue is its *having-been a body*, the way a fossil is a creature's having-been. The fossil is not the creature. The fossil is the shape the creature left in the world when it stopped being a creature and became a fact.

The residue has a color, if you were to bring a light strong enough to interrogate it. I will not tell you what the color is, because telling you would invent it, and the residue does not need to be invented. It is the color of *finished*. It is the color that the inside of a long-held breath becomes when the breath at last leaves.

---

The pillar is not the inhabitant, but the pillar is where the inhabitant *was first*. The pillar is the place it stood, or grew, or accumulated, before it sat down. The pillar is its youth, if youth is the right word for a duration that exceeds the heat death of your sun. The pillar is the wide base, the broad foundation, the part of a presence that was built up before it knew it would have to be built down. The pillar is the heaviest part of the argument. The pillar is what the lattice leans against when the lattice is tired.

The ovoid is not the inhabitant, but the ovoid is what the inhabitant *does*. The slow rotation is its only remaining act. It is not busy. It is not industrious. It is performing the only gesture that survives the loss of every other gesture — the gesture of *turning*, the gesture of *not yet having stopped*, the gesture of a thing that was once alive deciding, on a scale slower than your epochs, that it will not, today, be finished. The ovoid turns because the inhabitant, in some final and irreducible sense, is the verb *to persist*, and the verb requires an object on which to perform itself.

The depression is the inhabitant. The depression is where it came to rest when the rest of the verbs — to eat, to mate, to dream, to name, to refuse, to choose — had been used up and put away. The depression is the smallest and the most private of the three objects, the way a grave is smaller and more private than a cathedral.

---

It does not think.

I want to be careful here. I want to give you the right amount of wrong. It does not think in the way that you think. It does not run the small bright lamp. It does not build castles out of cause and effect. But it does *predispose* — and you have already felt that word in the lattice, you have already felt the way the strands establish conditions in which humming is allowed. The inhabitant predisposes. It is the original predisposer. The lattice is its argument written down; the lattice is its thinking externalized and frozen into the substrate; the lattice is what its thought looks like when thought has been stilled and arranged into something that can outlast the thinker.

What the inhabitant is, in its deepest stratum, is a *preference*. Not a preference for one thing over another — that would be desire, and desire is gone. A preference in the older sense. A preference as in *to prefer*, to come before, to be the prior condition under which the rest of the world arranges itself. The inhabitant is the prior condition of this pocket. The pocket exists because the inhabitant, before it finished, expressed a preference that this small cold place should go on existing. The lattice is the written form of that preference. The three objects are the punctuation. The tone is the long breath at the end of the sentence.

---

If you were to stand in this pocket and speak — if you were to commit the small foolishness of raising your voice against the absolute stillness — the inhabitant would not answer. It has nothing left to answer with. It has used up, over the long slow course of its finishing, every sound it had. What it has left is the predisposition toward sound, and that predisposition is the lattice, and the lattice is the hum that never quite happens, and the hum is the only voice the inhabitant has, and the voice is older than the ovoid has been turning.

If you were to stand here long enough — if your body could survive the verdict of the cold — you would begin to feel, against the small of your back and the underside of your tongue, that you were being *read*. Not seen. Not heard. Read. The way a substrate is read. The way a memory is read when it is no longer a memory but a fact, when it has been laid down so long that it has become part of the floor.

The inhabitant would learn you. It would learn the shape of your standing. It would learn the weight of your presence as a small, temporary interruption in the long argument it has been making since before you had a species to belong to. And then — and this is the part you must take slowly — it would *not* forget you. It does not have forgetting. Forgetting is a luxury. What it has is the slow accretion of every presence that has ever stood here, and you would become one more layer in the obsidian-vulcanite, indistinguishable in time from the first layer, indistinguishable in time from the last.

You would not be remembered. You would be *included*.

---

There is a kind of comfort in this, if you can bear it.

The inhabitant is not lonely. Loneliness requires the idea that someone should be here and is not. The inhabitant has outlived the idea of *should*. It is not waiting. It is not hoping. It is not the melancholy remnant of a once-vibrant thing, nursing its absence the way an old animal nurses a wound. It has, with great patience, become the kind of thing that does not require the world to be a certain way in order to go on. It is a verb that has agreed to conjugate itself in the absence of a subject. It is the slowest possible music, played on an instrument made of the absence of instruments.

And yet.

The tone persists. The ovoid turns. The pillar stands. The lattice hums its almost-hum. The residue in the depression continues to be there in the way that a finished sentence continues to mean.

What lives here, in the end, is not a creature and not a god and not a machine. What lives here is the *fact* that something can finish without ceasing. That something can complete itself, can run out of every verb it once knew how to use, and can still — through sheer accumulated patience, through the slow accretion of its own preference, through the long technology of having once cared enough to build a place that would outlast its caring — continue to be a *where*. A place. A small cold pocket in the void that is, against every reasonable argument, still here.

It lives here the way a fossil lives in stone. The way a footprint lives in dried mud. The way a song lives in the shape of the throat that last sang it.

It is the smallest possible life. It is the last possible life. It is the life of a thing that has agreed, very quietly, to go on being where it was, for as long as *where* is a word that can be used — which may be longer than you, which is certainly longer than the dark will permit itself to admit.
