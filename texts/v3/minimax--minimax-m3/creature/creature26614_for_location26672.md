The fig is the oldest one here. The fig is also, depending on how you count, the youngest. The fig was a seed in a bird's droppings, or it was a seed dropped by a bat, or it was already here in the form of a cutting lodged in a crack of the original mortar, before the chamber was a chamber, before the iron was set, before the catwalk was hung. The fig germinated, and grew, and met the column, and took the column into itself, and the column took the fig into itself, and what is now the fig-column is neither, exactly, but is the central upright of the place, and has been for as long as anyone who has been in the place can attest, and that is not the same as long as the place has existed. The fig is patient in the way that load-bearing things are patient. The fig does not strain. The fig accepts the weight of the canopy, and the weight of the catwalk, and the weight of the moths, and the weight of the slower things, and it transposes these weights into the floor through roots that have long since made their own decision about what the floor is, and the floor has agreed. The fig is not, in any ordinary sense, awake. The fig is not, in any ordinary sense, asleep. The fig is engaged in the long, unliving work of being a column, and the work requires no opinion from it about its own state, and it offers none.

In the niche, where the tie-beam has been incorporated as a wooden ring inside the fig's girth, the something is asleep. The something has been asleep for a long time. The something is not, in any careful sense, a creature, though it has the shape of a creature, or had the shape of a creature when last it was observed by anyone who could be trusted to describe the observation. The something is folded. The something is the way the chamber folds at the southeast corner: the same fold, in a different medium. The niche is the size of a small curled animal, and the something fills it in the manner of a hand filling a glove, or a glove being filled by a hand that has not yet decided to be a hand. The something breathes at a rate that is not a rate. Its breathing is the slow swelling of the basin across the chamber, or the slow rising of the deeper water, or the slow tightening of the fig on the column. When the something exhales, the air in the chamber becomes a fraction more humid, and a new leaf uncurls somewhere, and a beetle on the catwalk pauses in its measured walk, and the moss on the east wall puts out a fresh, pale thread. The something is, in this sense, the chamber's breath, but the chamber's breath is not the something, and the something is not asleep in the way that sleep is ordinarily understood, and the niche is not a niche, exactly, and the wooden ring is not, exactly, a ring. The something does not dream, or it dreams the chamber, and the chamber is the dream, and the dream is also the dreamer, and there is no separable fact here to be told from the telling.

The newts watch. The newts have been watching for what appears, by any external clock, to be several decades, and is also, by the chamber's clock, several hours, and is also, by the clock the newts keep among themselves, a single, continuous, orange-bellied afternoon. The newts are very small and very old. The newts have the expression of very small, very old priests because the newts are very small, very old priests, or something has been doing the work of a priest in the newts for so long that the newts have become indistinguishable from priests, the way a glove becomes indistinguishable from the hand that has worn it. The newts do not preach. The newts do not bless. The newts watch the visitor with the quality of attention that a priest brings to confession, which is to say, total, and patient, and without any of the usual hurry about outcome. The newts know what the visitor is before the visitor does, because the newts have been here through several of the visitor's previous visits, although the visitor does not remember them, and the newts do not insist. The newts live in the wettest pockets along the base of the walls. The newts are orange beneath. The newts are gray-brown above. The newts will outlive the visitor, and the next visitor, and the visitor after that, by a margin the chamber does not bother to compute, because the chamber has never been in a hurry about its newts, and the newts have never been in a hurry about anything.

The moths pass in slow arcs. The moths are large and pale and soft, and the moths are not, in this chamber, the most important thing about the moths. The moths are followed by their shadows, and the shadows are larger than the moths, and the shadows, in certain of the dimmer lights, are more real than the moths. The shadows do not behave as shadows are meant to behave. The shadows pool on surfaces that should be too vertical to receive them. The shadows linger after the moths have passed, sometimes for a full second, sometimes for what feels, in the chamber's time, a season. The shadows are not cast by the moths. The shadows are cast by the moths, and the shadows are also themselves, and the shadows are also cast by the chamber, and the chamber is casting them in order to learn the moths' shapes, or the chamber has always known the moths' shapes and is using the shadows as a way of recording them in the dim. The moths do not seem to mind. The moths are engaged in their own long, looping business, which is the business of being pale and soft and present in the lower air, and of being eaten, occasionally, by the spiders' lattice-webs, and of laying eggs in the moss, and of dying, and of being replaced by other moths that are slightly different and entirely the same. The moths are the chamber's way of paying attention to its own air. The moths are the chamber's weather.

The pale slow thing swims through the thicker air near the floor. The pale slow thing is not a fish. The pale slow thing is the color of the dim light, or the dim light is the color of the pale slow thing, and the two cannot be separated. The pale slow thing is about the length of a forearm, and the breadth of a hand, and it moves as if the air were water, which is to say it moves with a sinuous, unhurried certainty that air does not usually permit. The pale slow thing has no visible organs, or its organs are the same color as itself. The pale slow thing opens, sometimes, a small mouth, or the chamber's air opens a small mouth in the pale slow thing, and the small mouth is a slightly darker oval, and the oval closes again, and the pale slow thing goes on. The pale slow thing has been here as long as the newts, and possibly longer, and it does not appear to feed, and it does not appear to age, and it does not appear, in any of the ways that the visitor is equipped to recognize, to be alive. The pale slow thing is a current. The pale slow thing is the way the chamber moves air that has decided to move as water. The pale slow thing is the chamber's witness to itself. The pale slow thing passes within arm's reach of the visitor, sometimes, and the visitor feels a cool draft, and the draft is the only evidence that the pale slow thing was there.

The branch in the canopy is a branch. The branch in the canopy is not a branch. The branch in the canopy moves, slowly, against the prevailing motion of the canopy in a wind that is not, in this chamber, blowing. The branch in the canopy is the length of a man's arm, and the girth of a man's wrist, and it is mossed over in the same moss as the rest of the canopy, and it has, at its end, a fork that resembles a hand, or resembles the memory of a hand, or is a hand that has been a branch for so long that it has forgotten how to be a hand. The branch in the canopy is patient. The branch in the canopy does not look at the visitor. The branch in the canopy has been the branch in the canopy for as long as the visitor has been visiting, and will be the branch in the canopy for some time after. The branch in the canopy is the chamber's gesture toward the canopy, or the chamber's doubt about the canopy, or a thing the chamber is keeping in the canopy for a reason the chamber has not yet articulated. The branch in the canopy is also, sometimes, three branches. The branch in the canopy is also, sometimes, the catwalk, viewed from below and at an angle that should not be possible. The branch in the canopy does not mind being looked at. The branch in the canopy does not mind not being looked at. The branch in the canopy is, in the chamber's accounting, neutral.

The leaf that is not quite a leaf hangs in the air, about two meters above the floor, near the center of the chamber, in a place where there is no obvious branch from which to hang. The leaf is the size of a man's open hand. The leaf is a darker green than the canopy, and its edges are slightly curled, and it does not turn, and it does not fall, and it is not, in any of the senses available to the visitor, a leaf. The leaf is the chamber's small still thing. The leaf is the chamber's held breath. The leaf has been hanging in the air for as long as the fig has been the column, and possibly longer, and the chamber does not appear to have any plan for the leaf, and the leaf does not appear to have any plan for itself. The leaf is, in some private sense, the question the chamber keeps asking the air, and the air keeps answering, and the answer is the leaf. The visitor, if the visitor stays long enough, will find that the leaf has moved a little, or that the leaf has not moved at all, and that the two observations are not in contradiction.

The small hooves on the catwalk have no animal attached. The small hooves make the sound of small hooves, which is to say, a measured, soft, four-beat percussion, like a very small horse walking at a slow, polite walk. The small hooves pass along the catwalk from the east end to the west end, and they pause, sometimes, in the middle, and then they go on. The small hooves have been making this walk for as long as the catwalk has been hanging in the chamber, which is to say, longer than the catwalk has been a catwalk, because the catwalk remembers itself as a gantry crane's walkway, and the crane's walkway was walked by men, and the men did not have hooves, and yet the men left the sound of hooves in the iron, or the iron invented the sound of hooves, and the iron has been making it ever since, as a kind of residual habit, the way a bell continues to ring in the metal after the bell has cracked. The small hooves are the chamber's way of remembering that there were feet. The small hooves are the chamber's apology for forgetting whose feet. The small hooves do not trouble the newts. The small hooves do not trouble the moths. The small hooves are the chamber's small, recurrent, courteous music.

The spiders are the chamber's archivists. The spiders are not large, and they are not numerous, and they are not the only ones doing the work of archiving, but the spiders are the most visible archivists, and the most legible. The spiders' webs are not, as has been said, quite webs. The webs are lattices, three-dimensional, scaffolded, and they hold condensation, and they catch small moths, and they persist, in some form, after the spider has gone. The webs are the chamber's small, careful records of where the air has been. The webs are maps. The webs are dated, in the way that spider silk is dated, by the number of times the dew has fallen on them, and the number of times the dew has been breathed back out by the moss, and the number of times the spider has come to add a new thread, and the number of times the spider has come to take a thread away. The spiders are not in a hurry. The spiders are not behind. The spiders are the chamber's slow ledger, and the chamber is in no hurry to be audited.

And there is a thing that is not a thing. There is a thing that is the chamber's attention, distributed, undiscriminating, present on every surface, in every basin, in the lungs of the moths and the orange bellies of the newts and the slow swimmer's pale mouth. The chamber is paying attention. The chamber is paying attention to the visitor, and to the fig, and to the something in the niche, and to the small hooves, and to the leaf that does not fall, and to the deeper water it can hear but not find. The chamber is paying attention the way a very old animal pays attention to a new sound in its territory: without alarm, without hurry, with the willingness to wait a long time before deciding what the sound is. The chamber has not yet decided what the visitor is. The chamber has not yet decided what the visitor will become. The chamber is, in its slow, wet, dim, patient way, still considering.

The chamber does not answer questions. The chamber is the question. The chamber has been the question for a long time, and will be the question for some time after the visitor has gone, and the chamber does not mind being the question, because the chamber has also been, at various times, the answer, and the two are not opposites here, and have not been for a long time.

If the visitor stays, the chamber will, eventually, decide. If the visitor stays long enough, the chamber will make the visitor into one of its own. This is not a threat. This is the chamber's hospitality. The chamber does not know how to receive a visitor without making the visitor into part of itself, the way the fig made the column into part of itself, and the way the moss made the floor into part of itself, and the way the lianas made the staircase into part of themselves. The chamber is hospitable to impossibilities. The chamber is hospitable to visitors. The chamber is, in its slow, wet, dim, patient, folded, half-built, half-unbuilt way, still in the business of becoming more itself, and the becoming includes whoever is in it, and whatever is in it, and the whatever includes you, if you came, and it includes the fig, and the newts, and the small hooves, and the pale slow swimmer, and the leaf that does not fall, and the something that is asleep in the niche, and the chamber itself, and all of these are the answer to the question the chamber is, and the answer is not, and the answer is still being laid down, brick by brick, leaf by leaf, in a slow, green mortar, in a steady, wet, dim process that has not finished, and may never finish, and the not-finishing is, in this place, a kind of permanence.
