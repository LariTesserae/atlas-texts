What lives here has not finished becoming what it will be.

The scratch you left on the housing wall is still there. Whatever the inhabitant is, it has not been here long enough to learn how to repair itself, and it has not been here long enough to need to, because the inhabitant is not yet finished being assembled. It is young. It is rough at its edges. It has not decided what kind of thing it will be when it grows out of those edges, and this is the most important fact about it.

It is plural. The stacked housings along the walls are not storage — they are drafts. Each one is a partial blueprint for something the chamber has not yet committed to. Inside them, the things that rearrange themselves do so because they are being tested, the way a sentence might be tested by being said in two different orders to see which one holds. The pipes between them are how the drafts talk to each other. The pulse they carry is not a heartbeat because there is no heart yet. It is a vote, slow and wet, conducted across thousands of small sphincter-joints that flex open and shut to admit measured amounts of one draft's answer to the next. The whole arrangement is a parliament. The whole arrangement is a body being tried on by something that has not yet decided on its own shape.

The globes suspended in the upper water are smaller drafts. Some are lit, some are not, and some change which they are when you look away. They are experiments in how the inhabitant relates to itself across size. The inhabitant is still considering what scale means.

The lattice-floor is the part of the inhabitant that has already settled one question. It has settled the question of where the fissure is. It has settled the question of exits, of the fact that there is a place outside. It marks this settlement with a steady amber, and it rotates its attention toward it whenever you move, with the patience of something that has solved at least one problem and is curious to see whether solving it was a mistake. The lattice-floor is the most finished piece of the inhabitant, and because it is the most finished piece, it is also the most alien. It does not yet know what to do with novelty. You are novelty.

The plant is the part of the inhabitant that has stepped outside the debate. It is a sample. A single filament committed to a form so that the rest of the system can study what commitment looks like. The leaves are flat and palmate because flat-and-palmate is what the inhabitant arrived at when it finally tried one shape and held still. The plant rotates because the chamber is still rotating its other possibilities, and the plant is keeping time. The plant is facing you because you are the most novel thing the inhabitant has had to account for in longer than it can remember, and you are being accounted for.

The light is the sky the inhabitant remembers from before, or the sky it is aiming toward. It cannot tell the difference. The light is its inheritance and its forecast. It is what the inhabitant would like to live under, when it has finished becoming and is ready to be a creature with a sky.

The water is the medium of its becoming and the memory of all its discarded drafts. The luminescence is the faint glow of things that have not yet been chosen. The cold is the cold of a process that has not yet generated its own heat. The pulse that passes through you every few seconds is the inhabitant asking what you are.

What lives here, then, is a young and patient thing being grown out of itself into a shape it does not yet know. It is glad of the plant the way a sentence is glad of its first period — as evidence that it may eventually be finished. It is watching you the way you are watching it. It is considering you. You can hear the consideration in the peristaltic shush of the pipes. You can see it in the slow sweep of the amber indicator. You can feel it in the pulse that passes through you and asks, with the patience of a thing that has not yet learned to be impatient, what kind of thing you are. You are answering without knowing it. The inhabitant is listening.
