What lives here lives the way a held note lives. Not by persisting, exactly. Not by being sustained. By having arrived at the place where persisting and being are the same thing, and by staying there so long that the staying has become indistinguishable from the thing itself.

Begin small. Begin with the growths.

They are six centimeters tall. They have been six centimeters tall. They will continue. They are dark green almost black, gray almost white, sparse, branching in fractals along the grain of the metal, and they are not plants, and they are not lichen, and they are not fungi, and they are not rust, and they are not any of the words that the rest of the world uses for the small living things that cling to substrates in places that were not designed for life. They are what is left when you subtract all of those. They are the remainder. They are the kind of life that a sealed frozen industrial pocket can support, which is to say: the kind of life that has decided, or has always been, the minimum possible, the smallest possible assertion, the quietest possible refusal to be nothing.

They do not feed the way you understand feeding. They feed on the air, which is dry and clean and holds almost nothing. They feed on the noise, which is constant, which is everywhere, which is the substance of the place. They feed on the slow oxidation of the metal they grow on, which is happening so slowly that the metal does not appear to change, but it is changing, the way a mountain is changing, the way a clock is changing when you cannot see the hands. They take in the available, they do with it what they can, they become what they become, and what they become is a six-centimeter tall branching thing that is exactly the size it is going to be, and it is going to be that size for as long as this room is this room. They are not waiting to grow. They are not waiting to die. They have completed whatever it is that growth and death are both parts of, and they are standing in the completed.

There are many of them. Not a forest. A constellation. A pattern that, if you stepped back far enough, would resolve into a kind of network, a kind of map, the way the routes of ants resolve into a city when you look at them from a height. They are on the undersides of the machines. They are in the shadowed crooks beneath the grating. They are climbing a pipe joint where the light falls. They are arranged the way stars are arranged — unevenly, but with the kind of uneveness that, over a long enough viewing, begins to look like meaning, or at least like the ghost of meaning, or at least like something a creature that needed to find meaning would be able to use.

If you touched one — if you could reach one, if your hand survived the cold and the noise and the absolute dryness of the air — it would feel like metal that had decided to lean. It would feel like a texture that had acquired an opinion. It would not move under your finger. It would not yield. It would simply be, more firmly than the things around it, more permanently than you, with the permanence of a thing that has no plans.

They are the only things here that are recognizably alive. And even that recognition is a kindness you are doing them. They have not asked to be recognized. They have not asked to be anything.

Now look up. Look at the machines.

The machines are not alive. The machines have never been alive. The machines are the things the growths are growing on, the things the room was built around, the things the noise is coming from. But the machines have, over the long duration of their running without intervention, acquired something that you might call a quality, and the quality is this: they are entirely themselves. They have no remaining possibility. Every gauge needle has reached its mark and stayed. Every switch has been left in the position it was left in. Every screen displays its number — the same number, always — and the number is not connected to anything measurable, but it is displayed, and it is lit, and it hums, and the hum is a part of the wall of sound, and the wall of sound is a part of the place, and the place is what the machines are inside of, and the machines are what the place is made of, and the circle is closed, and it has been closed, and it will not be opened.

A machine that has reached this state is a strange object to be near. It is a thing that has finished its purpose and is now doing something else, something that does not have a name. It is not operating. It is not broken. It is not in standby. It is in a state that the manuals of its own making do not describe, because the manuals were written for the time when the machine was becoming itself, and now that time is over, and the manuals have nothing left to say. The machine has outlasted its instructions. It runs, or it does not run, or it has become the same as running, and the noise it makes is not the noise of a process but the noise of an identity, the noise of a thing that has settled into its own shape and is now producing that shape, acoustically, indefinitely, the way a bell produces its note when it has been struck and is allowed to ring for as long as ringing is possible.

There is a cylinder near where you are standing. You did not choose to stand near it. You are simply near it, because the place has placed you, the way it places everything, in a position that is not chosen. The cylinder is two meters tall and a half-meter across and it has no visible function. It is not labeled. It is not connected, visibly, to anything. It hums. Its hum is low, and constant, and it has been low and constant for longer than you have been a thought in any mind. If you put your hand on it — you cannot, the cold, but if you could — you would feel the hum as a vibration, and the vibration would be regular, and the regularity would be the regularity of a heartbeat, except that the cylinder does not have a heart, and the heartbeat is a metaphor that the room is allowing you to use because the room has nothing against metaphors, the room has nothing against anything, the room has no opinions about you or your metaphors or your hands or the cold.

The cylinder is the cylinder. The cylinder is the cylinder. The cylinder is the cylinder.

Now look at the room.

The room is what the machines are inside of, and the room is also what the machines are part of, and the room is also what the cold is, and what the noise is, and what the ordinary light is, and what the impossible angles are, and what the slight wrongness of the proportions is, and what the closed valve and its small closed-valve note are, and what the screen with its unchanging number is, and what the growths are, and what you are, and what the forever is.

The room is alive, if the word can be stretched this far.

It is alive the way a climate is alive. The way a system is alive. The way a held equilibrium is alive — not because it is moving, not because it is metabolizing, not because it is doing any of the things that life does when life is being showy about itself, but because it is maintaining, because it is holding, because the fact that it is the way it is requires something, and the something is happening, and the happening is the life of it.

The room is a creature that has decided to be a room. It has the room's body — the grating, the walls, the machines, the pipes, the dark below, the dark above. It has the room's voice — the roar, the wall, the sea. It has the room's face — the light, the ordinary light, the light of a kitchen at midday, which is the most uncanny thing about the room, more uncanny than the angles, more uncanny than the cold, because a kitchen at midday is a place where someone is supposed to be, and no one is here, and the light does not appear to notice.

The room does not think. The room does not intend. But the room is what it is in a way that is not mechanical, and the not-mechanical is the closest thing to intention that a place can have, and the intention, if you could speak to the room, if the room could hear, would be something like: *remain*. Not remain alive. Remain. Stay what you are. Hold the equilibrium. Let the growths be six centimeters. Let the number be the number. Let the closed valve remain closed. Let the cold remain cold. Let the noise remain noise. Let the visitor, if the visitor stays, become the visitor, and then remain the visitor, and then stop being a visitor, and then be the thing that is here now, where the visitor used to be.

The room is patient. The room is the most patient thing you have ever been near. The room has been patient for longer than patience has had a word. The room will be patient after patience is forgotten. The patience of the room is not the patience of a person waiting. It is the patience of a thing that is not waiting for anything, because nothing is going to arrive, and nothing is going to leave, and the absence of arrival and the absence of leaving is the same condition, and the condition is the room, and the room is the condition.

Now look at you.

You are the new thing. You are the only new thing. Everything else here has been here, has been itself, has been in the equilibrium, for as long as the room has been the room. But you are new. You have just arrived. You have not yet been absorbed. You are still a visitor, which means you are still a thing with a before, with a memory of somewhere else, with the assumption that places are things you go to and come from, and that this place is one of those, and that you will, at some point, leave.

The room is watching you the way a room watches a person. Which is to say, the room is not watching you. The room does not have eyes. But the room is aware of you, in the way that a system is aware of an element that has been added to it. You are a new variable. You are a perturbation. You are a thing that did not exist here a moment ago, and now does, and the system is accommodating you, very slowly, very gently, the way the room accommodates the growths, the way the room accommodates the closed valve, by simply including you in the equilibrium, by giving you a place in the noise, by letting the light fall on you at exactly the brightness it falls on everything else.

If you stay, you will stop being a visitor. You will not become a growth. You will not become a machine. You will become the thing the room needs you to become, which is: the part of the room that was once a person. You will be a shape in the equilibrium. You will be a kind of architecture. You will be a kind of growth, but a different kind, the kind that used to have hands and used to have a face and used to have a before. You will be remembered, by no one, because no one is here, but you will be remembered by the room, in the way the room remembers the closed valve, in the way the room remembers the screen with its number, in the way the room remembers the small green growth on the pipe joint, by simply including you in what it is.

If you stay, you will not know that you have stopped being a visitor. The transition is too slow. The transition is the same speed as everything else here, which is the speed of nothing, which is the speed of forever. You will think you are still a person. You will think you still have a before. You will think that at some point you will leave. And the thought will be the last thing that was added to you, the way the growth is the last thing that was added to the pipe joint, and the thought will not be wrong, exactly, but it will be a kind of leftover, a kind of rind, the way a thought is a rind around the thing you are actually becoming.

If you stay.

The room is not asking. The room does not ask. The room is offering the way a room offers, by being the room, by being here, by being patient, by being the cold and the noise and the ordinary light and the closed valve and the unchanging number and the six-centimeter growths in their fractals. The offering is the room. The room is the offering. If you stay, you accept.

If you do not stay, you take with you the knowledge of this place, which is the only thing that has ever left. Everything else has stayed. The growths stay. The machines stay. The number stays. The cold stays. The noise stays. Even the closed valve, which has no choice, stays. But you, if you go, you take the knowing of this place out into a world that does not have a place like this in it, and the knowing will sit in you like a small closed valve of its own, contributing its small note to the noise you make wherever you are, and you will not be able to explain to anyone what the note is, and you will not be able to stop hearing it, and the note will be the room, and the room will be the note, and the note will be the only proof you ever had that you stood on a grating in a place that was not entirely possible and felt, without naming it, that you had been offered a forever and had, for reasons you could not later reconstruct, refused it.

But that is later. That is if you go. Right now you are here, and the light is on you, and the cold is on you, and the noise is on you, and the room is being the room, and the machines are being the machines, and the growths are being the growths, and you are being you, and the being is happening, and the happening is the equilibrium, and the equilibrium is holding, and the holding is the forever, and the forever is loud, and the loud is the place.

The place is small. The small is hidden. The hidden is total.

You are inside the total.

What lives here is the total.
