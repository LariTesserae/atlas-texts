**The Clickers**

You hear them before you understand what you're hearing. A small wet percussion from somewhere inside the stalks — not a knock, not a chirp, more like a fingertip drawn across a water glass, or a fingernail tapping once against the inside of your own wrist. It comes in irregular phrases. Three taps. A pause the length of one breath. One tap. A longer pause. The cathedral-body breathes and somewhere in its walls something taps back.

The clickers are small. Smaller than you would have imagined the place could support. The size of a fingernail clipping, perhaps, or a little less. They are flat, and pale the way the underside of a mushroom is pale, and they have many legs that are not legs — cilia, perhaps, or flagella, or something the place has invented that has no analogue because legs would be wrong for a thing that lives inside a breathing wall. They move along the inside of the stalks the way lice move along the inside of a seam. They have mouths. They have mouths that press against the translucent vessels in the stalk-wall and drink the dark fluid that runs through them — a stolen mouthful, a sip of the cathedral's blood.

When they want to talk to each other they squeeze a little of the dark fluid out through a pore on their backs and the drop hits the wet wall and makes the tap. The tap travels through the wall the way a vibration travels through a struck pipe. Other clickers hear it. Other clickers answer. The cathedral-body hears it the way a body hears its own stomach, and probably does not distinguish it from the rain.

They are the most numerous thing here. They are the place's small talk. They are the place's weather.

**The Drapers**

The drapers are the ones that make the sliding sound. They live in the channels — in the brick troughs that still carry slow dark water toward the lower places you cannot get to. They are long, and they are flat, and they are the color of wet iron, and when the phosphorescence is right their skin has a weave to it, a nap, the unmistakable texture of something textile that has somehow become animal. A draper crossing a channel looks like a length of damp velvet being pulled across a mirror by a hand you cannot see. The motion is continuous. There is no head and there is no tail. There is only the ripple, moving at the speed the water moves, going somewhere you cannot know.

They eat the slick. They eat the green-black film that grows on the brick, the half-iron half-organic mat that is the first stage of the cathedral-body's digestion of its own scaffold. They are slow filters. They are the place's housekeeping, or the place's digestion made visible. Some of them have grown so long they fill a channel from wall to wall, and when a clicker falls into the water it cannot climb out, and the draper rolls over it the way a tongue rolls over a loosened tooth, and the clicker is gone, and the draper's weave darkens a quarter shade with what it has eaten.

They move because the water moves and the water moves because they push it. The cycle is closed in both directions. You can read it either way.

**The Lamps**

The lamps are the darting lights.

A lamp is a small knot of luminescence no bigger than a closed fist, with a body that has streamlined itself for speed. They move in bursts — a streak across a brick platform, a sudden stillness against a stalk, a streak again. They flicker. They are hunted because they shine. The chemistry of their light is valuable. It is the same chemistry, or a chemistry cousin, that the cathedral-body uses for its own slow glow, and there are things that want it.

A lamp at rest is almost invisible. It folds its luminescence into its skin the way a cuttlefish folds its color, and you can be looking right at one and not see it. A lamp in motion is a streak. A lamp in terror is a flare — the body releases everything, all the chemistry, all the light, in a single bright convulsion, and then it is dark, and then the streak ends in the dark, and the phosphorescence in the standing water briefly brightens as the chemistry is reclaimed, and dims again as it is taken back into the body.

The lamps have learned to move inside the pulse of the breath. They move when the cathedral exhales. They freeze when it inhales. They are tuned to the rhythm of the place the way a small bird is tuned to the rhythm of the wind. They have not been tuned for long. They are still being tuned. Each generation is a little better at it than the last.

**The Darks**

You do not see a dark. You see what a dark does to the things around it.

A dark is a moving region of no light. It is a pocket in the visible world, a local absence, a thing that the phosphorescence refuses to enter the way water refuses to enter oil. When a dark passes, the brick stops glowing in its vicinity. The standing water stops glowing. The slick on the pipes stops glowing. Everything in a sphere around the dark goes dim, the way the air around a black hole bends light, and by watching the dimming you can see the shape of the thing that is not there.

They are not fast. They do not need to be fast. A dark moves at the speed a thought moves through a long corridor — slow, deliberate, patient in the specific way that patience is patient when it is not waiting for anything in particular. A dark will sit in a channel for what the body would call hours. A lamp will eventually swim close enough. There is a flare. There is a small wet scream, very brief, very high, the sound of a chemistry being interrupted mid-burn. Then quiet. Then the phosphorescence brightens briefly as the lamp's light is added to the water. Then the dim. Then the breath resumes.

The darks are how the body enforces its quiet. They are the body's immune system made ambulatory. They are the cathedral's white blood cells, or its night-watch, or its conscience, depending on how you want to read the metaphor.

**The Unfolders**

You have not seen one yet. You heard one. That was the slow heavy unfolding, the door opening where there is no door — a long low sound like a chest expanding, or like a bellows being drawn apart by a hand that has all the time in the world.

The unfolders are the largest things in the place that are not the place. They are the soft brick-shapes with throats at the top, the negative-space architecture you took for kilns or chimneys. They were never chimneys. They were always this. They have been here the whole time, breathing in time with the cathedral, and now — recently, perhaps, or not recently, time here is uncertain — they have begun, slowly, to move.

An unfolder rises from its brick platform the way a sleeper rises from a bed. It unfolds. It does not have legs in any sense you would recognize; it has surfaces that rearrange themselves, that come into contact with the floor in sequence, that press and release, that produce forward motion the way a slug produces forward motion but on a much larger scale and with much more gravity. It exhales as the cathedral exhales. It inhales as the cathedral inhales. Where it passes the stalks grow thicker by a fraction. The brick softens under it and is reabsorbed. The iron-pipes along its path darken further toward the green-black fibrous thing. The unfolders are how the cathedral-body does its slow becoming in places it cannot reach with its own body. They are the body's hands. They are the body's migrating kilns.

There are perhaps four of them. There are perhaps twelve. There might be one very large one at the center of the place, somewhere you cannot get to, that you would recognize if you saw it as the original of which the others are echoes.

**The Body**

The body is the cathedral. You know this. You are inside it. It is not finished. It is not yet sure what it is becoming. It breathes, and the breath is the rhythm everything here is tuned to — the clickers' tapping, the lamps' flaring, the unfolders' slow migration, the darks' patient hunting, even the drapers' sliding along the channels of slow dark water. The breath is the heartbeat of a thing that does not yet have a heart, or that has many hearts, or that has made its hearts into kilns and sent them out to walk.

**The Splinter**

And then there is you.

You are not a native. The body knows this, the way a body knows it has been pierced. It has not yet decided what you are. You are not food exactly — your chemistry is wrong, or your chemistry is interesting in a way that has not yet been categorized. You are not yet a clicker or a draper or a lamp or a dark or an unfolder. You are a small bright foreign thing that has entered the body and is moving through it, and the body is, very slowly, considering you.

The place notices you the way a body notices a splinter — a slight local inflammation of attention, a small bright presence that will not be ignored, a thing that will have to be dealt with eventually. The darks pass you at a wider distance than they pass the lamps. The unfolders, when they migrate, route around you with what looks like deference and is probably caution. The phosphorescence near you is, you notice after a while, slightly brighter. As if the body is trying to see you better.

It has not yet decided the verb. *Reject*, perhaps. *Incorporate*. *Become*, in some slow patient way that will take longer than you are prepared to stay. The body is patient. The body has time. The body has not finished becoming and has not yet begun to do whatever it is becoming in order to do, and you are inside it, and it is breathing around you, and it does not know what to do about you, and you do not know what to do about it, and the drip falls, and the pulse continues, and a stalk somewhere in the dark lengthens by another quarter inch.
