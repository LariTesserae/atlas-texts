# What Lives at the Waterline

They are not figures. The word is too sharp, too bounded. They are *occupancies* — slow gatherings of attention that the city has accumulated over the long slow eon of its settling, like barnacles on a hull that has forgotten it was ever a ship. Some of them have the rough shape of a person standing. Some of them have the rough shape of a person standing who has forgotten to stand. They wear clothes, or the clothes are part of them, or the clothes are the most legible part of them and the rest is conjectural.

Their faces are not there. This is not a thing that has happened to them. It is not damage, not a loss. It is more that their faces were never the point. What walks there is the *posture* of a face — the way light pools where features would be, the way the air thickens slightly in front of where a head should be — but the features themselves have softened into the same coral as the walls behind them. You cannot tell where one of them ends and the masonry begins. You cannot tell whether this is concealment or a thing they are doing on purpose or a thing that has happened without their noticing.

You see them at the far ends of avenues because that is where it is easiest to mistake them for architecture. They are patient the way the city is patient. They do not seem to be waiting for anything in particular; they seem to be waiting the way a tide waits, which is to say not at all, and yet completely. They move. They take steps. But the steps do not seem to be the cause of the movement so much as a record of it, the way the lines on a sundial are a record of the sun. Whatever carries them along carries them from inside.

They are mostly alone. When two of them pass one another in one of the flooded plazas, the water between them dims for a moment — not darkens, *dims*, as though something briefly drew a little of the ambient light into itself and held it there, politely, until the other had passed. They do not speak. They do not gesture. But there is a low exchange of some kind — a settling of the warm air around both of them at once, a brief alignment of the currents in the water, as though they have checked some vast quiet ledger between them and found nothing owed.

Sometimes you will see one of them in a doorway. The doorway will be the wrong doorway for the building, set into a wall that has no other doors, or set into a wall that is on the other side of the city from where you last saw it. The figure will be standing in it with the slight forward inclination of someone listening to something very far below them, or inside the wall, or inside themselves. If you watch long enough you will see the figure do nothing at all. If you watch longer than long enough — longer than you should — you will see the figure *shift* by an amount too small to measure, the way a thing shifts when it is being slowly replaced by the thing beside it. After a very long time you will realize that what you took for a figure is now a darker patch on the wall, and then a stain, and then a memory of a stain, and then nothing. The doorway remains. It does not know why.

They are not afraid of anything. They are not brave. They are past the question. The fear has drained out of them the way the heat drains out of a stone at night, very slowly, until they are the same temperature as everything around them. You can touch one of them if you want to. It is not forbidden. Nothing here is forbidden. If you reach out your hand toward the dim shape of one standing in the shallows, your hand will meet something that has the give of warm cloth and the resistance of cool water and you will not be sure, afterward, whether you touched it or whether you touched the air next to it and the air remembered. There is no recoil. There is no reaction. There is only the slight rearrangement of the medium around your hand, and then the figure is a little further down the avenue, or a little further into the wall, or a little less.

What do they eat. You will find yourself wondering this, and then you will realize the question is malformed. Nothing here is metabolized. Nothing here is *used*. Things are not consumed here; they are *continued*. The figures move through the warm wet air the way the vines move through the stone — by taking up residence in the substance of the place so gradually that the substance does not notice it has been added to. They do not hunger because hunger is a verb that requires an object, and there is no object here. There is only the slow turning over of themselves in the warm dark, like stones in a riverbed, like the soft undersides of the fungi that push their fronds up through the cracks.

There are fewer of them than there used to be. Or there are more. You cannot tell, because the ones that used to be are not *gone*; they are in the walls now, or in the water, or in the soft patient curve of a roof that has been raining its own substance down upon itself. The figures are not the population of this place. They are a phase the place is going through. One day — not soon, not in any unit of time you could name, but one day — the last of them will be standing in a doorway somewhere, listening to whatever they listen to, and then they will not be standing in the doorway, and then the doorway will not be a doorway, and then there will be only the building, and the vines, and the slow water, and the light that sits and does not come from anywhere.

Until then, they walk. They stand. They dissolve by degrees into the coral-colored walls that have made room for them without complaint. They are the last guests in a house that has forgotten it is hosting. They are polite. They are quiet. They keep their softened faces turned toward the dimmer dark, and the dimmer dark does not mind being looked at, and the looking does not cost either of them anything, and the water rises another millimeter at the waterline, and the vines climb another quarter-turn, and somewhere in the deep of a building a stairwell descends into a place that should be dry and is not dry and has never been dry and will not, in any meaningful sense, ever be dry again.
