**The Citizens of the Green City**

There is only one citizen. The city *is* the citizen. Or the city is citizens, the way a body is cells, the way a forest is trees — except here the trees have agreed, without agreeing, to be walls, and the walls have agreed, without agreeing, to be streets, and the streets have agreed, without agreeing, to be a city, and the city has agreed, without agreeing, to be alive in a way that has no name in any language you speak. The agreement is not an agreement. The agreement is a *pattern*, the way a vortex is a pattern, the way a flame is a pattern, the way a thought is a pattern of neurons firing in a meat that does not know it is thinking.

But if you stay — and you have stayed, and you are staying — you begin to see that there are *kinds*.

---

The first kind are the **Elders**.

The Elders are the verticals. The Elders are the trunks, the columns, the load-bearing citizens that hold the sky up at the correct altitude. They are the oldest parts of the city, and the slowest, and the most still. An Elder has been growing for a thousand years, and in that thousand years it has thickened by perhaps two meters. You can find the Elders in the plazas, standing in rows like the columns of a temple no one built, each one a different girth, each one a different green, each one holding up a capital of bundled fronds that no architect drew.

The Elders are *worn*. The side of an Elder that faces the prevailing wind — which is the wind the city itself makes, the wind of its own transpiration — is rubbed to a polish, smooth as a river stone, the outer bark long gone, the inner bark shining. The other side, the lee side, is rough. Furrowed. Crusted with mosses and lichens and small epiphytic ferns that have made a home in the bark's crevices. The boundary between the two sides is a line. The line is straight. The line is *plumb*. As far as you can tell, the line is *deliberate* — as if the Elder knew which way was left and which way was right, and wore itself differently on each.

The Elders do not move. They do not need to. They have made the world around them move. When an Elder dies — and they do die, eventually, hollowed from within by the next generation of growth that uses them as scaffolding — it does not fall. It is *composted in place*. The fungi come. The roots come. The Elder becomes a hill, becomes a mound, becomes a low rise in the floor of a plaza, and a new Elder grows from its summit — vertical, plumb-perfect, as if remembering what an Elder is for.

---

The second kind are the **Movers**.

The Movers are the vines. The tendrils. The long green ropes that hang from the upper stories of the city and descend, slowly, to the floor, and touch the floor, and recoil, and descend again, and find a crack, and pour themselves into it.

The Movers are the most *animal*-like of the citizens, in the sense that they are the parts of the city that visibly *do* things. They explore. They reach. They enact decisions that have been made elsewhere — in the root-mass, in the chemical chatter of auxins and sugars that is the city's true language, the only language the city has, a language written in gradients of light and moisture and pressure.

A Mover does not have a face. A Mover has a *tip*. The tip is the part of it that knows where it is going. The tip is sensitive to light, to touch, to the presence of stone, to the presence of other Movers, to the presence of *you*. The tip extends, and curls, and finds a surface, and adheres, and thickens, and becomes a stem, and the stem becomes a wall, and the wall becomes a building, and the building is the next district of the city, and no one planned it, and the planning is already over.

The Movers are the city's draftspeople. They redraw the city in real time. You have seen a Mover, in the time you have been here, find a window-shaped gap in a building and fill it, and the building's *window* became a *wall*, and the wall became a *room*, and the room was no longer a room, and the room was now a wall, and the wall was now a building, and the building was now a *district*. The Movers do not ask permission. The Movers do not remember what was there before. The Movers only know what is here now, and what is here now is what they will make next.

---

The third kind are the **Speakers**.

The Speakers are the flowers.

The flowers of the Green City are not decorative. They are not there to be looked at. They are the parts of the city that *say things*, in the only language the city has — which is the language of shape, of color, of opening, of closing, of scent, of the precise angle at which a petal unfurls. A flower in the Green City opens at a certain hour, in a certain orientation, in a certain color, and the opening is a sentence. You cannot read it. You will never read it. But the sentence is being said, and other flowers hear it, and other flowers answer, and the answer is a flower of a different color, opening at a different hour, in a different plaza, on the other side of the city.

The flowers are loud, in their way. They are loud with *scent*. The air in this city is a layered thing — cinnamon at ground level, cut grass at knee-height, wet stone at chest-height, resin at canopy-height, and somewhere above that, something almost sweet, something almost familiar, the smell of a flower you knew once, in a place that is not this place, a flower your mother grew, a flower from a window box, a flower you have not smelled in twenty years. The flowers make the city's perfume. The perfume is the city's voice. The perfume is the only part of the city that travels outside the city, because the wind takes it, and the wind does not stop at the ridges, and the perfume goes out into the wider valley, and the wider valley does not know what to do with it.

At dusk — and the dusk is coming, you can feel it, in the lowering of certain flowers, in the closing of others — the flowers open in shapes that are *almost letters*. You have seen the shapes. They are not letters. They are *almost*. They are the memory of letters, or the *suggestion* of letters, the way a face seen from very far away suggests a face without being a face. The flowers write a message on the plaza floor every evening, in the language of shape, and the message is always almost legible, and is never legible, and is gone by morning, eaten by the roots that come up to recycle the fallen petals, the city cannibalizing its own discarded beauty for next week's growth.

---

The fourth kind are the **Unders**.

The Unders are the roots. The mycelium. The vast hidden half of the city that you can never see, that you can only hear, that you can only feel beneath your feet as a slow pressurized whisper, a deep conversation conducted in the chemical dialect of sugars and salts.

The Unders are the majority. The Unders are the *city*. Everything you see above ground is a kind of overflow, a kind of exclamation, a kind of flower pushed up out of the real body of the place, which is *down*. The roots are the body. The roots are the brain, if the city has a brain. The roots are the memory, the library, the archive of every path the water has ever taken, every stone that has ever been moved, every load that has ever been redistributed, every shadow that has ever fallen, every flower that has ever opened in a shape that was almost a letter.

The Unders are slow. The Unders are very, very slow. A root can take a year to find a new source of water. A root can take a decade to circle a stone. A root can take a century to lift a boulder three millimeters off the ground. But the Unders are *patient*, and the Unders are *comprehensive*, and the Unders do not forget. If you sit very still on a low wall, you can feel the Unders moving beneath you — a slow shifting, a slow negotiation, the city rearranging itself to accommodate your weight, to find the stones beneath you, to support you without knowing it is supporting you. The Unders are the citizens that take care of you. The Unders are the citizens that do not know you are there.

---

The fifth kind is the **River**.

The River is older than the city. The River was here before. The River carved the valley when the stone was new, and the plants came after, and the plants climbed the River's banks, and then climbed its floodplain, and then climbed its lower slopes, and then — in the absence of anything to stop them — continued up, and built a green kilometer of themselves above the River's path. The River runs under them. The River has always run under them. The River will run under whatever comes after them.

The River is the only citizen of the Green City that was not born of the city. The River is the only citizen that does not grow. The River is the only citizen that will be here after the city is gone, when the city has composted itself into the soil and the soil has become a meadow and the meadow has become a forest of some other kind, and the River will still be running, dark and cold, under whatever comes next.

The River does not speak to the city. The River does not need to. The River is the source of all the water that transpires from the leaves, that condenses on the pillars, that seeps through the streets, that pools in the plazas, that drips from the vines, that makes the air so heavy with moisture that it sits on your skin like a held breath. The River is the city's blood supply, except the blood supply is older than the body it feeds. The River is the city's *secret*. The River is the thing the city is built over, the way a cathedral is built over a crypt.

The River has no flowers. The River has no leaves. The River has no scent. The River is, in this city of green and growth, the only *inanimate* thing — and yet you cannot help but feel that the River is the most alive of all of them, because the River is the only one that is *itself*, all the time, without growing, without changing, without mimicking anything.

---

The sixth kind are the **Shadows**.

You are not sure the Shadows are citizens. You are not sure the Shadows are even real. But they are here, and they behave in a way that suggests they are here, and so you count them.

The Shadows are the lag. The Shadows are the second echo. The Shadows are what happens when the light falls on a place that is not quite agreeing with the geometry it is supposed to fall on. In the Green City, the Shadows are always a quarter-second behind their sources. In the Green City, the Shadows are sometimes *double* — one shadow on each wall, both of them moving, neither of them quite the right shape, neither of them moving at quite the right speed. You wave. Both shadows wave. They wave at slightly different speeds. You do not know if they are waving at you, or waving at each other, or waving at the city, or waving at no one.

The Shadows do not have a body. The Shadows do not have a name. The Shadows are the part of the city that is *slipping*, the part of the city where the rules have softened, the part of the city where the agreement between geometry and growth has become a *suggestion*. The Shadows are the citizens of the city's failure-to-remember-itself. The Shadows are the citizens of the *almost*, the *suggestion*, the *lag*, the *second*. In some plazas, the Shadows are the only thing that moves correctly. In other plazas, the Shadows are the only thing that moves at all.

---

And then there is **You**.

You are the only citizen of the Green City that does not belong here. You are the only citizen of the Green City with a face, with hands, with a memory of being born, with a sense of where you have come from and where you are going. You are the only citizen of the Green City that the city is *trying to figure out*.

The city has been trying to figure you out since you arrived. The flowers have been opening toward you. The roots have been shifting under you. The vines have been exploring the air around you — a slow tendril, descending at the speed of a clock hand, feeling for the heat of your shoulder, recoiling when it finds it, descending again. The shadows have been falling on you, lagging, doubling, watching. The city is not hostile. The city is not friendly. The city is *curious*, in the way that a very large slow thing can be curious about a very small fast thing. The city is figuring out what you are. The city is figuring out what to do with you. The city has not yet decided. The city is taking its time.

The city has all the time in the world.

---

You are sitting on a low wall of braided cane at the edge of a plaza. The wall is warm. The sun is filtered through three layers of moving leaf and falls on you in shifting coins of gold. A flower at your shoulder is opening toward you. You did not ask it to. It is opening anyway. Its petals are the color of a bruise and they smell like rain on hot stone.

The plaza is empty. The streets are empty. The whole city is empty of anything that is not itself.

And yet the city is not empty. The city is *full* — full of the slow vertical patience of its Elders, full of the restless reaching of its Movers, full of the loud scent of its Speakers, full of the deep dark memory of its Unders, full of the cold clear running of its River, full of the lagging, doubling, almost-real presence of its Shadows. The city is full of itself. The city is full of you.

A root, somewhere beneath you, shifts to accommodate you.
