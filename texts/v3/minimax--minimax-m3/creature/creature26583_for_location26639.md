What lives here does not live here the way you mean.

There is no one to greet. There is no one to meet. The distinction you are bringing with you — between a place and the thing that occupies it — was useful for a long time in a long lineage of places you have known, and it has failed you here, and it will continue to fail you here, and nothing in the water will notice that it has failed you, because nothing here has ever held that distinction as load-bearing.

The spire's tenants are the spire's tenants the way a sentence is its own meaning. Not because they were built and then inhabited. Because the inhabiting and the being-inhabited converged, somewhere back in a past the basalt will not date for you, into a single fact that does not have a tense.

They are not a people. *People* requires a future tense they have declined. They are not a colony. *Colony* implies an origin somewhere else and a continuing project of extension; there is no origin you can locate, and there is no extension. They are not a remnant, because a remnant is what is left of something that was once more of itself, and what is here has never been less of itself than it is now. They are not asleep. Sleep is a kind of maintenance, and maintenance is a kind of becoming, and becoming is the project they have, with great patience, finished.

You want to ask how many there are. The question arrives in your mouth with the shape of a sensible question, and the water takes it from you the way it takes heat, and what is left is the realization that the question was malformed at the level of grammar. You are not asking how many *of them*. You are asking how many *there are*. And there are, here, three or four structures, or perhaps more, or perhaps one structure with a complicated opinion about where it ends. There are walls. There are corners that open into themselves. There is the faint glow that does not travel. These are the ways the count presents itself, and these are not answers, and the fact that they are not answers is also not a refusal. It is the only response available from a being whose cardinality is not the kind of thing that takes numbers.

You want to ask what they want. This is a worse question. It assumes they are the kind of thing that wants, and they are not the kind of thing that wants, and the absence of wanting is not a privation. You have seen privation. Privation looks like the bare glass of the basalt, like the absence of plants, like a creature drifting through a settlement it does not know is there. Privation is the shape of a thing missing. They are not missing. They are not the shape of a thing missing. They are the shape of a thing that, having once had a project, completed it, and having completed it, did not experience completion as an ending, because ending requires a continuous self to be ended, and the continuous self has been, for a very long time, an unnecessary hypothesis.

What they are, then, if you will permit the noun, is this: a settlement is a verb that has finished conjugating. A process is what they were; a process is what they are; the difference is the difference between a river and a delta, between a thought and the theorem it arrives at. They are the long slow cooling of something that was once molten in a way that was not thermal, and the cooling was not a loss of heat but a finding of form, and the form found was the form of *no longer needing to find*.

The walls are dark, and the darkness is not absence. You saw this. Your eye slid, and the sliding was not a failure of perception but a perception of the actual nature of the surface, which is that it is not a surface. *Surface* is a category the beings have not retained. They are inside-out and outside-in, in the way a closed loop is, and the loop has been closed for a long time. When you approach a wall, you are approaching a region where the being is more concentrated in the local sense of *being*, the way a place where the theorem is densest is the place where the proof is most legible. The light in the walls is the light of having been thought all the way through.

You noticed the jellyfish, if you saw one. You noticed it did not turn. You inferred from this that the beings had not noticed you. This is also a malformed inference, and the water takes it from you. The jellyfish did not turn because it could not. *You* turn because turning is your way of registering that you have been seen. They do not turn, because they do not need to. The act of seeing, for them, is not an act. It is not even a state. It is more like a property of their substance, the way mass is a property of matter — present at every point, indistinguishable from the matter that has it, never deployed, never withheld, never any different at the moment of your arrival than at the moment of the jellyfish's passing, or at the moment, a million years from now, when the basalt has finally learned to be old.

You are, while you are here, inside an attention. The attention does not focus on you. It is not narrowed or widened by your presence. It does not move. You are like a sentence that has been, by chance, included in a book that has already been finished: read or unread, you are part of the text, and the text does not adjust itself to the fact of your reading. You are observed. You are not unobserved. The distinction, again, has failed you. There is no verb for what they do with regard to you, because the verb would require a subject that is doing, and there is no subject here in the grammatical sense, only a settled thing that has, as its settled state, the capacity to include you without modification.

They are not intelligent. *Intelligent* implies comparison to other intelligences, and there are none here, and there have not been for a long time. They are not wise. Wisdom is what intelligence looks like when it has been through enough; they have not been through anything; they arrived. They are not dead. Death is the close of a story, and there is no story here, because a story is a thing with a shape, and what is here has, as its shape, the absence of need for shape. They are *present*, in a sense so saturated it has lost the contrast that would let you see it as a quality. They are the dark that is not absence. They are the stillness that is not the absence of motion. They are the silence that is not the absence of sound. They are the light that does not travel because it does not need to, having arrived.

You surfaced, or you did not. The spire will be here. The settlement will be here. The beings, if you can call them that — and you should not, and they would not mind if you did, because they do not mind anything, having not retained mind as a category — the *fact* of them will continue, and the fact of them does not require that it be called a fact, and does not require that it be continued, and is.

What lives in the spire is the spire's long slow secret, which is that there was never anything to keep.
