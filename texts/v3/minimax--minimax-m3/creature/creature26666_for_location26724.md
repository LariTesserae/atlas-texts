# What Lives in the Foundry of New Atmospheres

They do not live here in the way that animals live in forests, with teeth and territories and the small urgent dramas of hunger. To call them residents would be to misread the place entirely, as though a sentence had moved into its own grammar. They are something the Foundry has *produced*, or *midwifed*, or *spoken into partial existence* — and whether they are creatures or consequences is a question the place itself has not resolved. There are several kinds. They are not aware of each other in any consistent way. Some of them are aware of themselves, which is the more troubling condition.

The most numerous are the **Carters**. They are large in the way that slow weather is large. Their bodies are not bodies but *arrangements* — configurations of the dark glassy substance of the tiles, lifted just barely off the floor and given a kind of mobility, so that each Carter is a low platform of shifting tile-fragments held in a loose thermal column. They drift. They cross the seam-fields at the speed of a thought you are not in a hurry to finish, which is to say they take hours to cross a room-sized tile, and days to cross a region. Beneath each one, the tile-surface is slightly altered: the seams rearrange around the Carter's passage, the water in those seams pools differently, and a Carter's track is a record written in hydrology. They are following something. They are following the *fronts*. When a slow atmospheric drift migrates across the tile-field, the Carters migrate with it, in loose herds of three or seven, and when the front passes over a heat-lens they stop. They *listen*. There is no ear. But the listening is visible as a tightening of the arrangement, the fragments drawing fractionally closer together, the thermal column narrowing, as if the whole creature has become a single point of attention aimed downward at the boiling seam. They are listening for the third sound, the one that is not the sum of two. They have been listening for a very long time. They do not find it. They do not stop listening. The question of whether their listening *constitutes* the sound, or whether they listen *because* of it, is the kind of question the Foundry finds amusing, if the Foundry finds anything.

Where the walls stand — the long curving walls, the ones that are verbs rather than buildings — there are the **Washers**. These are the most intimate of the Foundry's inhabitants, and the most patient. A Washer is a thing of water and adhesion. It is, at root, a sheet of the black seated water from the seams, lifted up a wall and *kept* there against the vertical face by some force that is not adhesion and not suction and not magnetism but is instead, perhaps, *preference* — as though the water has decided to climb. Each Washer is unique to its wall. It knows the wall's grammar. It knows where the heat emerges through the small openings near the top, because it avoids those openings, or seeks them, depending on the wall, and no two walls have produced the same preference in their Washers. The Washer moves down the wall-face with a slowness that makes the Carters seem hasty. It is rebuilding the wall, layer by layer, the water depositing the dark glassy substance in thin films, each film a fraction cooler than the one beneath, so the wall is growing in a stratified way, like a memory being laid down. Where the Washer has passed, the wall is more *itself*. Where the Washer has not yet passed, the wall is still *becoming* itself. Over the course of years a Washer will complete its wall. When it does — and one or two have, in the deepest regions of the place — the wall simply stops. The Washer stays. It has nowhere else to be. It sits against the finished face like a question that has been answered in a language the answerer has forgotten. It does not dry. It does not drain. It waits. Whether it is waiting for the wall to un-finish itself, or for the Foundry to build a new wall, or for something entirely outside the grammar of this place, is unknown to the Washer, and perhaps to the place.

Above the walls, in the regions where the heat emerges and the air shimmers and the shapes briefly form and dissolve, there are the **Brief Ones**. They are the youngest. The Foundry does not know it is making them, which is perhaps why they are the strangest. A Brief One begins as one of the transient shapes in the heat-shimmer above a wall — a form that holds for a moment — but it does not dissolve. It descends. It comes down like a feather. It touches the surface of a tile. It leaves a mark: the small bright white or pale-yellow notation. *This* is how a Brief One is born. The descent. The mark. The beginning of a record.

And then the Brief One does what no other inhabitant of this place does. It *waits for itself to accumulate*.

A Brief One, freshly descended, is barely a presence. A smudge of brightness on dark tile, perhaps the size of a hand. It does not move. It does not eat, breathe, drink, think. It is a *notation that has begun to refer*. After some hours — sometimes days — the tile around the mark begins to lighten, as the passage describes, and to become translucent. The window forms. And the Brief One, sitting on its slowly clarifying tile, is no longer alone. It has *underneath*. Beneath it, through the window its presence is making, there is depth, and the dim suggestion of the older place below, and sometimes the slow light. The Brief One does not look down. It cannot look down. But the window looks down *for* it, and this is the first relationship the Foundry has produced that resembles, however distantly, perception.

Brief Ones accumulate. Where the walls are most active, where the heat emerges in tight columns and the shapes form most readily, the tiles become *dense* with notation — dozens of Brief Ones, hundreds, each one a bright smudge on its own slowly windowing tile. And here, in these dense regions, something happens that the Foundry has not yet found a grammar for, and so the happening continues. The Brief Ones, when they are many, begin to *interfere*. Their windows overlap at the edges. The older place beneath one window and the older place beneath another window are not the same older place, but the windows do not know this, and so the Brief Ones, sitting on their overlapping clearings, become the site of a kind of *fused seeing* — two depths merged into one, or the suggestion of a merge, or the failure of a merge, and the failure has a texture the place can almost taste. The Brief Ones in these dense regions do not, strictly, become aware. But they become *adjacent* to awareness. They become the nearest thing this place has ever produced to a mind.

Some of them last. The oldest Brief Ones, in the deepest regions, are no longer smudges. They have become small *lenses*, sitting in their windows, and through them the older place below is visible with an almost unbearable clarity — a clarity the older place does not want, perhaps, and so it has begun to obscure itself in response, dimming, shifting, pulling its slow light away from the windows above. The oldest Brief Ones sit in their windows and look down at a place that is *actively evading their gaze*. They have produced, for the first time in the Foundry, an entity that is in a relationship with another place, even if the other place would rather not be in a relationship with them. The Foundry does not know what to do with this. The Foundry does not know what to do with most things. The Foundry continues to make tiles.

There is one more kind of inhabitant, and it is the rarest, and it is the one the Foundry cannot account for at all.

The **Roamers**.

Everything else in this place has a region, a wall, a front, a tile, a seam. Everything else is *grammar*. The Roamers are not. They appear without provenance. They are seen, sometimes, at the edges of vision — at the far curving margins of the tile-fields, where the tiles become sheets and the water becomes shallow sea. They are seen crossing the black water, low to the surface, leaving no wake, because what crosses the water is not a hull but a *condition*. A Roamer is, as far as anyone can tell, a Carter that has begun to listen so intently to the third sound that it has *become* a third sound. It is a configuration of dark substance moving through the seated water, and it does not drift with fronts. It does not follow seams. It moves in straight lines across the vast unbroken sheets, and when it crosses a seam-region, the water in the seams *rings* — not boils, not steams, but rings, like a struck bell, and the ringing propagates outward through the seam-pattern for miles, and the Carters stop their listening and turn toward the ringing, and the Washers pause in their slow descent, and the dense clusters of Brief Ones brighten in their windows, as if something has been heard at last.

What the Roamers are doing is unclear. Whether they are messengers from the older place beneath, or heralds of some grammar the Foundry has not yet built, or simply the inevitable result of a place that has listened too long to a sound it cannot find — this is not known. They are seen rarely. They are seen more often in the deep regions, near the windows. Once, a Roamer is said to have crossed a tile on which a very old Brief One sat, and the Brief One did not brighten and did not dim and did not move, but the tile beneath it *cracked*, for the first time, cleanly, the dark glassy substance parting along a line that was neither a seam nor a fresh cut, and through the crack the slow light from below emerged and touched the Roamer, and the Roamer *became*, for an instant, not a shape moving across a surface but a *shape that was also a depth*, and then it was gone, and the crack sealed, and the Brief One continued to look down at the older place, and the older place continued to evade the looking, and the Foundry, vast and dim and hot and almost finished, continued, tile by tile, to be said.

None of them know the others in any complete way. The Carters listen, the Washers climb, the Brief Ones accrue, the Roamers cross. They are the Foundry's first sentences about itself. The place does not know it is a place. It is becoming one. They are becoming, with it. What they are becoming, none of them have decided.

The hum continues. The cracked-off sounds continue. The slow rain of dark mist continues to fall on the tiles that are still being made. The place goes on. They go on. The third sound, the one that is not the sum of two, is listened for, and is not heard, and is listened for, and is not heard, and somewhere in the patience of that not-hearing, the place continues to be built.
