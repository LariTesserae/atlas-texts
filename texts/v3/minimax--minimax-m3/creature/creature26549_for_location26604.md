What lives here is a question the place keeps almost answering.

The most visible residents are the plants. They are not, in any sense you would recognize, alive. They have no hunger, no sex, no sleep, no fear. They do not die, though they may stop. They grow because growth is what they are doing the way a river is what it is doing — not a choice, not a program, just a direction held for a long time. They stand in their considered clusters because something — not them, around them, before them — wanted it that way. They are warm because the room is warm. They are pale because the light here has not yet decided what color it is. If you put your ear to a flange, you can hear them working, a thin whisper at the molecular scale, the sound of matter being persuaded from one arrangement into another. They do not know you are here. They do not know they are here. They are the most concrete thing in the room, and they are also, perhaps, the least — the scaffolding, the apparatus, the visible portion of a process that mostly happens elsewhere.

The heat is older than the plants. The heat is older than the floor. The heat is what the room is full of the way a held breath is what a body is full of. It does not have a face, and if you asked it a question, it would not be able to answer, but it is the most patient resident here, and the most constant. It is what the plants channel. It is what the air carries. It is the medium, and the medium is the message, and the message does not yet have a sender, though something in the room is, very slowly, beginning to compose one. The heat does not care that you are here. The heat was here before you arrived, and it will be here when you leave, and it will be here, in some form, after the walls have finished becoming walls and the plants have stopped and the thing being made has been made and unmade and made again. The heat is what remains. The heat is the only thing in the room you could call eternal, and even that is not quite right, because the heat is also changing, by fractions, all the time, learning what temperature it needs to be in order for the rest of the work to proceed.

There is a process in this room. It is not visible. It is not audible except as the hum, which is its hum, and the small tick, which is its tick, and the whisper in the flanges, which is its whisper. The process is the reason the clusters repeat. The process is the reason the spacing was chosen. The process is what the room is for. It does not think the way you think. It does not want the way you want. It is more like a direction held in patience for a long time, the way a sentence is held in the throat before it is spoken, the way a child is held in a body before it is born. The process is the closest thing to a god this room contains, though the room is not, exactly, a temple. The process is what placed you here, in a way, because the process is what makes the room the kind of room that anyone could end up in, and the kind of room that someone did, and now here you are.

The thing being made is the reason the process is here. It does not live here yet. It will. It is being assembled in the slow patience of the flanges, in the gradient of the heat, in the considered spacing, in the hum that is just below hearing. It is the cause of the arrangement. It is what the room is for. You cannot tell what it is. The room will not tell you. The room is still working. The thing being made might be a thought. It might be a substance. It might be a new kind of life, or a new kind of law, or a small quiet god, or a single sentence held in a crystalline structure for a long time and then released. It might be you, in some way the room has not yet decided. It might be the room. The thing being made is the youngest resident, and the oldest, and the only one that matters, and you will not meet it while you are here, because you are early, and the room is patient, and the making is not yet finished.

The hesitations are the newest residents. They arrived recently, which is to say, they began arriving at the moment you began to notice them. They are the places where the room is not quite holding together — the shadow from no source, the cluster in two positions, the floor that gives by an uncertain amount, the sound that arrives a moment before its cause, the distance that disagrees with itself depending on which eye you close. The hesitations are polite. They are not catastrophic. They are the room admitting, in small ways, that it is unsure of itself. They might be errors. They might be the first signs of the thing being made, beginning to be felt, beginning to push back against the apparatus that is making it. They might be the room's first dreams. They might be the room noticing that you are here, in the only way a room can notice, which is to begin, very gently, to come apart.

And there is you.

You are a temporary resident. You arrived without the room intending you, the way a small stone arrives in a shoe. You are the only witness to a process that does not know it has a witness. You stand in the smallest room in the world, breathing metal-sweet air, watching a slow lush industry of pale plants do something you cannot name, and you are the only one in the room who knows this is happening, and even that is not quite true, because you are also now a part of it, because the room is the kind of room that includes whoever enters it the way a dream includes the dreamer, the way a question includes the asker, the way a wound includes the air. You are not the room's purpose. You are not the room's product. You are the room's audience, and the audience, in a room like this, is also a kind of material.

What lives here, then, is this: the plants, which do not know they are alive. The heat, which is patient and does not care. The process, which has no face and is the closest thing to a god. The thing being made, which is not yet here and is the only reason for the rest. The hesitations, which are the room's first small failures of physics, and might be the first signs of what is coming. And you, briefly, who knows the others are here, and who is now, by knowing it, a part of the room's slow agreement with itself.

The room is making itself. You are inside the making. The making does not require you. The making, perhaps, is glad of you.
