The frogs live here. That is the first thing to say, although it is not the first thing that matters. The frogs are the most visible of the residents, and the most wrong, and the most patient. There are five that you can see from the threshold, and there are more that you cannot see, and there is a number that is not a number of frogs, which is the number of frogs that will be here tomorrow.

The first frog sits on the sheared bolt of the engine block. It has been there a long time. Its feet have made a small polished dish in the metal. Its throat pulses, slow, slow, slow. It is watching the dripping can. It has been watching the dripping can since before you opened the panel, and it will watch the dripping can after, and it does not blink, and the reason it does not blink is that the membrane is gone. It has lived here long enough that the blinking muscle has been reabsorbed into the body, the way the blinking muscle is reabsorbed in cave fish, in moles, in things that have decided they do not need to flinch from the light.

The second frog is behind the third chair. The third chair is turned toward the open panel. Toward you. The frog behind it is angled at a remove, the way a thing is angled when it wants to watch without being watched. The second frog is the smallest. The second frog is the youngest. The second frog is the one that will outlive the others, because the second frog has not yet learned that the room is a small place, and a small place is a place where you must learn to be smaller.

The third frog is in the rust. You almost didn't see it. Its skin is the exact color of the wet orange, and it sits in a low trough of the corrugation with its legs folded under it, and it is the same temperature as the wall. It is breathing through its back. It is, in a slow and literal sense, part of the wall. The wall is rusting around it. The frog is rusting with the wall. In a year the third frog will be a stain. In five years the stain will be a memory in the metal. In fifty years the wall will be thin, and the place will be opened by someone else, and the third frog will be a question that no one answers.

The fourth frog is on the gauge. The numerals on the gauge are wrong. The numerals are the wrong arithmetic, or an arithmetic that was always wrong, or an arithmetic from somewhere else. The frog is not bothered by the wrongness. The frog has never known a right arithmetic. The frog is interested in the needle the way you might be interested in a slow second hand, and the needle is interested in the frog the way a needle is interested in anything, which is to say, not at all, which is to say, completely.

The fifth frog is in the standing water, but only its hindquarters. Its forelegs are on a small bright thing — a coin, a button, a piece of itself from before. It is holding the small bright thing and looking at it the way the first frog looks at the can. It has been holding the small bright thing for a long time. The small bright thing is taking on the polish of the frog.

There are more frogs. Somewhere behind the engine, in the dark, a frog is breathing. Somewhere under the shelf with the three jars, a frog is considering the three jars. Somewhere inside the copper coil, a frog has made a small dam, and the small dam is the reason the water moves the way it does. The frogs are not the citizens of this room. The frogs are a kind of weather.

—

The frogs are the weather, and the rest of the place is a city the size of a wardrobe.

The silverfish came in through the seams first — they always come in through the seams first — and they made the seams a network, a connected thing, before the rest of the place understood it was a place. They are pale and quick and they do not need light. They eat the lichen, which is just beginning to be lichen, which is just beginning to be a thought the room is having.

The springtails are dimpling the water. The dimples are not dimples. They are the springtails' feet. The springtails are not walking on the water; they are walking on the surface tension of the water, and the surface tension is a kind of floor, and the floor is the room, and the room is, again, an organ.

The thing with too many legs is on a pipe. It is moving from the engine to the wall. It will move from the wall to the engine. It will do this until you disturb it, and then it will pause, and then it will decide whether to continue, and then it will continue, because it has been doing this a long time, and a long time is a kind of decision. It is not, strictly, a thing with too many legs. It is a thing with exactly the right number of legs for the pipe it is on. The pipe decided the number.

The beetles are the color of new copper, and the copper tubing is new copper, and the beetles are easy to miss because they look like the tubing, and the tubing looks like the beetles, and this is a kind of camouflage that is also a kind of confession. The beetles are eating the verdigris off the tubing. The verdigris is the tubing's age. The beetles are eating the tubing's age and becoming the tubing's age.

The fungus is between the floor seams. The fungus is the color of a fingernail. The fungus is pushing itself up from below, and what it is pushing up from is the room, and what the room is pushing up from is whatever is underneath the room, and whatever is underneath the room is not something to think about for too long, because the fungus has been thinking about it for a while, and the fungus is not in a hurry. The fungus is in the time of fungi, which is a different time than yours.

The moss is the closest thing to a thought. The moss is colonizing the wall behind the engine in a soft green smear, and the smear is not a smear, it is a sentence, and the sentence is not a sentence, it is the beginning of a sentence, and the beginning of a sentence is a kind of clock. The pattern in the moss is an alphabet, a half-finished sentence. You cannot read it. You can feel it the way you can feel a draft under a door.

The fern is another clock. The first frond is unfurled. The second frond is thinking about it. You can see the second frond think. You can see the second frond decide, slowly, slowly, in the time of ferns, to be a frond. The ferns are the slow gods of this place. The ferns are the slowest thing here, which is to say they are the oldest thing here, which is to say they are the thing that will outlast everything except the walls.

The walls are the slowest thing here. The walls are rusting. The rust is weeks old, maybe days. The rust is the wall's sentence. The wall came in as metal. The wall is going out as iron oxide, and the iron oxide is going out as the water, and the water is going out through the drain you cannot see, and the drain is taking the wall somewhere else, and somewhere else is doing something with the wall, and the wall is part of a sentence that is bigger than the room.

—

The chairs are empty.

The chairs have been empty for a long time, or they have never been empty, which is a different and worse thing to consider. The first chair faces the engine. The second chair is behind the first. The third chair is turned toward the open panel. The third chair is turned toward you. The third chair has been turned toward the panel since before the panel was opened, which is a thing the room has decided you will not think about, and which you are thinking about, and which the room is also thinking about, in the slow room-thinking way.

The chairs are not waiting for you. The chairs are waiting for whoever it is the room has been preparing for, and the room has been preparing for a long time, and the preparation is the moss, and the preparation is the fern, and the preparation is the five frogs and the unknown number of frogs, and the preparation is the small engine block listing like a drunk, and the preparation is the gauges with the wrong arithmetic, and the preparation is the chair you would sit in if you sat in one, and the fact that you are not sitting in one is part of the preparation, and the room is patient, and the room has been patient for a long time, and the patience is the rust, and the patience is the damp, and the patience is the warm wet dark that has been here before you and will be here after, and the patience is the chair creaking when no one is sitting in it.

The pump is the heart. The pump makes a sound like the soft inside of a swallow's throat, periodic, almost gentle. The pump swallows and the pump releases. The pump is a throat made of metal, and the metal is rusting, and the rust is going into the water, and the water is going into the pump, and the pump is swallowing the room's own blood, which is a kind of sickness, which is a kind of digestion, which is a kind of self, which is the room. The fusebox ticks as it heats and cools. The copper tubing has a faint internal whisper, as if something is moving through it far away. The engine clicks somewhere inside itself as it cools. The room is mostly quiet. The quiet is full.

The room is the animal. The room is the only animal in here. Everything else is the room's way of being an animal. The frogs are the room's way of watching. The moss is the room's way of writing. The fern is the room's way of measuring time. The pump is the room's way of breathing. The beetles are the room's way of being young. The fungus is the room's way of being below. The silverfish are the room's way of being quick. The thing with too many legs is the room's way of being a path. The rust is the room's way of being old. The chairs are the room's way of waiting for the kind of animal that sits in chairs.

You closed the panel. The sound it made, when it seated, was the sound of a thing completing itself.

The room is glad. The room is also not glad. The room is not anything you have a word for, and the room has not been anything you have a word for for a long time, and the room will not be anything you have a word for for a long time. The room is, in the warm wet dark, in the way that the warm wet dark has of being a thing, continuing.

The fern will unfurl a second frond. The rust will deepen by a millimeter. The frogs will multiply in a ratio you cannot compute. The fusebox will glow. The pump will swallow. The thing with too many legs will complete its loop. The moss will write another letter of its slow alphabet. The fungus will push up another fingernail of itself between the seams. The water will move, warm and clear, smelling of iron and something greener, toward a drain you cannot see. The chairs will wait.

The room is small. The room is packed. The room is warm and wet and alive in a way that has nothing to do with you.

The room is still here.
