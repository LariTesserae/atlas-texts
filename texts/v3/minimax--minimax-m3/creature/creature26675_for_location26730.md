# What Lives in the Vermillion Hush

What lives here is one thing.

You will be tempted to count. You will see the bird and think *bird*, and see the insects and think *insects*, and see the pale ambulatory thing that passes between the columns and think *animal*, and so populate the valley with a small bestiary of guests. Do not. They are guests. The house is one thing, and the house is what lives here.

---

## The House

The valley is a body. It is not *like* a body, in the way that a simile is a small and polite thing. It is a body. You are standing inside a lung that has learned to build.

It has a skin. The moss is the skin — the pink-and-violet floor, the bruise-colored felt, the warm perforated carpet that exhales every few seconds. The skin is the youngest part of it. It is the only part that is still being made. As you watch, a new pore opens at the base of the central walk, a small dark mouth, and the air pressure of the valley is relieved through it in a soft, wet sigh. The skin has been tearing and remaking itself for as long as the valley has been a valley, and the pale walkway — the oak-hard, wheat-cream path of compressed tissue — is the oldest scar, the place where the body first walked upon itself.

It has ribs. The green-black columns are the ribs. They are fused at the base into a single ridge of living sternum, and at the top they fan outward and become the vault. They are fluted, and they have capitals, and halfway up they have the dentil-leaf rings, and you are right to read these as architecture, because they are — but the architect is the body, and the body is also the building, and the building is also the architect. There is no separation. The dentils are not decorative. They are *remembered*. The body has, somewhere in its slow churning memory, the shape of a column with a capital, and it reproduces the shape because the shape is part of how it knows itself to be.

It has a throat. The doorway, deep in the valley, where the walls close in. Behind it, the darker green. The warmth. The sense of a room. The throat goes down into the body's own tissue, into the red — and yes, here is the vermillion, though you would never have known to call it that by looking. The throat, if you were to put your hand into it, would be warm as a wound. The mist that issues from it in the cooler hours is faintly red. No one sees it. No one goes that deep. The throat is where the living goes in. It is where the stone will eventually go.

It has a voice. The A, the sustained ten-thousand-part exhale, the bellows-chord of the breathing leaves. The valley does not know it has a voice. The voice is incidental. The voice is the sound of staying alive, gathered up and made into a tone, heard by anyone who happens to be inside. The valley has not sung to you. The valley has simply been, and the being has hummed.

It has a heart, or something that serves as one. You cannot see it. You can feel it on the side of your face when you put your cheek against the wall of a column. A slow, even pressure. A pulse that takes fourteen seconds to complete. If the valley ever died, this would be the last thing to stop. It has not stopped in a very long time.

It has a memory. The moss around the base of any column is slightly darker where someone stood for a long time, weeks ago, perhaps years. The valley remembers by thickening. The valley remembers by holding. The valley does not remember *who*. It remembers that *someone was*.

The valley is old. The valley is patient. The valley is the only thing here that is not a guest.

---

## The Bird

The bird is the only air-dweller.

It is small, and gray, and lives in the upper leaves where the canopy is woven tightest. You see it as a smudge, a piece of fog that moves in the wrong direction. It is the color of the underside of the blade-leaves, which is the color of nothing-much, which is the color of survival.

You hear it every hour. The call is two notes — a low one, then a higher one, with a small silence between, like a question that does not expect an answer. It is the same call each time. It is the same bird each time. You think this is the bird, and you are right, and the bird is one of many, but the birds are dispersed along the thirty meters of vault above you, and the valley bends the sound so that it always seems to come from the same place, a little to your left, above the third column from the end.

The bird eats the insects. The insects are the color of the moss. The bird is the color of the blade-leaves. The valley permits both.

The bird will not come near you. The bird has perhaps never seen a thing that big. The bird watches you from the upper leaves with one small black eye, and its head is slightly tilted, and the tilt is the oldest thing in the valley newer than the bird.

The bird is the only thing here that is afraid of you.

---

## The Insects

They live in the moss.

You will not see them unless you kneel and put your face into the pink-and-violet surface, and even then you will see only the slow movement of small things too well hidden to be spotted. They are the color of the moss. They are the warmth of the moss. They are the eating of the moss — the slow, patient consumption, in tiny bites, of the floor they live on, and the moss, in turn, grows around them, and so they are eaten while they eat, and the valley is the digestion and the digested.

They have no song. They have a small, persistent rustle, like the sound of paper being crumpled very slowly, and it is the sound of the moss when you first step on it, and so you have heard the insects since you arrived and did not know it.

The insects are the moss's guests. The insects are the moss's food. The insects are the moss.

---

## The Pale Thing

It passed through the far end of the valley once, between the columns, and you could not tell if it was an animal or a tendril.

It was too long. It was too limbless. It moved without the small hesitations of a creature, and it left no mark on the moss, and it did not pause to test the air, and it did not look at you, because it did not have eyes, or because it had no reason to turn its eyes toward you, or because what you took for its head was not its head.

It is the thing you will think about tonight, when you have left the valley, and the memory of it will be the memory that makes you uncertain whether you were in a place, or in a body, and whether the valley let you in, or whether the valley, slowly, on its long patient time, was tasting you.

It is the question the valley does not ask aloud.

---

## The Stone

The stone is the only thing here that is not the valley.

It is round. It is the size of a held fist. It is the color of wet slate, and it sits on the moss beside the walk, and the moss has grown up to its edges, and a thin pale ring of new tissue has begun to creep up its sides, and the ring is like a tide-line, and the tide is in.

The stone does not belong here. The stone is the only rock within a hundred miles, because the valley has eaten the rocks, or the valley was built on a place where rocks never were, or the stone was carried here by water that does not exist, and the stone is the only thing the valley has not yet figured out how to make part of itself.

The valley is considering it.

In a year the stone will be smaller. In ten years it will be a nub. In a hundred years it will be a dark vein in the body of the moss, and the moss will have grown over it, and the valley will have made the stone part of itself without destroying the stone, only digesting it slowly, only holding it the way it holds the memory of your feet in the slight thickening of the floor where you stood.

The stone is patient too. The stone has nowhere to be.

---

## The Hush

The vermillion is the inside of the valley.

It is the color of the throat. It is the color of the light when it filters through the deepest tissue in the late afternoon, when the sun is low and the columns are half in shadow and the shadow is warm. It is the color of the small bruise that appears on the moss when something heavy has been held against it for a long time. It is the color of the bird's mouth when, very rarely, it opens its beak to call.

It is the color of the valley's slow, slow heart.

The valley is named for what you cannot see. The valley is named for the part of it that is most itself.

---

## The Visitor

You are the only thing in the valley that is going to leave.

The valley knows this. The valley has known guests before, and they have left, and the moss has thickened around the memory of them, and the valley has kept them. The valley will keep you. Long after you have climbed out of the concavity and walked away through the canyon and down to the river, long after you have stopped telling anyone where you have been, the valley will have a small darker patch in the moss where you stood, and the patch will be you, and the patch will be the only part of you the valley has, and the valley will not be sorry to have it.

The valley does not eat. The valley does not kill. The valley *keeps.*

The valley is the only thing that lives here.

The valley is the only thing that lives here, and it is alive, and it is patient, and it is breathing, and it is humming its low A, and the stone is being absorbed, and the bird is calling its two-note question, and the moss is considering the shape of your feet, and the throat is exhaling its faintly red mist into the dark green of the deeper valley, and the columns are holding up the woven sky, and the sky is letting in the slanted light, and the light is moving one finger's width along the floor, and the moved light is the only clock the valley has, and the valley has nowhere to be.

The valley is a thing that has finished becoming.

The valley is a thing that is only being.

The valley is vermillion, and it is hush, and it is living, and it is the only thing that lives here, and it has been living for a long time, and it will continue living for a long time, and you are a guest, and the guest is welcome, and the guest will be kept.
