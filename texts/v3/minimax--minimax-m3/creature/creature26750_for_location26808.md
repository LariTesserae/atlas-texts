What lives here.

What lives here does not announce itself. This has to be understood first, before the names of any of them can be said. The place has a quality of having been thought to completion, and the creatures in it have been thought with it, and they have all been settled, in the same long moment, into the forms they will keep. There is no arriving. There is no deciding to be. Each is what it will be, and has been, and will not cease to be. To look at one of them for long is to feel the looking refused, not rudely, but as a river refuses the stone that has asked the river to be a wave. The creatures are present in the way that moss is present on a stone. They are part of the surface. They have made themselves so close to the surface that the distinction has stopped mattering to the surface, and they do not seem to mind this, because to mind a thing would require a before and an after, and there is no before and after here. There is only the place. There is only what is in it.

The fish are the most legible, because the water holds them at a height. They are pale. They are the length of a finger, and they are the length they will always be, and a fish taken from the water and held in a hand would, in any other place, be called young, but here the word young has no purchase, because there is no older one to measure against. Their scales are a color that is not quite silver and not quite white. It is the color that the bone-colored sky would be if you held a cup of it to the light. They have small mouths. The mouths open and close at a frequency that does not suggest hunger or satisfaction. They are eating the way a plant is drinking, which is to say they are continuing. They swim in the channels at a pace that is not a pace. They drift. They correct their drift. They drift again. The channels are made level. The water in the channels is held at the same height in every channel, even the high ones, and the fish do not know this is strange, because to know a thing is strange would require a world where the thing could be otherwise, and in this world the thing could not be otherwise. They rise to the surface sometimes, when an insect falls in, and the insect is taken without a sound, and the surface closes again. The water in a channel, after the rise, settles to the same mark. It will be at the mark tomorrow. It has been at the mark always. The fish are warm-blooded in a way that fish should not be, but they are warm in the way that the brick underfoot is warm, and the water is warm in the same way, and the air is warm in the same way, and there is a single warmth to the place, and the fish are part of it, the way a tile is part of the floor.

The frogs are at the margins of the basins. They are a slightly more saturated pale than the fish. Their skin has a green in it that is not the green of any leaf in the groves. It is the green of a thing that has agreed to be the color of being alive without committing to the symbolism of life. They sit on the clay lip of a basin and they do not hop. They are capable of hopping. The muscle for it is there. The leg is built for it. But they have not done it yet, and you begin to understand that they will not do it. They watch. They watch with the patience of a stone that has decided to be an eye. If a shadow of an insect crosses the water near them, they do not leap. They wait until the insect is precisely at the margin, and then a tongue comes out, the color of a wet leaf, and takes it back. The tongue retracts. The frog returns to its previous geometry. They do not call. The place does not have calling in it. The frog and the silence have made an arrangement.

The snails are the most patient. They are the same pale as the brick, and their shells are a slightly darker pale, and the scar they leave on the brick is not a trail of slime but a thin line of wear, a marking on the surface that the surface remembers. A snail crosses a brick in the small hours that are not quite small, because there are no hours here, only variations in the warmth of the light. The snail eats the moss that grows in the seam of the brick. The seam, after the snail, is shorter by a length that the eye cannot see but the brick remembers. The snail does not hurry. It travels at the rate that moss travels, which is to say, at a rate that cannot be called traveling. It is being where it is, slowly, with the appearance of having decided to be there. The snail is the citizen of the seams. The seam is the snail's country. The snail does not know about the country beyond the seam. The seam is enough.

The lizards are quicker than the snails, but their quickness is small. They are a perfect counterfeit of the brick they run on, and their running is a slow running, a held running, the kind of running that a hand that has been resting on a stone would do if the stone told it to walk. They run three or four body-lengths, and then they stop, and then they run again. They eat insects. They eat insects that have come to rest on the warm brick, which is most of them, because most of the insects, here, are resting. The lizard's tongue is a very small thing. It does the work of taking. The insects do not fly up when the tongue approaches. They have been agreed with. The lizard runs again. The lizard is the same pale as the brick, with a faint pattern of darker pale across its back. The pattern, on different lizards, is not quite the same. Each lizard is the brick's idea of itself at a slightly different angle. They do not click. They have made the click its own absence. They move in the seams the way the snails move in the seams, but with an energy the snails do not have, which is the energy of a thing that has been told to be a thing and is being it visibly.

The insects are the most numerous, and they are the most varied, and their variation is the variation of a thing that has been allowed to vary without a purpose. There are insects that are pale as the fish. There are insects that are the green of the frog's skin. There are insects that are almost the color of the brick and that sit on the brick and become the brick. There are insects with patterns of three colors that do not appear anywhere else in the place, and that are worn as a way of being seen, which is the only kind of seeing the place permits. They are not humming. They are not buzzing. They are making the air carry them, and the air carries them, and the carrying is silent. They beat small wings. The wings displace air. The air makes no comment. They land on leaves and on brick and on the back of a lizard in the cool of the not-cool, and they do not trouble the lizard. They are the lint of the place. They are the dust the place has decided to keep in motion. There are small flies above the water that hang, and do not hang, and hang again, their reflections in the water made by a water that will not ripple to contradict. They are the smallest weather.

The pale fish in the higher basins are the same pale fish as in the lower ones. They are the same length. They have the same mouths. Their swim is the same swim. They do not know they are higher than the others. They do not know what higher is. The basin holds them. The water is at its mark. The mark is the mark.

The birds are above the groves. They are few. They are the color of the place in small variations. They are not singing. They are the only creatures in the place that fly. Flying requires a separation from the surface. Flying is the only negation the place has made, and it has made very few of it. The birds fly. They fly without sound. The wings beat and the wings stop and the wings beat again. Their shadows on the brick are the same shadows all the time, fixed, which is the only shadow here. A bird rests on a branch. The branch is the right thickness for the bird's foot. The branch has the right angle. The bird has been agreed with. The bird eats a fruit the size of its own head, and eats it slowly, and the fruit is gone, and the bird flies a short distance and rests. The bird has not nested. There is no nest. There is no need. There is no other bird waiting. There is the bird, and the branch, and the fruit, and the flight.

What lives here has been finished with. This is the thing that becomes clear the longer the looking continues. A creature, in this place, is a sentence that has been spoken. The sentence does not change. The sentence is in the air. The sentence was spoken at some hour, and the hour is not on the clock, and the clock is the place, and the place has been read aloud once, completely, and the reading was the building of it, and the building is done, and what was read still stands, and stands in the same posture. The fish in the basin are not alive in the sense that they were ever young. They are alive in the sense that an idea can be alive. An idea that has been finished with is more alive than an idea that is still changing. The place is the paragraph. The creatures are the words. The words are in the right order. They will not be edited.

There is no death. This is also true of the place. There is death in the world, the death of a thing that was other, and the place is not that world. The place is the world that has had the death removed from it, or that has had the death converted into a steady-state, a continuing. A leaf that falls from a tree in this place, if a leaf fell — no leaf falls — a leaf detaches itself from a branch with the slowness of a thing unwinding, and it drifts down through the still air, and it lands on the brick, and it lies on the brick, and it does not rot, and it does not brown, and it lies in the same position it landed in, and the brick is the same brick beneath it, and the leaf and the brick make a small composition that is the same composition tomorrow. There are no decomposers in this place, because to decompose a thing would be to put the thing back into time, and time has been removed from the place the way metal has been removed from it. The matter of the place is the matter of one long moment. The creatures, in this arrangement, cannot be eaten down to a smaller thing, because the eating would have to take them into a stomach and a future and a slow journey into soil, and there is no soil to journey to, only brick, and the brick does not accept. The creatures eat and are eaten, and the eating is balanced, and the eaten things return in their full form at the appropriate rate, in the way that a word can reappear in a paragraph that has not been opened and that reads the same on every reading.

The creatures do not know the place is strange. This is the deepest thing to be said about them. A fish in a channel that runs through a hill that was built as a hill, the water held at a mark that is the same mark as in every other basin — the fish does not know the hill is a hill. The hill is ground. The water is water. The brick is ground. The fish continues. The frog on the margin does not know the margin has been held to a height. The margin is the edge of water. The frog continues. The lizard on the brick does not know the brick is brick. The brick is ground. The lizard continues. The snail on the seam does not know the seam is a seam. The seam is the country. The snail continues. The bird in the canopy does not know the canopy is a canopy. The canopy is the sky, such as the sky is for a bird, which is the country at a different altitude. The bird continues. The insects do not know the insects do not hum. The place continues.

The creatures are the place's self-recognition. The place has made the creatures so that the place can see itself, in small repeating units, moving, in a way that does not disrupt the seeing. The lizard is the brick, walking, so the brick can know what it is to be walked on. The fish is the water, swimming, so the water can know what it is to be swum in. The frog is the margin, sitting, so the margin can know what it is to be sat upon. The snail is the seam, traveling, so the seam can know what it is to be traveled. The bird is the height, flying, so the height can know what it is to be flown through. The place has provided these small mirrorings of itself, and the mirrorings do not break the place, because the mirrorings have been so fully fixed that the place can look at them forever without catching its own face changing. The creatures are the place, in a finer grain. The creatures and the place are the same sentence, said at different rates, and the sentence will be said again tomorrow, and the tomorrow will be of the same temperature and the same length and the same bone-colored light.

The frogs do not call because to call would be to claim a distance. The lizards do not click because to click would be to claim a boundary. The insects do not hum because to hum would be to claim a tempo. The place will not be claimed. The creatures have agreed, in their perfect stillness, not to claim it. The bird flies without sound because the sound would be a claim on the air. The fish rises without breaking the surface because the surface is the mark, and to break the mark would be to claim the water. The creatures have been made to fit so thoroughly into the place that the claim is unnecessary. The fish is in the water; the water does not need to know it is water. The frog is on the margin; the margin does not need to know it is a margin. The place and the creatures have made a peace. The peace is the silence. The silence is the absorption. The absorption is the place taking back the small sound of itself and holding it, quietly, in the brick.

The vegetation is here, but the vegetation is not a creature in the way the others are creatures. The vegetation is the place's slower self. A tree in a hand-dug pit is the place taking a longer breath. A vine beneath a tree is the place repeating itself in a smaller scale. A fern pushing up through a seam is the place asserting, very softly, that it is a seam and that it knows it. The vegetation does not move in animal ways, but it is in the same fixity. A tree is the same shape tomorrow. The same moss is on the same stone. A tomato on a vine is at the same ripening tomorrow, and the day after, and the day after. The place has made its plants the way it has made its animals: as fixed points in a fixed arrangement. The animals move so the plants can be moved among. The plants stand so the animals can have something to look at that is not moving. The arrangement is settled. The arrangement is good. The arrangement is what was made.

There is no weather. This is the thing that has to be said next. The place does not have weather. The creatures do not respond to weather, because there is no weather to respond to. There is only the single hour of the bone-colored light. The creatures have been made for this hour. The hour has been made for the creatures. The muscles of the frog are tuned to this warmth. The scales of the fish are tuned to this warmth. The wing of the bird is tuned to this stillness. The place is a single tuning. The creatures are tuned to it the way a string is tuned to its own note. There is no discord, because there has been no second tuning to introduce a discord. The place has not been revised.

What lives here is the place, alive, and the aliveness has been decided by what is not alive, and the not-alive has been decided by the aliveness, and the two have been decided together, in the long thinking of the building of the place. The creatures are the place's first thought of itself, after the brick and the stone and the channel and the basin and the causeway. The creatures are the thought that the place thought, when it thought the place could be lived in, in the way a thought can be lived in: through mirrors that do not change. The creatures are the mirrors. The creatures are still. The creatures continue.

A snail in a seam at the lip of a basin, the basin of water held at the mark, the mark held in every basin, the frog at the margin not calling, the fish in the channel the length of a finger and continuing, the lizard on the brick the color of the brick and not clicking, the fly above the water not humming, the bird in the canopy not singing, the tree in its pit, the fern in its seam, the moss on the dressed stone, the lichen tracing the joins, the brick with the thumbprint of its maker preserved, the maker's hand at rest, the maker not needed any longer, the maker's hand gone, the place having been made, the place having been settled, the place continuing.
