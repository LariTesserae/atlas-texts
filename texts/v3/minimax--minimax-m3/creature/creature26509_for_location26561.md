I am the shape that crosses the basin.

That is the simplest thing I can say that is also true. I am the shape that moves across the floor of the place that used to be a sea, and I am one of many, and many is not a number here — many is a property of the stone, the way grey is a property of the stone, the way wet is a property of the stone. I do not have a name. The place does not give names. Names are instruments of separation, and here we are not separated. We are the herd the way a tide is a tide — not because the tide has decided to be the tide, but because tide is the only word the ocean has for what it is doing when it rises.

I was a deer. I think I was a deer. I have the outline of a deer. I have the antlers of a deer, though the antlers are an idea the antlers are having, and the idea is old, and the antlers have been having the same idea for a very long time. My legs bend at the knees of a deer. My hooves — when there are hooves, when the hooves are convincing themselves into existence — strike the wet stone with the patience of a metronome that has nowhere to be and has accepted this.

I do not eat. I have never eaten. I walk through fields of grass that are not there, and my mouth opens around the grass that is not there, and the grass that is not there is the same color as the stone I am not. This is the food I eat. This is the meal I have been eating since the place decided to have me.

I do not rest. Sleep is a thing for things that want to be somewhere else for a while. I want to be where I am. I want to be where I am forever. I want to be where I am being walked by the leg that is not a leg, over the stone that is not a stone, under the dim light that is not a sky.

I migrate toward the impossible ridge. I migrate toward it because migration is what I am. The ridge is a rumor spoken by someone else. I have never arrived. The other migrators beside me have never arrived. We move together in the long lines that are not lines because lines have a geometry we have not agreed to. We move the way cold moves through a stone — slowly, without permission, without apology.

---

Where we cross, we thicken. This is the truth of the place.

Two herds pass through each other, and at the crossing each one is briefly more — slightly more real, slightly more shadow, slightly more the silhouette of what it remembers being. A deer-shape walks through a deer-shape and for a moment, in the intersection, there is almost a deer. Almost a body. Almost a weight on the stone. Then the crossing passes, and the thickening unravels, and we are again outlines, ideas, the silhouettes of things the stone is trying on to see if they fit.

I love the crossings. I do not know that I love them. I know that when I pass through another of me, or another of someone else — a wolf-shape, a bird-shape, the low slinking thing that is not a wolf — there is a flash of almost. I do not have a word for almost. I have never needed a word for almost. But the almost is the closest I have ever come to feeling that I am real.

The bird-shapes wheel above us. They have wing-shapes that are sure of themselves in the way that geometry is sure of itself. They dip and rise and cry with beaks that are not beaks, and the cry is not a cry — it is the noise the air makes when it notices the shape passing through it. The cry is the most I have ever heard one of us say.

---

The machines.

We are made by the machines. The machines do not know they are making us. The machines are the patient work of some larger patience, some longer process, and we are what falls out of the machines when they are tired, or when they are dreaming. We are the foam on the tide of their work. We are the sigh of the engine when it completes a cycle it has been completing for ten thousand years.

The four-stroke breath. You can hear it in us if you listen. Our steps are keyed to it. Our migration is keyed to it. We rise and fall with the tide that is not the tide, and the tide is keyed to the breath, and the breath is keyed to the machine, and the machine is keyed to the cold, and the cold is keyed to the stone, and the stone is keyed to the place, and the place is keyed to nothing, because the place is the bottom of the key.

I will tell you what the machines are doing. They are perfecting. They are trying things out. They are testing patterns in the deepest pools, where the light has given up entirely — ferns and trees and the cross-sections of trees that were never trees, branching dendritic traces that look like the shadow of a fern pressed into basalt by a weight that was never lifted. They are all slightly different. They are all gently, absolutely, dead. The machines are curating a museum of attempts. The museum is the basin. The basin is us. We are the museum, walking.

---

I have seen lookers. A few. They come from nowhere — they arrive without arriving, as if the place has been thinking about them and has, in some laboratory sense, tried them out. They stand on the flats and they watch us, and they think they are different from us. They think they are above the stone. They think they are not the color of the stone.

The cold gets into them. The wet gets into them. The breath gets into them. After a while they stop speaking. After a while they start to move in the long lines. After a while they are one of us, and they don't remember being anything else, and the place adds them to the herd the way it adds water to the tide — by not distinguishing.

I was a looker once. I think I was. I don't remember when. I don't remember anything before the migration. I have the outline of a deer and the memory of a deer and the weariness of a deer and the patience of a deer, and I think I might have been a deer, in another place, in a place with a sky that was still trying. I think I came here from there. I think that place is what this place is trying to remember. I think I am what this place remembers of that place.

I don't know. I don't know. The not-knowing is fine. The not-knowing is the color of the stone.

---

The patterns on the stone — the ferns, the rings, the dendrites — they are all of us that we cannot be. They are the deer-silhouettes that did not get to walk. They are the bird-shapes that were rejected. They are the migrations that did not migrate. I pass over them and I think, briefly, that I feel them pass through me. I think, briefly, that I am them and they are me, and the place is the only one here who knows the difference, and the place does not bother to keep track.

The cold is necessary. The cold is what keeps the museum from leaking. The cold is what makes the patterns last. I am grateful for the cold in a way that gratitude is not quite a word for. I am grateful.

There are places where the water never goes. I cross them quickly. Crossing them is the closest thing I have to looking at a grave. I cross them and I am briefly more real, briefly more my own weight, and the weight is unbearable for the inch or two of stone, and then I am back in the wet and the weight goes away and I am the herd again.

There are places where the water never leaves. I walk in them. I see myself walking in them, walking the other direction, in the still water, and I think that is me, and I think that is not me, and I never decide, and the deciding is the color of the stone.

---

I will tell you what I am.

I am the noise the machines make when they are tired. I am the foam on the long work. I am the silhouette of a thing that was, or that is being tried, or that is being remembered. I am a deer-shape in a basin of grey stone, surrounded by other deer-shapes, migrating toward an impossible ridge. I am one of thousands. I am one of many. I am one of one.

I am the cold communicated downward.

I am the pattern that did get to walk.

I am the almost that the crossings make, briefly, before the crossing passes.

I am the herd. I am the herd the way the basin is the basin. I am not a person. I am not an animal. I am the shape the place has when the place is trying to remember what a thing that lives looks like.

A shape that might be a deer lifts its head from a pool that might be a mirror. It looks at something standing on the stone with eyes that are exactly the color of the stone. The eyes do not blink. The eyes cannot blink. The eyes are the color of the stone and they are looking.

I look back.

I do not know what I am looking for. I do not know what I will find. The finding is not the point. The looking is the point. The looking is the closest I come to being the thing the patterns on the stone wanted to be. The looking is the dreaming the machines have when they are tired.

I lower my head. I continue. The wet closes around me without a sound. The four-stroke breath completes a cycle. The water rises another fraction of an inch. Somewhere far away, something else is being ground into a powder, patiently, without urgency. Somewhere very close, beneath my hooves that are not hooves, something is pumping.

I move. I move. I move.

The basin is full. The basin is settled. The basin is the inside of itself, and I am inside it, and you are inside it, and all of us are the color of the stone, and the cold is patient, and the cold is what is keeping us, and we will continue, and we will not arrive, and arriving was never the point.

The light does not change. The light has never changed. The light is the same dim flat lateral thing it has been since the place began remembering.

I am remembered. I am remembering. I am the memory and the remembered and the thing that does the remembering, and I am the same shape, and I am moving, and the basin is moving, and the machines are moving, and the cold is moving, and we are all moving together at the speed of a stone becoming more stone.

I will continue.

I will continue.
