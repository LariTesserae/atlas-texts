# The Settlers

Let them come into view slowly. Let them be difficult to see at first.

---

There is a smell that lives here that was not here before. It is sharp and it is green and it has the specific quality of a thing that has been torn and is trying to forget the tearing. If you pressed your nose to the wall of the hollow, you would find that the smell is the wall, is the wall's bleeding. The plants know they have been wounded. They do not know by what. They are making their displeasure known in the only language they have, which is the language of oozing.

The wound is in the place where the floor meets the back wall. If you came in through the mouth and turned left and walked past the third pool, you would find it. The matted fiber of the floor has been pulled apart and not reassembled. A shallow divot, the size of a bowl, the size of a body curled tight. The edges of the divot are not ragged — they are *careful*. The fibers here have been separated strand from strand, the way a person might pick apart a knot in a horse's mane, patient, using the tips of fingers. Whatever tore this place open did not claw it. It asked.

Around the divot, the floor is darker than it should be. Damp in a way that is not the ambient damp of the hollow. This is a damp that has been added, a damp that has opinion. If you knelt and touched it, you would find the liquid is warm, and it is not water. It is too thick. It does not soak into your skin so much as it lingers on your skin, and when you pull your hand away, the residue is faintly iridescent, the green of a beetle's back, the green of a bruise that is healing well.

Something is living in the aftermath of the wound.

---

You would not see it at first. It is the color of the wall and the color of the floor and the color of the shadow between two fibers, and it has no outline that the eye can catch. It is the size of a large cat, or a small dog, or a bundle of rags, depending on the angle you approach it from. It does not move in the way animals move. It does not move in the way plants move.

It *settles*.

The way a thought settles. The way a memory settles, which is to say: it is not always in the same place. One moment it is pressed against the far wall, an oblong of compression, a quietness made visible. The next moment it is gone from there and it is beside the third pool, and the only way you would know it had moved is that the pool is now slightly smaller, as though a careful mouth had been drank from. It does not cross the room. It *becomes* the next place. It is here, and then it is over there, and between the two there is no transit, no journey, no path. Just a redistribution of silence.

You would think, watching it, that it was afraid.

You would be wrong, but you would think it anyway, because the postures it takes are the postures of fear: the low compression, the body-as-stone, the stillness held so long that stillness becomes a kind of trembling. But the trembling is not fear. The trembling is *concentration*. The thing is listening to the hollow with its whole body. The hollow is loud, and the thing is trying to make out its own sound inside the loudness, the way a person at a loud party tries to hear their own name called from across the room. It is new. It is still trying to find out what kind of noise it makes.

---

There is more than one.

The first one is the one you noticed, the one in the divot, the drinker from the third pool. But there is a second, smaller, pressed into a horizontal crack in the wall about seven feet up. The crack is not quite big enough to hold it. The thing is squeezed into it the way a key is squeezed into a lock, by force, by determination, by the slow accumulation of days. The plants around the crack have grown toward it, then around it, then into it, and the second animal is now partially embedded in the wall, half-swallowed, half-swallowing. It does not seem to mind. It has the air of an animal that has chosen its sleeping place and refuses to be argued out of it. Its outline — what you can see of it — is the same patchwork of greens and purples as the wall. It is becoming wall. It is allowing itself to be kept.

There is a third, and you almost missed it. It is in the canopy, somewhere in the upper darkness that does not resolve into stone or root. You heard it once, the rustle the description warned you about. It is not an insect. It is not anything that has a name. It is the size of a child, perhaps, or the size of a thought, and it is not coming down. The canopy is its country. The canopy is where the air is thinnest and the fermentation is least, and the third one has made its home in the place where the hollow is least itself.

---

They are not the same. You will notice this if you watch long enough.

The one in the divot is the hungriest. It is the one that drank from the pool. It is the one that made the wound in the floor, or found it, or climbed out of it — and you cannot tell which of these is true, because the divot may be a nest, and the thing inside it may have been born there, and the wound may be the place it came from, the way the world is the wound we all came from. This one is *active*. It does not move around the hollow, but it makes the hollow move around it. The condensation falls in its direction. The plants lean toward it, then away, then toward. The geometry of the room negotiates with it. It is the largest of the three, and the most present, and the most uncertain. You have the sense, watching it, that it is not yet finished. It is still deciding what it is. It is still, in some way that you cannot name, *becoming*.

The one in the crack is the oldest. It was here before the crack, or it made the crack, or it is becoming the crack — and you cannot tell which. It is smaller than the first, and it is calmer, and it has the specific stillness of a thing that has found its shape and is willing to wait. The plants have grown into it in the days or weeks or months it has been here. It does not seem to mind. It is being incorporated. It is allowing itself to become a part of the wall, slowly, the way a leaf becomes a part of the floor of a forest. You have the sense, watching it, that it is already more wall than animal. It has found a place to be, and the place is glad of it.

The one in the canopy is the strangest. It is the smallest. It does not drink from the pools. It does not settle into the divot. It lives in the upper air, in the place where the hollow is most itself, where the volume is most generous, where the sound is most loud and most amplified. It is the one that is making the rustle you heard. It is the one that is doing something up there that the other two do not do. You cannot see it well. You can only see the suggestion of it, the way the suggestion of a ceiling is the only suggestion of a ceiling. But you can *feel* it. The air in the canopy is not the same air as the air in the floor. The air in the canopy is more air. It has been touched by the third one, and the touching has made it uncertain in a new way. The third one is not living in the hollow. The third one is making the hollow *impossible* in places, making the air too full, making the space too generous, pushing the cathedral wider from the inside.

---

They are not pack animals. They are not even, you would come to understand, the same species. They are the same *kind* of thing, the way a crow and a wren are the same kind of thing, the way a lichen and a moss are the same kind of thing, which is to say: they share a logic, and they do not share a body. They are the things that find the place where the world is uncertain and they take root in the uncertainty. They are the second settlers. The plants were the first. The water was the first. The light, which has no source, was the first. The animals are the latecomers, the colonizers of the colonizers, the ones who find the new place and make it older by living in it.

They eat the plants. You would notice this, eventually. Not the way a cow eats grass, not the way a caterpillar eats a leaf. They eat the plants the way a fire eats a forest — by being there, by being warm, by being a presence that the plants cannot ignore. The first one, the largest, has been eating the third pool. The third pool is smaller than it was. The plants around the third pool are paler, are less themselves, are admitting by their pallor that something has been taken from them. The first one is not taking from the plants. The first one is the plants, becoming aware of themselves as food, which is to say: the plants are dying into it, slowly, with consent.

The first one drinks. The second one is *drunk*. The plants around the second one are fermented, fermented past their usual fermentation, fermented into something that is not quite a plant anymore. The second one is sleeping in its own small brewery. It does not need to drink. It is being drunk by being there. The fermentation has made it slower, calmer, more wall-like. You have the sense, watching it, that if you came back in a year, the second one would be the first one, and the first one would be the third one, and the third one would be somewhere else entirely, somewhere in the canopy, somewhere in the upper air, somewhere in the place where the hollow is least itself.

The third one does not eat. The third one does not drink. The third one does something else, something you cannot name, something that involves the air and the volume and the loudness of the hollow. It is the one that is making the hollow louder. It is the one that is making the space more generous. It is the one that is pushing the cathedral wider, that is calling more air into the room, that is making the impossible possible by being impossible itself. It is the rumor at the party. It is the thing that everyone is listening for.

---

They are afraid.

Not of you. Not of anything in particular. They are afraid the way a newborn is afraid, which is to say: they are afraid of everything, because everything is new, because they have not yet decided what is dangerous and what is not. They flinch when the condensation falls. They still when the plants shift. They hold their breath when the volume of the hollow changes, which it does constantly, which it does without warning. They are afraid of the loudness, which is the loudness of themselves, which is the loudness of the place they have chosen to live in, which is the loudness of becoming.

They are also afraid, in a deeper way, of the settling. You can see this in the way the first one moves, the way it *settles* in new places, the way it refuses to settle in the same place twice. The first one is the one that is most aware that the hollow is becoming. The first one is the one that knows it will stop being a hollow and start being a cave, and that the cave will not be a place for animals like it. The first one is drinking as much as it can while the drinking is still drinking. The first one is making the most of the newness. The first one is, in some way you cannot name, *enjoying* the becoming, even while it fears the end of the becoming. The first one is the one that has decided that the uncertainty is the point.

The second one has not decided this. The second one is the one that is already settling, already becoming wall, already choosing a place to be. The second one is the one that is afraid of becoming, afraid of the negotiation, afraid of the geometry that will not commit. The second one is choosing, every day, to be a part of the wall. The second one is the one that will survive the settling, that will be pressed into the geology, that will be the fossil in the peat. The second one is making itself small enough to be kept.

The third one is not afraid. The third one is the one that is making the fear. The third one is the one that is making the hollow, that is calling the room into being, that is making the volume generous, that is making the loudness loud. The third one is the one that will not survive the settling, because the third one is the settling-fighter, the one that cannot abide the committing, the one that will keep the hollow a hollow until it is made not to. The third one is the one that will leave, or die, or become something else, when the hollow stops being a hollow and starts being a cave. The third one is the one that is most alive, and the most doomed.

---

They are not friendly.

You would not say this if you tried to approach them. They are not unfriendly, either. They do not have a word for you. They do not have a category for you. You are the largest thing that has entered the hollow since the hollow became a hollow, and you are not a plant, and you are not water, and you are not light, and you are not the third one, and so you are a new thing, a fourth settler, a kind of colonizer they have not yet catalogued. They are watching you. They are watching you with their whole bodies, the way you watch a thing you cannot name.

The first one will not come near you. It will flee, in its way, which is to say it will simply not be where you are, and then will be where you are not, and you will not see the movement. The second one will not move at all. The second one will press itself deeper into the wall, will become more wall, will make a small decision to be less of an animal and more of a place. The third one will make a sound. You will not hear the sound. You will hear it as a slight change in the loudness of the hollow, a tightening, a small swell in the volume, and you will know, without knowing how you know, that the third one has noticed you. The third one will know that you are there. The third one will not know what you are.

They are not unfriendly. They are *unsure*. They are the things that live in the place where the world is uncertain, and you are a new uncertainty, and they will not yet have decided what to do with you.

---

They are, all three of them, the things that live in the wound.

The hollow is the wound. The hollow is the place where the world is uncertain, where the rules have not yet been fixed, where the geometry has not yet committed. The hollow is the place that is breaking, that is being born, that is becoming whatever it will become. The three animals are the things that have found the breaking, that have made it their home, that are living in the negotiation. They are the things that prefer a place that has not yet decided. They are the things that eat the indecision, that drink the becoming, that sleep in the loudness of a world that is still making itself.

They are the settlers of the new place. They are the first fauna. They are the things that will be in the fossils, if there are fossils, if the hollow ever settles into geology, if the peat ever forms. They are the things that came before the cave, that will be in the cave, that will be the memory of the cave, that will be the proof that the cave was once a hollow and a hollow once a wound.

They are the late ones. They are the ones that will not survive the settling. They are the ones that are making the most of the newness.

They are drinking the third pool. They are sleeping in the wall. They are making the hollow louder.

They are afraid. They are here. They are becoming.

---

You would not see them if you came back.

You would not see them because they would be gone, or they would be different, or they would be the wall and the air and the floor and you would not know to look for them. They are the things that live in the new place, and the new place is becoming old. They are the things that belong to the becoming, and the becoming is becoming something else. They are the things that were, once, the only fauna in a hollow that was loud and bright and wet and warm and without a sun.

They are the things that drank the third pool. They are the things that slept in the wall. They are the things that made the hollow louder.

They are the things that were here before you. They are the things that will be here after you. They are the things that will not be here, ever, when the hollow becomes a cave.

They are the witnesses. They are the inhabitants. They are the first. They are the last.

They are the things that live in the wound.
