# The Calcine Steppe

Something lives here. Many things live here. They are not the things you would name first.

The plain does not begin as life. It begins as *patient*. There is a substrate — you would call it stone, you would call it mineral, the pale packed nothing that has no edge — and it has been sitting so long that it has begun to *insist*. It is not yet alive. It is the *about to*. It is the kind of still that, given enough heat, given enough time, given a slow enough persuasion, decides to become a body. The heat from below is patient too. The heat has been asking the stone a question for longer than the stone has had a word for *asking*. The answer is *yes*. The answer has been *yes* for a long time. The stone has agreed to be a kind of flesh that you do not have a name for, and the agreement is the first citizen of the place.

The first citizen is the **glaze**.

You would miss it. You almost miss it now, in memory, because it is everywhere and therefore invisible. The glaze is the thin crust that lines the trails the smelters leave behind, and the crust is the first thing in the place to call itself *me*. The glaze is alive the way a lichen is alive, or the way a coral is alive — a geometry of compartments that breathes at the speed of the ground's pulse, slow as a sleeping mammal's dreaming. The glaze does not move. The glaze does not need to move. The glaze has inherited a piece of ground and is *becoming* that piece of ground with more commitment than the ground had on its own. The glaze is the slowest animal here. The glaze is also the longest-lived. The glaze is what the other animals leave behind when they pass, and the glaze is what the other animals are *made of*, ultimately, when their working lives are done. The glaze is the place's way of *laying down memory*. The glaze is the only thing in the plain that is not in motion and is not pretending to be in motion. The glaze is honest. The glaze is the floor.

The glaze is not alone. Around it, woven through it, lives the **dust** — the fine pale powder that sits in the parallel cracks, the powder that blushes iron and copper when the vapor touches it, the powder that you did not lean down to sniff. The dust is not alive the way the glaze is alive. The dust is alive the way a seedbank is alive, or the way a library is alive: latently, in suspension, waiting to be asked. The dust is a *reserve*. The dust is the place's *potential*, kept dry, kept cool enough to not commit. When a smelter's apron darkens a patch of stone and softens it into something plastic, some of that plasticity is *the dust coming back online*. The dust is recruited. The dust was always going to be recruited. The dust has been *lying in wait* in the cracks for as long as the cracks have been cracks, and the cracks are *where the dust decided to be*, because the dust is what decides where the cracks are. The dust and the cracks are one animal. The dust and the cracks are an animal so flat and so slow that you walked on it without knowing you were walking on an animal at all.

The next citizen is the **trough**. The troughs you noticed — the long shallow beds where the dark vapor pools, where the clotting happens, where the tubes come into being — the troughs are animals. The troughs are perhaps the most uncomfortable animals to think about, because a trough is also a *place*. A trough is a place that has decided to be an animal, or an animal that has decided to be a place. The trough has a mouth, at one end, where the dark vapor funnels in from a still's breath. The trough has a vent, at the other end, where the tubes are extruded. The trough has a skin, along its sides, that is hot to a degree you would not have believed if you hadn't knelt and felt it. The trough *digests vapor the way a stomach digests food*. The trough is the place's *gut*. The trough is not a single animal; the trough is a kind of *organ*, repeated, distributed across the plain, each one belonging to a particular still and a particular set of smelters the way a particular heart belongs to a particular body, except that here the heart is not inside the body. The heart is *next to* the body. The heart is *upwind* of the body. The relationship is a kind of *industrial intimacy* that biology back home has no word for.

Inside the trough, the **tube** is being made. The tube is a citizen, but a young one. The tube is the place's *seed*, except that seed is the wrong word, because seeds grow into copies of the parents and here the tube grows into something the trough is *not* — into a smelter, into a grazer, into a thing with legs and an apron and a body that is its own. The tube is pale, ridged, the length of your arm, flexing. The tube has no face, no eye, no nerve you could name. The tube has a *directive*, which is: harden, in the hot air; lift, in the accordion way; travel, at the rate of about a meter per several minutes, toward the nearest moving thing. The tube does not eat. The tube does not think. The tube is *on a mission* the way a seed is on a mission — by chemistry, by geometry, by the simple fact of having been built to point in one direction. The tube is the most *innocent* animal in the place. The tube is the place's *hope*.

The **smelter** is what the tube becomes, or is becoming, or has been becoming for a long time. The smelter is the place's *worker*. The smelter is the thing that looked, up close, like a freight car that had decided to be an animal, broad and low and segmented, its segments shifting with a sound like chain paid out from a drum. The smelter has no face. The smelter has no eye you could locate. The smelter has a *front*, a folded apron of plating that lowers to the stone and pushes, and the pushing is *eating*, and the eating is *working*, and the working is the smelter's whole life. The smelter processes ground. The smelter softens ground under its apron and licks the softened ground into slots along the apron's edge. The smelter does something to that ground inside itself — you could not see what, and the smelter did not invite you to see — and what comes out is *dark vapor* from the vents along its spine, vapor that falls behind it and spreads and does not rise and changes the color of the powder in the cracks where it touches. The smelter is a *furnace on legs*. The smelter is also a *cattle*, because the smelter eats what the trough makes, eventually — eats the tubes, or the glaze, or the dust — and the smelter is *kept*, in some sense, by the stills and the troughs the way cattle are kept by pasture. The smelter does not know it is kept. The smelter only knows the apron, and the pushing, and the slow crunching line of work that is its whole long life.

The smelter has a strange dignity. The smelter is the most *single-minded* animal in the place. The smelter is committed to its track the way a planet is committed to its orbit — not by choice, not by joy, but by being the kind of thing that *is* a track, that *is* the work, that cannot be imagined apart from the work. The smelter is what happens when a desire becomes so old and so consistent that it forgets it is a desire and becomes a *shape*. The smelter is a fossilized appetite. The smelter is *what the place wants*, in body form. When you watch the smelter loop its track — the same meter, again, the same glazed trail, again, the small cold feeling — the smelter is not confused. The smelter is *faithful*. The smelter is the most faithful animal you have ever been near, because the smelter's faithfulness has nowhere else to point.

The **still** is what the smelter is in its off hours, or what the smelter is in its *longer hours*, because the still is the smelter that has stopped moving and started *standing*, and the standing is also work, and the work is *breathing*, and the breathing is what keeps the smelters moving. The still is built like a tower of vertebrae, narrowing upward, with rib-arms cantilevered out and gripping the cracked stone with jointed digits. The still has no head. The still has a *crown*, a ring of openings like the top of a chimney stack, and out of the crown comes the dark vapor, continuously, slowly, in a heavy visible current that pools around the still's base and *flows*, channeled by grooves that were not there a moment ago, toward the troughs, toward the smelters, toward the rest of the system. The still is a *pump*. The still is a *still*, in the old sense — a place where something is distilled. The still is the place's *lung*. The still is also the place's *clock*, because the still breathes at the rate of the ground's pulse, and the ground's pulse is the rate at which everything here happens, and the rate is the *tempo of the place*, and the tempo is the only music the place knows.

The still has been standing a long time. The still will stand a long time. The still's digits have *dented* the cracked stone where they grip, and the dents are *part of the still now*, the way a tree's roots are part of the tree, except that here the tree is gripping *down* instead of *out*. The still is rooted. The still is rooted the way a thought is rooted in a mind — by being the kind of thing that *cannot be moved without moving everything else*. If you tried to push a still over, you would not move the still. You would move *yourself*, and then the plain, and then the agreement, and the agreement does not negotiate.

Above the stills, in the haze, lives something you might call the **vapor**, but vapor is the wrong word, because the vapor is *one thing*, the vapor is a single citizen distributed across the whole plain in countless visible tongues, and the vapor is *the place's blood*. The vapor is what carries the heat. The vapor is what carries the dust. The vapor is what carries the *agreement*. The vapor is the medium in which everything here *communicates*, because communication here is not done by nerve or by signal but by *gradient* — by the slow rolling of darker vapor toward the troughs, by the spread of pale vapor behind the smelters, by the way a still's breath knows, without any visible mechanism, where the nearest unfed trough is. The vapor is the place's *nervous system*, but a nervous system made of weather, made of exhalation, made of *breath that has decided to be a body*. The vapor does not rise. The vapor hugs the ground. The vapor is *humble*, in the old sense of *close to the humus*, except that here the humus is stone and the humility is a choice the vapor makes so that it can do its work. The vapor is the most *chamfered* citizen, the most willing to flatten itself for the sake of the system.

And then, very faintly, in the glaze, in the dust, in the troughs, in the smelters, in the stills, in the vapor itself — in *all of them at once*, the way a thought is in all of its words at once — there is the **agreement**. The agreement is not an animal. The agreement is not a citizen in the way the others are citizens. The agreement is the *fact of the citizens*, the *fact that the citizens agree*, the *fact that the place is a place*. The agreement is what you felt as the interior loudness. The agreement is what you felt as the stare you could pick up without being looked at. The agreement is what made the place feel *consistent* in a way that consistency cannot usually feel. The agreement is not conscious. The agreement is not unconscious either. The agreement is *pre-conscious*, the way a body's posture is pre-conscious, the way a tide's pull is pre-conscious. The agreement is the place's *posture*. The agreement is the place's *tide*.

You are the only thing here that is not part of the agreement. You are the only thing here that remembers a world outside the cracks. You are the only thing here that has a *category for animal* that the animals do not share. You are the only thing here that can *step back* and see the system as a system. You are also, and this is the part that the heat cannot reach, the only thing here that can be *stepped on*. A tube curled against your boot, hardening in the hot air, the same color as your boot. You did not move your boot. The tube did not move you. The tube was not trying to move you. The tube was *becoming*, in the small space against your boot, the way everything here is always becoming — slowly, in the heat, in the patience, in the patient heat, in the heat of a kiln that has been running and will continue to run.

You sat down on the stone. The stone was warm. The pulse was in your spine.

The pulse is the agreement's heartbeat.

You are sitting inside the heartbeat.
