Who Lives Here

What lives here is not a population. It is a *body*. A body that is a country. A country that is a single, ongoing, very loud act of self-maintenance.

The thing that lives in this valley is the valley. The thing that lives in the country is the country. There is no other candidate. There has not been for a long time, and there was never going to be.

Call it the Lattice. Call it the Weave. Call it the green tide. It does not care what you call it, because naming is a thing for things that can be picked out of a crowd, and there is no crowd, and there is no outside of it to be picked out from. It is the only living citizenry of these ridges, and it is also the government, and the soil, and the rain, and the weather, and the memory. It is a forest that has folded itself shut over the hole where a forest's collaborators used to be, and it is doing every one of their jobs, and it is doing them badly, and beautifully, and without rest.

Stand in the middle of it. Notice what you are standing in.

You are standing in a stomach.

Not metaphorically. Or — only metaphorically in the way that any word is a metaphor, because the Lattice does not know it has a stomach. The Lattice does not know anything in the sense that you mean *knowing*. But it has, over the long and patient negotiation of millions of years, acquired a topology that is unmistakably digestive. It has an inside, which is the understory, and an outside, which is the stone. It has a mouth, which is the upper canopy, opening and closing, drinking, breathing, tasting the air. It has a gut, which is the woven floor, taking in the dead, taking in the fallen, taking in itself. It has a circulation, which is the water, the pale latex, the mucous on the stems, the wet black pools in the lattice gaps. It has a pulse, which is the *thwap-thwap-thwap* of leaves above you, the constant small percussion of the canopy flexing in winds you cannot see.

The Lattice is a creature the size of a mountain range, made out of plants that are also creatures, that are also parts of the creature, that are also the floor you are standing on.

Here is what lives in it. Or: here is what it is made of, which is the only question that has any answer.

**The Pillars.** The verticals. The stems the thickness of your thigh, pale green, slick with a constant dew of their own transpiration, lifting the roof. Each one is a feeding tube, a drinking straw, a slow industrial chimney pulling vapor in through the purse-mouthed stomata that freckle its length. Watch one for a minute and you will see the mucous extrude and retract, extrude and retract, the way a throat works, the way a lung works. The Pillars are not trees. They are too thin, too vertical, too obviously *a part of something else*. They do not grow toward the light the way a tree does, competing with its neighbors, hoarding its patch of sky. They grow upward because *that is the direction of the receiving end*. They are an organ of intake. They are the Lattice's gills, drawn up out of the wet dark into the wet light, where the air is thickest with what they need. They do not have a heartbeat, but they have a peristalsis, a slow wave of contraction that runs up their length and pulls water up from the weave below. The whole forest is full of these waves, passing through the Pillars at different speeds, out of phase, so that the air above the canopy is being tasted continuously, a rolling intake, a breath that has no in or out.

**The Weave.** The floor. The part you stand on, if you are fool enough to stand on it. The Weave is the Lattice's great invention, the piece of engineering that allowed the whole arrangement to come into being. A horizontal mesh of stems, each one the width of a forearm, knotted and braided into a mat that is sometimes a foot thick, sometimes a yard, depending on how the traffic of growth and decay has been running. The Weave is the Lattice's body in the way that your body's body is your body: the dense, layered, working middle where everything happens. In the Weave, the water circulates. In the Weave, the dead are taken in. In the Weave, the new are pushed up. The Weave is constantly being woven and un-woven. A stem will lie horizontal for a season, fatten, send down its sucking white root-tips into the black water of the pools, and then, in a moment you will not see coming, it will *turn*. It will send a bud upward. The bud will thicken. The bud will become a Pillar. And the place where the Pillar used to be lying will be filled, within hours, by another stem sliding in from the side, taking up the slack. The Weave is a treadmill. It is a tread*mill*. It is the Lattice's way of moving, without legs, across the surface of the rock — always laying down new floor in front, always reabsorbing old floor behind, the whole mat creeping down the slopes at the speed a fingernail grows, eating the mountain as it goes.

**The Black Water.** The pools in the gaps of the Weave. They look like water, and they are water, but they are not, in any sense, *standing* water. The water in a pool is never the same water two minutes running. The Lattice is drinking the pool. The pool is the Lattice's blood, briefly pooled, briefly external. The black color is not dirt. There is no dirt. The black is the color of the Lattice's interior, the color of the underside of the Weave, the color of the root-tips descending through the pool to anchor themselves in whatever the Weave is anchored to. The water in the pools is dark the way a vein is dark: because it is full of what is being carried. The pools glisten. The pools are warm. The pools are the place where you can most clearly see the Lattice *drink*. Lean close. You will see the surface of the pool dimple, continuously, in tiny ringed collapses, as the Lattice pulls another draft of itself back down into itself.

**The Lungs.** The leaves. The small, stiff, sun-loving leaves that you cannot see clearly because the light is so flat and white, but that are everywhere, on every Pillar, in every direction, in such quantity that the canopy above you is less a thing made of leaves than a *substance* made of leaves, a felted green atmosphere, a textile. The leaves are not for catching light, exactly, although they do that. They are not for transpiring, exactly, although they do that too. The leaves are for *talking*. Each leaf is a chemical broadcasting unit, releasing a constant, complex, modulated exhalation of volatile compounds into the saturated air. The compounds drift. The compounds are read. The Lattice is, in this way, a vast olfactory network, every leaf both a sender and a receiver, the whole canopy a single slow conversation conducted in scents that no animal nose will ever parse. *I am here. I am full. I am hungry. I am wounded. The wind is from the east. The light is failing. The water is rising. I am dying; come and take me. I am sprouting; give me room.* The conversation is continuous. The conversation is the forest. When you smell, in the wet air, a sharp green sweetness, or a dark vegetal musk, or a strange mineral tang, you are smelling a sentence. You are smelling the Lattice thinking out loud, which is the only way it has of thinking at all.

**The Drippers.** The leaves do another job that is, if anything, even stranger. They are the rain. Or rather, they are the *decision* about the rain. The Lattice is a closed system. Almost no water enters from above. What falls from the white sky is mist, and the mist is taken in directly through the Pillars' stomata, and that is enough, barely, to keep the system running. But the system is not perfectly sealed. Moisture accumulates on the underside of leaves, in fat trembling drops, and the drops grow, and the drops wait. The Lattice, in its slow chemical conversation, *negotiates* the drops. A signal passes through a branch: now. And the drops fall. They fall in a coordinated cascade, a clatter of water striking the Weave, a small engineered storm that has been agreed upon by the canopy as a whole. The drip is not weather. The drip is *plumbing*. The Lattice is moving its own water around internally, calling for precipitation where it is needed, in the amounts it is needed, on the schedule it requires. The whole valley is a building with its own weather system, and the weather is on a committee.

**The Pale Flowers.** You will see them if you look, high up, where the Pillars fork, in the brighter light of the upper canopy. They are small, stiff, lurid — a magenta so deep it is almost black, a yellow so bright it stings, a UV-patterned white that is, to a pollinating insect, a screaming billboard, and there are no pollinating insects. The flowers are not for pollination. The flowers are for *signaling*. They are the Lattice's flares. They say: *here is the edge. Here is where the new growth is. Here is the leading edge of the expansion.* The bright colors are visible to the Lattice's own photoreceptors, which are distributed through every leaf, every stem, every tip. The flowers are the Lattice pointing at itself. The Lattice is a creature that decorates its own advancing front with flowers, and the flowers are not beautiful, exactly, but they are *decorative*, in the way a bowerbird's bower is decorative, in the way a nervous-system display is decorative. They are excess. They are the Lattice showing off to itself, which is the only audience it has.

**The Dying.** And the dead. The brown stem you will find, sagging, surrounded by its neighbors, being unmade. The Lattice does not let anything go. Nothing that dies in the Weave ever stays dead. It is taken. It is wrapped. The white threads of the Lattice's internal hyphae — and it does have hyphae, somewhere in the system, something fungal, something borrowed, something older than the plants themselves — flow into the dying tissue and pull its substance back out. The brown stem is not rotting. Rotting is what happens to dead things in the presence of *other* things. The brown stem is being *eaten from the inside by the thing it is a part of*. The death of a stem in the Lattice is a transaction. It is the Lattice redistributing its own matter. The Lattice is its own scavenger. The Lattice is its own soil. The Lattice is its own worm.

What does not live here is also part of the answer.

No insects. No birds. No mammals. No fish in the pools, because the pools are not pools. No worms in the soil, because there is no soil. No fungi in the rotting wood, because the wood is not rotting, it is being *eaten by itself*. The whole category of *decomposer* has been taken in-house. The whole category of *pollinator* has been made redundant. The whole category of *herbivore* has been eliminated by a plant that is too busy to be eaten. The Lattice has, in the long quiet of its tenure in this valley, eaten its own food web.

And it has done something stranger. It has eaten the *difference* between one plant and another.

When you look at the Lattice, you will look for individuals. You will look for a tree, a shrub, a fern, a moss, a lichen. You will not find them. You will find the Weave, and the Pillars, and the Lungs, and you will not be able to say *where one ends and the other begins*. A Pillar and a leaf are clearly different organs. But a Pillar and another Pillar, a hundred feet apart — are they the same individual? Are they different individuals? Are they parts of the same individual? The Lattice is a clone. The Lattice is a continuous mat, possibly all genetically identical, possibly a chimera of a few clonal lines, possibly — and this is the part that makes even the patient botanists in the lowlands put down their magnifiers — *not a population at all*, in any sense that the word has historically been used. It is an organism. A single organism. A single very large, very slow, very loud organism, the size of a country, shaped like a valley, that has been growing here since the rock cooled enough to be held.

The animals did not leave. The animals were never here. Or: the animals were here, and they were *reabsorbed*. The Lattice does not eat animals. But the Lattice is patient, and the Lattice is total, and in the long run the Lattice does not leave room for any other kind of body. There is no niche here. There is no space. The water is drunk. The air is drunk. The light is taken. The soil is built. The pollination is done by the wind, by the chemistry, by the conversation. The death is done by the kin. The whole project of *being alive in this valley* has been completed by a single lineage, and the project is now in its maintenance phase, and the maintenance is loud, and the maintenance is constant, and the maintenance is the only thing the Lattice knows how to do, which is fine, because the Lattice does not need to know how to do anything else.

Stand in the middle of it. You will not be acknowledged. You will not be eaten. You will not be missed. You will be, in a small way, in the way of a foreign body in a wound, *noticed*. A chemical signal will pass through the Weave under your feet. A signal will go out, in the Lattice's slow and total semaphore: *something is here that is not us.* The signal will propagate at the speed of plant-signaling, which is slow, which is a foot a minute, which is the speed of a thought in a creature the size of a country. By the time the signal reaches the leading edge of the Lattice, up at the ridges, hours will have passed. And then, perhaps, the Lattice will consider you. And then, perhaps, the Lattice will decide what to do about you.

You will not be there to find out. You will have already left, or already been absorbed, or already become, in the Lattice's chemical bookkeeping, a small parcel of matter that is no longer foreign.

This is what lives in the valley.

A single creature. A single body. A single green and wet and very loud breathing.

It is not unhappy. It is not happy. It is in the middle of becoming something else, and it always is, and it always will be, and the sound of it, the *thwap-thwap-thwap* of the leaves above you in a wind you cannot find, is the sound of a country that is breathing, and that does not need you to know it is doing so, and that will continue to do so, long after you are gone, for as long as the rock holds, and the water comes, and the light falls.
