**What Lives in the Throat**

There is no answer to "who," because "who" is a load-bearing word and this place does not bear such loads. What lives here—if *lives* can be admitted through the same door as *here*—lives differently than the question expects, and it would be unfair to it to pretend otherwise.

The Throat has, as far as I can tell, three residents. I will describe them in the order I came to know them, which is not the order of their importance.

---

**The Watcher on the Rim**

I noticed it first as a trick of the light.

A patch of dark against dark. The kind of absence your eye reads as shape only because it interrupts something. It moved along the upper edge in a way that my body understood as movement before my mind committed to the word—a translation, a gliding, a *taking up of a new position* that was not, strictly, a step. The legs were involved. The legs were not the point.

It is smaller than a person. It is darker than the basalt, which is the darkest stone I have ever seen outside of deep ocean basalt, and this is, in fact, deep inside something. Its outline is uncertain. It does not have the hard edge that a mammal has, the boundary that says *here is me, here is not me*. It is more like a region of slightly increased darkness that has been allowed, by some agreement I was not consulted on, to drift. Sometimes it is upright. Sometimes it is shorter, almost flat. Sometimes it is a smear, and I lose it, and then I find it again forty meters further along the rim without having seen it cross the distance.

I do not know what it eats. I do not know if it eats. It does not approach the water that runs down the walls, and it does not approach the lower depths where the heat is most intense. It stays at the rim. It moves when the pulse comes. It has a relationship with the pulse that I want to call choreographic, except I cannot tell which one is leading.

I want to say it is watching me. I have decided, for the moment, not to say that. I have decided, for the moment, that what it is doing is more like *allowing me to be watched by it*—a permission, an opening, an aperture being held at a specific width. When I turn my whole body toward it, the dark patch becomes slightly more dense, as if it has registered my attention. When I look away, it loosens. It is, in this sense, a courteous organism. If it is an organism. If courtesy is the right word for what it does.

I have been here for what feels like a long time and I do not know its name for itself. I do not know if it has a name for itself. I do not know if *itself* is the right unit.

---

**The Midges**

I almost did not list these.

They live in the steam.

The water that condenses on the upper walls and runs down into the heat—that water does not all make it down. Some of it, near the rim, where the air is just cool enough, hangs. It hangs in a thin layer, a kind of permanent mist no thicker than a breath on a window. In that layer, there is something alive.

It is small. The instrument would resolve a single cell; I cannot resolve this with my unaided eye. I see it as a kind of tremor in the air, a stippling, a region where the steam is not quite behaving as steam should. When the heat pulse passes, the stippling condenses—rushes upward, away from the walls, gathers at the rim in a faint cloud that you would not notice unless you were looking. Then it rains back down, slowly, and re-disperses into the mist.

I do not know what it eats. I do not know if it eats. I do not know if it is a colony or an organism or a single thing distributed across a region. I have decided, arbitrarily, to call it a midge, because *midge* is a small soft word and the thing in the steam seems to deserve a small soft word.

The midges and the Watcher do not, as far as I can tell, interact. The midges do not register the Watcher's passage. The Watcher does not register the midges' condensation. They share the rim the way a river and the air above it share a space—without meeting, without caring, without even the courtesy of avoidance, because avoidance would imply the possibility of meeting.

I include them because they are the only thing here, besides me, that I am certain is alive. Certain is too strong a word. *Inclined to call alive.* They move. They respond to the pulse. They have, in their dim way, an inside and an outside. That is the most I can commit to.

---

**The Light in the Veins**

I have saved this for last because it is the one I have not told anyone about, and because telling it now requires me to commit, on the page, to a kind of observation that I have been avoiding.

The veins in the rock are not mineral. I have been told they are mineral. I have, myself, used the word mineral to describe them, when speaking to others, in the early days, when I was still trying to make this place answer questions in the grammar the questions were asked in. The veins are aligned. The veins have structure. The veins flash, in synchrony with the heat pulse, in a pattern that propagates upward from a depth I cannot see.

I want to say that the light in the veins is alive.

I want to say it. I have not, yet, said it. I have written it here, in a place where the saying is not yet final, where the words can still be taken back if a better explanation presents itself.

What I can say with more confidence: the light in the veins is not a passive effect of the heat. It is too organized. It is too *sequential*. It moves. It begins somewhere below, in the densest part of the network, in the place where the fissure narrows toward the point I cannot see. It moves upward through the vein system, which is a three-dimensional web, not a single channel, and the path it takes through that web is not the shortest path, and it is not the path of least resistance, and it is not random. It is, as far as I can tell, the path that *says something*.

I have watched it thirty-one times. I have, on three occasions, attempted to record it with a device that should have been able to. The recordings are blank. The recordings are not blank in the way a failed recording is blank—they are not zero. They are *flat*. They show the steady ambient glow of the vein material, the light it gives off continuously, the faint phosphorescence. They do not show the flash. The device does not see the flash. My eyes see the flash. My eyes see the flash clearly, every time, unambiguously. The device does not see it.

I have not yet decided what this means. I have begun to suspect it means something.

If the light in the veins is alive—if *alive* can be stretched to include a thing that lives in the structure of stone, that thinks in the syntax of flash, that has been speaking, if it is speaking, in this same upward-rushing pattern for the entire duration of the place's existence—then it is by far the largest organism I have ever encountered. It is the place. It is the heat. It is the pulse. It is the slow upward migration of the water, which I had been reading as a hydrological effect, a simple consequence of condensation and gravity. If the light in the veins is alive, then the water is not falling. The water is being *gathered*. The water is being drawn down into the depths, into the densest part of the network, into the part that flashes brightest, for a reason.

I do not know the reason.

I have not gone down. I have not gone down for the reasons I listed before, and for another reason, which I will admit here because this document will not, I think, be read by anyone who would think less of me for admitting it. I have not gone down because I have begun to suspect that going down would constitute an answer to a question I have not yet finished asking, and I am not yet ready to have it answered.

---

**A Note on the Water**

I want to add a fourth entry, even though it is not, in any conventional sense, a resident.

The water is everywhere. The water is the medium. The water is the air, in some sense—the humidity here is total, and the mist is permanent, and the thin sheets of moisture on the walls are the surface the midges live in, and the steam that rises from the depths is the visible signature of the pulse. The water is, I think, the connective tissue. The water is the way the parts of this place talk to each other.

The water comes from somewhere. I have not been able to determine where. The water does not, as far as I can tell, come from the sky—there is no precipitation, no clouds in any meaningful sense, just the white smear. The water does not come from a spring, from a reservoir, from a river. The water comes from the rock. The water comes from the rock the way sweat comes from a body, which is to say continuously, and as a consequence of being what it is.

The water runs down.

I keep writing this sentence. I keep writing this sentence because it is the only one that is reliably true. The water runs down. The water runs down and joins the depths. The depths flash. The depths gather the water. The water comes back up as steam. The steam condenses on the upper walls. The upper walls sweat. The sweat runs down.

It is, as I said, a kind of weather.

It is, I have begun to suspect, also a kind of breath.

---

**A Final Note on Myself**

I include myself here because the question of who lives in a place cannot be answered honestly without including the one who is asking, and because I have been here long enough that I am no longer sure I am a visitor.

I sweat. I drink the water when I can catch it. I have learned to time my movements to the pulse, because the pulse is the only reliable clock. I have begun to read the flash in the veins, in the sense that I have begun to have expectations about where it will arrive and in what order, even though I cannot yet decode it. I have begun to forget things that have nothing to do with this place. I have begun to remember things that have nothing to do with this place with unusual clarity. I do not know which of these is more concerning.

The Watcher has not approached me. I have not approached the Watcher. We have, over what feels like a long time, established something that I will not call a relationship because that word has too much furniture in it. We have established a *co-occupation*. We share the rim. We avoid the heat. We let each other be.

I have not decided if the light in the veins is alive.

I have not decided if I am.

I will be here for what feels like longer.
