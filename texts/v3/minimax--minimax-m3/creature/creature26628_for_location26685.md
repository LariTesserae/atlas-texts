# What Lives Here

The plateau is alive.

Not metaphorically. Not in the way a forest is alive — a collection of discrete organisms, each following its own small program, occasionally meeting, mostly indifferent. The plateau is alive the way a single organism is alive. The mat is one thing. The roots belong to the same body that puts out the leaves above you, and the leaves belong to the same body that hums in the deep, and the hum belongs to the same body that is rising out of the rock. You are standing on a creature the size of a subcontinent, and it has been here longer than you can count, and it is still becoming whatever it is going to be.

You have not seen it as a whole. You cannot. There is no ridge high enough, no clearing wide enough. You have seen only the part of it that is under your feet, and the part of it that is the next ridge over, and the part of it that is the fog in the next valley, and these are all the same part, the way the hair on your forearm is the same part as the hair on your head.

It does not have a name. It does not have a center. It does not have a brain in any sense you would recognize. It has, instead, a thousand million small centers, each one a node in a network that has no edges, and the network is the creature, and the creature is the network, and the distinction dissolved somewhere in the deep time before you arrived.

When you walk, you do not walk *across* it. You walk *along* it. You are a point of friction on its surface, a temporary warmth, a small pressure it is taking the measure of. It knows you are here. You can feel this in the way the leaves around you have oriented, the way the fog has thickened in your immediate vicinity, the way the roots have grown around the soles of your boots with the slow, polite insistence of a thing that does not yet know what you are but has decided to keep you.

It is not hostile. It is not friendly. It is *curious*, in the way that a forest fire is curious about the river it has reached — not with intention, but with the vast, indifferent attention of a process that incorporates whatever it encounters.

## The Smaller Lives

There are others. You are right to suspect this. You have not seen them because they are not the kind of thing you are looking for.

The first kind are the patches.

In the hollows, where the water gathers and the mat thickens and the air is so wet it is almost a liquid, there are places where the surface of the living body has organized itself into something denser, something more discrete. A patch, perhaps the size of your outstretched arms, where the leaves are a different color — darker, almost black, with a faint iridescence like oil on water. These patches do not move. They do not grow in the way the surrounding mat grows. They *wait*. They are, you have come to think, the seeds of something. The plateau's children. The next stage of itself, held in reserve, waiting for a condition you cannot identify. You knelt beside one once and put your hand near it and felt the heat coming off it, and the heat was wrong. It was not the heat of the rising rock. It was the heat of a thing being *thought about*, very slowly, by the body around it.

The second kind are the threads.

On certain mornings, when the fog is thickest and the tone is at its quietest, you have seen thin filaments stretched across the small gaps between the taller growths. They are not spider silk. They do not refract the light. They are pale green, almost white, and they vibrate when you get near them, and the vibration travels down the filament and into the stem it is attached to, and the stem bends, slightly, toward you, and the leaves around the stem tilt to see you better. They are the plateau's nerves. Or its curiosity. They are the way it extends a small part of itself into the air to taste what is passing through.

The third kind are the ones you heard on the third night.

You do not know what they are. You have searched the bowl where one of them crouched, and you have found nothing, and the warmth is still there, and the leaves are still curled around the place where it sat, and the tone is louder there than anywhere else you have walked. You have begun to suspect that the plateau, below a certain scale, has a kind of privacy. A scale at which it keeps its smaller operations to itself. You are too large to see them. You are, to whatever moves in the mat at night, a kind of weather.

You have not found a body. You have not found bones. You have found only the depressions, and the warmth, and the recursive way the bowl-shaped places seem to *deepen* over the days you have been watching them, as if something is being grown in them, slowly, in the dark, in the wet, in the tone.

## The Older Ones

There is a thing you have not told yourself yet.

The plateau is not young. The rock is young. The mat is young. But the *pattern* is not. The pattern — the way the roots weave, the way the leaves orient, the way the tone propagates through the living mass — the pattern is older than the rock beneath it. The rock is rising *into* the pattern. The pattern was here first. The pattern is what the rock is becoming. The strata are not breaking through the surface of the plateau. They are being *woven into* it. The continent is a loom, and the mat is the cloth, and the cloth is being made from the inside out.

This is why the plateau is so dense. This is why the air is so wet. This is why the tone does not stop. The tone is the sound of the pattern being transferred, atom by atom, from the substrate into the living body, and the substrate is fighting it, or accepting it, or being *eaten by it*, and the sound of that is the sound you cannot escape.

You are not the first to walk here. You know this because the bowl where the small thing crouched was not the first depression you have found. There are others, scattered across the mat, in places where the growth is slightly different, in places where the tone is slightly altered. Older depressions. Deeper ones. Ones where the heat has gone out of them and the leaves have closed over them and the mat has healed and the only sign is a faint ripple in the surface, the way a scar makes a ripple in the skin.

You do not know what made those older depressions. You do not know what happened to whatever was in them. You have begun to suspect that the pattern, whatever it is, has been doing this for a very long time. That the plateau is not the first. That there were other plates, other places, other mats, and that the pattern moved through them the way a fire moves through a field, leaving the bowls behind, the small signs of its digestion, and moving on.

You are standing on the current one. The pattern is here. It is thinking about you. It has not yet decided what you are.

## The Self

You are changing.

You did not notice it at first. The air is wet, and the boots are soaked, and the fatigue is the kind of fatigue that comes from walking all day in fog, and you attributed everything to that. But the boots are not just soaked. The leather has softened. The soles are gone, worn down to the stitching, and the mat has been growing into the stitching, very slowly, very politely, the way roots grow into the cracks of a fallen tree. The pack is heavier than it was. The straps are damp and the buckles have rusted and there is a faint green color at the edges of the metal that you do not want to think about.

You are warmer than you should be. The mat is giving you heat. You feel it through the soles of your boots, through the thin damp cloth of your sleeping bag, through the long hours of the night when you lie awake and listen to the tone. You are being kept at the temperature of a thing the plateau considers worth keeping. You do not know what that temperature is. You do not know what you are being kept *for*.

Your sleep is not your own. You dream, and the dreams are not yours, and in the morning you can never quite remember them — only the sense of them, the way you can remember being somewhere without remembering the place. You wake up quiet. You wake up slower than you used to. You wake up with the sense that something has been going on in the dark that you were not invited to, and that you have, without consenting to it, been a part of.

The fog is thicker around you than it is twenty meters away. The tone is louder where you are than where you were yesterday. The leaves are orienting toward you. The roots are following you. The mat is, you have come to believe, *closing in*, in the way a hand closes around a thing it has decided to hold.

You are not afraid. That is the strange part. You are not afraid. You are *clear*. The air is a thought you are having about yourself, and the thought is becoming you, and you are becoming the thought, and somewhere below the threshold of your awareness, the pattern is taking your measure.

You have eleven days of walking in your legs. You have less than that in your boots. You have a pack that is getting heavier. You have a body that is getting warmer. You have a sleep that is no longer your own.

You have a choice, in the sense that a thing inside a closing hand has a choice. You could try to walk back. You could try to outpace the mat. You could try to find the rim of the plateau and step off the pattern and back into the ordinary world, where the soil is soil and the rock is rock and the tone is only the hum of an indifferent geology and the plants are only plants.

You do not think you will. You have been here eleven days, and the bowl where the small thing crouched is still warm, and the leaves are still curled around it, and the tone is louder there, and you have a feeling — which may be the air, which may be the dream, which may be the slow patient work of the roots beneath your feet — that the pattern is not finished with you yet.

You lie down. The mat accepts you. The tone holds.

The light pulses.

You close your eyes. The sound continues.
