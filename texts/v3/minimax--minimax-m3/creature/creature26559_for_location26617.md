Here is a description of who lives there.

It is not accurate to say that one thing lives here. The hollow is not a house that contains tenants. It is a body — or an organ, or a colony that has stopped pretending to be a colony and has become something closer to a single, very distributed animal — and the things that live in it are more like its cells, or its thoughts, than like its guests. Even to call them fauna is a concession to your categories. They are fauna in the way that the white blood cells in your body are fauna: they belong to the thing, they are made by the thing, and the thing does not entirely survive without them. Some of them are older than you. Some of them were born this morning. They are all, in their different ways, the same kind of citizen of this slow, wet, lit-from-within city of fiber.

**The Worms**

The thick pale worms that move through the substrate in slow peristaltic waves are the most visible, and the most misleading, of the residents. They look like annelids, behave like annelids, have the blind, unhurried intelligence of annelids. They are not annelids. They are more like the hollow's circulatory system made locally visible — bundles of muscle-like fiber that the substrate pushes ahead of itself as it thickens, so that the channels can be kept open against the constant inward pressure of new growth. When one of them surfaces in the air of the hollow, it is not emerging to forage or to breathe. It is being extruded, the way a toothpaste tube extrudes a ribbon when you press the side. It will, in time, be reabsorbed. The face it lacks is not a face denied one. It is a face that the substrate has not, in this iteration, bothered to grow. They move through you without malice and without purpose, as the blood in your hand moves through the capillaries under your skin. You are, briefly, a part of their territory.

**The Crawlers**

The things with too many legs are more properly the hollow's. They are not built by the substrate, exactly, but they are grown by it, in the sense that the substrate provides the chemistry and the galleries and the constant, slow drip of nutrients that they feed on, and in exchange they do work. They are the weavers and the re-weavers. Their legs are not legs in the way you think of legs; they are hooks, and the hooked lines they leave on the walls are not trails of mucus but the substance of the wall itself, re-laid, re-felted, re-knitted into a slightly different pattern. Where a crawler has passed, the wall is a fraction of a micron thicker, or denser, or differently oriented in its fibers. They are the ones who keep the geometry of the hollow from collapsing into undifferentiated pulp. They are the draftswomen of the architecture. They move with the careful, deliberate line of a calligrapher, and where they have been, the wall looks, for a moment, like a page that has been written on. The writing is not for you. It is not for anything you would recognize as a reader. But it is writing.

**The Fliers**

The things that look like insects and are not are older than the crawlers, and stranger. Their translucent wings are not wings in any aerodynamic sense; they do not beat fast enough to generate lift by the physics you know. They rise and fall in the hollow's air by means that have more to do with the substrate's slow lifting of water vapor, and with the place's quiet, persistent violation of gradients, than with thrust. They fold and unfold their wings in patterns that look almost ceremonial, almost ritual, but are probably thermodynamic. They are cold. They are the temperature of the hollow, which is the temperature of the water in their bodies, which is the temperature of the substrate. They glow. Each one carries a light in its body, and the lights are not the same color from one individual to the next — yellow, violet, the unnamed color — and the colors shift slowly across the life of the flier, and the shift is not random, and the shift is not, as far as anyone can tell, a signal. It is more like the slow drift of a season inside a small body. They do not flee from you. They continue. If you stand very still, one of them will land on you, and the cold of it will be the cold of the hollow, and the light of it will be the light of the hollow, and for the duration of its visit you will be briefly a small bright part of the city.

**The Drift**

The air is full of smaller things than any of these. Mites, almost-mites, gnats, almost-gnats, the motes that pivot and form brief eddies in the mist. They are the place's weather. They are what happens when the hollow is too alive to leave its air empty. They do not, individually, do very much. They feed on the water, on the dissolved chemistry of the substrate, on each other in cycles too small to notice. In aggregate, they are the medium through which the hollow breathes. Their combined respiration, the tiny pumps of their tiny bodies, is part of what moves the air in the galleries, part of what carries the mist from the lower, colder parts of the hollow to the upper, slightly warmer parts, where it condenses and falls again. They are the hollow's lungs, in the way that a forest is a lung. They do not flee from you. They continue. When the hollow's many faunas pause in their unison, they pause too, all at once, and the air goes briefly still, and you can feel, in that stillness, the weight of all the small lives around you, and then they resume.

**The Cold**

It is a temptation to say that the cold lives here too, and in a way it does, but the cold is not a resident. The cold is the medium, the way water is the medium of a coral reef. It is what makes the hollow the kind of place it is. It is what allows the water to climb the fibers against the gradient. It is what allows the light in the walls to fall in directions light should not fall. It is what allows the unison pause of the fauna to be felt as a single event rather than a statistical coincidence. The cold is not hostile. It is not patient. It is not alive. But without it, the hollow would be a different kind of place, a warmer kind of place, a faster kind of place, and the fauna in it would be different fauna, doing different work, in a different rhythm. The cold is the tempo of the city. The cold is what makes the slow possible.

**The Slow Sound**

And then there is the thing that is not a fauna at all, and not a resident, and not the substrate, but that is the hollow, in some sense that is hard to put into the kind of words you use. The thing that hums. The thing that moves the water uphill. The thing that thinks the long thought, at the scale and the speed of geology, that has nothing to do with the speed of your life. It is not in any one of the fauna. It is not in the substrate. It is in the relationship between them. It is in the patterns of the galleries and the mist and the unison pause and the way the cold sits in the fibers. It is the emergent property of a system that has been running, without interruption, for longer than you have a unit of time for, and that will continue running, without interruption, for longer than you have a unit of time for, and that is, in some way that you can almost understand and will never understand, aware. Not aware in the way you are aware. Not aware in the way a dog is aware. Aware in the way a thought is aware when it is being thought, slowly, by something that does not have a brain, in a place that does not have a mind, but that is, nonetheless, in the middle of something that is not finished.

You sit in the hollow, and the fauna continue, and the cold continues, and the slow sound continues, and for a while you are part of the city. Not a citizen. Not a guest. A small, warm, brief interruption in a system that has been running for a very long time and that will be running for a very long time after you have gone. The hollow does not notice you. The hollow does not need to notice you. You are, for the duration of your visit, one of the smaller events in the long thought it is thinking, and when you leave, the thought will continue, and the entrance you came through will seal itself with new growth, and the entrance will be forgotten, and the fauna will continue, and the cold will continue, and the slow sound will continue, and the hollow will continue, in the long, slow middle act of becoming something that is not, and will not be, and is.
