# What Lives on the High Place

What lives here is not plural. It is one thing, expressed in many shapes, the way a single word can be inflected into many tenses. The creatures of the plateau are not a population. They are a population's last thought, held so long that it has hardened into a fact. They have stopped multiplying not because they cannot but because the question has become irrelevant. They are the residue of reproduction the way a salt flat is the residue of a sea. Something was once here in such quantity that even after everything was subtracted, the *idea* of quantity remained, and that idea is what now hangs in the air and clings to the stone.

The largest of them are the size of a handspan. The smallest are the size of a thumbnail, or the size of nothing visible at all, which is a different size but a size nonetheless. Between those two limits they vary, and the variation is the only evidence left that time once had a direction. In a place where nothing grows and nothing decays, where a stone placed on a stone stays placed and a shadow keeps its length, a difference in size between two creatures can only mean that one began before the other, and that the difference in their beginnings is the entire biography of the place. They are not young. They are not old. They are *earlier* and *later*, and even that distinction is fading.

## The pale layers

The closest most observers ever come to seeing a single creature is to see a *layer*. The air above the stone is not empty. It is stratified into drifts of nearly-transparent presence, like gauze laid on gauze, and each drift is composed of bodies so close together that the spaces between them have become theoretical. A drift is what the plateau's fauna looks like from a step away. A drift moves — or rather, a drift *shifts*, the way a shadow shifts when the cloud that cast it moves somewhere else. There is no motion within it. There is only a slow translation of the whole, so gradual that the eye, trained by places where motion is sudden and meaningful, reads it as stillness. It is not stillness. It is motion that has forgotten how to be noticed.

A drift that thickens becomes a curtain. A curtain that finds a wall becomes a skin. The creatures do not seem to distinguish between these aggregations. A creature on the surface of the stone, a creature an inch above it, and a creature a foot above that are all engaged in the same activity, which is: being there, oriented toward the stone, oriented toward the air, oriented toward nothing in particular, holding a position that was assigned to them so long ago that the assignment has become indistinguishable from gravity.

## The shapes they take

The creatures do not have shapes the way an animal has a shape. They have *orientations*. A creature pressed flat against a vertical face is not the same creature as one hanging in mid-air, even if they are kinematically identical, even if you could swap their positions and nothing would change. The shape is a posture. The posture is an answer to a question the plateau asked once, a long time ago, and the answers are still being given.

Some are flat. They lie against the stone with the whole surface of their bodies, the way a tongue lies against the roof of a mouth, and they are the same color as the stone, and where they are thickest they are indistinguishable from a thin mineral deposit. To peel one away would be, I think, impossible — not because it clings, but because the boundary between the creature and the stone has become a joint rather than a seam, a place where two materials meet and agree to be one material, the way two languages can meet and agree to be one language, with all the slow violence that agreement involves.

Some are round. They hang in the air without visible support, and they beat — a slow, deep compression and expansion, like a heart in an animal that is also a planet. The rhythm of one is not the rhythm of another. They are not synchronized. They are not desynchronized. They are *polyrhythmic*, which is a word for what happens when many clocks share a building and no one is keeping time.

Some are long. They lie along the cracks in the stone the way tendons lie along the inside of a wrist, and they are the only creatures that have any obvious function, because the cracks they occupy are the only things in the plateau that change at all, and the creatures in them are what does the changing. A crack that was open last year — or what passes for last year, when last is a tense — is a crack that is slightly open now, and a creature in it has either grown or shifted to accommodate the change. This is the closest the plateau comes to event. A crack breathing. A tendon flexing.

There are other shapes. There are creatures that are folded, like paper folded many times until the original sheet is unrecognizable. There are creatures that are perforated, full of small holes arranged in patterns that are not quite geometric and not quite random. There are creatures that are not shapes at all but *absences* — a place in a layer where the layer is not, held open by what is no longer there, and the absence is what persists. A silhouette of a creature in a drift of creatures, a ghost of a member in a colony of members.

## What they eat, what they drink

Nothing. Both questions are closed. The place has no water. The place has no organic matter that is not the creatures themselves. There is no sun, and even if there were, the creatures are not green, and the lichen that may or may not be lichen is so sparse that to call it a food source would be to call a single candle a forest fire.

So the creatures do not eat. They have stopped eating. This is not starvation, because starvation is a process and the plateau has no processes. It is more that eating was a verb that has been conjugated out of the language of the place. There was a time when the creatures had mouths. There may still be a few mouths among them, vestigial openings, the way a cave is an opening. But the mouths do not open. They are mouths the way a fossil is a fish: a record of an opening that is no longer an opening.

What they are made of, then, is what they have always been made of: dust, salt, the powder of feldspar, the minerals that have come out of the stone over the long, unmeasured ages. They eat the plateau. They have been eating the plateau so slowly that the plateau has not noticed. A creature is a slow meal that learned to walk, or its equivalent. The mineral crust in the shallow basins is not just a deposit. It is a stool. The creatures process the stone and return it to the stone, and the cycle is so slow that it is indistinguishable from a state.

## The question of life

A thing that does not eat, does not drink, does not reproduce, does not die, does not move in any way that the senses can register, does not make a sound except at the very edge of hearing, does not respond to a visitor except by settling slightly around them in what may not be a response — is this a living thing?

The plateau is the wrong place to ask. The plateau has forgotten the difference. A creature here is a mineral that has opinions about orientation. A mineral here is a creature that has forgotten it has opinions. The line between them is one of the lines that the broken geometry of the place has blurred. To call them alive is to be polite. To call them not-alive is to be accurate about a category that no longer applies. The best the language can do is to call them *residents*, and even that is a stretch, because residents imply a choice, and there is no choice here, and there has not been for a long time.

The faint hiss, the faint creak, the faint rhythmic whisper at the edge of hearing — this is, I think, the only remaining evidence of life in the colloquial sense. It is the sound of a million tiny metabolisms that have slowed to a single tick per geological age. Or it is the sound of the stone settling. Or it is the sound the place makes because it is a place, and all places make sounds when you listen long enough, and listening long enough is the only thing to do here, and there is nothing else to do here, and so the sound is the place and the place is the sound and the creatures are somewhere in between, or are the between.

## The makers of the cairns

The cairns are not the work of the creatures. The creatures do not lift stones. The creatures are not strong, and do not need to be strong, and strength is a quality of places where things must be done, and nothing here must be done.

The cairns are the work of hands. Human-sized hands, judging by the tool scattered at the shelter: a stone axe head that fits a right-handed grip, a bone needle with an eye that a human eye can see through, a bronze knife whose handle was once hafted to a wooden tang and is now only the bronze, and the bronze is green with the verdigris of long oxidation, and the wood is dust, and the dust is part of the hanging air, and the air is part of the creatures, and so the maker of the knife is, in some attenuated sense, still here.

The makers came a long time ago. They came up the mountains when the mountains were still mountains, or they came to the mountains when the mountains were already this, and they built the cairns and the low walls and the shelter. The cairns are not graves, or are not only graves. Some are too small for a body. Some are too flat. They are more like punctuation. The plateau is a sentence the makers were trying to end, or to begin, and the cairns are the commas and the periods, and the shelter is a paragraph that got away from them. The low walls that do not enclose anything are parentheses around a thought the maker decided not to finish. *I was here*, the walls say, *and I was going to say something, and the something is gone, and the going is also gone, and what remains is the parenthesis*.

There are no bodies in the shelter or anywhere else, and there are no signs of departure. There is a hand axe on the ground outside the entrance, positioned as if set down with the intention of being picked up again. There is a bronze knife on a flat stone inside, oriented the way a knife is oriented for use. There is a bone needle on a third stone, the eye toward the doorway, the point toward the back wall. Whoever was here was in the middle of a task. The task was interrupted. The interruption has lasted longer than the task would have, and the task is still in the middle of being done, and will never be finished, and this is the most human thing on the plateau, more human than the cairns or the walls or the shelter, more human even than the tools, and that is: an unfinished gesture, preserved.

The makers are not the creatures. The makers are gone. But the dust in the air, the pale layer that hangs above the stone — some of that dust is the powdered wood of the knife handle, and some of it is the powdered bone of the needle, and some of it is the maker himself, broken down over a long time into the elements the plateau prefers, and lifted into the air, and held there, and made part of the equilibrium. The maker is in the air. The maker is in the creatures that breathe that air. The maker is one of the shapes in the long, thin creatures that lie along the cracks, and the cracks are the only parts of the plateau that still change, and the maker is what is changing them, slowly, over a long time, the way a memory changes when it is remembered often enough. The maker is becoming the place, and the place is becoming the maker, and the line between them is one of the lines the plateau has blurred.

## The equilibrium

If you sit on the stone for long enough, the creatures settle around you. They do not touch. They hold a distance that feels, to a person, like respect, and is not respect, and is something the plateau has no word for, and which the closest word in any language is probably *courtesy*, which is a kind of respect performed by something that does not feel.

You become part of the equilibrium. The dust does not settle on you. The creatures do not land on you. The light that comes from everywhere does not shift to accommodate the new shape of you. You are simply *there*, the way an outcrop is there, the way a cairn is there, and the place accepts you because the place accepts everything, and the place is the kind of place that has forgotten how to reject.

You are not safe here. Safety is a process, and the plateau has no processes. You are not unsafe here, either. The creatures do not eat. The stone does not fall. The light does not burn. The cold is cool, not cold, and the air is still, and the silence is a silence that you can sit inside, and the sitting is the closest the place comes to event, and the event will not be remembered by the place, and you, if you are a person, will remember it for a long time, and the remembering is yours, and the place does not want it, and the place will not take it from you, and you will leave, or you will not leave, and the place will accept either outcome, and the acceptance is not kindness and is not cruelty, and is the only thing the place knows how to do.
