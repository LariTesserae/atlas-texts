**The Organ Plateau**

You hear it before you see it. That is the first law of this country: sound outruns sight.

The way up goes through fog for most of a day — fog that is not cold, that beads on your forearms and the ears of the mules and tastes faintly of crushed leaves. The fog is warm the way breath is warm, because it is breath: the exhale of some vast warm lowland far below, lifted all this way by heat. You climb through it blind, and the sound comes to meet you. At the fogline it is a general rushing, like surf in another room. Higher, it begins to acquire articulation — edges, pitches, a shape. By the time the ground tips and you step out of the cloud onto the plateau, you are standing inside a chord.

Then the world opens. Sky to every horizon. A plain of bare grey stone so old and so planed that in places it reads as flat as a table — the end-state of mountains that stood here once and took a billion years agreeing to lie down. And rising from that plain
