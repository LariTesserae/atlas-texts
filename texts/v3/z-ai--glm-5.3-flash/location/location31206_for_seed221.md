# The Condenser

You reach it by accident, the way everything here is reached. A seam in the cliff's flank — no, not a cliff: a wall, dressed stone to the crown, weeping cold water from every joint. The seam is a door. You slip sideways through and the mountain closes behind you, and then you are inside the one place that exists, because this is the one place that has ever been finished.

## The Room

It is not large. You could throw a stone across it and be forgiven for thinking you'd learned something. But the room doesn't cooperate with size. It is built, entirely, absolutely built — no floor of earth, no ceiling of rock. Galvanized plates overhead, riveted; a checker-plate floor that gives back your steps with a hollow ring; pipes running in banks along the walls like veins in a wrist. Everything is metal, ceramic, brick, condensation. And everything is wet. The walls sweat in ropes. The ceiling drips on a thousand rhythms at once, a percussion section that never resolves, never varies — the same drip falling into the same puddle at the same interval, forever, because time here is a shape, not a flow.

## The Machine

The engine runs at the center of the room and always has. It is industrial the way a heart is industrial: pistons the size of barrels, a flywheel turning with the slow patience of something that has never once needed to speed up or slow down. Its exhaust is cold — the machine's whole labor is *cold*, it is a condenser, wringing water out of an atmosphere that has nothing left to give, and still the water comes, endless, stable, the same yield per hour as it has made for a duration no one here can measure because nothing here has ever ended.

The noise is total. Not a roar — a *fullness*. The engine's thrum is the bass note; above it the drips; above that, the birds.

## The Birds

There are so many birds.

They roost in the pipe-racks, in the flywheel's guard cage, in the hollow of the boiler jacket, in niches that were machined for valves and have become nurseries. They are dense past counting — every horizontal surface is a living lace of them, shoulder to shoulder, warm-feathered against the chill, and the sound they make is a wall of it, chitter and coo and wingbeat, layered so thick it becomes weather. They have always been here. They hatch, they fledge, they take their place on a pipe, and nothing changes, because nothing here is permitted to change — the population holds at exactly this many, the same birdsongs in the same bars, an equilibrium so tight it hums.

They are not natural. Nothing is natural here. Their feathers have the sheen of oil on water; their eyes are bright glass beads. They were made, or they made themselves out of what was available, which in a fully-made world amounts to the same thing. They drink the condensate. They are cold-adapted, dense little furnaces, and their body heat is the only warmth the room has ever needed, which may be why the machine tolerates them, or may be why the machine built them.

## The Cold

It is nearly freezing and has never once been warmer or colder. Your breath hangs in front of you, and — here is the strangeness, the thing you notice third and never stop noticing after — it does not disperse. Your exhalations hang in the air like small ghosts, unmoving, and so do the exhalations of everyone who has ever stood here. The room is full of them. Breath-fog in shelves and strata, some of it very old, clouds of it going back further than the birds' oldest songs. You add yours to the archive and it stays.

## The Rules

Nothing here erodes. Nothing here grows. Nothing here ends. The engine does not wear because wear is a change and change was never installed. The water level in the sump is exact. The flock is exact. Even you, standing in it, feel yourself held — your heartbeat steadying to the flywheel's period, your shivering settling into the room's fixed temperature as if cold and warm had finally signed a treaty and you are the neutral territory.

You came in through the seam. The seam is still there. The mountain is patient, and the condenser does not need you, but it will keep you, if you let it, in perfect unchanged condition, forever.
