You come down to it through stone that has finished remembering water.

The descent is not far — the country above is low, soft-contoured, already halfway to being a basin itself — but down is the only direction the place knows. A dry throat in the rock, a scree slope that was once the stair of a waterfall, and then the air changes before the space does. Cool. Then cool in a way that carries metal and wet skin. Then the sound arrives, which is the true entrance. It comes up the shaft the way water comes up a spring: not as noise arriving but as a medium you are suddenly standing inside. By the time you see anything, you have been hearing it for a long time.

The hollow is a fossil river. Somewhere in the deep past a current ran through here hard enough and long enough to carve a system of chambers the size of a valley, scalloped walls, a floor of pale rock veined with rust. Then the river left. Now the water only visits. What remains is the shape of flow without the flow — erosion in its settled form, an argument between water and stone that has been concluded, with the terms still standing. The dripstone columns that hold up the dark are not built; nothing here is built. They are the stone's own slow work, grown at exactly the pace the floor dusts away, so that the hollow neither grows nor shrinks. It has been in this state longer than anything comparable has been in any state.

And there is light — a little — though you could not say where from at first. It is gray-green, sourceless-feeling, strongest near the ground and near the pools, and it is not light falling on things. It is light leaving them. The walls are filmed with life: bacterial films, red as iron, ochre, sulfur-yellow, growing wherever the stone stays wet, eating the rock itself — iron, sulfide, the mineral body of the hollow — at a pace that makes a glacier look hungry. Nothing here has leaves or stems. Nothing greens. The film is the base of everything, the only agriculture, and it glows faintly as it works.

Which gives the hollow its first law: **wet shines; dry is dark.** The light is not a ceiling. It is a map. Where the stone is damp, the film blooms and glows; where the stone has dried, the film dies back to powder and the wall goes dark. So the hollow's brightness is really its weather report, its water-table drawn in glow. And the light moves. Graze-fronts cross the walls like cloud-shadows run in reverse — herds of small segmented grazers traveling a wall in a loose quilt, eating the glow as they go, so that a patch of darkness migrates slowly across the stone, and behind it, in the eaten wet, the film blooms again and shines. Light here is a renewable. Darkness is a place somebody has just finished lunch.

The water arrives as breath. The hollow breathes on the long lung of the world's pressure: when the pressure overhead drops, the hollow inhales through a hundred hair-thin channels you will never find, drawing in air from the dry country above. That air is cool. The stone is cooler. And when moist air meets stone cooler than its dewpoint, the world sweats. Every surface beads. The ceiling begins to tap. This is the hollow's rain — not falling from anywhere, but condensing out of the walls, the rock sweating in the dark — and it does not last. When the pressure returns, the hollow exhales, the surfaces dry, the films flake to powder, and a fine snow of dust and shed shell settles over everything. Pockets of water in a larger dryness: a pool like a coin of black glass, rimmed with calcite crust; a runnel in a wall-scallop that lives an hour and is gone; a tap under the ceiling that drips once every eleven breaths of yours and has a congregation waiting beneath it, patient as furniture. The floor is mostly dry. The damp is an event, not a state.

Nothing about the place is more overwhelming than its population. There is no surface in the hollow that is not also a crowd. The walls are upholstered in shelled condensers — ridged, combed, ribbed like organ pipes — each one harvesting fog from the air into runnels down its own back, packed so near-touching that the rock seems upholstered in them, clicking softly as they feed. The floor carries its moving carpets of grazers, tens of thousands strong, flowing around stones and columns like slow weather. The pools skate and seethe: skaters walking on the living skin that films the water, pale swimmers below grazing the underside of that same skin. The mid-air is a population in itself — the swarm, clouds of tiny fliers cropping the film-dust suspended in every breath — and their combined wingbeat is the hollow's continuous note, a mid-frequency rush you feel in your back teeth. And the ceiling hangs with drinkers and roost-shells, molting constantly; their castoffs fall as rare shell-snow and are gone within a day, processed by the floor's detritivores, because in a closed system everything is food twice. The dust under your feet is measured in generations.

The predators walk the walls on legs too long, listening. In a place with no light to speak of, sound is geography — every chamber has its signature echo, and the young learn the hollow by its reverb the way other creatures learn a skyline — so a hunt is an acoustic event. You hear it as a hush that travels: a moving shadow made of silence, spreading outward from the wall-walker's path, the swarm tightening into a trembling ball, the individual clicks going sparse. Then the strike — a snap you feel in your chest more than hear — and the noise resumes all around the kill like water closing over a dropped stone. Everything in the hollow dies loudly and is eaten quickly. Nothing is wasted; nothing is exported. The stone feeds the film, the film feeds everything else, the breath brings the water, and the whole transaction stays in the room.

The loudness itself has tides. After each inhalation — each condensation pulse — the hollow swells: everything drinks, everything calls, and for a while the sound is total, a percussion of drips and shell-taps under a rush of wings under a chorus of stridulations and croaks, all doubled and redoubled off wet stone until the noise is less something you listen to than something that happens to your ribs. Then it ebbs, though never to quiet — there is no quiet here, only degrees of roar. And once in a long while, beneath all of it, the bass animal speaks: one low note from somewhere in the upper dark, felt in the sternum rather than heard, and the entire chorus re-tunes itself around that note like an orchestra acknowledging its keel. What it is, nothing in the hollow needs to know. It calls; the hollow adjusts; that is the whole of the contract.

If you stand still in it, you find out quickly that you are a resource. You are warm, dry, smooth, and loud — your heartbeat carries; your breath fogs nothing but is a new sound to everything nearby. Within minutes a film of condensation coats your shoulders. A grazer carpet pauses at your boots and tests them. A shelled condenser climbs your sleeve, anchors, and drinks the dew it harvests off your own skin. A wall-walker paces you at a polite distance, reading you by echo, unable to decide what you are — too large to eat, too warm to ignore. You cannot speak; the roar permits nothing. You learn the tides instead, and after a while you stop hearing noise at all and start hearing what it is: a language of populations, abundance and drought and fear all said at once, in voices that never pause.

Around the hollow, for a long way, the rock is dry — dry for seasons of walking, dry as the shaft you came down. This is the region's one kept pocket of water, hoarded in the dark at the bottom of everything, paid for in sound. And the sound is the last thing that stays with you: you climb away from it, and it does not fade so much as thin, the roar narrowing to a rush, the rush to a hum, until at the mouth of the throat the dry stone swallows it entirely.

Behind you, the hollow goes on breathing. It inhales; the films brighten; the taps begin; the chorus swells and re-tunes. It sings to itself in the dark, the way it has for longer than anything above has had a name for time — and the dark, being dense with listeners, sings back.
