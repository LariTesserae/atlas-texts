You find it by ear.

The plain around it gives you nothing to aim at — low, flat, wet, green from your boots to the horizon, one kind of stillness repeated for miles. A low country hides its hollows well and its sounds badly, and under all that green the water table is close enough to taste. So: a drone. Deep first, overtones after it, arriving from no direction in particular. You walk toward it through thicket that closes behind you the way water closes behind a swimmer, until there is a seam — two walls of braided stem leaning apart, a shadow between them — and the drone touches your sternum, and you go in.

Steps. The seam contains steps: root-steps, grown fat and level, worn glassy. No one cut them. Roots do this when a thousand years of feet would have, if anything had ever come here to put its feet down. The steps are smoother than the rest of the wood, that's all — wear, polished into form. The seam descends, turns once, and opens.

The pocket is a bowl, a hundred paces across, its floor thirty feet below the plain, and the ceiling is a vault of interwoven limb with two or three eyes in the braid where it thins. Through them comes ordinary daylight, slanted and wet-looking, grey or gold depending on the hour, and it falls on pools that hold pieces of plain sky. The light is the least strange thing in the bowl, and its ordinariness is itself a little strange: nothing down here is dark, nothing glows. It looks like a summer morning anywhere. It only behaves like nowhere.

The walls weep. The braid is warm at the height of your hand and cool above, and the warm air rising off the floor lays its water down on the cool weave in beads that gather, slide, and fall the whole height of the wall into grooves the falling itself has worn into the wood — channels smooth as lead pipe, though no pipe was ever made. The air is a degree or two warmer than your skin, thick, faintly sweet: a greenhouse just after rain, spore-sweet, green at the back of the tongue. It always smells like just after rain here. It is always just after rain here.

The floor is root-mat, leather-firm, warm through your soles, faintly springing. The pools sit in shallow saucers, dark and clarified to nothing, so that the bottom — which is also root — looks close enough to touch. Everything you stand on, drink, or breathe in this room began as plant and still is. Even the soil is fiber. Even the water arrives pre-filtered, squeezed clean through living membrane on its way down.

And the machines are few.

That takes adjusting to. From almost any point on the mat you can see most of the floor, and between one engine and the next lie minutes of emptiness — mat, pool, the drone rearranging itself across the gap, arriving before the machine does. The intervals are part of the design. Each clearing is a cell that resonates; the emptiness is what lets the sound be loud. Nothing maintains the clearings, and nothing needs to: the mat simply declines to rise in them, and the grazers keep it declined.

The nearest engine is a piston-stem — a trunk as wide as your spread arms, sheathed in hide like tanned leather, and it throbs. One stroke every eight seconds, a compression you feel through your soles a half-beat before you hear it, drawing pool-water up through channels in its own heartwood and driving it, in pulse, into the wall. Every wall in the bowl weeps because every wall is being pumped through.

Farther on, a cluster of boiler-sacs: swollen bulbs, translucent at the crown, warm as sleeping animals, exhaling through stub vents a slow steam that rises to the cool braid, condenses, and falls back as a fine local drizzle that never reaches the floor. The bowl makes its own weather and recycles its own sky, and the weather it makes is this one.

In the largest clearing, a disc of dense pale wood twice your height turns on a living axle with such patience that you have to watch a full minute to confirm it moves. Cords of contracted fiber drive it from a piston-stem out of sight, gripping the spokes on the downstroke. What the disc does is not immediately its business to explain. It turns. It is the pendulum of the room.

Put all the sounds together and they close into a single drone with no seams: the stroke-throb interleaved with vent-hiss and wall-drip and the low boil of the sacs, loud enough to press on your sternum, and perfectly even. Not frozen — everything moves — but nothing alters. The sound has a loop in it somewhere. You cannot find the length of the loop.

Once, a boiler-sac sighed and the tissue across its flank thinned, and inside you saw a curved plane of grey metal, riveted along its seam. The plant holds it the way flesh holds a splinter; all the work has long since been taken over by grown equivalents, and the iron does nothing, and the plant keeps it anyway. And in the western braid, one rib runs straighter than growth runs, if you step back far enough to see it. Either engines stood here before the weave and were engulfed, adopted, and finally replaced part by part, or the shapes were plant from the beginning. The bowl offers no testimony. It has worn its own history into form.

The fauna are everywhere once you stop counting the big ones. Copper beetles by the hundred work the sacs' vents, eating scale, so the vents never clog. Midges hang in slow columns over every intake pool. A long-legged wader steps through the shallow saucers with filtering toes, clearing the intakes, and never muddies what it steps in. A bird nests inside a steam flue, riding the exhalations, taking the insects that would otherwise colonize the pipework, and its call lands precisely in the gap between two piston-strokes, as if slotted there. In the ceiling a heavy grey browser moves on a fixed circuit, cropping the canopy to a constant height, and beneath it dung-beetles roll their loads to deeper pools that sink and are reabsorbed. Inside the walls, borer-grubs aerate the braid at exactly the density the braid wants; the walls let them. Count the animals today and count them tomorrow: the same numbers, different individuals. The bowl holds its fauna the way a filter holds a current — as a shape, not a set.

There is not a cut stem in the place, not a stacked stone, not a knot of cord. The birds nest in flues that were already hollow. Even the browser's circuit is only a path it prefers.

And the rule underneath all of it, which you understand slowly, standing on the warm mat with the drone in your ribs: nothing is made here and nothing accumulates. Production equals replacement. Whatever the stems pump, the walls drink; whatever the sacs exhale, the ceiling rains back; whatever the fauna eat, the pits reabsorb. Every leaf is younger than a season and the forest that made it is older than memory. Wear and repair match stroke for stroke, so the weathering finished long ago and is now merely maintained, the way a note is held. The bowl does not remember rain — rain on the plain slides off the braid; drought below cannot reach the mat. It has weather the way a lamp has weather.

Your footsteps are the only sounds in the room that don't belong to the loop. After a while they embarrass you, and you climb out. The seam closes; the plain resumes; the drone thins to a suspicion and then to a memory you keep checking against the quiet.

Somewhere behind the green, at exactly the tempo it has always kept, the disc goes on turning. It was mid-turn the whole time you were gone. It will be mid-turn when you come back, if you come back — and you will come back, because low country hides its hollows well, and once you have heard one, silence in a flat green field never quite settles again.
