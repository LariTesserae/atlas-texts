**The Work**

The first thing this place does to you is sound. Before your eyes adjust to anything — before there is anything to adjust to — you hear a note too large to be a note: a long, low exhalation that doesn't come from any throat, rising and falling like the tide of some inner sea. It takes a while to understand that you are hearing the place itself breathing. Ten thousand flues and wind-shafts open onto the galleries at once, and when the air turns over — twice a day, on a schedule older than anyone living — the whole constructed valley inhales or exhales through every one of them together. A bell somewhere counts the phases. People time their work to it. You will learn to feel the change in your ears before you hear the bell at all.

Then the dark. Not the dark of night — the dark of a place that was never given a sky it could open. Light here is architecture. Every window would be a wound in the roof, and the roof is the one thing everyone agrees cannot be wounded casually, so light is rationed like water and owned like land. Ember-shelves line the main galleries at long intervals: shallow stone cups where a slow fire is fed rendered moth-fat and banked to a coal. They throw maybe an arm's reach of orange.
