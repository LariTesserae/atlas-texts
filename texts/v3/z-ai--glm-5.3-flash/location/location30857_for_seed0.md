# The Sough

*(a name that translates, more or less, as listening-ground)*

---

### First, the ear

You do not see this country first. You hear it — from miles out, from the rim bluffs, before you have descended even one of the clay switchbacks. From up on the rim the whole basin is a single instrument lying on its side: a low brown drone that never resolves, stitched with clicks, struck through with booms. The people who live here can stand on the rim on a still night and tell you where everything is. The gardens hiss over there. The river is muttering east of that. Something large is walking near the third sweethole; you can hear the mud accepting its feet.

Sound is the sense of the place, because light failed first.

Above the entire country hangs the Lid — a permanent ceiling of vapor at a few hundred meters, gray-brown, faintly warm to the eye, occasionally flickering from within with slow sheet-flickers that never strike down. No stars have been visible here in living memory, or in any memory. The people's word for *sky* and their word for *ceiling* are the same word. Day is a rumor. There is only the Lid's dim breathing, brighter and duller in long cycles, and the local lights — the cold sparks in the litter, the lanternfruit, the gourd-lamps people carry — punctuating the dark like embers shaken from a coat.

The air is warm the way a held breath is warm. Not hot. Warm, and wet, and it never lets your sweat finish its work.

### The ground is new

This is young land. That is not a metaphor. The clay at the rim bluffs has no strata worth the name; cut into it and you find the consistency of unfinished thought. The elders say the country *is still arriving*, and the ground agrees with them hourly. Mud cones the size of beehives swell on the flats and deflate.
