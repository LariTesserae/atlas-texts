**The Rise**

You hear it before you can be said to have arrived. There is nothing to see — the dark here is not a time of day but a condition, total, unbroken, a ceiling of cloud or something worse that has never once opened in the memory of anything alive — and so the place announces itself as sound: a low, sustained tone, felt in the sternum before the ears admit to it, and over that, spaced like quarry-work, three knocks, a pause, two knocks. The rhythm comes from everywhere. It comes from the ground.

Nothing here is quick, and nothing here is quiet. Hold both of those at once; the whole landscape lives in the gap between them. Millions of small labors, constant motion, and yet the sum of all that bustle, measured over a year, amounts to a skin of new stone about as thick as your finger. The place is loud the way a hive is loud, the way a glacier is loud underneath: sound without consequence, effort without hurry, a city that works in every direction and finishes almost nothing you can watch.

You are standing on a lid. The plain underfoot is sea ice, forty meters of it, glazed and wind-scoured and stitched with pressure cracks, and beneath it the ocean goes on black and slow. This is the whole region: water, water near the surface, water *at* the surface, wearing ice as a garment it never fully buttons. Out from the plain rise low islands of raw basalt — new rock, unweathered, edges sharp as struck flint, no soil anywhere, no rounding, no softening. The geology here is embryonic and you can feel that. Every so often the plain emits a long groan from below, the sound of a young crust still cooling, still setting, a planet under construction. Frost has begun its work on the basalt islets but has barely gotten a grip.

And at the mound's foot, the thaw. Where the great structure meets the plain there is a ring of soft ground — the only soft footing in this world — a marsh-edge of brine pools and slush flats, steaming faintly in the dark, fog ghosting off warm water into air far below freezing. The warmth comes from the mound. The mound exhales. Warm air climbs through flues in its flanks, drags cold air across the ice behind it, and where the two meet, the atmosphere condenses into permanent low fog and everything on the meltline wears a coat of rime.
