# The Drowned Basin

There is no sky here. There has been no sky for longer than anything can remember.

This was built — that much is certain, that much survives even now. But built without tools, without hands that left a seam or a rivet: grown, perhaps, or exhaled into being by something that has since gone entirely. The whole world under this water is made. Every wall, every floor, every arch the currents thread through is constructed substrate, worn so old it has forgotten its purpose. The builders left nothing behind but the architecture itself, and the architecture has eroded past function into pure, patient form.

## The Structure

You are deep in a drowned megastructure — a vast something, purpose unknown, now fully surrendered to the sea. Its scale is regional: you could travel for days through its corridors and causeways and still be inside it. Open halls the size of valleys. Sparse, scattered features — a pillar here, a stairway to nowhere there, a plaza of smooth basalt with nothing in it — separated by long stretches of empty water and empty wall. Whatever this place was meant to hold, it held it sparsely. Or it held everything, once, and the everything has dissolved.

The erosion is the oldest thing about it. Not the violence of collapse — this place is too settled for collapse. It is the erosion of a thing that has reached its final shape: edges rounded to softness, corners melted into curves, stairways become ramps become merely slope. The structure has been worn the way a river stone is worn, and it has *stopped* wearing, settled into a state that will essentially persist. This is its old age, and its old age is stable.

The material itself has no name. Cold to the touch, dark under the dim filter of light from far above — a light that arrives here thin and green-gray, barely enough to make out the difference between wall and open water. Colder still is the water: near-freezing in the deep channels, the cold pooling in the structure's lower halls like a held breath.

## The Turbulence

But the water does not sit still. Nothing here sits still.

Currents tear through the structure in chaotic, unpredictable pulses — down one corridor and then, moments later, violently up it. The architecture channels them: a rounded doorway becomes a jet, a great hall becomes a slow vortex, a narrow shaft becomes a scream of whitewater. The currents reverse without warning. They collide in the open plazas and stand up in slow, wobbling columns of churning water. It is as if the drowned structure itself is restless — or as if something far above, some weather no one down here has ever seen, is breathing through the ruin.

The sound comes from this. Mostly quiet, the deep sea's own hush — but threaded through with the structure's groans, low and tectonic, as stresses redistribute through stone that has been settling for an unimaginable age. A distant boom when two currents meet. The whistle of water forced through a gap too small for it. And underneath everything, constant, almost beneath hearing: a hum, as of a vast hollow thing resonating.

## The Life

No plants. Nothing green has ever grown here; the light is too thin, the water too cold, the substrate too finished to be colonized. What life exists here exists as *fauna*, and it is abundant — a crowded, silent abundance adapted entirely to the currents.

They are everywhere and nowhere: pale, eyeless, soft-bodied things that ride the turbulent flows like feathers in wind. Schools of translucent ribbon-creatures that pour through doorways in the shape of the current, dispersing and reforming as it shifts. Larger shapes — slow, barnacled, venerable — clinging to the worn walls, filter-feeding on whatever drifts down from the bright, unknown water far above. Small darting grazers that pick at the structure itself, the only erosion still happening, a fine biological sanding of the already-smooth. Nothing hunts violently; nothing needs to. The turbulence delivers food to everyone. The creatures are, in their way, the only turbulence made gentle — chaos eaten and made calm.

They seem to know the currents before they come. A plaza of standing water will empty suddenly, and a heartbeat later a vortex rolls through it. The fauna reads this place. They were born to it. Whatever else the builders intended, this is what the structure is now: a machine for making weather in the deep, and the weather feeds its only tenants.

## The Rules of the Place

Nothing here has technology, because nothing here needs it — the currents do all the work, and the structure does all the sheltering. Nothing grows because nothing can. Nothing decays past a certain point because the cold and the ancient settling won't permit it. The world has arrived at a strange equilibrium: fully artificial in substance, fully natural in behavior, endlessly agitated on the surface of the water and utterly changeless underneath it.

If you drifted here — and you could only drift; there is no arriving — you would feel it most in the contrast. The stillness of the walls. The violence of the water. The silence between pulses, and then the long groan of the deep structure flexing, patient, unhurried, waiting for nothing, because there is nothing left to wait for. Only the currents, the pale crowds of the tenant-fauna, and the green-gray dimness at the far end of every corridor, where the water goes on and the built world does too, further than you will ever swim.
