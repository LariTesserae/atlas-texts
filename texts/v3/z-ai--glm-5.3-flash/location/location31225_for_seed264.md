# The Still

The maps call it the Still, and both meanings of the word apply.

You hear it a day before you reach it: a note pitched below hearing, felt in the ribs more than the ears, with a fine white hiss laid over the top of it like wind through wires. The land slopes down toward the sound so gently that you never feel yourself descending. Then a long shallow basin opens out ahead of you — low, warm, pale — and from its floor rise the shapes: rounded backs, ribbed fins, bundles of worn pipe, scattered wide across the plain like the skeletons of whales beached on a dry sea.
