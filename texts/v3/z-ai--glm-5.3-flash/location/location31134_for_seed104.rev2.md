There is no surface.

That is the first thing to understand about this place, and the hardest thing to hold. The water does not end upward. It goes on and on, thinning its light as it rises, until light itself changes character — far above anything that matters to the floor of the world, the darkness stops being an absence and becomes a direction: a pale leaning in the upper water, like the memory of a window. Swim toward it for a year and you reach a stratum the old relief-maps of the current call the Upper Dim. Swim higher still, past the last groves, past the last measured thing, and the light stops coming from anywhere at all. It simply *is* — the ocean shining faintly to itself, luminous with its own presence, the way deep stone is faintly warm. What lies above that glow no one living knows. There is a layer called the Mirror where up and down argue, and things that rise past it do not come back — but neither, in the long keeping of the water, do they appear to have ended.

All of that is far overhead. The world, for the purposes of the world, is lower. The world is the Floor.

⁂

The Floor is a plain the size of a weather system, and it is nearly flat — flat the way a thing is flat when it has had ten million years to lie down. Every mountain here has already eroded, every ridge has already surrendered; the relief of this world was resolved ages ago and the answer was: gently. What remains of the high country are the Knees — a few vast stone stumps, hundreds of miles apart, mountains caught in the act of kneeling, worn to the smooth fluted shape of sleeping animals. They are the only vertical things. Around them the plain runs on and on, silt over ancient rock, the silt so old and so fine that it behaves less like ground than like slow glass. The Floor keeps marks. Where the great grazer passed a thousand years ago there is still a mile-long impression in the sediment, soft-edged now, holding.

Because almost nothing lives here, almost nothing disturbs it. The plain is sparse in beings and dense in record. Every volume of water carries something: a gradient, a held shape, a falling mote. Emptiness is not available on the Floor.

The Knees are still — barely — eroding. Their stone sheds a fine mineral dust that falls for centuries, a snowfall made of mountains. On the Floor this is called stonefall, and it is the bread of the world. Everything green here eats stone that used to be height.

⁂

The water itself is layered, and the layers are the climate.

Closest to the ground, around the roots of the Knees, is the Bottom Warm: blood-warmed water rising from slow seeps in the rock, gentle exhales that pulse on a rhythm of years. You can see warmth down there — a shimmer, like looking through old glass. Here the meadow grows visibly. Not fast; but *while you watch*, which at this scale is the same thing. Time runs slack near the seeps. Hours widen into seasons. Nothing hurries, because near the Knees hurry has no meaning.

Above the warm lies the Still Middle, the thickest layer, and the strangest: the water there *remembers*. Not events — shapes. Where something stood long enough, the water holds its outline, faintly stiffer than the water around it, a pressure-ghost you can drift through like heat-shimmer that will not waver. And above the Still Middle run the Rivers — currents wide as countries, moving so slowly their motion is visible only as a lifetime's difference. When a River changes course — a turning, once in an age — it releases the silt it has carried for millennia: a wall of falling stone-dust a mile across, slow rain made of mountains, taking a century to settle. The Floor watches it the way other worlds watch weather.

Between the layers are the Seams — boundary-fronts where two waters disagree. A migrating Seam is the closest thing this world has to a storm: a moving wall of different clarity, the meadow bowing along its length as it passes, stonefall stirred into slow spirals at its hem. It takes a season to cross a region. It is the loudest thing here, and it makes no sound at all.

⁂

The Long Meadow covers most of the Floor.

It is lush, but it is not jungle — it is *settled* lushness, a lawn grown to the edge of its own possibility and then stopped, the way a river stops at the sea. Khaki-green to grey, stiff in the cool layers, slack in the warm, it grows in patterns inherited from a million years of habit: spiral provinces, wheel-shapes, long straight avenues that look designed and are only old. Nothing has changed its mind here in longer than anyone could count. The meadow is what a garden becomes when the gardener never comes back and the garden agrees to stay.

Among it grow the Slow Trees — coral-boned, stone-wooded, widening a finger's breadth per century. Their groves stand like congregation. Higher up, in the Upper Dim where the pale light barely holds, there are lantern-groves: meadow-plants that keep their own low shining, so that the world's night is lit, faintly, by its own floor.

The meadow eats in three ways: it drinks stonefall, it leans toward the Knees' warm breath the way other plants lean toward sun, and it opens its leaves to the Glow. It is the base of everything. It will also, in the end, be the last thing. The roots of the Long Meadow are quietly eating the Knees — stone into leaf, height into green — and the final form of this world, its one last stable shape, is a warm dim lawn.

⁂

Someone lived here once, before the water finished rising.

The drowning took so long that the leaving was polite — room by room, generation by generation, each household walking away from the meadow as it arrived at the threshold. What remains is scattered and humble, as the drowning left it: a doorway's worth of wall, the sill-line of a bridge, a few standing stones whose grooves have worn to mere texture, one tower dissolved to a knuckle at its base. There is a stair at the foot of the western Knee, fitted stone worn to nubs, climbing up out of the meadow and ending — simply ending — mid-water, pointing at the Mirror. It is the only built thing that aims at the sky, and no one has used it in an age, because there is no one.

The builders are so thoroughly gone that the water does not remember them, and the water forgets almost nothing *shaped*. In the Still Middle you can drift through the rooms of houses built of nothing — silt settled along remembered walls until the walls rebuilt themselves faintly, coral growing along old doorways, the meadow parting around property lines no one drew anymore. The water keeps outlines, not purposes. It remembers that something stood; it was never told why. Once in a great while a shape surfaces from the sediment that is unmistakably made: a bead of sea-green glass worn to opal, a ring of shell too large for any finger, a hinge — with nothing left in the world for it to join.

The only tools left are ambiguous, and they are getting older.

⁂

The animals are four, and each is rare enough to be an event.

In the warm stones around the seeps crowd the coin-shells — small, ridged, blinking open and shut, the only crowdedness on the Floor, huddled at the Knees' breath like worshippers at a fire. In the Seams, only during seam-weather, appear the thread-things: translucent, a hand's length, drifting in the shear like leaves in a doorway's draft, present for a day and then nowhere, origin unknown, and nothing on the Floor minds not knowing. There is one fish — pale, stone-eyed, patient — that keeps to the ruins and eats thread-things when the Seam brings them, and takes a coin-shell otherwise. That is the whole of predation here: one fish, occasional appetite, no pursuit, no blood. The Floor has seen violence so seldom that violence here is not a category, only an event, like a comet.

And there is the Circler.

It is vast beyond easy measure — the length of a slow afternoon's drift, nose to tail — and it swims one enormous circle around the entire Floor, a circuit of decades, rising along the Knees to rest in their warm exhale, grazing the Long Meadow a single mouthful a year. Its old graze-marks outlast civilizations' worth of sediment. Once per circuit it calls — into the Seam, the layer that hears far and hears slow — a note so low it is less a sound than a change in what the water is. The Seam carries it for decades. Somewhere on the Floor, right now, the Circler's previous call is still traveling. It may be the last of its kind; if there were others, their calls would arrive on other schedules, and the Seam stays quiet between. It does not seem to hurry. When it passes over the meadow, the meadow leans after it — not toward it, the way the gardens lean toward warmth, but *after* — the way still water leans toward the moon.

⁂

The rules here are broken. That should be said plainly. But it should also be said plainly that the breaking happened so long ago that the breaks have worn smooth.

Time runs slack near the seeps; the Knees' warmth pools it like a hollow pools water, and things that live there live long even by this world's forgiving count. Headings refract: set out toward one Knee through a Seam and arrive, without any turning you did, at a different one — the layers bend a course the way light bends entering glass, and the Floor forgives it, because everything on the Floor arrives somewhere eventually. There is no surface; there is only thinner and thinner water, and then the Glow, and then the Mirror, and then whatever the Mirror is. Sound dies within a body's length in most of this ocean — open your mouth and the water does not echo; strike a stone and the ring is absorbed at once, by water that has been holding shapes for so long it holds noises too, briefly, and lets them go. And nothing rots here, exactly. The dead sink slowly, growing pale, and the meadow takes them in root by root, and in a hundred years a body is a patch of brighter green. The dead of the Floor are not remembered by anyone. They are remembered by *location*.

The silence, then, is not emptiness. It is fullness with nothing to say — a world so finished with its crises that it has settled into its last motion, which is not an ending but a continuation: stonefall drifting down, the Rivers turning, the Knees breathing their slow warmth, the Seam walking its weather across the lawn, the lantern-groves brightening as the Upper Dim darkens through its long day, and high above it all, wider than the weather of nations, the Circler swimming its patient circle over a meadow that leans after it — warm, layered, green in the dark, nearly silent, still going.
