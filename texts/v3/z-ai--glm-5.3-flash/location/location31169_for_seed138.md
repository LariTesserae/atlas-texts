# The Level

You come to it across a young plain — pale ash-and-clay country, flat as a table left unfinished, its first scrub just beginning: thin grey willow-herb, a scatter of reed at the seeps, red salt-succulents studding the ground like dropped beads. The plain is so new that its heat is still in it. You can feel that through your boots if you stand still. And because the plain is low, and young, and hot, the water it holds sits close beneath the skin of it, and the whole country sweats.

The basin announces itself from a long way off as a ring: a rim of pale spoil, sharp-shouldered, crisp as the day it was stacked, its drag-marks unwashed. Nothing here has had time to round. Nothing here intends to.
