The pocket lies beneath the lowest shoulder of a low hill, where the ground never quite dries and the grass grows in a darker band than anywhere around it. From the outside there is nothing to see: a turf bank, a broken fence post leaning in the wet, a low grey sky that seems to belong to the ground as much as to the air. A hairline seam runs through the stone at the bank's foot, too thin to notice unless the light rakes it. Rainwater goes into that seam and does not come out. Sheep step around a patch of ground they have no reason to avoid, and avoid it.

The seam is new. That is the first thing the stone itself insists on, if you put a hand to it. Its lips are sheared bright, mineral still wet-raw, edges sharp enough to remember being one piece. Grit sits in the crack unsettled, waiting for water that has other plans. Nothing here has had time to weather. The break happened recently — a season ago, or ten; the rock cannot say, and neither, it turns out, can the place.

The crack admits a shoulder where it should admit a finger. This is the first wrongness and the governing one: the seam widens *inward*, as if width were a direction rather than a distance. From outside, the hollow is the size of a barrel — the seam's length tells you so. Inside, the water opens downward and outward into depth that the hill above has no room for. Forty feet to the floor of it, by any eye's guess. The floor is further down than the hill is thick.

Almost everything here is water. Cool water, cellar-cool, holding the temperature of the rock's slow breath; condensation beads on every shear-face and falls once an hour. There is exactly one exception: a knuckle of air trapped under the highest lip of the hollow, a dome a hand-span across, where the stone is dry and faintly warmer and the drips gather for hours before they fall. The dried wings of a moth rest on that dry ledge. No crack large enough for a moth remains. The wings arrived before the closing began, and whatever arrived with them is not in evidence.

Light is nearly absent and does not behave as light. The seam lets down one grey thread that dies within a meter of the surface, and below that, the water carries its own dimmance — a faint lucence with no source, no direction, strongest where the life is densest, and the life is dense everywhere.
