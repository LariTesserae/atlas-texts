## The Quiet

It is the loudest quiet place in the world, and it is a single room, and it sits a few steps below the top of a mountain that is higher than almost anything.

Outside, the air is too thin to carry a shout further than your own ears, and the sky is a fixed iron color that has not moved in any lifetime — no cloud drifts, no bird crosses, nothing up here has anywhere to be. The summit rock is dark, dry, anciently worn into its final shapes. All its weathering was finished long ago; the stone settled into itself the way a voice settles after the last word. Near the highest point, half swallowed by rock-fleece of the same dry gray as the stone, there is a door. Riveted iron, small, a handle whose grip has been worn into a smooth scoop by hands that finished with it generations ago. The wear is old wear. Nothing has touched that handle since it took its shape.

Put your hand in the scoop anyway. It fits. It was made to fit.

Through the door, before anything else, comes the hum — not through your ears but through your boots, your sternum, the roots of your teeth. A note so deep and so constant that hearing it is less like hearing than like standing inside a fact.

—

The room beyond is oval, maybe eighty paces end to end, carved into the summit massif, and it is completely, greedily full. Nothing in it is bare. The floor is a carpet of felt-moss an inch or six deep — a dense nap of dry velvet, moss-colored, moss-soft, but with no dampness in it at all. It has grown over everything: over the pipes that run the walls, over the rails and crates and the base of a gantry, over a stool, over the lower rungs of a ladder. The machines that sit in a sunken well at the room's heart wear the felt like old trees wear ivy, so that the great compressors seem less like machinery than like living mounds. Green-brown, tallow-lit, patient.

Light is scarce and does not move. Small filament lamps burn behind sooted glass with a steady yellow that has not flickered — not once, not ever — because the current feeding them has never varied by a whisker. Seams of the ceiling glow faintly, a green like dawn under a door. Shadows lie on the floor stitched in place, fixed as paint. Stand still and your own shadow is just one more fixture. Deep in the foliage along the walls the light simply gives up, and those recesses are as dark as the inside of a closed hand.

It is cool in here. Coat weather. The chill comes up from below — the mountain breathes through its own roots: a shaft drops hundreds of fathoms down into warmer, denser rock, and the engines draw their air from down there, piped slowly up through cold stone so that it arrives cold and heavy and perfectly dry. Your lips chap in it. Your breath does not fog. There is no water in this room, and never has been. Nothing sweats, nothing condenses, nothing drips. Where a cold line runs near the floor it wears a white bloom, like frost, but it is not frost — it is wax, sublimated out of the plants themselves, a chalk-bloom of their own exhalation. The garden's breath turns to grease on the pipes and greases the machines.

—

Because the garden eats the machines' output, and the machines eat the garden's.

The vegetation here is lush in the way a choir is loud: layered, woven, thorough. Felt-moss everywhere as ground and skin. From the ceiling hang pendant fronds, dry as fleece, flexible as leather, which do not stir because there is no wind — the air only moves in slow, fixed circulation, in and down the shaft, and even that is less a motion than a posture. Along the walls stand the reeds: hollow jointed stalks, each one cut to a length and no longer, each one humming. Every reed is a pipe. Every pipe is in tune. Whoever tended this garden once pruned each stalk to its pitch, and now the stalks maintain themselves — each grows at its tip and slims away at its tip at exactly the same rate, so that every reed is forever exactly its finished length. The garden prunes itself. The garden tunes itself.

Their sap is not sap; it is grease, clear and warm-smelling. Their breath is not breath; it is pressure. What they feed on is the sound. The drone passes through their hollow tissues and rounds them, fattens them, the way wind fattens a sail, and in return they exhale the wax-vapor that lubes the engines and the spore-dust that re-felts the floor. The loop is closed. Nothing enters, nothing leaves — except the note, which goes nowhere and comes from nowhere, and the faint spent air, which sinks back down the shaft to be drawn up again. The place has been running this circuit so long that it has polished its own parts smooth. That is what the erosion here is: not damage but completion. Every surface you could ever touch has already been touched into its final form.

—

The loudness, when you walk into the middle of the room, is enormous — but it is not noise. It is a chord. A deep bednote you feel in your ribs; above it the reeds' braided voices; under everything the constant whisper of a hundred relief-vents, each leaking one fixed breath. The chord has no desire to resolve. It has no desires at all. Your ears try for a minute to parse it and then give up gracefully, and after that you stop hearing it as sound and start wearing it, the way you wear pressure, the way you wear altitude.

Try an experiment. Bend one of the hanging fronds. Hold it. Let go.

It springs back to the hundredth of its position. There is no sag, no memory of your hand, no tremor — or rather there is a tremor, exactly enough tremor to cancel itself and stop. Set your boot into the felt: the print closes behind your heel like the wake of a fish. Lean your warmth against the wall for a while and when you step away the wall is already at its set temperature, your heat refunded to you with interest. Nothing here accumulates. Nothing here is spent. All the change this room will ever undergo was spent long ago — spent earning these shapes, this wear, this tuning — and what is left now is the note. Time in this room is not flowing. It is stored.

—

Evidence of builders is scattered thin and half-absorbed: the door, the gantry, the ladder going down into the machine-well, a stencil on the wall — one word, HOLD, in flaked enamel paint. A coat hangs on a hook by the gauges, stiff with white mineral bloom, sleeves still holding the shape of arms that left them. On a shelf sits a tin mug in a perfect collar of felt that grew up around it and then, politely, no further.

The gauges are brass circles in a bank along one wall, and every needle in every gauge rests exactly on its mark. They are not stopped. They are arrived. Pressure, frequency, temperature — all at set-point, all the time, a wall of instruments whose whole long working life consists of one single reading.

Beside the gauges lies the logbook, open, a pencil resting in the gutter of the page. The filled pages are all the same hand, all the same sentence, dated day after day after day — a record not of events but of the absence of events. The last entry ends mid-word. The pencil lies where it was set down. That unfinished word is the newest thing in the room, and it is older than anything you have ever touched, and it was never completed, because completion would have been one more change and the room had, by then, spent its allowance.

—

And the animals — there are none. In all this green there is nothing that flees, nothing that burrows, nothing that knocks itself against the lamps. No moth has interrupted these filaments in their whole unbroken burning. No wings. No droppings in the felt, no tunnels, no bones. The lushness is entire and unchewed, an abundance nobody eats — or rather, an abundance with one eater only, itself, feeding on the room's one held meal, the chord, which the engines cook and the garden eats and the vents breathe down the shaft and the engines cook again.

Climb back out, when you go. The scoop in the door handle fits your hand on the way through too. Behind you the note does not fade, because fading is a change, and there is none. The mountain closes over the room the way a bell's bronze closes over its struck tone — the whole summit, you realize, is just the container the note keeps itself in.
