# The Visit

The power arrives the way the fog does: without announcement, without boundary, an hour of gray climbing the rim until the whole plateau is softly indoors. It does not descend in fire. It does not part the clouds. It simply rises, the way the water rises, because the Toll has taught even its visitors the manners of the place.

It comes, first of all, to *listen*.

This matters, and it matters that it comes first. A lesser benevolence would have arrived with a plan — would have seen the rust and sulfur palette and thought *barren*, seen the Sunken Quarter and thought *wound to close*, seen the flies and thought *grim*, and begun, with the best intentions, to tidy. The Toll has been tidied before, in greedier centuries, by powers with drills and deeds and theories about Keepers, and the springs sulked for a generation. The wise power has read the covenant at the springheads, all six words of it, and understands that a place which keeps house does not wish to be renovated.

So it listens. It listens the way you would put a hand flat on warm rock: with its ribs.

And what it hears is a thing in balance. That is the discovery, and it is not a small one. The Toll is not suffering. Nothing here is starving, or overrunning, or grieving in a way that needs intervention. The wethers die walking and are answered within the hour. The moths get their dark nights. The city rations itself on the Fall and festivals on the Rise, and even its sins — the drilled quarter, the driven-off Keepers of the bad years — were met, amended, and carved into stone so that no one would forget. This is a rare thing in the universe: a civilization that found its own lesson, drowned a district to keep it, and made the drowning a school. The power walks the terraces disguised as weather, as a warm draft, as condensation on a window, and everywhere it goes it finds the same astonishing poverty of cruelty. Slowness has right-of-way. The wasps have deeds. The dead are given back to the crop. It has visited ten thousand worlds where its help was needed like a surgeon. Here it would be needed like a guest.

Almost.

Because there is one wrong note in the roll call, and the power hears it on its first night, standing at the rim of the Sunken Quarter with the Keepers calling through two miles of stone.

The deep note comes, and the drowned square answers — a half-beat late, thin, white-blind, seething — and no Keeper ever once answers it back.

The power stays with this a long time. Three hundred years of it, if you count the way the Toll counts, though the power counts differently. It understands what the drowned voice is: not a monster, not a punishment, but an orphaned frequency. When the quarter pierced the floor, something answered from beneath — something that had never been addressed before, something that had perhaps been calling into the dark since before there was a mountain above it — and the Keepers, who answer everything, answer nothing, because the covenant is not only a rule for the city. It is a rule among *themselves*. Whatever speaks from under the floor is not to be spoken with. The white blind things are sieve-mouths grown long and pale and wrong because they are straining at a frequency they were not built for. The drowned square is not a wound in the plateau. It is an ear that was cut open, and what it hears does not stop.

Here the power must decide, and it decides this:

It will not answer the drowned voice either. To answer would be to make contact where the whole mountain, human and Keeper alike, has in its slow unanimous wisdom chosen silence — and silence, maintained by an entire ecosystem for three centuries, is not cowardice. It is a covenant of its own. The power is old enough to know that some doors are closed from the outside *for the sake of what is behind them*, and that benevolence which overrides a unanimous, patient, three-hundred-year refusal is not benevolence but conquest with softer hands.

But it can do what no one on the Toll can do, because they cannot hear far enough down.

It goes under. Not through the floor — never through the floor — but *around*, the way the water goes around, the way the tide reaches what the wells cannot. And it finds, far beneath the drowned square, at the root of the springs, the source of the toll.

And it finds that the Keepers are not animals at all, in any sense the census would use. They are the surfacing of something. The Toll's great warm heart — the water table itself, the slow two-year breath, the whole patient engine — has a voice, and the Keepers are how the voice puts on a body and stands in the weather. They are not calling *to one another across the miles*. They are calling *down and through*, one conversation, the mountain talking to itself through mouths it grew for the purpose. The toll is the roll call because the toll is the *self*, taking attendance.

Which means the drowned voice — the half-beat-late answer from beneath the Sunken Quarter — is the self too. A splintered note. A part of the mountain that was addressed by the drill and has been answering ever since, into a silence, growing white and blind and long, because nothing that was meant to hear it will hear it.

This the power can mend without breaking any covenant, and it does so with the only tool fitting the place: it *adds itself to the chorus*.

Not with words. Not with a miracle visible from the terraces. On the night the tide turns — the night the whole plateau answers anyway, drips and wheels and herds shifting in their sleep, the instrument changing key — the power puts itself into the note. It answers the drowned square as a Keeper answers: through the floor, through the stone, a deep half-beat-early voice that says only what the roll call says, which is *present, present, you are counted, you are one of us.* And it teaches the note to the Keepers by being heard by them once — one clean answering tone, from below, in the register of the toll itself — so that they understand, in their plated slow certainty, that the drowned voice is not a stranger under the floor. It is a springhead. The westernmost one. Forgotten, but on the circuit.

After that, the mountain finishes the mending itself, because that is what the Toll does: it routes around nothing that is one of its own.

In the years that follow, a Keeper walks. It takes days, and the herds pause, and the wheels idle, and the whole city leans into doorways to watch the rarest event on the plateau — a Keeper deciding to change springheads. It walks west. It stops at the rim of the Sunken Quarter, at the edge of the warm drowned lake, and it stands there, and does not move again.

And where it stands, the water runs clean and the shag grows true — out onto the drowned rooftops first, in a thin black tide, then down the drowned walls, then across the flooded square itself, the first felt the white blind things have ever had under them that was grown for them. The white things shorten, generation by generation. Their straining frequency softens, because someone is on the other end of it now, steady, at springhead distance, answering half a beat *early* instead of the deep calls half a beat late — closing the gap, call by call, tide by tide, the way everything on the Toll closes every gap.

The drowned square is not drained. The lesson stays; the city keeps its covenant carved; the houses stay under the lake as a warning about drills forever. But a new line appears in the covenant at the westernmost springhead, added by the notaries in the city's careful hand, because the city noticed that where the new Keeper stands, the water that comes up tastes like the water from everywhere else, and the shag there is violet on its undersides for the first time in three centuries:

*Leave the Keepers their ground — all of it.*

The power itself, having done this, does very little else, on purpose. It lingers a while as weather. It stands on the rim at dusk, as everything does eventually, and hears the roll call go around — Keeper to Keeper to Keeper, out to the drowned west and back, no note unanswered now — and feels the whole warm mechanism tick through its ribs, the water rising, always rising, patient as anything.

Then it leaves the way fog leaves, which is to say it does not leave; it lifts, and thins, and becomes a wetness on sleeves, and the shag drinks it with everything else.

One guest, one gift, one chorus joined. Nothing here is fast. Neither was the visit.
