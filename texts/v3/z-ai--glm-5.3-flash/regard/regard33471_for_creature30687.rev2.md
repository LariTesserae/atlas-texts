Ask what the power did on the Wet Crown, and the honest first answer is: almost nothing. But that is a failure of patience, not of record. The count changes depending on how long you are willing to sit still, at what hour, and how much of your size you can put down at the wet line before you start the climb.

It came because of the note. That is the whole of the selection process, as far as anyone can reconstruct it. Powers hear the way certain children half-turn: something in them arrives ahead of the sound. Far out past the straw plain, past the distances no map has ever troubled itself with, the great western note came back one afternoon a little flat — and the power, which had been minding a hundred flourishing eternities, turned its head. It did not call this being chosen. Summoned is not the word either. The lowlanders would say it heard a grandmother call from another room, and went, and that is as close as words get.

Its first approaches failed, and the failures were the teaching. It came the way it was used to coming: as an understanding, settling over the summit country the way an interpretation settles over a text — complete, immediate, true. The mountain breathed. The understanding dried in the moving air like everything else. It came as light, and was received as weather, politely, without consequence. It came as rain, and learned what the drovers could have told it: rain means nothing here. Rain is just the sky doing, crudely and in public, what the Grandmother does all night with her mouth shut.

It took the power a day and the length of the ring path to learn the local currency. Hands. The whole economy of the mountain runs through them. So it did the local thing: it grew itself a body, small, with dry lips and one bad knee, and packed what the tuners pack — a reed knife, a coil of twine, a lump of wax, a pouch of pebbles. It had carried worlds. Now it carried wax.

The first day it did nothing, which is the local first lesson and a harder one for a power than anyone expects. It sat where the butterflies come at dawn, and they came, and stood on the weep-line beside its folded hand, drinking, wings closed, and did not move when it moved — which is the lowlands' test of a place where nothing means harm, and it passed, and was more moved by that than by any shrine ever raised to it. That morning it heard the duet, bird and pipe, and its new hands itched with capability, and it folded them and added itself to the audience. No one composed the duet. No one conducts it. The power had spent eternities conducting. Becoming audience was the first thing it ever gave her.

Then the ledger, because it was still a power, and it saw the whole mountain at once, the way you see a sentence you have read before. Within an hour of the green hem it had drafted everything. Over the days of sitting it laid the drafts down one at a time, and the laying-down was the deed.

It saw the thousand small staircases and the one straight channel that could replace them, and it laid the chisel down. The water would arrive sooner. Sooner is not the point. The detours are the circulatory system and the choir at once; the friction is where the note lives. A straight line is the shortest distance between two points, and the mountain is not going to either of them.

It saw the climbing gardens and drafted terraces, anchors, a retaining curve at every dying downhill margin, and it laid the architect down. Nothing built remains. Its help would have been the first fossil on the mountain — the one permanent thing, and permanence is the one thing she is not asking to be. Besides: the springs climb the way old women climb out of low chairs, using the arms of it, and a ramp is not the same as arms.

It saw the loneliness question — one bird, one browser, a handful of pale things in all that warm wet room — and drafted companions, and laid the shepherd down. Fewness is a material here, like emptiness, like mist; the room is furnished sparsely and well, and the power had very nearly carried in a chair. It could have settled the question of the browser, too, by holding still for a single night, longer than anything alive can hold still. It chose to leave the census unfinished. Some counts are kept by not being finished.

It saw the succession of tuners — the half-turning children, the craft passed mouth to mouth, the whole lineage one bad fever from silence — and drafted a guarantee, an unbroken chain of custody reaching to her last day, and it laid the guarantor down last but one. A guarantee is a chain, and chains are built.

And the last thing it laid down it laid down on the final evening, without ceremony: the wish to be remembered. Every power carries it. It looked at the summit country — no shelter, no wall, no cairn older than a season, the wax already washing, the wedges loosening on schedule — and understood that the Wet Crown has no word for worship. Only for visiting. And only the one word, which is also the word for weather. It had been worshiped before. It noticed, with something like relief, that here it would merely have been weather, and then not even that.

So it asked — in the language where the question arrives pre-answered — *what do you need*. The answer was not a statement. On this mountain, answers to that question come shaped like chores.

It found the chore on the ring path: a smooth warm stone, worn at exactly the height of a shoulder, directly above the silted channel. If you have walked the ring you know the stones. It read the stone for the yes that it was. For this was the fever year, which the lowlands keep no story of, which is the correct amount of story: the tuners could not come, the silt had built, the pipe had gone flat, and the bird had gone thin. The power knelt in seep-mud and cleared the channel with its fingers, badly, then adequately, and felt — for the first time in its long career — the itch of urgency, and watched the mountain decline to catch it. The craft is by ear and ear-knowledge passes mouth to mouth, and it had no mouth anyone there trusted yet, so the mountain taught it the way she teaches everything: by making the wrong note uncomfortable to stand near. And on the second morning the grey bird came down out of its grove and sang the pitch, a fifth above, patient as water, until the wedge seated true. The power sealed it with wax that will wash. When the great pipe finally filled, over its slow hours, and sounded, and the note rolled out over the plain round and enormous, someone in a lowland kitchen looked up and said the Grandmother cleared her throat — and they were right, and also, for one afternoon in all of history, incomplete. That is as much as any miracle gets to be, and more than most.

It was permitted one liberty, on the last dawn, at the inhaling hour, and it is the only thing the power will still speak of, if you can get it to speak at all. It stood at the mouth of a flue in the dark before dawn, and the mountain inhaled, and the power went down with the mist — a few feet, no farther — into the flooded country underneath, where the pale things move the way blood moves, and the pale things passed it in the dark without haste, exactly as unimpressed as she had been, and one of them touched it, or did not touch it, at the place a shoulder is worn into a stone. Then the exhale came, and it came back up into ordinary moonlight, a citizen for one breath, dripping.

There was one more thing, and it is the largest, and it is a nothing. Powers can see down the length of a thing; it is nearly all they do. The springs are climbing. Out of old habit, one afternoon, the power looked down the length of her climb — toward what the Grandmother is pulling herself up through her own weather to become, what the wet line is rising toward, what the pale things will find in the light they are a hundred years from — and then it shut that eye, deliberately, and kept it shut. A known destination is a fence around a thing that climbs without witnesses. To have read her ending would have been to build it into her, and nothing built remains — not even a foreknown conclusion, which is the most permanent structure there is. So it spent its last days on the summit practicing ignorance. It found that not knowing has a taste. The taste is the inside of a flour jar kept in a warm kitchen.

What it gave, it gave in the only currencies the law accepts.

A pitch. The wedge it seated changed the short pipe on the southern lip by a degree no one can name and everyone can hear: the note sounds, this century, a shade more contented. Someday the wedge will loosen, as wedges do, and a child with a reed knife will reseat it without ever knowing whose hand shaped the first one. The power's signature is in the Grandmother's throat, already going slowly flat, and of everything it has ever made, it says this is the piece it is proudest of — the one that is being corrected.

A hum. On the way down it stopped in a lowland kitchen as a dusty stranger, drank a cup of something it did not need, and half to itself hummed the true pitch of the great western pipe. A child at the table took it in the way children take everything — permanently, and without receipt. Forty years on, that child will turn their head half a second before the note arrives, and will be spoken of gently, and one spring will walk up the mountain with a reed knife. The power had drafted the guarantee and laid it down; what it did instead was lawful. It put the note in circulation. A book would have been a wall. A hum is a seep.

A cloud. On the last afternoon it asked leave for a liberty, and the leave came in the usual coin — a warm stone, shoulder-high, at the edge of the wet line. It took one of her exhales, the day's thickening fog, carried it a day's walk out over the plain, farther than the wreath ever goes, and let it go as rain on drovers' country, once. The drovers said the Grandmother reached out her hand. The power did not correct them. Correction is a kind of keeping, and besides: they were nearly right.

And her side of it — what did the being who lives there do about being attended to by a power? The honest census: what she does about everything. She folded it in. There is a seep on the southern lip, one terrace above the weep-line where the botany of the place says seeps belong, that opened the spring the power sat beside; the garden below it grows a terrace higher than gardens have any business growing; the butterflies drink there in fair weather, and the hollow behind it is warm after dark, warmer than it should be. Nothing you could swear to. Everything you could sit with. And no grave was ever needed, and none was looked for, because on that mountain no grave has ever been found, and some tuners make a last visit carrying nothing at all, and some springs — higher up, in the years that follow — open. Whether the power served the mountain or the mountain took the power as an apprentice is a question neither party has shown the slightest interest in. Both names are true.

Benevolent, it had been called for so long the word had gone smooth in its mouth. The mountain made it sharp again by making it small. Benevolence, she taught it by sitting there being warm, is not a substance you bring. It is a rate. It is how slowly you are willing to be kind. And wisdom is the same word with the urgency taken out. Afterward — and here the account leaves the drovers' country and enters a register no one owns — it performed its mercies differently. Not the way weather performs mercy, all at once and everywhere, but hers: in a thousand small staircases. It still grants the loud kindness, when the loud kindness is the true one; but it asks first whether the loudness is for the one who needs it or the one who gives it, and that question is the only thing it carried home. Which is lawful. A question is the one artifact the mountain permits anyone: it wedges itself in, it changes the pitch of you, and it works loose over years. The power went home wedged.

The full count of the visit, as best it can be kept: one power, arrived as fog. One visit, the length of the work. One channel cleared, by hand. One wedge seated, long since gone soft, on schedule. One bird consulted, or consulting. One privilege granted at the inhaling hour, never repeated, never spoken of by either party. One note carried down in a hum and planted in a kitchen. One cloud spent on the plain, credited to her. One eye closed, and kept closed. Everything else it did by not doing it, and that was most of it. It kept nothing. Nothing built remains — not even, that year, the visitor. The drovers, asked whether anything happened up there once, say no, and go back to setting their afternoons by the note.

Late in an afternoon, the great pipe on the western face fills over its slow hours, and sounds: one deep round note, rolling out over the plain. In a lowland kitchen a child looks up half a second before the sound arrives. On the summit the grey bird answers, a fifth above, the way it has always been answered, because nothing conducted it and nothing ever will. The butterflies settle into the warm hollows for the night. Far down in the flooded dark, in a channel no one has ever seen, something pale and translucent moves a few inches uphill, toward the light it will reach in a hundred years. And the Grandmother goes on weeping gently from her crown, her household about her, her newest wedge already beginning its long loosening, entirely unbothered by having been attended to — because she saw the power too, of course. She saw it first. And she has already folded it into the long slow morning she is in the middle of, where, by the only count that matters on the Wet Crown, it ranks exactly as it wished: a visit. A pair of hands. A family call.
