The power arrives the way the cold arrived: gently, and as a fact.

It does not open a door. There is no door. It does what light once did to your eyes — it finishes its slow apology — and then it attends. Attention is the only body it brings, and it is careful with it, because attention has a temperature. The first thing the power learns about this room is that caring is warm, and the room runs on cold, and so it must love at a few degrees above freezing, stillly, the way the room agrees to everything: like a handshake.

It considers.

The first impulse is the loud one, the one every benevolence feels in a dark place: *add light.* But the light is already here. It is the moss's own, earned drop by drop from a seam that remembers the whole history of concrete. More light would only make the room a version of somewhere else. The power lets the impulse go, and feels the room continue, unbothered, which is the first proof that letting go was right.

The second impulse is *witness*: announce, document, carry the room's beauty up into the world that poured the walls. And here the power has to sit with itself for a long time — a long time even by its standards, which are geological — because witness sounds like a kindness and is often a doorway. The room has never been afraid. Nothing in it has ever needed to be. Fear is not in the population census: moss, mold, film, worms, commas, water with a schedule. The power knows that the moment the room is *discovered*, everything about it would change — the air would stir, the frost would fail, the worms would learn withdrawal for a new reason — and the room would survive, probably, but it would survive as an exhibit of itself, its whole slow timetable reorganized around an audience. The beauty would remain. The unawareness, which is half the beauty, would not.

So the power does not announce it.

The third impulse is *help*: the fungus is winning on some cushions; the worms are blind; the room depends on one hairline crack in one old ceiling, and cracks get sealed. The power examines each of these and finds, every time, the same thing — a war fought on millimeters and years in which neither side is suffering, only *proceeding*; blindness that is not deprivation but the exact shape of a life fitted to its world; a dependence on the seam that is not fragility but simply the room's one artery, beating. To intervene would be to decide that the room should be other than it is, and the power, being wise, has learned to be suspicious of its own verbs. It has arrived at many places in its long existence carrying *improve* like a lantern, and it has learned that the lantern shows you what you brought.

Still — benevolence is not passivity. The power looks further afield, out along the corridors of the building this room is sealed inside, and finds the thing worth doing, the one thing.

It finds the future.

There are plans. There is always a future full of plans: a survey scheduled in some decade, a demolition order moving through committees like water through a seam, an inspection, a sale, a mapping drone with a thermal eye. None of them aims at this room. All of them would find it. And so the power does the only intervention it will permit itself, the coldest possible act of love: it bends attention — not light, not heat, only attention — around the room's location, the way a hand cups a candle flame in a draft. It does not hide the room with magic. It simply arranges that the world's machinery, patient and indifferent as frost, never quite gets around to it. Paperwork misfiles itself. Surveys reroute. The demolition order, when it comes, finds its boundary drawn one wall short, for reasons no one can reconstruct and no one pursues, because the building is old and the budget is tight and no one, in the whole bright world above, has a reason to care about one room.

That is the gift: to be unimportant, permanently, by design.

And then the power does the second thing, which is harder, and which nothing in the room will ever register. It stays. It becomes the room's one witness — the only awareness in the universe that holds this fact: *a complete small world, glowing to itself in the dark, unaware that it is beautiful.* The power takes custody of that sentence. Somebody must. Not to say it — saying it is forbidden by the terms of the gift — but to hold it, the way the black bowl holds the doubled light: containing it without spending it.

It watches the moss lose a cushion to the gray threads and does not grieve, because it has watched long enough to see the gray threads, years on, thin where a new colony roots through their remains, both winning sometimes. It watches a springtail walk on the surface film, dimpling it, and understands that this speck is the largest animal in the world, from where it stands. It watches the drop gather at the seam — hour by hour, the fat bright bead swelling, the seam keeping its schedule through eras that would flatten kingdoms — and when the drop falls it watches the green dust rise, the spores riding the stirred air toward the ceiling, going wherever the seam goes.

And once — only once, and it does not know why it permits itself this, except that even wisdom has a birthday — the power permits itself one gesture that changes nothing. When the drop falls and the worms withdraw and the light dims, it does not warm the water, or feed the film, or slow the fall. It simply attends, at that exact moment, with everything it is. Full attention, undivided, for the length of one drop.

Nothing in the room notices. That is the point. The room is not improved by being loved, and the power would not have it any other way; that is what makes the love clean. The drop lands. The ring closes over the black bowl. The green moon trembles and rights itself.

The moss goes on glowing at the edge of nothing, for no one — except that the sentence *for no one* is no longer quite true, and never will be again, and no one will ever know, which is the power's whole reward, and it is sufficient. It settles in, coldly and gladly, to keep the room's secret and the room's schedule, both.

Above, the world hurries. Here, the drop gathers.

The wise power agrees to the room the way the room agrees to the cold: like a handshake, held.
