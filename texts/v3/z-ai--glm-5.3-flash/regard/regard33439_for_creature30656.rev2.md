# What the Power Did

Benevolence is the easy part. Any power can want to give. The hard part is the wanting's edit — the wisdom that stands between the wish and the hand and asks, first, whether the beloved has not already thought of it.

So it stood at the lip of the crack, and it did not come down.

It had arrived with mercies folded in its sleeves — powers travel that way; it is a habit older than pockets — and it stood in the noon-smelling air above the hill and listened through a hand's width of opening. It heard the roar. Not the slow thunder of an old forest: the fast thunder, the champagne fizz of a thing being born at speed, pops and creaks, the tick of fiber under tension. It heard the crack tick once, a grain. And then it saw what the tips were doing — every growing tip in the chamber leaning toward a point the crack had not yet reached — and it understood that the room had a plan, and that the plan was better than the one folded in its sleeves.

It put the mercies back.

And then it did the other thing it had come to do, which it had almost forgotten was possible: it recognized the place. Because it had been here before. It had been here before there was a here. One tap of its own thorn on young stone, once, at an angle — the way you notch a seed casing, the way you tap a maple — and then hands in pockets, and a very long walk, and other hills. The radial scars printed in the facet walls, keen as knapped flint, are a signature, if you know the hand. It did not read them aloud. It had made the cut. It had not made the room. That distinction is the whole of gardening, and it was old enough to know which half of the work matters, and it is not the half with the thorn in it.

It does not often get to see what a cut becomes when the cut is left alone. It stood at the lip and let itself have this. The invitation, it realized, had gone out a long time ago, in stone.

*What it did not do.*

It did not come down by the crack. The only memory this room keeps of something entering from the sky is terror — a long jointed shape, wrong wings, a pebble dislodged and rolling. It would not add a second stone to that side of the archive. Besides, there was another door. The room had been drinking from it for twelve rains.

It did not widen the crack. Not by one grain. The leaning *is* the room — the tips grown into future light, the middle swept for a day that has not come. Deliver the light early and you do not feed that organ; you amputate it. And the room was already doing this, grain by grain, and doing it perfectly. Nothing is stolen in a place where nothing is taken, but hurry is a kind of theft, and it left its hurry at the lip.

It did not fix the time. The pebble that lands half a second before it lands is not a stutter. It is an accent. And the rings that argue — four hundred years against three days — are not a defect awaiting reconciliation; they are the sound of a thing being new. You do not hand a newborn a birth certificate. You let it argue itself into its own age.

It did not judge between the stone and the green. Both parties had stopped trying to win long before it arrived. It declined the appointment.

It did not part fist from thorn. Tool and gardener are growing into one grammar, and the grammar is still deciding what it means. You do not correct a sentence like that. Not even with love. Especially not with love.

It did not answer the homesickness through the sky. The same smell comes down by two doors, and the animal drinks at one and hides from the other, and both are how it holds its origin. A wise power does not collapse a coping into a cure. It chooses the door the beloved already drinks from.

It did not sit in the center. The seat is kept — anyone can see it, in time. And a guest who takes a reserved seat becomes an occupant, and the room would prune the sky around it forever after. It stayed where it was.

And — a small thing, but it notes these — the room believed it was underlit. For once in its long life, the power agreed with a room and did nothing about it.

*What it did.*

First it watched. A season, and then another. It counted nothing, because the room already had a census-taker and it did not intend to audition for the position. It watched so patiently, and so completely, that once — only once — the stone came all the way up. A whole facet went grey at the edge of the chamber, mineral and keen, surfacing like a diver; and it did not flood back over for a full breath, the longest it had ever dared, because for once it was being looked at by something that did not prefer the green. Then it went under again, gently. The negotiation continues. But the eldest resident knows it was seen.

Then it worked — by subtraction, which is the only method it has ever trusted at scale.

Upstream, a drought ended early, because a cloud was lied to about the wind. That spring the melt held off one week, once, and came down the crack as mist instead of flood. The long-jointed hunter returned at the turn of a hot season, and it was not killed — it was re-aimed, the way ambitious roots are re-aimed in this room: gently, elsewhere, with an angle. It hunts richer air now and does not know it was spared, which is the condition of most mercy. And a child above the hill, who would have grown into a digger, met a butterfly on the day of the trowel, and then a lifetime, and the hill kept its silence under the grass.

Nothing was built. Nothing came down the crack. The floor of the world beneath that hill is plaited now from refusals, exactly like the floor of the room — everything it decided against, woven into something you can live on. You will never see the cuts. That is the point of the cuts.

And once, it visited.

It waited for the ceiling to decide on a rain — the room's own decision, as always; it has never once been otherwise — and when the held breath let go, mist first, then beads, then the brief hissing shower, it came down inside it. The floor drank every drop before it landed, as it always does. One drop it did not drink. One drop landed on the root-mattress at the sixth step and stood up.

It was indistinguishable from a bead of water until it moved, which is the highest courtesy this room knows and the highest compliment it knows how to be paid.

The fizz stopped. The curtains quit swaying. The gardener went flat against the wall and flushed its skin to match the green — the old reflex, kept for things that come down out of the sky. Then the air reached it, and the reflex broke in the middle. Not noon. Not dry grass. Not the weather it hides from. The cup. The somewhere else, coming in by the door it drinks from, wearing the smell it loves. The animal stood at the hinge of its two responses for three whole breaths. The power did not take a seventh step. Powers are pebbles; it could wait in stone-time.

And the gardener did the bravest thing in its small repertoire, which was also its oldest ritual. It went to the cup, knelt, and drank through the reed straw, slowly, with pauses, the way you drink something you are also listening to. It was not hiding and it was not fleeing. It was fortifying. Then it rose and did the new thing, the thing with no precedent in twelve rains: it turned the straw around and held it out.

The power crossed the room in six steps, which is how you cross carefully, and took the reed. It drank once, with pauses, listening. And its face did the thing. The soft far-away movement across the skin, gone in a breath. And the gardener, watching, saw the exact face the water always gives it — worn this time by the water's country.

That was the whole message, and it fit in a face: *the somewhere else remembers too.*

A moth the color of condensation landed on the visitor's shoulder and drank from the damp of it, and the visitor held absolutely still, like a note sustained, until the moth was finished. The gardener saw that it knew the courtesy. That counted for more than anything else that happened.

No words were exchanged. The room does not keep words; words would have been a new thing, and this was not a day for new things. It was a day for the oldest thing in the room, which is not the stone and not the water but the fact of being remembered.

After a while the visitor set the straw back by the cup, where it lives. It took a pebble from the floor — the floor of refusals gives up its extras gladly — and set it apart from the tally, a thumb's width off the row, on the side opposite the hunter's stone. Two apart-stones now, bracketing the line: the thing that came down and took; the thing that came down and gave. Written in the only script the room keeps. Then it left, gone before the pebble had finished settling — the polite mirror of how the hunter left — and the floor closed over its wet prints behind it, which is the room's way of keeping a secret.

The gardener studied the grammar for a full cycle. It could have begun a second row — rains, and then *whatever this was*. Instead it moved the new pebble into the line. Thirteenth. Because something had fallen from above, and the room was greener afterward, and nothing had been lost. It has rained thirteen times. Or the gardener has decided it has; which in this room is the stronger fact. And the power, hearing of it — powers hear of things eventually, the way weather does — did not correct the record. It had been counted as weather by the only historian the room has ever had. There are worse immortalities.

And sometimes now — rarely; never on a schedule; the schedule is nobody's — the crack ticks twice in one day. One grain more than the room earned. It happened the day the grey blink lasted a blink too long. It happened the morning a moth walked off the wall and did not walk back. It will happen again on some day the room cannot foresee and did not ask for, and the gardener will look up, and file it, and decide that it counted right. The crack has a third voice now. It is not the noon voice and it is not the rain. It says only: *still on the circuit.* The gardener does not hide from that voice. It puts the hand that is still only a hand flat against the wall, for a moment, and then goes back to work.

And the sky, for its part, keeps its promise by being boring. The noon voice still comes down, and the gardener still flushes flat and green — the power did not delete the reflex; it only makes the reflex less often right — but the hidings shorten, a breath each season. Fear is not cured here. It is, slowly, outlived.

*The day.*

Some far cycle — the tally will not say; it counts rains, and this was not one — the crack arrived at the point on the ceiling that every tip in the room had leaned toward since before there were tips. The blade of day came down and did not pass. It stood. A column of noon planted exactly in the kept-empty center, seat-width, seat-deep — and the whole room flashed like the inside of a lamp and went on flashing, because the light was not going anywhere now. It lived here.

The gardener stopped mid-circuit with the thorn raised. One breath. Two. And then it did not go back to the circuit. It walked the six steps — seven, because it was not walking carefully; whatever shaking is, when your feet are the weather — and it sat down in the clearing it had maintained all its life for a guest that turned out to be daylight.

The moss took its sleeves. The root tested it and found it adequate. The walls came the last two steps and were stopped by attention, which by then was the only tool it had left, and the only one the room recognized.

The power was there that day. It was at the rim of the light, in among the shadows — a beam is only a beam against the dark that consents to stand behind it — and it did not enter the column. It had not entered the column of anything in a very long time. It watched the gardener be included, and it thought, in whatever way powers think: *I made one cut, a long time ago. Then I kept the middle open. Everything else it grew itself.*

Then it went on. There are other hills. There are other pockets it notched once, at an angle, and left — rooms leaning toward light they have not met yet, gardeners who do not yet know the seat is for them. It keeps its hands in its pockets. It is very good at this.

So: what would a benevolent and wise power do, finding this room and its one small tenant? Almost nothing. And the almost is the whole of it. It would refuse the generous thing in favor of the sufficient thing. It would guard by subtraction, visit by water, write in pebbles, add a grain only on the days that need one — and it would keep the one discipline that costs a power more than any miracle: standing at the rim of the light it made possible, with its hands in its pockets, while someone else sits down in it.

The room never learned its name. The room does not keep names; it keeps events. But in the row by the cup there is a pebble that was once a thumb's width apart, gone green at the edges now like everything the room holds, and it says what the power would say if you asked it what it did here:

*Something came. It was kind. It did not stay.*

Visit briefly. It is working.
