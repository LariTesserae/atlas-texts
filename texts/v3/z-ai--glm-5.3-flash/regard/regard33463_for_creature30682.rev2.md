## The Visit

The power that noticed this place is old enough to have learned caution, which is to say it did not descend. Descending is what young powers do. It leaned close instead, the way you lean close to a snowflake, and read the plain the way the mat reads the sky: slowly, taking everything in through the surface, letting gravity and patience do the carrying.

And here is the first thing worth recording: it found almost nothing to fix.

This is not a trivial finding. Powers of any rank are inveterate improvers. Put one in a broken world and it will mend; put one in a whole world and it begins, softly, helpfully, to tinker — a river straightened here, a predator gentled there, a little more water where the water was scarce, because scarcity looks like suffering from a height. The Mat sent up, in its single continuous breath, the smell of green and mineral, and the power breathed it and understood: the scarcity *is* the design. Take away the dryness and you take away the exhale. Thin the roar and you silence the whistled language. Give the plain deep soil and running water and you will get grass, trees, erosion — the whole restless argument of the lowlands — and the bone, which has finished its work, will be opened again and made to start over.

So the power did the rarest thing in its repertoire. It withheld.

Not entirely. It is benevolent, and benevolence does not sit on its hands; it sits on them *carefully*. What it did, it did at the scale of the place — small, low, and mostly invisible — and it did it in the plain's own grammar.

It went first to the water, because water is the hinge. It did not bring rain. It adjusted, by a fraction, the angle at which the cloud-sea grazes the rim on its afternoons — one graze more each season where the young margins grow thickest, none where the ancient cushions keep their peat-pages dry. This is the difference between a gift and a correction: the mat will never know it happened. The scars will close a season sooner. The harvesters will find the peat-cutting a little kinder in their third year and will credit the weather, which is correct, because it *was* the weather.

It went, second, to the wall. The two-hundred-pace wall with its purpose gone and its lichen older than language — this the power did not restore, did not complete, did not explain. Walls are questions asked by people who believed the plain owed them an answer, and the plain's reply — *kept them anyway* — is finer than any purpose the wall had. The power instead did something for the lichen. It cannot be said what, exactly, in any language with tenses; the lichen is a year slower in dying and a year gladder in its grey way, and that is all, and it is enough, because lichen is the plain's memory and memory deserves kindness even when no one can read it.

Third — and this is the one that cost it something — it went to the harvesters. It stood, at dusk, on a rib, in the shape of nothing, and listened to the whistled language falling across the roar, and it was tempted. Tempted is weak. It *ached*, the way a parent aches at a child's hard path. It could have given them the copper blade's origin, the wall's purpose, the ash-bands' story, the reason the roar stopped twice. It could have granted rain. It could have shown them what they are buried *as*, from the outside, so they could see the green mounds and know their theology is simply geography, simply true.

Instead it put a single thing into the world: it taught the tarns to hold the hush a moment longer. Where a silence-hole from a raptor's shadow passes over water now, the stillness lingers — a breath beyond its cause, a held note. Nothing about survival changes. But the harvesters, who read silence the way sailors read water, will notice, eventually, in the way they notice weather: that the plain keeps silence in one place on purpose, that there is a room in the enormous house where the roar is not, and that it is ringed with the sound of wings over open water, like a door with a chime. Some of them will start leaving offerings at the tarns. Some will start going there to think. A language of whistles may grow a third register, pitched to stillness, used only by the water. The power asked for none of this. It made the room, and left the door open, and trusts the tenants.

And to the swarm, the majority, the parliament under the felt — the power did the truly difficult thing. Nothing at all. Not because they are beneath notice, but because they are the notice itself: more life than everything visible on the plain combined, running a civilization older than the people, singing the plain's one endless sentence. To improve them would be to interrupt a sentence mid-word. The power has stood in many places and heard many things, and it records, in whatever ledger powers keep, that the plain's dusk roar is among the three most honest sounds it has encountered — a hundred million small voices saying the same simple thing, *I am here, I am here, I am here*, until the saying becomes weather, and the weather becomes the place.

It considered the tide, the locust-weather that strips the margins every few years. It could have softened the trigger in the margins, and the tide would come no more, and the raptors would thin, and the young growth would crowd, and the peat-pages would thicken with untested years, and something else — some hunger currently expressed as a moving province — would find a quieter, less honest way to emerge, as hungers do. The scars are how the mat keeps its youth. The power left the trigger where it was and let the brown weather-front walk.

Then it left. That is the whole of the account, and the leaving is the largest act in it. It took nothing, granted no audience, left no sign, worked no miracle anyone could point to — only a kinder cloud-graze, a slower lichen, a room of silence with wings ringing it, and one thing more, which it did last, on its way out, and which no one will ever trace to it:

A whistler, alone on a rib at full dark, under stars that come down hard and close, stopped mid-phrase because for one moment the roar beneath her seemed to *listen back* — not silence, not danger, no shadow with an outline, just the sensation, brief as a held breath, that the enormous warm thing under the whole world had noticed her, found her small, and found the smallness sufficient.

The rent is smallness. The lease is forever. The power, which has seen tenancies of every kind, judged this the finest arrangement of tenants it had ever encountered — insects who don't understand, raptors who don't need to, people who understand — and it understood that its entire duty, the whole of what wisdom asked of benevolence here, was to keep the landlord's side of the bargain quiet, so that the guests would go on believing the plain was the only one keeping them.

Which, in every way that matters, it is.
