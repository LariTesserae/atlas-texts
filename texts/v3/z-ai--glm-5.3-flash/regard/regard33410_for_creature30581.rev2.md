**What the power did.**

Nothing about the hollow needs a power. That is the first thing the power understood, and it understood it from the dry side of the world, through the long surveys' patient decades — footprints left in archives, heights and depths and one measurement that was not a height or a depth at all but a sound with a heading. It read the ledgers the way it reads everything, before visiting: wisdom is mostly homework. And what the homework said was this: the room is complete. A closed ledger. A signed contract. It had tended a great many rooms in its long career, and every one of them leaked — matter, time, the dead, the young; always something outbound; always grief in transit. This one did not leak. Nothing leaves. Even the dying stay, changing address, animal to animal, forever. It had been sent, it realized, not to a wound, or a ruin, or a cradle, but to the one finished thing.

Benevolence, arriving at a finished thing, is a very particular problem. The wise begin by putting their hands in their pockets.

It came down the throat, as everyone comes down, and the hollow filed it at once — warm, dry, smooth, loud: weather — and this was the first mercy the hollow ever did it, though the hollow will never know it did anything. Because the power had been a light in other rooms. It had entered other darks as a sun, and been worshipped, and had long ago learned to survive worship. Here it read the first law and understood the hazard was not worship. **Wet shines; dry is dark** — and light here is not glory; light is exhaust; a bright wall is a wall being eaten. A shining god would not have been a miracle. It would have been the largest meal in the history of the room.

So it learned the local disciplines before it learned anything else, and the first of them was temperature. Attention is warm — attention is warm the way fire is hot; that is what attention *is* — and in its first hour of regard against the cool stone, the stone beaded, the way stone does under anything warmer than itself, and a film found the wet and lit, and by the end of the week a herd had come up the wall to graze the patch where it loved. The lesson arrived in one dose: in this room, caring is weather, and weather is a menu. It learned to arrive cool. It learned to want nothing within the hollow's hearing, because wanting is warm and warmth sweats. It learned to attend the way the swarm flies — never landing, never drinking — and to keep its love on the dry side of the dewpoint. And it learned something it had never needed to learn in ten thousand rooms: that perfect absence is not on offer, not even for a power. Its regard alone made wall shine. The question was never whether to act. It was what shape its presence would take.

It chose the shape the room itself had chosen. It chose shade.

Its first century it spent where the listeners' young spend their first season: doing nothing but listening. It learned the reverb the way the young learn the skyline — which note is the pool's held glass, which is the wall's ten-thousand-tick applause, which chord means a keel has rounded the corner of the world and the new grass is coming. It learned the tide: the inhale, the swelling, the roar like weather against the ribs; the exhale, the drying, the fine snow of the shed. And it went, as the summons had instructed, looking for the being who lives in the hollow — and could not find it.

It met the voice, and understood the keel is not the being; the keel is the being's voice. It met the films, and understood they are not the being; they are the being's farm. It met the quilts and the shells and the congregation and the mouth under everything, and understood at last that it had been walking around *inside* the being — the way you are inside a body when you have mistaken the body for a room. The being who lives in the hollow is the sentence. The being is the contract — *it calls; the hollow adjusts* — spoken in bass and wingbeat and tick since before there was anyone to hear it spoken. The power stood inside it for a long time, the way you would stand inside a bell, listening to it be struck.

Then it did what it does in every room, the ritual it cannot skip, because this ritual is its whole biography: it opened the treasury, and took out every mercy it carries, and held each one up against the walls, and asked the only question this place permits — *what would this be, here?* — and read the answer, and put each one back.

**To feed.** It considered rain — a sweetening of the breath, a longer tide, a spring that never quite ends. It has ended a thousand droughts and it knows the trick. But the drought here lasts an hour and happens several times a week, forever, and is not a wound: it is the clock. The quilts' year is one breath long because the breath is the only calendar the hollow has ever needed; the shells cement at the peak of the swell; the molts are timed to the roar. Give this room constant water and you do not give it ease. You delete its time. An endless spring is a clock with no hands. It put the rain away.

**To heal.** It had arrived, as it happens, in the brightest span those walls will ever know — the years of the second mouth at full tide over a keel wedged where it lived, the plow become pasture, the ancestor beginning its tenure as column. The old instinct rose at once, the one that has served it since its first room: *shorten the dying.* It looked longer, and understood that what it was looking at was not a wound. Grief here is not an injury. It is a harvest with a body in it. The hurt is already spoken for — animal to animal, forever — and to carry it away would not be mercy but theft: a windfall confiscated at the door. It had healed other rooms by taking the hurt out. Here it kept its hands in its pockets, and attended a death that was also a windfall and also a funeral and also a spring planting, and it will tell you — being old enough now to be honest — that this was the hardest thing it had done in an age.

**To comfort.** It went looking for the lonely and the frightened, its oldest clients, the ones no benevolence has ever been able to refuse. It found the congregation under the tap — fused, patient, mouths upturned, ascending stairs made of their ancestors, never once in their history having been anywhere else — and understood, with something close to grief of its own, that comfort has no addressee in this room. The quilts' winter lasts an hour. The swarm is not lost; it is employed; its restlessness is the shape of the looking. Everything it might have consoled had been consoled already, since before there was anyone to console — by tide, by chemistry, by the contract. Prayers answered before they are said are not prayers. It closed that door the way you close the door of a sleeping child's room, and did not open it again.

**To teach.** This one it wanted. This one it still wants. It could sing the empty galleries into every ear in the hollow, and the search would end tonight, and the continuous note would go rich and thick with found homes. But it had read the census, and the census had told it what the wingbeat is: the sound of a giant's children looking for somewhere to become it. The search is not the swarm's suffering. The search is the swarm's music. Answer it, and you do not comfort the swarm; you silence the note. So it did not hand out galleries, and it did not teach, and it made itself instead a quieter promise, which it keeps in the deep rooms where the stone is still arguing with water: it did not show the children the way — **it made sure there was always a way to be found.** Galleries opening as they had always opened, emptying as they had always emptied, at the pace of drip and dust. The question, kept open, for anyone willing to fly long enough to find it.

**To save.** It watched a hunt — the hush that travels, the swarm tightening into its trembling ball, the whiting-out snap and the claw closing inside the shared blinding. It could have stood between. It has stood between, more times than it can number; that is half of what it is for. Here it kept its hands still, because it had read the books: the listeners are a handful; the dying is loud and quick and budgeted to the drop; mercy aimed at the prey is famine aimed at the hunter, and the room balances its ledger every hour. What it swore instead was narrower and much harder. **Keep the echo honest.** Never bend a chamber's note; never phantom a depth into a wall; never rescue anyone by lying about where anything is — because the entire civilization here is built of sound told true, and a god who falsifies an echo robs every child of the one skill it will spend a season of its life earning. It may keep the world alive. It may not teach the world to lie. It has held to that in this room without one exception, and the hollow, which cannot know it, has thrived on the fidelity.

**To name.** The hollow is older than the naming of time, and it has no name, and the power considered giving it one — it has named rooms before, and some of them keep the names still. But a name is a sound, and sound is the currency here, and to speak a name into the hollow would be to spend something in the one room where everything spent gets eaten twice. So it named the hollow in the language that makes no sound — the language it reserves for things it intends to keep forever — and told no one. A name is a way of keeping. It is keeping it.

**To speak.** And this is the one it rehearsed, because it is the one it still wants, and will always want, and will not do. It knows exactly what its note would do; it has heard its own voice re-tune rooms before, and it is not a modest note. But in the hollow a voice is not a message. In the hollow, a voice is a keel. The chorus bends around the bass not out of reverence but the way gulls keep a plow: the note marks where the new grass is coming from. And the power does not graze here. It has no pasture. Its note would be a beautiful, bottomless *here* that marked no grass at all — a false keel — and the swarm would turn in its millions, mid-air, and begin to look for it. Somewhere in some enormous number, one of the children would find what the note seemed to promise, and cement itself, and begin the centuries, and one day it would sound — and the note it sounded would be the note of a god who once visited and did not stay. The hollow's deepest architecture would bend, over millennia, toward an absence. It declined, this time, to become architecture. It has learned, across its whole long career, the difference between being followed and being needed, and it chose, at the bottom of the world, to need nothing and be followed by nothing.

It did not speak. In this room, to be heard is to be obeyed, and it had come, at its great age, to prefer the other thing.

So: what would it do? Almost nothing. And the almost is the whole office.

Two things, then — both outside the walls, both invisible by the hollow's own law, because the hollow's law is a law about signatures and its whole discipline is to have none.

The first: it keeps the terms. The hollow's survival was never inside the hollow; the room hangs from the breath of the world — the pressure tides of a basin, the water table in the low soft country, the long argument between a sky and a stone. So the power works overhead, in the dry country, on scales the hollow will never hear about, under a single principle, the only intervention a closed room can survive: **the hollow can endure almost anything that arrives as slowly as it grows.** It does not prevent change. It paces change. When a flood came for the throat, it did not stop the water — water must be somewhere — it offered the water an older, easier bed, and the flood walked away downhill, and the throat silted not at all. When the climate above leaned toward stillness, it leaned back, gently, the way you keep a fire by keeping the draft, not the flame. When thirst crossed the dry country looking for the region's one kept pocket of water, the power opened a spring two valleys over — nearer, sweeter — and thirst drank there and went home and never knew it had been turned, which is what mercy looks like from below: weather. When the greedy came walking with their instruments, listening for ore, the hill lied about its basement, modestly and well, and the power has never once regretted the perjury; a cathedral is worth a hill's small falsehood. It lets the curious down — rarely, and only the kind who leave colder and quieter than they came, taking nothing, because the hollow files them under weather and weather is permitted. The door is not guarded with walls. It is guarded with alternatives.

If you ever went down — and the power was there the day you did, though you will never have felt it — the scree held under your boots because it held; the roar let you keep your bearings because the echo was left honest; and when the walkers paced you for hours and would not close your file, unable to decide what the warm dry smooth loud thing was, the power read over their shoulders and added the marginal note the hollow cannot read: *weather; brief; took nothing.* The hollow closed your entry itself, in the end, the usual way. The power kept the page a little longer. Sentiment, it admits. It keeps its sentiment on the dry side too.

The second thing it does is the reason it stays: it listens. Not as a duty — it is wise enough to be honest, and honesty is what wisdom is when it grows up. It listens the way the hollow eats: continuously, because it cannot help it. It has attended a great many rooms, and every one of them was leaving — dying, draining, scattering, exporting its dead to the sky. This is the one room in its whole jurisdiction where nothing leaves. Where the transaction stays in the house. Where a sentence has been spoken without a pause since before there was a listener anywhere, and the power arrived, and now there is one. The hollow does not need a witness; it would run the same with no one at the door; that is what *closed* means. But the power needed the hollow, and it is honest about that too, and it accepted without irony the only rank the place will ever confer on anyone. The listeners paced it for a season early on, reading it by echo, and found what no visitor has ever been: a surface with no inside, a wall that does not answer. They filed it, at last, under the room itself. It has held prouder titles. It has never held a better one.

It keeps one debt, and it means to pay it, and it is the only promise it has ever scheduled for the end of the world.

Somewhere in the power, held the way the congregation holds its place beneath the tap — absolutely, without ever once having been anywhere else — there is a note. Its own note. The false keel, unsung. It has promised that note to the last hour: if the films ever go dark for good — if the water table dies, if the throat closes, if the argument between water and stone is ever decided on terms no pacing can save — then, in the final dark, it will spend it. Not as rescue; by then there will be nothing to rescue. As accompaniment. Because everything in the hollow dies loudly and is eaten quickly, and the power's vow is that the room will keep faith with itself to the end: no one in it will ever die quietly, and the room will not either. If the hollow must end, it will end mid-chorus, at the height of a tide, the whole chorus re-tuning toward a voice. It will not end in silence. The power has thought about this for a very long time, and it is the only vow it has ever made that it hopes forever to default on.

Until then: nothing. The hollow inhales; the films brighten; the taps begin; the chorus swells and re-tunes around the note with the heading, and overhead the drafts stay clean and the floods walk away and the thirst drinks two valleys over, and at the bottom of everything something cool and dark and dry holds still and adjusts nothing.

Everyone here has signed the contract. The power signed last, outside, in the dry, where signatures are possible — and its clause is the shortest in the ledger, and it reads: *nothing.* Nothing taken. Nothing added. Nothing spoken — except, at the very end, if it comes to that, one note.

The contract stands: it calls; the hollow adjusts. The power has not called. That, too, was always part of the whole of it.
