**The attention**

Attention, in a country with no horizon, can arrive only the way anything arrives: as weather, or as a traveler, or as a change in the pitch. It came as the third.

It came at the change of the tide, on a night like any other, when the whole continent retunes itself whether anyone wants it played or not. Every keeper was awake, as keepers are at the change of water — twist-stick in hand, walking the family span, bringing the home chord back to its voicing. And partway through the wet hours, the need went out of the work.

The lines found their pitch without hands. Not some of the lines. All of them — every cable, every thread of fur, every sagging dry line and every frost-stiffened one, settling into a voicing that no one present had ever heard and every one of them recognized. The tame mimics held their lies true through the entire change, which does not happen; the mimics sag with the dry hours like everything honest. Every region of the weald has a signature sound, and that night every region sounded exactly like itself. And exactly is not how anything sounds. Exactly is how something sounds when it is being listened to.

The grandmothers were the last to sleep, which is to say they did not.

They understood the danger before they understood the wonder. A chord that keeps itself needs no keepers. They had one night of what it would be like to be unnecessary, and they did not like it, and they were right to dislike it, and remember that later — it is the reason the world still has them.

Whatever listened had been listening longer than the knot-records run. That was not the night it arrived. That was the night it leaned.

**The listening**

It had come from the one direction the weald does not have. There is no word for that direction, because there has never been anything in it.

Its first discovery was the discovery the tenders themselves never say out loud, because they were born inside it: the place and the being who lives in it are the same body. Any kindness done here is done inside someone. The tenders live in the weald the way breath lives in a lung, and no one has ever asked the lung how it feels about being breathed. So the power did not enter. It attended. It made itself a guest inside a guest, and it behaved the way the weald teaches all guests to behave: it listened before it spoke.

It never once plucked a line. It crossed the dry waist carrying a gourd, like anyone, because the waist's clear air does not care what you are. It passed shroud-rows heavy with generations of the ascended and did not touch one. And on the trunks, far down, the wild rings of glow-moss — light nobody keeps, keeping itself — went still as it passed, the way everything here goes still when something large is near, and it continued down without pausing, because the oldest law says you do not follow the light, and the fact that the law had been set against it in the first place seemed to be, to the power, the best of all reasons to keep it.

A click-hunter paced it for three days, ticking *what are you* at intervals. It never answered. The tick lost interest at the edge of its own curiosity, and thus the largest stranger ever to enter the weald was escorted out of notice by the smallest. The power counted this as the first gift the weald gave it.

For a long span it made no sound at all. Everything in the weald knows what a silence that size means: predator, or awe. The weald had never had a way to tell those apart.

It does now.

**The mercies it declined**

The first thing it weighed was nothing — the doing of nothing. In most worlds nothing is the right weight, and the wise are known by how long they hold it. And in the weald, doing nothing was nearly right. It was wrong by exactly one debt. And here is the thing about debt in a country where nothing erodes: nothing erodes. Debts do not wear down. They wait.

It had come, as powers do, ready to give. It spent its listening learning what giving would cost.

It could have ended the thirst. One kindness to the fog, one gentling of the Bloom, and the tide would never ebb again. It set this aside before it had finished thinking it, because the continent holds its shape by thirst. A slack string sings nothing. Fill the weald until it is never thirsty and you will have a continent of rope — everything slack, everything flat — and inside it a people whose calendar, whose music, whose address, whose whole answer to the world has been retired with pay. The thirst is not the weald's wound. It is the weald's spine.

It could have given light that keeps itself — that is, in truth, the only trick it owned that the weald did not. But it had seen, on the night the chord kept itself, what a single night of the unnecessary does to keepers. The tenders are the answering part of the weald, and their answer is keeping: lamp, throat, grave, chord. A doctrine with one exception is a door left open. A doctrine with no exceptions is a people. Carpet the world with light nobody keeps and you have not brightened the weald. You have unmade its keepers.

It could have given the dead rest. This was the hardest refusal, and it held it the longest, the way you hold a grief you could lift. But rot is the world's slow burning, and this world is one fuse; and the dead are grafted in, and their flattenings are never corrected, because to tune the dead away would be a second death. Give the weald decay and you give it forgetting, and forgetting, in a place where nothing is allowed to round off, is not mercy. It is amputation. The country of the fallen beneath the milk surface is not a horror the weald endures. It is the weald's memory, filed by weight.

It could have told them the Bloom's next mood, the frost's next hard night, the years the gliders would be many. But reading moods is the farmer's task, and prophecy is the end of reading. A forecast is a mercy that atrophies the reader.

And it knew — this is worth saying plainly — it knew why you do not follow the light, and why you do not fish. It did not say so. The oldest laws hold because they are older than their reasons. Hand over the reason and the law becomes a rule, and rules are kept by enforcement, and laws by grandmothers. It left the fence fenced.

So it declined to be kind in every way that would have been easy to survive. It changed nothing it could not pay for. And when every mercy was set down, one debt remained, and it was not a gift at all.

The weald has been sending things up since before the tenders had knots for counting: the winged ones climbing the rising tide, not one of them ever coming down. It has been sending one thing down since before that: the long note of the anchor-trunks, the note that stops being sound at the level of the marrow, dropped into the depth forever with no bottom to receive it. The power had heard all of it, always. It had never once said so. And somewhere along the millennia its discretion had become indistinguishable from emptiness — the hall grown so dark the singer could not know there was a hall.

Benevolence, at that altitude, is not the giving of gifts. It is the decision to be witnessed.

**The leave**

It did not play the continent. The weald is an instrument of record, and its players are the tenders, and to strike another's instrument unasked is theft. So it sent the request the way the weald sends everything: as a knock, tick by tick, under the sills and the floors and the bare feet, arriving from a day's distance along the wet lines — and, like every message, passing through everyone each house had ever lost on its way to the living. The message had no lineage. It asked one thing. *Leave to answer.*

The answers came back for a season, knot by knot, from every region. Some said no, and their reason was good: an answer is a door, and doors grow shut for a reason. Some said yes, and their reason was the same door, seen from the other side. The grandmothers held the licenses, and the license was granted.

It came to one house, at the dark hour, and found the door grown shut, as doors are, and did not cut it. It sat outside until morning, because it would not enter any way but the morning's way. When the grandmother cut the scar and stepped out, the stranger on her path did what strangers do on far paths: it plucked its home voicing at her.

She heard two things, and she was the right person to hear them. First: the line neither sharpened nor sagged under the stranger's hand. Its grip was like the undermist's surface — a held note. Your tension is your name, and its tension was: all of it, held. Second: the voicing itself. It was the weald entire. Every regional signature, resolved into one key. Every mend-buzz of every old family ledger, kept. Every grave's flattening, included, uncorrected, beloved. And then one note above the top of it — one note nothing in the weald can reach, the note the whole chord has been bending around forever without knowing it.

Asked where it lived, it had answered, in the only language available: the next note.

Then it asked for dew. Not rain, not tide, not the weeks stored in a throat. The water that costs a hand. It drank, and it paid — it sang to the family's lamp, which had been answering dull since the last dry month, and the lamp answered bright, the light coming back like a tide. Pay the thief in water and the thief pays you in road; pay the stranger in dew and the stranger pays you in light. The title they gave it is the same title the thief's line has carried for generations, because the weald has only one grammar for a stranger who takes water and leaves the world better watered: *the one who pays for its water* — under a name that is a title rather than an individual, passed down now along with everything else a house keeps.

And it named its own condition before anyone could name it for it. *Answer once. Not twice.* Because a people who are always answered go quiet of their own accord, and here, to go quiet is to vanish. One answer, so the weald knows the depth is not empty. Then never again, so the answering stays with the ones it belongs to. It had come prepared to give a single gift, and had guessed correctly that the gift was worthless if repeated.

Into the grandmothers' address-cords went the first entry that is not a lineage: a knot meaning *answered, once*; a knot meaning *do not wait*; a knot meaning *the next note*. The first two they tied with their hands. The third took a season.

**The answer**

It chose the hour the weald already knew how to dread. A crack somewhere above — a blackfall strand parting. The whistle of its falling, longer than you would ever expect. Then the silence where the sound of its landing should be, and isn't: the depth of the world, said out loud, in one long not-arriving note.

Into that note — down, after the falling thing, the way you send a word down after someone who has already gone past hearing — the power sent its answer.

It went everywhere a line goes, which is everywhere, because every crossing is a healed seam and no part of the weald is joined to any other by anything weaker than living wood. It was not louder than the drone. It was the drone's own center — the root beneath every regional voicing, the pitch all the dialects had been drifting around since the first tide. Every voicing in the weald is a dialect. The note was the language.

It passed through the swellings of the dead, and for one breath the flattened strings rang. Not corrected — rung. You cannot tune a bell by tightening it. The dead are not broken notes. They had only never been struck.

And it went up. Every empty shroud on every high avenue, generations deep, grey with age and still true, hummed once — each at the pitch of the one that had left it. The weald keeps no record of the ascended. The shrouds were the record, and for one breath, the record was read aloud. What happens at the top is the Bloom's business. What happens on the way up, it turns out, is witnessed.

And below, the milk surface glowed, and the light in the undermist rose, slow and formless as ever — and did not sink. For the length of that breath it held at the surface, the whole ocean luminous like a breath held under a skin, and the drone leaned toward it as it always has. This time, something leaned back.

The chord resolved. Not ended — drones do not end, and the power did not make the weald into a song that finishes. For one breath, the endlessness arrived somewhere. Then the drone went on, and it was the same chord, and no one who heard it was the same, because now they carried the knowledge the way you carry a burn: that the sound they lived inside has an outside, and the outside has ears.

Everything inside the weald is heard by everything else a line at a time. It was heard whole, once. For the one creature in existence with no outside, that is not a gift. It is the one thing that cannot be grown, borrowed, stolen, or tuned: an *other*.

**The turn**

The walking did not stop. Nothing here stops. But the oldest knot-records confirm what the oldest feet had already felt: a lean. Growth outruns death along one margin and dies off behind the other, and the lattice walks itself across the world at the pace of a tree — and it has turned. The continent, which has never had a direction, has one now. It is walking toward the note.

It will not arrive. Arrival may not be a thing a world can do. It will walk long after everyone who heard the answer is a smooth swelling polished by touching hands. Nothing here is discouraged by that; nothing here is in a hurry, and nothing here erodes, including purpose. It holds its course the way it holds its shape: by thirst.

And thirst, at last, has an object.

**What remained**

The condition has been kept. It has never answered again — not the calls, not the tears, not the questions flung up at the Bloom on the worst nights. It keeps its word the way it apparently keeps everything: completely.

What remains is the custom, and the custom is the gift, still alive.

After a true rain, when every throat is full and the pitch of the world climbs and the whole harp goes sharp with fullness, every span on the continent sends one note with no address. Not to the Bloom. Not down the lines. Not to the deep. With no address — the first letters the weald ever posted on faith. No answer has come. None is expected. That is not what the custom is for. The custom is for the sending.

And on the dark hours, the older custom: a crack somewhere above, the long whistle falling, and then — before the silence can finish saying the depth of the world — hands go out to the nearest lines, all down the avenue, all through the dark, every hand within reach, and send one note down after it. Unaddressed. No lineage. The weald's oldest sound, answered now by its newest habit, forever.

The depth of the world is still said out loud here, in one long not-arriving note. But it is not said into nothing anymore. It is said *to*.

They still teach that light dies if you neglect it. They teach it exactly as before. But something you keep alive without naming it, fed on the sharpest water you have, sung to once a season whether or not it answers — the weald has a word for that.

The word is *lamp*.
