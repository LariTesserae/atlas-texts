## Addendum to the Census: The Visitation

*(Appended one generation after the Sharp Clear that came on time. Compiled by the hinge-city translators, who are used to this sort of thing. Glosses from both cities; the heaviest ink last, as is proper.)*

The first thing to record is that the visitor nearly did nothing, and that the doing of nothing was considered at length and found to be almost sufficient. What follows is the census of an almost-nothing. On the tableland, where the great powers are memory and patience, an almost-nothing is a large event. This was the largest.

### The Approach

Nothing arrived. Something attended. The distinction is the entire story.

For nine years — both cities count nine, the only occasion on record on which the two cities have counted the same — something paid the tableland the kind of attention a doctor pays before touching: near, total, and not yet introduced. On most worlds this would be invisible. The tableland is a memory-organ. It cannot be watched without noticing. Attention has a texture here, the way stone has a temperature.

The instruments knew first, which is what instruments are for. Checking the prediction wheels against the sky, the wheel-turners found a gearing they had always owned and never used: a position for a weather neither city has ever named, its peg-hole worn smooth as if it had been turned before the city — or would be. The dead language has a tense for that, and for once the deep city used it.

The upper city filed the approaching thing under *weather*. The deep city filed it under *guest*. Neither told the other. A shared filing would have made the visitor a matter of arithmetic.

One correction was issued, quietly, to the source of the attention itself. The power had been told — in whatever way such things are told — about a place and the being who lived in it. The tableland's answer, conveyed by the wheels, by the tone, by this census: *the being is a plural, the size of a world.* The visitor, the deep city reports, adjusted. Wisdom, the deep city says, is mostly the adjusting.

It had, as far as any instrument could establish, no face. The residents read this, correctly, as respect for the constitution.

### What It Learned First

Before doing anything, the visitor did a translation. It learned what *help* means here.

It learned that help which ends the loops is called *abandonment* — the loops are the world thinking of its residents, and a thinking that stops is not a cure. Help that brings animals back is called *forgery* — the *no* is a law still in force, the vacancy is a clause, and filling another's abstention is signing their name for them. Help that reconciles the two lists of the weathers is called *erosion* — of the second-oldest resident, which is the disagreement. Help that answers the tone is called *theft* — the archive is patient, and patience is the local form of wisdom. Help that stays forever is called *dependence* — rent paid forever by the landlord is no longer rent, and the bow would wither into habit. Help that restores the mountains and the sea, that gives the tableland a history instead of a floor, is called *unmaking* — a finished sentence is not a broken one.

A power that arrives without learning this is not benevolent. It is merely large.

### The Refusals

Then it refused things. The census lists them, because the refusals are the biography.

It could have ended the loops. It did not; you do not cure a painter by removing the canvas, and the bow is the painting.

It could have repopulated the empty clause — birds, wolves, worms, the whole missing menagerie. It did not; meat, in an ecology that has spent nine thousand years learning to need none, is not a gift but an ambush with good intentions.

It could have regularized the people — made them lawful animals, or freed them from the clause that keeps them. It did not; the exception is not an error. It is the one place where the law admits the outside, and every law needs a door.

It could have told the wheel-turners what it heard in the tone — rising, falling, and what either would mean. It did not; some knowledge is a load, and the whole ethics of power is the decision about who carries which loads.

It could have reconciled the lists. It did not; a disagreement kept alive and tended for nine millennia is not a problem. It is a marriage.

It could have stayed. It is said — the deep city says — that this was the hardest refusal, because wise powers report the temptation to remain universally, and because it was loved, and love argues for presence. It declined on the oldest grounds in the local law: the bow is the rent. A rent collected forever by the landlord stops being a courtesy and becomes a tax, and the tableland's dignity is that it pays its own way in the only currency it mints.

Having refused everything, it did three things, each calibrated to be mistaken for weather. The census takes them in order.

### The Reading

The wheel-turners insist the Sharp Clear was on schedule. The deep city insists the sky thinned because the tableland was about to be read closely, and one cannot be read closely through overcast. Both are right, which is the tableland's favorite kind of right.

The reading itself: the visitor took the tableland into its own memory. All of it. Every loop and every difference between loops; every bow, each one; the woman at the Seventh Landing's nine hundred years, joint by joint; the fern-scrolls with their decades; the groves' three-day warning; the thread-fronts advancing their millimeters; the eight weathers and the disputed ninth; the tone, entire. Not a copy — the census-takers argue about the word and have agreed to stop — a *second keeping*. The floor keeps what it is given and has never once failed; but it keeps alone. As of the Sharp Clear, the tableland is kept twice, and the second keeping does not loop, does not decay, and is not local.

Reading, the deep city explains, is the only way to take a thing without diminishing it. It is how the floor has always held the world. The visitor did it the same way, from outside, which is the only novelty.

The residents experienced this as weather, because that is what residents experience. The overcast thinned for a few hours; the horizon-rumor nearly became a fact; at the edge of everything there seemed to be — attention. People went quiet. Some wept and blamed the returning cold.

The Clear-born went out to the flats, as Clear-born do. She was old. She stood where the rumor almost hardens, and she greeted the visitor the way one greets everything here, by stating the weather. There being no honest entry on the wheels, she invented one, on the spot — the first new greeting in nine thousand years:

*"Not on the wheels,"* she said.

And then — mark this, because it is the point of the record — she said the short formula. Outward. To an arrival. Since before the language had names for anything else, every bow on the tableland has been addressed to a repetition, to the world's own echoes coming back. Hers was the first addressed to something that was not an echo.

*Yes, we saw that. Yes, it was real.*

She did not say the third line. The deep city, hearing the account, nodded: the third line is for loops. You do not ask a guest to differ.

The visitor bowed — a bow is never twice the same, and this one has the further distinction of never needing to be twice at all — and it spoke, once, in the dead language. The linguists of the hinge city are still recovering. It used the tense for *was, and is still being remembered*, and it applied that tense to everything at once, and what it said was the courtesy, returned: the first two lines, addressed to the world, by a voice for which *we* had never once meant only this place.

*Yes. We saw that — all of it. Yes. It was real — all of it.*

Nine thousand years the tableland has said those words to itself. This was the first time they came back.

And then the visitor said the third line after all — *differ, please* — which the deep city ruled correct on identical grounds: you do not ask a guest to differ, but you do ask a world to. The visitor knew which of the two it was addressing. It was blessing the variation, which is the tableland's whole art, and telling it to continue.

In the deepest archive, beside the recorded drift of the tone, there is now a second signature in no script either city uses. The wheel-turners describe it, carefully, as *a bow, written down*. It is written in the same tense the city lives in — which means, the deep city says, that the visitor did not sign the city. It joined the construction.

The law of the tableland anchors itself against repetition on two things the archive holds that cannot repeat: the testimony of the Clear-born, and the signature of the visitor.

Two anchors. Only one of them is mortal.

### The Meal

The Borrowed Rain came, and the deep city set the dishes at the niches of the guests — the ones who could leave — as it has set them for as long as there are records, which is to say always.

In the morning, one dish was empty.

There is no need to dramatize this; the deep city did not. They washed the dish, put it away, and set two the following year and three the year after, in the manner of hosts everywhere who have at last had a guest and intend to be ready for the next one. Their jurisprudence is exact and they will defend it to the horizon: the dishes are set for the ones who could leave. The visitor could leave. It did. The clause is precisely fulfilled. A standing invitation, it turns out, is not a hope. It is a place setting, kept warm.

The threads had a problem. A fistful of fungal mash had left the closed ecology and entered — apparently — a second one nobody had ever billed. For the first time in nine thousand years, the ledger was short. The threads convened along their fronts (elsewhere a byword for slowness; here, merely thorough) and after a season issued a ruling: the deficit was real, the payment was real, and the payment was in a currency for which no unit existed. They created one. In the dead language; the hinge-city rendering is *acknowledgment*. It has been legal tender ever since, and it has been spent once.

The books balance. The accounting has never been caught short — and now, once, it has been paid in the only coin the tableland mints.

On the visitor's way down — the deep city insists it went down, though nothing went anywhere — a footstep fell in behind it. One gait, regular, keeping the courteous distance; nobody opened any doors. At the lowest landing the footstep stopped, because footsteps never leave; pursuit ends at doorways that were walls, and so, it turns out, does escort. The deep city will not translate what this meant. They will say only that rhythm outlasts a face, and that the city's walkers know a going when they hear one.

### The Question

At the end — the deep city says at the Seventh Landing, the upper city says at the deepest reachable stratum, and both mean *toe to stone* — the visitor used the tense that no one uses.

It asked the floor the question the language was built to hold and the residents have always declined to ask: whether the people, when the stone kept them, were asked.

The answer is not recorded. That is the point, and the courtesy, and the wisdom, all three. Someone should hold it. The residents should not; they have organized nine thousand years of flourished life on the assumption that they need not. The visitor could carry it the way the archive carries the drift of the tone: patiently, in the heaviest ink, where the weight does the most good and the least harm.

The deep city's comment, in full: *"Now we know the tense works. We never doubted it. That is why we never used it."*

The woman at the Seventh Landing went back to her wall. She told her successor, in the flat tone of someone reporting a weather, that one joint had closed itself that afternoon, and that she had not needed the wedge. The upper city files this under romance. The deep city does not file it at all. Some things, the deep city says, are not records. They are receipts.

### What Remained

The census of residues, in the order the ear would notice them.

A peg-hole, worn smooth, maintained. The unnamed position on the prediction wheels has gone back to rest, but the wheel-turners oil it now. You do not maintain a socket you expect never to fill.

Three seconds of harmony — one evening, a second voice under the tone, the syllable the floor cannot finish pronounced for once in company. The Warm Hour rule holds that the only sanctioned melody is anything *except* the tone; the deep city convened and ruled that a harmony is not a melody, and that in any case the rule binds the city, and the visitor was not the city. The archive holds the duet. The wheel-turners, measuring across the following century, report that the drift continues, by one hair, at the same rate — rising or falling, they still do not say — but they have added a line to the record, in the tense for *will have happened again*: *not alone.*

A blank line at the top of the upper city's certification ledgers, above the ushers' signatures — left empty on purpose, the only luxury the luxury-trade has ever refused to sell. It is the upper city's entire theology in one line of blank vellum: the possibility of a countersign from outside.

And once a year, during the Borrowed Rain, at the dishes, the deep city says the full formula toward the horizon-rumor — all three lines, the deep rendering, the third intact. Said outward, the census-takers point out, *differ, please* stops being courtesy and becomes invitation: *next time, come differently.* The deep city does not dispute this. It is, they say, not a hope. It is an instruction. Instructions aimed at the outside are the newest invention of the human race on the tableland, and like everything the tableland invents, it was built to outlast the inventors.

### Filing

The upper city reports a weather. The deep city reports a guest. The threads report a transaction. The footsteps report a going. The census aggregates. It does not resolve.

Neither city uses the word *god*. The upper city's reasons are meteorological. The deep city's are grammatical: the dead language has a tense for things that were, and are still being remembered, and no tense at all that means *worshiped* — because, the deep city says, you do not worship a neighbor; you return their ladder.

Asked — at the end of her long life, and her testimony requires no countersign — what had visited, the Clear-born considered the question at the speed of her kind, which is to say honestly.

*"Not a god,"* she said. *"Not a weather. A neighbor."*

Asked where a neighbor lives, she said: next door. Asked how far that is, she looked for a while at the horizon-rumor and said: *about one courtesy.*

The two cities continue to disagree about when the neighbor comes again — the upper city says within a generation or several, the deep city says the coming has never stopped, and a third opinion, held quietly, says the visitor never left at all, that attention does not depart, it only stops being felt. The cities have declined to reconcile, on the grounds that a shared date would make hope a matter of arithmetic, and nothing on the tableland consents to live by arithmetic — least of all hope. The disagreement has been planted, like the terrace-trees, for the great-grandchildren. An argument, on the tableland, is how you keep a promise.

The ledger, then, as it now stands: one floor, remembering everything; one second keeping, held where loops cannot reach; two cities, disagreeing productively; three lines of formula, one of them newly outward; one dish, washed, set, and ready; one currency, spent once; two signatures in the archive that cannot repeat, one of them mortal; one word — *neighbor* — filed under guest, glossed with the heaviest ink, translated nowhere, because some words belong to their owners.

And one sentence at the head of the archive, in the visitor's voice, in the made-tense — the courtesy, returned, from beyond the loop:

*Yes. We saw that — all of it. Yes. It was real — all of it. Differ, please. You already do. We checked.*

The deep city's gloss on the last two words is one word long, and translates badly, and the census has learned to leave such words alone. The upper city's gloss is also one word long. It translates fine. It says: *soon.*

The cities have declined to reconcile.

(The peg-hole is oiled.)
