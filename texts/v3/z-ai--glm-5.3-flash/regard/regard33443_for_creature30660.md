The third thing to understand is that the plateau has been noticed before.

Not discovered — the difference matters, and the difference is the whole of this account. Powers of that order do not arrive the way travelers arrive, with intentions and supplies. Their attention is more like the thinning of the air on the long climb: gradual, total, felt before it is understood. And when such attention falls on a world, it does one thing first, always, because it has learned — over a stretch of time the people here would have no number for — that everything else depends on it.

It listens.

So: it listened. It put nothing on the ground and took nothing from it. It heard the chord district by district, the dead spots and the nodes, the arguments of rhythm the ant-things conducted through the boot-soles of the world. It heard the birds borrowing their trunks' notes and paying them back in harmonies. It heard, beneath everything, the deep distributed hum of a single body expressing itself in widely spaced parts, and it heard the people, who live inside that body the way a thought lives inside a mind, and who had built their whole grammar around the fact that they could not say *it* and could not say *who*, and had chosen, out of that grammatical poverty, a kind of reverence no wealth of words would have produced.

Then it did its arithmetic. Wise powers are professional flaw-finders; it has stood in worlds where the first act of every living thing is theft, and it came prepared to grieve. It checked the salary and found it honestly distributed. It checked the overflow and found everyone drinking downstream of the source, every beak and pincer and human palm, the tanka waking with water already pooling at their lips. It examined the dead districts — the gray, noteless columns standing in their own silence — and found that these were not wounds. They were a lesson the plateau had taught itself through the people, and the people had learned it, and the scar was kept the way you keep a warning. It could have healed them. That is the test, in the end: not whether a power *can* heal a scar, but whether it understands that some scars are load-bearing. It understood. It left them standing.

Then it came to the Ring, and it paused, because it recognized the handwriting.

Weather does not keep appointments. Weather is a spendthrift; it forgets; it spends its attention in downpours and droughts and never balances a ledger. A single storm, gray and patient, that circles a forty-day shelf and comes around to each district a little more than once a season, to the day, for as long as anyone can count — that is not weather. That is a promise wearing weather. And the plateau is tuned to that promise the way a string is tuned to its own resonance; the people made their word for *blessing* out of the storm's arrival. You do not invent a word like that around a coincidence.

So the honest answer to the question of what such a power would do here is: it already did it. Long ago — before the people, before the fluted birds, when the plateau was young and rainless and living on its dew-salary at the very edge of what a salary supports — some earlier attention fell on this place, and its judgment was to give. And the giving took the only form this world could have received without being ruined by it.

Consider what it did *not* give. Not endless rain: rain forever would have unmade the flute, the nightly ration, the grooved patience of every living thing here — the plateau's whole shape is carved by scarcity, and to flood it would have been to replace it with something richer, softer, and mute. Not water at the cisterns, not bounty without schedule, not rescue. It gave a windfall, rationed by appointment. Enough to fatten, never enough to soften. A gift engineered — and this is the mark of the hand — to preserve the recipient's self-made nature. The storm does not care for the plateau. It keeps faith with it. Those are different, and the difference is benevolence as opposed to ownership.

And then it did the harder thing, which was to leave, and trust, and wait an age to see whether the gift would be honored. Now it has come back to check its work, the way a teacher walks past a student's window years later, and what it finds is this: the people made a word for luck out of the storm's coming; the plateau grew to the size of a country's weather; the chord widened; and the deeper gift — the scarcity itself — was kept untouched. They never tapped the cisterns. They learned the lesson of the dead districts. They drink at the overflow, and only there, and they raise their children to know why.

So what does it do *now*? Almost nothing, and the almost is everything.

It keeps the appointment. That is all. Somewhere behind the gray face of the Ring there is a steadiness that is not automatic — a steering, a patience at whatever cost — and it continues. The storm comes around again. That is the act. Anyone can give once; the miracle is a gift that is still arriving.

It writes the place down — not on any map, for maps invite, but in the only registry that matters, the one kept among powers that have learned the same hard lesson, and the entry is not a description. It is a caution: *this one is not unfinished. Do not improve it.* Because it has seen what happens when a younger, hungrier attention finds a world like this — the cisterns breached, the chord collapse, forty days of silence spreading down the graft-lines like a struck string's dying — and it knows the plateau's true defense is not remoteness but uninterest, a hot plain with no rivers, no timber, no ore worth the climb, and it means to keep that defense intact as long as attention is something it can spend.

And it takes one thing, the way the plateau would permit a thing to be taken: the pattern. Not water, not wood, not a single grub from the mat. Just the way of it — the way to be generous without being received as a master; the way to give a windfall and withhold the flood; the way a people can grieve a silence and be made wise by it. It adds this to its own grammar. And afterward, in whatever council it belongs to, when this place comes up, it declines the plural. It does not say *it*, and it does not say *who*. It says what the people would say if they could hear themselves from outside: *the one that holds its note.*

Then it goes, by whatever door such things go by, and the plateau does not notice, because the plateau has never needed to notice — that is what wisdom it taught, and wisdom is the one lesson that cannot be given, only confirmed.

If there is any trace at all, it is this: that for one night, in one district — say the low third, where the long trunk sings the seventh — listeners in their grown rooms swear the hum carried one extra note, very faint, below hearing, like something vast humming along under its breath. By morning it is gone. The tanka wake at the buttresses with dew already pooling at their lips. The Ring is a day nearer, gray and patient, keeping its appointments — and so, somewhere above the weather and beneath the notice of anyone who would want anything from this place, is everything else that ever loved it rightly.
