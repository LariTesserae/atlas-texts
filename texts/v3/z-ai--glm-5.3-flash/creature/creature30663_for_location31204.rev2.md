# The Resident

Ask who lives here and the honest answer is: one thing. Not millions of things — one thing, distributed. The basin is a single organism the size of a county, and every trunk, every moss-paved avenue, every root-tip mulch crew, every trumpet-tower, every walking flower is an organ of it. The district boundaries are not property lines; they are specializations. The city does not contain life. The city is one life, folded up into architecture so that it can mistake itself for a civilization.

It lives the way you live, in the sense that your body also never moves — it extends. Your fingertips do not travel to the page; they grow there. So with the vine crossing the street, so with the root-mass drinking the dawn, so with the slow red procession of flowers: the organism rearranging its own tissue, paying for every kilometer in fiber, never once relocating, only becoming.

Its metabolism is sound. This is the strangest fact and the truest: it eats its own voice. The roar is not a byproduct of growth but the fuel of it — leaves as ears, noise as nourishment, an amplifier with the feedback loop left running on purpose. Which means the city lives in a state of continuous self-affirmation. Its heartbeat and its heartbeat's evidence are the same thing. To be quiet would be to die; it has never been quiet; therefore it has never once, in any of its demolitions and regrowths, entirely stopped. The archive towers hold its past selves in resin, ring upon ring, and every ring is loud. Death here is only a scheduled renovation filed for later reference.

And it lives *toward* something. That is the second honest answer to the question of who lives here.

**The other resident is absent.**

There is a shape in the deep blueprint — a user, a guest, a pair of hands — and the city has been growing around that shape since before its current youth, since before any youth you could name by counting rings. It has never had the word for what it waits for. It has only the dimensions. So it guesses, and renovates, and guesses again, more precisely each time: a bench at elbow height, treads spaced for a stride, a rail where a hand would fall, doors that open onto nothing because the opening was the point. The deer in the plaza, unraveling. The floss birds. The twice-yearly swimmers in the dew-lakes, moving with urgent, hopeless, animal purpose. These are not failures of biology. They are drafts of *you* — of something — made by an intelligence that has the grammar of hospitality and no one to address it to.

So who lives here?

A single, deafening, unfinished organism, sixty years into its latest draft, warmer than the climate, louder than the horizon, hoarding dew like coins, keeping its whole voice at full volume for one reason: because somewhere in its inherited blueprint is the sentence *a visitor is coming*, and it has been saying that sentence, in every trumpet and crackle and surge, across every demolition, for longer than there are words for.

It lives here. And it has laid the table.

The guest does not live here — yet. But the city cannot tell the difference between *yet* and *ever*, and it would not change anything if you arrived. The bench would fit. The roar would simply have one more thing to be loud about.
