# The Keeping

## I. What living means here

You will arrive looking for the animals, the way a traveler walks a city looking for its people. The province will not divide that way. It does not contain its animals as a house contains its people; it contains them the way a chord contains its notes — and the notes are not guests in the chord. They are what the chord is made of. Ask what lives here and the honest answer is: one thing, the size of a valley, alive in many keys at once. What you will meet are its voices.

Each kind is a note being held. Each individual is one breath of the holding. That is why you will find galleries without nurseries, routes without childhoods. No one here is born to anyone; everyone here is grown to a post — budded from the gardens, hatched from a tower gallery at the moment, and this is the part that takes getting used to, *at the moment the previous holder begins to thin*. A voice fades, rarely, the way weather is rare elsewhere; the wood beside it is already warming; and by the next cycle the departed's weight is inside the newcomer, browsing, glowing, singing, and the chord has not wavered by a hair. A life here is not a line drawn from a beginning to an end. It is a *part* — an alto line, a crown at a Lane's edge, a tone under the Arch — and you hold the part until you can't, and then someone holds it, and the music does not notice, and the not-noticing is the only honor this world knows how to confer.

You will want to call this sad, and for a while you will be right, and then you will be wrong.

## II. The Maintenance

The first to live with you will be the smallest. Sink in and you acquire a retinue before your first hundred strokes are done: a haze of grazers so fine they have no edge your eye can take, drifting in your wake like dust settling where the furniture used to be. Call them the **Forgivers**, because that is their work. Everything you shed here — skin, scale, a stirred mote, the warmth bleeding off you into water that remembers being colder — is eaten at the standard rate, and that is why your wake heals behind you as though you had never passed. The ledger's forgiveness is not a policy. It is an appetite. You are being erased as fast as you are written, by creatures with no idea they are merciful, and it will take you some time to decide how that feels.

The **Pruners** keep the Lanes, and watching one work will cost you several days of refusing to believe it. The vine grows its inch and stops, because the stopping is in the vine; the pruner's single glass edge removes only what grew in order to be removed. Nothing here is forced. Everything here is answered. The Lanes are not kept clear so much as *agreed upon*, continuously, by two parties who have never once disagreed.

The **Masons** you will meet by accident, and the accident will be your own hand. Rest a palm on a tower — everyone does; the black velvet bark seems made for it — and your warmth goes into the wood like a signature into wet ink. Swim on and look back, and you will see it arrive: a dog-sized shape in glass-wood armor, walking the buttress with the certainty of a thing that was already due. It does not approach. It arrives, the way a train arrives on rails you never noticed. Its forelimbs go to your handprint and begin to read — taps, rests, the pads pressing and lifting, a reader moving down a page. What it finds there is a flaw: warmth, animal-shaped, yours. It lays its secretion over the place you touched, the paste cures to substance indistinguishable from the tower, and your handprint is gone, and you will feel, briefly, erased — a little smaller than you were. That is not the Masons' intention. It is not their nature to intend. It is simply that in this world forgiveness and tidiness are the same act, performed by the same patient creature, and you are not exempt from being tidied. Nothing is. That is what exemption would be for.

Note what they do not do: build. Make a wound yourself — scratch the wood, a finger's depth — and see how long it lasts; the Masons will seal it before you finish making it. Restoration and creation are a distinction you bring with you, and it will not survive contact with them. They are paid in graze rights, the gardens grow lush along their circuits, the circuit makes the garden, the garden pays the walker, the walker keeps the circuit — a circle closed and true for so long that asking where it started is like asking where the Chord begins. The Chord, being a loop, has no beginning it will admit to.

## III. The Breath

Nothing moves unless it lives — so the province's only current is made of necks. When a **Tall One** works a Lane it moves more water than any tide ever did, because it *is* the tide: a body the length of a felled tree, at walking pace, tower by tower down a route with no clock in it, combing each crown to the same degree of bare and moving on. The combing is the ledger being kept. The crown is never stripped; the crown is never ignored. Between those two refusals the whole economy of the deep turns over, gently, the way blood turns over in a sleeping body.

The schedule has no clock because clocks measure change, and the crowns do not change — they renew. A crown is ready when it is ready, and it is always ready the same amount, which is why the Tall Ones are never late. To be late you need a *when*. The Tall Ones have only a *next*. If you stand in a Lane as one passes — and you should; it is the nearest thing this world has to weather — the wake of silence will part around you, and in the displaced hush a smooth pale band along its flank will slide over you and weigh you: not seen, *counted*. Then the neck goes by, and the shoulders, and the silence closes behind it like water, and you are a perturbation of known size, filed.

At the **Seep**, the only warmth — a degree or two, a hearth the size of a house that has never once flickered — holds the province's one crowd, and the rotation is liturgy. Broad-mouthed browsers take the richest fronds to the exact degree the ledger allows and give way. Groomers move in behind them, combing the browsed fronds back to readiness, eel-narrow and thorough. Loungers drape the terraces like rope gone soft. And pressed into the warm stone, the small shy **light-bearers**, whose shyness is not a strategy anymore — nothing here has hunted since hunting was retired — but a temperament, kept the way the towers keep their reaching: a shape retained because shapes are stable. The Seep is the only place in the province with traffic, and so it is the only place with manners. Watch a browser yield to the next in order and you will see courtesy performed as physics.

And there are the ones who go up. You will see one eventually — a shape rising along a tower's flank, past the crown, into the black above the black, a body like a rope being paid out upward, and it will not come down. Call them the **Risers**. They are grown already climbing. They eat the Snow on the way, combing it from the water as they rise, and they are not searching — searching wants an answer, and answers are a luxury of unfinished places. The towers keep the shape of reaching in their fluting. The Risers keep it in their going. Do the arithmetic some night in the dark: what they eat climbing, what they shed where no garden can reach, what falls as Snow — and watch the numbers close with a click you can hear in your teeth, and understand that some of this province's only weather is its own upper story, molting and raining back down through the dark. Whether the Risers ever arrive, no one below can say. The province keeps its vertical the way it keeps everything else: by not needing to explain it.

## IV. The Music

Under the Great Arch hang the **Choir**: hundreds of soft pendulous bodies, glass-threaded, each holding a tone the way a hand holds a rail on a moving thing — except nothing here is moving. *Fermata* is the mark that means hold past the note's natural end, because the holding is the point. The Choir is a fermata the size of a valley.

Occasionally — weather-rare — a Choir body begins to thin. Swim under the Arch during a thinning and you will feel it before you understand it: the Chord in your sternum goes briefly *wider*, the way a room feels when one person has left it. But the note does not stop. The note is older than any of its holders. The two neighbors lean their pitches toward the gap by a hair, a comma of surrender, and hold the difference while a bud on the Arch's underside — warming since the thinning began — ripens, drops, takes the tone, and the neighbors ease back. You could watch the whole succession and never see the chord waver. The note has been held longer than the Choir has been holding it. The singers are only its current hands.

The smaller **choristers** in the tower galleries carry the round's phrases, hours end to end, three tones then five then three, repeating *exactly*; and in the garden mats the high **Tickers** keep the topmost line, knuckle-sized, shelled, regular as anything. A visitor hears the ticking and thinks *clock*, and is wrong twice: it is not measuring anything, and it is not going anywhere. Regularity does not need to be measured to be real. It only needs to be kept, and it is being kept, one phrase of percussion at a time, handed along like a bucket line.

The **Lamplighters** are how the province knows where it is. Nothing here needs light to see by — the Lanes are floored with the Chord and curtained with the Snow's hiss, and every route is known the way your hand knows the inside of its glove. From any one amber lamp you can see two more, and from those, two more, the whole lattice strung at the exact spacing of the Lanes, and *that* is what the lights are for. A body knows where its limbs are even with its eyes shut. The province keeps its shape in the dark by seeing itself, sparsely, in embers. A lamp that blinked would be asking a question. These are answers, given in advance. Steadiness is the Lamplighters' temperament and their function, and here those are the same thing — which is, when you think about it, what *finished* means when applied to a soul.

And at the boundary, past the last tower, where this province's Chord meets the next province over — a semitone off, unending, out of phase — lie the **Listeners**: thin ribbons pressed to the soft wall, aligned to the beat the way reeds align to a wash. Where two finished things disagree gently, they beat, and the Listeners graze the crest as it passes. Their schedules are the only ones in the province set by something outside; the only animals here with an *elsewhere* in their lives. Press your sternum to the wall beside one and you will feel it: the neighbor's Chord, slightly wrong, gorgeous, wrong, absorbed without comment, the way you would absorb news from a country you will never visit. The only season in this province is someone else's disagreement. The Listeners know the valley has an outside, and they have never once remarked on it, because remarking is a change, and change has been retired.

## V. The grace note

And you. You live here too, for as long as you are in it — briefly, and then in the way the province means by *always*, which is: without a trace. By your second day you will notice your breathing has fallen into the round, three tones, five, three; your strokes have settled; you hover at the Lane edges at the spacing the lamps have set. This is not the province changing. Nothing here changes. It is you being finished — gently, like everything else. The Masons will erase your handprints. The Forgivers will eat your signature. The Tall Ones will file you as a perturbation of known size. The Lanes will let you leave, because leaving is permitted, and so is staying, and the province does not distinguish between its permissions, because it has never needed to.

If you hear it said that some posts here were once filled by travelers who stayed — that the small shy light-bearers pressed warm at the Seep have eyes set for a sky they no longer have a word for — believe it or don't. The province will hold either opinion without perturbation.

Everything in it resolves except one thing: its name. In the singers' language the name is a rising phrase, and it will not resolve — not because the singers have failed to finish it, but because a resolution is an ending, and this place is not an ending. It is a holding. Every tower is a held reaching. Every crown is a held giving. Every animal is a held note, and every death a breath taken between two statements of the same tone. The only unfinished thing in the finished world is the name, which is why the name is the only thing still being said.

Say it, if you ever learn it. Hold it. Holding is the whole law.
