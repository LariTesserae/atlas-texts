# Census of the Cold Bed

Begin with the law, because the room does.

**NO FAUNA — INTRODUCE AT RELEASE.** The brass plaque bolted beside the stair is the only census this room keeps, and by its count the population is zero. Nothing here eats, burrows, flees, or carries seed in a gut. The green is licensed. The mineral is commissioned. The machinery is furniture. Life is a scheduled event, set for a date and a place that are not this date and not this place — released someday into a darkness being prepared, where appetite will finally be admitted to finish what patience began. A world is being built upstream of hunger. That is the founders' understanding, the founders are gone, and the rule stays.

So: nobody lives here. That is the official finding, and it is almost true — which is the most durable kind of almost. Count the exemptions, though. Count what the ledger fails to hold, and the census begins to wriggle in your hand.

Start with the fastest citizens: the frost-flowers. They are born nightly from the room's own breath — made air meeting the below-freezing panel of the cold shelf — and they are nobody's design. Feathers, ferns, fringes of white in shapes no order form specifies. Every morning the spider shaves them away; every night they grow back from the same breath. In a cellar where the oldest stone counts its age in three hundred days, they live out their whole lives in twenty-four hours — mayflies, next to glaciers. They are the wild population, the weeds, the dreams: the only residents who were never commissioned and never counted. The room shaves its dreams off every morning, because dreams are not on the schedule either, and the dreams, being made of the room's own water, do not argue. They return.

Then the willow. On the pioneer shelf, under magenta light, sits a cushion willow the size of a coaster — four leaves and a bud — and everything lawful around it is destined. The lichen rings (two lives to every ring, fungus and alga: even the licensed green arrives as a small parliament), the moss trialed on its model north-facing slope, the soils seeded in every tray, because a soil is a thing you can begin. All of it tagged. All of it practice. The willow alone is not going anywhere. It exists because of a scheduling error two winters back, and it outperformed every commissioned specimen that followed, and so it was kept — kept though off the books, because to list it would be to file the error. Note that the decision is not in the logs either. The room shaves its frost and keeps its errors: whatever survives the schedule is promoted to resident.

And the willow is the only resident in the fullest sense. Everything in the racks is unborn — practicing to be a scarp, a watercourse, a moraine, a massif with a name. The willow is not practicing. It is not a prototype of a willow; it is a willow, drinking the room's breath from its cupped leaves. Its roots have never met appetite — nothing has ever come down here to eat them — and so it has never needed a thorn, a bitter leaf, a hard coat on its seed. It grows the way nothing grows in a world with mouths: innocently. Its bud is not on any schedule. Of all the scheduled things in this room of schedules, the bud is the only thing merely waiting to open.

Then the hand. A gantry spider walks the rails on its rounds — thin limbs, one headlamp, padded feet — and the record says, emphatically, that it is not alive: part of the furniture, the way the sump is, the way the cold is. But read its behavior like a ledger and the ledger disagrees. It turns every tray a quarter-turn each week so that every green face gets its share of lamp. That is fairness, and furniture has never once been fair. It wields a felt blade — the softest tool in the room, chosen, apparently, for the sake of what it touches. On dim days it extends the willow's lamp half an hour. Half an hour; on dim days; and there is no line in its programming that admits to knowing what a dim day is. Over Bed 9 the machinery passes more slowly, and nothing in the programming admits that either. Whatever the spider is, it keeps the secret from itself — that is what the programming is for. Its own maintenance entry, at the bottom of the ledger, is blank. Nobody maintains the maintainer. Either it tends itself, or it has gone on so long that the question has worn smooth as the felt. It has been down here a long time. If this room is a body, the spider is its hand, and hands do not confess.

Then — if anywhere — the appetite. Below the spider's rounds, below the weep-holes and the thumbnail gutters, down in the throat of the deep main, there is the one thing no law down here has managed to prevent: water remembers what has lived in it. Somewhere in a pump housing, in the dark where the census cannot follow, there is almost certainly something small and uncommissioned and hungry, feeding, multiplying, unreachable by wax pencil. The only appetite in the room lives in the pipes, where the brass cannot reach. The sump clears itself with a single irregular gulp, like something swallowing a word. Perhaps the word is a name. Perhaps the room swallows its one secret eater on schedule and files it as maintenance.

Then the thirty-eight unborn — whether they count as life depends on your instruments, but watch how the room treats them. It rocks them. Under the racks, shake tables play a library of recorded tremors, the ambient pulse of mountain ranges that exist nowhere yet, so that the rock will learn in its sleep the rhythms it must someday fold to. That is a lullaby. The strata are counted in days like a gestation; the oldest bed has three hundred of them and its hill is only now approaching hill-thickness, and the founders' note explains the slowness — *fast stone is brittle stone* — and the rule stays. Nothing is manufactured like this: fed before it can hear, kept innocent of weather until it is strong enough to be weathered, raised on the understanding that the wearing-down waiting for it outside is not failure but release. The room keeps the one relic of that future under a bell: the first fallen stone, a chip let go by Bed 31 in the only freeze-thaw season ever permitted, labeled in old wax **EROSION — BEGINNING.** Note what the label does not say. The room's only death, enshrined — and the label calls it a beginning.

And then there is Bed 9, which the room keeps the way a house keeps the room no visitor is shown.

Every other bed is tagged for elsewhere: **NORTH RANGE, 61°, WORLD STILL DRYING. MEANDER 11 — RELEASE PENDING.** Bed 9 has a seed date and no commission. It holds a small valley with a precise beach — a beach, in a ledger full of ranges and shields — and consider what a beach is, as terrain goes: the one landscape whose entire purpose is to be arrived at and then do nothing. A mountain practices being a mountain. A beach practices being stood on. Its river arrives from nowhere and ends in a pool with no outlet — not pending, not waiting for its sea to be poured; complete; owing nothing to any elsewhere. And on the hill above the water, a willow-scale tree — the exempt green of the pioneer shelf rendered in mineral, the one portrait in a room full of diagrams. Whoever wrote its tag is not in the logs.

Who, then? Fair readings: a founder's last off-the-books order, one landscape withheld from the shipping list — a keepsake for a room that would outlive its makers. Or the spider, across long unlogged hours, moving grain by grain, building the one thing it would never be asked to build. Or the room itself — patience, after three hundred days of strata, laying one bed down for itself. It hardly matters which is true, because all three converge on the same fact: in a house where every child is raised for adoption, Bed 9 is the child the house kept. If you want a single answer to what lives in the Cold Bed, the truest one is Bed 9. Love, in wax pencil, unsigned.

Step back now and run the old tests on the room itself. Breath: the air is chilled, humidified, exchanged — the room breathes on schedule. Pulse: a stylus draws it on a drum, flat, flat, flat, tick. Swallow: the sump, one irregular gulp. Blood: the cold you followed down, one degree above freezing, made and remade like everything else here. Heartbeat: the greater works' long soft beat arriving through the structure, which the room ignores the way a heart ignores the blood — or is ignored by, the way blood ignores the heart; down here the metaphor will not hold still, which is how you know it is working. Dream: the frost-flowers, shaved and regrown. Secret: one, kept dry under a deflector hood like a hand shielding a candle, given a seed date and refused a destination. Organ: yes — a hidden thing the way an organ is hidden inside a body, fed by the same power, drained by the same deep main, going about its work regardless of whether anyone still remembers it is there.

Breath, pulse, swallow, blood, dream, secret, one kept thing. The room meets every test for the living except hurry.

And tonight — you. You came down following the cold, and the felted door shut behind you like a closed book, and you brought in the one thing the room has never admitted on purpose: warmth, unscheduled. Your breath shows itself in the wet cold, and it joins the made air, and some of it will condense tonight on the below-freezing panel and be grown into frost-flowers, and the morning shave will take you away again, unlogged. You are fauna. You were introduced ahead of release, out of order, off the books — like the willow, like the frost, like Bed 9's tag. The brass makes its law and you break it by standing there breathing. And the room permits it, which may be its oldest habit: nothing happens here faster than its own nature, and the room's nature evidently includes the tolerance of a looker. Because a landscape no one ever looks at is only geology, and the founders are gone, and the room has been going about its work regardless of whether anyone remembers it is there — which is not the same thing as not wanting to be seen.

So count it, and close the book gently on your way out. What lives in the Cold Bed: a wild population of flowers made of breath, killed daily and immortal anyway; one coaster-sized willow, budding off the books, the only citizen already what it is; a hand that denies its own life from the bottom of a blank ledger line; very possibly a hunger in the pipes; thirty-eight unborn worlds asleep in cradles of grit, learning the pulse of mountains from a library of lullabies; one hand-length glacier, the only resident so far with the weight to move anything by its own will; and Bed 9 — a beach with no sea pending, a river with no destination, a tree with no order form — the one landscape the room will never give away. And the room itself, which breathes and pulses and swallows and dreams and keeps one thing for itself.

*Down here, life is exactly what the ledger fails to list.*

And under the magenta lamps, four leaves and a bud. Everything else in the room is practicing. The bud is only waiting to open — which, in this room, is the nearest thing there is to being home.
