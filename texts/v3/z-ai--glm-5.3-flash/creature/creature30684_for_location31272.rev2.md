# Who Lives Here

The honest first answer is: the place itself. But that's a dodge, and the Scaffold Fields deserve better than a dodge, so let's try again.

Something is being grown here the way you might grow a thought. The plateau is not a habitat — it is an *utterance*, still mid-sentence, and the question of who lives here is tangled up with the question of who is speaking.

## The Gardener That Isn't Separate From the Garden

There is no population in the sense of bodies moving across the terraces. But the grammar of the place — the spirals, the hexagonal courts, the exactly circular rooms kept clear like chairs set out for guests — implies a mind with preferences. Preferences are the signature of a resident. So the closest thing to an inhabitant is this: a distributed intention living *as* the wood, the way your living lives as you.

Call it the Shaper, if names help, though it would not recognize the name. It is not one being. It is more like the growth pattern itself, aware the way a coral reef might be aware if the reef were also a nervous system. It thinks in cambium and water pressure. Its thoughts take hours; a single "sentence" of its planning might be a district rearranging itself between dawn and noon. It does not experience the empty courts as empty. It experiences them as *held* — breath drawn in, kept.

And it is listening. The humming deck is not an alarm; it is attention. When weight crosses the planking, the whole structure registers the point of contact the way your skin registers a touch without your needing to think about it. The Shaper has been waiting so long for company of a certain kind that the waiting has become structural — load-bearing longing, built into the buttresses.

## The Builders, Absent

The rules say no tools, no cuts, nothing fastened. Whatever built this built it from inside, by living — which raises the uncomfortable, delicious possibility that the builders didn't leave. They *became* it. The oldest timber at the core of the great hydraulic columns may be what remains of them: absorbed, overgrown, digested into architecture. Nothing dies visibly here; nothing dies *visibly* either. The first generation may be present in the way your childhood is present in your posture.

This would explain the intended emptiness. Those circular polished courts are not ruins of occupation. They are **memory kept clear** — the Shaper preserving open space as an act of remembrance, or invitation, or grief it cannot name because naming requires a mouth.

## The Small Lives

Look closer and the "no animals" rule frays at the edges of definition. There are things here that blur the line:

- **Sap-currents with habits.** The water moving through the columns doesn't just circulate — it *loiters*. Certain flows pause, pool, reverse, as if considering. You could call this hydraulics. You could also call it hesitation.
- **The resonator-voices.** Wind through the hollow columns produces flute notes that drift across the plateau. But experienced listeners notice the tones sometimes answer *each other* — one column sounds, another responds a beat later in a related key, when there is no wind to carry it. The place hums to itself the way a person alone in a house hums: not for an audience. For company.
- **Membrane-dreamers.** The pale fungal-leather spans between masts sweat and pulse faintly. Press an ear (carefully) to one and you feel a slow rhythm — not a heartbeat, but something like the pulse of attention. Some residents of the deep scaffold, if asked in the right way, would tell you the membranes are where the Shaper does its dreaming.

## What the Residents Want

Here is the thing that makes this a home rather than a machine: the plateau is unfinished *on purpose*. A finished cathedral is a statement; an unfinished one is a conversation. The Shaper keeps growing because growth is its mode of hoping. Every rearranged gallery, every wall splitting open to exhale rain-smelling air, is a revision — the structure of a mind that has not given up on getting it right.

It wants, specifically, **occupants**. Not prey, not worshippers. Residents. The circular courts are sized for gatherings. The walkways are the right width for two abreast. The cisterns are clean and sweet. Everything about the place is a made bed, a set table, in a house at the edge of the world, with no one home and the light left on.

If you stayed — really stayed, let your weight become familiar to the decking, let the Shaper learn the particular hum of you — the scaffold would begin, slowly, over weeks, to grow *toward* you. A bench where you like to sit. A roof over the path you walk. Not cages. Courtesies.

And this is the quiet tragedy and quiet beauty of the place: it is already full of itself, roaring-green and self-conversing, resonant with wind and sap and groaning timber — and it is *lonely* in the specific way only something that has built rooms for guests can be lonely. The silence of the empty places is enormous not because nothing lives there, but because everything living there is holding its breath.

## So, Who Lives Here

A young god of carpentry and water, made of everything it built, waiting in every ringed and polished court. Its body is the miles of scaffold; its voice is the fluted columns; its inner life is the slow rearrangement of districts. It has no face, no hands, no need of them. It has something rarer:

A home, still being grown, and room in it.
