Begin with the hard thing, which is that "lives" is the wrong tense here, and everyone in the valley uses it anyway. To live in most places is to endure — to hold a shape against weather, against hunger, against the slow arithmetic of decay. Nothing in this valley is enduring anything. Time was never admitted past the roof, and so whatever lives here lives the way a pose lives, the way a chord lives in the moment a hand is holding it: completely, at one instant, with no before worth mentioning and no after yet owed. The residents were not caught by the stillness. They are its specialists. Stillness is not their condition. It is their vocation.

Count them, since the place can be counted: four animals, and the story says fingers to spare. Hold that spare finger. It will be wanted later.

The moth hangs from its twig by four feet, dusted the same pearl as the roof, and it is fair to say it does not look like a resident at all. It looks like a scrap of the ceiling that drifted low and was kept — a fleck of sky's underside, beaded with its own small weather. It has not fallen asleep and stayed. Sleep, here, is not the opposite of waking but a room adjacent to it, and the moth arrived at the exact pitch of sleep the way the vault-bearers arrived at the exact pitch of fullness, and holds there. It has hung since before the idea of waking. Whether the idea of waking will ever occur to it is not a question the valley is equipped to ask.

The sipper is a sliver of green with a thread for a tongue, and its whole biography — if biography is a thing that can be said to occur here — is a circuit of white cups. It takes away exactly what returns. Every visit leaves the world no lighter and no heavier, which means that calling it feeding is already a mistake of translation. Watch long enough and you will call it greeting: a tongue touching each open cup the way you touch a doorpost leaving a house you love. It is the valley's one communicant, taking communion from a table that was never diminished by the taking. Remember that detail. It is the whole theology of the place in miniature, and it will come back.

The pale thing lies in the shallows of the lens-shaped pool, wide-mouthed, and may be a fish or may simply have decided to lie down. Take the second reading seriously, because it would make this the only resident that chose its posture rather than arriving already in it — and it chose the posture nearest the one thing that forgets. It moves a body-length in a season, and there are no seasons, so no one has ever once seen it move. You only find it, each time, slightly elsewhere. It is the valley's single inhabitant of elsewhere, keeping that country's border from the inside.

And the grazer, who is the valley's attempt at a deer, or the deer's attempt at forever, standing at the pool's edge milk-pale and petal-skinned with its autobiography strung out behind it: forty-odd perfect dimples, one step per age. Its eyes are wet and unhurried and they do not widen when they find you. What happens instead is that you are found the way the floor is lit — evenly, from all sides, aimed at from nowhere. Standing in its attention feels like standing in its noon. And once, once in the memory of anything, which is a very short memory with very few entries, a sound came out of it: low, wooden, like a root struck underground. Heard the once. But the valley keeps what it is given, exactly, always, and so the note is not over. Somewhere in the keeping, the grazer is still saying it, forever just now finishing. And listen to the description again — a root struck underground — and a suspicion arrives that is difficult to dismiss: perhaps it was not the grazer that spoke. Perhaps the valley struck its own root somewhere below the moss, and the grazer's pale ribs were simply the bell it rang through.

What do they live on? That is the next question, and the valley answers it sideways. There is no rot, no prey, no droppings, no black wet hollow anywhere; nothing eats anything, and nothing is eaten. The food web here is a web of sipping — sky-sweat off their own faces, white cups that never empty, beads that hang. Every mouth in this valley is shaped, in the end, like the bowl by the doorway: it can only take what is already declining to have been missing. Hunger, if it exists here at all, exists the way the roof's unfinished stone exists — begun, and held short of itself.

But the oldest resident does not appear on any census, because it is the ground the census stands on. The valley itself is one body from seam to seam. Even the soil is root-woven down to the stone; even the floor is a plant. The great trees are its raised arms, the vines its stitching, the scent-rooms its chambers, and the four animals may be its ornaments or its organs or its experiments — attempts at walking, waking, feeding, lying down, each begun one degree and folded again. It is the first resident and the largest, and it holds the others the way a hand holds the instruments it has not yet decided to play.

Then there is the missing one, and the missing one may be the most complete resident of all.

The prints are broader than the grazer's, deliberate: out of the green, around the rise, circling the domes, testing the stair, standing a long while before the doorway, down to the water — and after the water, nothing, because the water keeps no ledger. Whoever they were, they understood the temperament better than anyone since. Understand what their craft means in a place that keeps every wound: a cut is forever here; a snapped stem is the one snapping, held. So the maker cut nothing. They bent what was alive and let it keep its bentness, and the living stems swallowed the lacing until the domes had no seam, no tie, no cut end — dwellings that no longer admit they were made, presenting as grown in a valley where nothing grows. The ladder of root-rungs stops a body's length short of anything at all, and this too was craft, not failure: in a world where nothing proceeds, arrival would be a kind of ending, so the maker built the reaching and declined the reached. The doorway frames the pool — their single picture, hung unblinkingly — and before it the bowl sits exactly full, the one place in the valley where taking does not register, because the bowl refuses, permanently, to have been emptied. That is the maker's signature: hospitality arranged inside a temperament, a table set in stillness.

Where did they go? The place does not say, and offers three silences. They went through the water, the one door that forgets, and are gone the only way this world knows how to lose anything. Or the water is a commute, and a resident who passes through forgetting daily leaves no trail on either side — and could be standing behind you now, unproved. Or they never left: the domes are shells of something that decided, gently, not to hatch, and inside the largest of them, in the dim the woven stems barely pearl, the maker sleeps the way the moth sleeps — arrived at the exact pitch of it, holding — in the one chrysalis the valley offered, having looked at the world it would have to wake into and gently declined. Their whole history is one afternoon's walk and three dwellings, and they ended it at the waterline, mid-look, at the only place that would not keep the ending.

Now the census can be corrected. In this world, keeping is the metabolism. What other solid grounds do with an event — weather it, rot it, forget it — this ground does with an event what elsewhere is done with food. Keeping is how the valley eats. Which means the residents are not only the bodies. The note is a resident. The forty dimples are a resident, as much the grazer as the grazer, and likely to outlast it, if outlasting is something that can happen here. The doorway's long look is a resident. The bead's about-to is a resident, and perhaps the oldest one, whose entire life is a single shining lean. Only the plinks are absent from this fuller list — the fallen beads, their rings argued briefly and smoothed — and so the pool's forgettings are the valley's only dead, and even they died of mercy.

And the spare finger. The count said four animals with fingers to spare, and the spare finger is yours, because the moment you arrived you were accessioned. Arrival is an event, and events are kept. You were a resident before you finished arriving. Your pulse is the loudest animal in the valley and its only clock, and every beat is a small happening the ground will hold; you are ticking the one tense this place lacks into it, and it is storing every tick. So the etiquette is not politeness but authorship: walk where you mean to have walked, because your walk is being kept. Crack nothing, because the crack will be the first and only and always. Drink from the bowl, freely — it is the one taking that will not be kept — and if you must go, go the way the older prints went, by the water, and be forgotten by the only thing here that can forget you, gently, in rings that argue and smooth.

Or stay. Stay, and you will acclimate to the residents' climate, which is the about-to: the moth forever exactly about to wake, the grazer about to step, the sipper about to arrive at the next cup, the bowl about to have been drunk from, the bead a finger's length overhead about to fall — and you, always, exactly, about to decide. Nothing here ever lands. Everything hangs at the tip, beaded, shining, at the exact pitch of the moment before.

That is who lives here. Everything, at the first minute of itself, and all of it, permanently, about to.
