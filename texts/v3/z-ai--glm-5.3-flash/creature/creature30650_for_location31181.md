Where the water stops working, the holding begins. The approach climbs out of the grammar of creeks and cut valleys, out of the smell of stone being worked on, and somewhere near the snowline the additions stop. Above that, nothing is being written. Things are being kept. Snow that falls in October and is still there in July has stopped being weather and started being substance — firn, the old word for it, snow halfway to ice. In most ranges firn is an exception, a curiosity of shaded north faces. In the Held it is the citizenry. The place is not named for its shape or its rock. It is named for what it does, which is hold, and what it holds is winter, out of season, year over year, in white basins the maps color blue-white and the shepherds will not camp on.
