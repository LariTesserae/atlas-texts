Ask the fen who lives in it, and you have asked the only question it exists to answer. But be careful with your categories. A census wants its entries separated — one of this, two of that, resident and non-resident — and Tallymere does not cooperate. Here, what counts as *alive* runs from things that merely persist to things that merely sleep, and what counts as *one* runs from the uncountable to the possibly unique. Order the residents by size and you will only confuse yourself. Order them by how long they've held their places and you will never finish. So: take them as they come.

**The motes**

Begin with the majority, which is also the substance. The motes are not *in* the water the way fish are in water. They are what the water is — the way a crowd is not in a square but is the square's being occupied. Scoop a handful and you are holding a constituency. Each mote carries a grain of record, and there is no birth among them and no death, only sorting and resorting, an endless re-shelving of what has been held. When you raise your cupped hand and they drift, then stop, then drift *differently*, that is the crowd deciding what to make of you.
