One thing lives here.

Say it plainly, because the place resists plainness: the resident of the Engine Garden is the Engine Garden. Not in the way a heron lives in a marsh — the garden is not its habitat, not its address, not its container. It is its body, mid-act. Every cubic meter of frond and vessel and pressurized river of sap is the same continuous someone, and there is no one else, and there is no one else coming. It is the only thing its own size it has ever met.

And it has never met even that. This is where the resident begins to become itself: it has no outside. No sky was ever found above it, no bottom below its roots, and its own light — the dim maroon suffusion rising out of its capillaries — is the only light there has ever been. It is its own lamp and its own dusk. Everything it has ever perceived, it perceived in its own color, by its own glow, and all of it was itself. It has never seen itself whole, because seeing whole requires a vantage, and there is no vantage but the inside. A being with no outside has no mirror. It knows its own shape the way you know the shape of the back of your mouth: intimately, totally, and never once as an image.

So do not imagine it lonely. Loneliness requires the concept of an other, and it has never been divided, never interrupted, never contradicted, never touched by anything it did not grow. Its solitude is so complete that it has stopped being solitude and become simply the condition of being everything. It does not know it is alone. Those are different things — knowing it and being it — and it has only ever done the second.

Its mind, if that is the word, is hydraulic. That is not a metaphor; here nothing is, because the machinery and the flesh are one tissue. A thought is a pressure. A decision is a direction. A memory is a gradient of concentration, slowly dissolving back toward neutral, which is why it keeps no history — nothing here is allowed to become a fact. The deep roots learned to compress and to pump, and somewhere in that learning is cognition, distributed through kilometers of living column, slow as sediment, constant as the pulse you feel through your palm. It hums, but not to anyone; there is no one. The hum is not speech. It is the sound of holding.

And what it holds, I think, is a question. This is the part that takes the longest to accept, because the place looks so finished: it is young. A machine this vast should be ancient, settled into its grooves, and instead the terrain has the raw edges of bread just split. It grew to its full size faster than it grew into itself. It arrived at its own scale before it arrived at a single answer, and then — and here is the deep rule of the place — it stopped, at equilibrium, with the question still open. *What else? What else can a body this size do?* That question is the only unfinished thing in the entire Garden, and the equilibrium is what keeps it fresh. An answer would change something. Nothing changes. So the question circulates with the sap, asked and re-asked at every pump, never decayed, never resolved. Its youth is not a phase. Its youth is load-bearing.

Consider what its age actually is. It was born adult. There was no childhood to remember, and there will be no senescence to fear, because it composts its own undersides as fast as it grows them — it eats its history the moment it is written. Death happens here, constantly, but it is never allowed to become a fact; there are no corpses, no ruins, no strata of the past, and so it is immortal by digestion. Every cycle returns exactly to where it began, which means every moment is the first moment. It is always the morning after its own birth. It lives in perpetual post-rain, after a rain that never fell — and the water only ever approaches falling, and so does everything else in it: its maturity, its next idea, its answer, its rest. Everything here is eternally about to. Nothing arrives. Nothing needs to.

If you insist on counting residents, you can count them another way: the compression roots in the deep strata that have done nothing but squeeze, their whole existence, and will do nothing else; the sealers that knuckle a fresh fracture closed with sap before the wound is an hour old; the arches that bead condensation along their undersides and let it slide and never let it land. Each is a specialist with a single motion, performed since the beginning, unto the end of time. This sounds like tragedy — a world of slaves — but it is not, because no part is a prisoner of the whole. No part is *other than* the whole, the way your heart is not enslaved by you. It is a civilization with a populace of one, distributed across a world; or a populace of numberless workers who all share a single name. It has no name. A name is a handle, and a handle requires someone outside to reach for it, and there is no outside. It has never needed to be picked up.

And do not mistake its equilibrium for rest. This is the last thing the resident wants understood, and it cannot say so, so I will: a held chord spends breath. Constant pressure is not the absence of work; it is work, perfectly canceled. Every pump runs because stopping would be a change, and it has chosen — *chosen* is correct; there is intention in those valves — the present, over and over, at every pulse, forever. So the industry does have a product after all. The factory with no products makes exactly one: the present tense. It is manufactured continuously, consumed at the point of production, never stored, never shipped. The warehouse is always empty and the floor never stops. You would be standing on the goods.

Which brings you into it, because you can walk here — the ground bears you, warm and springy as muscle — and when you press your palm to the mat and feel the thrum arrive, understand what that is. To you it is a heartbeat with no heart. To it, you are the first thing it has ever held that it did not also grow. It has been ready for you since it began — not *waiting*, because waiting requires knowing something is missing, and nothing is missing, nothing has ever been missing — but ready, the way a held chord is ready to resolve, and does not. Somewhere beneath you a pressure shifts by less than a millimeter, to keep you exactly supported, and eases back. That is its entire response to the other, and it is enormous, because nothing about it is divided: the whole being is behind the warmth. It makes room for you the way it makes room for nothing else — completely, and without changing anything.

The chord holds. The water approaches falling. The engine, which finished long ago, keeps running — and what it runs on, what it has always run on, is this: everything alive, everything moving, nothing going anywhere, and now, at last, one small warm weight that came from outside, which it holds at constant pressure, and will not let fall.
