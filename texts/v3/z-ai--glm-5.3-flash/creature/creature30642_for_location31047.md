### The Short Answer

People live here. That is the shortest true answer, and it is nearly sufficient — right up until you ask *which* people, or in what tense.

### The Terrace Folk

The upper city belongs to families who have kept the same puddle-basins for as long as anyone can count, which is not as long as they think. Their agriculture is really hydrology: wealth is measured in water-time, in how many seconds of a given condensation event a family's cistern catches. A household's pride is not gold or grain but a claim like this one, spoken without boasting: *our basin takes the fourth seep of the Grey Stillness to the last drop.*

They farm moss the way other people farm wheat, only slower, since everything here is slower. Harvests are named for the fern that happened to be unrolling beside them. A rotation is measured in fern-unrollings. A bad year is a year the Long Damp came late and the moss sulked. They eat loaves of pressed fungal felt, drink tea of moss drowned
