# Who Lives in the Cold Nursery

The answer, unsettlingly, is: everyone you see is a *first draft*.

The chamber is a nursery in the literal sense — a place where beings are being *gestated*, not born. And the tenants of this room are not so much residents as they are **works in progress**, each one an early sketch of something that has not yet decided to be itself.

## The Builders and the Built

Start with the stone. It glows because it is *busy* — this is rock still under construction, still knitting itself together along seams of mineral, still deciding where its walls go. And the fauna — the drifting grubs, the glow-eaters, the eyeless tilting congregation — are the *same project* as the rock. They are not animals living in a space. They are the space, in motion. Soft-limbed, translucent, provisional, they crawl and drift and process the light into something the room needs, the way an embryo processes nutrients without ever being called a diner.

Nothing eats. Nothing flees. Nothing dies, apparently, because nothing has finished *starting* yet. Predation requires a settled relationship between hunter and hunted, and no one here has settled on what they are. The migrations across the floor — patient processions of small soft bodies — are not journeys to anywhere. They are rehearsals. Movement being tested. Legs being auditioned.

## The Individuals

But look closely, because generalities thin out here, and the strangest truth is how *specific* each creature is:

- **The drifting grubs** ride updrafts on wings that don't exist yet but are clearly *scheduled*. Each one emits a single clear tone as it passes your face — a wet finger on glass — and no two tones match. The room is tuning itself, one note per creature, and the grubs *are* the notes. When their tones drift near each other, they bend slightly toward consonance. Somewhere in this chamber, a chord is slowly being assembled.

- **The glow-eaters** patrol the walls in tidy spirals, rasping luminance off the stone — not starving, not greedy, just *working*. Behind each one the rock dims, then heals itself. They are sanders, polishers, perhaps editors. They leave their dark trails like margin notes in a manuscript the wall is still writing.

- **The congregation** in the condensation basin — pale, eyeless, shoulder to shoulder — tilt in slow synchrony. There is no audience. Or there is exactly one: you. Their behavior serves no function you can name, which suggests it serves one *you can't*. Possibly they are listening to the water-thread. Possibly they are the water-thread's instrument.

- **The nesters** in the prototypes — the half-staircase, the pointless arch, the too-regular ring of pebbles — have claimed every discarded draft. They sleep inside the failures of the room. This may be shelter. It may be apprenticeship: hatching inside the sketches, learning from the mistakes.

## What It All Adds Up To

And there is the fold in the air, and behind it, the *earlier draft* of this chamber — smoother, less finished rock, an earlier self of the room itself. The fold drifts over hours. So the room has versions. So the room is iterating on *itself*.

This is what lives here: not a species, not a community, not even a place. A single, sprawling, distributed **embryo** — geology and fauna as one organism in its first trimester, made of hundreds of small tentative bodies, each one an organ not yet assigned to an anatomy. The grubs are proto-wings for a thing that doesn't exist yet. The congregation is a proto-something. The rock is a proto-skin. The silence is attentive because attention is the only sense it has finished.

You are standing inside something that is deciding what it will hatch into.

And the small pale things, patient in their processions and their spirals and their tilted listening — none of them know you're there. Not because they're blind, though most are.

Because *you* haven't happened yet.

You came in through a crack that no longer exists, into a room that hasn't finished existing, among creatures who are only partially real — and the most honest description of the population of the Cold Nursery is this:

**It is full of not-yets. Every last one of them.**

Including, possibly, the door you came in by.
