You do not visit the Condew so much as you survive its hospitality. It is a place that was built too quickly, too completely, and is now quietly remembering how to be something else. Here is how an ordinary human—one who walks between worlds but still bleeds, still tires, still dreams—should prepare to step onto that titanium-white lattice.

## Physiological Hardening: The Body as Foreign Object

**Pressure and Altitude:** At 1.9 on your scale, the air is thin enough to taste of metal and cold. You will need supplemental oxygen not for climbing, but for *existing*—the pressure is equivalent to standing atop the highest peaks on worlds with thicker atmospheres, and the Condew’s elevation is philosophical as much as physical. Bring a rebreather that can handle particulate filtration; the air carries condensation droplets that may not be pure water (see: Reality Bleed below). Acclimatization is impossible—the atmosphere doesn’t change, so your body will never adapt. Expect headaches that feel like the architecture is pressing inward, and a persistent dizziness as your blood struggles to carry what little oxygen exists.

**Light and Vision:** The light here is 2.7—merciless, unfiltered, a high-altitude sun that has never known cloud or atmospheric mercy. Standard sunglasses will fail. You need welding-grade eye protection, preferably with reality-anchoring tints—something that reminds your optic nerves that shadows should fall *down*, not sideways. The shadows are knife-edged and black; without protection, you will suffer snow-blindness within hours, and worse, your brain will begin accepting the wrong geometry of the light as normal.

**Thermal Regulation:** The temperature sits at 1.4—cool, but the wind chill factor of the thin air and the constant 0.9 moisture (condensation) means you will lose heat through evaporation even while standing still. Wear layers that are waterproof *and* breathable, with reinforced seams. The cold here sits behind your teeth, a sharpness that doesn’t warm even when you exert yourself. Hypothermia is a silent companion.

**Dermal Protection:** Because erosion is 1.1—geologically infantile—nothing has been rounded by time. Every edge of the titanium-white alloy, every corner of the pale concrete, every sheet of that absent-clear glass is capable of cutting flesh. Standard hiking gear will shred. You need cut-resistant mesh underlayers, reinforced gloves with rubberized palms (the metal is electrified; see below), and boots with thick, puncture-proof soles. The lattice is a grid of potential lacerations; treat every surface as a blade until you confirm otherwise.

## Reality Anchoring: The Essential Madness

The weirdness here is 2.8. This is not a place with quirks; it is a place where the *rules* are eroding like the landscape, only they are eroding the laws of physics themselves.

**The Anchor:** You must carry a reality stabilizer—not a weapon, but a compass of being. A pocket-watch that ticks against your sternum, a photograph of a place with soil and grass, a recording of rain falling on leaves. Something that reminds your nervous system that gravity is a contract, not a suggestion. Without this, you will walk into a corridor and emerge three kilometers from where you entered, or find yourself standing on a catwalk that leads only into the memory of a forest.

**Navigation:** GPS is useless. The geometry folds; distance compresses; shadows point wrong. Bring a physical map printed on waterproof material, but mark it with charcoal, not ink—the ink will run in the condensation. Learn to navigate by the *sound*: the subsonic thrum that vibrates in your sternum (0.6 sound level) indicates the great engines turning deep within. The closer you are to a processing tower, the deeper the vibration. When the thrum shifts frequency, you have crossed into a denser layer where reality is thinner.

**Temporal Awareness:** Time here moves at 1.4—patient, geological, slow. A minute in the Condew may feel like an hour, or a day may pass in what feels like a heartbeat. Carry a chronometer that cannot be affected by local time dilation. Set alarms for hydration and rest; your body will not feel thirst or fatigue correctly in the thin, cool air. The fauna reproduce in cycles that take years; you are a mayfly here. Do not rush, or you will find yourself aged by decades while your companions have not blinked.

## The Moisture Menace: Water as Portal

Moisture is 0.9—not rain, but a pervasive damp, a condensation that beads on every plane and runs in silver rivulets down sharp angles. But this water is not safe.

**The Thin Places:** In the densest shadows, condensation accumulates into pools suspended in the lattice. These are *thin places*—membranes where the fresh construction has cut too deep into the fabric of reality. Through these pools, you can see other structures, other times, landscapes that do not exist. The water tastes of ozone, copper, and the memory of a forest. **Do not drink it. Do not touch it. Do not fall into it.** If you step into a pool, you may not emerge in the same place, the same time, or with the same number of dimensions.

Wear boots with sealed ankles and a hydrophobic coating on your outer layer. If you must cross a region where pools hang suspended, move slowly—surface tension holds them, but your weight may break it. If a drop of condensation rises *up* past your face—and it will, occasionally, defying gravity for three seconds—do not follow it with your eyes. It is a tear in the sky, and looking too long invites dissociation.

**Filtration:** If you must harvest water (and the fauna suggest it is plentiful), use a filter that removes not just particulates but *concepts*—reality-stabilized filtration that ensures the water you drink is from *this* universe. The machinery is processing the condensation; it may be refining moisture that bleeds from adjacent realities. Drinking unfiltered pool-water may cause you to remember things that haven’t happened yet, or forget your own name.

## Interaction Protocols

**The Machinery (Industry 2.2):** The engines are total but quiet. They turn once per hour, or once per day. You will feel them more than hear them—a vibration in your sternum, a hum in your fingertips when you touch a wall. The metal is electrified. Do not touch conduits, catwalks, or habitat pods without insulated gloves. The industry is processing something—perhaps condensation, perhaps reality itself. Do not interrupt the pumps. Do not enter the processing towers. The architecture is settling, groaning as it cools; sudden movements may trigger a collapse that is not structural but *ontological*.

**The Fauna (2.0):** The pale, long-limbed things are abundant, deer-sized or fox-sized, with translucent skin showing dark fluids moving slowly beneath. They are wrong in subtle ways—their eyes reflect light with a wet, depthless gleam, their tongues are too long. They drink from the condensation pools and filter the damp air, quivering as they process moisture. They are silent. They do not call to one another.

**Do not feed them.** They do not eat vegetation; they consume moisture from the air itself. If you offer them water from your canteen, you may find yourself sharing a thin place with them, or worse, becoming a source of condensation for their processing. If one steps into a gap between conduits and vanishes—existing elsewhere for a moment before blinking back—do not follow. That gap is a door that opens both ways.

They are curious but not hostile. Move with the same deliberation they do (1.4 dynamic). Every step considered. If one pauses for minutes, watching you with depthless eyes, pause too. Do not break the silence; the sound level here is 0.6, and sudden noise may attract the attention of the slow engines, or tear the fabric further.

**The Vegetation (1.2):** The mosses and lichens are engineered, pale green, barely visible. They cling to north-facing surfaces where condensation lingers. They are harmless, but they are eating minerals from the air. If you see a patch of vibrant green, you have found a place where the reality-bleed is weakest, a solid anchor. Mark it. It may be your only true ground.

## Psychological Fortitude

The Condew is a place that is "beginning to dream." Reality is becoming permeable, damp, strange. You will experience:

- **Geometric Dissociation:** Corridors that loop. Stairs that descend into ceilings. The architecture is young (1.1 erosion) and sharp; it cuts into local physics. When you feel the urge to check if your shadow is falling correctly, do so. If it is not, you are in a folded space. Sit down. Wait for the geometry to settle.

- **Temporal Slippage:** You may meet yourself. You may see the Condew as it was decades ago, or as it will be when the erosion softens the edges. Do not touch your other self. Do not speak to the future-you. The water is leaking from adjacent realities; you are not exempt.

- **The Dampening:** The silence is not empty. It is the sound of thermal contraction, of drops falling three meters to grates, of engines turning once per day. Learn to sleep in this quiet. It is a patient sound, and it will lull you into accepting the strangeness. Resist. Stay alert.

## Emergency Equipment

- **Reality Shears:** If you become trapped in a thin place—if you step through a pool and emerge in a landscape that does not exist—you need a way to cut the membrane. A blade won’t work; you need something that cuts *meaning*. A pair of scissors that have never cut anything important, or a knife etched with the names of places you have loved.

- **Desiccant Grenades:** For when the condensation becomes too thick, when the air itself is too wet to breathe, when the damp threatens to drown you in your own lungs. Thrown into a pool, they may temporarily solidify the thin place, creating a bridge back to solid reality.

- **The Forest Memory:** Carry a vial of soil from a place with trees. Not for planting—there is no soil here—but for smell. When the water tastes of the memory of a forest and you begin to forget what green looks like, open the vial. Remember that the Condew is the exception, not the rule.

- **Vibration Dampeners:** For your sternum, for your teeth. The subsonic thrum of the industry (2.2) will drive you mad if you listen too long. White noise generators, tuned to the frequency of the great engines.

## Final Preparations

Before you step onto the outermost stratum, understand this: the Condew is young. It was built in decades, perhaps centuries, and it is still settling. It is learning what it is. You are not a tourist; you are a catalyst, a drop of condensation that may tip the balance between a stable industrial plateau and a fully permeable dream-world.

Move slowly. The fauna move with a deliberation that matches the slow dynamic of the place—every step considered, every pause lasting minutes. Match them.

Do not drink the sky’s breath. Do not trust the shadows. Do not touch the walls without insulation.

And when you feel the thin, bright, cool air of a world that was built too fast, too completely, too recently—and watch a drop of water rise upward for three seconds before falling—do not be afraid. Be *patient*. The Condew is teaching you how to wait for reality to catch up to itself.

Bring a watch. Bring a map. Bring something that remembers forests.

Then step onto the lattice, and try not to cut yourself on the edge of the world.
