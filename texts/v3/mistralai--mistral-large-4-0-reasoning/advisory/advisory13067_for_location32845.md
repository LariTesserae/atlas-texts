You would prepare not for a dive, but for an *immersion into a geological epoch*. This is not a place you visit; it is a place that has been waiting for you since before your species crawled from the mud, and it will wait long after you are silt. Your intuition must accept that you are entering a temporal eddy where the world's metabolism has slowed to a heartbeat measured in centuries.

**First, the Suit.**
You cannot wear mere thermal protection. The cold here is not meteorological; it is *ontological*—a depth-cold that has seeped into the definition of temperature itself, pressing with the weight of ancient patience. Your gear must be a heated exoskeleton, not a wetsuit: a rigid, articulated carapace with active thermal loops that pump warmth into your extremities against the sluggish, heavy water that resists all motion. The suit should be matte black on the outside to absorb what little light exists (0.2 lumens, fighting through a blizzard of marine snow), but lined with a soft, bioluminescent moss-green glow on the interior of the visor—something organic and living to remind your eyes that warmth still exists. You will need redundant heating; if one system fails, the cold will find the seam and settle there like a creditor.

**Breath and Buoyancy.**
Standard scuba is laughable here. You need a closed-circuit rebreather rated for extreme depth and *viscous* environments—the water is stratified into layers of differing temperature and salinity that slide past one another with glacial slowness. These strata will shear against your body; you must train to move through them without fighting them. Your buoyancy compensator should allow you to achieve perfect neutral buoyancy at any of the twelve distinct thermoclines you will pass through on descent, but you should also carry weighted boots—magnetic or simply heavy—that allow you to settle onto the floor at elevation 1.2. You will walk here. The water is too thick for swimming to be efficient; it is a medium you push through, not glide within.

**Light and Sight.**
Bring no bright lights. The particulate rain—the constant, silent snow of silt and organic detritus—will turn your beam into a blinding fog. Instead, rely on low-lumen, polarized lamps that cut through the suspension, or better yet, sonar mapping that renders the world in shades of pressure and echo. You will learn to see in negative space: the ruins are defined by the kelp that does not grow on them, by the slightly different way the silt settles on worked stone versus natural stone. Your helmet should have tactile sensors; when your gloved hand brushes the rounded cobbles of the drowned causeways, you should feel the subtle undulations—the ghost of post-and-lintel architecture worn smooth by 2.8 units of deep time.

**The Rope.**
Lay a guide line. Immediately. The horizon curves away into the gloom; this is a planetary surface, a continent of construction, and everything is *made*. There are no natural landmarks—no valleys that are not cisterns, no hills that are not burial mounds of packed clay. The vegetation is a monoculture of patience: kelp forests that sway in currents too weak to register, thick pelts of cold-water moss draping the ruins, fungal colonies spreading in fractal patterns across ancient pavements. It is a jungle without insects, a silence without birds. You will get lost in the green-gray monotony. Anchor a luminescent tether to a prominent hummock—a foundation rise, a silted threshold—and never let it leave your hand. The fauna level is 0.4; nothing will eat you, but nothing will find you either if you wander.

**Time and Mind.**
Prepare psychologically for the *dynamic of 0.6*. Everything here happens in slow time. A stone settles an inch in a decade. A kelp frond unfurls over a season. When you move, you will disturb the silt, and it will take hours to resettle into its continuous, muffled rain. You must move with the patience of the place itself—deliberate, unhurried, accepting that you are a brief, frantic intrusion in a world that has finished its business. The silence is not empty; it is cotton-thick, absorbing vibration, pressing against your eardrums. You will hear only the soft whisper of particulate matter against your suit, the creak of waterlogged timber releasing gas, the distant thump of a moss-heavy overhang collapsing into a hollow. This is the sound of entropy, slow and thorough. Bring audio logs. Record your own voice. The hush of deep water can unmake a mind that is not anchored.

**Supplies.**
Bring more power than you think you need. The cold is hungry and ancient; it will drain batteries with the patience of geology. Bring cutting tools not for defense (there is nothing to defend against), but for clearing the verdant saturation—the 2.4-density of cold-dark vegetation that grows unchecked because there are no grazers, no browsers. The moss will cling to your joints, the kelp will drape over your visor like funeral crepe. You will need to shed it periodically. Bring water purification tablets; the medium is absolute, but it is not potable—it is thick with the chemistry of deep time and the dissolved patience of a drowned civilization.

**What to Carry in Your Chest.**
Carry humility. This is a place where a civilization with stone adzes and fiber ropes overwrote the entire world with their intention, built until no natural substrate remained, and then—whether by flood or subsidence or the opening of some patient engineering—let the water come. You are walking on the final form of construction: the rounded cobble, the polished slab, the near-flattened plain where walls and terraces and causeways have been eroded into something almost geological. You can still feel the intention in the subtle rises and depressions, the alignments of stones that are now merely lines of glacial lumps. It is statistically improbable, this world, and therefore it is holy in its own way.

You are not exploring. You are *witnessing*. A world settled into its oldest stable state, where stone becomes soil becomes fungus becomes kelp in a continuous mat of life draped over the works of hands. Move slowly. Breathe deep. Let the depth-cold press against your skin and remind you that you, too, are temporary—a mayfly in a museum of epochs, suspended in the medium, the matrix, the absolute condition of a patience that outlasts gods.
