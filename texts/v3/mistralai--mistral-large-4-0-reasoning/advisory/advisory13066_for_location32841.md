You must begin by abandoning the assumption that you are a visitor. In this place, you are a chemical event, a pressure differential, a thermal anomaly—a disturbance in a system that has achieved a terrible, patient equilibrium. The city does not see you as a guest; it sees you as a question it has not yet finished asking. To survive, you must prepare not just your body, but your *intent*, your *chemistry*, and your *tempo*.

### The Suit: Becoming Invisible to the Mineral Mind

First, discard any standard environmental gear. The atmosphere is a paradox—low pressure that sits lightly on your ears, yet thick with supercooled humidity that coats every surface in a film of liquid that is neither rain nor dew, but condensation incarnate. Your lungs cannot process this air; the ozone is metallic and corrosive, a byproduct of geothermal violence and exotic physics. You need a **pressure-retentive exoskeleton**, not a soft suit—a rigid, articulated shell that maintains one atmosphere of breathable gas around your body while allowing you to navigate the near-touching vertical canyons. The suit must be transparent in sections, or equipped with wide-angle visors, because you will need to see the bioluminescent pulses that are your only landmarks.

But pressure is only half the battle. The architecture is *smart matter*—programmable mineral matrices that respond to pressure and chemistry. You, as a warm, carbon-based, chemically noisy organism, are a screaming anomaly to this geology. If you step onto the volcanic glass or the sweating facades of the calcified towers, the city will *notice*. It may try to dissolve you, incorporate you into its growing lattice, or simply extrude new mineral around you to immobilize the "error."

Therefore, your suit must be **chemically camouflaged**. You cannot wear synthetic polymers that read as foreign. Instead, you must be sheathed in a living skin—harvested (carefully, reverently) from the bacterial mats that coat the condensation pipes, or woven from the mineral leachate that gives the stone its wet, dark sheen. You must *smell* like the city. Your suit should weep the same supercooled water, at the same temperature, with the same trace minerals. You must become, to the smart matter's senses, a particularly dense and mobile piece of geology. If the buildings sweat, your suit must sweat in sympathy. If the architecture flexes under pressure, your gait must distribute your weight so broadly that you register as tectonic pressure rather than a point load—**snowshoe soles** fused with flexible, glass-resistant polymers, perhaps magnetized if the volcanic substrate proves ferrous, allowing you to grip the jagged scree without triggering the mineral matrices' defensive or assimilative responses.

### The Thermal Lie

The fauna—those pale, translucent, segmented things that press against the skin of the city with a presence scale of 2.7—are not insects, but they are *hungry*. They feed on waste heat, on minerals, on the slow metabolism of the folded-space reservoirs that make water flow uphill. You are a walking furnace, a beacon of infrared radiation in a world of cold stone. If you do not mask your thermal signature, you will be descended upon, not as prey, but as a *resource*—a mobile geothermal vent to be mined.

You must wear a **thermal cloak**—not merely insulation, but active masking. Perhaps a suit lined with conduits that siphon the same zero-point fluctuations powering the city, using that exotic energy to power a cloaking field that renders your body heat invisible, or redirects it into the surrounding rock. Alternatively, you must move so slowly, so glacially, that your metabolic heat dissipates into the ambient steam before it can attract attention. You must learn to *be* cold, to match the temperature of the supercooled water film that coats everything, so that you are no warmer than the stone itself.

### The Senses: Navigating the Absolute Dark and the Wet Roar

The darkness here is not the absence of light, but a *presence*—a polar night compressed by a ceiling of geothermal steam. It is absolute, yet not empty. It is filled with the slow-drifting bodies of the fauna, with the bruised glow of vents far below, with the faint bioluminescent pulses of the city itself.

You cannot carry a bright light. It would be a sunrise in a cave, blinding and dangerous. It would attract things, or disrupt the delicate chemosynthetic ecology. Instead, you need **low-light amplification that feeds on the bioluminescence**, or better, **active sonar and lidar**—mapping the dense, vertical urban lattice by sound and laser, since GPS is useless in this canyon of smart matter that grows and shifts while you watch. The architecture is embryonic, perpetually under construction; new layers extrude from the raw rock even as you stand there. You need a constantly updating 3D map, because the path you took an hour ago may now be a wall.

Your ears are under assault. The sound is a constant, wet roar—a white noise of dripping water, hissing vents, and the wet, resonant chorus of a million segmented legs clicking and chittering in the dark. It is not merely loud; it is a *pressure* against the eardrums, a physical force that can cause damage. You need **active noise cancellation** or physical acoustic dampeners, but you cannot become deaf. The clicking of the fauna is their language, their warning system. You need **bone-conduction receivers** that bypass the damaged air-conduction of your ears, translating the vibrations of the city into intelligible data within your skull, while protecting the delicate membranes from the acoustic pressure.

### Movement: The Glacial Imperative

You must unlearn human speed. Here, time moves at the pace of growing crystal. The creatures drift through the mist in synchronized waves, their movement so gradual you might mistake them for sediment. If you walk at a human pace—if you rush, if you hurry, if you panic—you will create a shockwave in their density, a tsunami in their slow tide. You will be buried, not by malice, but by physics, by the 2.7 scale of their presence pressing against your suit, filling your airlocks, seeking the warmth of your joints.

You must move at **geological speed**. One step per minute. One breath per step. You must synchronize your gait with the drift of the steam, with the slow flex of the fauna. You must become patient to the point of meditation. Impatience here is fatal; the city has all the time in the universe, and it is still deciding the final shape of its mountains. You are the temporary thing. Act accordingly.

### Hydration and the Uphill Water

Do not drink the water. It is supercooled, saturated with minerals leached from raw geology, and potentially alive with bacterial mats. It flows uphill, defying gravity, powered by folded-space reservoirs—drinking it would be like drinking liquid physics, a chemical soup that might crystallize in your veins or rewrite your cellular structure. You need a **closed-loop hydration system** within your suit, recycling your own sweat and breath condensation. The supercooled film on your visor is not for drinking; it is a hazard. Your suit's exterior must be hydrophobic, or constantly heated just enough to prevent the water from freezing onto your optics, while the interior remains warm and dry.

### The Psychological Armor

Prepare your mind for the **temporal vertigo**. You will feel the urge to run, to escape the density, the dark, the wet roar. You must suppress it. You must learn to perceive time as the city does—as a slow pulse, a geothermal heartbeat. Centuries pass in the blink of a vent. You are a mayfly in a cathedral.

Prepare for the **claustrophobia of life**. There is no green here, no photosynthesis, no gentle breeze of leaves. Instead, there is an overwhelming, saturating presence of alien biology—2.7 on the scale of being. They will touch you. They will brush against your suit with a million legs, pale and translucent, segmented and clicking. You will feel them on your skin, even through the armor. You must accept this intimacy. You are not alone; you are *besieged* by life, but not by malice—by ecology. You are the new mineral in their diet, the new heat source in their dark. Learn to breathe in rhythm with their chittering. Learn to see the beauty in the calcified coral of the architecture, in the frozen lightning of the towers.

### Emergency Protocols

Carry a **chemical distress flare** that mimics the death-pheromones of the local fauna, or the mineral signature of a landslide, should the smart matter decide you are a defect to be dissolved. Carry **cutting tools** made of obsidian or harder—volcanic glass is sharper than steel, and you may need to carve an escape route through the scree or the soft, growing mineral matrices if the city begins to enclose you.

Most importantly, carry **patience**. It is your only weapon against a world that moves at the speed of erosion reversed, that grows rather than rises, that dreams in the dark while it builds itself from the mountain up. You are visiting a place that is still being born. Treat it as you would treat a cradle—with reverence, with slowness, and with the full understanding that you are the temporary thing, and it is the eternal.
