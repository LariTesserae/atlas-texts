You are going to a place that has not finished deciding what it is. The water came first, and it remembers; the stone is still learning to be solid. As an ordinary human, you are a fragile, temporary thing—warm-blooded, air-breathing, accustomed to light falling from above and ground that stays where you put it. This world offers none of those comforts. To survive here, and to truly *visit* rather than merely endure, you must prepare not just your pack, but your physiology, your perception, and your sense of time itself.

Here is how you prepare.

### 1. The Lungs of Liquid Air
The atmosphere at elevation 2.4 is not air in the way you know it. It is a warm, thick soup—2.5 degrees on the world’s scale, bath-temperature, saturated to the point of liquid density. When you inhale, you are drawing in water as much as oxygen. Ordinary lungs will flood; you will feel the drowning panic of a man submerged, even as you stand on the terraces.

**Prepare thus:**
- **Acquire a positive-pressure rebreather** with an aquamembrane filter—one that extracts dissolved oxygen from humid air while venting the excess moisture as vapor. Train with it in steam rooms until you can breathe without conscious effort; your body must learn that wet air is not death.
- **Condition your respiratory system** for high humidity. The constant drizzle means your airways will be perpetually coated. Bring saline rinses and expect to cough up condensation. Do not fight the wetness; adapt to it.
- **Pack a desiccant suit lining.** Even with a mask, the humidity will seep through every seam. Wear a base layer of hydrophobic, quick-drying material that wicks moisture away from your skin to prevent maceration and fungal infection. You will be wet always. Learn to be comfortable being wet.

### 2. The Song That Lives in Your Teeth
The unweathered obsidian and basalt do not merely exist; they *sing*. When the warm rain strikes the glass-sharp stone, it creates low, resonant tones—a geological hum at frequencies felt in the teeth, vibrating through the cliffs and into your bones. It is beautiful, but it is also infrasound, and prolonged exposure will cause nausea, disorientation, and potentially micro-fractures in your dental enamel.

**Prepare thus:**
- **Wear bone-conduction dampeners.** Not simple earplugs, but active acoustic shields that cancel the specific low frequencies of the singing stone (likely in the 15–40 Hz range). Carry spare batteries; the dampeners must never fail.
- **Use a dental splint.** A custom-molded mouthguard worn during waking hours will protect your teeth from resonant damage. Remove it only to eat, and eat quickly.
- **Train in infrasound chambers** before departure. Learn to recognize the onset of vertigo from vibration. When the stone sings loudest—during heavy rain or when the tide presses against the peaks—you must know your limits and retreat to higher ground where the vibration dissipates.

### 3. The Inverted Sky
The light here is wrong. It comes from below. Billions of phosphorescent organisms drift in the green-tinged depths, casting an upside-down twilight upward. Shadows fall *upward*. The sky is a rumor of brightness obscured by vapor. Your brain, evolved to expect overhead illumination, will rebel. You will misjudge depth, stumble on flat ground, and feel a profound, vertiginous wrongness.

**Prepare thus:**
- **Undergo visual reorientation training.** Spend weeks in environments where light sources are exclusively from below. Learn to navigate by the bioluminescent glow of the plankton and the verdant pulse of the plant-sap. Your eyes must learn that "up" is shadow and "down" is light.
- **Equip "reorientation goggles"**—not to flip the image (that will confuse you further), but to enhance the low-light spectrum and provide depth-cues via sonar or structured light. The dimness is absolute; bring low-light amplification tuned to the green-blue spectrum of the deep.
- **Accept the slowness of sight.** The vegetation grows with visible patience (1.8 abundance). Train yourself to watch things grow. Your eyes will adjust to the dimness, but your mind must adjust to the lack of solar markers for time.

### 4. The Glass Mountain
The peaks are young, raw, unweathered. Obsidian and basalt cliffs are glass-sharp, ringing, and still settling. There is no soil, only a desperate film of lichen. The stone is *emerging*—rising, shifting, deciding its geometry. A decade here is a moment; a century is the span in which a cliff-face visibly moves. The ground beneath the city of Vorn is a temporary agreement, not a foundation.

**Prepare thus:**
- **Abandon conventional climbing gear.** Ropes will be shredded by glass-sharp edges. Carry **cut-resistant everything**: suits woven with spectra-fiber or ceramic thread, gloves with diamond-impregnated palms, boots with soles of advanced composite that can grip ringing basalt without slipping.
- **Use magnetic assistance if possible.** The young basalt may contain ferrous minerals. Magnetic climbing boots could allow you to adhere to vertical faces where friction fails. Test this before you rely on it.
- **Never build permanent shelter.** The mountains rise. What is a ledge today may be a cliff tomorrow. Use the suspension bridges of woven vine-fiber, sleep in hammocks lashed to growing timber, and expect to move your camp every few years—or accept that the locals will rebuild around you as the stone lifts their terraces skyward.

### 5. The Thirst of the Drowned
You can drown in the air, yet thirst. The water is everywhere—2.9 on the scale of presence—warm, luminous, and full of drifting life. But the sea is thick with plankton and unknown chemistry; drinking it unfiltered may poison you or fill you with microscopic life that is not meant for human guts. Meanwhile, the mist is pure, but you must know how to drink it.

**Prepare thus:**
- **Master mist-harvesting.** Your suit should have condenser panels—surfaces cooled slightly below the ambient temperature (perhaps using Peltier elements or chemical cooling packs) that cause the saturated air to weep droplets. Collect this distilled water in clay or metal cisterns.
- **Bring broad-spectrum purification.** If you must drink from the sea (and you may, in desperation), you need filters that remove not just particulates but the phosphorescent plankton, and chemical treatments to neutralize any toxins. Assume all water is biologically active until tested.
- **Learn to thirst.** In this place, thirst is a discipline. The warm rain will soak you, but it will not hydrate you unless captured. Carry a hydration pack, but learn to sip from the condensation on your own skin and gear. You must become efficient at extracting water from the world that surrounds you.

### 6. The Patience of Geology
Time moves at 1.1 here. It is geological time made visible. The society of Vorn measures life in the slow cycles of a world learning to be solid. They hunt with stone-tipped spears, fish with woven reed traps, and cultivate fungus in dark crevices. They do not use wheels; there are no forge-fires. The sound level is 1.3—subdued, intimate. The air is mostly quiet, filled only with the whisper of rain-mist, the creak of swelling timber, and the lowing of water against stone.

**Prepare thus:**
- **Train in temporal dilation.** Spend time in isolation chambers, in meditation, in environments with no clocks. Learn to exist without the urgency of the ticking second. You will need to match their rhythm; haste here is violence against the stone.
- **Pack for psychological endurance.** Bring books, music, games—but also bring the capacity for silence. The quiet is profound. The density of the city is high (1.6 presences per volume), yet it is silent of industry. Learn to move without sound, to speak only when necessary, to respect the acoustic intimacy of the place.
- **Bring trade goods of patience.** They value copper—hammered flat with stone tools. Bring copper wire, copper sheets, perhaps seeds (for there is little soil, and they cultivate in crevices). Do not bring plastics that will never degrade; this world has 0.4 erosion, and your trash will outlast the mountains. Bring knowledge, stories, and respect for impermanence.

### 7. The Green Dark and the Deep
The vegetation is lush—translucent, broad-leafed plants with bioluminescent sap pulsing in rhythm with the plankton below. Vines thicker than a man’s waist drape between cliffs, knitting geology into a breathing organism. The plants grow in impossible geometries, following the magnetic pull of the rising stone.

**Prepare thus:**
- **Carry a resonant blade.** A machete or kukri made of folded steel (or local copper if you trade for it) to cut through the tough vine-fibers. The plants grow faster than the stone; paths will close behind you.
- **Respect the fauna.** It is sparse (1.2 presence), but what exists is strange. Silent, winged things nest in the highest crags—their feathers are translucent, like the leaves. In the depths move great serpentine forms, glimpsed only as shadows against the glowing water. **Do not enter the water.** The sea is 2.4 deep at its high-water mark, and those shapes move with the slowness of the landscape itself. Use sonar if you must navigate near the shore, and never swim.
- **Test the bioluminescence.** The sap in the leaves pulses with the plankton’s rhythm. It may be harmless, or it may be a neurotoxin, or a hallucinogen. Touch nothing without testing. The mist itself may carry spores. Wear a filtered mask even when not using the rebreather, to avoid inhaling the green-dark.

### 8. The Final Preparation: Becoming Temporary
Above all, you must prepare to be impermanent. The people of Vorn treat the ground beneath their feet as a temporary agreement. They re-lay foundations each decade as the mountain shifts. They do not build to last; they build to adapt.

**Before you go:**
- **Shed your expectation of permanence.** You will not leave a mark that remains. The stone will forget you, or the water will claim your bones, or the rising rock will lift your grave skyward. This is not nihilism; it is participation in a world that is still being born.
- **Learn to listen to the stone.** Place your ear against the obsidian (carefully—it is glass-sharp). Feel the hum. Understand that you are visiting a place where the water came first, and the stone is merely deciding what shape to hold. You are a guest in a nursery.
- **Prepare to change.** When you return—if you return—you will move slower. You will find ordinary air thin, ordinary light harsh, ordinary time frantic. You will carry the green-blue dimness in your eyes, and the song of the stone in your teeth. You will thirst for mist, and drown in dry air.

Go carefully. The mountains are still rising. The water remembers when there was nothing here but light and water, and it waits, with infinite patience, to see what you will become.
