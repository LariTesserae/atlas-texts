**A Field Guide for the Breathless: Preparing for the Mnemosyne Plateau**

You are going to a place that has forgotten how to sweat. You are going where the air itself is a mineral, where silence has weight, and where the ground is dreaming you into its architecture whether you consent or not. This is not a journey for the careless. An ordinary human—flesh, blood, water-dependent, reliant on Euclidean geometry and audible speech—must undergo a metamorphosis of preparation before setting foot on the salt-pumice crust. Below is the protocol for survival, sanity, and return.

---

### I. The Apparatus of Breath and Pressure

The atmosphere here is a *metallic absence*—thin enough to taste the void on your tongue, a flavor like licking a battery in a high wind. At this altitude, the horizon curves away like the inside of a bowl, and the pressure differential will attempt to pull the fluid from your lungs and the moisture from your eyes.

**You must bring:**
- **A pressure-suit with active equalization valves.** Not a simple oxygen mask—a full environmental envelope that maintains 1 atm against the exterior vacuum-thin air. The suit must be rated for *desiccation*; the anhydrous environment will pull water from your skin through any permeable membrane. Seal every orifice.
- **A closed-loop hydration system.** There is no water here. No rivers, no rain in epochs beyond counting. You must carry every molecule you will ever drink, sweat, or excrete, and recycle it with 99.8% efficiency. Dehydration at this altitude is not thirst—it is a rapid crystallization of your own tissues.
- **Mineral filters with copper and sulfur scrubbers.** The wind carries dust that tastes of copper and old bone. Inhale it, and you will coat your alveoli in heavy metals and desiccated organic particulates. Your suit’s intake valves must be HEPA-mineral grade, capable of filtering particulates smaller than red blood cells. Expectorate frequently; the dust will accumulate in your throat like ash.

**The temperature** is a perpetual 10°C, but it is the cool of old stone—a thermal inertia that never warms, never quite freezes. It will seep into your marrow. Pack insulation that operates on radiant barriers, not loft. You need to trap your own heat against a thermal sink that has been cooling since before your species existed.

### II. The Management of Silence and Subsonic Resonance

The silence here is rated **0.3**—an absolute, a pressure in the ears that feels like diving to the bottom of a dry ocean. Sound does not travel; it dies in the thin air. What little noise exists is *subsonic*—a tectonic grinding, the whisper of dust against stone, frequencies felt in the sternum rather than heard.

**You must bring:**
- **Otic pressure-regulators.** Devices that equalize the pressure in your middle ear against the exterior void, preventing ruptured eardrums and the vertigo of silence.
- **Bone-conduction dampeners.** The plateau *breathes*. The spires resonate at frequencies below hearing, a thrum that travels up through the salt crust and into your teeth, your sternum, your femurs. Left unchecked, this resonance will induce nausea, dissociation, and eventually, a kind of lithic hypnosis. Wear dampeners on your mastoid processes—felt, not heard, but neutralized.
- **A communication system that does not rely on air.** Radios will fail. Sound carries poorly. Instead, carry **tactile transmitters**—devices that convert speech into subsonic pulses through your suit’s contact points, or visual displays that project text into your helmet’s eye-line. Learn to read lips; your companions will be shouting into the void, and you will only see their mouths move.

### III. Navigation in a Geometry That Lies

The Weirdness here is **2.9**—a reality breakdown. The geometry is *wrong*. Staircases ascend forty steps and deposit you at the base. Courtyards are larger on the inside than their exterior walls allow. Shadows fall at angles that defy the overhead light (which is a dim, directionless **1.4** on the luminescence scale—shadowless, soft, a gradient of ash and pearl).

**You must bring:**
- **A reality anchor.** A physical object that exists in baseline reality and resists local distortion. A lead-weighted compass from Earth (though the needle may spin uselessly), a photograph laminated in diamond-hard polymer, or a mathematical constant carved into a titanium tablet. Hold it when the walls begin to breathe. It will remind your brain that up is up, that distance is linear, that you are not falling into the stone.
- **Lidar or active sonar.** With no shadows to judge depth, your eyes will lie. The light is directionless; edges are soft. You need active scanning to map the true topography, to see the staircases that loop, the canyons that are streets, the plazas that are flat and sunken.
- **Trail markers that the stone cannot eat.** The Drift-Kith secrete enzymes that dissolve pumice. Rope will rot. Paint will be consumed. Instead, carry **resonance beacons**—small devices that emit a specific subsonic frequency. The stone may remember (it is *mnemosyne-mineral*), but it cannot erase a vibration. Place them every fifty meters. If the city shifts while you sleep, you will hear them singing through your bone-conduction dampeners, guiding you back.
- **A sleep protocol.** *Never* sleep without anchoring your shelter. The walls slide along hidden tracks of quartz. The street plan reconfigures while you dream. You could wake up entombed in a newly secreted chamber, or find that the path you took to the plaza yesterday is now solid rock. Anchor your tent to bedrock with pitons that drill into the tectonic plate itself, not the weathered crust. Set alarms. Check your exits every hour.

### IV. The Biological Hazards of Dust and Spore

The ground is a crust of salt and pumice, pale and porous. It is dry—desiccated, anhydrous, a chemistry without water. But it is not sterile.

**You must bring:**
- **A sealed suit with positive pressure.** The dust is not merely abrasive; it is *alive* in the way chemistry can be alive. It carries spores of fungal colonies that cling to stone overhangs—vegetation rated **0.5**, a biology of crust and lichen, no green, no photosynthesis, just slow metabolism of rock and gas. These spores may be alien, may colonize your lungs, may dream in your nervous system. Keep the suit sealed. Decontaminate before entering any airlock.
- **Chelation therapy.** The dust tastes of copper and old bone. Heavy metal poisoning is a certainty without prophylaxis. Carry chelating agents—pills or IV drips—that bind to heavy metals and flush them from your bloodstream. Expect your sweat to turn blue-green.
- **No organic food.** There is no photosynthesis here. No green. Bring nutrient paste, hydroponic algae bricks, or whatever sustains you in the void between worlds. Do not attempt to forage. The lichen is not food; it is architecture.

### V. Preparation for the Drift-Kith and the Hollow Parliament

The inhabitants are rare (**1.4**)—tall, impossibly thin, crystalline entities of silicon and quartz, moving on dozens of jointed legs like collapsed wheel spokes. They glide, frictionless and silent. They feed on mineral dust, sulfur compounds, the slow chemical weathering of stone. They are the architects of this place.

**If you encounter them:**
- **Do not touch their work.** They secrete enzymes that dissolve pumice into chambers, staircases, doorways. Interrupting this process is like stepping on a termite mound. They will not attack you, but your presence may cause them to abandon a chamber, collapsing the local geometry further.
- **Do not cast shadows.** Their bodies refract light into rainbows that have no source. Your shadow—falling at wrong angles, pooling in corners where no darkness should exist—may confuse their crystalline perception. Move slowly. Stay in the dim, directionless glow.

**If you visit the Hollow Parliament** (the massive geode, miles across, at the heart of the sprawl):
- **Upgrade your pressure suit.** The air inside is thinner, colder, more silent. The crystal cathedral is a vacuum of sound and warmth.
- **Bring vibration translators.** The Drift-Kith communicate through seismic vibrations—pressure and resonance traveling through the stone floor and up your legs. You will feel their language in your knees, your hips, your spine. It is a language of tectonic patience. Bring a device that can visualize these vibrations, or you will be deaf to their "dreaming," their maintenance of the city.
- **Stand still for days.** They stand in patterns, motionless, maintaining the city. If you wish to witness this, you must learn stillness. Bring meditation aids. Bring patience. The city is dreamed into continued existence by their silence.

### VI. The Psychological Architecture

Finally, prepare your mind. This place is **dynamic 2.5**—the motion is glacial and silent. The plateau breathes. The city is in perpetual, quiet renovation. Erosion has reached an equilibrium (**2.1**)—established, in-balance—but the stasis is one of active change.

You will experience:
- **Temporal distortion.** The silence and the subsonic hum will alter your perception of time. Hours may feel like minutes; minutes like epochs. Bring a mechanical watch, not digital—electronics may fail in the mineral-rich air. Wind it daily.
- **Loneliness.** You may walk for hours through empty mineral streets without seeing another Drift-Kith. The density of the place is nearly void (**0.2**). You are alone with the stone and the wind. Prepare for isolation.
- **The memory of the stone.** The walls are *mnemosyne-mineral*. They remember. They may remember your footsteps. They may remember your fear. Do not be surprised if, after you leave, the city has grown a chamber in the shape of your silhouette, a doorway where your hand touched the pumice.

### VII. The Final Ritual

Before you step onto the plateau, perform this:
1. Drink a liter of water. You will not feel thirst again for hours, but your body will be screaming for it.
2. Equalize your ears. Feel the pressure release.
3. Touch your reality anchor. Feel its weight, its certainty.
4. Step forward.

The horizon will curve away like the inside of a bowl. The air will taste of metal. The silence will press against your eardrums. And the city will begin to breathe around you, indifferent to your presence, dreaming its mineral dreams, waiting for you to either adapt or be ground into dust—another flavor for the wind, another memory for the stone.

Welcome to the place that forgot how to sweat.
