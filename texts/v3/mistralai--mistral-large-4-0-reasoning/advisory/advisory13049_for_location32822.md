To visit this place—this room-sized wound where the world is still sweating itself into existence—you must prepare not merely your body, but your ontological status. You are entering a space that regards solidity as a kind of violence, that maintains its rawness through an immune system of luminous, undecided fauna, and that treats the human form as a premature fossil. Preparation, here, is a kind of careful unmaking.

**Physiological Recalibration**

First, you must learn to exist without gravity as law. Begin vestibular training in chambers where "down" is a suggestion, where the floor periodically decides to become a wall or the ceiling. Practice falling sideways, then upward, then not falling at all—learning to hover by relaxing the muscles that brace against weight. Your inner ear will scream; you must teach it that orientation is a negotiation. Bring stabilizing gyroscopes, yes, but also train yourself to *want* the disorientation, to find the moment when up and down exchange places not frightening but erotic, a kind of gravitational intimacy.

Your lungs require preparation for an atmosphere that tastes of lightning and wet stone. The air here carries a static charge, the pre-sneeze potential of storm fronts compressed into a cool, mineral breath. Train yourself to inhale ionized air without triggering the cough reflex; the ionization will try to crystallize in your bronchial tubes, forming temporary geology in your chest. Carry a respirator that does not filter the air—you must taste the becoming—but that humidifies it, preventing your own moisture from being stolen by the hungry, porous stone. Practice breath-holding, for your exhalations will be swallowed whole by the geology, absorbed before they can echo. Learn to speak without sound, or carry a vibratory communicator that transmits through bone conduction, for the silence here is not empty but *textured*, a pressure that will crush unprotected eardrums and muffle all acoustic communication.

Your skin must be prepared for contact with surfaces that shift when unobserved. The stone is translucent, weeping condensation that gathers in beads too heavy to fall, suspended in the air like tears that have forgotten gravity. This moisture is not water but *potential*—it will try to dissolve your boundaries, to drink your solidity. Wear a suit that is semi-permeable, a membrane of your own, allowing the place to taste you without consuming you. The fabric should be hydrophobic yet breathable, coated in a film of calcite or salt, so that when the condensation clings to you, it forms a second skin rather than seeping into your pores. Be prepared to feel the walls breathing against your back—an arrhythmic pulse, almost a heartbeat—and do not mistake it for your own.

**Sensory Armor**

Your eyes require the most delicate preparation. The light here is not illumination but *exposure*—the raw, unfiltered radiation of genesis, before the universe decided on day and night. It is harsh, white, and multi-directional. You will cast three shadows, perhaps more, each falling toward different walls, each moving at different speeds. Train yourself to track multiple shadows without vertigo. Wear lenses that polarize at impossible angles, filtering the white light into something your retina can process without being "developed" prematurely. You must not look directly at the shifting surfaces—they will blur, will become negative space, will tempt you to step into areas that are not there. Practice peripheral awareness, learning to see the room in fragments, accepting that the corner you glance at will rearrange itself when you turn your head fully.

The silence is a physical pressure. Prepare earplugs that generate white noise, a counter-pressure against the texture of absolute quiet. The dripping water makes no sound; the translation of wings is inaudible; even your heartbeat will seem to fade into the porous dark. You must learn to hear the silence, to read it like braille against your eardrums, to understand that the quiet is the sound of creation happening too slowly to register.

**Temporal and Spatial Navigation**

You will need a tether that exists in non-Euclidean space. A simple rope will not suffice, for the distance to the far wall expands when you walk toward it and contracts when you retreat. Carry a temporal anchor—a device that pins you to a specific moment of becoming, preventing you from being caught in the loops where the fauna are born, die, and are reborn in cycles without beginning or end. You may see yourself in the corners where time pools, a version of you that is already a fossil, already compressed into the mineral matrix. Do not touch these versions. They are the scaffolding of your future ruin.

Study the doorway that frames not another room but the same room, angled differently. Learn to recognize when you have walked through it and arrived where you began, but upside down. Mark your path with pheromones rather than chalk, for the stone will drink any solid mark. Better yet, memorize the topography of newness—the stalagmites that have forgotten whether they grow up or down, the sparse forest of mineral teeth. Navigate by gradients of potential reality, learning to feel which direction leads toward "more unfinished" and which toward premature solidity.

**Ontological Preparation: Becoming Unfinished**

Most crucially, you must prepare your sense of self. This place maintains its state of becoming through antibodies—the luminous, insectoid fauna that consume the emerging erosion, that drink the condensation before it can fall, that graze on the boundary between formed and unformed. You, as a solid, decided human, are anathema to them. You are too real, too finished, a premature crystallization in their unfinished world.

Therefore, you must practice becoming larval. Carry a device—biological or technological—that temporarily dissolves your edges, that makes you porous, that renders you as potential rather than actual. You must appear to the fauna as a question mark, as a state between egg and form, as something that has not decided what it is yet. If you appear solid, they will descend upon you not as predators but as surgeons, attempting to break you down into raw material, to keep you unformed, to maintain the cave in its state of becoming. Move by translation rather than walking—shift from coordinate to coordinate without ripples, without displacing the moisture, without beating the air with your presence.

Accept that you will be exposed. The light here is photographic, developmental. You will be changed by it, fixed into a new chemical state. Your memories may become fossils of buildings that will exist millions of years hence, leaking backward through the stone. Leave something behind—a shed skin, a thought, a strand of hair—that can serve as a fossil of your visit, a presence of the present pressing against the unformed future. Take nothing that is solid; the structures here are negative space, chairs defined by absence, tables of compressed moisture. You can only carry away the damp, the potential, the sweat of formation.

**Rituals of Entry**

Before you descend below the negotiable surface, practice holding your breath until your lungs feel like the porous stone—hollow, hungry, absorbent. Practice casting multiple shadows by standing in light that comes from everywhere and nowhere. Practice walking toward a wall and finding it receding, then retreating and finding it advancing. Practice being silent until the silence becomes a texture, a pressure, a second atmosphere.

Bring a doorway—not a physical one, but a conceptual frame—that you can carry with you, so that when you find the doorway that leads back into the same room, you have something to step through that is truly *elsewhere*, a membrane of your own making that separates you from the infinite recursion of the unfinished dark.

And bring gentleness. This is a wound in the earth, a pocket of pain where the world is being born. Treat it as you would treat a newborn—with awe, with delicacy, with the understanding that your presence is an intrusion into a gestation, and that you must leave no scar, only the faintest suggestion that a traveler once passed through, becoming, for a moment, as unfinished as the stone itself.
