To visit the Condensation Scarp is to agree to be *unmade* and *remade* slowly, over the course of days that may not follow one another. You are not preparing for a hike; you are preparing for an apprenticeship in impermanence. The air at four thousand meters does not merely thin—it *hesitates*, thick with a humidity that has no weather, only the exhalation of a planet still deciding its shape. An ordinary human traveler must prepare not just their body, but their ontological expectations, because here the ground is a suggestion and time is a local ordinance.

### I. The Body: Becoming Porous

**Altitude and the Wet Lung.** Acclimatize as you would for the Himalayas, but understand that the condensation will betray you. The moisture here is not rain; it is *potential* water, squeezed from cooling magma and atmospheric indecision. It will seep into your alveoli like a memory. Bring a portable oxygen concentrator that works in high humidity—most fail when the air itself is wet—and a nebulizer loaded with surfactants to keep your lungs from drowning in the perpetual drizzle. Train your cardiovascular system not for oxygen debt, but for *viscosity*; the air is cool enough to ache, but heavy with suspended droplets that resist each breath.

**The Suit of Second Skins.** You cannot wear "waterproof" gear; you need *condensation-permeable* architecture. Pack a suit lined with hydrophobic membranes that allow your body heat to vent while preventing the external mist from colonizing your skin. The dampness is absolute—it will find the seam of your glove, the zipper of your pack. Wear garments of treated moss-fiber (harvested from a stable timeline, not the local flora, which is too embryonic to be reliable) that breathe but do not absorb. Your boots must be the opposite: heavy, magnetic-soled, with cleats that can grip stone that is still learning to be solid. Expect to sink. The moss carpets are deceptively deep, and the basalt beneath flows like cold honey in places. You need boots that distribute your weight across a meter of surface area, or you will find yourself ankle-deep in a valley that is actively dreaming itself into a canyon.

**Thermal Layering for the Bruised Twilight.** The light is 0.6—perpetual bruised dusk. There is no solar warmth to speak of, only the diffuse glow behind the cloud membrane. Pack phase-change thermal packs that activate at 12°C; the temperature is cool, but the humidity makes it feel like a cold bath. However, do not overdress—you will be moving through steam vents and condensation harvesters that emit heat in slow, geological pulses. You want to be *cool and damp*, never hot and sweaty, for sweat in this atmosphere condenses instantly on your skin and freezes into a rime that the local fauna find fascinating.

### II. Reality Anchors: Staying Rendered

The weirdness here is 2.6—high enough that you may phase out of sync with the scenery. You must carry **ontological ballast**.

**The Anchor Stone.** Bring a rock from a finished world. Not a crystal, not a gem—something *eroded*. A river stone from Earth, smooth and ancient, worn by water that has a source and a destination. Hold it when you feel yourself flickering. The Condensation Scarp is still rendering; your anchor reminds the local reality that you are a completed thought. Carry it in a lead-lined pouch (the rust-writing on local metal emits low-level ontological radiation) and touch it when buildings appear in two places at once, or when you catch yourself walking through a wall that remembers being magma.

**Temporal Dampeners.** Time pools near condensation. You may stand beside a mist-bank and watch your own hand age, or de-age, or simply *repeat* an action until the mist shifts. Pack a chronometer that does not rely on pendulums or quartz—mechanical watches here run at the speed of tectonic settling. Instead, carry a **resonance watch**: a device that hums at 432 Hz, a frequency that seems to stabilize local time dilation. When the factory hum (which vibrates in your teeth at a subsonic level) begins to harmonize with your heartbeat in a way that makes you nauseous, press the resonance watch to your sternum. It will not stop time from pooling, but it will prevent you from being trapped in a loop where you watch yourself arrive, over and over, like a film strip burning.

**Phase-Shift Goggles.** The fauna phase slightly out of sync—they exist 0.3 seconds before they appear, or after they disappear. To an ordinary human, they will seem to flicker, to blink in and out of existence. This is disorienting, but dangerous: if you cannot see them coming, you cannot avoid them. Pack goggles with a slight temporal lag—military-grade night vision with a 0.5 second delay buffer. You will see the creature as it *was* half a second ago, giving you time to step aside as it phases *into* the present. Also, the goggles will help you see the **inverted lakes**—pockets of mist that pool in the air, heavy with water that falls upward. These are gravity eddies. Avoid them, or if you must pass through, do so quickly; standing in an inverted lake is standing in a time eddy.

### III. Navigation: Mapping the Unmapped

Do not bring a map. The city is sparse (density 1.4) but *sprawling*, a nervous system of nodes connected by bridges that span chasms still widening. The architecture compensates with flexible joints and adjustable stilts, meaning buildings move. A factory that is here at dawn may be three meters to the left by dusk, or it may be still under construction, scaffolding visible where there was a smokestack yesterday.

**The Condensation Compass.** Navigate by the mist. The condensation follows patterns—updrafts where the stone weeps, downdrafts where the earth inhales. Learn to read the *flow* of the moisture. Where mist flows upward, you are near a vent; where it pools like a slow tide, you are in a valley of fluctuating gravity. Carry a hygrometer that measures not just humidity but *directionality*—which way the water wants to go. This will keep you from walking into a gravity well where the ground is still liquid.

**The Rust Reader.** Metal here rusts in patterns that resemble writing. This is not corrosion; it is *communication*. The young earth is trying to speak. Bring a portable spectrometer and a translation algorithm trained on pre-formation languages—proto-scripts, geological graffiti. The rust will warn you of sinkholes, of time pools, of fauna nesting in the cooling vents. Do not touch it with bare skin; the writing can be infectious, causing your own skin to develop temporary topographies that mirror the landscape.

**The Slow Pace Protocol.** Move at 0.5 kilometers per hour. The steam engines turn with the patience of glaciers; gears mesh in rhythms that match tectonic plates. If you walk faster, you will outpace the rendering of reality. You may find yourself arriving at a bridge before it has finished being built, or walking through a smelter that has not yet condensed from steam into stone. Match your footsteps to the settling of the earth—one step per millimeter of crustal adjustment. This sounds absurd, but it is the only way to stay *on* the map.

### IV. Provisions: Eating the Unformed

**Water.** Do not drink the condensation. It has no origin and no destination; it is the sweat of a planet that has not decided if it is ocean or mountain. It may contain prebiotic compounds, or it may simply unmake your cellular structure. Bring sealed water from a timeline where H2O is stable, enough for three times your expected stay—time here is negotiable, and you may be trapped for weeks in what feels like hours.

**Food.** The local flora feed on mineral seepage and atmospheric water. You cannot eat them; they are too new, too *draft*. They lack the nutritional completeness of evolved plants. Pack protein bricks and electrolyte gels, but also pack **spore supplements**. When you walk through the moss, you will release clouds of faintly glowing spores. Do not filter them all out. Inhale a measured dose—these spores contain enzymes that help human lungs process the thin, wet air. They are symbiotic, a gift from the embryonic biosphere. But do not overdose; too many spores and you may begin to photosynthesize, which is disorienting and eventually fatal.

**Medicine.**
- **Anti-temporal drugs:** Medications that prevent cellular aging or regression when near condensation pools.
- **Gravity sickness tablets:** For the nausea that comes when gravity fluctuates in the lower valleys.
- **Phase-stabilizers:** Pills that keep your atoms vibrating in sync with the local reality, preventing you from walking through a wall that hasn't decided to be solid yet.
- **Antibiotics:** The fauna are abundant because evolution is rushing; they carry primordial pathogens that your immune system has never seen. Also, the fungi are translucent and filled with mist—do not let them touch broken skin.

### V. The Sound: Protecting the Mind

The silence here is loud. The wind shrieks through the sparse structures. The fauna fill the mist with calls that do not echo correctly, that seem to come from slightly before they are made. This is not a malfunction of your ears; it is the sound of reality *testing* itself, a pre-echo of causality.

**Sonic Dampeners.** Bring earplugs that filter frequencies below 20 Hz—the factory hum that vibrates in your teeth. But do not block everything. You need to hear the fauna approaching, the creak of a bridge that is widening beneath you. Use bone-conduction headphones that play a low, steady drone (the "glacial rhythm"—one tone every 4.7 seconds) to mask the pre-echoes. This will prevent the auditory hallucinations that come from hearing sounds before they happen.

**The Loud Silence.** Be prepared for the psychological weight of the quiet between sounds. In high altitudes, silence is expected; here, silence is *active*, a held breath. It will feel like the world is listening to you. Do not whisper secrets. The young earth is still learning language, and it might adopt your words as its own.

### VI. Interaction Protocols

**The Fauna.** They are curious, not hostile. They have too many joints, eyes that reflect impossible colors, bodies that phase slightly out of sync. Do not run—running suggests you are prey, or worse, that you are *solid* in a way that threatens their embryonic existence. Move slowly. Offer them moisture—pour a small amount of your sealed water onto the stone near them. They are drawn to the new stone, to places where the world is still soft. If you offer water, you acknowledge their reality, and they will acknowledge yours. Do not touch them. Their bodies are first drafts; contact may cause you to *edit* yourself, to lose a finger that hasn't finished growing, or to gain a joint you didn't ask for.

**The Industry.** Respect the slowness. The steam engines are dangerous not because they are fast, but because they are *inevitable*. A gear that turns once a day will still crush you if you stand in its path. When you see a factory humming—that low thrum that vibrates in your teeth—do not approach the machinery. The condensation harvesters are safer; they are designed to collect the hanging moisture. You may touch their collection surfaces, but expect your hand to come away damp with water that feels slightly *wrong*, like it remembers being magma.

**The Buildings.** They exist in superposition. A processing plant may be finished, unfinished, and non-existent simultaneously. Do not enter a building unless you have seen it *become* solid. Knock three times; if the sound echoes correctly (not before, not after, but *with*), the building is currently real. If the echo comes early, wait. If it comes late, the building is remembering being a blueprint. Step inside only when the echo is present-tense.

### VII. Emergency Procedures

**If Gravity Inverts:** You are in a lower valley near a condensation pool. Lie flat, spread your limbs, and become as horizontal as possible. Do not try to stand; you will fall upward into the mist, or sideways into a chasm. Wait for the fluctuation to pass—it lasts only seconds, but those seconds are long enough to be swept into an inverted lake.

**If You Begin to Phase:** You will feel yourself becoming translucent, two places at once. Immediately grasp your anchor stone. If you do not have it, find the nearest metal surface and read the rust-writing. The language of corrosion is a stabilizing force; it reminds you that you are made of iron and time, not mist. Recite your name aloud. The sound of your voice, correctly echoed, will pull you back into a single frame of existence.

**If the Ground Swallows You:** The moss is deep, the stone is honey. If your boots sink past the ankle, do not struggle. Struggling makes the earth remember being liquid, and it will flow around you, trapping you. Instead, lie back, spread your weight, and *wait*. The earth is still dreaming itself into shape; it will eventually decide you are a feature, not a flaw. Call for help on your resonance frequency. The locals—human or otherwise—understand that being swallowed is temporary. It may take days, but the mountain will cough you up, or grow around you, incorporating you into its geology. This is not death; it is *accelerated fossilization*. Accept it.

**If You See Yourself:** The time pools can show you versions of yourself that have not happened yet, or have already happened. Do not interact with these echoes. They are drafts. Wave, but do not speak. If your echo speaks first, you are in a time loop. Break it by performing an action that is geologically improbable—sing a song from a dead timeline, or recite prime numbers backward. This disrupts the local causality enough to snap you back to the present.

### VIII. The Final Preparation: Becoming Unfinished

Pack light. The weight of certainty is too heavy for thin air. Leave behind your watch, your schedule, your expectation that the world is *done*. Bring only what can bend: flexible morals, permeable boundaries, a mind that can hold paradox without breaking.

When you arrive, stand at the edge of the scar. Do not step forward immediately. Let the condensation settle on your shoulders. Let the factory hum find your teeth. Let the bruised twilight teach your eyes to see in 0.6 light. You are entering a place that is still being born. To survive, you must agree to be born again, slowly, in the image of the mist.

Walk slowly. Breathe the wet. Hold your anchor stone. And remember: here, to stand still is to sink; to move is to become part of the becoming.

Welcome to the Condensation Scarp. The world is still learning how to hold you.
