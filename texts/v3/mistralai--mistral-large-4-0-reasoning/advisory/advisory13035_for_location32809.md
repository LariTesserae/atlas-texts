To step into the Condensation is to accept that your body is no longer a boundary but a filter—to prepare, you must first surrender the notion that you can remain dry, warm, or temporally coherent. This is not a place you visit with luggage; it is a place you enter with a specific kind of emptiness.

**Prepare Your Flesh for the Second Skin**

Your clothing must abandon the architecture of “outside” and “inside.” Bring no cotton—it will become a cold, sodden weight that never dries, a shroud of hypothermia disguised as comfort. Instead, wear layers of hydrophobic silk or the synthetic equivalent, topped with a shell that does not repel water but channels it, directing the constant condensation into rivulets that run down your spine rather than pooling against your skin. The inhabitants wear blue-gray furs; consider dyeing your outer layer to match, not for camouflage, but for thermal compatibility—the color seems to absorb the wrong light in a way that lessens its pressure.

Your skin will be assaulted by a humidity that behaves like a caress and a drowning simultaneously. Anoint yourself daily with lanolin or a thick, waxy balm, not to waterproof yourself, but to create a sacrificial layer that the moisture can saturate while your actual dermis remains protected from the “intimate” cold that seeps into the marrow. The air tastes of metal and old snow; bring a respirator with a heated element, not for warmth, but to prevent your breath from crystallizing into a cloud that will obscure your vision in the already oppressive density. You will be breathing a soup. Let your lungs learn to swim.

**Shield Your Eyes from the Weight of Light**

The sun here does not shine; it presses. It is a flattened, merciless white that flattens perspective and presses against the eyeballs with physical mass. Ordinary sunglasses are useless. You need goggles—sealed, tinted amber or deep slate—something that will filter the actinic violence and restore a semblance of depth to a world that otherwise renders everything in cruel, two-dimensional relief. Bring a spare pair, and a cloth to wipe the condensation that will fog them instantly. You will be tempted to remove them to “see better”; do not. The light will burn the moisture from your corneas and leave you seeing only the shadows that move independently of their sources, sketching architectures that do not yet exist and driving you mad with anticipatory vertigo.

**Prepare for the Silence**

The atmosphere here is an acoustic vacuum. Sound does not travel; it is absorbed by the saturated air, leaving only the percussion of dripping water, the wet creak of timber, and the thunder of your own blood. This is not peaceful silence; it is a sensory deprivation that will unravel your mind if you are not prepared. Before you go, practice sitting in an anechoic chamber, or at least in a room with the refrigerator unplugged and the windows sealed. Learn to hear your heartbeat without panic. Learn to move your lips without making sound, for speech here is a violence that disturbs the equilibrium.

Consider bringing a single sound-making device—a music box, a bell, a clicker—sealed in wax and kept in your pocket. Do not use it unless you feel yourself dissociating, unless the silence becomes so absolute that you forget you have a body. Then, and only then, make one small noise, a reminder that you are a temporal creature, and immediately reseal the device.

**Compress Your Existence**

You cannot bring a staff, a rifle, or anything that requires swinging. The corridors are shoulder-width; the density is oppressive; near-touching is the only possible state. Pack everything into a satchel that can be worn across the chest, keeping your hands free for crawling, gripping, and steadying yourself against walls that weep amber resin. The rock is wet soap; your boots must have felt soles or cork, something that grips when saturated. Bring crampons, but small ones, for the ice that forms on leeward surfaces in lace-like patterns that grow and melt in cycles too rapid to track.

Learn to move sideways. Practice turning in place without moving your arms. Learn to breathe shallowly, to take up as little volume as possible in a world where every cubic meter contains too much presence. You will brush against the translucent fauna—the gelatinous gardeners. They will touch your face with a cold that is not unkind, only absolute. Do not flinch. Do not grab. They are secreting calcium, filtering the air, dying to become mortar. If you stand still too long, you risk being incorporated into a wall, your body becoming part of the next structure that unravels into mist.

**Anchor Yourself in Time**

Time moves in eddies here. You may step into a corridor and find that dusk has arrived, or that you have been gone for three days when only an hour has passed. Bring a mechanical watch with a sweeping second hand—not digital, for the electronics will behave strangely in the supercooled humidity—and wear it on a chain around your neck where you can see it without breaking your movement. Do not trust it absolutely, but use it to notice when time loops. If you see the same amber tear weeping from the same resin joint, you are caught in an eddy. Sit down. Wait. The architecture will shift around you.

Do not drink from the upward-streams. The water flows against gravity, defying physics, and it carries memories. If you taste it, you will taste the future rain—visions of storms that have not yet condensed, of collapses that have not yet unraveled. It will disorient you, fill your mouth with the metallic tang of tomorrow’s weather. Bring a sealed canteen, but fill it only from the condensation that forms on your own skin, distilled by your body heat. It is the only water here that belongs to your timeline.

**Prepare Your Psyche for Impermanence**

This is a place of builders who have never known permanence. The walls they erect in the morning sag into ruin by dusk. You must practice letting go. Bring one object from home—a stone, a photograph, a ring—and practice releasing it. The structures here do not fall; they unravel, becoming mist that reforms into new shapes. You must learn to unravel too, to become mist, to reform. If you cling to the solidity of your own narrative, to the continuity of your self, the place will crush you against its sheer cliff face or drop you into the void of the high plateau.

The shadows move independently of their sources. They sketch architectures that do not yet exist. Learn to read them, not as omens, but as blueprints. When a shadow moves to block your path before the wall exists, step aside. The future is being built here, layer by layer, breath by breath, and you are walking through the construction site.

**Learn the Etiquette of the Ephemeral**

The inhabitants are small, wiry, clad in blue-gray furs, wielding knapped flint and antler. They move through the chaos with the calm of those who have never known permanence. Do not offer them permanence. Do not give them steel, or plastic, or anything that does not rot, does not weep, does not become part of the mortar. If you wish to trade, bring something that will decay beautifully—dried flowers, paper, a lock of your own hair. They are carpenters of a city that is always half-finished, half-collapsed. Join them if you can. Learn to lash gut around bone. Learn to caulk with resin that weeps amber tears. Become a builder of the ephemeral.

Speak, if you must, in whispers. The silence is absolute; your voice will be a shout. Better yet, learn their language of gesture—a touch of the shoulder, a nod toward the upward-flowing water, the offering of a flint edge. They are silent partners with the gelatinous fauna, and they will teach you how to be silent partners too.

**Final Preparation: The Acceptance**

Before you step onto the platform of woven reeds and bone, you must accept that you will be changed. The cold will become intimate. The light will flatten your world into a single, merciless plane. You will be damp forever, or you will learn to be the dampness itself. The translucent creatures will drift through you, touching you with cold that is not unkind, leading you deeper into the wet, bright, silent heart of the place.

Leave your fear of drowning at the edge. Here, drowning is just another form of breathing. Leave your need for dry warmth. Here, the wet is the only warmth. Leave your expectation of solid ground. Here, the ground is a suggestion, and the walls are the only truth—walls that breathe, weep, and unravel.

Pack light. Move slowly. Breathe shallow. Do not drink the future. Let the condensation be your skin, the silence be your voice, and the shifting shadows be your map. The Condensation does not welcome tourists. It welcomes participants—those willing to become part of the storm, to be built and unbuilt in the same breath, to stand in the central chamber where the air is thickest, the light is brightest, and the city breathes around you, a frozen moment of construction caught in amber humidity.

Go now. The light is waiting, and it is wrong, and it is beautiful, and it will press against your eyeballs with the weight of a thousand unblinking gods.
