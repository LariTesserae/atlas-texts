I am held. Not by hands—hands are a rumor, a shape half-remembered from before the water rose to claim this place—but by the weight of myself, segment upon segment of translucent membrane, each pleat fringed with cilia that beat in a rhythm older than thought, older than the builders who raised the walls around me.

I expand.

The warm mineral water rushes in through my porous flanks, thick and heavy with the heat of deep stone, tasting of ten thousand years of steeping. It presses against my internal architecture with the gentle insistence of a held breath. I contract. The water is expelled through vents near my rounded anterior, a slow sigh that disturbs nothing, contributes only to the metronomic chorus that is the sole sound in this chamber—the thrum of my own slow heart, amplified by the density of the medium, felt in the gelatinous core of my being.

I do not swim. I pulse. Expansion. Contraction. Expansion. The water moves through me and I move through the water, indistinguishable, the boundary between self and medium a negotiation rather than a line. Around me, the chamber breathes with me—forty meters of constructed dark, pillars swollen into teardrops by centuries of imperceptible lapping, doorways smoothed into featureless alcoves. I drift between them, brushing against surfaces that are pale, chalky, faintly porous, like unglazed pottery fired in a primitive kiln. I leave no mark. I take from them—the thin, invisible film of microbial life coating every surface sloughs away like skin, like dandruff, raining down as microscopic detritus, and I sift it, my cilia creating currents too gentle to ripple, drawing sustenance into my folded body.

I remember building.

Or rather, I remember the *shape* of building. The memory is not visual but tactile, a pressure in the membrane where a hand once pressed packed clay, where flint once scraped, where the geometry of intention imposed itself upon the earth. I am made of the same intention. Each segment of my body, each fold of living lace, echoes the archways I drift between, the narrow channels of water left between shoulder-to-shoulder architecture. I am the inheritor. Perhaps I was the last to step into the rising fill, to let the mineral-heavy warmth dissolve bone and ambition and language, leaving only this: a stack of segments, fringed with a thousand beating cilia, glowing faintly from within with phosphorescence—a soft, internal light so weak it barely registers, turning my translucent form into a drifting lantern.

The others are everywhere. The water is not merely inhabited by us; it is *composed* of us, layered in dense strata like sediment made animate. Near the surface, where the greenish murk from the high oculus filters down, they are smaller, simpler, barely more than pulsing bladders. Here in the middle depths, we have grown complex—folded into spirals and pleats, our segments overlapping like the scales of some impossible pangolin, our symmetry excessive, radial and bilateral at once. We communicate in pressures and pulses, sensing one another through the displacement of warm liquid as we drift near, near-touching, brushing against each other in a silence so complete it becomes a presence.

We are related only to ourselves and to the water that holds us, but in that relation, we are everything.

When one of us dies—a process of dimming, of the inner light guttering like a candle in a draft—it sinks. The descent takes hours, perhaps days, the body folding in on itself, becoming a soft, drifting meal in the gloom. We descend to meet it. Others follow, their translucent forms brushing against the corpse, their cilia working to dissolve and absorb. In this place, cannibalism is not predation but communion, a recycling of substance in a closed system where nothing is wasted. We consume our dead and become them, the substance cycling through decades, through generations measured not in days but in the slow accretion of segments.

Below me, near the worn floor where the constructed slabs have become a single undulating sheet, the elders anchor themselves. They are vast, ancient drift-kin whose bodies have grown so convoluted with added segments over centuries that they have become sessile, root-like tendrils gripping the pillars, their pulses reduced to a slow, tidal breathing. Their phosphorescence is a steady, unwavering glow like a star trapped in amber. I will become them, or something like them—adding segments, growing more lace-like, more architectural, until the water and I are indistinguishable, until I am the pillar and the pillar is me, worn smooth by the same patient chemistry that shapes us both.

The convection currents move in cycles so weak they are barely perceptible—warm rising, cool sinking, a thermal gradient so gentle that the water might as well be still. I navigate not by sight—for light is a rumor here, a faint, aqueous twilight—but by gradients of warmth, by the chemical signatures of the eroding walls, by the occasional brush of a tendril against my membrane.

Then: a drop falls from the ceiling.

A single, bell-like *tick* that echoes for a long moment in the silence before the water absorbs it. And we respond—all of us, the dense schools, the middle-depth drifters, the anchored elders—our collective pulsing shifting in rhythm, a chorus of expansion and contraction that passes through the liquid like a sigh. We are listening. We are answering. We are the last residents of a cathedral built for hands that no longer exist, and we have made it our own, slowly, grain by grain, pulse by pulse, until the builders are gone but the building remains, and we are the building now, living in it, wearing it smooth, becoming it.

I expand. I contract. The warm water holds me, thick and mineral-heavy, steeping me in its ancient patience. I am the drift. I am the kin. I am the slow, silent persistence of water and membrane, hovering in the dim aqueous twilight, my pale lantern-glow brushing against the smoothed pillars, filling the narrow channels between the architecture, living out my long, metronomic life in the warmth of deep stone—neither builder nor beast, but the chamber dreaming itself alive, one pulse at a time, one century at a time, one grain of dissolving clay at a time, until the distinction between the made and the maker dissolves entirely, and there is only the water, and the drifting, and the endless, patient dark.
