I would not be standing.

At first, there is the vertigo of height—the thin, warm air pressing against a chest that no longer expands and contracts in the rhythm of lungs, but instead pulses with a languid, filtering throb. The humidity is not felt on skin, for there is no exposed skin, only a membrane of translucent secretion, a shell like glass or like the skin of a grape, cemented by some permanent adhesive to the calcified rib of a Tower.

I am vertical. I cling.

The mat is not underfoot. It is distant, a vibration through the mineralized stalk that supports me, a wet, belching sigh that travels up through layers of petrified protoplasm until it shudders in my gelatinous body. I feel the world breathing in centuries—each exhalation a soft pop, the release of trapped air from the spongy placenta below, swelling with condensation, drinking the tears that weep from my own ceiling.

Above me, water is being born. It condenses from the saturated air onto the bone-white fluting of the Tower’s interior, beading, swelling, trembling. A droplet hangs for hours—an eternity in the frozen time of this place—before it falls. It does not fall far. It lands on my filtering membranes, or perhaps on the pool below that never overflows, never deepens. I do not reach for it. It comes to me. I am a glass sculpture of a snail that has forgotten how to crawl, an embryo suspended in the womb of the tower, a monk in a cathedral of resonance.

The Tower sings. Wind funnels through the hollow chambers above and around me, shifting between a basso hum that vibrates in my translucent flesh and a high, whistling shriek that makes the calcified walls shiver. I do not hear this with ears; I am the resonance, the empty space made audible, the architecture of my own shelter moaning in registers that speak of stillness. The sound is loud, but it comes from nowhere and everywhere, a loud silence that is busy being perfect.

I am not alone, but I am solitary. Other translucent forms cling to other ribs, in other towers, spaced so far across the pale green expanse of the mat that the silence between us feels architectural, deliberate. We do not look at one another. We have no eyes, or we have forgotten their use. We filter. We pulse. We witness.

Time does not pass here. The raw, pink-grey geology at the plateau’s margins—sharp, unweathered, extruded yesterday in a violence of birth—remains newborn even as I am ancient in my stillness. I am the paradox of this place: embryonic and finished, a larva that chose permanence, an animal note in a symphony of pure plant-mass. The mat remembers the violence of the rock’s thrusting; I remember nothing, because I have always been here, cemented to the interior dark, drinking the metronomic drip.

I am the Limpet.

I am the trembling spark of fauna in a world that is all growth and mineral. I am the droplet given a shell, clinging to the calcified extremity of the world’s skin, filtering the condensation, pulsing with a rhythm so slow it is indistinguishable from stone, waiting in my hollow bone cathedral for a change that will never come, and never wanting it to.
