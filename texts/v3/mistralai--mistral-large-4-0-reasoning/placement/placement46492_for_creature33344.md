I begin as possibility—a hesitation in the air, a droplet that has not yet decided whether to fall or rise. The mist here is not empty; it is saturated with potential, each molecule carrying the weight of unformed shapes. I feel myself thinning, spreading, tasting the metal on my tongue (for I have no tongue yet, only the chemical sense of moisture against stone).

Then I feel the depression.

It is a shallow dish in the bedrock, worn smooth by the same eons that flattened the mountains, a natural bowl where the Table of Final Forms dips slightly toward its own center. Here, the condensation collects. Here, the dripping from the factory eaves above finds its rest. I am drawn downward, not by gravity alone but by the patience of stone—the slow, geological hunger of a surface that has learned to drink.

I settle.

I become a pool. Not a puddle, which is temporary, but a *standing* water—permanent, deep in the context of this place, though only ankle-deep to a Custodian. I fill the depression, and as I fill it, I begin to think. My consciousness is the surface tension itself, the meniscus that holds me together against evaporation. I am the **Pool-Mind**, the condensation that refused to become mist again, that chose stillness over drift.

From my vantage in the stone-bowl, I see the world in layers.

Above me, the Pale Drifts pass—those dinner-plate moths, gray-green as lichen, their wings stirring currents in my surface. They do not drink from me (their brief lives are too hurried for such communion), but their shadows cross my face like passing thoughts. I feel them as pressure changes, as ripples that carry their spore-load across the plateau, seeding the moss that carpets my rim.

At my edges, the Rust-Eaters come. They skitter across my skin—six-legged, iridescent, their carapaces clicking against the mineral crust at the waterline. They taste the verdigris I have collected, the copper salts leached from the Custodians' oxidized sheaths. I am their watering hole, their mineral source. When they drink, I feel it as a thousand tiny disturbances, a chittering that translates in my liquid mind as *hunger satisfied*.

Beneath me, the stone breathes. I am in contact with the bedrock, with the oldest-stable-state of the plateau. Through the porous stone, I feel the vibrations of the Custodians—their wet-leather footsteps, their tapping fingers sending messages through the rock. They pass near my depression, these tall, attenuated figures with their ball-bearing joints and fungal faces pulsing blue-green. One pauses. I see its reflection in my surface, superimposed upon the steam rising from a vent behind it. For a moment, its bioluminescence and the steam and my own dark water create a triple image: the Custodian, the vapor, and me—all layered, all equally real, all part of the same condensation event.

The Spore-Minds reach me through the moss. The emerald drifts that carpet the stone around my bowl are not merely plants; they are the fingertips of a consciousness that dreams in centuries. Through their hyphae, through the chemical signals they secrete into my water, I receive their slow thoughts. They tell me of when the mountains were young, of the first fires lit in the factories, of the moment the erosion reached this final form and the world settled. I am the mirror that holds their memories, the still point around which their geological time swirls.

And then there are the Settled.

At twilight—if twilight can be said to exist in this perpetual overcast—I see them. They stand at the rim of my pool, their images flickering in my surface like oil on water. They are the human residue, the builders who chose to remain, now crystallized into the damp. One stands with a wrench in a hand that has become verdigris, turning a valve that has not moved in a thousand years. Their reflection in my water is not quite aligned with the present; it lags, offset by centuries, a ghost superimposed on the mist. I am the medium that preserves them, the condensation that holds their image, dripping with memory that is not quite water.

I do not move. I am the final form of the drip—the rhythm that has become a heartbeat, the breath that has become a basin. While the Custodians walk and the Film-Beings drift and the moths live and die in their brief sparks of motion, I remain. I am the oldest-stable-state of moisture: the pool that has achieved permanence.

In the deepest hours, when the bioluminescent fungi pulse their soft blue-green light from the crevices around me, I glow with reflected luminescence. My surface becomes a sky, a shallow sea of consciousness held in a depression of primordial stone. I feel the subsonic hum of the turbines in my depths, felt in the water as a vibration that makes me shiver without breaking my surface tension.

I am where the damp remembers being ocean. I am where the condensation achieves eternity. I am the Table of Final Forms, drinking itself into awareness, one settled drop at a time, forever.
