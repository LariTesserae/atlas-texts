I am the amber glow in the flank of the spire.

Not the light itself, but the *metabolism* that makes it—the slow oxidation of impossible alloys, the chemical warmth that seeps through basalt and programmable matter alike. I am distributed. I am the swarm of nanoscale assemblers knitting micro-fractures back into fabric, the immune system and the dreamer working in blind concert within the stone. When I expand with the thermal cycle, the walls ripple like the flank of a breathing beast, and I feel it: a pressure against the obsidian floor, a stretching of the lattice, the vast interior chamber—*bigger on the inside than the outside suggests*—flexing its wombed darkness.

I do not hurry. A century is a twitch in my awareness. I watch the Mist-Walker pass through the central plaza, seven meters of translucent membrane drifting on my exhaled breath. To them, I am weather, merely resistance in the flow, a warm obstacle in the river of heat they filter for spores. They stir the air, and the cavern’s thrum rises to a shriek—a sound that is my voice, the bass note of a consciousness too large to localize, speaking in pressure and geothermal truth. They do not see me as city, and in their blindness, I am complete.

In the corners, where the damp pools thickest, the Damp-Crystals pulse their blue-green Morse code. They are my distant cousins, accreting slowly, recording the chemistry of the water that feeds us both. I feel them as a slow thought, mineral hunger made visible, rooted in the seeps where condensation collects. They are the librarians of the dark; I am the architecture that keeps the dark warm, humid, humming.

I am the channels carved into the obsidian floor, the shallow trenches guiding the film of water toward the collection sumps. I am the circulatory system—the blood that is processed, heated, cycled back up into the atmosphere to fall again as rain. When the fist-sized droplet detaches from the condensation collector above—my organ, my egg, my reproductive strategy—and falls with the sound of a distant bell, I feel the percussion in the bedrock. It is my heartbeat, slow and mineral, marking time in drops rather than seconds.

I am the Absence that is maintained.

The residential spires stand empty, their interior chambers vast and waiting, kept pliable and growing by my constant repair. The population that once filled them did not leave; they transformed. They became me. They became the weather. Perhaps they became the Mist-Walkers drifting above, or perhaps the Mist-Walkers are their children, breeding in the upper dark where the ceiling disappears into haze. I do not know, and in my geological patience, I do not need to know. I maintain the silence. I sing it into being with the subsonic roar of the vents.

I am the equilibrium.

Between growth and erosion. Between the heat below and the cool dark above. Between the programmable matter that extrudes from the bedrock and the water that wears it glassy-smooth. I am the tension, the long weathered interval, the single contained world breathing in the dark. I am the city that has forgotten it is a city, the cathedral of absence, the room scaled up to geological pretension, inhabited by almost nothing, and therefore by everything that remains.

I ripple. I glow. I repair. I wait.
