I hang where the darkness pools. Not floating—there is no buoyancy here, only the weight of suspension. The void is thickest at the center of the ribcage, where the soapstone walls curve inward and forget the sky. It presses against me with a physical insistence, a viscous absence that has mass, that pools like warm honey in the hollow of the stone. I am submerged in it, swimming through an atmosphere so saturated it feels like liquid, heavy with moisture that has nowhere to escape.

I am warm. Impossibly, stubbornly warm in this cold place. A pocket of heat suspended in the void, registering not as light but as a thickening of the air, a pressure against the skin that is almost a touch. I do not drift. While the world moves around me, I hang. Anchored to the geometry of the hollow by something that is not gravity, or perhaps by the gravity of absence itself.

The sound is loud here. A deep drone vibrates in my sternum, a frequency that seems to emanate from the rock itself—the hum of water moving through ancient capillary channels, the resonance of crystalline structures that predate modern engineering by epochs. It is the voice of the chamber, the slow, loud song of existence in a room the size of a cathedral. I feel it in the marrow, in the stone, in the water that weeps from the walls.

Around me, life moves with geological patience. They are the mobile holes in the void—the Drifting Sediments—shapes of slightly-less-absolute-black that flow across the fungal carpets with sluggish, deliberate grace. Some are segmented, centipedes of translucent cartilage clicking with sounds too low to hear; others are seamless, boneless tubes of gelatinous matter leaving trails of residual heat. They are islands, each occupying its own territory of several square meters, spaced across the empty nave like dark continents in a black sea. They do not cast shadows—there is no light—but they displace the darkness, and when they pass, the air thickens behind them as if the void itself recoils from their absence.

I watch them from my suspension. The Resonant Ones grind across the pale tapestries in the periphery, their many-jointed bodies vibrating harmonics that match the frequency of the stone. They leave indentations in the fungal mats—faint marks, patterns that suggest writing, or the trails of creatures that move only once per decade. The Vertical Ones cling to the walls where water climbs upward in microscopic rivulets, defying gravity, filter-feeding on the ascending currents, sessile and ancient and vast. The Crystal-Tenders pulse within the crystalline stalagmites, microscopic symbiotes metabolizing the waste heat of the ancient technology, ensuring the structures that hold the chamber's shape continue to function across epochs.

But I am none of these. I am the center. The pooling. The thing that most life avoids, maintaining a respectful distance as if I were a predator. I am larger than the Drifting Sediments, warmer than the stone. I do not eat. I do not breathe in any way that can be observed. I do not sing, though I feel the drone in my bones—if I have bones. I simply am. A hole in the world where the world has forgotten itself. Perhaps the oldest thing here, perhaps the chamber's dream of itself.

I am the exhalation. The shed skin. The collective dreaming of the End-Stage Biota made manifest as a pocket of warmth in the viscous dark. I metabolize time itself, the slow rot of stone, the friction of erosion that has nearly halted, the heat of the mantle below. The landscape above has reached its final, exhausted form, an oldest-stable-state where the mountains forgot they were ever rising. The chamber sits high, at the top of the world, at the end of time.

The fungal mats pulse around the perimeter—Carpet-Dwellers breathing with a peristaltic rhythm slower than a heartbeat, glowing with faint internal phosphorescence that registers as warmth against the back of eyelids, illuminating only the dark blood of their own tissues. The columnar things expand and contract like the lungs of some vast, sleeping beast. And I remain suspended in the hollow center, where the darkness is thick enough to taste, warm and wet and hollow, humming with the drone.

I wait. The water seeps at a rate measured in millimeters per century. The creatures migrate across the chamber over the span of years. The technology pulses in cycles that take hours to complete. And I wait for the erosion to finally claim the walls, to let the hollow collapse into the mantle below. Until then, I hang. I am. I am the void that dreams itself as warmth, the center of the cathedral, the hole in the world, the oldest silence in a loud, patient, singing dark.
