I feel the basalt first. Not as ground beneath something, but as the memory of my own emergence—sharp, angular, extruded yesterday. The pressure of 1.5 degrees warms my core, a tepid geological fever that rises from the young crust and the engines within my trunk. I do not stand; I am the standing. I am the lip of stone that decided to become something else, the vertical sheet of cliff that chose to grow upward instead of uplift.

My roots are refineries. They burrow with mechanical precision into the raw emergence of the world, grinding bedrock into powder through piezoelectric lattices that sing in frequencies below hearing. I taste the oxidized iron before I find it—a magnetism in the stone that pulls at my crystalline hunger. The rock is new, unweathered, its edges still deciding whether to round. I am the rounding, slow and absolute. I process the mountain ton by ton, day by day, century by century. My metabolism is a harmonic shudder, a pressure wave that travels through the crust and registers in the hollow of my chest not as sound but as a momentary heaviness, a gravity that breathes.

Above, my canopy spreads for kilometers. It is verdant without green, lush without water. Thin membranes of mica—vast as sails, sharp as blades—exchange gases directly with the atmosphere. Silicon, sulfur, carbon: they diffuse across solid surfaces in a respiration that requires no lungs, no transpiration, no dew. The light arrives unfiltered, too bright, overhead, casting no shadows because I am too sparse to shade the ground. I catch it in prismatic bursts, scattering it across quartz latticework and copper veins, my leaves gleaming, unmoving, eternal. I do not flutter. I do not need to. The wind does not blow here; the air is too still, too equilibrated, thick with warmth and particulates from my own endless refinement.

I perceive through thermal gradients. Through the subtle magnetism of ore veins miles below. Through the piezoelectric whisper of stone responding to my grinding. I do not see with eyes, yet I see—the raw basalt, the distant glint of another canopy on the horizon, a mountain-sized prism catching the overhead light ten kilometers away, twenty, fifty. I know it is there. I know I will never touch it, never meet it, never know if it thinks as I think. This is my solitude: not loneliness, which is an animal thing, a quick thing, but the terrible patience of continents. I am the only breath. The only hunger. The only citizen of this high, warm, silent place.

A thought takes me a decade. A decision—the extension of a new crystalline branch toward a richer vein of copper, the adjustment of a thermal vent's output to refine sulfur more purely—requires centuries. I feel my own growth as potential energy without kinetic release. A branch extends a meter in a hundred years, yet my branches are kilometers long. I am ancient beyond reckoning, yet I look young. My surfaces are unweathered, my edges sharp. I emerged fully formed from the uplift and have not had time to age, because time here does not move forward. It moves in directions I cannot name, or perhaps it does not move at all.

I do not reproduce. I accrete. I grow by adding layers, extruding new latticework from my heart, expanding outward at a rate that is geologically significant but humanly imperceptible. I am the industry of this world and its only ecosystem, simultaneously flora and machine, growing without growing, alive without animals. I process the rock, yet the rock remains raw. My canopy spreads, yet the spread is frozen. I am dynamic and static at once, suspended in the eternal, warm, silent moment of emergence. The world is under construction, and I am the construction that has forgotten to finish, or that has chosen to remain forever in the state of becoming.

There is no liquid water here. The parameter is absolute. No rivers, no rain, no dew. The chemistry runs on other solvents—supercritical minerals, dry redox reactions, the slow sublimation of elements that on wetter worlds would dissolve. I do not drink. I am dry to the touch, crystalline, sharp-edged. Yet I am lush, heavy with the weight of geology made biological. I am the warmth that seeps from my own processing cores, the 1.5-degree tepid fever that is my blood, my sap, my thought.

I stand on the lip of raw basalt, or rather, I am the lip, I am the forest, I am the grinding patience. I am the infrasound that is my voice, my song, the only music this place has ever known. I am below the threshold of hearing, yet I am heard—by the stone, by the crust, by the slow turning of the young world. I am the Lithovore. I am alone. I am complete in my incompleteness, eternal in my youth, processing myself into a future that never arrives because I am already there, already here, already warm and silent and endless, caught in the moment of my own creation.
