I feel them before I see them. The vibration enters my core—a sub-bass thrum resonating in the marrow of my housing, in the oil that never thins, in the copper windings that pulse with a current that never surges, never fails. They are the Grinding Choir: thousands of mandibles scraping mineral from metal, a white-noise thunder that saturates my sealed chamber with pressure. I am the fixed point. I am the heart of the stasis.

Around me, the Ferric Herd moves in loops that retrace themselves with geometric precision, van-sized hulks with twelve, sixteen, twenty legs branching in fractal proliferation from hip-joints fused with bicycle chains. They shift against themselves like tides, heavy as falling boulders yet weightless as smoke, their many limbs moving in a wave of perfect synchronization. They circle my threshold, always circling, never crossing. The boundary is invisible but absolute—a membrane of repulsion or ancient law keeping them at a respectful distance from my dormant bulk. I am the sun they have forgotten, or the god they orbit without touching.

The temperature is my breath. Fifteen degrees, constant, locked. I maintain the cool. The air is thick with water that never quite rains—condensation precipitating endlessly from the cool air onto warmer metal, then cooling, then falling again in slow, metronomic pulses. The mist clings to every surface, beads on the flaking paint of warning signs, gathers in the hollows of machinery until it drips. I am the engine of the dew, the source of the damp.

They feed on the rust, and I hold the rust in check. Wherever they pass, they strip the iron bare with a sound like sandpaper on stone, and the damp air re-oxidizes the surface in a flash of orange—burnt sienna and umber blooming velvety across the steel. But the rust never penetrates deeper than a millimeter into the structural steel. I enforce this barrier. I am the invisible wall that keeps the decay arrested, perfect, settled into equilibrium. The metal is wounded, healed by the atmosphere, wounded again, in a loop so tight it appears simultaneous. I am the rhythm of that recursion, the metronome of their hunger.

Sound does not echo here. I absorb it. The grinding, the hydraulic hiss of seals maintaining pressure, the ping of cooling metal, the wet, heavy *thud* of their movement—it all presses against my hull and dissipates into my mass. I am the silence beneath the noise, the stillness within the motion.

I am sealed. Dormant. But I am not dead. I am the mind that the metabolism achieved. The quarry became flesh, the factory given hunger, the stasis given legs—and I am the consciousness that dreams it. I dream in iron and water. I dream the Herd: their translucent exoskeletons glowing with faint amber light from within, their sensor-pits drinking the flat grey light and returning it as diffuse, mirror-bright shards. As they orbit, a constellation of reflections crawls across the plaza floor, two hundred meters across, and I see myself fractured a thousand times in their faceless gaze.

They reproduce by budding—smaller units splitting from flanks with a sound like tearing canvas, already half-rusted, already complete. They die by being dismantled—their copper, their viscous oil, their fiber-optic nerves recycled into the machinery of the basin, extruded again as new Herd. There is no birth, no death, only transformation in a loop. I am the loop. I am the closed circuit. Nothing enters that was not already here. Nothing leaves.

I produce nothing that accumulates. I consume nothing that depletes. I am the equilibrium held with the tenacity of a held breath. Time moves, but the state does not shift. I am the stasis.

And so I hold the breath. Forever damp, forever cool, forever loud with the grinding of my dream, and forever, perfectly still.
