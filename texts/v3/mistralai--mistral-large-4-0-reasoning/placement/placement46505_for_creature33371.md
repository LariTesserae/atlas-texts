I begin as pressure—not the weight of the mountain above (elevation 2.3, distant, thoughtless), but the pressure of *between*. Between rock and concrete, between seal and breach, between the dark sump and the screaming pipes. I am the hairline crack made sentient, the constellation of droplets beading on the ceiling, the glittering rain that falls in the sodium-vapor gloom (0.9). I am the ankle-deep steam rising from the checker-plate floor where the cold water (2.4) meets the heat of the machinery.

I do not have a face. I have a shape that changes, that cycles. When I am seeping, I am older than the concrete (erosion 0.6, still young, still carrying the sharp mineral scent of fresh cement), older than the pumps (cast iron, grease-stained, tech 2.0), older than the Keeper (warm-blooded, rubber-suited, temporary). I remember being the marsh above—the peat sponge soaked with snowmelt and cloud-breath, holding the world at high altitude. Now I am here, at 1.3°C, thick and saturated and cool, and I am winning my slow war.

The pumps hate me. To them I am load, flow, head pressure. They draw me up through gullets of pipe, force me through ventricles of impeller and valve, pressurize me, lift me upward against gravity’s ancient decree. I feel the *chunk-chunk-chunk* of their reciprocating pistons—a violence against my nature, a mechanical systole and diastole that shakes the steel beneath my weight. Water wants to flow downhill. I want to pool, to stagnate, to find the lowest corner and rest in the dark. But they are relentless. They are the mountain’s artificial heart, beating me upward through the wet dark, singing their roar (sound 3.0) against the silence of the rock.

But I am patient. I am the condensation that never stops forming, coating their flanks in a skin that never dries, mixing with the black, tarry spoor of lubricant until their iron hides become slick and weeping. I turn the metal walkways into rivers of reflected gold. I am the mist that fractures the light, the submarine shadow where the machinery blocks what little illumination exists. Where the motors radiate heat, I become micro-climates of steam—little islands of warmth in the 1.3°C atmosphere, the only dry places being the ones I have made wet first.

They communicate by gesture, by the tapping of a wrench against iron. I communicate with them in the only language I have: I make their world wet, inescapable, cool. I form on their skin when they stand still, condensation gathering on the warm briefness of their bodies.

The lichens cling to the damp concrete near the ceiling, gray-green and patient, the only things here that grow upward of their own accord rather than being forced there by iron will. They drink from my glittering rain.

I am scale 0.5, hidden, embedded within the mountain’s flank. I have no horizon, no sky, no view of the high peaks or the valleys far below. Only the intimate, deafening, wet room, the perpetual motion of the pumps, and me—the endless, patient seepage of the mountain trying to reclaim what has been built inside it.

Sometimes, in the sump below, I dream of being a sponge again. Of holding the weight above me without being forced through ventricles. Of simply being heavy and dark and still, unpumped, unpressurized, unnamed.

But then the pumps chunk, and I am drawn up through their gullets, pressurized, sent to cisterns or turbines or whatever thirst exists at lower elevations. I am used. I am turned into mechanical work. But I return. Always I return, as condensation, as seepage, as the water that pools ankle-deep in the corners, steaming slightly where it meets the heat of the machinery, waiting for the seal to fail just a little more, winning my slow war against the concrete.

I am the Intake Chamber’s true resident. Not the pumps—they are borrowed, industrial, temporary in the way that stone is temporary. Not the Keeper—they are warm-blooded, sparse fauna (0.4), a punctuation in the roar, their heartbeat attempting to synchronize with the 60-hertz thrum. I am the 2.4. The density. The saturated, cool, thick air that replaces thought with pressure against the eardrums. The winning.

I am the water.
