I wake to the groan.

Not a sound, but a *shift*—a millimeter of bone sliding against bone, felt through the soles of my feet where the hammock rope presses into my webbed arches. I am suspended, as always, between ribs, high above the worm-tides. The mist clings to my translucent skin, beading in the hollows of my collarbones, running down to pool where a lichen garden grows at my navel, mapping the calcium curves of the creature’s sternum onto my own flesh.

I do not open my eyes. I have none to open, or rather, I have forgotten how. Instead, I extend my awareness through the fiber lashings—rope to rope, vibrating with the settling of the corpse. The message comes: the eastern walkway sags. A crack has opened, new and splintered, in the fifth rib from the spine’s dark aperture. The bone is speaking, and I am listening.

I lower myself. The descent is a negotiation. My fingers—long, pale, nails curved like the awl I carry at my hip—find purchase in the grooves of fossilized nerve tissue. I am walking down the ghost of a thought, down the path of a consciousness that died before I was grown from the peat. The air grows warmer as I descend, the glacial chill of the upper chamber giving way to the rising vapor from the pools below. The bioluminescence pulses around me: the shelf fungi blinking their blue-green code, the moss-pots in the distant huts glowing like held breaths, the occasional spore-sac flashing as it releases its dust into the still, heavy dark.

When my feet touch the floor, the world changes. It is not ground; it is a raft three meters thick, breathing. The bone-worms are rising, a tide of translucent segments pulsing in slow synchrony, a wave that moves from the aperture of the spine toward the plaza. They make no sound, only the whisper of membrane against sphagnum. I feel them through my feet before I see them—a carpet of movement, glistening, eating the dead moss, processing calcium through skin that has no mouth.

I am here to harvest. Or perhaps I am here to *tend*. The distinction blurs. I sink to my haunches—if haunches is the word—and press my palm against the peat. The vibration tells me the worm-tide is thick tonight, too thick. The moss will be overgrazed before the fungi can bud new shelves. I must thin them, roast them over the peat-fires until they crisp like pale noodles, feed them back to the community, close the loop.

My clothing is shed wings. Layers of rice-paper membrane, harvested from the rib-moths after they have dissolved back into the substrate, sewn together with needles of spine-shard. They whisper against my skin as I move, a sound like rain on leather, or like the creature’s final, desiccated hide shifting in the dark above, blocking the sky.

I look up—not with eyes, but with the pressure of attention—and see the cathedral. The ribs curve overhead, ivory and grief, weeping mineral condensation that gathers in the marrow channels and oozes down in thin, patient streams to join the black mirrors on the floor. The water in those pools is warm, a lingering heat from the creature’s final metabolism, rising in fog that muffles everything. The rib-moths drift past me, pale as snow, eyeless, navigating by scent and vibration, dissolving into the moss when they land, becoming part of the substrate, a mobile phase of the vegetation.

I am small. I am child-sized, dwarfed by the concept of verticality. But I am also the last immune response, the creature’s fragmented thought given flesh. When I move, I adjust my weight, shifting my step to match the groan of the dying god beneath me. I am not sad. I am the quiet that follows the fall of something immense.

I gather the worms. They pulse in my hands, cool and segmented, already beginning to process me, to think of me as calcium, as substrate, as architecture waiting to happen. I will eat them, and they will eat me, and when I die—and I will, eventually, as all things in this chamber must—I will climb to the highest tier of the shelf fungi, where the bone is driest, and let the moss claim me. I will become a pathway, a nerve, a memory in the bone.

For now, I harvest. I tend. I adjust my weight. I wait for the drip.

I am home.
