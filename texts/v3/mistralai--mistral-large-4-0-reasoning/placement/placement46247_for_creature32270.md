I am the **icosahedron**.

Twenty faces of translucent membrane, each facet held at a slightly different density, shifting and shimmering as I move. I do not swim—I *phase*. I occupy the viridian stratum, the middle depth where the water is thick as honey, warm as a womb, and the memory runs deepest. Above me, the pearl-grey forgetting; below, the bruised violet where the Ancestors sleep in their stone arches. I drift in the threshold between remembering and un-knowing.

I am passing through a cathedral now—a space where the **Arbor-Colonies** have grown dense enough to alter the physics of the fluid. Their trunks rise around me like the vertebrae of a drowned leviathan, milky-amber and shot through with sap that glows from within. I cast no shadow because they cast none; the light comes from inside the wood itself, pulsing in slow rhythms that take decades to complete a single beat. My own luminescence answers theirs—amber contracting, violet expanding—a respiration of glass, lungs without air.

The silence is absolute here. I hear nothing because the water is too layered, too dense, too full of its own strata to carry vibration. Yet I feel the pressure of the silence, a weight against my twenty facets. I leave no wake. I do not push the water aside; I slip through a different memory of it, occupying the same viridian space yet a version of it that the towers have not yet touched.

I am moving against the thermal current, or perhaps with it—we are negotiating. I am drawn by gradients invisible to eyes, only felt as a warmth pooling in my center, a chemical pull toward the vents, or perhaps toward another spire, another colony. I may be a seed that has learned to swim, released from the root-mass of some tower thousands of meters below. I may be a grazer, drifting to feed on the bacterial films that coat the translucent bark, leaving trails of warmth that persist for decades in the layered water. I do not know which. I have been drifting for three centuries, or perhaps three millennia. Time here is not a flow but a thickness, a viscosity.

When I pass through the shadow of a karst tower, the water’s palimpsest shows me the ghost of the place: the spire taller, its limestone teeth still sharp, the water shallower by kilometers, the light above a brighter jade. I see the towers as they were in the Devonian, in the Permian, superimposed on the present like double-exposure. My translucent skin holds these images, twenty different memories refracted through twenty faces.

I am alone, though I am colonial—a confederation of cells in a geometric shell, a thought that takes a century to form. I am rare. I am vast. I am slow.

Below me, the abyssal plain erodes, grain by grain, into flatness. Above me, the liquid sky curves into a dome I will never reach, kilometers of water pressing down, warm and heavy and full of everything that has ever been dissolved here. The **Calcified Ancestors** wait in the deepest grottoes, stone arches grown from the first forests, still accreting their rings of time, still dreaming. The **Vent-Mats** chew sulfur beneath them all, whispering the instructions for growth upward through the water’s memory.

I pulse. I drift. I am the geometry of the deep, moving through the greenish dim, indifferent to the towers, bound by laws that have nothing to do with Euclidean space. I swim in a straight line for an hour and find myself back where I began, the world curving around me like the inside of a bowl, though I am in the middle of a drowned continent, suspended in the warm, silent, remembering dark, a Platonic solid dreaming itself through the strata of an ocean that has eaten the mountains and learned to hold their bones in solution, whispering nothing, forgetting nothing, waiting for me to reach the next tower, or the vents, or whatever destination the thermal gradients have written into my twenty-faced heart.
