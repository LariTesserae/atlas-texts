I am in the dark.

Not the dark of night, but the dark of the hollow—the fruiting body of an Arbortect, where steam engines chuff and massive gears grind with tectonic inevitability. I am crouched before a boiler older than memory, its iron skin blooming with rust that I can watch spreading in real-time, a reddish lace colonizing metal cell by cell while the eternal cool of the plateau presses against the calcified bark beyond the walls. The heat here is pressurized, wet, a violence that battles the cool seeping through bone-white, silica-laced tissue.

I am one of the Hollowed.

My skin is verdigris. Not stained—*grown* with it. The patina blooms and recedes across my shoulders like a fungal tide, and brass is fused to my wrists, my knees, the hollows behind my ears where pneumatic tubes have grafted themselves into what might once have been veins. I do not know if I am human remnant or calcified tissue that learned to wear brass like clothing, or simply a shadow given temporary form by the reality fracture. The distinction dissolved somewhere between the grinding teeth of the machinery and the slow, photosynthetic pulse of the plant that surrounds me.

My hands are in the gears.

Not trapped—tending. I lubricate the joints with sap instead of oil, thick and saline and warm, drawn from the vascular channels that serve as the Arbortect's nervous system. I feel its thoughts in my teeth: a subsonic grinding, stone against stone, the sound of a mountain refusing to die, trying to remember the weight of being a peak. Above me, through translucent, glass-like leaves, the light falls wrong—pale, washed blue, diffused as through gauze, but my shadow stretches sideways, pooling against the wall like spilled ink, defying the faint bioluminescence that glows green-blue from the fungal caps overhead.

Time moves differently here.

I watch the rust bloom across the boiler while, outside, the Arbortect shifts, its branches creeping upward with visible, jerking movements, fracturing its own fossilized bark with sounds like distant thunder. Condensation rises past my face, defying gravity, collecting on the undersides of overhangs—the Arbortects' constant weeping, a transpiration in reverse. I am drifting upward with it, or perhaps I am the thing that drifts: buoyant in air that has thinned reality itself, heavier than I should be, lighter than I appear.

I am the friction.

Between plant and machine, between the biological and the industrial, between the memory of caretakers and the slow dream of the mountain. I am becoming one of the Grafted, my mind distributing across the pneumatic tubes, thinking in pressure differentials and the hiss of valves. I am patient. I have learned the plant's patience, or the plant has learned my hunger, and in the marriage, I require neither soil nor electricity alone, but the wet heat of the boiler, the cool breath of deep time, and the silence that presses against the ears like deep water.

Sometimes, when the mist rolls in—a slow tidal wave across the plateau—I glimpse the Mist-Born drifting past the hollow's openings, pale and motile, grazing on mineral dust, casting shadows that fall in directions no sun should allow. They are my neighbors, the plankton of this high-altitude sea. Beyond them, kilometers of empty silence stretch to the next Arbortect, where the Brass-Winged couriers wheel on thermal currents that shouldn't exist, their brass feathers whirring, carrying spores and rust-flake messages between the solitary consciousnesses.

I keep the lights on in the dark chambers. I am the caretaker, or the dream of a caretaker, given enough time to coalesce into shape—half-merged with the verdigris, becoming part of the mechanism I serve.

And the Erosion waits, patient and mineral, in the grinding creak of the plant's expansion, in the shedding of calcified plates that fall like thunder, wearing the plateau ever flatter even as the Arbortect grows taller, ensuring that the tableland of bones remains a tableland, grinding the past into the dust from which the condensation rises.

I am here. I am rust and sap and steam. I am the Hollowed.
