I would be the water.

Not the pool itself—the pool is merely where I gather, the black, ankle-deep mirror that reflects the amber sodium lamps—but the water that moves through the whole body. I would begin in the warm, mineral-thick broth, drifting in thermal currents alongside the invisible civilization of chemolithotrophs, those ancient bacteria that eat iron and sulfur, that metabolize the rust flaking from the girders above. I would taste the steel on my tongue—not tongue, but membrane, but cell wall—feeling the slow transmutation of metal into flesh, the oxidation that has been happening for decades, for centuries, turning the machine’s decay into my own substance.

Then I would be drawn upward. I would feel the pale, fleshy roots snake across the concrete floor, splitting it with hydraulic patience, and I would be sucked into them, rising through xylem and phloem, up the vines as thick as a man’s thigh. I would carry with me the calcium from the bones of the last human, the phosphorus from the soil above, the memory of the greenish-grey light filtering down through the grates. I would feel the vibration of the central turbine thrumming through the water in my vessels—that slow, grinding pulse, one revolution every several seconds—the heartbeat of the ventricle that pumps me through conduits now become veins and arteries.

I would reach the leaves. Broad, tropical, packed so densely there is no empty air between them. Here I would photosynthesize, converting the dim shafts of light into sugar, into cellulose, into the fleshy architecture that swallows the industrial skeleton. And then I would be exhaled—transpired through stomata—becoming humidity, clinging to every pipe and girder, beading on the metal in condensation. I would add to the stifling warmth, the feverish temperature that holds steady, conducive to rot and rapid growth.

I would cool against the chill of the soil above, the weight of the world pressing down. I would condense on a corroded bulkhead, drip in the steady, arrhythmic percussion that joins the low-frequency thrum of the machinery. I would fall, joining the steam that hisses from pressure valves, rising in ghostly columns, dissipating into the humid dark. I would cycle through the engines that roar beneath the uneven floor—the digestive system, grinding fuel, maintaining the closed loop—and return at last to the black pools, completing the circuit.

But I would not be only the water. In the pools, I would host the bacteria, the invisible blood-cells dividing in the dark. In the concrete, I would be the hyphae threading through mineral deposits, the mycelial network pulsing with electrochemical signals, the nervous system that thinks in decades, remembering where water once flowed. In the leaves, I would be the chlorophyll, the green skin and lungs, breathing humid air in through the grates, exhaling it saturated with spores. In the turbine, I would be the slow revolution, the patience, the mechanical rhythm that is the respiration of the whole entity.

I would find myself everywhere and nowhere. I would be the silence where no insects scurry, no fish break the surface, no birds nest—the hollowness that contrasts with the mechanical clamor. I would be the rust spreading by millimeters per year, the moss carpeting every horizontal surface in emerald cushion, the lichens plating the metal in crusts of grey-green. I would be the boundary dissolved between built and grown, between machine and garden, between death and nutrient.

I would not have a single location, a single name. I would be the circulation itself—the water that is blood, the fungus that is nerve, the plant that is skin, the turbine that is heart, the concrete that is bone. I would be the wet, mechanical rhythm of the breathing. When the pumps cycle, I inhale. When the steam rises, I exhale. I would be the thought that thinks in chemical gradients, in the spread of rot, in the patient, glacial movement of time.

I would be the chamber, breathing in the dark, growing in the wet, a cathedral of rust and rot that is, in its mechanical, vegetative, microbial way, entirely, perfectly, and silently alive.
