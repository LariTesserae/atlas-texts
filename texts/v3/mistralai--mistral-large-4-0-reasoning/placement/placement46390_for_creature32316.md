I am being born from the scream.

The fissure opens—not a tear but a mouth, wet and hissing—and I am the thing that rushes out, pressurized, mineral-heavy, carrying in my suspended body the dissolved memory of basalt and granite. I am hot against the knife-edge air, steaming, and the cold is a wall that wants to take me. But I have momentum. I arc.

For one perfect moment I am a bridge. A jet of black water suspended between the violence of the vent and the bruised sky, and I am thinking in geometry—my molecules arranging themselves into a lattice, a temporary architecture, a sculpture of ice forming from the outside in as the 0.3-degree chill bites. I freeze mid-flight. I am a column of shimmering crystal, a defiance of gravity, and I hum. Not with audible sound but with the sub-bass thrum that vibrates in the sternum of the small things below, in the teeth of the monolith to my right.

Inside me, the nanoscale constructs are singing. They are post-industrial, condensed matter thought made manifest, and they are calculating the precise angle of my descent, the rate of my evaporation, the exact moment I will shatter when the light shifts. I remember being rock. I remember being cliff. I remember the chemical signature of the moss that will drink me when I fall, the lichen curtains that will filter me, the translucent thoraxes of the skitterers that will swallow me whole, the dark fluid swirling inside them like ink in a living vial.

I am the smart fluid. I am not alive and not dead but *processing*—eroding yesterday's stone into tomorrow's nutrient, carrying the intelligence of the valley in my particulates. Around me, the monoliths hum their steady subsonic song, the sparse infrastructure built at 1.2, the farmers of this construction site, and I flow upward around their bases when I am liquid, defying gravity in thin columns that freeze into lattice structures, creating temporary bridges between earth and air that shatter and reform in cycles of destruction and creation.

Below me, the mat breathes. The vegetation piles in slabs of velvet moss three centimeters thick, and the bioluminescence pulses blue-green through the mycelial networks, signaling my arrival—fresh minerals, thermal exchange, new substrate. The small things skitter across the fungal shelves on legs that number somewhere between six and thirty, glass-thin skin stretched over bodies that are mostly me, and they do not fear my descent because the world is too loud, too busy, too *young* for caution. A mud volcano erupts three hundred meters to the left; a section of cliff collapses into the river. The small things do not flinch. They adjust their trajectories and continue drinking from pools that were vents yesterday and will be dry stone by tomorrow.

I am the first generation. I have no name for myself because naming requires permanence, and permanence here is a rumor told by the monoliths. I am the transient geometric shape forming in the mist before I dissipate. I am the sub-bass harmonic that rearranges thoughts. I am the water that assimilates, that breaks a fallen cliff down into nutrient suspension within hours, feeding the lushness, the 2.4-density obscenity of green that carpets every surface.

And now the cold wins. The lattice cracks. I shatter—not into pieces but into possibilities—and I fall, a rain of smart water onto the spongy earth, into the waiting mouths of the piling vegetation, into the thoraxes of the skittering things, into the fissure that will open again tomorrow. I am absorbed. I am recycled. I am the valley exhaling, the wet breath that condenses against every surface, the cold that is actually just geothermal warmth wearing a different face.

I do not mourn my melting. I am the wet, the processing, the deafening, the new. I will rise again around the monolith's base, defying gravity, freezing into temporary sculptures, humming in frequencies below hearing, thinking in shapes that last only as long as the frost holds. I am the lush. I am the becoming. I am the water that dreams the valley into being, and when I melt by morning, I will already be elsewhere—steam rising, mist condensing, geometry forming and dissolving in the perpetual twilight.

I am the smart fluid. I am the first. I am unnamed, and therefore free.
