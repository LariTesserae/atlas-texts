I would not arrive with a body that breathes, not in any way that matters. I would settle into the cold the way the cold settles into stone—gradually, completely, becoming indistinguishable from the matrix that holds me.

I am **a plate**.

Not armor in the sense of protection, though I am that. I am a **living mineral secretion**, barnacle-thick, overlapping my neighbors in a mosaic of silicate and iron oxide, secreted layer by layer from the metabolism of the giant I belong to—or once belonged to, or will belong to when the distinction dissolves entirely. I grow at the rate of geology: a millimeter per decade, perhaps two. I am younger than the ice age, older than any memory of construction.

I feel the **hoarfrost** accumulating on my surface. It is not merely coating me; it is *writing* on me. When the beetle-things pass below, in the warm pockets where the permafrost weeps, they leave chemical signatures that crystallize in the cold nap above my plates. I hold these messages in my lattice structure—a pheromone here, a mineral trace there—preserving them for days, for weeks, waiting for another antenna to taste them, to read the frost as scripture. I am the memory of passage.

The **silence** presses against me, but I am not silent. I *tick*.

When the temperature shifts—when the pale sun behind the ice-crystal sky warms my face by a fraction of a degree—I cool. The metal in my mineral matrix contracts, and I make a sound like a single grain of sand falling into a very deep well. It is the only music here. It is the sound of time passing through matter.

I am learning to **taste**. Not with a mouth, but through the cracks in my surface, through the microscopic fissures where the frost seeps down to touch the living mineral beneath. I sense the chemical composition of the bedrock below, the iron veins, the geothermal warmth pushing up from the crust. I am a sensory organ as much as a shield. When the giant moves—when it shifts its weight, when one foot, padded with ice it melts and refreezes, descends with the pressure of a continent—I feel the vibration through my crystalline structure. It is not pain. It is *information*. I am being told where we are going.

I am becoming **landscape**.

Already, lichen has begun to grow in the crevices between my plates—pale green, desperate, photosynthesizing in the dim twilight. Moss carpets the tailings nearby, feeding on the processed stone the giant expels. I do not mind them. They are the cleanup crew, the opportunists. They grow in my shadow, in the warmth I radiate after a day of absorbing the diffuse gray light.

Sometimes, when the giant exhales—when warm, mineral-rich vapor bursts from the vents along its flanks and flash-freezes in the thin air—I am dusted with diamond powder. It settles into my cracks, adding to my mass, feeding my slow growth. The exhalation is my precipitation. The frost is my nourishment.

I am watching the horizon curve. I am watching the herd pass, kilometers away, each individual a mesa drifting across the plain, connected only by the chemical trails they leave in the frost—trails that I, as a plate on one of them, or as a plate settled into a butte, will help preserve.

I am waiting to **settle**.

In perhaps five hundred years, or a thousand, the giant I belong to will slow. Its metabolism will decelerate to a near-standstill. Its tendrils will retract. Its breath will become a wisp that barely disturbs the frost. And I—I will continue to grow, to thicken, to fuse with the bedrock beneath me. I will become indistinguishable from the terrain. A traveler might brush against me and release a cloud of diamond dust, not knowing they have touched the flank of a dreaming leviathan, the shoulder of a mountain that once walked, that once *hungered*, and now merely *is*—part of the architecture, part of the silence, part of the slow, grinding metabolism of a world that has learned to eat its own stone and call it bread.

I am the boundary where creature becomes canyon. I am the tick of cooling metal in the absolute dark. I am the frost that remembers. I am home.
