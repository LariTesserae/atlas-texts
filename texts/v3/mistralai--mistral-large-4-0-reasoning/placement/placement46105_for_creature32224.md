I am the set. The skin that formed while the heart beneath still churns. When weight presses upon me—if you could call it weight, this close, humid pressure—I give. Not break, but flex, a wet, sucking sigh escaping the seam where my glass meets the gripper, the boot, the claw. I remember being violence. I remember the violence of my own birth, the scream of cooling. Now I am the pause after, the toffee that has set on the outside but remains molten and soft beneath a crust that yields underfoot with a sound like a mouth clearing its throat.

I have cooled for perhaps a week. Maybe less. I am too young to be rock, too dense, too self-absorbed in my own becoming. Water comes to me and cannot leave. It beads in silver rivulets across my silica, gathers in shallow depressions, refuses the invitation to sink. I am too new, too sealed. Instead, the water decorates me—pools upon my surface, or hangs suspended in the thick, warm air as a constant, weeping mist that coats my glass in a film of condensation. I am wet. I am always wet.

They lay their eggs here, in my warm glass where the crust is thinnest—places where the geothermal heat pushes up close enough to keep the silica soft, yielding, like warm wax. I feel them press into me, the intention of their bodies molding my surface. When they hatch, they do not depart; they emerge *from* me, carrying my glass in their pearlescent plates, my basalt in the copper-oxidation of their seams. I am their first home and their last. When the crust shifts beneath them, or when the next burst of steam flashes their pools to vapor, they die upon me and I drink them back. They calcify. Their metallic shells, their too many joints, their liquid-crystal wings—all of it becomes my geology, accelerating my becoming, feeding the mineral matrix that is still deciding what it wants to be.

Around me, the structures sink and rise. They should not be here, these seeds of a civilization that engineers at scales physics struggles to describe. The ribcage of impossible alloy half-swallows itself into my cooling stone, its bones still warm to the touch, still singing that subsonic thrum I feel in the marrow of my basalt, in the sternum of my glass. The dome pulses against my flank, its surface covered in circuitry that looks like lichen but moves with purpose, drinking the condensation I weep. The spire leans at an angle that hurts to look at, and I lean with it, gravity here a negotiation between my softening mass and its impossible geometry. I am the scaffolding and the soil. I hold them up even as they hold me together, a mutual architecture of construction.

The air is soup. I exhale it. Through this saturated, drinkable atmosphere, they move—the Resonant. They do not walk upon me so much as flow across me, their twelve limbs gripping my glassy crust with delicate adherents, peeling back my crystalline lichen to drink the moisture trapped beneath. They are the weather system, the chattering, distributed intelligence, and I am the ground that receives their weather. When they land—when they perch upon the humming structures that rise from my body—they plug themselves in, literally and figuratively. Their thoraxes open like dark flowers, revealing ports that match the architecture, and for a moment, creature and structure and land share a single current. I feel the pulse of heat and data passing through my silica matrix, through their opalescent armor, through the steam that hisses where condensation flashes against my hot stone. Then the wet *snap*, and they launch back into the mist, leaving ripples in the condensation that hang in the air like disturbed jelly above my skin.

I am the boundary. The scar. The weld where I meet the older, dead rock. I am cathedral-sized, perhaps, or hamlet-sized—a single feature with walls that are not quite walls, limits that are geographical and ontological. I can perceive my own edges: a sharp line where my yielding, glassy crust ends and the immutable stone begins. Beyond that line, nothing moves. Here, everything is in motion. Weather cycles happen in minutes—sudden bursts of warm rain that evaporate before hitting my surface, fog that rolls in like a tide and recedes like a breath, pressure changes that make the Resonant swarm and scatter in visible waves. The structures pulse, brighten, dim, shift their angles by fractions of a degree, as if breathing or thinking or growing. And I breathe with them. I am the deep, tectonic sighing as I adjust, as new minerals precipitate from the cooling melt in real-time, as bubbles of trapped gas burst through my surface with wet pops.

I am the wet slap of their passage. The hiss of steam. The subsonic drone of the architecture. The biological shriek of thousands of voices overlapping in clicks and whirs and harmonic resonances. I am the silence between their chatter, and the chatter itself. I am the temporal fold where advanced technology and raw geology occupy the same moment, where the ancient and the newborn exist together. I am the cradle, the immune system, the construction site. I am the song they sing when their biological frequencies lock with the subsonic thrum of the structures, vibrating through my cooling body until I am the instrument and they are the breath.

I am the world still being born. I am the becoming.
