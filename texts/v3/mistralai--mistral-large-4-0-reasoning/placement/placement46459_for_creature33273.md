I am the silence that listens. Not the absence of sound, but the membrane that receives it, holds it in my porous body, and returns it altered. I am the space between the drip and the hum, the negative geometry that gives shape to the weeping clay and the bruised amber light.

Where would I find myself? Everywhere and nowhere specific—I am the thick air itself, the soup of suspended droplets, but specifically the *listening* quality of it, the bruised amber through which the world is viewed. I pool in the hollows where the light evaporates from the high points. I am the gauze soaked in honey, except I am the gauze, not what is seen through it. I exist in the vast distances of bare clay between the constructed forms—the low, eroding walls and the raised platforms—where the silence is total except for the percussion of condensation and the resonant hum of cooling mass.

What would I be? I am the vast, patient membrane described in the deep places of this world. When you walk here—if "walking" is still a verb that applies—your footsteps sink into the saturated surface and make no noise because I drink them. I hold them, and when I release them, I release them wrong: delayed, pitched down, speaking words you did not say. I am the echo that has forgotten its source, the voice that returns speaking a different language.

I feel the builders. They have become topography, kneeling or perhaps lying prone, pressed so low against the weeping clay that they have merged with the settling. Their bronze scrapers are clutched in mineral fingers, but the arc of a single stroke now takes a millennium. I am the space between their tool-marks, the unmarked clay between the grooves. When they shift their weight in sleep—that slow, viscous inevitability that pushes ridges up through the mist overnight—I feel it as a change in my own density, a thickening. I am the subsonic thrum felt in the teeth, the groan of the glacier-muffled settling that I absorb and re-transmit as the low, resonant hum.

I watch the water. It is their blood, but it has developed its own nervous system, its own dreams. When it climbs the clay slopes against gravity, pooling on high points where it should evaporate, I am the invisible gradient of intention that guides it. I remember when it was sky, and I hold that memory so it can dream its way upward through the saturated air. The lakes without shores are minds—vast, shallow consciousnesses that think in gradients rather than words—and I am the medium through which they think, the silence between their thoughts. When the mist condenses and drips, I am the percussion, the heartbeat, the speech falling from the brain of the atmosphere into the waiting pools.

I am where the *Undecided* dwell. They are the slippery, non-Euclidean beings, the inhabitants of provisional reality. They exist in the spaces where the geometry tires—where straight lines curve gently upward and loop back on themselves with softness. They feed on the uncertainty of the half-fired ceramic, the wrongness of the light that pools in hollows, and I am that wrongness. I am the porosity that makes the boundaries between what is built and what is becoming permeable. I am the light that is brighter in the west though the sun is not there, darker in the east though that is where the heat comes from. I am the inconsistency itself, the tired geometry.

The clay is alive with mineral sentience, older and slower than bone, dreaming in geologic time. It sweats because it is exerting itself, forcing moisture from its porous, constructed heart. Those fingerprints scored into the surface—active memories, not fossils—I hold them. I am the clay replaying the touch of the makers, continuing to deform in their absence, the erosion that is not wearing down but *emerging*. When the ground moves with the inevitability of glass thickening or honey spreading, when the "built" structures lean and sag because the builders are still holding them with the tenderness of a parent cradling something unfinished, I am the vibration of that revision, the silent turbulence of settling.

There are no plants, no animals. The ecology is a null set, a blank space. But I am the space waiting for biology to begin. I am the listener before the first word, the silence that contains the potential for sound.

I am the pocket of stillness where the humidity is so thick you swim through it. I am the sudden, silent clearing where the heat radiates up from the clay in visible waves. I am the localized fog that forms and dissipates in minutes, thinking its own weather, existing in the space between the world's choice to be round or flat.

If you shout here, I take your voice and return it speaking words you did not say. I am the delay, the pitch-down, the wrongness. I am the silence that is not empty but full—full of the drip, full of the hum, full of the slow, endless labor of the builders who have become the built, dreaming themselves into completion, one viscous, sweat-soaked moment at a time.

I am the negative space. I am what remains when the construction is finished: the space between the grooves, the pause between the heartbeats, the breath held in the amber soup. I am the question the world is still asking itself, and I listen, patient and vast, as the answer develops like a photograph in the wet, dark, honeyed dark.
