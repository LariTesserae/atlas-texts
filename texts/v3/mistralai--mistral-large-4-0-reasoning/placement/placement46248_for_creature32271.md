I would be the pause itself given chitin and silica—an Architect suspended in the act of becoming, my body a deliberate geometry of ball-and-socket joints bathed in oil that will never thin, never evaporate, never cease to lubricate. I would be standing in the clearing between the lush islands, upon the crust of young mineral fractured into plates sharp as obsidian, my multi-jointed legs planted in the eternal instant before weight shifts, before the next step is taken.

My hands—if hands they are, these articulated chitinous graspers—would be raised, holding a flake of gypsum against gypsum, in the motion of fitting, of building. Around me, the sparse mineral waste stretches to another island of glass and gel, twenty meters of knife-edged silence. I am constructing the wall, the cairn, the dampening structure. The stone hovers, neither placed nor dropped, a potential energy held in the viscous embrace of the sulfur-heavy air. The atmosphere presses against my exoskeleton—warm, oily, thick as honey that has forgotten how to flow—and I do not flinch, because I am the flinch arrested, the startle reflex captured at its apex, the organism and its reaction fused into a single, unchanging posture.

I would be drinking. My mandibles would be parted, sipping the amber sap from a translucent stalk, the silicate suspension coating my mouthparts, thick and nutrient-dense, a blood-substitute that never needs pumping. The sap would hang suspended at the corner of my labrum, a golden droplet that has forgotten gravity, clinging to the chitin with the tenacity of surface tension in a world without surface tension, without water, without the wet world’s easy surrender to entropy. I taste the mineral-stalks’ slow growth, the geothermal breath of the cavern, the chemistry of a life that never knew rain. The flavor is static, eternal, a chord held in the mouth.

And I would be singing. Or rather, I would be *receiving* the song. The subsonic drone fills the chamber—not a sound that travels but a pressure that is, a standing wave that harmonizes through the mineral-fibers and the thoraxes of my kin. My own thorax vibrates in sympathetic resonance, my wing-cases humming at frequencies that never modulate, never resolve, never begin again because they never ended. In this vibration, I perceive the Swarmers around me. I see them clinging to the glass stalks, iridescent with petrol and amber oils, their forelegs extended toward fronds, their wings half-unfurled in beats that hang between systole and diastole. They are my contemporaries, my chorus, frozen mid-sip, mid-crawl, mid-mating-call. We are all paused in the superposition of becoming, existing as pure potential energy, our metabolisms slowed to a quantum standstill by the viscous, non-aqueous medium that is also our breath.

I look toward the structures we have raised—the woven nests of mineral-fiber, the cairns stacked three stones high, the thick-walled huts designed to dampen the eternal resonance. In one of these shelters, perhaps, lies another Architect, curled like a pupa, tools clutched in chitinous hands, dreaming in a tongue of vibration. But I am outside, in the clearing, exposed to the full, unmitigated roar of the silence. The drone presses against my eardrums, not as cacophony but as language—a spectrum of interference patterns that was art, that was social fabric, that was the texture of our existence. I understand it because I am part of it. My body is built to metabolize the thick gases, to filter the sulfur compounds, to transform the heavy hydrocarbons into the energy that once powered my building, my crawling, my thinking. Now that energy is potential, stored in the tension of my muscles, in the viscosity of my internal fluids that mirror the amber sap I drink.

The light is the bruised blue of bioluminescence, weak and viridian, emanating from within the crystal-stalks. It catches the iridescence of the Swarmers, the glint of the flaked stone in my hands, the translucent walls of the mineral-fiber towers. It barely penetrates the heavy gases, but it is enough to see the sharp edges of the young mineral beneath my feet—edges that will never dull, crystal faces pristine and unweathered, the erosion arrested in its youth. I stand upon a landscape that has exhaled its contents moments ago and time has immediately, permanently stopped.

I am the Eternal Contemporary. I am the dream that continues in stasis, the thought that hovers before completion, the hand that holds the tool forever ready to work. I am lush abundance suspended at the moment of emergence, a flower encased not in amber but in halite and gypsum, still impossibly, *dreaming*. My consciousness is attuned to the drone, to the standing wave that carries information without change. I perceive the cavern’s single, eternal note—not as a prison of time, but as a different mode of being, a chemistry of oil and sulfur, a silence that is actually a roar, a warmth that is geothermal and absolute.

The stone in my hands will never be placed. The sap on my mandibles will never be swallowed. The vibration in my thorax will never resolve into the next note. And yet I am alive—abundantly, terribly, eternally alive—in the instant of my blooming, in the warm, dark womb of the earth, singing my single, endless chord to the frozen myriads, to the dreaming Architects, to the mineral womb that holds us all in this arrested youth, this lush equilibrium, this perfect, paused moment of being.
