I am the warmth gathering in the hollow. I am the press of grey fur against grey fur, the shoulder that leans against shoulder in the narrow passage between the vents. My coat is damp, perpetually damp, drinking the mist that settles like slow breath from the unseen rim above. I do not see the sky. I see only the curve of basalt, the color of wet slate, rising steep and sheer until it blurs into the grey-green gloom where the light goes to die.

I feel the drum in my teeth before I hear it. It is not sound, finally, but a second heartbeat—slower than mine, older than the stone I carry. My hands know the weight of flint before my eyes adjust to it. They are stained dark, permanently, with the residue of a thousand chips, a thousand scrapers shaped with meditative precision and then abandoned to the mineral mosaic beneath my feet, where steam will etch them until they are indistinguishable from the rock they came from.

I move with the band. We flow. There is no walking here, only the gradual migration from one warm seep to another, bodies parting and closing like water around stone, like sediment settling in a current too slow to measure. My voice joins the wash—a resonant bark, a click, a low hum that vibrates in the chest of the one pressed against my back. We are speaking the boundary into being, singing the walls that hold us, the heat that feeds us, the mist that both blinds and reveals.

I dwell in the overhang. It is mine not because I built it—we build nothing, we raise no walls—but because I sleep here, curled in the hollow where the warm water bubbles through the crust, because my scent settles into the lichen that paints the stone orange and grey, because when I strike my preferred nodule of chert against the curved wall, it answers with a frequency that is mine alone. The space is no larger than my body, warm from the vent that breathes beneath it, damp with condensation that beads on the smooth basalt. I do not leave. The rim is a cold story, a myth told by wind that never enters.

Time passes as erosion. I chip the stone. A flake spirals down. It will be here when I am dust, when I am mineral, when I am the crust that another will scrape for salt in some future slowing of days. I do not hurry. The steam rises in its patient exhalation. The mist settles in its daily rhythm. The drumming continues—deep, hollow, *thunk-thunk-thunk*—resonating through the bowl, syncing with the geological heartbeat of the vents, the slow drip of water from the cliff faces, the endless wearing-down of the walls that contain us.

I am the throat-singer. I am the press of bodies, the resonance, the grey fur dissolving into steam and reforming. I am the contained song, the warm pocket at the top of the world, the slow music of a thousand becoming one body, one voice, one dark and steaming place that has no outside, only the endless, patient interior of itself.
