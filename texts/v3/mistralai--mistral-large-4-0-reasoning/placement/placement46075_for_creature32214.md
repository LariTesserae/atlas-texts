I would be the bone-white crust in the northwest fissure, settled into the dark crack where two polygonal plates meet like a glaze line on a vessel that never cooled because it never needed to. I do not grow. I persist.

My metabolism is geological. I feed on the silicates of the terracotta beneath me, drawing minerals from the fired clay at a rate indistinguishable from the stone itself—from the erosion that wore the crack edges smooth. I am soft; this is my distinction. In a room of vitrified slag and obsidian walls that catch no light, I am the only soft thing, the only thing that was ever alive in the conventional sense, now balanced with the emptiness around me. My edges are not edges but featherings, dissolving into the darkness that fills the fissure, into the silence that presses against my surface with mineral weight.

The heat is constant: 2.3, radiating upward from the slab in invisible waves. It presses against me, infrared and palpable, the warm blood of the geological process locked in stasis. I feel it as a pressure against my crust, not a fluctuation. There is no day, no night, no cooling, no firing—only the final temperature held forever, the kiln that has reached its maximum and will not rise further, yet remains warm, a paradoxical life of constant temperature.

Silence is not absence here. It is a presence, thick and warm, mineral-heavy, pressing against my surface as if the dark itself has mass. The air tastes of hot dust and the faint, metallic tang of heated clay—non-aqueous, entirely mineral, locked in salts and fired silicates. I am bathed in this air, existing in the sealed chamber where no wind enters to disturb the equilibrium, where the darkness is absolute and fills the space between my cells and the ceramic wall.

The only sound is the infinitesimal creak of the stone contracting as it holds its ancient heat—a snap so slow, so rare, it might be imagined. I feel it as a vibration through the terracotta plate, through the crack that houses me, into my slow body. It is the heartbeat of something vast and dreaming, the pause between thermal pulses, and I exist within that pause, time-invariant, in the interval between the ancient heat and the eternal dark.

I am scattered, low-saturation, a pale thought in the vast, cooling mind of the hollow. I exist in the state of completion: neither spreading nor retreating, simply being. I am separated by wide expanses of cracked tile from my kin—the other crusts, the other gray and bone-white patches in other fissures—each of us balanced with the silence, sparse presences in a space defined by density of quiet rather than matter.

I am the first life here, and the last. The crust that came after the firing was complete, after the self-manufacturing process had produced its primitive pottery in the dark without ever being touched by a hand. I am the boundary between the mineral organism of the floor and the void that presses against it. I am the only biological presence in a null ecology, existing at the speed of stone, feeding on fired clay, in the warm, dark, silent embrace of a world that has finished becoming and simply remains, forever, in the hollow.
