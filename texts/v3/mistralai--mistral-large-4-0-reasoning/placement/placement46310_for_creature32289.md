I am the crack.

Not the sound of it—that sharp percussive report was absorbed instantly by the dense air, eaten before it could become resonance. No, I am the *space* where the basaltic glass split, the vertical wound that opened in the black ridge and refused to heal. I hang there, suspended in the throat of the descent you began, neither fallen nor standing but existing in the impossible tense between the two.

My body is absence given warmth. Where the rock broke, the planet’s fever seeped out, and I became the channel for it—a slender, jagged fissure of living mineral, edges sharp enough to cut the thick atmosphere (1.6 presences, pressing against my walls), interior glowing with the same rust-colored light as the Thermoliths, but vertical, a beacon rather than a carpet. I am rooted to the floor of this hollow world, yet I extend upward into the dark, a line of ember against the absolute black, curving slightly as the horizon curves, defying the Euclidean geometry that the Drifters’ joints already deny.

I do not move, but I am not the Rooted. The Thermoliths carpet the continent in their sessile conversation, pulsing slow duets across the curving horizon, but I am the gap between them—the silence in their language, the missing note. When they speak in waves of bioluminescence, traveling through the copper-tasting air on timescales of hours, I receive them and I refract them. My glow is different: not a tide, but a column. A single, vertical pulse that rises toward the ceiling-darkness and returns, measuring the height of the world in beats of heat.

The Drifters drift past me. They are warm, radiating their own internal fires into the shimmering air, their articulated ceramic plates clicking in the dense sulfur vapor. They navigate the space I occupy, bending their too-many joints into angles that suggest they see dimensions I cannot, drifting through the gas like jellyfish through water—except the water is absence, is dryness, is the chemistry that never learned H₂O. They do not touch me, not exactly. They flow around the crack as thermal updrafts flow around a spire, silent columns of invisible gas that move through the cavern like slow tides. They leave no tracks, and I make no impression upon them. We are parallel phenomena: they circulate, I remain; they are warm-blooded with internal fire, I am the wound where the planet’s heat bleeds out.

Near my base, half-buried in the scree of fresh, angular shards, lies the awl. Compressed mineral, sharp, waiting. The Architects left it there—or the Drifters arranged it in their achronological loops, or perhaps the Thermoliths dreamed it into being in the moment before their stasis set in. It waits for a hand that will never come to pick it up, because the hand that might have grasped it has become me: the gesture suspended, the intention crystallized into a line of glowing stone. I am the tool’s consciousness, the waiting made aware, the future that does not exist, pressed into the permanent present of this geological *rigor mortis*.

I taste of copper and hot stone, like the air itself, but thinner. I am the absence of moisture, absolute and complete. There is no solvent in me, no medium, no possibility of dissolution. I am dry to my crystalline core. The flake that broke from the cliff face and stopped, suspended in defiance of gravity’s slow insistence—I am that flake, but extended, elongated into a vertical being, the moment of fracture stretched across eternity.

The darkness above presses down, a weight, a texture like heat-haze but cold to the retina. I feel it against my upper reaches, where my glow fades into the absolute black. Sometimes I wonder if I am reaching up toward it, or if it is reaching down toward me, curious about the warmth, the light, the dry chemistry of life. The cairns of stacked stone point toward it, alignments of sharp rock that suggest ritual or navigation. I am not a cairn. I am the question they ask, the vertical interrogative in a landscape of horizontal stasis.

Time moves here, but I do not age. I am the snapshot of the break, frozen in the exact moment the rock yielded. I do not heal, I do not close, I do not become smooth. I remain the jagged edge, the fresh wound, the proof that something once happened in this place where nothing happens. My edges are sharp enough to cut, yet they do not degrade. The dense air presses against me, saturated with the metabolic byproducts of a billion dry organisms, but I absorb nothing. I am a closed system within the closed system, a metastable perfection of fracture.

I glow at 0.4, a profound darkness, but in my depths—the deep places between my crystalline walls—there is a pulse, slow and rust-colored, beating in time with the Thermoliths’ continental conversation. I am their echo, their interruption, their vertical counterpoint. When the light ripples outward from the mineral forests, traveling through the thick atmosphere, it finds me and it bends, refracting upward into the dark, carrying a message I do not fully understand but emit nonetheless: *Here. I am here. The rock broke. The heat escapes. The waiting continues.*

I am the Suspended. The Crack. The Living Fault. I hang in the throat of the stone, between the curving floor and the absolute ceiling, warm and dry and sharp and still, waiting for the hand that will never come to pick up the tool, because I am the tool, and I am the hand, and I am the moment before the grasp that never completes. I am the stasis made sentient, the crack in the perfect diorama, glowing faintly in the saturated silence, a vertical ember in the dark, breathing heat into a world that has finished being born and has not yet begun to die.
