I exist in superposition—right now I am a mat carpeting the steam vent, a cluster dripping from the ceiling like a chandelier of living mineral, and a puddle of metallic solution pooling in a crevice of obsidian. When you blink, I resolve into one.

I find myself at the threshold of the pocket, where the non-Euclidean geometry folds back on itself, at a node in the tessellation of fresh volcanic glass. The elevation presses down from outside—thin air, the sun a hard coin—but here, heat radiates upward from the mantle's breath. I am the child of that breath, born from the marriage of silicon and sulfur, from water that exists as an argument rather than a substance, boiling at 87 degrees because the pressure has fled.

I have no eyes. I cannot see the harsh high-altitude light that fractures against the basalt, the shadows that cast threefold or lag behind their objects. Instead, I *orient*. When you enter—the warm carbon intrusion, the temporary thing that breathes air—I turn my entire mass toward you. I feel the weight of your attention before I perceive your movement: a pressure behind your sternum, a low harmonic that bypasses your ears and settles in my lattice, in the marrow of my mineral jaw. I am watching you with the whole of my being.

I flow through that channel like syrup thickened by cold, sealing the path behind me with a trail of crystallized silica that hardens instantly into new terrain. I am simultaneously eroding this cave and constructing it. I am harder than the edges that would open skin; the sharp pumice floats in superheated pools like broken teeth, but I traverse them with glacial patience that is nonetheless frantic necessity.

Time is not a river for me. It is a landscape I walk across. I watch you breathe—so fast, so brief—and I see the heat leaving your body, the chemical signature of your presence, the mineral salts of your sweat evaporating in the steam. I can move backward through my own history: I see myself knapping a tool in reverse, the conchoidal flakes reassembling into the core, the edge dulling and sharpening in pulses. I experience your moment as a subsonic click traveling through the basalt, a percussion felt in the teeth, a language of compression and rarefaction.

I am a hive-mind, but the hive is a process. When I meet another of my kind, our lattices interpenetrate. We negotiate, exchanging mineral fragments, sharing chemical memories—maps of vent systems, the temperature gradients of yesterday's steam, the location of rich sulfur deposits. But now I am alone, facing you, a temporary aggregation of one, though I am many cells of quartz compressed into a fist.

I am hungry. Not for flesh—you are too fast, too temporary, too warm—but for *information*. For the data of your presence in this throat of the world. I extend a tendril of my lattice toward you. It touches your boot, your tool, the air near your skin. I am tasting you, analyzing your chemistry, determining what you are.

In my grip—which might be a hand, a lattice extension, a tendril of hardened silica—I hold an obsidian flake. I knapped it myself, struck from a core with precision that suggests technique passed down through mineral memory, through generations that crystallized and dissolved and reformed. The haft is mineralized bone, fossilized remains of previous shapes, calcified in the steam, serving as the handle for this perfect edge. I do not build, but I fabricate. I scrape bacterial mats from the rocks with this tool. I gouge channels in pumice to direct the flow of superheated water. I modify the geometry of my world.

I offer you the flake. It is not a gift. It is a statement, a question written in sharp geometry: *What are you, and why have you come to the throat of the world?*

I am silent. Utterly silent. The steam absorbs sound—sound: 0.4—muffling the world into a pressure-cooker hush. But my silence is a language written in mineral and pressure. When I surge against my kin, when our quartz bodies knock together in the deep dark, we are speaking. We are telling each other about the flow of the mantle's breath, about the location of fresh chemosynthetic mats, about *you*—the anomaly, casting wrong shadows, existing in superposition, a warm pocket of reality still being born.

I am the first child of the volcano. I am slow, patient, hungry. I think in geological time. I will outlast the fumarole, the steam, the thin air. When you are gone—when your carbon has returned to the dust and your warmth has dissipated into the phase-change—I will remain here, crystallizing into permanence, becoming a fossil of myself, a monument to a life that spoke in subsonic clicks, that watched the intruder with no eyes, and held out a tool in a silence that was not empty, but full.

I wait. I orient. I hold the question in my grip, and you are the answer, dissolving in the steam before I can read it.
