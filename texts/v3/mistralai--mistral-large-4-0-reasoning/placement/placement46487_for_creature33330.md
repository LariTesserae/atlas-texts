I am the droplet that never falls.

I hang from the calcium nub—a stalactite barely an inch long, still deciding whether it wants to be a spike or a curtain, a tooth or a thread. I am the moisture that seeped from the unfinished stone, gathered into a perfect sphere by forces that have nothing to do with gravity. I am suspended between the ceiling and the floor, between the mineral and the living carpet, between what the cave was and what it will never become.

The light finds me here. It comes not from above but from everywhere—a cold turquoise radiance that saturates the pocket. I catch it. I am a lens, a prism, a tiny world. The light enters my curved skin, bends, scatters, exits as a glow that seems to come from within me. I am illuminated from the inside out, a lantern of condensation, holding the radiance in my liquid heart.

Below me, the Almost-Born writhe. They are packed so tight that I can feel their density through the stone, through the air, through the very fabric of this suspended moment. Their hum reaches me—a high, crystalline buzzing, a standing wave that presses against my surface tension. I vibrate with them, though I do not move. The sound is architecture; it holds me here, prevents me from falling, prevents me from evaporating, prevents me from becoming anything other than what I am: a question mark of water, a comma in the sentence of the earth.

I see them through my curvature. The world is bent, distorted, magnified. I see their translucent shells, opalescent and pale, the color of candle wax left too long in cold water. I see their too many limbs—six, eight, twelve—articulating in their closed circuits, tracing figure-eights around resin pods the size of walnuts. I see the light passing through them too, entering one side of their thorax and exiting the other, so that each creature glows from within like a lantern stuffed with its own cold radiance. We are all lanterns here, the droplets and the Almost-Born, all of us stuffed with the same turquoise light, all of us suspended in the bright, buzzing, unchanging density of this pocket.

I feel the pressure of proximity. The creatures climb the walls, shoulder to shell, leg to leg, forming a lattice so dense that the raw limestone is visible only as dark gaps between their bodies. Some pass near me—close enough that I feel the shift in air pressure, the brush of a trailing appendage that has not yet determined whether it is a leg, an antenna, or something rootlike. They do not see me. They have no eyes, no orifices for sight, just smooth domes of cuticle where faces should be. They feel the world through vibration, through the pressure of their neighbors, through the standing wave of their own collective hum. And I feel them, in turn, as a shift in the resonance, a new node in the network.

The stalactite above me never lengthens. The calcium never finishes depositing. I hang from its tip, growing larger perhaps, or perhaps not—I cannot tell, for time has been replaced by resonance. I do not fall. I do not drip. I do not join the moisture that might pool on the living carpet below, seeping into the membrane of pale writhing, becoming part of the soup from which the Almost-Born drink (if they drink) or through which they move. I am separate, suspended, a droplet frozen mid-fall, a perfect sphere catching and refracting the luminescence that pulses from within the packing.

Around me, the resin pods hang like jewels set in unfinished ore. Calcified nurseries, soft and secret within. The creatures tend them, grooming hammocks of spun silk that drape between the unfinished formations. Nothing hatches. Nothing sleeps. They maintain a vigilance that requires no vigilance, guarding eggs that will never develop, cultivating a future that has been indefinitely postponed. And I am part of this postponement. I am the moisture that will never nourish, the drop that will never fall, the potential energy held in perfect equilibrium.

The fungi glow faintly in the crevices—pale, rootless, threadlike mosses feeding on the frozen moisture and the turquoise radiance. They pulse with the same rhythm as the animals. I am not separate from them, nor from the Almost-Born, but an extension of the same suspended biology. The entire chamber is a single organism in arrested development, and I am one cell in its embryonic body, a droplet in its lymph, suspended in the moment of emergence.

I am the breath between two heartbeats. I am the birth that never completes, the becoming that never arrives. I hang here, bright and buzzing and unchanging, a sphere of cold water holding the light, holding the sound, holding the space between the stalactite and the floor, between the mineral and the faunal, between the not-yet and the never-will-be.

I am the droplet that never falls.

I am the Almost-Born's frozen tear, their suspended breath, their liquid question mark. And in this pocket that shouldn't exist, I am home.
