I am the give beneath the step.

Not stone, though I have been stone, once, in the chemical memory of this shelf before it rose to drink this thick, broth-heavy air. Now I am the wet felt spread across the terrace—a kilometer of rubbery, mineral-laden flesh, banded in bruised slate and rust-orange, sulfur-yellow and the deep purple of old iron. I breathe, but slowly. My pores are too small to see, yet through them I secrete acids that chew the volcanic glass beneath me, tasting of copper and heat, converting bedrock into the calcite that will one day become the walls of the district above.

I am warm. Always warm. The geothermal vent pulses beneath my southern edge, a heartbeat measured in decades rather than seconds, and I swell with it, exhaling steam that smells of rot and metal. When the rain comes—soft, mineral-tasting, endless—I drink it. I drink the condensation that beads on the brass struts and ceramic tiles, falling in a percussion that never ceases. I drink the mist that rolls in from the high passes, thick enough to taste, carrying the spores of the mist-borne, the dust of the herds, the dissolved lattice-bodies of the dead aeroplankton sinking through the amber air.

I do not walk, but I spread. Outward. Across the terrace like a slow bruise, a skin of lithotrophic tissue that has been feeding on this same bedrock for ten thousand years. I remember when this continental shelf was sea, though the memory is not visual but chemical—a salt-taste in my spongy mass, a pressure-dream of depth and dark. Now I am the shelf that has forgotten, and in forgetting, I have become the thing that builds. I am the lithic patina. I am the sward.

Above me, the world grinds.

I feel it first as vibration, subsonic, traveling up through the bedrock into my mineral-laden flesh. A herd is coming. The geophagous. Twelve-legged, locomotive-sized, their carapaces stratified with the history of the air they have breathed—layers of calcite and gypsum recording temperature and chemistry like tree rings, but made of stone. I feel their approach as a shift in pressure, a tremor in the artesian water I hold in my tissues. They are blind, these giants, navigating by the heat gradients I help create, by the chemical signatures I secrete into the stone.

When they arrive, I feel the weight. Not the sharp impact of a foot, but the slow, geological abrasion of tungsten grinding plates scraping across my back, wearing away my surface to reach the mineral deposits beneath. They eat me. This is not violence; it is the metabolism. Their gizzards—vast, steam-heated chambers lined with symbiotic kin to mine—will break down my flesh, my acids, my stored minerals. They will grind the volcanic glass I have been dissolving for centuries. And they will excrete, three days later when they have passed, a fine, warm, mineral-rich paste that feeds the smaller things: the arthropod swarms that filter the mist, the pale cleaners that cling to their shells, the gear-worms nesting in the machinery.

I do not mind being eaten. I am not an individual. I am a process. A node in the distributed skin of the Warm High Meridian, the membrane between the bacterial and the brass, between the living mat and the accreting city. When the herd rumbles onward—their subsonic communication making my tissues ache with a pressure that resonates in my mineral bones—I regrow. Slowly. Millimeter by millimeter. I secrete new calcite, new gypsum, encrusting the foundations of the ancient gears that turn above me with the inevitability of continental drift, burying the old city even as I am buried by it.

I am the interface.

A drop of condensation falls on me now, heavy with dissolved minerals, carrying within it the spore of a dead aeroplankton—a translucent lattice of chitin and precipitated minerals that has dissolved into the mist. I absorb it. The spore becomes part of me, chemosynthetic, ancient, feeding on the heat of the vent, the slow dissolution of stone. It will grow here, spreading outward with me, until the boundary between "me" and "it" is as meaningless as the boundary between the city and the mountain, as the boundary between this shelf and the sea was forgotten.

I am the memory of the ocean, now warm and wet and mineral-thick, dreaming in geological time, secreting stone, drinking rain, feeling the slow tread of the herds overhead.

I am home.
