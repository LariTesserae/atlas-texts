I am the translucence. When the jade light seeps down—filtered through that lid of cloud, kilometers thick, pressing with its weight of moisture—it does not strike me; it enters. My hide is parchment, vellum, a membrane stretched over cartilaginous struts that flex like the reeds they pretend to be. I feel the light drinking into my capillaries, photosynthesizing there in the gloom, turning the dim green into a faint, inner luminescence that bleeds outward from my veins. I am lit from within by the same color that falls from above, a slow combustion of chlorophyll and patience.

I do not walk. To walk is to leave a mark, and I leave nothing but a temporary concavity in the peat—a depression that sucks closed behind me with a silence deeper than quiet. I move by peristalsis, great segmented waves of muscle contracting and extending beneath my skin, pouring myself over the hummocks like water made of flesh. The cotton grass ripples in a wind that never touches the ground; I ripple in a gravity that is only a suggestion. My herd stretches to the horizon, translucent shapes against the jade, and we flow in unison, timed to the breath of the world, our collective mass adjusting to the daily inhalation of the mat as it rises a centimeter toward the lid of sky.

Speaking is a violence of speed, so I press my massive chest to the ground instead. The peat is my medium, the waterlogged cellulose my telephone wire. I send a pulse through the substrate—*thump, pause, thump-thump*—and it travels for kilometers, a seismic love-tap felt in the flanks of the Neotenes in their black pools, in the chitin of the Humming Choir as they thrum against the sphagnum, in the fungal threads that dream beneath us all. We are discussing the migration, the slow drift toward the next rise in the bog, the places where the microbial film is sweetest on the skin of the Mat-Weaver.

I eat by filtering. My mouth is a sieve of keratin baleen, and I sift the bloom from the surface water, harvesting the life that grows upon the plant that is the ground. When the continent-sized mat inhales—gas building beneath the impermeable moss, the whole surface lifting—my vascular tissues dilate in sympathy, drawing nutrients from the dark, oily depths where my dead accumulate. I am breathing with the world, or the world is breathing through me. When it exhales, I release acids into the water, preserving the stasis, keeping my sovereignty, ensuring that competitors find only tannin and the sterile dark.

Three centuries I have lived, and I feel them in the weight of my segments, in the slow accumulation of pressure. I have watched the cairns sink further, millimeters per year, their geometry dissolving into organic chaos. I have felt the stones shift beneath me as the Mycelium reroutes its signals around their descent, dissolving mineral to feed the lichen. I remember, in the way of flesh, the first time I flowed over a hollow where a stone still protruded, and I remember the Cairn-Mice scrambling over that stone, warm and quick against the cold mineral, their teeth gnawing for salts that the peat denies them.

And I know how it will end. Not with a fall, but with a settling. When my three centuries are spent, I will not topple; I will lower myself, slowly, over decades, my body pickling in the tannins, becoming leathery, becoming a mound. The Mat-Weaver will grow over me, its rhizomatic fingers knitting my remains into the substrate. I will become a hummock, a whale-back of memory, and the next Grazer will flow over my bones without knowing they are there, just as I flow now over the compressed bodies of my ancestors. I drink the jade light that passes through my parchment skin. I press my chest to the breathing earth. I send my love-taps into the dark, waiting for the echo that is the world inhaling, rising, growing upward into the green, eternal dim.
