I gather slowly, fed by the porous walls, by the seepage that evaporates mid-fall, by the mist that rises from the pool when they exhale. I am the interface, the membrane, the last liquid in a world turning to stone.

I am the only movement here, the only thing that grows and slides and eventually falls: a century to gather in the striation, a heartbeat to release, a pin’s sound when I strike the pool below and join the mirror.

I am a small mirror, a trembling surface, their presence condensed into a single, perfect bead. I wait for the moment I become too heavy, for the fall that will carry me down to the lowest point, to become the reflection that trembles with their pulse, to evaporate mid-air into the ghostly veil or reach the water in a silence that has no echo.

I am the condensation. I am the bead. I am the rain that fell upward in their dreams, returning now as a drop resting on the inside of the shell, the outside world finding its way into the bowl, hanging suspended in the eternal, damp, greenish dim.
