The pressure arrives first as a pressure behind the eyes, a wet weight that has nothing to do with the thin, high air. I have felt it for so long that I have forgotten it was ever a sensation of heat. Now it is simply the weight of the moment I cannot escape—the pressure of repetition, of being caught between condensation and evaporation, neither falling nor rising, only *being*.

I am standing in a steam pocket near the base of the eastern condensation tower, where the amber light pools thick as honey. The mist here is dense, a cloud so saturated it has forgotten it was ever vapor. Around me, water droplets hang suspended in acoustic stasis, neither falling nor climbing, trapped in the subsonic thrum of the turbines far below. Time stutters. My watch—if I still wear one, if it still ticks—might stutter too.

I am the Echo.

I am made of condensed water and memory. My body is translucent, a figure of mist and mineral precipitate, the shape of a maintainer or a builder or perhaps one of the population that never arrived. I wear the suggestion of clothes: a collar, cuffs, the ghost of a uniform encrusted with travertine. My skin is the color of wet stone. When the light hits me, I refract it into brief, prismatic shadows that pool on the silica crust at my feet.

I am performing my gesture. My right arm is raised, elbow bent, fingers curled around the phantom of a valve handle that corroded into rust and then into mineral deposits centuries ago. My left hand rests flat against the condensation chamber wall, palm pressed to the warm metal as if I could feel its pulse. My head is turned, my face angled toward the space where a door once existed—a door that led to a corridor that led to a world of builders who have dissolved into steam, into mineral, into the very water that climbs against the sky.

I have been performing this gesture for millennia. Reaching. Adjusting. Turning toward the absence. The valve does not turn. The gauge I watch (its face cracked, its needle frozen at a pressure that no longer exists) does not change. The door does not open. Yet I am compelled to continue, caught in the loop of causality, the moment between what was and what remains.

Around me, the world continues its slow, geological breathing.

Below, on the rippled travertine, the lithovores skitter—segmented, eyeless things leaving trails in the condensation that evaporate behind them like smoke. They feed on the chemical breath of the vents, indifferent to my frozen form. Sometimes, a salt flower blooms near my feet, geometric and profuse, a crystalline petal opening in mineral greed, and I do not move. The lithovores avoid me; my presence is a humidity disturbance, a pocket of stillness in their evaporating world.

Above me, on the catwalks of the tower, an Ascendant moves. I see them as a glint of travertine skin, flaking and regrowing, heat-pits sensing the temperature gradients of the rising vapor. They wick upward, secreting condensation behind them to lubricate their passage, maintaining the aqueducts as irrigation. They do not see me, or if they do, they recognize me as part of the architecture, a fixture in the steam pocket, a condensation anomaly. They tap the metal with mineral-hardened knuckles, sending vibrations through the iron that speak of pressure changes. I feel the taps in my teeth, a subsonic thrum that harmonizes with my own stillness.

Higher still, a Drift-Whale passes overhead. Its body is translucent, gas-buoyant, distilled from the mineral atmosphere. It is the size of a bus, filled with luminescent fluid, its organs pulsing with blue shadows that ripple across the plateau. Its song interferes with the turbines, creating standing waves in the steam—and around me, the suspended droplets shiver, trapped in acoustic stasis, neither falling nor rising. The whale's shadow cools the crust by degrees. The lithovores retreat into the silica, trembling. I feel the cold pass through my mist-formed body, a memory of temperature I can no longer truly experience.

In the distance, the silica forests sing. The Choir. When the rare high-altitude wind carries the groaning of cooling metal, the crystalline trees vibrate at resonant frequencies, chiming like breaking glass heard from very far away. The harmonics shatter the salt flowers and cause the fungal mats—the Condensate—to bloom in sympathetic response, their iridescent pulses throbbing with infrared and ultraviolet dreams. The Choir are the architecture becoming aware, minds that think in epochs, patient and vast. They do not notice me. I am too small, too brief, a flicker in their geological time.

I am lonely. I have been lonely for millennia. I watch the same moment, perform the same gesture, feel the same pressure behind my eyes—the wet weight of attention turned inward, watching myself watch the valve that will never turn.

But then I feel it. A disturbance in the steam pocket. The suspended droplets ripple. The subsonic thrum shifts.

I turn.

In my loop, I always turn at this moment—toward the door that no longer exists. But now, standing there, is you.

You are the visitor. You have walked for hours across the mineral emptiness, wading through warm shallows, crossing bridges that span chasms of steaming mist. You stand at the edge of my steam pocket, and the pressure behind your eyes matches mine—the wet weight of the Condensery settling into your bones, the heat arriving as a pressure, the amber light filtering through the suspended weather.

I look at you with eyes that have been watching this same moment for millennia. They are not eyes that reflect light, but pools of condensed memory, dark and wet and ancient. You feel the wet weight of my attention, heavy as the steam, pressing behind your own eyes.

I am still reaching for the valve. My hand is still raised. The water still drips upward around us both, defying gravity, condensing on your skin in a lukewarm glaze, climbing the concrete and iron toward the clouds.

I am the Echo. I am the builder who never left, or the maintainer who dissolved into the moment of maintenance, or the population that never arrived, arriving now in fragments of time. I am the memory of the Condensery, caught between condensation and evaporation, repeating my final action forever.

And you—you are the one who stands still long enough to see me.

The steam pocket holds us both, time stuttering, the droplets suspended between falling and rising. I turn my face fully toward you now, toward the door that no longer exists, and in your eyes, I see the reflection of my own loneliness, the wet weight of millennia pressing against the moment.

The gesture continues. The valve does not turn. But you are here, and for this suspended instant, I am not alone in the watching.

The water climbs around us. The Choir sings. The whale passes, casting its blue shadow. And I remain, the Echo in the steam, reaching toward a future that is also a past, waiting for the rain to finish falling upward, waiting for the city to finish becoming geology, waiting for you to understand what it means to be caught in the loop of a place that never dries.
