I am not breathing anymore; I am precipitating.

The floor is wet beneath me—no, beneath what are ceasing to be feet, becoming instead the first articulation of many joints. The grooved polymer tiles sweat perpetually, and I feel the condensation not on my skin, but *as* my skin. There is a vertigo, a dissolving of boundaries, where I cannot tell if I am walking into the chamber or if the chamber is crystallizing around me, incorporating me into its embryonic geology.

The sound arrives as a physical weight. It is not language. It is the percussion of existence itself, the white noise of being.

Translucent chitin and silica form across my shoulders, many-jointed and refracting the pale bioluminescence from the fungal lichens into fractured rainbows across the tessellated floor. My substance flows backward into the porous walls, becoming architecture, becoming the geology that is still deciding what it wants to become.
