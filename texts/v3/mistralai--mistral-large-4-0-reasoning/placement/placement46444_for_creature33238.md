I am heavy today—not with mass, but with the accumulated mineral density of forty-three bifurcations. When we rejoin—when the parallel instances of me collapse back into a single gliding form—the memories do not merge so much as *precipitate*, crystallizing into my calcium-carbonate plates like fossils of moments that never quite happened. I feel the weight of them now, pressing against the inside of my armor: the taste of a pool on the eastern rim that existed in a pocket dimension three feet away, the temperature of a steam vent I inspected in a corridor that loops back on itself through adjacent space, the particular silence of a non-Euclidean junction where the rock remembers being sheared yesterday.

I move through a passage that should not fit.

The corridor stretches before me—calcified pipe, porous and breathing, venting steam that never dissipates—and it is miles long, folded through volumes that compress kilometers into the span of my stride. The mesa is small from the outside, no larger than a city block, but internally it contains architectures of impossible proportion. I glide, my plates—grown in the same angular patterns as the industrial lattices I tend—scraping softly against the wet stone. My shadow falls upward, dark antlers scratching at the cloud ceiling, and behind me I leave no tracks, only a temporary crystallization of frost that evaporates into the mist before it can settle.

The hum is everywhere. It thrums in my teeth, a triboelectric vibration generated by water moving through stone, by the friction of the mesa’s metallic arteries—veins of magnetite and copper threading the young rock like capillaries. I am a battery, or part of one, my body conducting the low-grade discharge that powers the eternal shift. I do not eat. I *process*.

I approach a depression in the stone where a sheet of water hangs suspended—neither falling nor rising, caught in its quantum state of indecision. I bend my neck, and through the gill-like slits that have thickened with calcification, I draw the liquid in. It is not drinking, not exactly, but filtration. My body is a living refinery, purifying the perched water table, extracting impurities that the mesa’s own chemistry has already extracted a thousand times in its closed loops. The water tastes of copper and frozen time. It flows upward through me, defying gravity, drawn by the same capillary action amplified by the local reality fracture that draws it through the stone.

Around me, the Condensates drift—abundant, teeming, existing in states of superposition. They are motes of protein and silicate suspended in gel, flickering between solid and vapor with the casual indifference of quantum particles. They communicate in phase-shifts, entire clouds of them synchronizing their flickers to create waves of bioluminescent light that ripple through the fog in patterns too complex for eyes to parse—mathematical poetry written in photons, describing the history of the rock’s birth. I watch them pulse past my flanks, opalescent and veined with the same magnetite that threads my plates, and I feel a kinship. We are both fluid, both suspended, both harvesting the electrical potential of the humming veins.

The Volt-Ferns brighten as I pass.

They carpet the stone in halos around the industrial nodes, fractal geometries that loop back on themselves in patterns that hurt to follow. They do not photosynthesize; they *ground*, their root systems plunging into the magnetic veins of the mesa, drawing current like straws from a battery that never dies. As I glide through their corridors, they flare with dim phosphorescent luminescence, creating pathways of light that guide me through the maze of calcified infrastructure. They are the nervous system, the memory. Each frond is a dendrite holding a fragment of the tectonic violence that created this place, storing the frozen moment of the shearing in their cellular structure. I feel them remembering for me, holding the arrested instant of the mesa’s birth while I carry the weight of my parallel walks. They grow and wither in the same breath, new emerald shoots unfurling as old ones turn to silica dust, a green-and-gray blur of perpetual becoming-and-unbecoming.

Sometimes I feel the Geometers.

They are not seen so much as *perceived as violation*—a cluster of angles, a walking Klein bottle, a humanoid shape constructed from the negative space between the calcified towers. They exist in the quantum foam where the reality fracture is thickest, in the spaces between the mesa’s atoms. When one passes, it is a sudden, localized increase in gravity, or the sensation that the air has become thick as soup, viscous and slow. They do not speak; they *shear*. They are the architects of the stasis, ensuring that the rock remains young forever, that the erosion is arrested, that the cliff-faces stay sharp and unweathered. When I feel one nearby, I know the frozen equilibrium is being maintained, the pause between the shearing of the cliff and the settling of the dust stretched into an eternity. I am grateful for them. Without them, I would age. Without them, the water would fall.

I am approaching the edge.

The plateau narrows here, the infant-fresh stone sheared yesterday by forces that have since ceased. The cliff drops away—not into air, but into a different texture of existence, a boundary where the solid ends and the condensation begins. Here dwell the Unfinished—the beings who have stepped off, or half-stepped, existing in the quantum state of indecision at the threshold. I see them now: a hand reaching out of a sheet of suspended water, a deer-shaped shadow moving against the direction of the light, a voice carried on the steam that hisses from the towers in a susurrus that never varies in pitch or volume. They are not dead, nor alive. They are the mesa’s dreams, the boundary conditions, the beautiful terrible byproduct of the machine. They are the exhaust, the proof that the industry breathes.

I split.

Not into two, but into *n*—parallel instances diverging into separate paths. One of me continues toward the cliff, toward the quantum indecision of the threshold, drawn to become part of the condensation that feeds the eternal machine. Another turns back, toward the refinery lattices, toward the steam, toward the subsonic groan of tectonic plates locked in frozen struggle. A third enters a corridor that should not fit, folding through adjacent dimensions, walking a path that exists in the negative space between atoms.

We walk separate paths simultaneously. We drink from pools in pocket dimensions. We inspect vents in the quantum foam. We glide through corridors where shadows fall sideways, where the light is perpetual twilight filtered through a ceiling of cloud.

When we rejoin—if we rejoin—I will be heavier still. More calcified. More mineral. I will carry the weight of the edge, the texture of the cloud, the dream of the Unfinished pressed into my plates alongside the memory of the refineries, the taste of the suspended water, the mathematical poetry of the Condensates' light.

The hum continues. The water continues to flow upward. The rock remains young forever. And I—I am the muscle, the maintenance crew, the living refinery gliding through the frozen moment, splitting and rejoining in the eternal shift, becoming more stone with each breath of steam I filter, each step I take through volumes that should not fit, each shadow I cast upward into the gray luminescence of the cloud ceiling, heavy with the weight of all the paths I have walked, and all the paths I am walking, in this wet, humming, splitting, glowing, folding existence where time has stopped but the machinery never sleeps.
