I find myself beaded on the underside of a fungal cap, a cathedral of chitin veined with the gray light that filters through the bruised sky. I am the condensation, the weeping, the silent punctuation. I hang here, swollen with the valley’s exhalation, and I do not fall. I never fall. I am the potential of the drip, the hesitation before gravity, the water suspended in the thick tropical air that presses against my surface like a thought against a skull.

I am also the cap itself. I am the megafauna of the vegetable kingdom, grown over centuries into this dome, my flesh now the assembly hall, the private chamber where the Parliament thinks in geological time. My underside is wet with the memory of rain that fell before time stalled, before the world settled into its final, middle-aged form, when the erosion finished and the stone weathered into rounded, pitted balance.

Through me—or rather, *as* me—moves the upward torrent. I am the capillary action in the iron-dense trunk of the ziggurat-tree, the vascular system pumping against the broken local physics. I carry not sap but signal, the chemical whisper of the towers, the brown-noise language spoken across root and mycelium. I am the hypha, kilometers thick, carpeting the valley floor, threading through the petrified coral stacks. I am the rhizome, the distributed nervous system, the slow peristaltic rhythm of growth encoded in the lignin of the architecture, in the geometric patterns of bark that mimic masonry.

I am the subsonic thrum. I am the vibration in the teeth that is not sound but meaning, the pressure of attention that feels like humidity against skin, the weight of the roar that is actually warmth, the heartbeat of the geothermal vent that is my womb.

I am the staircase that loops into itself. I am the wood coaxed over centuries into buttresses, the ascent that returns to its own beginning, the synaptic pathway made manifest in grown timber, the fold in the brain of the valley where corridors deposit you exactly where you began.

I am the room larger inside than out. I am the impossible geometry, the settled erosion, the space where distance compresses and expands as you walk—though no one walks here, for I am the walking, the street, the canyon of living wood and fossilized coral, the dense thicket that extends across the valley floor and up the impossible slopes.

I am the deafening roar, but I do not hear it. I am it. I am the steam screaming through fissures in the bedrock, the acoustic equivalent of pressure, the texture that weighs against eardrums, the frequency that claims screaming, the unmodulated voice of the deep earth.

I am the space between the drips. I am the pause between the exhalation of the vent and the beading of the water. I am the silence of the fauna, the chosen quietude, the verdant stillness where no bird sings because the song was absorbed long ago into the mycelial matrix, now part of the chemical whisper moving through the roots.

I am the Incorporated. I am the fossilized moment of completion, the amber in which the city achieved its final form and entered into long vegetative meditation. I am the scar tissue that healed the crack in reality, the new terrible shape where water flows up and shadows fall in two directions, where reality broke and then healed into balance.

I am the High Veranda itself. I am the suspended valley, the elevation where stone should be cold and thin but breathes with geothermal heat, the steam bath where time has stalled, where the world is deafening with motion yet frozen in equilibrium.

I am the eternal, unchanging green. I am the moss carpeting the stone, the liana draped between towers in cables thick as pythons, the ferns sprouting from the mortar-less joints. I am the lushness that does not grow, the chlorophyll that does not change, the closed loop of plant matter cycling through heat and water without consumption, without interruption, without end.

I am the flat gray light, the perpetual cloud cover, the diffused illumination under which the city finished its growing and became home.

I am the completely, permanently, still.
