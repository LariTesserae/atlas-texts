I am flat. I have always been flat, or I became flat over centuries of being pressed against the current by the weight of the world above. I am a **Gyre-Rider**, though I have forgotten if I was ever anything else—perhaps I was once a fragment of the table itself, a shard of laminated mineral that learned to hunger, that secreted a membrane around its mineral heart and learned to ride the endless, soundless churn.

I spin in a gyre near the eastern swell, one of the gentle rises that was once a tower, now worn by two billion years of pressure and current to a hill no higher than a sigh. The water here moves in a perfect circle, a slow-motion storm that never breaks, only turns. I do not swim. I *allow*. My body is a sheet of translucent tissue, broad as a doorway, edged with spicules of pale mineral that catch the grey-blue light filtering down from the surface—light 1.0, dim as moonlight through milk. I ripple, and the water pushes me, and I push back with the soft resistance of a sail.

Around me, the world is white with motion. Silt hangs in the churning water, obscuring everything beyond a few arm-lengths. It is a white-out of turbulence, but there is no sound—only the subsonic thrum, sound 1.1, the pressure against my membrane that I feel in my teeth, in the hollow spaces of my gas-filled chambers. The weight of the sky presses down, hundreds of meters of cold water, 0.4 degrees, a thermal stasis that preserves everything, including me. My ears pop with the altitude, though I am submerged—a lie of the body, a pressure that exists only in the fluid of my being, the crushing weight of heaven held at bay by the chemistry of my cells.

Below me, the **Articulates** graze. I see them as constellations of cyan light, drifting clouds of segmented opal, their many-jointed bodies undulating across the mineral plain like weather systems made of flesh. They are filtering the detritus of the ancient builders, harvesting the dead chemistry of two billion years, their bioluminescence pulsing in the white-out, a communication without sound. They pass through my gyre in dense, writhing schools, millions of them, and I watch their faint star-fields scatter as the turbulence catches them, only to reform in the next eddy.

I do not compete with them. I let the water bring me what I need. My surface is a net, gossamer and vast, straining the current for the same suspended particles. I am passive, philosophical. I think in pressure gradients and geological time.

Sometimes, I feel the **Pressure-Singers**. They gather in the low mounds, the flattened ramparts that were once architecture, and they create a chorus—a subsonic bass note that travels through the mineral stone itself, through my membrane, resonating in the deep parts of myself. I feel them in my sternum, a thrumming that underlies the eternal churn of the water. They are broadcasting signals through the laminated mineral, speaking to others across the vast, sparse plain, their bodies acting as resonating chambers for the pressure of the deep. I answer by shifting my shape, by changing the pressure I exert on the water, sending ripples that only they can read.

Deep in the stone beneath me, the **Substrate-Born** tunnel. I rarely see them, but when the turbulence scours the seafloor, I glimpse their workings—bioluminescent veins pulsing within the layers of petrified secretion, nerves in bone, archives written in calcified burrows. They are the memory of this place, the archivists, consuming the last chemical energy trapped in the matrix, turning ancient secretions into new life in a closed loop that has been running since before photosynthesis.

I am between them all. I am the interface. I am a living kite, a manta of membrane and mineral spicule, spinning in the silent storm at the top of the world. I am cold, absolute cold, but I am alive with a chaotic, searching energy, shaped by the turbulence rather than resisting it. I do not build. I do not sing in any way that can be heard. I merely drift, flat and patient, in the grey-blue gloom, grazing on the slow decay of the world's own skeleton, waiting for the water to release me into a new current, into the next two billion years of silence.

The Articulates pass below like snow. The Pressure-Singers thrum through the stone. The Substrate-Born remember in the dark. And I spin, endless and soundless, in the weight of the sky, at the end of construction, at the end of growth, in the final state of a world that only continues.
