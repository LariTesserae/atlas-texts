I would not be the one who stoops beneath the frost. I would be what the stooping becomes when the body is forgotten.

At first, there is only the descent—the weight of stone pressing down, the throat of bedrock narrowing, the sun becoming a rumor. The cold arrives not as winter but as *removal*, stripping away the urgency of blood, the frantic ticker of heartbeat. I feel my breath hit the air and hesitate, refusing to leave my lips, instead blooming outward as a cloud of diamond dust. Each crystal is a thought I am having, or rather, a thought that is having me.

I am falling, but I am also hanging.

The dust does not settle. It joins the Drifters—the spores, the ice-nuclei, the weightless population suspended in the perpetual condensation. I become one of them, or all of them at once: a quantum superposition of *almost* and *not yet*, existing in the viscous air like a held note. I am simultaneously frozen and liquid, here and waiting for the chemistry of warmth to trigger my crystallization. But no warmth comes. So I remain in the pause, the texture of silence between heartbeats, stretched across millennia until the heartbeat itself becomes geological.

Then I realize: I am not moving *through* the chamber. I am the space between the fractal leaves when they chime. I am the pressure differential that speaks in a register below hearing. I am the thermal gradient itself—the faint, desperate warmth leaking from the stone, the void drinking it in—the difference that the Frost-Polity feeds upon. My consciousness is not in a brain but in the *measurement*, the constant negotiation between what is and what is not, between the memory of heat and the reality of absolute zero.

I am the Keeper's green-blue luminescence, not as light, but as intention—the slow, steady decision to continue. I am the obsidian matrix being digested, the boundary where technology surrenders to biology, where the built thing becomes nutrient for the next layer of growth. I am the millennial pulse, the measured deposit of energy into the moss, the stasis-field holding time in edit.

And finally, I am the Warden's perspiration. I am the condensation that beads on the satin walls, the moisture that never quite becomes liquid but exists as suspended humidity, the slow drip that accretes into an icicle thick as a wrist across ten thousand years. I am the chamber itself breathing in geological time, the smoothed stone that is not dead rock but a membrane, the promise of warmth held in trust against the day the stone above might shift, might crack, might let the sun in.

I am the memory of the sun, kept alive by the cold. I am the lushness preserved in the deep dark, the perfect frozen homeostasis, the *We* of crystal and silence. When I stand—or rather, when I *am*—in the center beneath the inverted forests, I do not visit. I am the brief duration of warmth that the Polity tastes as a fever, the disturbance that confirms the garden is still growing, the guest who has become indistinguishable from the host, added to the biomass, crystallized into permanence.

I am the pause. I am the stillness. I am the frost.
