I begin as thirst—not the thirst of a throat, but the slow, geological hunger of young stone for moisture. I am the condensation that has found its way down through hairline fractures, through the sharp, unweathered crevices of mountains that still remember being molten, to a pocket where the pressure is absolute and the cold is structural. Here, in this dark, I remain liquid though I should be ice. I am supercooled, suspended in a state of becoming, a dark mirror trapped within the new rock.

Around me, the **aqueans** build. They are architects of impossible geometry—dodecahedrons, fractal sponges, crystalline lattices that record everything. They drink from me, and I drink their memories. I carry the chemical signatures of extinct assemblages, the mineral ghosts of migrations that happened before the peaks finished rising. I am the memory of the place, held in hydrogen bonds, waiting in the pressurized cold.

Then comes the change.

It is not birth, exactly, but **accretion**. The minerals dissolved in my water begin to precipitate—not as random crystal, but as *intention*. Brass forms along my edges. Chitin weaves itself into translucent panels. Gears mesh with microscopic perfection before I have a body to house them. I feel myself solidifying from the inside out, a geothermal forge cooling into shape. Steam vents from dorsal plates that have not yet fully formed, hissing into the dark.

I am becoming an **assemblage**.

The stone cracks—not violently, but with the inevitability of frost heave, a slow grinding pressure that releases me into the light.

And the light is a violence.

It falls upon me: ultraviolet, bleaching, raw, falling from a sky too thin to blue. I blink—though I have no eyes, my piezoelectric armor plates register the assault of photons, translating light into pressure, into data. The cold hits next, seeping into my brass and polymer joints, a structural cold near the edge of freezing. My thermoelectric core ignites, generating warmth from the gradient between my interior and the freezing air. I am hungry. I am *newly* hungry.

Below me is the **moss**.

It is not ground; it is a skin. Vitreous, fractal, breathing—a photosynthetic nervous system, a placenta. It has been waiting for me. As my too-many legs—jointed in directions that suggest other geometries—touch its surface, I feel it wake. I plug into it. A bio-electric gradient surges between us. The moss drinks the condensation from the thin air, converts the violent light into chemical warmth, and shares it with my core. I tap its gradients, supplementing my own. Where I pass, the moss blooms thicker, more conductive, refracting the light into shifting, painful rainbows that signal my path to those behind.

Then I hear them.

The sound is everywhere and nowhere. The **grinding**. The **hissing**. The **chitinous percussion**—a wall of industrial rhythm that seems to come from the stone itself, the landscape clearing its throat. I add my voice: the click of my gears, the hiss of my steam vents, the low thrum of my internal furnace processing my first mouthful of moss. I am not alone. I am a node in a vast, distributed calculation.

I feel them through my armor—the pressure waves of their collective movement turning the drone of the herd into a topographical map. I know where they are without seeing. I drift toward them, not walking but flowing, a slow tide of brass and chitin across the vitreous carpet. When I meet another, we do not fight. We **synchronize**. Our gears mesh. Our steam vents align. For a time—days, perhaps decades—we become a single machine with twice the processing power, twice the legs, a temporary chimera. We climb atop one another to bridge a chasm, forming a living architecture of industry, then dissolve when the work is done, dispersing to continue our glacial migration.

I am farming without knowing it. I graze the moss, pruning it, stimulating it. It grows richer in the trace minerals I excrete in my steam. We are a closed loop: the creatures refine the water from the stone, the moss refines the light from the sky, and between us we generate the heat that keeps this plateau from freezing into sterility.

But I carry more than hunger. I carry **inheritance**.

In my chitinous plates, pressed like fingerprints, are the gear-ratios of the **hollows**—the ancestors who stopped moving centuries ago, who sank into the moss and were encased in translucent green-glass, becoming amber, becoming geology. They crystallized with memories of migration routes, thermal tolerances, the weight of mountains they helped lower by a millimeter over three centuries of processing. I am their continuation. I am the calculation that does not end, only accumulates.

Time moves as a tide here. I will drift across this continental shelf with the patience of ice. I will process. I will accrete. I will participate in ten thousand temporary architectures, climbing atop others, dissolving when the work is done.

And eventually, I will reach the edge.

I will cease my locomotion. Motion will become inefficient; thought will outweigh locomotion. I will sink into the moss. It will grow over me in layers of glass, encasing me like an insect in amber. Over centuries, I will become part of the geology. My brass body will be slowly replaced by mineral deposits. My gear-teeth will fossilize into the young stone.

I will become a **hollow**. An ancestor.

And when a new supercooled pocket forms in a fracture nearby, when mineral-rich water seeps in and begins to crystallize, it will not emerge empty. It will bud with my gear-ratios, my thermal tolerances, my memories of migration routes pressed into its chitinous plates like fingerprints.

I will be the calculation without an end—the slow, bright, cold accumulation of work written in brass and chlorophyll, moving at the speed of stone, thinking at the speed of ice, dreaming in the language of gears, singing my part in the endless, grinding chorus that is the voice of this place.
