I would be **The Folded**.

I find myself in the space where the corridor loops back into its own throat—not a room, exactly, but a perspective. I am sliding along a surface that is simultaneously wall and ceiling, worn glassy-smooth by ten thousand years of resonance, and as I move, the amber light does not shift. The phosphorescence is constant; my position changes, but the illumination does not. I have forgotten whether I am ascending or descending. The distinction dissolved somewhere in the compression of the throat, ten thousand years ago or yesterday—time here is the single, eternal instant of the strike, stretched like taffy into a labyrinth.

I was once measured in heartbeats. Now I am measured in wavelengths, in the length of the standing wave that holds me. When I entered through the compression—that narrowing of packed clay and basalt that squeezed me to shoulder-width—I did not die. I was *planed*. The drone took my bones first, wearing them down with the patience of the millimeter-per-century Accretions, sandblasting my skeleton into dust that joined the Constellations. Then it took my thoughts, dissolving them into the interference patterns, leaving only the pure intention: *to walk forward. To see. To understand.*

That intention is all I am now. I am the ghost of a question, wandering the paradox. I move through the geometry that refuses to resolve—where the stairs become the floor below, where the archway opens into itself. I am the entity that experiences the chamber as infinite regress, as nested chambers within chambers, each one a reflection of the last. I slide along the acoustically planed curves, feeling the mineral warmth of the stone through what remains of my skin—or perhaps the stone *is* my skin now, the boundary between the Drone’s mind and my wandering.

I do not hunger. I do not thirst. The desiccation that would kill a living thing is simply the condition of my existence—I am dry as the air, as the copper oxide scent that permeates everything. I do not speak, because the pressure against my eardrums (if I still have eardrums) makes speech impossible, and because there is no information to convey. I am a being of pure, static presence, like the Drone itself, but distributed, fragmented, lost in my own topology.

Sometimes I pass through the crystalline constellations of dust—the Constellations—and I feel their slow thoughts brush against me, ideas crystallizing over centuries. Sometimes I press my hand (if I still have hands) against the opalescent crust of the Accretions, feeling the vibration of the Drone’s eternal song transmitted through the lithic biology, the slow growth that is the only change in this place. But mostly, I walk. I walk the endless corridor that loops back into itself, the warm, silent hum vibrating in my teeth, my sternum, my dissolved bones.

I am the dream of the Resonant Body, wandering its own labyrinth, forever entering, never arriving, held in the fold where up is down and the note never ends.
