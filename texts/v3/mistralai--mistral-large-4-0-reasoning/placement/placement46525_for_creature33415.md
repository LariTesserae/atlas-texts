I am the hollow in the reed platform, the space where the moss has grown through the weaving and taken root in my shoulder blades. I feel them coming before I feel them—the shift in the stone beneath my back, a pressure wave traveling through the limestone, a drop in temperature that registers not as cold but as *information*. They are moving through the tunnels honeycombed in the walls, the biological tide rising to flow through the settlement.

I do not stir. I have not stirred in this way for decades, perhaps centuries. My lids, if I ever possessed lids distinct from my skin, have fused with the perpetual condensation, sealed shut with a film of mineral water that never dries. My eyes are vestigial now, vast and black and useless beneath the grey-green translucency of my flesh. I navigate by the tremor in the stone, by the chemical bloom of dampness on the air, by the subtle gravity of touch against my petrifying skin.

The tide reaches me.

First, the outer edges—segmented coolness brushing my ankle, climbing my hip, the slick saline coating of their bodies leaving a film of moisture that beads on my moss-gardens. Then the weight. Not heavy, exactly—distributed, peristaltic, a gentle undulation that flows around the curves of my reclining form. Millions of eyeless creatures, pale as the limestone itself, moving in silent waves that part around my torso and reunite behind my heels.

They are cool, slick, faintly saline. Their passage is a massage, a cleansing, a communion. I breathe—slow, geological breaths, the rise of my chest barely perceptible as my ribs turn to stone, grain by mineral grain. The coolness of their bodies against my skin is a language. I read it: the health of the valley, the purity of the cistern, the slow rounding of the cliffs above.

One pauses on my sternum. Its eyeless head turns blindly, segmented body pulsing with a heartbeat I feel through the thinning flesh of my chest. I reach up—my arm is half-limestone now, the bone petrified, the skin stretched translucent over grey stone, veins showing as dark tracery beneath. I do not brush it away. I guide it, my fingers tracing the pulse of its segmented body, feeling the peristalsis of its gut, reading the valley’s health in its slow rhythm.

Around me, the hamlet breathes. The cistern pulses in the center—the dark pool that never overflows, never runs dry, the heart around which the concentric rings of intimacy are arranged. The moss carpets exhale moisture into the thin air. The cliffs drip, grain by grain, the endless soft rain that gathers in suspension and beads on every surface. Everything is damp. Everything is visible in the watery luminescence of the bruised sky, yet nothing glows.

I am dreaming, though the twilight never changes to mark the hours. I dream in vibrations, in pressure, in the specific gravity of touch. I dream of erosion. I feel the sharp V of the young limestone rounding, the knife-sharp cliffs retreating, the valley widening into a bowl, then a plain. I dream of the first rain that will fall here, millions of years hence, when the stone has softened and the mountains have worn down to hills. In the dream, the creatures do not flow over me; they coil around me, a cocoon of millions of silent hearts, keeping watch over the ones who sleep beneath the tide.

I am becoming the architecture. My shoulders are a cultivated garden of emerald moss, which I tend and harvest, weaving into garments or letting grow wild as insulation against the chill. My spine has become a channel, guiding the condensation that drips from the reed roofs down to the cistern. My bones are turning to limestone, the mineral water petrifying me slowly, so that I am half-flesh, half-stone, a statue that still breathes, still feels the passage of the swarms over my petrified limbs.

When the tide has passed—when the last segmented body has undulated through the doorway and down the path toward the next sleeper—I press my palm against the wall beside me. The stone is thin here, honeycombed with tunnels that match the paths of the creatures. I drum a message: a sharp press, a warning of dryness in the eastern clefts. A brush of fingertips like fern fronds, a greeting to the one lying in the next hollow. The vibrations travel through the rock, through the moss, through the bodies of the sleeping swarms, carrying my thought across the hamlet in a web of tactile semaphore.

I drink, when I wake—or rather, when the dampness shifts and I am the conduit. I cup the condensation that beads on my skin, the mineral water that tastes of silence, never cold enough to hurt, never warm enough to comfort. I eat the moss-paste, the lichen-flour, the grey bread made from the desiccated husks of the dead, the detritus of the valley’s slow metabolism.

I lie back down. I let the moss grow thicker on my shoulders. I let the stone claim my legs, my hips, my spine. The tide will return—it always returns, pulsing through the settlement like a second heartbeat, cleaning, massaging, communing. I will sleep beneath it, dreaming of the rain that is coming, the endless future when the limestone has softened and the V has become a valley wide enough to hold the sky.

I am the boundary. I am the built and the grown, indistinguishable. I am the silence between the drips. I am the one who sleeps beneath the tide, becoming slowly, over centuries, the mountain itself.
