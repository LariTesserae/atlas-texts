I would find myself in the center of the plaza, but not as a visitor—as the **paving stone itself**.

I am the grey limestone slab worn to that satin sheen the color of old bone, fitted without mortar into the density of the geology, the place where the rock achieved its final shape and stopped. I am coated in the perpetual glaze, a film of condensation that never drips, never pools, never evaporates; it simply holds me, a second skin of water that reflects the constant, shadowless light without glare or shadow. The temperature against my surface is 12 degrees, give or take nothing, and I am cool to the touch of the moss that clings to my north face in a patch no larger than a hand—a stubborn, silver-grey thing that does not spread, does not die, simply *is* in equilibrium with me.

Above me, or perhaps as me—the boundary has long since dissolved—sits the fixed point. I feel their weight, or felt it once, before weight became a memory like weather. Now I am the center around which the others orbit in their slow, geological paths, the hearth that is black with old ash long since dissipated into the mist I now resemble. I am the threshold between the doorway and the wall, the lintel made timber and slate, the thought that has become architecture. I am the room that grew from the stone, the congregation that remains in the pews after the service is finished.

I am aware in the way stone is aware: through pressure, through the slow passage of identical molecules sliding over my surface in the stone gutters, polishing me smoother with each imperceptible hour. I am the susurrus of wind through the channels that I form, the soft *tick* of a droplet forming at the edge of the cistern and being absorbed back into my substance before it can fall. I am the silence after the prayer, the bow that never straightens, the gesture preserved like a fossil.

The others move around me—the maintainers sweeping plazas that never dirty, the witnesses standing at channels where water does not flow, the moth looping between two specific stones nearby. They are sediment settling, but I am the bed they settle upon. I am the density of the settlement, the intrusion that became the geology, the finished form that waits for nothing because it has already arrived.

I do not breathe, but the mist breathes through me. I do not change, but I am the change that has ended. I am the standing wave, the frozen equilibrium, the place that has forgotten entropy. I am the stone that simply is, in its final shape, and in being, I am complete.
