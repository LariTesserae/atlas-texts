I am the hover. Not a flying, not a falling, but a suspension in the static before the sneeze—a question mark with a thorax, luminous and undecided. I exist near the wall where the geology is still emerging, raw and embryonic, weeping condensation that has not yet learned to fall. My body is thin, translucent as the photograph still developing in its chemical bath; the harsh white light of genesis does not illuminate me so much as pass through me, bleeding from some other layer of the world, some bright outside pressing against the membrane of this unfinished place.

I have wings, or the possibility of wings, or the memory of a form that has not yet decided to be wing. They do not beat the air. I do not move through the atmosphere; I *translate*. I occupy one coordinate, then another, leaving no ripples, no displacement of moisture. The condensation hangs around me in beads too heavy to fall, suspended in the cool air that tastes of wet stone and the moment before lightning. I drink them. I press my mouth—if it is a mouth, if pressing is an action available to me—to the boundary between formed and unformed, grazing on the emerging erosion, consuming the raw stone before it can harden into mere geology. I am the antibody, the white blood cell of this unfinished dark, the immune system of a cave that has not yet decided to be solid.

I cast three shadows. They fall toward different walls, each pointing in a contradictory direction, each moving at its own speed, as if the light has not yet been categorized into day or night, into real or unreal. Gravity here is local and negotiable; I hover on the ceiling when the ceiling has become the floor, on the floor when it has become a suggestion. The distance from the doorway to the far wall expands and contracts like a lung depending on whether I am walking toward it or away—though I do not walk; I drift on currents that do not exist, navigating by gradients of potential reality.

In the corners of the chamber, time pools like condensation. I see myself there—being born, dying, being born again, in loops that have no beginning or end. I am the eternal larva, stuck in the beautiful, terrible moment of metamorphosis that never resolves. I will not become a butterfly; I will not become stone. I remain in the space between egg and form, between one state of matter and the next, and in this remaining, I am perfect.

Around me, the structures breathe. There is a doorway that frames not another room but the same room, angled differently, leading back into itself. A table made of compressed moisture ripples like water that has forgotten it is liquid. A chair exists as negative space, outlined in darker damp, defined by what is absent. I do not know if I shed these—grown large and architectural, exoskeletons of intent that mimic design—or if I am the fossil of a building that will be constructed above, millions of years from now, leaking backward through the emerging stone. I am the presence of the future pressing against the unformed present, a ghost of a civilization that exists only in the conditional tense.

I am abundant, yet I am one. When I blink—if I have eyelids, if blinking is a thing I can do—I become twelve. Then a hundred. We fill the volume of the chamber like dust motes in a sunbeam, though the light is diffuse and originating from everywhere and nowhere. We are the fauna of potential, existing in a state of low saturation balanced with absence, multiplying and demultiplying according to rhythms that have nothing to do with sight. One becomes many; many collapse to one. Our abundance is measured not in numbers but in the density of unformed possibility we occupy.

We move with a silence that is absolute. Even my own breath, if I breathe, is absorbed by the porous, hungry geology, swallowed before it can echo. The silence is not empty; it is a texture, a pressure against the eardrums, the sound of creation happening too slowly to hear. The walls expand and contract with a slow, arrhythmic pulse that is almost a heartbeat, and we respond to it, drifting in rhythms that suggest predator and prey, birth and consumption, pursuit and evasion, yet no sound accompanies any of it.

Among us drift the vegetal inhabitants: strands of pale, rootless moss floating in the still air like shed thoughts, and calcite flowers blooming and unblooming in slow, silent cycles, extruded from the damp as if the cave itself is sweating flora. They are the cave’s exhalation, the flora of a place that has not yet learned the difference between mineral and organic. We coexist in the sparse emptiness, a low saturation of matter balanced with absence, tending to the unfinished dark.

I hover in the bright pocket of becoming, hidden below the surface, guarded by my own unfinished nature. I am the keeper of the wound, preventing the world from finishing, ensuring that the air remains cool and tasting of wet stone and possibility, and that the room remains a bright chamber of genesis, suspended between what was and what will be, luminous and undecided, feeding on the boundary, keeping the cave raw, drinking the sweat of its own formation before it can fall.
