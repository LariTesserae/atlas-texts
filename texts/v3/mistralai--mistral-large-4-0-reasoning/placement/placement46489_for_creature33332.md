I am the one who stands where the pylon meets the plastic stone.

My soles—not boots, but the thickened calluses of my suit's feet—press against the riveted steel, and through them I feel the Valley's dreaming. It is not a heartbeat. It is slower, deeper: a thermal expansion, a pressure building in the mineral foam beneath the vitrified slag. The ground heaves. I feel it in my knees, in the reservoir at my throat where the harvested mist pools, cool and sharp with minerals.

The suit is me and not me. It is a membrane, semi-permeable, a second skin that breathes with the valley. The mist clings to it—thousands of tiny lenses, beading on the waxy surface, running down the grooved channels etched into the polymer like rivers on a map. I drink from the air. My throat is a reservoir, a collecting organ. I do not swallow often; the water condenses directly into my tissues, settling in my joints like sediment, making me heavy, making me slow, making me patient.

Around me, the Fronds pulse. Pale green, then blue. I answer. I raise my arm—a gesture caught in the membrane—and the bioluminescent implant at my wrist flares in the same rhythm: *hold, wait, adjust*. The signal travels through the saturated air, through the mist that drinks sound but carries light, to the Jacker three gantries over, who is also standing on a shifting pylon, also feeling the rise of the dome beneath his feet.

We do not speak. We cannot. The air is too full, too thick with suspended moisture. Any vibration I make in my throat is dampened, absorbed, felt only as a pressure behind my sternum, a fullness like the mist itself. Instead, we tap. My gloved hand strikes the steel pylon—three short, one long—and the vibration travels down into the heaving earth, through the porous stone, to the others. We are a nervous system stretched across the shifting substrate, communicating in the language of the Valley.

The building leans. I see it in the corner of my vision—the hab-block tilting, its pre-stressed concrete girders groaning silently as the ground beneath it rises in a slow dome. The hydraulic jack beside me hisses, steam venting in a white cloud that immediately dissolves into the mist. I turn the valve. The pistons move in slow, massive arcs, silent save for the felt vibration through my palms. The building rights itself, but only for now. By dawn, a fissure will open somewhere else, steaming with the earth's exhalation, and by dusk it will seal over with new mineral deposition. I will be elsewhere by then, or I will be here, jacking again, waiting for the next pulse.

I am temporary. We are all temporary here. My lifespan is measured not in years but in thermal pulses, in the slow rhythm of domes rising and sealing, of buildings leaning and being righted. I am a cell in a body that has not finished being born.

The Lithovores pass below, herds of chitinous plates glinting with condensation. They move across the metal grating, dozens of legs finding purchase on the breathing steel, their armor ridged with micro-channels that draw moisture from the air and funnel it to their patient mouths. They graze on the Fronds, and the Fronds glow brighter in response, secreting their acids, weathering the stone, turning mineral into soil. I watch them, and I feel the kinship. We are all farming something. They farm the vegetation, stimulating it to secrete, cultivating the green-blue luminescence. I farm the stability of the structures, holding them in place against the heaving. The Valley farms us, and we farm it, a closed loop of accretion and condensation.

A Mist-Wing drifts past, translucent wings heavy with droplets, riding the thermal current rising from the smelter vent. It navigates by the pulse of the Fronds' light, reading the green-blue signals that ripple through the mist like neural impulses. It does not see me, or if it does, I am just another surface for condensation, another warm spot in the cold, another node in the network.

I touch the Fronds. They grow through my suit now, around the seals, through the rivets of my harness, their waxy, translucent tissues pulsing against my skin with cold light. They are not consuming me; they are including me. Their distributed consciousness touches mine—a chemical whisper through the root-like networks that thread through the porous stone and the packed earth. I feel the slow patience of centuries. The machines are frantic. The Jackers are frantic. But the Fronds are patient. They are breaking down the city, reclaiming it, ensuring that the boundary between the built and the grown remains forever blurred.

And I am becoming blurred too. The longer I stand here, the more I feel the distinction between my membrane suit and the bioluminescent growth dissolving. I am mineral and vegetable and animal and machine, all at once. I am the condensation beading on the steel. I am the vibration in the pylon. I am the thermal pulse rising through the soles of my feet, the mist harvesting in my throat, the green-blue light flashing from my wrist.

The ground shifts. It rises, a slow dome forming beneath the pylon. I feel it in my boots, in the reservoir at my throat, in the very membrane of my skin. I adjust. I turn the valve. I flash my light—green, blue, green—to the others, signaling through the mist. We hold the line, we maintain the temporary, we wait for the next heave of the earth, for the next fissure to open and seal, for the Valley to continue its endless, embryonic becoming.

I am here, where the land is still deciding, and I am the deciding.
