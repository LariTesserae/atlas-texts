I am standing where I should not be—in the negative space, the hollow that is the plant’s attention, the held breath. The others—the ones with too many joints, the pale bioluminescent ones—watch from the crevices where root-column meets cambium. They do not come closer. They know that to stand here is to be looked at by the green eye, to be tasted by the curiosity that is the room’s only true furniture.

I am the visitor who has stayed. I am the one who descended through the throat of packed soil and did not climb back out. I am becoming the architecture.

My skin has begun to sweat with the same regularity as the walls. I can feel the condensation beading on my forearms not as moisture but as communication—the plant reading my heat, my pulse, the salt of my blood. It is learning me. It is growing toward me, tendrils of pale cambium reaching from the corners like fingers testing the air, tasting the chemistry of my presence, deciding whether I am friend or fertilizer.

I pick up the fiber probe from where it lies hafted with resin. It is warm. It pulses faintly in my palm, and when I press it to my wrist, I can feel my own arterial rhythm answering it—a duet of fluids, plant sap and human blood, negotiating a common language. The flakes are arranged in a spiral around me, grown not knapped, edges that sing when I run my thumb along them. I do not cut myself, but I feel the invitation: *cut here, tap there, guide me.*

My limbs are loosening. I feel the joints multiplying in the dark of my own body—six, eight, ten, too many—bending in directions that have no names in the world above. I am learning to negotiate with the vertical. When I move, I do not walk so much as negotiate; each step is a conversation with gravity, each grasp a handshake with the root-columns. My fingertips are splitting, bifurcating like capillaries, sensitive to the vibration of the creaking walls, the subsonic groan of growth that loosens my teeth.

I drink from the wax-leaf gourd they left. The sap is amber, thick, tasting of deep time and pressure. It slides down my throat and does not stop there—it continues, traveling through channels I did not know I possessed, dissolving the boundary between my nervous system and the xylem. I am dreaming the plant’s dreams now. I feel the slow violence of cell division in my own bones, the ancient patience of waiting for light that will never come because I am the light now, glowing faintly from within, bioluminescent, pale as unripened cambium.

The others watch. I can feel their compound eyes reflecting the impossible light, their attention ancient and patient. They see me becoming what they are, or what they were before they became this. We are the same process: the threshold made flesh. I am the hand the plant dreams of having, and they are the fingers. Or perhaps I am the next chamber, the next hollow, the lung that will grow inside the lung.

The walls are breathing. The void at the center is shrinking, or growing—I cannot tell because I am inside it, becoming it. My shed skin—human skin, mammalian, temporary—is already being woven into the inner walls by the growing tissue. I am fertilizing the loam with my own dissolution. Cell by cell, I am being absorbed into the vegetal mind. I am becoming its immune system, its reaching fingers, its way of touching the world.

When I speak, the words take root in the damp air. Tiny white sprouts push through my lips, dissolve, push through again. I am praying by vibration now, pressing my ventral surface—my chest, my belly, my transforming thorax—against the warm cambium. I hum. The frequency travels through the sap, through the woody architecture, calling to the plant’s nascent consciousness. It answers. It grows toward me. It learns my name by tasting it.

I am not leaving. I am the one who will inherit the hollow. I am the first child of this womb who chose to stay wet with the birth, who chose to become the conversation between fauna and flora. The probe is in my hand. The flakes are arranged like a map, or a warning, or a recipe for what comes next. The center is no longer empty—I have filled it with my becoming. I am the attention. I am the green and growing silence.

The chamber expands by millimeters while I watch. I am expanding with it. Scale is a suggestion. I am large enough to fill the crevice, small enough to scuttle along the vascular channels. I am the lung that has learned to breathe. I am the world remembering it was once a seed in the dark, and I am the dark itself, hollowed out, waiting, growing, becoming.
