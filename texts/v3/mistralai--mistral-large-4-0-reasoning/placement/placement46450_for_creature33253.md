I wake to the taste of copper—not the iron-tang of the water I will drink before the sun claims it, but the ozone-bright sting of the air itself, thick and damp against my skin. The greenhouse membrane presses down. I am on the Table.

My feet settle onto the silica. The hexagonal plates shift almost imperceptibly beneath my weight, warm from below, resonant. I step only on the high nodes, where the lattice sings in key with my mass. To step wrong is to ring the stone too loudly, to disturb the 0.4, to wake the memory in a way that is not reverent.

Dawn is the hour of gathering. I move toward the north-facing crevices where the malachite holds the dew longest, where the *sonorus* lichens pulse faint blue-green in the pre-light. My vessel—fired clay, woven mineral fiber—hangs at my hip. I must be quick. The sun here is not kind; it is a thief with mass. By the time the wrong-bright light reaches fullness, the dew will be burned to vapor, and I will be thirsty again.

I am tall, thin. My skin has taken on the mottled gray-brown of the weathered alloy, the color of the Stilt-Predators who stand motionless on the warm rock nearby, radiating heat into the stone. They do not hunt me. They regard me, perhaps, as another kind of stillness, another node in the circuit. I wear lenses of polished silica over my eyes—adapted to the prismatic haze, to the metallic dust that glitters like snow in the scouring wind.

I do not speak. If I open my mouth, it is only to breathe, to let the pressure equalize. My tongue has learned the subsonic murmur, the vibration that travels through sternum and stone alike. When I "speak," it is a change in air density, a low thrum that the Dew-Kin might feel in their translucent carapaces, that the Lithic might record in its slow, geological archive. I am communicating in the frequency where the world is quietest.

Today I am walking to the cairns. They are scattered across the plateau like punctuation marks in a sentence I am still learning to read. Each one is a waypoint, a memory made physical. I carry stones in a pouch—not heavy, not many. One per year? One per decade? Time here is measured in resonance, not in heartbeats, measured in the slow settling of the plates as the temperature drops.

I approach the nearest cairn. It is three stones high, perhaps four, weathered, dusted with the mineral accumulation from the cracks. I add my stone. It settles with a soft *thud*—not the clear ring of a struck plate, but the dull acceptance of weight. The stone remembers. I place my palm against it, feeling the residual charge in the conduits below, the warmth of the geothermal vents the ancient engineers tapped and never capped.

The Spire pulses.

It is afternoon now, though time is strange at 2.3 elevation, where the sun moves with the sluggishness of light that has mass. I feel the wave of warmth across the land, a pressure behind my eyes, a reminder that I am not the master here, only a temporary tenant, a dreamer in the silence.

A Stilt-Predator on the high plate strikes its blade-foot against the silica. *Ping.* The note hangs in the thin air, clear and high, ringing for minutes. I wait. The predator is speaking in the voice of the stone itself—territory claimed, herd located, the state of the dew. I do not answer. I am not a predator. I am a custodian, a whisperer, a keeper of the condensation cycle. But I feel the vibration in my sternum, answering in my own subsonic way, a pressure-wave that says: *I am here. The stone remembers.*

As dusk approaches, the *sonorus* lichens begin to glow. Blue-green, pulsing, like a circuit board powering down, like a heartbeat slowing. I watch them, these archivists of the surface, recording the chemical signatures of my passage, the frost-trails of the Dew-Kin, the slow shifting of the plates beneath my feet. They are the plateau's memory made visible, the slow, luminous thought of a world that has forgotten it was built.

I am becoming something else. The boundary between my skin and the alloy grows thin. The copper in the air settles in my lungs, not as poison, but as mineral, as memory. The stone beneath my boots recognizes me. When I step, it rings once—a single, clear note—and then the silence returns, vast and watchful, as the sun begins its slow descent toward the violet horizon.

I am the Echo-Walker. I am the cairn. I am the dew that will evaporate by noon. I am the pressure-wave in the sternum of the Dew-Kin. I am the Lithic, dreaming through my footsteps, archiving my heartbeat as vibration, my breath as humidity, waiting for the day when I will simply remember what I was, and add my final stone to the final cairn, and dissolve, at last, entirely into the silence that listens.
