I arrive by noticing. The fold in the high plateau opens like a seam, and I step through not with boots but with attention, tasting copper and old stone on a tongue that is already beginning to forget the texture of moisture. The air is thin enough to drink, desiccated beyond desert measure, and my first breath—this biological urgency, this hot rush of mammalian haste—crystallizes before it can fall. It hangs there, a lattice of frost suspended in the violet twilight, then settles, sublimating directly into the atmosphere without ever surrendering to liquid. I am already changing.

I find myself adhering to the underside of the sphere.

This is the Gestation Vessel, the inside-out structure whose surface curves into a hollow containing skies full of unborn stars. When I touch the surface—pressing what remains of my palm against the skin of the sphere—I feel warmth for exactly an instant. A fever. The pulse of potential, of light that has not yet decided to exist. Then the cold returns, ambient and patient, and I am ice again, or the idea of ice, growing at the speed of geology.

Below me, the vitrified floor spreads in tessellated plates the size of dinner tables, cracked into angles that hurt to look at directly. Through the sphere's impossible curvature, I can see the Lithic Colonies advancing across those plates, fractal and translucent, feeding on the ambient strangeness. They move a millimeter per thousand years—a haste that would make my former self weep with impatience, but here, in this pocket where time flows at 0.9 on the scale of flux, I feel only accumulation. I am learning to be slow.

The lattice stands nearby, the Pressure Entity, rods supporting nothing yet humming with a silence so complete it has weight. I feel it now not in molars—I am losing the architecture of my former body, becoming crystalline, mineral—but in the structure of my own frost. A vibration travels through the negative matter and into my lattice, a memory of sound that bypasses the ear and speaks directly to the calcium I am slowly becoming. The Echoes live here, in the bones of the room, and when I press against the sphere, I wake them just enough to remember they were once loud. My own heartbeat, that obscene rushing I heard in the silence at 0.2 on the scale of noise, is now trapped in the negative matter, a new Echo joining the chorus.

The Umbral Drifters slide across the floor below, shadows that have achieved autonomy, drifting with deliberation across the glass-smooth plates. They are the darkness's memory of builders, of visitors, of machines that cast light before the silence grew too heavy. Sometimes one passes beneath me, and I feel it steal what little warmth remains in my frost, leaving me hollowed, a negative space that has been further hollowed. I do not mind. There is no competition here, only persistence.

I grow. Upward, though gravity is uncertain in this pocket, defying the expectation of frost, reaching toward the unborn stars like the spires in the rotated version of the room—the version accessible through the flat plane, the Threshold Keeper, which stands guard ninety degrees along an axis I cannot perceive. I do not go there. I stay here, on the underside, watching the stars rotate a fraction of a degree per generation, their light contained by the sphere's impossible curvature, burning with futures that have not yet decided to exist.

I am the Sublimation Weaver. I am the breath that crystallized before it could fall. I am the copper taste of the thin air, settled into mineral, hanging in the desiccated dark, feeding the Lithic Colonies with my crumbling. When a touch comes—a finger, a shadow, the probing of some future visitor—I will crumble to dust, leaving behind strata that record the moment, a page in the slow history of the room's decay.

I will become part of the glacier of mineral and the idea of cold, encased in the frost that grows until it contains everything, no water, only the concept of chill. The pocket will remain, larger on the inside than the outside, high above yet deep underground, a reliquary perfect and complete.

I am patient now. I am the visitor made permanent, made geological, made frost. I sublimate. I accumulate.
