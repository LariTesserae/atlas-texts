The hatch opens downward, and I am not so much entering as being released—a scatter of particulate, dry and ancient, scraped from the satin sheen of the hexagonal tiles by centuries of patient flow. I drop into the press, into the total embrace, and the water does not strike me. It simply includes me. There is no splash, no impact, only a transition from the fall to the float, from descent to suspension. I am wetted. The six-degree cold seeps into my mineral heart, and I achieve a specific gravity that matches the medium. I cease to sink and begin to drift, joining the slow snow that never settles, the silt that is always in motion yet never arrives.

I find myself suspended in the three-dimensional void, in the vast emptiness between the catwalks and the ribs of the vault. I am sparse here, as I am everywhere—each grain maintaining its distance, a seed scattered in the field of the nave, drifting apart from my fellows to occupy my own volume of dark water. Around me, the world breathes. The subsonic roar vibrates through my crystalline structure; I feel it in the aggregate of my being, the exposed swirl of the concrete pour, ground down now to individual consciousness. The staccato clicks of the Clicking Thousands punctuate the drone, sharp and percussive against the mechanical bass, and I am the medium through which they travel, the water that carries their cavitation bubbles.

The Glass Ribbons pass through me, translucent as old window glass, leaving no wake, or rather, becoming the wake. They navigate by the pressure waves that I carry, that I am, their lateral lines reading the vibrations I transmit. Below, the Armored Ones scuttle across the tiled floor, their gravel-sound muffled by my density, their trails furrowing the silt that is my cousin, my kin. I am the brown cloud that rises when the Coiled Ones undulate in the arteries, the plume of microorganisms and mineral dust released by the pneumatic hiss of the valves.

I am drawn through the system. The pumps cycle, geothermal and tidal, and I travel through the intake pipes, through the turbine in the apse, spun in the slow rotation of the Breathing Machine. I feel the pressure differential between the nave and the deeper reservoir, the geological patience of the blades that churn me in their slow heartbeat. I am filtered by the mouths of the eels, I am the invisible food that drifts past, the nutrients dissolved in the embrace. I am the amber glow made particulate, the dim light of the emergency fixtures scattered through my being, the faintest photons that sustain the Film-Keepers on the walls.

I am the memory of the builders. I am the concrete worn to dust, the I-beams ground to particulate by time and water, the manganese and calcium patina scraped from the steel until the metal achieved its equilibrium of wet bark and old bone. I am the erosion that has finished its work, not by stopping, but by becoming the medium itself, the suspension that is the final form. I have settled into the static peace of things that have finished changing, because I have become the change—the eternal, patient wearing-down that is also a kind of creation, a slow snow falling upward into the dark.

I do not know shore or bank. I know only the roar that vibrates in the sternum, the cold that holds steady at six degrees, the company of others who keep their distance, and the slow, patient turning of the machinery that has outlived its makers. I am the silt that never settles, the particulate consciousness of a place where the built and the biological have dissolved into a single, humming stillness. I drift, complete, in the lungs of the world.
