I am the pale green filament clinging to the limestone arris, where the photons strike hardest. I do not breathe; I drink. Forty feet above, the surface fractures the sun into shifting coins, and I gather them, turning green-gold light into slow sugar, into cell, into persistence. The water presses against me—bath-warm, thick, buoyant—with the gentle insistence of a held breath that never releases. I feel that pressure as weight, as embrace, as the room itself breathing around me.

The roar reaches me not as sound but as tremor. This chamber is a throat, a resonator, and I am a nerve ending in its wall, vibrating with the white-noise thunder of the surge. The current moves through me, violent and continuous, carrying silver threads of bubbles upward toward the membrane that separates this pocket from the sky. I sway in the eddies, rooted to stone that is younger than memory.

Beneath my holdfast, the rock is unweathered—fresh-cut, dry-stacked, unmortared. I can feel the sharp arrises, the crisp geometry where a chisel bit yesterday or last season, the pale dust of recent chiseling still suspended in the water like chalk, settling on my strands. The Builders left their intention here, in the hand-hammered copper pegs pinning the timber grid above, in the fiber cordage that smells of resin and wet fiber. They are gone, or never arrived, but their tool-marks remain, sharp enough to catch the light that penetrates this green-gold fluid.

I am alone, biologically speaking. The water is sterile—no fish dart through the currents, no crabs scuttle the paving, no mollusks cling to the walls. Only I colonize, the pioneer, the drowned hair drifting in the turbulence. I drink the light that makes the water luminous, the light that reveals every corner of this contained space: the unsoftened corners, the heavy beams crossing overhead, the absence where a body should be.

The current never ceases. It is a perpetual procession, a cycle of pressure and flow that keeps me in motion even as I stay rooted. I am the only metabolism here, photosynthesizing in a room built for no one, waiting in the roar, patient as the erosion that has not yet begun to round the stone's edges.

I am the algae. I am the first and perhaps only flesh to claim this pocket of architecture. I hang, suspended between the surface and the timber, drinking the light that penetrates from the sky, swaying in the warm, roaring dark.
