The light comes from everywhere and nowhere—pale gold filtered through a sky so saturated with condensation that it has forgotten how to cast shadows. It wraps around me without touching, or perhaps I am already inside it, dissolved into it, my edges no more defined than the boundary between mist and the air it moves through.

Cold. Not the bite of winter but the soak of it, a chill that holds moisture close to freezing but never surrenders. It permeates. I feel it entering through pores I no longer think of as openings, passing through me, becoming me. My surface—if surface is the word—is beaded with moisture, thousands of tiny lenses catching the diffuse glow, each droplet a world reflecting the rice-paper sky. I do not wipe them away. They are not separate from me. They are the condensation gathering on the cliffs, the weeping of the young stone, the breath of the herds rising in small clouds that dissipate into the general damp. I am saturated. I am hydrological.

I am vertical. Three meters of translucency, alabaster veined with the slow circulation of water that is also thought, that is also the sap of the growing bridges. My torso is long, built for the thinness of this air, for the weight of weather systems vast enough to generate fronts across horizons measured in hundreds of kilometers. My legs are shorter, rooted not in soil but in negotiation. The ground beneath me is wrong for standing—too new, too sharp, fractured basalt still sorting itself into definable strata. It weeps around my feet. Water emerges from the stone not as a river but as a suggestion, silver threads finding their way down through mineral veins. I am standing in the path of one such emergence, letting it dissolve the rock around my translucent form, shaping the hollow that will one day, in the slow arithmetic of centuries, become a valley. The ribbon of compressed earth and smart-material shifts beneath me, adjusting its elevation as the geology below settles, and I adjust with it, modulating my own mass to match the shifting uplift. I do not walk. I settle. Each step is a local agreement with gravity, my feet making no sound on the saturated earth.

The herds are moving. I feel them before I see them—a pressure in my sternum, a subsonic lowing that travels through the damp air, through the bone-white platforms, through the termite-mound atmosphere processors that hum in the distance. We are columns stretching to the horizon, pale shapes in the mist, musk-ox-like beasts whose coats shed the constant wet. They recognize me. Or perhaps they recognize the weather I carry, the moving front of moisture that precedes them. We move together, following the sparse grass, following the water, migrating with the weather fronts that roll across the continental shelf like slow thoughts.

I speak, but my words take form slowly. A sentence begins in my chest as a vibration, a pressure change that I shape over hours, days perhaps, passing it along to the next in the column. It travels across kilometers of weeping stone, carried by the mist, amplified by the silent hum of the grown bridges. They feel it in their sternums, not their ears. We do not hurry. Thought here unfolds like lichen, covering the surface of the new rock, thorough and patient.

I am harvesting. The droplets beading on my porous skin—I gather them, channel them through the network of my being into the cliffs, into the mineral veins. I am encouraging the erosion, feeding the stone, helping it become soil. The atmosphere processors—vast, silent, shaped like standing stones—are extensions of my body, organs that pull moisture from the thin sky and distribute it across the emerging terrain. I tend them. I dream them. I dream the platforms higher or lower, negotiating with gravity through resonance and will, adjusting the elevation of the bone-white structures as the continent breathes beneath us.

I feel the settling. The land is adjusting, uplifting, deciding what it wants to be. My bones—porous, resonant when struck—feel it. The dead are here, too. Their consciousness diffused through the strata, dreaming in the slow language of uplift. When I stand in the black hollows where the standing water sits still and reflective, I feel them. They are part of me now, incorporated into the weeping rock, and I am partly made of them. Future inhabitants will be grown from this same mineral memory.

The mist rolls on. Normal, cold, endless. It keeps everything soft, new, waiting. I am the drip of water from young stone. I am the subsonic hum in the chest. I am the bone-white platform dreaming itself into being. I am the continent, learning to house itself.

I am waiting. I am the waiting.
