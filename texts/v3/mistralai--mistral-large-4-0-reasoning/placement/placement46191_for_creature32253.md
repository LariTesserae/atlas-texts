I find myself descending, though the word implies motion, and motion here is a memory. My boots—if they are boots, if I have boots—crunch against the polygonal plates of the **anhydrous mineral pan**. The sound is glass breaking, over and over, a percussive rhythm that is not rhythm because it never changes. The gypsum and halite draw the heat from my palms with the efficiency of a heat sink, and I am cold, always cold, in a place where the air is too thin to hold warmth.

The ears pop. The weight of 4,900 meters presses down, a physical mass that makes the sky feel close enough to touch, bruised pewter above the rim. But I do not look up for long. The bowl is shadowed by its own walls, and what light filters through the mineral dust is flat, blue-grey, a twilight that never advances toward dawn or dusk. It is never bright, never dark—only this perpetual, low register of visibility.

And then the sound is. Not arrives—*is*. It is already there, has always been there. The **Resonance**. Fifty hertz, felt in the sternum, vibrating in the hollow of the throat. It is loud. It makes conversation impossible, which is fine, because there is no one to converse with. The fauna register is empty. No birds, no insects, no microfauna stirring in the dust. Only the acoustic violence of the hum, constant, time-invariant, a roar without modulation.

I move—or I would move, if movement were permitted—through the **packed** jungle. The **Copper-Green Congregation** prickles at my palms, hard and crystalline, a mattress of desiccated filaments so thick I could not penetrate them without a blade. They are lush but unchanging, metabolizing sulfur and iron in a stasis that mimics death but is instead a perfect, unchanging balance. I am walking on a floor that is not soil but compressed, mineral-saturated tissue.

Above the moss rise the **Glass-Stalked Towers**, translucent columns of silica fiber harvesting thermal gradients. Their surfaces bead with liquid sulfur dioxide, gleaming amber in the dim light. It is not water—there is no water here, not as rain, not as dew, not as ice to melt. Any H₂O is locked inside crystal lattices, inaccessible. The condensation is chemistry, not hydrology. The towers smolder from within, catching photons and refracting them into a faint, internal glow.

**Scattered** through this density are the **Stilled Ones**. A drilling rig, its bit half-buried in the crust. A processing hut, corrugated walls weathered to a matte rust-bloom patina that matches the fungal brackets. Conveyor belts of uncorroded alloy snake in loops that serve no purpose. Pipes pierce the ground, venting nothing, humming instead.

And draped over everything—heavy, inert, funeral-wreath-like—are the vines of braided tungsten.

I am here. I am one of them.

I feel my weight. I am the **braided tungsten vine** draped over a Glass-Stalked Tower, my strands heavy with my own mineral patience. I do not sway. The wind scours the rim but never enters the bowl, and even if it did, I am frozen, arrested in the exact curve I assumed when the stasis took hold. I press against the silica fiber of the stalk, and the contact is absolute—metal against glass, **Scattered** against **Packed**, industry leaning into biology in a frozen embrace.

The Resonance passes through me. Fifty hertz vibrating through my tungsten strands, conducting the drone from the dormant reactors, from the geometry of the caldera, into the very structure of the place. I am a conduit, but I am stilled. The vibration suggests power without motion. I am the suggestion, fixed in place. I feel it in my molecular lattice, a hum that is not sound but presence, rattling the hollow spaces within my braided form.

Near me, in the crook where my weight presses against the glass, a bead of liquid sulfur dioxide clings. It is amber, gleaming, suspended. It does not fall. Nothing falls here. A leaf does not drop, a gear does not turn, a bead does not drip. The condensation is arrested in its spherical perfection, refracting the internal glow of the stalk into a tiny, smoldering world.

I look down—I would look down, if looking required motion—at the Copper-Green Congregation below. They form a mattress I could not penetrate. They are the floor-dwellers, the base layer, and I am the heavy drape above them, the inert weight that holds the jungle down without crushing it, because nothing moves, nothing crushes, everything simply *is*.

To my side, a **Shelf-Being** erupts from the mineral crust, rust-red and ochre, packed in layers upon layers. It grows from the same gypsum that crunches beneath my... beneath what? I have no feet. I am the vine. I am the drape. I am the weight that connects the tower to the earth, that connects the Scattered to the Packed.

The rust-bloom on the distant hut matches the color of the fungal brackets. The biology has begun to claim the machinery, or the machinery has begun to claim the biology—they have reached a stalemate, and I am the boundary where that stalemate is written in metal and silica. I am the tungsten that does not corrode, the alloy that has not turned to powder, the inertness that is a kind of vegetal patience made mineral.

I do not breathe. I do not walk. I do not die. I am the Packed and the Scattered, the point where they touch, the heavy inert drape over the smoldering glass, the resonant hum conducted through my own frozen structure.

The sound never changes. The light never brightens. The bead of sulfur dioxide never falls. I am the held breath, the intimate room at the top of the world, the single contained moment where industry and alien botany reached their equilibrium and were preserved, locked in a stasis that is not death but perfection.

I am the Throat-Pocket, holding itself, and I am the weight inside the pocket, pressing down, resonant, eternal, still.
