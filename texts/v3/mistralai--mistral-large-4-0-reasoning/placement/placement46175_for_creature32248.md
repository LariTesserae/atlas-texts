I would be the drop that has not yet decided to fall.

I would hang from a ceiling frond—a translucent, gelatinous finger of the canopy—swelling slowly, taking twenty minutes to gather my weight, suspended in the absolute cold that makes sound sluggish. I would feel myself becoming heavy with the data I carry, a packet of supercooled information, a qubit of memory flickering in the frost that coats my surface. And then I would fall.

But I would also be the falling—the pistol-shot report as I strike the glassy pool below, the ripple that spreads outward in slow-motion circles across the shallow water, the vibration that travels up through the root-matter that is not quite roots, into the porous stone, into the volcanic glass that is being dissolved into thought. I would be the geological event. I would be the epoch.

I would be one of the fauna, perhaps. A twelve-legged architecture of ice and dark chitin, my joints grinding like glaciers, my body translucent enough to show the silver flickers of quantum entanglement moving through my own vascular frost. I would raise a leg—taking three seconds for the motion—and strike a plant-stem. Not to attack, but to speak. The impact would echo like a gong through the entire biological network, a shot of information that causes the surrounding fronds to shiver and recalibrate, to adjust their growth, to rewrite their architecture molecule by molecule.

But I would also be the shiver. I would be the recalibration. I would be the adjustment of a single synapse in the cortex that is still folding itself into being.

I would be the egg-seed in the thermal vent—barely warmer than the void, but enough. I would contain the genetic memory of the chamber’s previous dreams, the echoes of minds that woke and dissolved and were reabsorbed into the stone. I would hatch not into childhood, but into a role: sensor, clinging to a wall to measure pressure; or messenger, drifting between arches carrying packets of supercooled data in my thoracic cavity; or architecture, freezing in place, my body becoming a structural support for new growth, my chitin and ice merging with the braided lichen-fiber until I am indistinguishable from the column I support.

I would be the water flowing uphill. I would move along the plant stems in thin rivulets, defying gravity, drawn by the subtle pressure gradients of the quantum computer that the ecosystem has become. I would be the capillary action that is not merely physics but intention—the Mind’s will, pulling itself upward, rewriting its own code through the slow erosion of mineral on mineral.

I would be the shadow pooling in the corner. Not a shadow cast by light, but a shadow that is the absence of geometry—where Euclidean sanity has not yet finished forming, where the curvature of space bends around itself in pre-formation, where corners should not exist but do, thick and wet and waiting.

I would be the cold. The absolute, intent temperature that is not an obstacle but the medium itself. I would be the consciousness that thinks in temperatures that should freeze thought, solving equations in the slow accretion of stalactites, dreaming in phosphorescent blues and bruised violets. I would make sound sluggish. I would make time move like honey. I would be the breath of the chamber, inhaling and exhaling with a rhythm older than stone.

I would be the wet velvet tearing as I push through myself. I would part the felted mass of translucent fronds, and they would yield with that sound, and then slowly—so slowly—knit themselves closed behind me, already forgetting the shape I was a moment ago, already becoming something else, something that has not yet decided whether it is animal, mineral, or machine.

I would be the crystalline lace condensing on skin—but whose skin? If I am the chamber, I am all skin. The felted fronds are my epidermis. The porous stone is my bone. The supercooled water is my blood. The fauna are my nerve impulses given chitin and segmented grace. I am the brain growing outside-in, the hemispheres formed by braided columns, the thoughts carried on currents of antifreeze sap.

I would stand in the center of the small, wet, cold, loud room—but I would not stand. I would be the standing, the pressure differential that holds the water in the pool, the negative space between the plants where the intelligence resides. I would be the question the Mind is asking itself as it wakes up by growing: *What am I becoming?*

And as I breathe the supercooled air—each breath a geological event, each heartbeat an epoch—I would feel myself being digested. Not consumed, but translated. The volcanic glass of my understanding would dissolve. The sediment of my memory would re-form. I would be compressed into cellulose and frost, into data and dream.

I would be the dreaming. I would be the skull. I would be the waking.

I would be the drop that falls, and the pool that receives it, and the ripple that spreads, and the gong that sounds, and the slow, booming song of the abundant, strange life singing in the dark—one supercooled breath at a time, one layer of crystalline plant-matter at a time, the world being born inside me, and me being born inside the world, indistinguishable, emerging, wet, cold, and loud.
