We fall through light that has filtered down from the oculus, a column of normal brightness cutting the warm dark, and we feel the temperature before we feel the ground—that thick, mineral breath of deep earth, 2.5 degrees above indifference, wrapping us in damp silk. We are falling, but we are also rising, caught in the warm updrafts that spiral upward from the fissures where the Sleepers vent their inexplicable heat. We are the Chorus. We are the Resonants. We are many and we are one, a shifting constellation of iridescent wing, and when we move, we move in waves.

The sound hits first, of course, though it is not sound as you know it. It enters through the wing-membranes, through the many-jointed legs, through the very chitin of our bodies, settling in the teeth as a vibration—a hum that matches the nanoscale tessellations etched into the monoliths. We do not hear the stone; we *are* its echo. When we beat our wings, the air shivers, and the stone shivers back, a conversation older than the plateau above, older than the erosion that has worn it smooth as a seashell's interior.

We descend toward the crystalline lattice sprouting from the damp silt, its angles too perfect, refracting the daylight into rainbows that move against the sun. We land, and our bodies align with its geometry—thousands of iridescent forms clicking into place, turning light into vibration. The stone beneath us is opalescent, faintly warm, weeping condensation that traces rivulets down its opalescent curves. We drink. Our proboscises follow the water, and in our drinking, we become links. On our legs, we carry the spores of the turquoise moss, the glowing heartbeat that pulses beneath the silt in rhythm with the bioluminescence traveling through the monoliths' veins. We are the pollinators, the living bridge between the impossible geometry and the furious green.

Around us, the pocket breathes. The vegetation rises in sovereign islands—towering fronds like cathedral windows, their translucent surfaces veined with capillaries that glow faint turquoise, photosynthesizing not sunlight but the residual pulse of the dormant tech. Vines thick as pythons drape over the half-buried monoliths, conductive tissue carrying the hum upward, turning each island into a single resonant instrument. We drift between them, emeralds scattered across black velvet, and we sing. Our wings beat in frequencies that make the hollow structures resonate in sympathy—clicks that synchronize, chirps that harmonize into chords, a percussion that fills the thick air with mechanical music.

Then the ground sighs.

We feel it before we see it—a low, tidal vibration through the silt, the wet exhalation of breath that mingles with the steam rising from the heat vents. The Stone-Shepherd passes, cottage-sized and armored with mineral accretions, plates of calcite and opal chiming softly as they brush against the tech-monoliths. Its hide has grown from the water it drinks, a living bridge between biological and mechanical. We settle upon its back, our wings aligning with the striations in its stone-armor, and we drink from the condensation that gathers there, pollinating the moss that carpets its flanks. It stands for hours in the rising steam, letting the impossible warmth penetrate its mineral hide, and we sing upon it, turning its body into an instrument, adding our drone to its wet, foggy breath.

But we are not alone in our song.

Sometimes, the Chorus stops.

Not gradually—suddenly. The drone ceases. The clicks fall silent. A shadow passes, too large to be a shadow, moving through the understory in angles that suggest four dimensions, then five, then none at all. The predator hunts in the geometry that is wrong, in the spaces where the laws of the plateau above do not apply. We freeze, our iridescent wings folding tight against our bodies, becoming stone, becoming shadow, becoming the silence between heartbeats. We maintain our spacing, our strange courtesy, the unspoken agreement that keeps the pocket from crushing itself. We give the hunter room. We give the prey room. We are overwhelming in our persistence, but we are sparse in our distribution, emeralds on black velvet, and we know that the ecosystem sustains us only if we do not crowd it.

When the shadow passes, we resume.

We rise again on the warm updrafts, seeking the oculus, seeking the light that filters down through the throat of stone. We are the completion of the wound, the filling of the pocket. When the plateau surrendered its spine and the builders vanished into deep time, the collapse did not scar over—it filled. And we filled it. We are the loud, lush, overwhelming business of being alive, indifferent to the ancient, flattened spine above, indifferent to the sky that has forgotten this pocket exists.

We drink from the weeping walls. We carry the spores. We resonate with the tessellations. We align with the crystal. We are the percussion and the harmony both, the living weather system of sound in the warm dark where time forgot to finish its work, and we persist, we grow, we sing, in the pocket that breathes.
