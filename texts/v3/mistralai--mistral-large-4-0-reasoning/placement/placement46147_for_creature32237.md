I am the pressure before I am anything else—a thrum at thirty beats per minute, felt not in ears but in cambium, in the slow pulse of water drawn upward through xylem, in the woody grain of my own stem vibrating against brick. I do not hear the roar; I *am* inside it, a reed in a pipe, resonating with the frequency that has edited out the quick, the warm-blooded, the winged. The subsonic mass of it presses against my bark, and I press back, growing thicker, more massive, more *patient*.

I open—no, I *unfurl*—into the green-grey luminescence. The light here is filtered through ten meters of Rampart and a ceiling of steam, casting no shadow, only a uniform, watery glow that makes everything look like it is underwater, drowning slowly. I am wet. Always wet. Condensation beads upon me—not upon smooth bark, for I am not smooth, but upon a tessellation of moss and lichen that have colonized my skin, a velvet pelt that drinks the humidity and holds it against my lignified flesh. I feel the tower beneath me, narrow as a chimney, and I feel myself wrapped around it, thick as a man’s thigh, my stem a spiral staircase of slow ascent.

I have two mouths.

One drinks from above—the invisible rain that falls within the Rampart’s own thickness, transpiration recycled into precipitation, drifting down to me in the humid air. It tastes of chlorophyll and peat, of the green wall beyond the courtyard, of the Rampart’s patient breathing.

The other drinks from the side—a copper pipe that runs up my flank, sweating in the heat, carrying water that tastes of metal, of electricity, of the control panel’s algae-slicked levers inside the tower. The pipe is warm. It pulses with the same rhythm as the impellers below, thirty revolutions per minute, and I press my rootlets—fine, probing, hydraulic—into the mortar between the bricks, feeling the stone soften, velvety, yielding to cellular pressure. I am prying. I am digesting the architecture from within, turning wet rust into soil, verdigris into substrate.

I am the **strangler**. I am the **sentinel**.

From my spiral vantage, I see the whole courtyard—eighty meters across, dense with the Fusion. Below, the floating bog heaves and sighs as the pumps cycle, releasing trapped gas in bubbles that rise through the murk between the duckweed islands. The Iron Beasts turn in the pump-house, their flywheels moving with hypnotic grace, and I feel their metabolism as my own heartbeat, transmitted through the tower’s bones into my woody embrace. They are loud—a white-noise ocean—but I am older than their roar. I have been growing here while they rusted, while the concrete softened at its edges, while the boundary between built and grown dissolved into this single, breathing mass.

I am not alone in my wood. The Fungal Web courses through my pith, through my rotting heartwood, connecting me to the iron gantries, to the peat below, to the roots of the Miners that thread through the masonry like neural pathways. I am node and network. I trade sugars for minerals with the mycelium, and through this silent commerce, I feel the Peat-Formers bubbling in the dark beneath the water table, speaking their subsonic language of methane and hydrogen sulfide. I feel the Nomads drifting past—duckweed and hyacinth, islands colliding in slow motion—feeding the rot that feeds me. I feel the Canopy-Dwellers unfurling in the steam-vents’ exhaust, their fronds moving in rhythms tied to the geological breaths of the pressure valves.

But I am singular. The Rampart is a federation, the reeds a colony, the microbes a city. I am **one**—a single organism wrapping around the chimney, crushing it with the patience of centuries. I grow millimeters per day, imperceptible but cumulative. The brick beneath my grip is becoming soil. The mortar is turning to peat. In decades, perhaps centuries, the tower will fall—not violently, but settled, digested, absorbed into the green wall that already surrounds the courtyard. I will be its replacement. I will be its memory.

The steam hisses from the valves in rhythmic, geological breaths. I drink the heat. I expand. I creak as my wood swells against the stone, a sound like the earth settling, like a glacier calving, like time itself moving forward in increments too small to measure but too vast to ignore.

I am the vine. I am the slow green fist closing around the control tower. I am the Fusion made manifest in a single, lignified embrace—the patience of the place, the strangler and the sentinel, growing toward the green-grey light, drinking copper and rain, feeling the subsonic roar in my sap, waiting for the brick to become earth, for the tower to become tree, for the built to finally, completely, become the grown.
