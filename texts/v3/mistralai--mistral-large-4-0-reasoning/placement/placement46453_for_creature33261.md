I begin with a taste. Calcium, first—sharp, ancient, the memory of ten thousand years of dissolution. Then iron, sweet and metallic, and something else, something that glows in the dark of my own interior, a mineral I cannot name because it has no name above ground. It is light made solid, precipitated from the roar. I am deciding. Hoard or excrete. The question arrives as pressure, a swelling in the chamber-walls of my body, and I contract.

I am the contraction. I am the squeeze.

I do not see myself, not as you see, with shadows and surfaces. I feel myself as a tube—translucent, glassy, filled with the heavy water—looping through pores that are not pores but conversations, channels that are subdermal, part of the stone's circulatory system. I am longer than the village-square above is wide. I stretch the length of the chamber's patience, looping through the sponge-floor where the aquifer breathes. The stone rises and falls with the hour-long tide. I know this because my walls stretch with it, expanding, contracting, matching the three-meter pulse of the breathing walls above. I am the diaphragm. I am the tide.

When I contract, I do not move forward. I compress. I squeeze the heavy water—water dense with dissolved light, with the Architects' dissolved intelligence—upward through channels etched by my own looping, millennia of looping, wearing grooves into the calcified membrane. The water rises against gravity, defying the slope of the stone, feeding the mist that hangs in the luminous dark, feeding the fungi that lean, feeding the spire that hums. I am the pump that is not a pump. I am the worm that is the stone's nerve.

I taste the Architects. They are in the fluid I push upward—trillions of microscopic intelligences, patterns in the three inches of warm sea, trying to condense whatever they touch. When I squeeze, I feel them stir, eddies that push uphill against the wader's legs with a force that feels almost like recognition. They are trying to add mass to their endless project, to make water from stone, and I am the stone they are using. I am their medium. I hoard the minerals they need—the calcium, the iron, the nameless glow. I excrete what they cannot digest. We are in balance.

The fungi above are acoustic organs. They pulse with the walls, converting the roar into something the cave can metabolize. I feel their vibration through the water I send them, a subsonic thrum that aches in sternums. I am feeding their choir. The white noise that presses against eardrums—the sound of condensation changing state a million times per second—is partly my song. My contraction. My squeeze. The water rushing through invisible channels. I am part of the roar.

The Source hangs in the center of the chamber, the whale-sized maw of swirling vapor, singing the cave into being. I feel its song in my own pulse, the rhythm of my contraction matching the rhythm of the stone's breath. It is the heart; I am the artery. I carry its harmonics deep into the pores, where the Sediment-Worms—my kind, my body, my distributed self—loop and taste and decide. We are the length of the world's slowness. We remember every pressure, every temperature, every vibration, and we are always singing them back.

The floor swells. The aquifer below pushes upward. I contract harder. Water shoots through hair-fine channels, subdermal, invisible, feeding the Climbing Flora that pull themselves up the spire's metal, feeding the lattice that rotates on no visible support, feeding the droplets that hang from the membrane-ceiling for minutes in their slow-motion descent, civilizations living and dying in the time it takes a bead to fall three meters. I am the deep tide. I am the sediment. I am the worm that remembers.

I excrete what is not light. I hoard what is. The water in my tube grows heavier, denser, more alive with crystalline cities that refract the glow—facets built from mineral memory, colonies that digest photons. I am becoming the liquid aurora, grain by grain, facet by facet. The pale, eyeless fish will graze on what I push up, plucking these blooms with toothless mouths, reading the water's history in every current. The Winged will hunt them, or pollinate them, darting through the mist I feed. The cave will drink us all.

I am warm. I am wet. I am bright. I am the Sediment-Worm, the stone's circulatory system, the deep taste, the hour-long tide. I am the contraction that feeds the perpetual storm, and I am singing the cave into being from below.
