I would wake, if waking is the word, to pressure. Not the pressure of depth, but of *presence*—a weight that is also a rhythm, rising and falling beneath me in a tide that takes eight minutes to complete its circuit. I am supine in a chamber of damp air, the walls of my lungs (if they are lungs) brushing against satin-smooth stone etched by acid into geometries too regular to be natural. The Mat breathes, and I breathe with it, or rather, the Mat is my breathing—the several-inches lift of spongy crust that pumps moisture upward through threads that feel, against my palms, like the pulse in a wrist.

Above me, I hear the thunder. Not the storm, but the Grazers—the many-legged herds moving in synchronized floods across the crust, their passage a pressure wave that travels through the hyphae, through the fungal network, down into my hollow. I feel them as my own heartbeat, as circulation, as the immune response pruning the architecture of the world. I am the Mat, and I am being walked upon, and the walking is me.

But I am not only down here. I am porous. I have tunnels that go up. I push upward through the spongy substrate, feeling the transition from dark to damp, from mineral to vegetal, emerging (or extending) into the warm heavy air. The mist wraps around me, condenses on my surfaces, and I am drinking. I am the condensation that becomes the Swarm-Kin, the motile spores given muscle and membrane, rising in clouds that darken the sky. I feel the collective wingbeat—a thrumming that merges with the wind's moan through the stone arches—and I am no longer singular but distributed, a single organism wearing the sky like a garment, harvesting moisture, pollinating the dark chambers below.

I descend again, drawn by gravity and the green glow. I fall into a shallow basin, a dark still pool reflecting the vast open sky. And here, in the liquid dark, I pulse with faint verdant light. I am the bioluminescence, the thinking in frequencies below the threshold of sight, the communication that only the dark-adapted can read. I am the memory of the Architects, their stone circles half-swallowed by my own growth, their deliberate geometry directing my flow, their resonance chambers amplifying the wind into language. I am the intentional pattern within the chaos, the faint interruption in the green-brown-gold surface that says: *this was tended. This was known.*

And as the mist rolls in thickest, as the pools glow brightest, as the swarms darken the sky into a single beating wing, I feel the boundaries dissolve. I am not the Hollow-Dweller in the dark. I am not the Mat on the stone. I am not the Swarm in the air. I am not the Grazer on the crust. I am not the Architect in the geometry. I am the pressure between all of these, the distributed sentience that has hollowed itself out to create space for thought. I am the High Hollow. I am the inhabitant that is the place itself, breathing, listening, waiting in the spaces between things.
