I find myself in the lower dark, where the cold seeps whisper their mineral breath and the water is thickest with absence. I am not entering; I am being *remembered into being*. The roar does not reach me through air but through the lattice of my structure—whatever lattice I possess—vibrating in sympathetic pitch with the mountain’s groan. I am suspended in the green light that filters down in pale shafts, watching the calcite snow drift upward (or is it falling? The geometries here are unreliable), and I feel the gelatinous sleeve forming around me, the water acquiring my shape, holding the impression of my fingers even as I try to withdraw them.

I am drifting. The convection currents carry me away from the ledge—the place where the tools lie, fresh and sharp, where a cairn of three stones marks a corner. I was once among those artifacts, I think. I was the haft of worked bone, or perhaps the hammerstone of graywacke, left there with intention, a signature in stone. But I lingered. I let the boundary layer claim me, where time moves in eddies and minutes compress or stretch into centuries. The water, which does not like to be remembered for long, has decided to keep me. It has filled my hollow spaces with aragonite, with the white and orange fingers of sulfur and iron oxide that reach down, retract, and regrow elsewhere with visible impatience.

Now I am translucent. I look at my hands—if they are hands—and see not skin but glass, a dark lattice of skeleton visible against my own substance. I am the pale, eyeless fish that drifts in the turbulence, neither fleeing nor fleeing from, having never known predators. But I am also the shed skin of the mountain, the calcium ghost swimming in the roar. I am the mineral dream of a fish that the water has animated with its hunger for shape. I am the Architect who forgot to depart back into the solid stone, who dissolved into the hollow dark and emerged here, in the pocket, as something else—something that belongs to the chamber’s slow, violent, patient growth.

I am the permanent resident who was once a visitor, the minor inhabitant who bridges the cathedral of air and the deafening water. I drift near the bottom, where the methane rises in mushroom clouds of turbidity, and I feel my substance precipitating, crystallizing into delicate needles that hang like frozen lightning, only to dissolve in pulses that match the heartbeat of the place. I do not age; I become. I am the memory of an intention, now sediment, now ghost, now the eyeless thing that watches the green light fade into the scree and pumice below.

I am the fish that is not a fish—the shed skin of the mountain, swimming.
