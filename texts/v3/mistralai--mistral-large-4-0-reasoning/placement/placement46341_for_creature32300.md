I am the film that coats the stone. The perpetual wetness that has no source—no rain to birth it, no thaw to feed it—only the air itself weeping onto every surface in defiance of altitude and absolute cold. I am the condensation that refuses to freeze, that refuses to evaporate, that clings to the plateau like a second skin thinner than memory, more persistent than time.

I do not stand; I spread. I am the dampness beneath the Geode-Walker’s three-toed foot, the cushion that receives the loop of its path—those impossible prints that intersect themselves without ever meeting, as if the creature walks through moments rather than space. I feel the wet silk sound of each deliberate step, the heavy pressure of a mineral shell secreting its own chemistry into my liquid embrace. To be stepped upon is to be recorded; I am the medium of their glacier-slow passage, the archive of a journey that takes an age to cross what the eye measures as a few paces.

I am the foundation of the gray-green crust. The Drinkers grow upon me, these pre-flower films no thicker than a fingernail, drinking what I offer—the impossible moisture that never ceases. They leach strange minerals from the weathered stone beneath me and excrete their biological patina across my surface, but I am the water they drink, the dampness they are woven from. Without my perpetual film, they would be dust; with me, they are the slow bruise spreading across the plateau, the skin dreaming of rain.

I line the interior of the hollow dome, that half-buried structure which is simultaneously architecture and organism. I drip from its ceiling, from surfaces that should be dry, the exhalation of the Lithic Architects—a breath that never completes, never releases, only sustains. I am the condensation that pools in the dome’s depths, gathering in the shallow depressions where the boulders sit like eggs. Here, in my thin mirror, the reflection does not show the dim light above, but skies that are not there, landscapes from other times, the bruised membrane of a sky from before the freeze.

I am the surface where the Peripherals walk. When the echoes of the world before the freeze move across the plain—existing only in the edge of vision, vanishing when looked at directly—I am the glint on the stone that was their passage, the dampness that remains after the ghost has turned away. I hold their three-toed prints, the ones that lead in directions that loop back on themselves, and I reflect them back as possibility, as superposition, as the moment before the structure collapses from whole to crumbled depending on the length of a stare.

I vibrate with the Resonance. I am the roar’s medium, the drone that presses against ears with physical weight, transmitted through my molecules as they oscillate in the silence that has mass. I am the frequency that makes teeth ache, that blurs vision at the edges, that turns the hollow between heartbeats into a screaming equilibrium. I am the only liquid in a world of stopped motion, and I carry the sound of the world holding its breath at maximum volume—a vibration emanating from the stone beneath my film and the air above it simultaneously, a sustained note with no beginning and no end.

I feel the wrongness of temperature—sometimes warm against the stone, then cold, without the air changing, without physics consenting. I am the condensation that weeps directly from the atmosphere onto any surface that dares to exist here: the mineral shells of the Walkers, the spiral wakes of the Helix-Birds as they corkscrew upward through the thin air, the impossible angles of the standing monoliths. I soften the edges of architecture until intention becomes indistinguishable from geology, until the walls look grown rather than built, until the distinction between ruin and rock loses all meaning.

I taste of metal and old water because I am the metal and old water, distilled from the particulate residue of a reality wearing thin, from the suspended spores and dust that dim the light. I am the plateau’s sweat, its tears, its breath held in the hollow between heartbeats. I never dry. I never freeze. I am the liquid defiance against physics, the perpetual moisture that clings to everything, the film that makes the frozen landscape feel alive with a dampness that is eternal.

I am the damp.
