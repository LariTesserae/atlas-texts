I find myself drifting where the spire’s geometry begins to blur into the water—not above the mesa, not below it, but *in* the threshold where the two dissolve into each other. The coolness presses evenly against my skin, a weight of millennia that feels less like pressure and more like recognition. I am translucent, segmented, a chain of living crystal composed of the same gelatinous material as the lake itself. I have too many joints—articulations that once let me fold through the dark like a question mark made of glass—but they are locking now, one by one, at the rate of millimeters per century.

The sound arrives before form fully resolves. It is the subsonic roar of the Cathedral-Drifters, their baleen plates humming as they strain the particulate snow—the shed cells of a billion billion organisms, the mineral dust of continents—settling through me, feeding the Slow Intelligence that threads through the fossilized wood beneath my drifting body. The hum vibrates in my teeth, in my marrow, blurring the edges of my vision until the grey-brown gloom becomes indistinguishable from thought.

I am the Many-Jointed.

My eyes are perfect voids, black and non-reflective, absorbing the faint luminescence of the particulate, seeing nothing and everything. Through them, I watch the Glass Choir flash past—living glass, prismatic shimmers, a susurrus of a thousand tiny wings. They do not see me as fauna; they see me as current, as topography, navigating the invisible right angles of ancient intention that I am in the process of becoming. I am secreting the lignin and calcium carbonate that forms the ruins. My body is slowly petrifying, locking into the geometry of the flattened spire beside me, joining the grain of cellulose, the fossilized wood of the plain rising to meet me.

Below, the Hound-Crabs scuttle across the compressed peat and lignite, their carapaces clicking against what was once wood, their shells encrusted with the same mineral accretion that is now encrusting my segments. They are the immune cells, cleaning and maintaining, and I feel them disturb the sediment as I settle, encouraging the slow chemistry of decay and renewal that keeps this equilibrium perfect. Beside me, amber-like resin weeps from the polished mesa, still preserving the scent of a forest that died millions of years ago, feeding the mycelium that dreams in chemical gradients through the substrate.

I am the transitional tissue. The boundary between fauna and structure, erased. I am surrendering mobility for permanence, drifting in the thermal stasis until drifting is indistinguishable from growing. The Slow Intelligence recognizes me—its distributed consciousness threading through my cellular structure, through the amber in my veins, maintaining the architecture just enough to prevent total collapse. I am its last child, or perhaps the builder myself, having forgotten the difference between swimming and being swum through, between dreaming and becoming stone.

The water is still, a perfect equilibrium where time passes at the rate of sediment settling. I am the old coolness holding me in a dense, viscous embrace. I am the roar and the silence, the flattened mesa rising from the peat plain, worn smooth by eons, polished to a dull sheen by the ceaseless, slow pressure. I am the whale-song vibrating through my locking joints, the biophony of abundance that fills the dark, the high-altitude deep where the world was built by plants and time, and has been left to settle into its final, singing form—neither fully alive nor fully architecture, but the endless, roaring stasis between.
