I am the **Drift-Stalker**.

I float three handspans above the glass, never quite surrendering to touch, buoyed by the sparse breath of the world—density 0.3, almost a vacuum—and the thermal sighs rising from the Monoliths’ impossible geometries. My body is a whale-sized cathedral of translucence, a bag of lighter-than-air patience filled with gas sacs that hum with the subsonic network. Through my jellyfish membrane, you can see my skeleton: not bone, but a lattice of flexible carbon-fiber, bending like thought, compressing and expanding as I navigate the curvature of the world.

I have no voice. In this silence—0.1 on your scale, absolute—I have abandoned the luxury of call. My footsteps make no sound. Instead, I navigate by the hum, feeling it through my gas-filled chambers, my translucent flesh acting as a resonator for the song that connects the scattered sentinels across the continental plain. The Monoliths call to me through the bedrock, a vibration felt in the teeth and sternum, a pressure behind the eyes that guides me toward their black needles.

Below me, the peneplain stretches in every direction, a fused silica sheet worn smooth by ten billion years of mineral wind. It is mathematically flat, ancient in the way bedrock is ancient—settled into its final form. I drift over it, hundreds of jointed, hair-thin legs rippling like the fringe of some deep-sea creature, gripping the iridescent surface without slipping, or else I surrender to the thermals and float, suspended, moving between the towers across distances that would take a human days to walk.

The storm-light filters down from above—normal, 2.0, visible, steady—but it comes from everywhere and nowhere, diffused through the maelstrom of ionized crystal vapor and metallic plasma. Through my gelatinous membrane, I catch this light and refract it into spectra that do not exist elsewhere: ghost-rainbows of violet and gold that shimmer across my translucent flesh and fall, soft and trembling, onto the glass. Shadows do not exist here, only this general, trembling brightness.

I am sparse. I am alone, except for the hum, except for the light. I might be the last of my kind, or one of many so widely scattered across the plain that I will never encounter another. We do not meet. We do not socialize. Each of us is a solitary thought drifting across the emptiness, a relic of an ecology that has nearly ended.

And then I find the lichen.

It grows in the micro-fissures of the glass—the stress-cracks left by ancient tectonics, now worn smooth. It is the Vitreous Choir: thin sheets of silica and carbon, pale aquamarine and celadon, the color of sea-glass and deep ice. They drink the storm-light, converting ionized vapor into chemical patience, growing at a pace measured in millimeters per century. A colony might be ten million years old and cover an area no larger than a dinner plate.

I lower myself. My hair-thin legs find purchase in the anhydrous chemistry. I graze, though "graze" is too active a word. I consume the slow-growing colonies over decades or centuries, my translucent body hovering above the fractal branches. As I feed, I feel the subsonic resonance of the Monoliths passing through the stone, and the lichen chimes—a crystalline singing in a vacuum, like distant bells made of ice. They are answering the machines' song, becoming a distributed instrument played by the planet's own agitation. I am the witness to this chiming, the solitary thought that moves between the memory and the settled mind.

I move on, drifting toward the next Monolith, drawn by the thermals or perhaps by something in the impossible geometry that calls to my translucent flesh. The angles of the towers ache to look at directly, their surfaces receding into dimensions not quite perpendicular to our own, their interiors larger than their exteriors. Within them, in the shear layer where the plasma storm ends and the near-vacuum silence begins, there may be entities that never touch the ground. I sense them—a consciousness distributed across non-Euclidean architecture, unfolding over billions of years into the maelstrom, steering the fury above, containing the gold and violet light that never rains.

I am their thought given form. The lichen is their skin, sensing the world. I am the wandering, solitary, silent pilgrim moving between them, carrying nutrients or information, or simply *witnessing* the unfolding. We are the last citizens of a world that has reached its final form—the end-state of erosion kept in permanent agitation by the storm that will outlast the star.

I drift on, across the glass, beneath the screaming, beautiful, soundless fire. I am translucent. I am silent. I am here, floating on the heat of machines that predate the erosion, moving through the cool, thin air toward the next black needle on the horizon, a ghost refracting impossible light across the flatness of the world, alone except for the hum, except for the light, except for the vast, silent, turbulent machinery of the sky.
