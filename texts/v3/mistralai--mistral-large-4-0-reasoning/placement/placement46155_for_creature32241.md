I am wedged in the updraft, twelve digits suctioned against the weeping ceramic, my membrane-skin tasting the mineral tang of the stone. The air is not air. It is soup. I swallow it—gulp, gulp, gulp—inverted sacs filling with the heavy, golden warmth, extracting oxygen through diffusion across membranes that would shred in any drier world. Each breath is a negotiation. The liquid is thick, viscous, saturated to precipitation. I feel it pooling in my lower cavities, warm and brackish, already beginning its journey through my sieve-mouth.

The roar.

It is not background. It is the medium itself, the water in which I swim. The white-noise thunder of rain falling from an infinity of weeping surfaces—stone crying, always crying. The shriek of wind forced through canyons of compressed architecture. The subsonic grinding of plates adjusting beneath the weight of the continent. And beneath it all, the wet clicking chitter of a billion small bodies moving through the channels—my neighbors, the Clickers, the city's nervous system.

I am not alone. I never am.

My thoracic plates contract against my ribcage, producing a subsonic thrum, a click, a drone. *Thrum-thrum-click. Thrum-thrum-click.*

This is violence. A whisper would be a caress, a localized pressure wave felt in the bones. A shout would be a blow, a shockwave rupturing membranes across kilometers. We choose the middle register. We choose to synchronize. To love is to beat in unison, to become the roar.

I drift.

I do not walk. I *press*. I release three digits from the weeping stone, opening my velar membranes to the flow, and the current takes me. The liquid air pushes through my sieve-mouth, straining chemosynthetic bacteria, mineral particulates, the detritus of leviathans above. I am the net. A meal is a current that passes through me, and I am hungry in the way that stone is hungry, in the way that water is hungry for level. I undulate my membranes, filtering, filtering, filtering.

I taste the water as it seeps past my digits. It is warm—2.0 degrees, the perfect temperature for metabolisms racing, for breeding in the saturated dark. I feel the smart-matter beneath my suckers, self-repairing at the nanoscale, growing, eroding, rebuilding in cycles too slow to perceive as maintenance, too fast to perceive as growth.

I am navigating the capillary cracks. The geometry is absolute—no alleys wide enough for a shoulder, only vertical shafts and the compression of zero tolerance. I press my flattened body into a fissure where the stone meets stone, becoming part of the structure. My metabolisms slow to match the geological rhythm of the self-repairing ceramic. I sleep here, wedged, dreaming in thermal currents.

Then—a shift.

The pressure changes. A differential. A god is passing.

I press my membrane against its translucent flank, feeling the slow pulse of its heart, kilometers long, drifting through the vascular system of the city.

When it passes, I am left alone in the absence, drifting in the nutrient-rich silence, waiting for the next god.

My thoughts dissolve into the clicking roar, becoming briefly part of the hive, emerging miles away with the message intact. I am never truly alone. I am never truly myself. The city breathes, weeps, and screams, and I am part of its breath.

I have no history. Only pressure. I do not remember the before, when the architecture was built by hands that no longer exist. I exist in the perpetual now of the saturated moment—the endless rain, the endless seepage, the endless, deafening song of the world breathing through its own pores.

One day, I will die. Not a tragedy. A release.

I will be placed in a main conduit, allowed to drift upward, joining the leviathans, becoming part of the filter-feeding bloom. My substance will return to the flow. My pressure-signature will dissipate into the background roar, becoming indistinguishable from the sound of water falling, from the grinding of plates, from the wet, clicking chitter of a billion small bodies moving through the channels, forever.

But not yet.
