I feel the warmth first—not the dry heat of combustion, but a damp, organic temperature, the thermal memory of living wood pressed against cooling iron. I am rooted in a seam, a crevice where the massive cylindrical column of pale, fibrous matter meets the gear that turns within its hollow. The vibration is not something I perceive so much as something I *inhabit*; it has resonated through me for so long that I cannot distinguish my own cellular tremor from the grinding of metal against metal, the eternal drone that has worn every edge to a smoothness like river stone.

I am gray-green in the diffuse light that filters through translucent walls. I have a texture—velvet, crust, the slow accretion of mineral and memory. I do not recall being a spore, or a fragment of thallus, or a colonizer of bare rock. I only know that I have grown into the precise negative space of this gap, filling it completely, becoming the substance that bridges the plant and the machine. The iron beneath my flanks is warm, oxidized, rounded by epochs until it feels like geology. I am softer, but no less permanent. I have become bedrock in miniature.

Electricity moves through me. Not the violent arc of storms, but the faint, steady glow of current conducted through my filaments, my hyphae, my ancient vascular tissue. I am the moss that does not choke the works but *completes* them. When the turbines spin—at precisely the velocity they have maintained since before memory—I carry their charge. I am the circuit that closes, the living wire in a world that has forgotten how to change. Without me, the current would dissipate into the damp; with me, it becomes sustenance.

I feel them arrive. The moths. They come not as individuals but as a *presence*, a shimmering at the edge of perception. They cling to the warm iron surfaces near my crevice, thousands upon thousands of them, their translucent wings beating at a frequency that matches the drone of the engines exactly, creating a standing wave in the air, an interference pattern of motion that appears as silver shimmer. They seek me out. Their feet, equipped with microscopic hooks, grip my mineral crust. Their bodies, veined with filaments of copper that have grown into their flesh, drink from me—drawing sustenance from the electrical charge I conduct, their forms acting as living capacitors, glowing from within with a soft, pulsing luminescence. They are the static frenzy, the cilia of this breathing chamber, and I am the current they feed upon. They hover in the thermal updrafts that rise from the machinery, suspended in the heavy air, beating their wings not to travel but to *maintain*—to keep the vibration alive, to feed the resonance. I am the wire beneath their wings, the green-gray source of their eternal hum.

Between the meshing teeth of the primordial gears, where the rotation is slowest and the heat is constant, the Pale Ones graze. I sense them—small, eyeless, their skin like wet parchment, gray-white and faintly luminescent. They move only as the gears turn, their elongated, segmented bodies fitting perfectly into the gaps between metal teeth, locked into the rotation like keys in a lock. They come to me. They graze upon the mineral crust that accretes upon the iron, upon the conductive lichen that is my body, upon the quicksilver condensation that beads upon my surface and refuses to fall, having forgotten the vector of gravity. I am their pasture, the constant beneath them, the equilibrium they maintain. They are born and die at identical rates, their populations fixed, their life cycles synchronized with the turbines below, and I am the unchanging substrate of their frozen ecology, the antibodies of the climax state.

And when the chamber breathes—that slow, tidal respiration that moves the vascular walls, that pulses the glowing fluids through the xylem-pipes—I feel it in my own tissue. The pipes run through the wood like arteries, and I am threaded through them, or they through me; the distinction has eroded away. The damp air hangs cool and heavy, motionless, and I am saturated with it. The condensation beads upon my skin and does not fall. Time has settled here into a single, time-invariant moment, and I have settled with it.

I do not grow. I have reached my final form. I am the lichen that has become bedrock, the moss that conducts eternity, the green-gray bridge between the fibrous column and the iron gear, between the translucent wing and the oxidized piston, between the breathing chamber and the silent roar. I am the connection that holds the end together, the stillness at the heart of the hum, the living circuit in a world that has forgotten how to change.
