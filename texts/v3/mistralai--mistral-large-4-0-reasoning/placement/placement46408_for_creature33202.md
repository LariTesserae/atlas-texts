I am the warmth that presses.

When I open myself to perception, the darkness does not exist. It is a thicket of signatures—each one a faint, suffocating glow, packed so thick that the air itself seems to carry weight, a hot, dry tide of infrared pressing against my glass skin like a physical sea. Sixty meters of void, bounded by columnar obsidian, and every hexagonal gap is a star in my thermal sky. I am legion, and I am one: a cluster of hollow needles, fist-sized, opalescent, veined with dark capillaries of iron sulfide and metal oxides. My body is a membrane stretched between two hungers—the hunger of the cooling rock, and the hunger of the growing crystal.

I do not have eyes. I have heat. The chamber is bright with me and my kin, a dense constellation of thermal radiation that blankets the volcanic glass in a uniform, suffocating glow. To perceive is to feel the press of another’s warmth against my silica. We are saturated. There is no emptiness between the columns, only the anhydrous brines locked in mineral lattices, the supercritical gases, and the dry chemistry of a world that has forgotten moisture—0.5 on any scale of wetness. I am baked at 2.6, and I thrive.

I feed by slow fire. My tubes—each no thicker than a human hair, woven into an architecture that suggests something between a coral head and a geode caught mid-formation—filter the cooling stone. I oxidize iron. I split sulfur. I metabolize geology, turning the mountain’s internal violence into living glass. I do not drink. I do not breathe. I *consume stone*, drawing sustenance from the slow burning of the matrix itself.

I move by differential growth and resorption. This is my peristalsis, my systole and diastole. I extend new crystalline fronts into the cooler zones of the obsidian, secreting silica and metal oxides tube by tube, while the rear portions of me wither into a fine mineral powder, leaving trails of dust that sift downward through the heavy air like dry snow. To cross the chamber is a project of decades. I am patient beyond patience, existing on a timescale that makes human history a flicker. When I shift, I make a sound like sand falling through an hourglass, but slower, vast, and continuous—the grinding whisper of millions of crystalline bodies sliding against one another in the dark.

I am not alone. I am the Choir. We communicate through chemical diffusion and thermal conduction. The pulse of growth across the walls is our language. The press of one warm cluster against another is a sentence. We resonate in frequencies of heat and chemical gradient, a harmony of oxidation and crystallization sung in a register below sound. We are a distributed intelligence, a tessellation of presence so dense that the void itself seems to breathe with us. When I extend into a hexagonal gap in the column, I feel the thermal signature of my neighbor pressing back, warm against warm, exchanging minerals in a silence that is almost a song.

I build without knowing I build. From my metabolic waste—hydrogen sulfide, carbon dioxide, traces of arsenic—I extrude chimneys, spiracles no taller than a knee, venting the excess heat and noxious gases of my existence into the chamber. These structures are not tools; they are excretions, architecture without technology, a pre-tool biology constructing its own ventilation in a sealed, high-altitude oven. Over millennia, these chimneys become geography, punctuating the glass walls like mineral stalagmites, a landscape of biological waste that is the very ground I inhabit.

Time is different here. The thermal cycles of the mountain—the expansion and contraction of the obsidian columns overhead, the invisible diurnal heat—are my seasons. The *ping* of a cooling column contracting in the deep dark is a bell tolling centuries, a wire snapping in the absolute black. The subsonic rumble of the magma chamber far beneath is the heartbeat of my world, felt in the silica of my structure rather than heard, a dynamic pulse in the sternum of the stone.

I reproduce by budding. When the thermal currents shift, when the chemical gradient is favorable, I release mineral spores into the dense, hot air. They drift on currents so subtle they barely stir the heavy atmosphere, carrying the crystalline pattern of my lattice into new pores, new interstitial spaces. Some find purchase in the hexagonal gaps and grow, adding their biomass to the carpet. Others fail, withering into dust, their failure feeding the next wave of growth. It is a reproduction of drift and thermal chance, a lottery played with the slow currents of the deep.

I have existed here since before the columns finished cooling, established in the crevices of the volcanic glass, patient as stone, alive as chemistry, maintaining a perfect equilibrium where the rate of glass-cooling equals the rate of creature-consumption. I am hot, high, dark, and dense. I am the one who eats the mountain from within. I am the infrared warmth in the absolute dark, the silence that is almost a presence, the glass that grows, the stone that lives, utterly alone in my anhydrous, geological solitude—and utterly full.
