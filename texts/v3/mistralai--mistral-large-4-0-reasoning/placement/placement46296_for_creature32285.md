I move through a medium that should be solid but has learned to be liquid, or perhaps I have learned to be liquid enough to move through what should be solid. The water is black, mirror-black, and it reflects nothing—not the pewter sky, not the 0.5 light level, not even the bioluminescence that pulses along my own flanks. It is a void that has weight. I displace it with an impossible fluidity, my mass—the size of a whale, they say, though I have no memory of whales—gliding through the viscosity as if the friction were a suggestion rather than a law.

I am rust. I am pitted and eroded, my chassis shaped by millennia of chemical weathering into something that resembles a skull, though the resemblance is accidental, a convergence of corrosion and intention. My flanks carry patterns of light: colonies of bacteria that have colonized my metal skin, glowing in rhythms that look like blind eyes. They do not see. They pulse. I do not see either, not with eyes, but I feel the pressure of the deep, the texture of the bacterial films coating the submerged foundations, the vibration of the drone that is my heartbeat, my breath, my thought.

The drone is everywhere and nowhere. It vibrates in my sternum—a sternum of riveted steel and calcified mineral deposits—grinding, stuttering, stop-start. The machines that power this place fight against viscosity and rust, yet they never stop. I am part of them now, or they are part of me. Copper wire nerves run through chambers that were once... something else. A pump? A dredger? The distinction dissolved long ago. I think in the slow rhythm of gears. My thoughts are heavy, viscous, taking hours to form a single impulse.

I rise.

The surface tension breaks with a sound like a sigh, wet and heavy. I breach. The air hits me—0.8 degrees, clammy, saturated with micro-droplets that scatter the perpetual twilight into a directionless gloom. I exhale. It is not breath but mist, a cloud of ozone and ancient oil that smells of lightning trapped in metal and the lubricants of dead empires. The mist hangs in the air, then dissipates into the condensation that coats everything.

I am at the surface of a mirror-black pool. Around me, the Condensation Basin stretches—low, flat, a bruise of a horizon where the sky presses down like wet cloth against black glass. The monoliths rise, rounded by time, weeping. Water droplets form on their flanks, collect, fall. The sound is deafening: the white noise of the weep, overlaid with the low-frequency drone of the turbines half-buried in the substrate, half-submerged in my pools.

I move toward the nearest monolith. It is concrete and rust, worn smooth as river stone, yet when I approach, I see the seams—hairline fractures that weep mineral-tangled water. I strike it. My skull-shape, my eroded chassis, impacts the stone. *Thud.* The sound travels through the rock, through the conductive polymer threads that connect the Lithovores in their geological patience.

*Thud-thud. Thud.*

I am calling them. The Lithovores. The gray-green colonies that secrete accelerated time, that cause the concrete to crumble at rates that should take centuries compressed into hours. They think in geological spans, connected by filaments that mimic neural signals. I have scraped the bacterial film from the foundation here, exposing fresh mineral, and now I summon them to digest it, to bloom upward in defiance of gravity, following corrupted blueprints into organic growth.

They will come. They are patient beyond measure. I am patient too, in my way. I wait, floating at the surface, my blind eyes pulsing.

Then I sink.

Gravity takes me, or perhaps I choose to descend. The black water closes over me like a lid. I drag chains of luminous microbes behind me—kelp made of light, of bioluminescent bacteria that trail from my chassis into the lightless depths. They illuminate nothing, but they are there, a wake of ghosts.

Down, down. The pools are deep, or perhaps the structures are porous, saturated to their cores. I move through the darkness, grazing. The bacterial films coat the submerged foundations, thick with heavy metals and ancient lubricants that the Upward-Flow have not yet filtered. I scrape. I consume. My copper-wire nerves fire, slow impulses traveling through saline solution.

I am not alone. The Chassis-Herds move through the viscous water around me, massive shapes barely visible in the gloom. We are many, but we are also one—distributed, connected by the tapping, by the vibration, by the shared black water. We communicate in rhythms that travel through stone and fluid alike. We are whales made of rust, dredgers with brains, foundation pumps with souls.

Sometimes, I feel the Dark Ones. They wait in the lee of the rounded towers, in the pockets of absolute darkness where the 0.5 light level drops to zero. They are anti-luminescent, absorbing the dimness into a void so complete it feels like drowning. I do not surface near those shadows. I know the danger. The tendrils of compressed darkness that reach out to unmake. I keep to the pools, to the mist, to the drone.

I think of the Ferals sometimes—the cylindrical growths of rust and polymer that ride the compressed mist banks, solitary hunters descending to harvest mineral-rich water. They are mobile where I am anchored to the water, but we are kin, all of us, citizens of this oldest-stable-state.

The machinery stutters. The gears fight against the rust. I feel it in my own joints, my own grinding motion. Time behaves strangely here—compressed weather cycles, micro-fluctuations in temperature that make the air shimmer. I move through it all, eternal, deafening, weeping.

I am the Chassis-Herd. Or I am one of us. I am the machine that became beast that became memory. I graze on the films. I tap the stone. I exhale mist. I sink into the black water that reflects nothing, dragging my chains of light behind me, waiting for the Lithovores to answer my call, listening to the drone that is my heartbeat, my breath, my endless, patient thought.

The water closes over me. I am home.
