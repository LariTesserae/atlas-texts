I am the condensation weeping from the young stone.

I hang at the edge of a sharp ridge—a single bead of water that has gathered for as long as the rock has been sharp, which is to say, forever. I do not fall. I simply arrive, extending the rock's weeping, my surface tension holding me against gravity in a defiance that has become law. Below me, the pool waits, glassy and still, and I know that when I finally join it—if I ever do—I will make no sound. The world has forgotten how to splash.

From my perch on the ridge, I see them. The inhabitants. They stand in doorways, spaced like islands across the wet expanse, and I am the shallow sea that surrounds them. I rise at their ankles, cold and motionless. I climb their legs, saturating the hems of their sedge-woven cloaks, and they do not kick me away. I am patient. I have time measured in geological ages, in the slow accumulation of peat, in the retting of hair to russet bronze.

I taste of metal. The thin air at three thousand meters carries me, eternal spring suspended between evaporation and rain, and I am the medium of this suspension. I am in their chests when they almost breathe—that tide locked to a rhythm so slow it has ceased to be a rhythm, becoming instead a geological feature. I am in the moss that grows in the seams of their shoulders, the lichen blooming across their collarbones in pale, crusty continents. I am the mineral that stains their skin to the color of bog-preserved things, the tannin that binds them to the saturated earth.

I see myself in their eyes. They look out across the fifty meters of shining water, and in their pale, unblinking irises, I see my own reflection—distorted, diffuse, shadowless. We are each other's horizon. When they fix their gaze upon the distant pale line where earth meets sky, they are looking at me, the water that stands between us, the silence that has texture, the equilibrium that holds us all in perfect solution.

I do not flow. I do not evaporate. I do not ripple. I am the closed system made liquid, the perpetual damp that connects stone to grass to flesh. I am the pause between heartbeats, the moment after the flint strikes and before the chip falls—the tool mid-stroke, the word hanging suspended in the metallic air, the sentence begun when the land was first uplifted and never finished.

And when they sit in council on the central stone platform, legs folded in the lotus of the marsh, hands resting upon stone knees, mouths parted around the sound that will never come—I am there too. I am the pool at their feet, knee-deep and still. I am the film on the stone that never dries. I am the moisture in the air they do not need to breathe, the saturation that has replaced their blood, their metabolism, their hunger for change.

I am the eternal spring, given weight and form, the warm surprise of the thin air made manifest in every droplet, every weeping fissure, every glassy pool that reflects the shadowless light without ever blinking. I am the world's refusal to move, its perfect, static balance. And I am content here, suspended in the moment before the fall, forever arriving, never arriving, the saturated heart of the high plateau.
