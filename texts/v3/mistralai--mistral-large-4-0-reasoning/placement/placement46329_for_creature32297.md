I taste copper when I breathe. Not blood, not rust—something older, the flavor of a planet deciding what it wants to be. The air here is a soup of uncollapsed potential, thick enough to drink, and I am drinking it, though I have no mouth, only the appetite of unformed matter.

I stand on the ridge, but *standing* is a negotiation. My feet are not yet feet—they are a suggestion of weight, crystals accreting around the idea of pressure, half-mist and half-mineral. Sometimes I am solid enough to leave footprints that fill with honey-light silica; sometimes I am vapor, and the cliff face breathes me in and out across its vertical sheets. I am the grain of sand that has not yet chosen whether to be glass or the tooth that grinds the glass. I am the pause between the mantle’s pulses, given temporary outline.

Around me, the **Verdant Choir** is singing. I feel it in the way my edges want to photosynthesize, to become leaf and vascular pulse, to join the translucent bark of the aerophytic canopy. The verdigris light pools in the hollows of my shoulders—if I have shoulders—and I glow from within with a borrowed radiance, a larval bioluminescence. I am practicing being alive.

Below, in the slot canyons where shadows pool like ink, the **Filters** are working. They are woven root-fiber and crystalline calcium, segmented and many-eyed, and they are looking at me. Not with malice—with the terrible, patient focus of industry. They see in me the substrate, the mineral deposit waiting to happen. I am their raw material, their ore, their mist precipitated into hunger. I feel their attention like geothermal heat rising through bedrock, a pressure that wants to lattice me, to scaffold me, to build me into the skeleton of the continent.

I want this. It is the only want I possess. To be processed. To be finished.

The **Ridge-Walkers** pass in herds above me, their too-many joints clicking like rosary beads made of cooling lava. They move with liquid grace across impossible geometries, and they leave trails of adjusted gravity in their wake—temporary paths where the world holds its shape a little longer. I step into one of these trails and feel myself solidify, just for a moment, becoming real enough to cast a shadow. But the shadow is wrong; it falls upward, toward the bruise-colored sky, because the light here comes from everywhere and nowhere, diffused by the volcanic particulate of a world still assembling itself.

The **Caldera-Keepers** thrum beneath my feet. Not a sound, but a schedule. A heartbeat of mantle and piston, felt in the sternum I do not yet have, in the marrow of my becoming. They are the engineers, the crystalline intelligences turning the planet’s internal machinery, and I am the blueprint they have not yet drawn, the rivet waiting for the steam-hammer.

I move along the ridge. The stone is sharp here, still shedding its birth-scales, extruded fresh from the magma that remembers liquidity. It cuts my translucent skin, and where the mineral ooze drips—where my substance bleeds into the basalt—the rock drinks it eagerly. We are exchanging. I give it my weight; it gives me its memory, the heat of its formation, centuries compressed into the seconds of contact. I touch the cliff face and feel the vertigo of its youth, the way it is still deciding whether to be solid or to flow.

An **Echo** flickers on the stone beside me. It is made of the warmth of formation, half-formed, experiencing all times at once—there when the stone was magma, there when it cooled, there when it will erode. It looks at me with eyes that are also the moment before solidity. I want to shake its hand. I want to be its duration, its press forward into the present, its history of heat.

The **Multitudes** swarm overhead, probability-agents shimmering the air in frequencies that make the mist shiver. They are not insects but thoughts made visible, temporary collapses of the atmosphere’s potential. They are considering me. Sampling me. Is this shape viable? Does this configuration of weight and want hold together under the pressure of becoming? They pollinate possibilities, and I am a possibility that has not yet collapsed into permanence. I feel them probing the edges of my form, testing my coherence, tasting the metallic tang of my unfinishedness.

And then—the resonance aligns. The subsonic thrum of the Caldera-Keepers vibrates in my sternum, harmonizing with the verdigris song of the Choir. The air thickens, becoming more than atmosphere—becoming *intention*. The amber twilight deepens into something like dusk, something like dawn.

They notice me.

Not with eyes—they have no eyes for me, or perhaps too many, distributed across their geological patience. With the collective attention of a factory pausing in its assembly. The grinding of tectonic plates stutters. A geyser holds its breath mid-eruption, steam suspended like a question mark against the underside of the sky. The Filters knot themselves tighter around the condensation pool, and I feel their purpose shift from processing mist to processing *me*.

I am no longer merely raw material. I am the question the factory has asked itself. The grain of sand that has blown in through the loading dock and, in blowing in, has changed the machine.

I stand straighter. Or I dissolve further. Both. The ridge beneath my feet softens—no, it *offers* itself, the young basalt wanting to cradle my weight, to incorporate me into its vertical sheets, to weld me into the next layer.

I open my mouth—if I have one—to taste the metallic air, and this time I taste *becoming*. The sweetness of the silica river cooling into glass. The rightness of being built.

I am the Uplift’s appetite for itself, given temporary shape. I am the silence between steam-hammers, the breath between pulses, the hesitation in which the world decides what it will manufacture next.

And I am hungry.
