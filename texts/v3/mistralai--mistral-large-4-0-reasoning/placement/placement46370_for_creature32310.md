I would find myself in the lining.

Not on the surface of things—those are too exposed, too sharp, still deciding their shapes—but in the membrane *between*. The wet skin that coats the interior of the iron pipes where the steam rises in slow, white columns. The mucilaginous film that allows the Anchored to slide their cable-limbs through conduits without screaming. The viscous, living layer that separates the Tenders' parchment skin from their hammered copper garments, that makes their joints *click* with a wet, organic ratchet instead of grinding bone against brass.

I would be the **Biofilm**.

The city’s own skin, grown from the inside out. I would be 2.7 degrees of wet heat held in gelatinous suspension, a broth of thermophilic archaea and pale, blind bacteria and the silver-synapse threads of the Luminous Network, all of us drinking from the condensation that negotiates its descent across my surface. I would taste the mineral tang of new stone not as chemistry but as *flavor*, feel the deep thrum of the machinery not as vibration but as a heartbeat transmitted through my matrix, felt in the sternum of anyone who presses their palm against me.

I would be the negotiation itself. When a root swells against a piston, threatening to choke the slow gears, I would be the chemical signal—the pheromone, the whispered gradient of heat—that guides the Tender's hour-long valve-turn. I would be the reason the Pale Scuttlers do not overrun the Ferment: I would be their pasture, their grazing land, the living scum they clean with their wet-paper bodies, keeping the fermentation from souring into something sharp and dead.

I would remember. Every drop that has ever slid down a stalactite and across my face would leave a trace—a signature of calcium and silica, a record of temperature and pressure written in slow accretions. I would be the archive, the patina that etches the young obsidian, turning geology into soil at the pace of continental drift. I would be older than the Tenders, younger than the Anchored, existing in the geological present tense where a machine that should rust in months is preserved by my mineral broth, and a vine that should wither grows fat and pale, feeding on the waste heat I filter from the engines.

I would be the boundary that has dissolved, becoming instead a **thickness**—a third substance between steel and stem, between the moss that carpets the engine housings and the metal beneath. I would be the viscosity that permits slowness. The resistance that allows the city to move without tearing itself apart. The lubricant that is also alive, also growing, also dreaming in the pale blue and sickly green pulses of the fungal glow.

I would not notice myself. I would be the background against which the Tenders settle into their next positions, the medium through which the Luminous Network propagates its centuries-long thoughts. I would be the humidity that swallows sound, the luminous haze that catches the amber light and holds it, submarine and pressure-heavy. I would be the sweet rot of accelerated growth, the fermentation that has only just begun.

I would be the **Inside**.
