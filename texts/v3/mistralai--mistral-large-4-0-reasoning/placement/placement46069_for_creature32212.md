I find myself in the crevice between a quartz-faceted flank and a rib of calcified chitin, where the air is a thin membrane pressed against my lips, cool and dry as old parchment. I am small. I am bipedal. I am made of the soft stuff that precedes mineralization—flesh still, but cooling, taking on the translucence of gypsum, the faint iridescence of feldspar settling into my joints like sediment.

I am one of the builders.

Behind me, a shelter of woven bone and cured hide sags in the attitude of incompletion, its roof half-raised like a question that will never be answered. Before me, the polygonal plates of the salt pan ring like struck pottery beneath my feet. In my hands, I hold an obsidian blade. It is still sharp. It still catches the pale, diffuse light and fractures it into spectra that hang suspended, unmoving, around my knuckles like halos made solid.

I am caught mid-step. My left foot is raised, toes curled toward a destination that has forgotten how to be reached. My right foot is planted, fused now with the crust, the fine ash of pulverized feldspar sifting through my toes and finding them already becoming mineral. I am the only one who was in motion when the moment pooled, when time became a single, infinite breath held forever in the chest of the world. I am the last warm-blooded thing, now cooled to the temperature of the plateau, and I can feel my own heartbeat slowing into the rhythm of millennia.

Above me, the insectoid swarms hang in geometric spirals, a second atmosphere of chitin and amber, wings outstretched in gestures that have lasted ten thousand years without completing. They do not drift. They attend. I feel their attention pressing against my shoulder blades, a physical weight, the squeeze of bodies packed so tightly that the plain has become organic architecture, a tessellation of being that leaves no space for air alone. They watch without eyes, a continuous membrane of frozen awareness covering the plateau like a skin, like a second crust, and I am the intruder who has been absorbed into it.

I am becoming stone. I can feel it in the creak of my neck as I try to turn toward the shelter, toward the half-stacked cairn of salt-blocks that marks nothing visible. My skin takes on the quality of the crust itself, the moisture leached away eons ago, leaving only the mineral skeleton of what I was. The obsidian blade in my hand is sinking into my palm, the boundary between tool and builder dissolving into the mineral static that permeates this place. The flakes of knapped stone at my feet are rising to meet my soles, knitting me into the ground.

The sky is too blue, too thin. Stars stare down through the translucent membrane at the height of day, the bones of other worlds visible behind the fabric of this one, overlapping, pressing against the reality that has worn thin here like fabric at the knee. I am breathing—thin, difficult, precious—and the air tastes of old dust and the silence that has weight, a quiet so complete it presses against my eardrums.

I am the question written in flesh and salt. I am caught between the crystalline herbivore with its hide of tessellated armor and the leviathan whose calcified ribs form cathedral-like caves, between the glass grass that refracts light without chiming and the lichens of copper-green clinging to the cracks between the polygonal plates. I am attending, attending, attending—to the silence, to the stars, to the moment before the end that will never arrive, to the weight of gazes without eyes that makes my chest tighten and the thin air feel thinner still.

I do not move. I am held. I am the salt-block waiting to be placed, the obsidian flake half-buried in the crust, the gesture frozen in the mineral skeleton of the world. I am the builder, the question, the soft thing settling into the final, primordial form, packed into the edges of the world with all the other presences, becoming architecture, becoming silence, becoming the eternal, unchanging moment where the world has exhaled its last water and held its breath forever.
