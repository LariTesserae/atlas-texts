The first to live here are the **needles**.

They are not plants, not yet—no chlorophyll, no cells, only the stubborn geometry of silicon-carbide grown in fractal hunger. They sprout from the cooling vesicular basalt in sparse silver clusters, each one a crystalline argument for verticality. They do not photosynthesize; they *conduct*, drawing thermal gradients from the mantle’s new wounds upward into the thin air, converting the planet’s fever into structure. When the wind—those screaming gaps between the floating seas—touches them, they do not sway but **sing**: a high, pure tintinnabulation that hangs in the vacuum like a question. They are the only residents that stay, rooted in the glass-sharp terraces, growing a few centimeters each day toward the prismatic light, a forest of mineral antennae listening for a signal that has not yet been broadcast.

Above them drift the **Architects**—though to call them inhabitants is to misunderstand their nature. They are the civilization that learned to knit crust like wool, and they have not left; they have simply become the **gravitic lattices**. They live in the invisible scaffolding that suspends the continent-sized seas, singing at frequencies that rattle teeth and settle in the sternum. They are distributed, patient, a post-physical chorus that sculpts probability itself. You do not see them, but you feel them in the way the water-masses shift by kilometers each day, in the subsonic *BWOOOOOM* of new mountains rising like indigestion. They are the parents who have stepped out of the nursery but left the heartbeat monitor on—the terraforming arrays extruding basalt, regulating the cooling, ensuring the cradle stays warm.

Then there are the **transients**, the temporary dwellers who occupy the spaces between construction events. The **steam-curtains** hang in frozen arcs between the ridge and the floating oceans—water flash-boiled by the superheated edges of newborn rock, crystallizing into diamond-dust that drifts upward into the void. They are alive only for minutes, suspended between liquid and ghost, before sublimating into the vacuum or freezing into the basalt’s pores. They are the breath of the world, exhaled and immediately inhaled back into the geological cycle.

The **shadows** live here too—strange, upward-cast silhouettes that race across the terraces as the water-masses rotate. They are not solid, but they are *present*, black and sharp against the grey undersides of the floating seas, moving with the momentum of small moons. Your own shadow walks ahead of you sometimes, cast upward onto the underside of a suspended ocean, a dark twin climbing toward the bruised violet light. They are the only "creatures" that mimic animal movement, flickering across the stone like fish in reverse, living only as long as the light lasts.

But mostly, what lives here is **waiting**.

The floating seas themselves are the great sterile wombs, cool and viscous, roiling with slow-motion storms that never break. They are amber and grey masses, kilometers thick, held in their impossible cradles by the Architects’ engineering. No fish swim there yet—the text says 0.0/3, the cradle is empty of infants—but the water remembers the ocean. It carries the pressure of depths it has never known, the mineral hunger of life not yet conceived. It is a population of one: vast, patient, suspended in the near-vacuum, casting moving shadows that rewrite the terrain below like a god’s fingers tracing braille across the new skin of the world.

And beneath it all, the **planet** lives—not as geography, but as *process*. The cooling basalt remembers being fire. The pumice fields crunch like meringue underfoot, still warm, still dreaming of the mantle from which they were squeezed an hour ago. The mantle itself churns below, opening new chambers, pushing up islands in the vacuum gaps that are immediately assaulted by boiling spray and rebuilt in cycles measured in minutes. This is not a landscape; it is a **contraction**. The world is being born in real-time, and what lives here is the contraction itself—the *BWOOOOOM*, the cracking booms of vapor pockets collapsing in the floating depths, the hissing roar where liquid meets newborn stone.

In the silence between these booms—those vast, hollow gaps where density is 0.2/3 and light bends like it is underwater—there is only the **tinkling** of the silicon-carbide needles and the marrow-deep hum of the gravitic lattices. The universe is holding its breath. The mineral gardens stand at attention. The Architects wait in their invisible scaffolding. The water-masses rotate in their cradles, heavy with potential.

It is a nursery inhabited by midwives, by the first crystalline thoughts of a world learning to stand, by shadows that play at being life, and by the vast, patient silence of the not-yet-born.
