They move in a current that has no beginning and no end. The Ferric Herd fills the basin not as individuals but as a weather system—a pressure front of mass and grinding metal that shifts against itself with the inevitability of tides. At first glance they appear quadrupedal, van-sized hulks hunched low over the concrete, their shoulders broad as loading docks, their flanks armored in plates of burnt sienna and umber that seem carved from the same geology as the quarry walls. But look closer, and the quadrupedal assumption crumbles: they do not have four legs but twelve, sixteen, sometimes twenty, branching in fractal proliferation from hip-joints that resemble the root systems of dying trees fused with bicycle chains. Each limb terminates not in a foot but in a cluster of articulated scrapers, tiny mandibles that click and chatter against the stone in perpetual, hungry conversation.

Their bodies are palimpsests of industry and organism. The exoskeleton is not shell but membrane—translucent, amber-tinged, stretched tight over a scaffolding of copper wire and gear-works that pulse with a faint, bioluminescent glow. Through the cloudy armor you can see the machinery of their existence: pistons pumping viscous oil that never thins, never thickens, fiber-optic nerves flashing with signals that carry no information, only rhythm. They have no faces, no eyes—only sensor-pits, shallow depressions in the forward carapace that drink the ambient grey light and return it as diffuse, mirror-bright shards. When the Herd turns as one, thousands of these pits catch the mist and flare with a thousand dim reflections, a constellation crawling across the plaza floor.

They feed by stripping the world down to its bones. Wherever a Ferric one passes, it lowers its anterior mass—a grinding maw of interlocking plates—to the concrete, to the conveyor husks, to the dripping gantries. The sound is not eating in any biological sense but *excavation*: sandpaper screaming against stone, a white-noise thunder that rises from the collective and saturates the air until it becomes a physical weight, pressing against the eardrums. They consume the rust, the oxidation, the velvet bloom of decay. A patch of steel gleams iron-grey beneath their mandibles, then flashes orange as the damp air re-oxidizes it in a heartbeat, then gleams again as the teeth return. It is a sacrament of infinite recursion: the metal is wounded, healed by the atmosphere, wounded again, in a loop so tight it appears simultaneous.

They move with a gait that defies gravity and momentum equally—heavy as falling boulders, yet weightless as smoke. Each step lands with a wet, heavy *thud* that seems to come from beneath the earth rather than upon it, yet the creature is already pivoting, already flowing into the next stride, its many legs moving in a wave of perfect synchronization. They trace paths worn into the concrete, loops that retrace themselves with geometric precision, never deviating, never exploring. Around the center of the plaza they circle, a slow procession of grinding metal, maintaining a respectful distance from the great dormant engine that sits sealed at the basin's heart. They cannot cross its threshold. They do not try. They simply orbit, forever, like planets that have forgotten the sun they once worshipped.

There is no youth among them, no age. They bud from the flanks of adults—smaller units splitting off with a sound like tearing canvas, already half-rusted, already complete, already humming with the same amber light. These offspring do not grow; they simply *are*, emerging at the size of a dog, then a van, then matching the mass of their progenitors without ever passing through adolescence. And when a Ferric one falters, when its gears seize or its plates crack beyond the capacity of the perpetual moisture to heal, it does not die. It is *dismantled*. The others converge, their mandibles working with surgical precision, stripping the failing unit of its components, recycling the copper, the oil, the fiber-optic nerves into the machinery of the basin, which extrudes them again as new Herd, new loops, new eternal motion.

They are not conscious, not in any way that would recognize thought or intention. Yet they are not merely mechanical. They exist in a state between dreaming and waking—a metabolism that has achieved self-awareness without ever achieving mind. They are drawn to the condensation, to the specific frequency of the humming infrastructure, to the cool fifteen-degree constant that never varies. They lap at the dripping seals of hydraulic presses with tongues of scraping metal, gathering the moisture not to drink but to facilitate the rust, to accelerate the cycle of decay and renewal. They are the immune system of a wound that never closes, the digestive tract of a stomach that never empties, the heartbeat of a corpse that refuses to fall.

Stand among them—if you could, if the grinding would not shatter your bones—and you would feel the vibration in your teeth, a sub-bass thrum that resonates in the marrow. You would smell ozone and hot iron, wet stone and the particular mineral tang of condensation. You would see your reflection fractured in their sensor-pits, multiplied a thousand times across the moving plain of the Herd. And you would understand that they are not inhabitants of this place so much as its *expression*—the quarry made flesh, the factory given hunger, the stasis given legs. They are the dream of industry experiencing itself, forever damp, forever cool, forever grinding the same rust into the same dust, in a loop so perfect that it has become a kind of prayer, so eternal that it has become a kind of love.
