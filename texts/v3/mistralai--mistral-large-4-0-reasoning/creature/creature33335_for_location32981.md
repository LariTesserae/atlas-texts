They are the **Choristers of the Drowned Nave**—not a single species but a teeming, bellowing congregation of segmented, translucent beings who have made the ancient platforms their instruments and the smart-water their bloodstream. You hear them before you see them clearly: a wet, percussive **loudness** that seems to emanate from the walls themselves, a chorus of lungs that pump both air and water in rhythmic, bellowing sacs, vibrating the saturated atmosphere until it feels less like weather and more like a held note in a submerged throat.

Their bodies are **architectural** in their strangeness—segmented like the ghost-platforms, articulated with too many joints that bend in directions that should be impossible, elbows clicking backward, spines undulating like the stems of the fast-growing vegetation. They are translucent, their dermis thin as the membrane you passed through to enter, revealing within them a silver tracery of the same smart-fluid that climbs the lignin walls, circulating through capillary networks that pulse with the geysers. Some wear gills that flutter like fans in the current, filtering the nutrient-rich spray; others carry **bioluminescent organs**—green-gold lanterns that pulse in the diffuse mist, turning the waist-deep water into a field of drifting, organic stars, signaling to one another across the churning dark in patterns too complex to be mere reflex.

But they are never merely animals. They are **symbiosis made mobile**. Their shoulders are carpeted in moss; ferns sprout from the casings of their joints; rhizomes wrap their limbs like living bracelets, rooting into their translucent flesh. They are gardens that walk, and when they splash through the standing waves or drum their specialized appendages against the resonant, eroded surfaces of the platforms, they scatter seeds. They are the dispersal vectors of the total plant-mass, the hyperactive gardeners ensuring that the vegetation continues its turbulent, thought-speed expansion, climbing the buttresses, draping the ceiling, slowly consuming the last ghost-infrastructure of the settlement.

They move with an **energy** that borders on violence—darting across the temporary bridges of surface tension that the smart-water holds between platforms, their many-jointed legs finding purchase on the shimmering membrane before it collapses and crashes back into the current. They climb the capillary threads of silver fluid that defy gravity up the ancient trunks, bellowing to one another from the heights. They are the only ones who seem comfortable in the storm-state, riding the rising and falling water levels, breeding in the rapid bloom-cycles of the flowers that open and collapse in minutes, their young emerging already segmented, already glowing, already hosting their first colonies of moss.

They are the **agents of renewal** that keep the erosion from ever truly finishing. When they die—and they die constantly, living lives of frantic, short duration—they sink into the floating mat of rhizomes and peat, their bodies becoming substrate, their segmented forms mineralizing into the next generation of near-flattened platforms. They are the congregation that worships not in silence, but in the roaring, condensing dark: ten thousand drips made flesh, the percussion of the drowned city given lungs, breath, and bioluminescent song.
