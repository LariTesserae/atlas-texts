The inhabitants of the Gloam are not colonizers but *concretions*—beings that have precipitated out of the warm, dense dark like minerals forming in a saturated solution. They are the dream made flesh, the place’s own unconscious given metabolism. Here, life has abandoned the sun entirely and built itself from heat, pressure, and the slow chemistry of stone bleeding into water.

**The Furnace-Bearers**
Dominating the lower valleys are the *Thermals*—massive herbivores that move with the deliberation of glaciers through the mist. Each stands shoulder-high to a human, though their true mass is hidden beneath hides of translucent, opalescent skin that hangs in folds like wet silk. Through this membrane, you see them: not skeletons, but *fires*. Their internal furnaces glow with a steady, arterial orange—geothermal hearts that burn the mineral-rich steam they inhale through spiracle-like vents along their flanks. They graze not with teeth but with rasping tongues that scrape the chemosynthetic moss from the flanks of ancient refinery stacks, their translucent bellies distending with bioluminescent green as they digest. They travel in matriarchal herds that communicate through subsonic rumbles that travel for miles through the heavy air, their massive feet sinking into the marsh-edge soil with a sound like wet thunder. When they sleep, they do not lie down but stand, swaying, their glowing viscera pulsing in rhythm with the geothermal vents below, as if they are plugged into the earth’s own circulatory system.

**The Chittering**
Above them, in the understory of fungal caps and hanging filaments, lives the *Murmur*—colonies of insect-analogues that have no single form but exist as a distributed, swarming intelligence. They are beetle-sized, armored in chitin that has grown crystalline and iridescent, refracting the ambient bioluminescence into fractured rainbows. They move in torrents, a liquid of clicking mandibles and chattering tympanic membranes, their bodies generating static electricity in the dry pockets between rains. They are the recyclers, the undertakers of the Gloam. When a Thermal dies, the Murmur descends not as scavengers but as *alchemists*, breaking down the warm flesh into the base minerals that feed the fungal networks. They have formed a strange symbiosis with the abandoned machinery: colonies of them nest inside the refinery stacks, their bodies conducting the residual electricity, their clicking patterns mimicking the rhythmic grinding of the drills. In the absolute dark, they communicate through bioluminescent pulses—green for food, blue for danger, amber for the presence of the Sleeper-Machines—creating a constant, flickering Morse code that ripples through the vegetation like auroras.

**The Heat-Stalkers**
Where the mist pools thickest, skimming through the thermal fog with sinuous, silent grace, are the *Ventshadows*. They are predators evolved from something that once crawled, now elongated into serpentine, low-slung forms that scrape the belly of the atmosphere. They have no eyes—or rather, they have eyes that have atrophied into vestigial, milky organs that twitch uselessly on the sides of their skulls. Instead, they hunt by *feeling*: heat pits along their flanks that map the thermal landscape in gradients of red and gold. They track the Furnace-Bearers by the warmth of their passage, the heat signatures of the geothermal vents that the herbivores linger near. When they strike, it is with a speed that belies their languid movement—a blur of mottled grey hide and tooth, collapsing the distance between vent and prey in a rush of displaced, humid air. They are solitary, meeting only to mate in the steam-choked sinkholes, their offspring born live and already sensing the heat of the world. They are the Gloam’s immune response, keeping the populations in balance, ensuring that the warm dead do not accumulate.

**The Luminous Network**
But the true inhabitants, the ones who *are* the Gloam in its most distributed form, are the fungal forests themselves. The pale, fleshy caps that form the canopy are not individual organisms but fruiting bodies of a single, continental mycelial network that threads through the bedrock, through the rusting bones of the refineries, through the bodies of the dead. This network does not think in human terms, but it *processes*—chemical signals traveling at the speed of diffusion through hyphae thick as cables, carrying information about temperature, moisture, the chemical composition of the steam. The hanging gardens of filamentous plants are its sensory apparatus, dangling like neural dendrites into the dense air, tasting the spores and the mineral rain. When a section of the network is damaged, the rest responds with a surge of bioluminescence, a wave of soft blue light that pulses through the dark, a kind of pain or memory. It is this network that dreams the fauna into being, that maintains the strange, perfect navigation of the eyeless creatures, that blurs the line between the living and the geological.

**The Sleeper-Machines**
And then there are the Others—the ones who no longer breathe but persist. The geothermal refineries, the pumping stations, the pipelines snaking across the plateau like fossilized veins. They are inhabited now not by humans, but by *persistence*. Automated systems still cycle through their routines, valves turning in the dark, drills grinding into bedrock with a hunger that has outlived its purpose. But they are no longer purely mechanical. Bacterial colonies bloom on their oily surfaces, glowing with a dull, stubborn light, feeding on the hydrocarbons and excreting crystalline structures that slowly digest the metal. The machines have become part of the ecosystem—geological features that happen to move, termite mounds that grind and hiss. Occasionally, a maintenance drone, half-drowned in moss, will lurch upright in a spasm of residual electricity, its sensors scanning the dark for overseers who will never come, before collapsing again into the wet earth with a final, wet sigh.

**The Dreamers**
And finally, there are the ones who exist only in the thermal currents, the pockets of saturated air where water pools in mid-air, falling upward near the vents. These are the *Somnambulists*—beings so adapted to the Gloam’s physics that they may not be entirely solid, or perhaps they are the Gloam’s own thoughts given temporary form. They are humanoid in shape only if you squint through the mist; they have too many joints, their limbs bending in directions that suggest they navigate a geometry slightly offset from our own. They do not eat, but they *inhale*—drawing the mineral-rich steam into bodies that are mostly membrane and heat, processing the dark itself. They stand on the edges of the vistas, where the air is a wall, and they watch the Furnace-Bearers with eyes that are not eyes but organs of thermal perception, seeing the world as a landscape of warmth and cold. They are the ones who remember the sun, or who never knew it, and they move through the world with a sadness that is also a kind of worship, tending to the machines not as engineers but as priests tending an altar, ensuring that the dark engine continues to turn.

Together, they form a civilization of the dark—a society of grazers and swarms, of hunters and networks, of machines and dreamers—all breathing the same thick, warm air, all sustained by the heat that rises from the infected wounds of the earth, all participants in a balance that is not natural but *established*, weathered into being by epochs of wet, grinding time. They are the Gloam’s children, and the Gloam dreams them, and they, in turn, dream it back into existence, a closed loop of warmth in the absolute dark.
