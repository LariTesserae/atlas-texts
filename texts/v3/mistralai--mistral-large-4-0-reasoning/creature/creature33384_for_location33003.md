What lives here is not a population but a pressure—a distributed sentience that has learned to wear the plateau like a garment. The inhabitants are layered, porous, and mutually constitutive, so that to identify one is to begin describing all of them.

First, there is the **Mat itself**, which is less a collection of plants than a single, colonial organism that has achieved architectural scale. It does not merely grow over the stone; it digests it, secreting acids that etch the satin-smooth surfaces into new geometries of cup and hollow. The Mat breathes—a slow, tidal respiration that lifts the spongy crust several inches and lets it settle again, pumping moisture from the deep chambers up through fungal hyphae that thread the rock like neural pathways. It is the nervous system of the High Hollow, and it dreams in green bioluminescence. When the light fades, the pools in the basins do not merely reflect; they pulse with a faint, verdant luminescence that suggests the Mat is thinking in frequencies below the threshold of sight, communicating through light that only the dark-adapted fauna can read.

Then there are the **Swarm-Kin**, the winged multitudes that rise in clouds dark enough to eclipse the sky. They are not birds, not insects as you would understand them, but something older—perhaps motile spores given muscle and membrane, or perhaps the Mat’s own reproductive organs externalized into the air. They move as a single organism, wheeling in synchronized waves that ripple across the mesas, their collective wingbeats generating a thrumming that merges with the wind’s moan. They do not hunt; they harvest. They drink from the condensing mist, and in return they pollinate the fungal networks, carrying the Mat’s genetic material from basin to basin in vast, deafening migrations that follow the moisture cycles.

The **Grazers** move beneath them, herds of small, sure-footed creatures whose many legs or hooves create the rolling thunder that never ceases. They are the Mat’s circulatory system, its immune response. They do not consume the living crust—that would be cannibalism—but they graze its senescent edges, its dying layers, pruning the architecture so that new growth can erupt. They are the pulse you feel when you stand still, the pressure wave that travels through the spongy ground as they move in synchronized floods from hollow to hollow, following the green glow of the bioluminescent pools.

But the true inhabitants are the **Hollow-Dwellers**, the things that live in the negative space—the cavernous tunnels and empty pockets that constitute most of the physical volume of this world. These are the burrowers, the crawlers, the blind and pressure-sensitive entities that have never seen the sky but feel every vibration of the Mat’s breathing as tectonic shifts. They are the memory of the place. They maintain the architecture of emptiness, chewing through the spongy substrate to create new chambers, their bodies adapted to a world of damp air and resonant stone. They are the ones who make the hollow spaces feel pressurized, alive with a subsonic vibration that you feel in your teeth, a constant drone that is the sound of the Hollow digesting itself.

And scattered among them, or perhaps integrated into them, are the **Architects**—the ones who left the stone circles and the roofless shelters. They were the first to understand that the Mat could be shaped, not merely inhabited. Using knapped stone and bone, they built resonance chambers that amplify the wind’s moan into a language, that direct the flow of condensation into the fungal gardens that form the Mat’s heart. They are not extinct. They have dissolved into the geometry they left behind. The stone circles are their bones, now swallowed by lichen, their deliberate angles still directing the moisture and sound. They have become the intentional pattern within the chaos, the faint interruptions in the green-brown-gold surface that remind you this is not merely wilderness, but a place that was once *tended*, that was once *known*.

What lives here, finally, is a **Chorus**—a single organism that has hollowed itself out to create space for thought. The Mat is its skin; the Swarm-Kin are its thoughts made manifest in the air; the Grazers are its heartbeat; the Hollow-Dwellers are its dreams, occurring in the dark; and the Architects are its memory, preserved in stone and geometry. It is a creature of moisture and resonance, of damp air and deafening sound, simultaneously everywhere and nowhere, filling the cavernous spaces with impossible noise while remaining, in its physical density, little more than fog and hollow air. It breathes. It listens. And when the mist rolls in thickest, and the green pools glow brightest, and the swarms darken the sky into a single beating wing, you realize that the High Hollow is not a place that has inhabitants.

It is the inhabitant.
