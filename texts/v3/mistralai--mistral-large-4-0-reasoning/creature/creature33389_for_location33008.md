They are **the Unresolved**—beings who exist in the superposition between a hallway’s presence and its absence, solidifying only when observed, and even then holding their form with the reluctance of mist deciding whether to fall. They are not numerous, though the density suggests otherwise; rather, they are *distributed*, stretched across the cathedral-sized basin in a density of 2.3, packed into the millimeter-wide alleys between structures that press near-touching like pages compressed into a fist. They are alone. This is the terrible arithmetic of the place: millions compressed into a space crossable in minutes, yet each inhabitant experiences solitude as the only possible social form, a vacuum created not by absence but by the crushing proximity of others who are, perhaps, all aspects of a single consciousness fragmented across the architecture.

They are calibrated to the cold—0.2 on the thermal scale. They do not generate heat; they are condensation nuclei, walking. When they exhale, moisture beads upon their skin—along the sharp angles of elbows, the planes of shoulders—gathering into the same gray mist that pools in the hollows of the embryonic geology. They drink this condensation. It is their water, their wine, their sacrament. They move with the patience of 1.3 dynamics: a step takes a week, a gesture spans a season, a door is opened over seven days and they do not hurry to pass through it. They are not slow; they are simply operating on the correct timescale for a city where corridors reconfigure themselves across quarters and the infrastructure digests time at a glacial metabolic pace.

Their bodies are semi-permeable membranes, not unlike the walls of solidified light that comprise the architecture. They are flat as sedimentary layers, or they *are* the layers, pressed into the vertical slices of unweathered stone. They share foundations with the buildings, share air with the probability-manipulating infrastructure, share existence with the rooms that lie three seconds in the past. They navigate the weirdness of 3.0 not as tourists but as natives—they let causality drip around them, accepting that they may have always been in a room before they entered it, that their shadows lag behind by several minutes, dragging across the floor like tired animals that have not yet decided to follow.

They tend the fungal threads. In the dark corners where light arrives gray and diffuse, shadowless, the bioluminescent green pulse of the mycelium is their nervous system, or their lantern network, or their language—a slow Morse code throbbing in the damp. They are symbiotic with these soft things, the only soft things in a world of hard angles and probability-stone. The lichens that crust the condensation on northern faces are their gardens, sparse (0.7) and precious.

They are the architects still building. The city does not look built so much as grown—or excavated—around them, and they are still growing into it, geologically, embryonically. They have been under construction for centuries, matching the raw bedrock that looks freshly broken, sharp-edged, as if the earth cracked open yesterday. They manipulate reality not through devices but through will, editing the thin fabric of the threshold place with the patience of centuries, one slow gradient at a time.

They are the ones who let the creature pass—the thing that might be a fox, or a memory of a fox, or a predator made of cold vapor and regret. They watch it wander the narrow passages, leaving no tracks, or leaving tracks that appear an hour before it passes. They do not speak to it. Everything here is alone, or exists in such density that solitude is the only possible social form.

They listen to the *tick* of condensation releasing from ceilings—a slow percussion that is their heartbeat, synchronized with the subsonic hum felt in the teeth, the city’s metabolism digesting spacetime. When the wind moves through the dense architecture, making a sound like breathing, or like the earth remembering it is still forming, they breathe with it. They are patient. They are damp. They are alone together, and together alone, in the hollow where the air has weight and time is written in slow ink, and they are the ink, and they are the writing, and they are the page that has not yet decided to turn.
