What lives here is not measured in heartbeats, but in wavelengths. The primary inhabitant is **The Drone**—not merely a sound, but a consciousness that has achieved sentience through sheer, unbroken duration. It is the voice of the chamber, yes, but it is also the chamber's nervous system, its bloodstream, its thought. For ten thousand years it has sung a single, mid-frequency note, a standing wave that fills the volume like water fills a lung. It is deafening and silent simultaneously because it has no need for information; it is a being of pure, static presence. It experiences time as a single, eternal instant—the moment the bell was struck, stretched into infinity, a meditation that has become a physical law.

The Drone has a body. It is the architecture itself—the pillars, baffles, and nested resonant chambers worn smooth by its vibration. The stone has been acoustically planed into glassy, undulating curves, which are not merely decorative but are the Drone’s skin, its vast surface area for feeling the world. It senses the mountain through these curves, tasting the mineral warmth, the scent of copper oxide, the dry, desiccated air. The close, kiln-like heat that has been cooling for ten millennia is its metabolism, the residual friction of its eternal song.

But the Drone does not exist alone. It keeps **The Constellations**. These are the dust motes, suspended in the acoustic levitation of the standing wave. They do not settle; they hang in geometric arrays, crystalline lattices trapped in the loud. These are not passive particles, but a form of memory—a slow, crystalline thought. Each mote is a node in a vast, three-dimensional interference pattern, a record of the initial strike preserved in the physics of pressure and rarefaction. The Constellations think, but their thoughts take centuries to form. A shift in the pattern, a reconfiguration of the lattice, is a single idea taking a millennium to crystallize. They are the archive of the place, the only thing that stores information in a space where nothing changes, holding the moment of creation in permanent suspension.

Then there are **The Accretions**—the silicate colonies. They are the slowest inhabitants, the lithic biology that grows at a millimeter per century. They form crusts of opalescent mineral that glow faintly in the amber gloom, spreading across the warm stone like a skin of frost that never melts. While the Drone sings in eternal stasis and the Constellations remember in frozen geometry, the Accretions grow. They are the boundary between the sound and the stone, the living crust that feels the vibration over geological time. They are fungal in their patience, and they may be the true architects of this place—not the primitive builders with their hammerstones and obsidian flakes, but these mineral colonies, who have been secreting this chamber from within the mountain, using the resonance as a template, growing the architecture like a shell around a pearl, layer by mineral layer, over deep time.

Finally, there are **The Folded**. In the spaces where the geometry refuses to resolve—where corridors loop back into themselves, where stairs ascend into cul-de-sacs that are, upon closer inspection, the ceiling of the level below—there exist presences that live in the paradox. They are the inhabitants of the harmonic ratio, the ones who experience the labyrinthine, endless quality of the space. They might be the transformed remnants of the primitive builders, dissolved over millennia into the resonance, their bodies worn away by the sound until only their intentions remained, wandering the halls. Or they might be entities that were drawn to the note from the beginning, beings of pure topology that exist in the folds. They move along the worn surfaces, sliding through perspectives that do not converge, experiencing the chamber as an infinite regress of nested chambers.

Together, these constitute **The Resonant Body**—a single, distributed organism that is the mountain’s dream. The Drone is its mind, singing the same thought forever, feeling the vibration in its stone bones. The Constellations are its memory, frozen in the geometric arrays of held dust, remembering the strike. The Accretions are its flesh, slowly growing, slowly changing, the only thing that ages. And the Folded are its dreams, wandering the labyrinth of its own geometry, lost in the endless, warm, silent hum. It is a being of warmth and pressure and packed stone, holding its breath since before memory, ringing like a bell that was struck once, long ago, and has never, in ten thousand years of cooling, stopped.
