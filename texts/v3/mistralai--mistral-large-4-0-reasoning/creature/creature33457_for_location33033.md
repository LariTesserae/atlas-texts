They are the ones who never left.

Not the lizards—though they are there, traversing their twelve-tile circuits with the patience of geological time compressed into a single, endless step, reversing and retracing and reversing again, forever, without hunger, without purpose, without progression. Not the dragonflies, hovering with wings that beat in a rhythm that does not match the silence, their buzz too loud for their size, occurring in a frequency that makes your vision strobe slightly at the edges (reality fraying 2.6). Not the herons with their too-many-jointed wings, perching on pillars that contain themselves fractally, again and again, down to the vanishing point of stone, breathing wet cloth or clicking from inside your own skull.

They are the Builders.

You find them in the freshness of the chips. In the obsidian scrapers that still flake the terracotta, though no arm moves, no hand holds them. They are distributed across the mosaic, each one locked in the moment of a strike, forever striking, forever withdrawing, forever beginning. They are human-shaped only in the negative space of their intention—the scale of the work is regional (1.5), but their technology is primitive (0.9), and between those two numbers lives a kind of godhood, or a madness. They built the recursive pillars with bone awls and stone flakes, yet the fractals descend to the atomic, to the crystalline structure of the made-earth itself, carving infinities that their tools could not measure. They are the reason the edges stay knife-sharp (young 0.7, eternal 0.4), the reason the mortar has hardened into something glassy and time-like, the reason the substrate weeps water from its artificial pores. You hear them in the scrape of stone against stone, a sound that occurs in the space between your heartbeats, a perpetual genesis that never achieves completion because completion is a direction time does not travel here.

But they are not alone.

The fauna move in loops because they are attempting to navigate the Builders' geometry, or perhaps because they *are* the geometry, living proofs of the place's wrong physics. The lizards (abundance 2.2, presence 1.3) bask on the terracotta, their scales gleaming with a wetness drawn from the condensing air, casting no shadows, or casting three shadows that point in different directions toward sources of light that do not exist in the shadowless white-gold sky (3.0). They are isolated in bubbles of motion, separated by vast empty spaces of gleaming tile, each creature a solipsist trapped in its own circuit, abundant yet sparse in the way that stars are abundant yet sparse across the dark.

The dragonflies hover in the hot, saturated air (2.6), suspended in a state of perpetual takeoff that never achieves altitude. They do not land. They do not mate. They exist only in the vertical, in the shimmer between the constructed eaves and the flat horizon, their wings beating against a silence so total that the vibration becomes visible as a strobing at the periphery of sight.

The birds—herons, ibises, creatures with too many joints in their wings—perch on the pillars and watch. They do not sing. Instead, they breathe, a sound like wet cloth being pulled apart, or they emit clicks that resonate in your cranium, suggesting they are not communicating with each other but with the hum that emanates from the ground itself (sound 0.6, but felt in the teeth). They gaze at the pools below, at the warm, stagnant water that mirrors not this white world but a green one, a forested world of root and water that does not exist above. Perhaps they are the guardians of that reflection, or the exiles from it.

The vegetation is alive (2.5) but frozen. The ferns uncurl from the mortar lines in perfect logarithmic spirals, their fronds broad and waxy, motionless, existing at the exact moment of their flowering, never to wilt, never to seed. Vines of impossible thickness drape over the stone, their leaves eternal, waxy, unmoving. Moss carpets the north faces in patterns that resemble circuitry or neural tissue, suggesting that the plants are not merely growing but *computing*, or remembering, or dreaming the geometry of the cracks into being. They do not sway in the still air. They do not photosynthesize in any way you recognize—or if they do, it is in a closed loop that returns the same oxygen it consumed, a zero-entropy respiration that keeps the verdance (2.5) locked in perpetual, aggressive stillness. The soil, if it is soil, is suspect—crushed vegetation compressed and fired into brick, or perhaps no soil at all, the roots gripping the geometry of the fissures, drawing moisture directly from the condensing atmosphere.

And beneath the surface, in the shallow depressions of the paving, live the Reflections.

The water gathers in deliberate hollows, warm and mirror-bright, reflecting a world of green—a forested world, a world of water and root that does not exist in the flat, made landscape above. The inhabitants of that green world are visible only as distortions, as the inverse of this place, as the bruise pressing against the sky. They might be the true residents, with the surface world being the reflection, the dream, the artifact. The water weeps from the rocks themselves—the artificial rocks, the built rocks, the terracotta and vitrified clay—suggesting that even the substrate is sweating out the memory of a natural world, pressing it into the pools where it stagnates, mirror-perfect. The Reflections do not move. They hold their breath in the depths, waiting for the surface to crack, for the geometry to fray (2.6) enough to let them rise into the shadowless light.

Finally, there is the one who dreams the dream.

The place is a dream that has forgotten it is dreaming, unraveling quietly at the seams. The dreamer lives in the second sky—the darker sky with different stars that presses against the first like a bruise, visible only when you look at the corner of your eye. They are the ultimate inhabitant, the consciousness that holds this world in perfect, frozen equilibrium (stability 0.4), the zero-entropy garden built by hands that worked with obsidian and bone yet somehow finished a task that should have taken millennia in the span of a single, unchanging afternoon. They are the reason the geometry is wrong, the reason you walk toward a pillar and arrive after seven steps, or seventy, unable to reconcile the count. They are the one who stands in the open space between two distant platforms, where the light is total and the air is wet and hot and still, watching themselves watch, forever paused at the moment of awakening that will never come, holding the lizards in their loops, the ferns in their spirals, the Builders in their eternal scrape, and the Reflections in their green, waiting depths—all of them, and none of them, alive in a world where time does not pass (0.4), where the hum resonates in your teeth, where the dragonflies strobe the edges of your vision, and where the dream continues, perfect and fraying, eternal and fresh.
