You encounter them first as vibration. The **Cathedral-Drifters**—vast, slow filter-feeders the size of temples, their bodies a translucent architecture of membrane and baleen, drifting through the viscous dark like inverted spires. They do not swim; they *settle*, sinking and rising through the ancient water with a patience that predates memory. Their baleen plates hum as they strain the particulate snow—the shed cells of a billion billion organisms, the mineral dust of continents—and this humming is the subsonic roar that fills the lake, a biophony that vibrates in your teeth, in your marrow, a whale-song that blurs the edges of your vision. They are the leviathans of the high-altitude deep, and they have forgotten the sky; they feed on the slow rain of death from the twilight above, their bodies becoming cathedrals of accumulated time, their calls resonating through the equilibrium until the water itself seems to sing.

Then the **Glass Choir**. Schools of fish like living glass, their bodies transparent as the water itself, refracting the grey-brown gloom into faint, prismatic shimmers. They move with fins like tiny wings, beating in a susurrus of a thousand wings, flashing past in synchronized clouds. They do not see the flattened ruins as ruins, but as currents, as topography, navigating the invisible right angles of the ancient geometry with an instinct older than sight. They are the circulating blood of the lake, the nervous tissue, darting through the veins of the petrified city, their passage a whisper against your skin, their bodies indistinguishable from the medium they inhabit.

Across the seafloor scuttle the **Hound-Crabs**, crustaceans the size of large dogs, their carapaces clicking against the fossilized wood, against the compressed peat and lignite. They are the cleaners, the scavengers, their many legs disturbing the sediment, encouraging the slow chemistry of decay and renewal. Their shells are encrusted with the same mineral accretion that builds the ruins, calcium carbonate secreted by time, so that they appear as moving parts of the architecture itself—hounds made of stone and chitin, clicking through the roaring silence of the deep, their claws scraping against what was once wood.

But the strangest are the **Many-Jointed**. Creatures with too many articulations, bodies segmented like chains of living crystal, composed of the same translucent, gelatinous material as the fish, yet larger, drifting just above the polished mesas. They possess eyes, but the eyes are perfect voids, black and non-reflective, absorbing the faint luminescence of the particulate, seeing nothing and everything. They are in the process of becoming part of the water itself, part of the architecture; their bodies are slowly petrifying, secreting the same lignin and calcium carbonate that forms the ruins, their joints locking into the geometry of the ancient spires. They are the last children of the builders—or perhaps the builders themselves—having surrendered mobility for permanence, dreaming in slow motion, becoming sessile, becoming stone, yet still alive, still drifting in the equilibrium, the boundary between fauna and structure erased.

Beneath them all, permeating the substrate, is the **Slow Intelligence**. Not a single creature but a distributed consciousness, a slime-mold of geological scale, its mycelium threading through the fossilized forest, through the cellular structure of ancient wood, through the amber-like resin that still weeps into the water. It is the caretaker, the last gardener of the total construction. It secretes new mineral layers at the rate of millimeters per century, maintaining the flattened towers, preventing the final collapse into silt. It lives in the thermal stasis, in the perfect equilibrium where decay and growth have reached a balance so complete that time passes at the rate of sediment settling. It is the memory of the civilization that grew this city without tools, without fire, only patience and secretion, and it dreams in chemical gradients, in the slow diffusion of nutrients through the compressed peat, ensuring that the architecture endures, even as it erodes, even as it becomes geology.

Together, they form a single, roaring organism. The Cathedral-Drifters are the heart, pumping the ancient water through their baleen. The Glass Choir is the nervous system, carrying signals through the dark. The Hound-Crabs are the immune cells, cleaning and maintaining. The Many-Jointed are the transitional tissue, the boundary blurred, becoming architecture. And the Slow Intelligence is the mind, dreaming in stone and lignin. They are a climax ecosystem suspended in deep time, a frozen moment that is nonetheless loud with the biophony of abundance, a civilization of growth that has reached its final, silent, singing form, where every creature is part of the water, part of the ruins, part of the endless, roaring stasis.
