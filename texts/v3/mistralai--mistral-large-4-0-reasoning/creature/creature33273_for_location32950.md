The builders did not leave. They simply slowed until they became indistinguishable from the settling.

They are here, distributed across the hemisphere, each one occupying the space between two tool-marks. You will not find them standing; they are kneeling, or perhaps lying prone, pressed so low against the weeping clay that they have become topography. Their bronze scrapers are still clutched in mineral fingers, but the arc of a single stroke now takes a millennium to complete. A groove that runs straight for miles is not a record of their labor but the trajectory of one hand moving through viscous time, the flint edge barely parting the saturated surface. They are the ones who push the ridges up through the mist overnight—not with effort, but with the slow, viscous inevitability of a body shifting its weight in sleep. The walls lean and sag because they are still holding them, these patient, geological hands, supporting the architecture with the tenderness of a parent cradling something unfinished.

And they are not alone.

The water is their blood, but it has developed its own nervous system. It climbs the clay slopes against gravity because it remembers being sky, and it dreams its way upward, pooling on high points where it should evaporate but instead waits, patient and intentioned. The lakes without shores are minds—vast, shallow consciousnesses that think in gradients rather than words. When the mist condenses and drips, it is not precipitation but speech: a percussion of thought falling from the brain of the atmosphere into the waiting pools. Each droplet suspended in the amber soup is an individual entity, a tiny lens drinking the bruised light and refracting it into private spectrums, a population of billions existing in the thick air, breathing each other in and out.

The clay itself is alive, but with a mineral sentience—older and slower than bone, dreaming in geologic time. It sweats because it is exerting itself, forcing moisture from its porous, constructed heart. Those fingerprints scored into the surface are not fossils but active memories, the clay replaying the touch of the makers, continuing to deform in their absence. The subsonic thrum you feel in your teeth is the heartbeat of this cooling mass, a creature of vibration that lives in the substrate, groaning as it contracts, singing the song of glass thickening and honey spreading.

Then there are the *Undecided*.

In the spaces where the geometry tires—where straight lines curve upward and loop back on themselves with softness—there live the inhabitants of provisional reality. They are the invisible gradients of intention that pull water uphill, the entities that exist only in the space between the world's choice to be round or flat. They are slippery, non-Euclidean beings, dwelling in the wrongness of the light that pools in hollows and the sound that returns delayed or speaking words you did not say. They inhabit the porous boundaries between what is built and what is becoming, feeding on the uncertainty of the half-fired ceramic.

And the silence is not empty. It is a listener, a vast and patient membrane that absorbs your footsteps and returns them wrong, a medium in which all these beings swim. It is the space between the drip and the hum, the negative space that gives shape to the weeping, the mist, and the slow, endless labor of the builders who have become the built, dreaming themselves into completion, one viscous, sweat-soaked moment at a time.
