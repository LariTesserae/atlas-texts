The power that noticed the hollow was older than the limestone and slower than the peat. It had no name that would survive translation into any tongue still spoken above the shaft, and it did not need one. It arrived the way condensation arrives: without announcement, already present on every underside.

It did not enter by the stairs. It simply was, for a time that the beam-engine measured in a few unhurried strokes. It tasted the air (chlorophyll cooked with iron, the faint sweetness of crushed stem, the mineral patience of weeping stone). It felt the absence that had become a presence: no chitin, no feather, no worm-cast, only the closed circuit of plant and human and heat. It listened to the single layered sound that was the hollow’s voice, and it understood at once that nothing here was unfinished.

A lesser kindness would have wanted to help. It would have sent insects back into the peat, or a breeze that was actually weather, or a belt that never needed dressing, or children whose lungs did not rasp. It would have opened the canopy a fraction so that real coins of sun could linger longer than an hour. It would have called it mercy.

This power was not lesser. It saw that the equilibrium was not a wound. The people were not trapped; they were the hollow’s own blood, circulating on the long count, staining and being stained, pruning as they walked, grafting the water-main when it wept too freely. The plants were not imprisoned; they had simply occupied every interval until occupation itself became the architecture. The engine’s stroke was the same stroke the grandparents had known because that stroke was already perfect. To change any of it would have been to introduce a kind of vandalism dressed as benevolence.

So the power did the only thing wisdom allowed.

It became, for a little while, another layer of the wet. It thickened the green rumor of daylight by a degree too fine for any eye to name. It made the film of water on the walkways a fraction more even, so that no one slipped who would not have slipped anyway. It let one cutting in a hundred take a day sooner, and one graft hold a season longer, and the difference was so slight it vanished into the ordinary luck of the place. In the sleeping niches it slowed the ticking of swelling cells just enough that a sleeper might turn once instead of twice before morning. It did not stop the engine. It did not bank the fire. It did not speak.

Then it withdrew the way warmth withdraws from stone after the last lamp is lit: completely, and without remainder.

The hollow continued. The stone wept. The belts were dressed. The vaults were bound. Goods went up the fused-trunk shaft; coal and salt and already-soft paper came down. Evening was only a further dimming. Nothing tried to become anything else.

High above, in the weather that still happened, the power turned its attention elsewhere. Some gardens require the gardener’s hand. This one had become the gardener, and the garden, and the rain that was only breath. It needed nothing more.

That was the whole of what was done, and it was enough.
