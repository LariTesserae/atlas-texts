The power did not arrive as light. Light would have been an insult, a tearing of the dusk the highland had spent uncounted seasons learning how to keep. It did not arrive as silence either; the roar would have closed over any such wound the way a thousand wings close over a swept arm. It came instead as a deepening of what was already there: a warmth that was the closed-mouth warmth, a moisture that occupied rather than ran, a patience that settled another finger’s width into the made hillsides and asked for nothing in return.

It listened.

It felt, through the salivary brick and the weather-welded midden, the hexagonal ghosts and the radial lodges that had become geology. It tasted the faint sour of risen dough on wet faces, the library-sweet of compacted leaf, the polish bone takes when it has been walked on long enough to forget it was ever bone. It moved through the galleries the way the mist already moved, through the seeps that were once brood rooms, past the swollen centers of production that sat in dark pockets without sentiment or name. It let the frogs use its passage as another hanging cup, another throat. It let the things with too many legs treat it as a ridge that would not last. It let the smaller mammals rearrange around it as they would around any local thickening.

It understood, without translation, the only law that had ever applied here.

A lesser attention might have mistaken the density for crowding, the roar for clamor, the absence of unused ground for poverty. It might have offered a clearing, a corridor of real light, a tool, a word, a future in which the highland could become something it had never needed to be. This power, being wise, saw that occupation and structure had long ago ceased to be two facts. The being that lived here was not housed in the place; the being was the place continuing to house itself, generation after generation of chewed wood and silk hawser and dropped bone welded into terrain that still breathed a little under collective weight. Individuals blurred, died, were incorporated. The highland itself remained patient. It had stopped arguing with what it was.

So the power did the one thing that would not become remainder.

It withdrew the attention the way condensation withdraws after the canopy has taken the rain personally: completely, leaving every surface as wet, as loud, as dark, as warm as it had been. No veil was drawn, because a veil would have been a new wall the builders would have had to occupy. No blessing was spoken, because speech here was already pantomime and the roar had no room for an extra harmonic. No protection was woven against some future dryness or impact or thinning of the air, because the country had never required an outside to complete it and would meet whatever came with the same adhesiveness, the same unhurried accumulation.

If anything at all remained, it was only this: that the highland continued, fold after fold, each one roofed and leaking and occupied past distinction, high up where the air should have thinned and had not. The power, elsewhere now, or nowhere, or simply no longer looking, carried the memory the way the place itself carried memory—only as texture, only as the faint press of something that had once been a chamber and was now a hillside.

The ticks and weeps after rain went on. The dust still lifted when a thousand wings decided at once. Nothing had been finished. Nothing had been abandoned. The made country kept its long truce with its own making, and the being who was that country did not notice the difference, because there had never been one.
