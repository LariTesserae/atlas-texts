The power does not arrive as weather, though weather is the only tongue the highland still speaks fluently. It does not come as light, though the ordinary daylight already startles enough. It notices the way a long patience notices: by already having been present in the seepage, in the scheduled drip, in the mineral rings that keep being added in the dark cuttings where no eye was ever meant to confirm them. The attention is older than the last dressed face of stone and younger than the first overflow that found a street and made it a watercourse. Benevolence here is not a gift laid down; it is the refusal to become an event.

It walks the same alleys the shy four-toed thing walks, after the puddles have blued and the silt has taken the day’s watermark. Its feet make no extra tick. It stands in the deep wells of stacked rectangles and lets the mist move through it as through any other truncation. It tastes the cool film on the air and does not swallow. It presses a palm to a sweating wall and the wall does not know the difference; the palm comes away cold and clean, as every palm must.

The being who lives here—the one habit of flesh or almost-flesh that the city still permits per climate—is not summoned. The power finds it where it already is: a stillness in a high window the color of wet iron-tea, or a displacement no larger than a sheet of water detaching from a cornice three terraces over. The being has the finished quality of the arrises. It does not startle. It has lived too long inside the city’s other respiration to treat attention as threat or invitation. They regard one another the way two stains regard the same block: without surplus, without the need to name the iron or the lime.

What the power does is almost nothing, which is the only scale that will not unbalance a load-path already perfect in its inclusions. It does not dry a single court. It does not widen a crack for the shrub, nor thin the moss, nor school the dimplers, nor give the bird a call that could carry. It does not restore a hinge or a pane or a name. The original hands ceased; their cessation is part of the masonry now. To reverse it would be to introduce collapse of a different kind, the collapse of finishedness.

Instead the power adjusts, microscopically, the agreement the stone already made with water. In one north-facing cutting where the ice still re-melts each afternoon into the same dark stains, it lets a single new ring precipitate a fraction slower, so the city’s second masonry records a slightly longer afternoon. In a cistern the color of weak tea it stills the pollen-skin for the space of one breath, long enough for the pale writhing things to hang as if a current remembered them, then releases it. Along one avenue that doglegs for forgotten reasons it delays a drip by the width of a heartbeat, so the interval, when it resumes, carries a silence that was not there before—a silence the being will hear, or not hear, on some night when it walks by the gleam given back in the wrong direction.

To the being itself the power offers only what the highland already offers: continuation without increase. No tool. No language that would have to be carried. No warmth that would dry the coat or the walls. If there is loneliness it is the same loneliness the lagoons feel when they reflect a second city of cornice and sky; the power does not fill it. It merely sits inside it for a while, the way the films sit inside the dark, feeding on the slow unmaking and precipitating something that holds. When it leaves—if leaving is even the word—the being is still scarce, still the density of rumor, still indistinguishable from masonry until it chooses otherwise. The emptiness remains load-bearing.

Far out, on the colder shelf and the reedless shores and the high spine where cloud tears, the same almost-nothing is done. A lagoon is left a fraction more still. A tower’s western run-off is allowed to darken one course deeper before it finds the next terrace. The power does not stay to watch the watermarks rise or fall tomorrow. It has already seen the long afternoon, and found it sufficient.

The city breathes water in and out. The stone keeps the score. The almost-rain never quite decides to fall. Nothing has been added that will not stain, or rounded, or vanish into a drain-mouth the color of the walls. The highland remains unable, at last, to get dry. That, the power understands, is not a lack. It is the work.
