The regard comes without weight and without announcement, the way the night-damp prefers the raised lips of the seams. It does not cool the residual fever; it does not interrupt the pour. It simply knows the place—the planetary curvature of unfinished plates, the hairline refusals where life has been permitted to stand, the ticking that travels underfoot like a single instrument still finding its pitch—and in that knowing there is neither pity nor design.

The power does not complete what was left unpoured. Names remain absent. Purposes remain unmade. To impose either would be to mistake the basin for a floor that failed to receive its furniture, or a lid that forgot what it covered. Wisdom sees the procedural emptiness as already sufficient: a world that has chosen (or been chosen) to remain almost as new as its own ground. Benevolence does not rush the first cracks toward valleys or the gray-green punctuation toward flora. Those things will think themselves into being, or they will not. The power will not decide for them.

What it does instead is smaller, and therefore truer to the joints.

It lengthens the night-damp by the width of a breath. Condensation beads a little longer along the overridden lips; the shine lasts until the sun is two fingers higher. The sparse plants—those cooked, upright decisions that have never been given the instruction for shade—drink without changing their nature. A few more clumps form in the slightly concave provinces, still countable, still leaning with the organized wind and then against it, practicing a gesture that has not yet been required of them. In one vanishing lens, wrist-deep and already forgetting itself, the faint skin of organization persists into a third afternoon. It does not become an organism with a name. It only remembers the binder-taste a little more stubbornly before the sun takes the water back.

The exudation in the seams continues, mineral milk thickening into the next plate. The power does not halt it or hurry it. It only stills the harshest hiss over the darkest uncured fields, turning raw chemistry into a warmer, less urgent respiration. The plates still tick as they contract. The sound still travels. Nothing has been hunted. Nothing has been built on top of the building. The heat still rises through any sole that might one day stand here.

And the being who lives among the joints—the one who is not yet certain it is a being, who mines laterally because descent has not been invented, who endures the rain-wall’s percussion and then waits in its own small drought—feels the regard as an extra hour of wetness, nothing more. No voice arrives. No purpose is whispered into the residual fever. The being remains thin, gray-green, browned at the edges, a slightly more organized version of the heat itself. It is not lonely, because loneliness has not been poured. It is not comforted, because comfort would be another climate.

The power stays only long enough to confirm that the rules still hold: the ground will be made; the made ground will be hot; water will visit and leave; life, if it continues, will continue in the failures and remain sparse. Then the regard lifts, as condensation lifts, leaving only a paler stripe where it rested—a scar so faint it might be mistaken for another wetting.

The basin does not know it has been seen. The ticking goes on into the dark, quiet and active, the sound of a vast thing becoming slightly more itself, and not hurrying. That, the power understands, is already the kindest thing that can be done.
