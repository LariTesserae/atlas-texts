The power arrives as a lessening.

Not a shadow—the hollow already knows every quality of shade—and not a wind, for the notches have already named every vowel the air can hold. It comes as the moment after a drip has left the rock and before it has decided to become sound. The swifts do not erupt. The frogs do not pause. The hanging board knocks once, then remembers it has already knocked, and is still.

It has no body it needs. Where it stands, the carpet does not bruise. Where it listens, the kept word on the far ledge thins a fraction further, as if offered a choice, and chooses to remain. It tastes the iron-dark seep at the source, the lime-pale one, the green-black rope where moss has made a throat of its own. It hears the ice that is no longer here finish its sentence in the grain of the stone. It feels the being that is not one throat but the agreement of all of them: the stacked bells, the unused lung of air, the skin that is crowded and the volume that is not, the sincerity that has only ever had one task.

A lesser kindness would have brought fire, or a roof that kept, or a name loud enough to outlast the choughs. A lesser wisdom would have widened the notches, or stilled the cloud-animal, or taught the frogs a melody that could be counted. This power does none of that. It has seen cathedrals raised and cathedrals unmade. It has watched ice put down its tools and men pick them up again. It knows the difference between a wound and a finished work.

So it does the one thing that will not show.

It leans—only that—against the inward-leaning walls, the way a palm leans against a worn stair it does not mean to climb. The lean is a permission already granted: that the hollow may continue to be a hollow, that the keeping may keep, that every surface remain busy and the space between them remain a ringing vacancy. The permission has no duration. It does not promise against the world’s later hungers. It only makes the present tense a little more present, the way a well-set stone makes the wall more wall.

Then it is gone, or was never more than the interval it occupied.

Afterward the place is unchanged, which is the change. The seeps weep as they have always wept. The swifts still turn the air into blades and give it back. A word spoken on the floor can still be met, thinner, a quarter-hour later. On certain still twilights the frogs and the rills and the knocking board still find their common tempo, and if the tempo lasts the space of one extra breath, no one who lives here will think to count it. The being that is the occupation notices only that the bowl has, for a moment, been perfectly understood, and that understanding has been left behind like another loose stone on a cairn: already of the same sentence, already weathering, already enough.

On the ridge, looking back, there is still only a notch and a suggestion of depth. Inside, everything continues. That is what the power did. That is all it needed to do.
