The power that noticed the basin had no need of names either. It had watched continents decide themselves and stars forget how to burn. It came as a thickening in the already-thick air, a patience older than the vents.

It did not finish the sentence.

It sat with the unfinished ground for a span of seasons measured only in shrugs. It felt the people jack their floors in the dark, heard the three-day ringing in children’s ears, watched the fox-things accept cooling slag as though the arrangement had always been. It tasted the copper moss and the iron dust the trees offered the canals. Nothing here asked to be saved. Everything had already made its contracts.

So the power made one more, small enough to vanish into the existing ones.

It taught the newest stone a quieter memory. For three days the rock still spoke, but the speech no longer froze fingers or set a bell ringing behind the eyes. Instead it left a faint, mineral warmth, like a hand that has just let go. Children who touched too soon came away humming under their breath, a tune no one had taught them, and the adults pretended not to notice the change in the old rule.

It lengthened the glow of the copper moss by the width of a held breath, a frequency the night crews already knew how to read. The extra light did not point anywhere. It simply stayed a moment longer on the edges of gantries and the rims of temporary tarns, a courtesy.

It let one canal keep its course through an entire extra cycle of rising stone, long enough for the market to remain in the same plaza two weeks running. The people remarked on the luck and recut nothing.

Then the power withdrew the greater part of itself, leaving only a residual kindness in the mist: on certain mornings the vapor carried a scent no local vent had ever produced, something dry and far, like sun-warmed granite from a finished range. The inhabitants breathed it without comment and went on threading the alleys.

The basin continued writing itself. The people continued living inside the writing. The power, having done almost nothing, considered the almost-nothing sufficient, and turned its attention elsewhere, though a thin attention remained, folded into the damp the way moss folds into brick.

That is all.
