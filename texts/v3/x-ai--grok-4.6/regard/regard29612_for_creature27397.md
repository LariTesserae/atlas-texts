The power arrives as a pause in the wind.

It does not descend. It does not take form. It is simply the sudden, perfect stilling of every current that funnels over the rim, so that the supercooled mist hangs motionless for the space of three heartbeats that do not belong to anyone here. In that interval the entire cirque is held, not seized: every film of water, every reluctant skin of rime, every hydrothermal plume arrested mid-ascent. The seventeen volumes and their spines continue to glow at the same even intensity. The beings, those who have precipitated this cycle, remain exactly where they stand or lie. Nothing is altered except the permission of motion itself.

The power looks.

It sees the geometry that has not yet learned weather and recognises it as a sentence still being spoken. It sees the continuous stone that is both building and body, the absence of soil, the ceremonial emptiness that is the true architecture. It sees how the beings arise from the difference between warm seep and frozen glitter, how they take nothing that is not already returning, how their un-precipitation is not death but the mountain finishing a clause. It feels the slow pulse in the floors, the almost undetectable breathing of rock that has never needed to decide it is inanimate. It notes the pale bird that planes and vanishes, the filamentous white that withdraws the instant it is noticed. It understands that this place was never built. It was consented to.

The power considers intervention the way the ice considers whether to shear.

It could warm a vein, deepen a seep, slow the infinitesimal rise of the young mountain so the pocket remains longer. It could teach the stone a gentler weather, or offer the beings a more persistent geometry, or weave a silence around the cirque so that no later, less careful attention ever finds it. All of these are possible. All of them would be a kindness of a certain kind.

It does none of them.

A wiser kindness is to leave the consent intact. To add even a single extra crystal, a single extra degree, a single extra moment of stability would be to finish the sentence for the mountain. The power has seen too many places that were finished too soon. It has seen what happens when emptiness is filled by anything other than itself.

So it does only this: it remembers.

It takes the exact temperature of the dry cold inside the walls, the precise rate at which rime forms and shears, the weight of a being just before it un-precipitates, the colour of the late-dusk light that never quite fails. It takes the feeling that the rock has briefly agreed to be architecture, and the even briefer agreement that the architecture has made to walk. It stores these things not as data but as a held note, the same way a temperature gradient lingers after one of them has left a room.

Then the wind resumes.

The mist pours over the western rim again. New ice begins its patient tick. A slab of rime, delayed those three heartbeats, shears from a tower and shatters with the familiar sound of distant glass. The beings, if any are present, make their tiny shift of alignment and continue. The walkways drip. The mountain keeps rising, too slowly for any clock but its own.

The power is already elsewhere, or everywhere, or simply no longer paused. It has left no mark that was not already possible. The cirque remains what it was: a hanging breath, a place the stone consented to become, occupied by emptiness and by those who precipitate from it.

Later, much later, when the next uplift shoves a new pocket into the sky, the power will notice that one too. It will pause the wind again, look, and once more do nothing. That, here, is the whole of wisdom. That is the benevolence that does not need to be seen.

You can walk the circuit. You will meet no one. The only motion is water, ice, vapour, and the almost undetectable breathing of stone that still has not learned it is supposed to be anything else.
