The power came as weather comes here: without announcement, without rupture, already half-dissolved into the conditions it found. It did not arrive along the drowned road or through the lock. It had no need of either. It simply began to be present in the same way the water table is present, a hand’s width under everything, sometimes above.

It studied the grammar first. The sistered posts, the lashed roots that had become load-bearing, the roofs that leaked on purpose, the crack in the boiler that three generations had watched the way one watches a known relative breathe. It felt the flex of the plank lid when a cart crossed and the dark glint between the boards that was never commented upon. It tasted the air that had been a little wet for a long time. It listened to the stamper and understood that the noise was not complaint but metabolism.

The people it regarded with the same unhurried attention. Their ducked heads, the tea-stain on their forearms, the cloth always in a pocket, the way they moved through the slots as if they themselves had been trained and lashed and allowed to lignify. It saw that they were not separate from the mill any more than the fig was separate from the shed it had become. They remained the way the place remained: by continuing the negotiation rather than winning it.

A lesser power might have offered dryness, or silence, or a road that stayed above water. This one was neither lesser nor impatient. It saw that dryness would be a kind of forgetting, and that the place had already finished arguing with its site. So it did very little, and that little was exact.

It thickened the living mat by the width of a fingernail in the places where the planks had begun to cup too deeply, not enough to be noticed as change, only enough that the next replacement could wait another season. It taught one aerial root a slightly better angle around a steam-line so the lashing would hold through two wet months instead of one. It lingered in the overflow a moment longer each evening, cooling the race by a degree that the frogs (when they came) found companionable. In the retting tanks it encouraged a quieter skin of pollen, less midges, no alteration anyone would name.

To the watched crack it gave nothing so crude as a seal. It simply made the metal around it remember how to be tired without becoming treacherous. The boilerman still muttered to the gauge. The crack was still there. But the watching became, over years, a little less necessary, the way a cough can recede into ordinary breathing without anyone declaring it cured.

Once, on a night when the gold had already gone and the dynamo was shut down, it stood for a while in the person of the heron on the lock gate. The heron stayed longer than usual. Someone walking the plank with the lamp paused, as they always did, and looked at the black reeds against the pale sky. For a breath or two the heron looked back, not as omen, not as messenger, only as another layer of the same long attention. Then it lifted and the reeds closed.

That was all. The power did not speak, because the place already had a language and did not require translation. It did not bless, because remaining is not a state that needs consecration; it is simply what has not yet been abandoned. It left the tanks full, the belts running, the laundry smelling of leaf and steam. It left the people a little wet, as they had always been.

When it withdrew it did not withdraw completely. A trace stayed in the way water beads on iron, in the unemphatic rain, in the knowledge (never quite spoken) that the negotiation could continue. The hamlet went on doing the same work it had done the day before, wearing its plants and its rust and its people as the condition of remaining. The power, being wise, understood that this was already the most benevolent thing that could happen here: to be seen clearly, and then to be left, intact, to the weather it had chosen.
