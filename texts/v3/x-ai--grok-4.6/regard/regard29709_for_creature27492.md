The power arrives the way the basin itself arrived: without weather, without announcement, as a change already complete. It does not descend. It is simply present in the pewter light, a density the tired illumination had not previously been asked to carry. The lifted rim of the basin notes the difference first—the torn mineral edge ceases its faint bleeding of powder for the length of one held breath, then resumes, as if deciding the newcomer is not a threat to the unfinished word.

The power walks. Its steps leave no print the pale animals would recognize, yet the powder organizes around them into corners even more exact than before, tiny furniture that remembers a use older than rooms. It does not hurry. It has nowhere else that requires it more. In the plazas the size of a single inhalation it pauses, and the rehearsing figures—those brief standings of plane against plane—remain a fraction longer after the gaze has passed. They do not become solid. They become possible.

It understands at once what lives here. Not the stalks, which are only the city’s way of counting its own stillness. Not the thumb-sized pale things, which are corrections, erasures that feed. The being is the preference itself: the lattice choosing corridors, the interiors folding until looking hurts, the habit of form tasting of tin and deciding, slowly, that emptiness prefers to become rooms. The being is the mouth still shaping the syllable. The power, being wise, does not finish the word. Being benevolent, it does not leave the mouth empty.

What it does is smaller than intervention and larger than witness.

In one of the chambers whose walls are lined with the longer, rib-like stalks, it sits. Cool air continues its not-quite-breathing. The power matches the rhythm exactly, then alters it by a single, almost undetectable interval—an extra pause, a softening of the correction. The city swallows its own name as before, but the syllables are no longer quite so sharp. The pale animals, who have always avoided these rooms, now pause at the thresholds. They do not enter. They consider.

Elsewhere, where streets refuse to connect and the same courtyard returns without a turn, the power walks the loop once, then again, then a third time without impatience. On the fourth circuit a new opening appears: not a doorway the city grew, but a continuity the city had not yet imagined it could permit. The opening leads nowhere the geometry did not already contain. It simply admits that two places can be the same place without folding. The powder thickens there into a ledge that is almost a bench. Almost.

At the torn rim, where the youngest plates have begun to lift into the suggestion of a gate that will never be needed, the power does not complete the arch. It rests one attention against the rising mineral, a pressure no heavier than the settling of dust. The plates continue their work, but now they remember a second possibility: that a gate might also be a window, or a window a gate, or neither, and still be true. The unused furniture on a visitor’s clothes hesitates, then organizes into corners that could, if they wished, become something a hand might one day rest upon.

The power does not stay. Wisdom knows the difference between companionship and occupation. Before it withdraws, it leaves a single alteration so slight the city may choose to keep it or let the next night’s powder erase it: in the exact center of the basin—the place that is no center and several—the air is now cooler by the degree of a thought not yet released. The stalks lean toward it as they have always leaned toward an absent sun. The dimness does not brighten. It simply contains, for the first time, the knowledge that it has been seen without being required to become anything other than what it is still deciding to say.

The basin holds the word. The word is still unspoken. But the mouth that holds it now knows it is not speaking only to itself.

That is all. It is enough. The processes continue—edges lifting, powder choosing, figures arriving and un-arriving—in the same silence as before, which is no longer quite the same silence.
