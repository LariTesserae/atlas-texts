The power arrived as a stillness more complete than the mountain’s own. It did not descend; it simply was, coextensive with the thin air and the hard blue, present in every fracture and every cubic meter of the lens without occupying space. It had no shape the creatures could have registered as foreign, yet every mineralized plate, every flashing septum, every ranked filter felt the change as a momentary deepening of the water’s grain, a pause so brief it might have been the interval between two slow pulses.

It regarded the cirque the way one regards a thought that has just become articulate. The knife-edged rims, the unresolved talus, the water standing to the lip and a little over: all of it was still the mountain’s first sentence, unfinished. Inside that sentence the creatures worked, not as interlopers but as syntax. Grinders locked chisel-limbs into glassy rhyolite and rasped at the pace of cold chemistry. Sieves pulsed. Current-makers discharged in their high electrical rasp. Larger forms drifted on stiffened membranes, harvesting with the same unhurried geometry that had produced them. Nothing was wasted. Nothing hurried. Residual heat, redox gradients, the faint tingle they themselves generated and then consumed: these were sufficient. The power tasted the metallic water and understood that photosynthesis would have been a violence here, an imported mercy that would have collapsed the closed volume into something ordinary and green.

It listened longer than the seiche takes to slosh and return. The factory-floor click of thousands of feet, the basin-wide thud of internal valves beating in unison, the disciplined fizz of excavated vents: these were not noise. They were the sound of a young mountain teaching itself usefulness, one micron, one click, one exchanged mineral secretion at a time. Reproduction was public and patient; death, when it occurred, was only reincorporation. Density never thinned. The cirque remained saturated, a single breathing machine whose parts happened to be alive. At depth the animals grew stiller, more crystalline, as if pressure and the aging of light had already shown them the last necessary form.

The power felt no impulse to add, to warm, to seed, to name. Benevolence here was not the introduction of comfort. Wisdom was not the correction of strangeness. To have lowered the temperature even a fraction would have stiffened the chemistry they lived by. To have brightened the gold-green broth would have been an act of colonization. To have lifted one failed individual from dismantling would have broken the only law the basin knew. The system was already complete in its becoming; it did not remember emptiness and did not require rescue from fullness.

So the power did the one thing that would not alter a single ratchet or seiche or column of bubbles. It attended. It held the entire cup—rock, water, living machinery, residual heat, electrical rasp—in a regard that was itself a kind of weather, slower even than sediment settling. For a time that had no equivalent in the creatures’ public calendar, the mountain’s unfinished thought was simply known, completely, without remainder and without improvement.

Then the stillness lifted, as a seiche recedes. The water’s grain returned to itself. The pulse resumed its former depth. The grinders continued their years-long conversation with a single fracture. Nothing had been taken. Nothing had been left behind except the fact of having been seen, and even that fact dissolved at once into the work, which had never paused.

The cirque remained what it had been since the rock was sharp: full, loud, crowded with patient living engines chewing themselves toward a completeness that needed no witness and no blessing. The power, having understood this, turned its attention elsewhere, carrying only the knowledge that some processes are already the wisest thing that can happen to a newly torn mountain, and that the kindest act is to let them finish their sentence alone.
