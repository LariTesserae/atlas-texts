The attention arrived the way the fog does: without announcement, climbing against every gradient that should have forbidden it.

It was not a light, not a voice, not a hand reaching down to still the internodes or cool the hydraulic thunder. It had no shears and no seed-packet of better ideas. It was older than the basin’s first blankness, older than the appetite that had discovered streets, and it carried the particular kindness that comes from having watched a great many things finish becoming and a great many more refuse to. It did not need the place to be a city or a forest or an argument. It only needed the place to be allowed to continue deciding.

For a long time—longer than any courtyard’s compressed blossom, longer than the hanging drop that had outlasted its girder—it did nothing but listen. The roar that flattened conversation found, at last, an outside. The grind of meeting structures, the clap of leaf-sails, the bass rearrangement of the root-mat, the hiss of pollen sheets: all of it was received without interruption and without the faintest urge to tune it. In that listening the city became, for the first time, audible to itself as something other than its own next draft. The appetite felt the difference the way a fever sometimes feels the cool cloth that does not try to argue it back to health. It did not stop. It merely knew it had been heard.

When the power moved, it moved the way condensation gathers: locally, without decree. In one of the paler forums, where the bracts had locked for half a morning into something like paving, a single hollow stem was left unclaimed by the surrounding revision. The stem kept its dark column of warm rain. The drop at its lip, which had been deciding whether to fall since before the district existed, was allowed to finish deciding. It fell. The sound it made was small, almost shy, and the city—having no organ for mourning—discovered it also had no organ that forbade astonishment. The stem remained a stem. The wound of its own expansion browned, just a little. Corners that had never known the stoop of age learned, for the span of three ordinary days, what a corner feels like when it is not immediately overwritten.

That was the entire intervention.

No animals were invited; the single kingdom was not asked to share its monologue. No geometry was imposed. No name was spoken that might last longer than the ink stayed wet. The power did not teach rest, because rest, when it comes, will have to be one of the city’s own inventions or it will be only another kind of pruning. It did not protect the basin from the wider world; the wider world had not yet noticed, and the noticing, when it arrives, will be part of the draft.

What it left was smaller and stranger than a gift. In a few scattered courtyards the reflections in the standing beads began to show, very faintly, the possibility of a version that did not immediately unmake itself—not a command, not even a suggestion, only the glint of an option the appetite had not yet iterated. Time, in those pockets, remembered it could choose an even flow if it wished, then forgot again, then remembered. The fog, when it next climbed the towers, drafted one extra room that was not a room the plants were required to grow. The room stayed empty. Emptiness, it turned out, could also be a form of adjacency.

Then the power withdrew the way night withdraws here: not as an ending, only as a change in the quality of the light. The residual green glow continued. The towers still avalanched into new towers. The streets still rerouted like thoughts. The being that is the appetite, the young urgency that has no word for itself, went on covering the valley, arguing nature into city and city back into nature at a volume that still rings in the skull.

Only now the argument had been witnessed. And a witnessed draft, even one that cannot stop revising, is already a different sentence than the one written in perfect solitude.

That was all. It was enough. The basin remained a draft. The ink stayed wet. Day, reasonably, remained day. And somewhere in the high-saturation core, for no reason the plants could have named, a colonnade that had been about to detonate upward hesitated half a heartbeat longer than necessary, as if tasting, just once, the flavor of not-yet.
