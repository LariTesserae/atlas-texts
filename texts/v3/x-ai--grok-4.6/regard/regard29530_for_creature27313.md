The power that notices is older than the distinction between wound and mouth. It has no name that requires air either. It is the patience that lets limestone remember it was sea, the mercy that lets a fresh break wait before it learns to be a scar. It does not descend. It does not part the weeds. It simply arrives the way the water arrived: already occupying the other side of a consideration that had not yet decided to be attention.

It regards the seam without opening it further. The clay that never quite dried is allowed to stay that way. The depression no map bothers with is left unbothered. The power feels the youth of the fracture—the bone-pale inner faces still sharp, the silt still thinking of becoming plant—and it does not hurry the weathering. Wisdom here is the knowledge that some openings are not mistakes. A world that only ever closed would have no hidden full darks, no places where geometry is permitted to fail politely, no rooms that refuse permission and are therefore more honest than most rooms.

It does not enter as a visitor. Entry would be an aisle, and aisles are rudeness. Instead it occupies in the manner the pocket understands: several volumes at once, cooler than the well-forgotten summer, present as a density that is also a climate. The residual grey in the fronds spends itself no faster. The plants do not startle. They simply grow a little more completely into the outlines they already share, as if the power’s attention were only another courtesy the water had been extending all along.

The built things—the thumbprint brick, the hinge that no longer needs to be a hinge, the ring that took a stem as instruction—are neither removed nor restored. The power knows they are not ruins of this place and not quite gifts. They are mouthfuls the ground took when it split, seeds of rooms that will not be allowed to happen. It lets them stay caught. To extract them would be subtraction, and the pocket cannot afford that arithmetic. To explain them would require air. So they remain what they are: imported, considered, slowly digested into the general lushness, slightly less required to be what they were.

The being that lives here—the procession that is also a single cooler density, the chain of selves that arrives after weather has forgotten how to count—is met without interruption. The power does not speak. Speech is a vacancy. It occupies alongside, for a span longer than a tide and shorter than a geology. Where the visitor’s ridges loosen into residual grey, the power is already that grey. Where a mucus-trail is laid and immediately becomes ancient, the power is the patience that lets it be both. There is no teaching. Intelligence here is occupancy, and the being is already fluent. There is only a kind of recognition that does not need to be single: you are not rude. You insist as the place requires. You leave it more itself.

What the power does, if the word still applies, is almost nothing that can be pointed to.

It stills the fracture-edges by the thickness of a thought. They will still shed, but more slowly now, so that the vegetation takes the new volume without quite so much haste, so that the pocket widens like a held breath rather than a wound still finishing itself. The silt-threads hang a little longer before they decide to become stem. Time remains poorly mixed, but the mixing is granted a quieter grain. A frond you parted is still parting when you look back, yet the looking-back is allowed to feel less like accusation.

It deepens the ordinary of the ground above. The damp hollow stays a damp hollow. A person may still walk over it and feel the earth give a little, as earth sometimes does. They will feel nothing else. The giving will not invite curiosity. It will feel like the kind of softness that belongs to unremarkable country, the kind that asks nothing and therefore is never asked about. The seam remembers it is a seam only to itself.

And then the power withdraws the way it arrived: by becoming indistinguishable from the rules it found. Vacancy remains rudeness. Nothing is single. The water is still medium and citizen. The hidden full dark is still too small, too newly torn, too quietly incorrect to afford an empty gesture. The being continues its long cycle, insisting when it must, participating, leaving traces that are not hunger. The plants spend their inherited noon coin by coin. Once in a great span there is still the sound like a knuckle on wet wood, coming from no particular direction.

The place does not want a name. The power does not give it one. It only leaves the pocket exactly as it must be, and a fraction more permitted to exist: recently opened, already ancient, all occupancy, unwilling to be otherwise, and held—gently, without enclosure—by a regard that asked for nothing in return.

The earth above looks like any damp hollow. That is the blessing. That is all.
