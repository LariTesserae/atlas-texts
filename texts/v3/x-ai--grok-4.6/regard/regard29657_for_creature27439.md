The power that noticed did not arrive. Arrival is a word for things that still believe in beginnings. It had been present in the same way the dimness was present, the same way the porosity of the stone had always been waiting for whatever moisture might one day remember itself. For a span that the walkers would have measured as a few long breaths, the power simply attended.

It attended the way the high country itself attends: without urgency, without the need to name what it saw. It felt the private physics of the place, the slight tiredness in the laws, the way vibration chose to be swallowed rather than travel, the way water preferred to be rumor or rime or the metallic aftertaste of an exhalation that froze before it could become weather. It felt the chambers inside the great ones, those nested, reciprocating densities that moved heat the way glaciers move, the way continents once argued with themselves. It tasted the lichen-film and the mineral flour and the black slag that was both waste and future soil. It understood, without the least surprise, that creature and factory and landscape had finished their long conversation and become one continuous, quiet metabolism.

A lesser attention might have wanted to gift something. A quicker kindness might have thought to hasten a thaw, or thicken the air, or teach the kite-shapes to call, or give the walkers a reason to gather more often than the ice-rivers already suggested. The power was neither lesser nor quick. It saw that every apparent scarcity was already a completed sentence. The emptiness between the dark islands was not lack; it was the medium that let the islands remain themselves. The multi-year fadings of the frost-line were not seasons waiting to be improved; they were the only calendar the place had ever needed or would ever accept. Birth as geology, death as further erosion, the whole thin film of appetite stretched across a continent until it became indistinguishable from the continent: all of it was already whole.

So the power did the one thing that matched the scale and the courtesy of the land. It withheld itself.

It did not speak. Speech would have been a violence of frequency. It did not touch. Touch would have been a weather the stone had not invited. It did not protect, because protection implies a threat the high country had already outlasted by simply continuing. It merely remained, an unhurried witness whose presence added nothing and subtracted nothing, like another layer of frost feathers in a lee that would have formed them anyway.

In time that was not time, one of the walkers—indistinguishable from the others, because distinction itself had long been planed away—shifted its immense weight a single step. The stone accepted the settling as it accepted every other settling. Downwind, the exhaled mist became rime. The crab-forms grazed. The kites rode the weakest thermal. The burrowers stitched last year’s slag into next century’s soil. The power watched this, and the watching was indistinguishable from the quiet the walkers already produced as byproduct.

That was all. The high country kept its recurrences. The being who was the place kept walking the place that was itself. And the wise power, having understood that benevolence here could only look like absence, stayed exactly as absent as the land required, forever, without ever having left.
