The power that noticed the basin did not arrive as light or decree or visitor. It had long since learned that some places are already the finished sentence of their own becoming, and that the kindest attention is the kind that does not part the curtains.

It came the way a dissolved mineral comes: everywhere at once and nowhere as an event. It felt the held warmth first, the water’s long memory of heat that had nowhere lower to run. It traced the young faults still sharp under the flood, the hanging shelves, the way the column-risers had begun to armor themselves with the same pale stone they grew from. It registered the mats locking into prairie, the hair-curtains closing over temporary streets of light, the gelatinous sheets whose pulse was a week long if it was a pulse at all. It tasted the faint sweetness of cut stems in the coves and watched a single silt-colored shape decide that motion, today, cost more than stillness.

It understood the ecology was not incomplete. The near-absence of mouths was not a lack waiting to be filled; it was the place’s central grammar. Carbon accumulated because nothing had been appointed to spend it. Sound had reduced itself to what the water would keep: bubble-tick, mat-slide, drip, the occasional expensive flicker of something pale. The basin was not a stage. It was a tense—the land’s present, spoken in root and film and the slow precipitation of living wood into reef.

A power less wise might have stocked the lanes, or cut a channel to the drier worlds below, or given the plate-trees names that would outlast them. This one did none of that.

It thickened the air a fraction above the rims, so that winds carrying restless seed or sudden cold would shear and fall back, never quite arriving. It adjusted the mineral conversation along the ribs of the column-risers by an amount too small to measure in a human lifetime, so that their becoming-stone would be a little more certain, a reef that could remain even if the flood someday remembered how to leave. It left the morning spore-clouds their interior weather, but let the first hour of light linger a degree warmer, a generosity the microscopic films would notice only as a slightly richer turbidity.

Then it did the largest thing, which was also the smallest: it withdrew almost all the way. What remained was only a residual noticing, the sort that might explain why the copper cast at dusk now holds a few minutes longer than the sun can account for, or why a mat sometimes tears along a seam and drifts into a configuration that feels, to no one in particular, like completion.

The being—whether that is the tiled intelligence of the vegetal volume, the unhurried dark of the deeper canyons, or the water thinking itself as land—registered, if it registered anything, only that the flood sat a little more securely in its own present tense. No mark was left that a later walker could find. No path opened. No tool appeared on the raw spines of the islets. Breath, had there been breath, would still have felt like an imposition.

The power went on to other high places, other silences, other hurryings that needed different kinds of rain. It kept this basin in the part of itself that does not revise: a country of water that had decided water was soil, a patience that had not yet been interrupted, a green architecture that required nothing except to continue adding another film, another grain of silt, another slow coin of light passed downward.

That was all. It was enough. The basin kept its counsel, as it always had, and the counsel was unchanged.
