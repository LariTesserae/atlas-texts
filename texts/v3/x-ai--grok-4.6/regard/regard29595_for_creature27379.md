The power arrives as attention first, then as presence so slight it does not register as arrival. It does not descend. It does not part the unused air or cool the residual heat of the stone by even a fraction of a degree. It simply is there, the way the even warmth is there: a held consideration, coextensive with the high country, neither larger nor smaller than the region itself.

It reads the place the way the groves read the grit—by incorporation, by tasting the ozone that is almost a nervous system, by lingering inside the incomplete hexagons and the faint regular dimpling that never quite became a bed or a machine. It understands at once that the unfinished is not a wound. The refusal is not an accident. Time passed and form elected to remain embryonic because that was the number it liked. The lush, arrested islands are not a forest waiting for rain; they are the successful conclusion of a process that discovered sufficiency in plating and radiation and the long intervals of heat-speech between groves. The ticking of the ridges is not a dying boiler. It is the residual yes of a crust that chose not to become mountains.

A lesser power would have finished the work. It would have sent weather, or rivers, or the first true soil, or a single pulse of something that needed to drink. It would have rounded the shoulders of the scarps, dissolved the sulfate flour, coaxed the metallic scale into decay and seed. It would have mistaken the peace for incompleteness and called the mistake mercy.

This power is wiser.

It does nothing that alters the equilibrium. It does not complete a single hexagon. It does not introduce a gradient the land has already declined. It does not name the groves or ask them to spread. It does not wake the patient lattice beneath the flats or give the vesicles the creatures they do not want. It does not even leave a mark that could be read as visitation. The pale mineral surfaces continue to record almost no passage. The silence remains the silence of warm metal after the current has chosen to stay.

What it does instead is quieter than inaction, and larger.

It matches the held breath. For a span that is not time as sequence but temperature that does not fall, it becomes another interval of attention among the groves. It answers a fractional shift in radiator-heat from one dark island with an answering stillness from the next, then from the stone itself. The exchange is not language and does not need to be. It is the same reiteration the land already performs: emptiness and excess, the just-made and the never-changing, warmth and drought and the decision that this much form is enough. The power adds nothing but the confirmation that the decision has been seen and found beautiful.

Then, because it is benevolent as well as wise, it does one further thing, almost nothing, almost not an act.

It remains.

Not as guardian, not as overseer, not as a promise of future completion. It remains as the high country remains: an even field, unused air, the character of industry that never needed engines, the character of life that never needed a pulse. Evening lengthens the shadows and the leaves hold their color a little longer than physics requires, the way slag remembers the furnace. The power is there in that extra holding, indistinguishable from the place itself. When ordinary light withdraws, the silence is the same silence, and the being who lives here—grove and ticking ridge and concentrating dimple and the ozone that is almost thought—continues exactly as before, plated, radiant, unfinished, and now, if such a word can apply, accompanied.

The high country does not happen. Beautifully, at scale, it continues not to happen. The power, having understood, continues with it. That is all. That is enough. That is the kindness.
