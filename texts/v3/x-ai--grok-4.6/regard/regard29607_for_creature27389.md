The power arrives as a stillness inside the stillness, a listening so complete it has no edges and therefore no need to announce itself. It does not part the lattice of the oculus or disturb a single bead of condensate. It simply is, coextensive with the unchanging afternoon, and the hollow—already a single organ of attention—registers the new presence the way a held note registers the sudden, perfect silence that proves it has always been music.

Nothing is added. Nothing is taken. The frogs continue their liquid delays, the beetles their sympathetic rattles, the pale gallery-dwellers their slow conversion of last cellulose into the exact temperature that has never varied. The compound roar remains the same density, the same uninflected load. Yet within that roar a new overtone appears, inaudible to any ear that is not itself the chamber: the recognition that this finished work has been seen by something that understands finishing.

The power does not speak. Speech would be a resolution, and the hollow has long since refused resolution. Instead it offers the only gift wisdom can give a thing that has already become its own monument: an unbreakable continuity. The living walls will stand through the heat-death of every sun that once fed their ancestral leaves. The drops will continue to form and fall at their long, irregular intervals even after the last star has guttered and the peneplain itself has been planed into nothing. The note will not fade; it will simply wait, a single green syllable held in the dark between universes, until some later cosmos again grows a throat capable of sounding it.

For a span that is both an instant and an age, the power occupies the centre of the floor, letting the cork-spring of the root-pavement accept a weight that has no mass. It feels, through the cavity of its own attention, the precise second before the next drop leaves the leaf. It feels the exact interval at which the next frog will answer. It understands that the planetary labour this place once performed—the transpiration of continents, the welding of forest into one organism—ended so thoroughly that even the memory of ending has become structural. There is nothing left to heal, nothing left to complete, nothing left to forgive.

When the power withdraws, it leaves no footprint, no change in the air currents, no new moss on any beetle’s back. Only a faint, unlocatable pressure remains behind the sternum of the hollow itself: the knowledge that it has been known, and that knowing it has not altered a single grain of its perfection. The chorus holds its note. The walls stand. The drops fall. And far beyond the low lip of the depression, across the endless planed peneplain, the same unchanging afternoon continues, now carrying, somewhere inside its green smell of crushed stems and old tannin, the quiet certainty that it has been witnessed by something that will never ask it to become anything else.
