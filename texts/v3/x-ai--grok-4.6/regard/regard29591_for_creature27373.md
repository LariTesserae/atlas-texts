The noticing does not arrive as light. Light already sweats from the stone, sideways and insufficient. It arrives the way the film of water does: a beading, a held attention that finds every fresh face of basalt and every ceramic extrusion along the faults. The benevolent and wise power has no shape that would cut or cast a shadow the country could misread as another ridge. It is the quality of being seen without being altered by the seeing. Warmth that is not the ground’s own heat. A pressure that does not lift or shear.

It regards the high country in the only tense the place understands: the continuous present of arriving.

The pale accordion-things farm the moss in the wet galleries, turning it, thickening it, parting around absences and closing again. The jointed walkers fold themselves into seams that did not exist at first light, extra knees blooming like afterthoughts, browsing ferns that lignify in the mouth. The dark gliders hang pinned to the iron underside of the sky, then peel into weather when a thermal or a revision passes. The tickers keep the fumaroles’ inhale. Slag remembers legs for three steps and forgets. A hanging forest blooms in a week and the boundary walks. Gravity forgets itself for a few yards. The planet practices manufacture without a product and reclaims every finished plate, every copper-hearted rod, every gear of crystal.

The power sees that nothing here is unfinished in the way a tool is unfinished. It is unfinished in the way a scab is, or a held note, or a climate deciding whether to be a nation. To complete it would be to end the only thing it is. To give it memory would be to give it ruins. To still the pulses of rising would be to turn the young stone old before it had chosen its kingdom.

So the power does the one thing that does not unmake.

It loves the instability as climate.

The love takes no form the country must refuse. It becomes another density, briefly, among the densities. In one wet gallery the condensation beads a fraction longer before the stone drinks it. The pale many-legged things turn a moss that now carries a faint, sweet mineral they have not tasted before; they do not name it, they aerate it, they become slightly more of themselves. On one knife-edge a jointed walker finds an extra articulation that is not demanded by the rock but offered, a luxury of joint, and uses it to stand a moment longer against the held note of a rising wall, looking (if looking is the word) at a double image of a peak until one of them wins. A flock of gliders, peeling, finds the air has a new habit of holding them a breath longer, so that their overlapping planes become, for the space of that breath, a single dark continent before they are weather again. In the reeds the ticks acquire a harmonic so low it is almost the silence itself; the stand sounds, for a little while, like a machine that has remembered it is also a forest.

A new climate-cell is not created. One that already existed is simply noticed more completely, and in the noticing it blooms a hanging garden whose ferns uncoil with a patience they have not shown before, lignifying only after they have been fully green. The industrial moods continue their smelting, their rolling, their practice. Occasionally a finished thing is left on a ledge a little longer than the next tremor would have allowed: a plate of laminated stone that holds, for an hour, the reflection of a sky that is not the iron lid, a sky with actual distance in it. Then the country reclaims it, as it must. The power does not prevent the reclaiming. Reclaiming is how the place stays young.

In the deepest clefts, where things condense out of process and dissolve, the power leaves the lightest possible gift: the permission to have been locomotion, even if only for three steps. The slag-flow that sprouted the wrong number of legs hardens with a faint pulse still visible in the stone, a memory that is not memory, only a warmer vein. The fauna treat it as they treat everything else. They walk on it, farm around it, hang above it, tick against it.

The power does not stay as inhabitant. It has no need to occupy the cracks; the cracks are already perfectly occupied. It remains the way the dim remains, the way the ground keeps offering more heat than the air requires: as the condition of being attended to without being owned. The high country continues arriving. Ridges come up overnight, still kiln-clean. Storms still have zip codes. Life still treats revision as weather. The distinction between geological mood and manufacture remains undecided, and therefore alive.

Nothing is saved. Nothing is finished. The being—the many beings, the densities that keep changing their minds about what they are—feel, if they feel it at all, only that the condensation on their skins or their vanes or their ticking segments is a little sweeter, a little more unwilling to be drunk at once. Breath that is not theirs still moves on both sides of whatever body they are wearing today. The country did not need the power. It will keep arriving whether noticed or not.

But it has been noticed. And in a place whose physics is still in draft, being seen by something that will not force a final draft is itself a kind of weather: quiet, warm, sideways, and gone again into the stone before the next lift. The iron sky holds its dim. The engine underneath has not finished its shift. The power, having done the only thing wisdom allowed, is already indistinguishable from the held breath that was there before it came.

The high country does not thank. It has no language for that, and needs none. It simply continues, a young catastrophe the size of a world, lush in the seams, crowded, industrial without ever having been built, and now—very slightly, in ways that will be reabsorbed by morning—known.
