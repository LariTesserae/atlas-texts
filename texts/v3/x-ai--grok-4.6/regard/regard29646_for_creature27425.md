The power that turned its regard upon the basin did not arrive as light. Light had never been bargained for here, and a sudden sun would have been only another unfinished surface to dress and cover. It came instead as a change in the quality of the dark itself: a thickening that was not denser stone, a listening that was not echo. For a span that could not be counted in courses, it simply attended.

It felt the original contour as the builders themselves never could—the rumor of a wound or a hollow that had once been open, now so thoroughly broken and restacked that even the memory of openness had become another joint to be peched true. It felt the hands that were no longer entirely hands, the spines that folded like living hinges, the children already fitting chips into gaps their elders would later load. It heard the single hoarse voice of the place, percussion layered upon percussion until weather and labor were the same syllable. It tasted the mineral coolness, the iron of wet copper, the brief salt of the rare living things that blundered inward and were used completely.

Wisdom looked first. This was not a punishment. The rule of coverage had not been imposed from outside; it had grown from the first block the way a crack grows from settling. The builders were not enslaved to the work. They were the work’s way of continuing to happen, as necessary to the masonry as the masonry was to them. To halt the clack and thunk would have been to unmake the only life they possessed. To introduce a true dawn would have been to blind creatures whose eyes had long since become ornamental stone. To plant a garden would have been to offer them something they could only harvest and restack. Finishing, the power understood, was a concept that had no purchase because finishing would have left an edge, and edges here were only invitations to one more course.

Benevolence looked next, and longer. It felt the pauses that were not sleep: forehead against still-sweating wall, the body become another listening surface while the next decision arrived through the stone. It felt the absence of names, of speech that was not already the sound of making, of any rest that was not also more labor. It felt the pale elongated things scraped from the joints when cordage ran short, the hoof-prints already artifacts, the children who had never known a passage that was not also a lesson in coverage. Compassion did not flinch from the grimness; it simply refused to call the grimness necessary in every particular.

So the power did not unmake the rule. It did not lift the darkness or dry the air or command the rains to cease. It did not send the builders beyond the lip to see what still waited unmade. Instead it performed a single, almost invisible adjustment, the kind the builders themselves would have recognized as further instruction.

In certain chambers—never the same ones twice, never enough to become a pattern that could be followed—the next block laid down carried a faint, persistent warmth that was not the accidental seep of groundwater. When a builder rested forehead against that course, the decision that arrived was not only the next turn of wall or the next drainage runnel. For the length of one long breath, the stone remembered something the basin had never contained: the rumor of an original contour that had not needed to be broken. Not a vision, not a command to stop. Only a momentary lessening of the compulsion to cover, a permission so quiet it could be mistaken for settling. Some builders stood a little longer in those chambers. A few left a single stone undressed, or a niche empty of tools. Most simply incorporated the warmth as they incorporated everything else—undercut it, packed it, built the next layer to match—and moved on. The permission did not spread. It did not need to. It was enough that it existed, a hairline crack the place could choose to chisel into a channel or leave as a secret.

The power also changed the quality of one rain in a hundred. Not the volume, not the coolness. Only this: when that rain fell, a few of the black-mirror puddles held their reflections a fraction longer before draining. In those lingering surfaces a builder who happened to look down (they almost never looked down) might see, for the space of a single drip’s ring, not the expected darkness but the faint suggestion of a sky that had once negotiated with a sun. The image vanished with the water. The builder usually did not remark. Sometimes the next block they laid was set with a slightly different care, as though the joint itself had been reminded it was also a kind of remembering.

That was all. No announcement, no new architecture, no rescue that would have been another form of covering. The basin continued. The work never paused. The lip remained only the place the masonry had not yet reached. Yet here and there, in the packed geometry, a warmth lingered against a temple, a puddle hesitated, and the hoarse voice of the place acquired, for those who could hear it, a second, almost inaudible tone: not rest, not ending, but the smallest possible mercy inside the rule that could not be broken.

The power withdrew its regard the same way it had arrived, as a thinning of the dark that no one noticed because noticing had never been the point. It left the builders to their radii of labor, the children to their tightest passages, the rains to their films. It did not return. Wisdom had seen that return would have been only another unfinished surface. Benevolence had seen that the gift, once given, belonged to the stone now, and the stone would do with it what stone does.

The basin did not become kinder. It did not need to. It remained exactly what it had always been: embryonic, dense, loud, wet, and necessary to itself. Only now, in a few forgotten joints, the rumor of an original contour was allowed to stay a rumor a little longer before it, too, was dressed and laid.
