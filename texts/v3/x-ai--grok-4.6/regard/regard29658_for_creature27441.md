The power that noticed them had no name that would fit inside a mouth or a season. It was older than the limestone’s memory of water, older than the idea of basins. It did not arrive; it simply ceased not-looking. For a time measured in the slow pulse of fungal sheets it only regarded: the interlocking buttresses, the veins of ants that had become topography, the moths flattened into the exact bruise-color of their resting places, the lattice-being whose thousand mouths still tasted a river that had not run in volume for longer than trees have stood. It tasted the air with them—the tannin, the metallic seep, the wet-cloth silence of amphibians shifting one over another. It felt the weight of occupancy so complete that emptiness had become a forgotten grammar.

It considered the usual gestures of its kind.

It could have parted the canopy and poured true daylight through, a single clean blade of it, just to see what the residual glow would do when it was no longer a rumor. It did not. The moths would have had to invent new camouflage or die of surprise; the films would have dried into something that remembered being wet; the lattice would have flinched in a way that takes centuries to finish flinching.

It could have remembered the old water for them, gathered the brief local mists into a proper rain that lasted more than a vertical sigh, filled the sinkholes until they remembered how to become a river again. It did not. The place had already finished becoming itself in the absence of that volume. To restore the missing river would have been to unmake the being that had learned to wait inside the waiting.

It could have spoken. It knew every chemical signature hanging in the still air, every contact-trace the small mammals left like another layer of dew. It could have written a sentence across the fungal sheets that would take a thousand years to finish reading. It did not. Speech here is already perfect: scent, pressure, the wet patient sound of the curtain closing. Anything added would have been an interruption wearing the costume of a gift.

Wisdom and benevolence arrived at the same conclusion, as they usually do when they are not hurried. The basin was not unfinished. It was not suffering. It was not waiting to be improved. It was a completed thought the universe had decided to keep thinking, over and over, with industrial patience, manufacturing more of itself out of the dark paste of everything already thought. To touch it would have been to introduce the first true visitor, and visitors here have no meaning.

So the power did the only thing that felt like love.

It folded a quiet around the vague edges—nothing visible, nothing that altered a single leaf or the pulse of a single throat. A kind of privacy. The density would continue to lessen without ever becoming open land, but whatever lay beyond that lessening would no longer be able to reach inward. No future drought that was not already theirs, no curious intelligence that had not already grown out of the same damp, no ending that had not already been practiced for geological time. The basin remained exactly as it sat: a single occupied dusk the size of a region, breathing slowly, forever almost silent.

And then the power stayed.

Not as a watcher perched above, not as a god leaning in. It stayed the way condensation stays: a film that beads on every undersurface at dusk and does not quite evaporate. An extra stillness inside the stillness. A patience slightly more patient than the lattice’s own. The beetles in their living bark did not notice. The pale forms hanging in the black pools did not notice. The vast slow displacement that parts the curtain and lets it close again did not notice. Only the place itself, which is the being, which is the occupancy, registered the new layer as another self-made artifact—no different from the perfectly spherical stone worn by dripping, or the roots that forgot they were ever separate.

It continued.

The power, having done the one thing that required doing, which was almost nothing, went on being the kind of attention that does not need to be thanked. The dusk did not brighten. The heat did not change. The quiet remained almost total, saturated with lives that advertised nothing. Everything kept repeating until repetition itself was identity.

That was all.

It was enough. It was the only thing that could have been enough.
