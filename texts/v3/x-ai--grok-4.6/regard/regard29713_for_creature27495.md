The power arrived as a change in the quality of attention, not as a shape or a voice or a new weather. It had watched a great many worlds finish becoming themselves and then continue, and it knew the difference between a wound and a completed sentence. This plateau was the latter.

It did not cool the air. It did not plant a seed. It did not give the crews longer words or the kites a call. Those would have been the gestures of a younger, more anxious kindness, the kind that still believes every silence is an absence. Instead it sat with the three editors—heat, water, stone—and listened to the long conversation they had already concluded.

What it did, if it can be called doing, was this:

It thickened the film on the southern faces of the halls by a single molecule’s width, so that when a film-walker crossed open air the trembling lens it became held the white glare a heartbeat longer. The extra light fell across a grazer’s back and the grazer, without knowing why, rasped a cleaner line. The glittering track it left lasted until the next warm sheet, which is all any track here is asked to do.

It lengthened the pause of one old vent on the second bench by the space of three drips. In that interval the kites above tilted as they always had, but one membrane among them remembered, for the first time, the exact temperature at which its limbs liked to unfold. When the vent resumed, the flock realigned with a new and private precision that would never be named.

It left a single droplet of condensate on the underside of a valve the crews had calibrated a thousand times. The droplet tasted, if a tongue had ever reached it, of the original geothermal differential, the one the machines were built to outlast. A worker would find it at the next shift-change, wipe it away without comment, and carry the ghost of that taste into the hall. Speech would remain short and close. Nothing louder was required.

At the plateau’s edges, where the benches step down into haze, the power did not reveal the lower country. It only made the haze a fraction more honest, so that the crews who never looked would still, once in a generation, feel the true distance in their sinuses and know they were exactly where the weather had decided they should be.

Then the power withdrew the way humidity withdraws: not by leaving, but by becoming indistinguishable from what was already there. The factories pulsed. The stone wept. The arrangement inhaled. The place continued, one warm sheet of rain at a time, now carrying the lightest possible knowledge that it had been seen by something that understood finishing, and had found the finishing good.

That was all. It was enough.
