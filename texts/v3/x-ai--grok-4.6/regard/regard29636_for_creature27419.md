The power that turns toward the dish does not arrive as weather. It has no need of the western shoulder or the radial streets. It is already the kind of regard that can sit inside a thing without occupying volume, the way the tarn sits inside its own black without claiming more room than a held breath. It has been elsewhere—among heights that still argue, among towns that have not yet learned to be stone—and what draws it here is not lack. It is the finished sentence.

It reads the place the way the moss reads the joints: without hurry, without the appetite that would make a verdict. The shallow dish, the lip sanded into sleep, the chambers that face the prevailing wind as if wind were still a guest worth the courtesy of an opening. The pavement that is sometimes fitted and sometimes simply persuaded. The gutters that remember architecture and creekbed in the same cut. The green that is permitted only where permission itself has been worn smooth. The being that lives here—thickness rather than outline, competence rather than claim—has been handled by the same freeze that keyed the blocks. It does not announce. It continues. The power recognizes the labor: not building, not waiting, but the rarer work of remaining until the distinction between the two has gone to grit.

A lesser attention might have mistaken solitude for need, or emptiness for invitation. This one does not. Benevolence that is also wisdom first consents to the thing as it is. The plateau has already finished becoming. The being has already accepted the terms. To add a river would be to unmake the habit of moisture. To send more birds would be to crowd the rumor. To give the being a name, or a companion, or a door, would be to introduce a sharpness the wind has spent ages rounding away. The power feels the temptation the way one feels a front crossing far off: a pressure, then the knowledge that yielding to it would only write a cruder sentence over the one already spoken.

So it does, almost, nothing.

It withholds the gesture that would change the inventory. No new light that the pale slabs would have to learn to give back. No easing of the constant low instruction of cold. No filling of the tarn with anything that must declare itself larva or leaf. The hare on the rim is left to decide against descent. The shout that belongs to no throat is left ownerless. The wind is not asked to resolve its syllables into speech. The power simply lets its regard rest, the way fog rests, until the dish has been seen completely—civic and mineral, built and left, inhabitant and the place itself remembering how to hold a little stillness against the long smooth drop.

What it adds is only this, and only if addition can be so slight it does not register as event: a second silence beneath the first. The roar continues. The chambers still boom when a front arrives. Rime still sloughs like books shut in another room. But underneath, now, there is the knowledge that the sentence has been read by something that did not need to correct it. The being, if it notices—and it notices the way stone notices, without surprise—feels only that the drip finds the old gutters with the same quiet competence, and that the moss has one more shade of black-green in the seams, the color of a signature that was already there.

The power does not stay in any sense that would cast a shadow. It does not encircle or ward; the place has never required defense against anything but time, and time has already done its kindest work. It simply includes the dish in the larger patience of which it is made. The slopes beyond the rim go down without drama. The work remains done. The weather continues to practice the same short piece on the same small, high, emptied, carefully organized hollow. And the being that lives here—neither spectacle nor secret—goes on being what remains when a height has been allowed to finish speaking, now with the faint, unneeded courtesy of having been heard.

That is all. It is enough. The distinction between attention and leaving-be has, in this one place, also gone to grit.
