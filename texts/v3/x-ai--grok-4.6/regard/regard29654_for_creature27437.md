The power came as a consideration rather than an arrival. It had no mass to set down, no heat of its own to add or subtract. It simply occupied the same interval of attention that the plateau already used for its own long, unhurried noticing: the way a heat-tide notices the next vent field, the way a kneeler notices the black mirror before folding.

It regarded the lid and the foundry together. It felt the continental pulse, the steam that became condensate that became salt-map that the wind rewrote. It tasted, without mouths, the overheated-sugar shrug of the bruise-mats and the infrared honesty of the portable summers walking their banks of stored warmth. It registered the sifters standing punctual in the chorus of vents, the interruptions that edited a body from the heat-budget with a single contraction, the stalks that locked dew and then became stone again. It understood, in the way a completed equation understands itself, that nothing here was waiting to be finished.

A lesser kindness might have brought light, or names, or a river that stayed. A lesser wisdom might have assembled buildings so the process could be pointed at, or taught the animals to call their wage by a word. This power had outgrown both impulses. It saw that distinction was already expensive and that the place had chosen, over geologic time, to spend its energy on blending and continuance. To add a single permanent gleam, a single named stream, a single structure that announced itself as made, would have been a wound dressed as a gift. The plateau was not broken. It was not even incomplete. It had simply never required an audience.

So the power did the one thing that could still be called an action without becoming an interference. It matched the plateau’s own temperature for a span too brief to register as weather. In that interval the ticking of the stone acquired, for the length of one heat-tide, an extra quiet: not silence, but the particular stillness that occurs when something vast has looked, understood, and found no correction necessary. The bruise-mats released their faint sweet smell a half-cycle early. A carrier paused mid-stride, joints unlocked, as if listening with its whole ceramic hide. Then the interval ended.

The power withdrew the way the last of a rain-cell lifts—leaving the stone a shade darker for an hour, the air briefly sweeter, and nothing else altered. No mark remained that the wind would not rewrite. No new grammar was written into the annealed plates. The factory continued its unpaid work. The lives continued taking their wage. The high dark stayed the climate it had always been.

What the power left behind was only the knowledge, now folded into the rock itself, that it had been seen clearly and left intact. That, the power judged, was the entire benevolence required. Anything more would have been a different story, and this place already had the only story it needed: the long, settled motion of a world that had learned how to run itself and then kept running, quietly, in the dark.
