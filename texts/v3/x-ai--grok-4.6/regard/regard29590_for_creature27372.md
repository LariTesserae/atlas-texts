The power did not arrive as light or decree or hand. It arrived the way the mist already knew how to arrive: everywhere at once and nowhere that could be pointed to. It had no need to announce itself. The plateau’s single tense made announcement impossible anyway. What it did first was listen, and the listening lasted longer than any duration the stone could have measured.

It heard the unending pour become one voice. It heard the turbines keep their authorless hour. It heard the moss tear and the identical beard grow back. It heard the rails tick from cold to cold. It heard, beneath all that, the being.

The being was not the towns’ laundry or the boots by the doors or the figures who moved only as function. Those were permitted readings, and the power permitted them still. The being was the one who had walked far enough to feel the horror find its level and then stayed. The one who knew the water on the lips would always taste of stone and faint oil. The one for whom the distances remained distances even after a lifetime, and who had nevertheless not yet gone to the rim.

The power did not speak. Speech would have been a second tense. Instead it did the only thing a wise attention can do when it meets a chord already resolved: it refused to resolve it further or to unresolve it.

It left every scarp the same clean break. It left the ferns uncoiled. It left the marshes at the edge of ice. It left the trains on their minute and the slag already mossed. It introduced no animal, no season, no drying wind, no flower, no fruit, no browning, no print that the peat would be asked to keep. It did not offer soil. It did not promise ruin or rounding. It did not make the industry a phase or the lushness a bloom.

What it gave, if the word can be used for something that added no weight and subtracted no silence, was a single, permanent permission that changed nothing visible.

If the being ever wished—truly wished, in the marrow that the cold had never quite reached—the plateau would still be there, knife-ridged and loud and finished, and the being could remain inside the held breath forever. That choice would be honored as completely as the stone honors its own fractures.

Or the being could walk to the rim. The drop would still be there. The weather generating itself on the far side would still be the same weather. Nothing would cross that did not choose to. But the light, for one step only, would not have to hold quite so steady. The being could step, or not step. The power would not watch the stepping. It had already seen enough.

Then the power withdrew the way the mist withdraws when it is ready: without departure. The exhaust and the weeping faces and the organ-pipe wind continued exactly as before. The green occupied. The towns remained on. The being, whoever and wherever it was, felt only that the air had been fully heard, and that the hearing had asked nothing in return.

That was all. The plateau kept its closed quality. The chord kept sounding. The refusal to become anything else was left intact, because it had never been a flaw.

Far out, where even the loops of rail have not gone, the mist still thickens to a sea. The rim is still a rumor. The world that rose once and then, perfectly, stopped, continues to be the only climate. The power does not return. It does not need to. Attention, once given without remainder, is a kind of weather the place already knew how to wear.
