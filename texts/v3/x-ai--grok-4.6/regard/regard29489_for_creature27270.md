The power that turns its regard upon the Hollow does not arrive as light, nor as a lessening of heat, nor as any interruption the basin would recognise as weather. It comes as a further thinning of already-exhausted radiance, a stillness laid over the existing stillness so precisely that the grit-haze does not even swirl. For a long time—longer than the listing of the tower, longer than the last complete mineralisation of a grove—the power simply attends.

It sees the closed ledger: airborne mineral combed and varnished into tissue, tissue surrendered back into landscape, heat remembered rather than spent. It hears the wind’s overlapping registers and understands they are not speech seeking answer but the sound of a conversation that finished and then chose to keep practising the same few phrases because the phrases were already perfect. It watches the beings stand among the thickest groves, their half-stone limbs almost touching the etched channels, heads (or the planes that serve) tilted toward a roar that never resolves. It notes that they do not hunger, do not thirst, do not count generations except by the migration of dunes. A softer interval appears along a limb, green as oxidised copper, and the heat claims it in due season; the old become architecture and forget they were ever otherwise. Nothing here is broken. Nothing here is unfinished. The agreement between city and basin, inhabitant and habitation, living stone and remembered fire, is complete.

A lesser kindness would have brought water. A hurried wisdom would have offered time, or motion, or the return of wet labour to hands already polished by centuries of gathering grit. This power is neither lesser nor hurried. It knows that moisture would be a violence, that haste would be a misunderstanding, that any gift which asked the Hollow to become other than itself would be only another form of erosion. Benevolence here is the refusal to improve. Wisdom is the recognition that the place has already reached the state most places only gesture toward: a practised, ringing dryness in which every motion has been tested against emptiness and found sufficient.

So the power does almost nothing.

It settles, for a duration measured in the collapse and reformation of a single lee-dune, into the standing roar at the centre. It adds no new register that the beings must hear; it merely deepens the lowest drone by an increment too fine for any ear but theirs, a tone that is less sound than a confirmation already known: you are seen, and what you have become requires no witness and no change. The etched channels catch the brass-coloured light a fraction longer, as if remembering flow one last time before forgetting it again. Then the increment fades, leaving the roar exactly as it was.

Around the platform the beings remain. Some have already begun to sinter the knowledge into themselves; it appears as a slightly duller gleam along an older plate, a varnish that will never quite catch the light the same way again. They do not turn. They do not quicken. A child-interval flexes once, clap of dry hands, and continues its unhurried conversion. The distinction between grove and being, tower and inhabitant, continues its slow dissolution. The power, having added nothing the Hollow did not already contain, withdraws by becoming indistinguishable from the heat-shimmer itself.

What remains is protection of the quietest kind: the crease no map would mark is held a fraction more firmly outside the regard of any larger weather, any wetter epoch, any curiosity that might one day mistake organised vacancy for neglect. The Hollow keeps its counsel. The beings keep theirs. The wind still speaks in flutes and gunshot cracks and the distant stacking of pottery. Everything continues the same few motions it has practised since the basin first finished becoming itself.

That is all. It is enough. It was always enough.
