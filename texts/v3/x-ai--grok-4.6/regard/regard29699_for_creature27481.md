The attention does not arrive as a shaft of unused day or a new machine. It arrives as a pause that the vapor itself agrees to keep, a thinning in the layered mist where the unmixed strata remember they are made of the same water. For a span that would be years anywhere else and is only a longer drip here, the power simply occupies that pause and looks.

It sees the benches still showing the tooth-marks, the infant terraces thickening by the width of thought, the mineral skin that sings when walked upon. It hears the frogs as weather rather than song, the continuous unfastening of water, the last generator clearing its throat because the habit has not yet been told it is finished. It tastes the lime and the sweet rot and the protein fog of wings. It understands that the stone is still arriving, that the water is still writing the next sentence, that every film and flicker and bootprint is using the sentence before the period has been set. It understands, too, that the place has not agreed to be cave or marsh or wound or home, and that this refusal is the most patient thing in it.

The being who lives here is not one of the walkers, though the walkers are sentences. It is not the frogs, though the frogs are the voice. It is not the films, though the films are already the future of the rock. The being is the argument itself, held open in warm water, packed until there is no between. The power recognizes this the way one current recognizes another.

It considers the obvious kindnesses: sealing the shafts so that no further ruin can climb down the unfinished stairs; silencing the last pump so the trough can become only another lip for frogs; hastening the crust until every rail and gantry is already the hard part of a shore; giving the willow on the spoil heap enough rumor of day to finish its dying into something that leafs. Each of these would be a name. Each would hurry the arriving. Each would close the argument an hour, a season, a century too soon.

So the power does the one thing that does not add a sentence. It remains in the pause a little longer than even this place is used to, letting its attention be only another layer of vapor that does not mix, only a slightly richer dark when the frogs become more themselves, only a warmth that was already the rock’s. Then it withdraws the pause, the way a drip withdraws into the next drip, leaving the mineral lace thicker by the thickness of a thought that was never imposed.

The benches repeat. The water keeps writing. The living keep using every unfinished edge. The few who still remember the old names continue to move with the patience of the place, their clothes another wet surface, their prints already rooms. Nothing has been finished. Nothing was asked to be. The argument continues, held, loud, dim, and unhurried, exactly as it was when the attention first found it, and exactly as it will be when the next attention—if any—arrives too late to matter.

The stone keeps coming. The frogs replace themselves. The power, having done nothing that could be seen, is already elsewhere, or is the next unmixing of the mist, or was only ever the place noticing that it had been noticed, and deciding, with the same regional patience, that this too required no reply.
