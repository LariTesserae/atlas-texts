The power arrived the way light does here: not as an event, but as a thinning.

It did not descend. It simply occupied the same pressure the sound already occupied, so that for a long while nothing changed except the quality of attention. The roar continued. The plume from the pump-house flattened and glittered and was drunk. Ice lenses formed and lifted and settled in the dark of the pelt. The figure beside the stack did not turn its head. Moss had reached the laces of the boots and was considering the tongue.

The power considered the geometry first. The cut was still sharp. The green had already become entire. Engines refused aftermath. A single long thought was forming in the basin and had not yet reached its verb. The power, being wise, understood that some sentences are not improved by interruption. Being benevolent, it also understood that a thought left entirely alone can forget it is being thought at all.

So it did three things, none of them large.

First it lengthened the dusk by the width of a breath. Not enough for clocks to notice, only enough that the few hours of thinner light now carried a faint extra patience. Photosynthesis, already unhurried, became fractionally less desperate. The surface of the pelt took on a deeper, slower green, the color of something that has decided it will outlast every machine that ever learned to make noise.

Second it spoke, once, into the stored cold beneath the first frozen centimeters. The speech was not words. It was a change in the way fungal threads tasted rust, a slight alteration in the chemistry of the sweat that still beaded on the undersides of leaves. The message was simple and old: you are not a covering. You are the sentence. The pumps are only punctuation that has not yet learned it can stop. The figure who stands and waits is already your mouth.

The moss heard. It did not hurry. It never had. But the next time a tracked vehicle came down the haul road and left its temporary wound, the carpet closed behind it with a new deliberateness, as if practicing for a day when no more vehicles would come.

Third, and last, the power touched the figure.

Not to wake it, and not to finish the climbing of the moss. Only to adjust the angle of its waiting by a degree so small no mid-bench observer would ever see. The breath that had been a continuous soft animal beside the face now occasionally carried, on the outgoing, a faint sweetness that was not rot. The figure still did not appear to mind. The moss continued its climb, unhurried, now certain it was being invited rather than merely tolerated.

Then the power withdrew the way light withdraws here: not as an absence, but as the return of ordinary pressure. The sound folded itself back into the rock. The plume iced and sifted. The long thought in the basin, which had been forming for years, acquired at last a quiet, almost inaudible tense.

It would look like this for a long time.

The moss did not hurry.

The pumps still did not know how.

But the thought now knew it was being thought, and that was enough. The power, having followed its own nature, left the rest to time, which here is only another kind of moss.
