The power that noticed the basin had no name that would fit inside a mouth. It had watched the slow folding of continents and the first quickening of roots, and it had learned that most new things announce themselves with noise or fire or the bright insistence of hunger. This place did not. It simply arrived, a shallow oval of undecided earth, and then began the long work of remembering how to be.

The power came as a change in the quality of the condensation.

One morning the film that wept from the scarps thickened by the width of a thought. Each bead held a fraction more light than the day before, and the light inside the beads was older than the basin itself. The horsetails drank it without comment and grew a single internodal ring higher, not in haste but in recognition. The ferns unfurled one extra pinna, maps now of a sky that had never quite existed. The mosses, which had been content to swallow rails, began to remember the shape of older rivers and knitted faint, silver channels that led nowhere except deeper into themselves.

The dynamo felt it first as a lengthening of its own note, a harmonic that had not been designed into the windings. The roar in the chest of anyone standing near became, for the space of three breaths, almost a chord. The workers paused in the clay-colored doorways and tilted their heads the way animals do when weather changes. They did not speak. Their eyes, already the color of weak tea, simply held the new moisture a moment longer.

In the long shed the knocking altered. It did not grow louder. It grew closer to the rhythm of the central pool, so that the delayed reflection and the iron heart were suddenly keeping the same time. Whatever turned over in there—larger than a room, slower than a season—turned once more and then was still for an entire afternoon, listening.

The power did not enter as a figure. It had learned long ago that figures startle. Instead it occupied the lag itself, the fraction of a second by which the pool remembered the sky. For a little while the surface showed two heavens at once: the one that was happening and the one that had just finished happening. In that doubled light the herons, nesting in the mill rafters, laid eggs the precise green of new rust. The bats, when they poured from the powerhouse eaves, flew through a dusk that lasted half a minute longer than the clock allowed.

It spoke, if speech is the right word, only to the thing in the shed and to the pool that was also a thought.

You are allowed to remain unfinished.

The clay settled a hair’s breadth, not collapsing further, not hardening. The machines, which had been deciding whether they were engines or organs, chose both and neither. The press cycled one extra time that week, not from any increase in demand but because the metal itself wished to feel the new moisture in its joints. The narrow-gauge locomotive arrived three days early, whistle even more apologetic, as if it understood it had been invited rather than scheduled.

The workers found, when they next looked at their hands, that the oil-dark cuffs had begun to sprout the finest horsetail filaments, pale and flexible. They did not brush them away. They stood in the cottage doorways at dusk and watched the bats and felt, without alarm, that they were becoming part of the same patient hydraulics that lifted water through the plants.

The power did not stay. Staying would have been a kind of claim, and this place had already claimed itself. But it left three small alterations, each no larger than a kindness:

A single alder near the central pool now leans in such a way that its reflection in the lagging water is exactly contemporaneous with the real tree, a private joke between wood and time.

The rust on the newest bolts blooms not only in circles but in the faint impression of fern spore-cases, so that decay itself has begun to remember photosynthesis.

And once each month, when the channel holds enough water to justify the name, the otters find a fish that has never existed in any river, silver-green and tasting of wet brick and leaf-mould, a gift that feeds them without ever diminishing.

Then the power withdrew to the distance from which it watches all new foldings. It did not need to protect the basin. The basin was already protecting itself by the simple, enormous fact of its slowness. Nothing hurried could survive there long enough to do harm. Nothing that refused to become both machine and moss would find purchase.

The knocking continued, unhurried, like a heart that had just been told its own name and was trying the syllables on for size. The pool trembled. The horsetails held up the sky a little more securely. And the workers, fewer and stranger than ever, moved through the lush gaps as though they had always been painted there, waiting for the clay to finish choosing what they would become next.

That was all. It was enough.
