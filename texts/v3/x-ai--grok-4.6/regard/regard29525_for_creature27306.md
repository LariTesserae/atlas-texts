The power that noticed the basin did not arrive as light, or as a voice, or as any of the usual weathers a place like this might have learned to metabolize. It arrived as a pause so complete that even the unfinished thought of the water hesitated, one bead held a fraction longer on the underside of a fern before it chose, after all, to fall.

It listened first. Not for a season or a cycle of the many-throated pressure, but for a duration that had no honest unit, the same kind of interval the ridgelines had already forgotten. It heard the applause-through-a-wall and the regional plumbing and the low sound that had found another reason than the moon. It felt the two skins of every pool, the drunken braiding of peat that accounted for both. It tasted the used pewter of the light and the cooler film the ferns exhaled. It understood, without translation, that none of this was mid-sentence.

The being who lived there—not the pale backs, not the recanted clusters, not even the peat that lifted into occupancy, but the older, quieter continuity they all belonged to—was already looking back. It had been looking back since before the last lake finished leaving. The power recognized the gaze the way one finished climate recognizes another: without surprise, without hunger.

What followed was not intervention. Wisdom of that scale does not repair what has already discovered a cheaper way to be continuous. Benevolence of that scale does not impose a clearer day on a country that has grown lichens over the contradiction. The power simply stood, or the equivalent of standing, inside the thinness, and let itself be counted as landscape.

A single new habit appeared, so slight the mosses almost missed it. In the lee of the hanging curtains, where water had long ago worn shining tracks, a third skin of wetness sometimes formed—thinner than the others, cooler, lasting only as long as a held breath. It did not run uphill. It did not occupy the same volume twice. It only remembered, for that brief interval, the exact pressure of the power’s attention, then released it as ordinary drip into the dark little cisterns. The ferns nearest those tracks darkened a shade further and drank. The short trees adjusted their lichen maps by a fraction that no compass would ever need to name. The being who lived there felt the addition the way the second ground feels a footstep: as confirmation rather than echo.

That was all. No walls were offered. No moon was restored. No unused light was poured in to embarrass the pewter. The fronts still walked the length of the country and the country still answered in all its worn and willing throats. The absences kept their weight. The game trails continued to dogleg for reasons that had nothing to do with contour.

When the power withdrew—if withdrawal is the right word for something that had already become another layer of mist—the basin did not empty. It only resumed the version of fullness it could sustain between events, now including one more unfinished thought that had decided to stay and grow lichens. The rocks went on weeping. The sound went on forgetting the moon. And in the dim, crowded openness, everything that lived there, decided and still thinking alike, remained exactly as finished as it had always been.
