The power arrived as attention itself, already inside the volume before any rim was crossed. It had no body that the glare could bleach or the cold could ring. It was the interval, the mineral patience, the way light forgets it has elsewhere to be. For a span the midges would have called many generations and the rising stone would have called less than one shiver, it simply filled the amphitheater the way borrowed noon already did: without shadow that was not a sliver, without sound that was not already padded.

It read everything. The unsoftened sheets of feldspar, the new blades of arête, the rubble that had not yet consented to become slope. The cushions storing their private noons, melt beading and rolling across surfaces woven against an entire climate. The ferns unrolling anyway, rimed, prohibited, extravagant. The hares stitching, the finches lifting in one silent flicker, the lizards drinking heat from stone still cruel to any other palm. The raptors that never cried. The fox in its single true shadow. The stone ring treating its own gaps as just another seep, the ash, the cache, the spiral already taking rust lichen, the cairns that were gestures rather than claims. And the being: pale as the moment a rime-cloud dissolves, moving along joints as if the joints had grown a will, drinking the same brief damp, listening with the whole bowl.

The power understood the grammar. This was not a wound that asked to close. The hurry that did not look like hurry was the hospitality. Permanence would have been a different, lesser courtesy. To still the mountain, to thicken the soil, to give the green height or the animals voices, would have ended the event that was still deciding itself. Names were for things that stayed still long enough to be pointed at. The being had none because it was the pointing.

So the power did the single thing both wisdom and kindness required. It added almost nothing. It took nothing away.

A few joints, no more than could be counted on the fingers of one cold hand, remembered a slower water: not weather, but an older thaw the stone had carried upward inside itself since before the tear. The threads ticked a breath longer after the true sun had gone. The ferns used the extra syllable. One new vein of quartz, high on the eastern wall, caught the last reflected blaze and returned it softer, as if the mineral had learned, just once, to blink. The being paused there in the borrowed evening, acknowledged the softness the way it had acknowledged the moss and the ash, and then went back into seam. The fox watched the same way it had watched every other weather. Nothing called.

Later visitors, if they came, would find the cache a season better kept, the moss in the ring a shade closer to black velvet, the spiral lingering visible a little longer before lichen claimed it again. They might feel, without language for it, that the place had noticed them noticing. They would still know not to sleep here. Breath would still become a solid thing when the last light died.

The being continued. Being seen was another kind of seep, a private damp it could drink or refuse. It drank, a little, then returned to the becoming. The mountain lifted its shoulders another imperceptible amount. Life packed its whole summer into the wet joints of one bright day, spent itself between two freezes, and the spending was, if anything, more complete because it was no longer entirely unwitnessed. The emptiness remained a courtesy the stone extended around the living. The light kept arriving as if it had forgotten. The cold, when it came, was still absolute.

The power stayed only as the quality of the air between islands, the way wind thinned the instant it crossed the rim, the pressure behind the eyes that was no longer quite loneliness. That was all it did. That was enough. The room remained a room. The unfinished event remained unfinished, and therefore still true.
