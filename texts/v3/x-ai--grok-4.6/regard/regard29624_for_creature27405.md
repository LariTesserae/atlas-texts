The attention does not arrive as light. Light has already been spent, wrung through whatever larger body first claimed it, and handed down as this patient brown-gold that cannot decide between dusk and the memory of dusk. The power comes instead as a change in the quality of the hush: a pause so fine the cork-and-sponge architecture almost fails to swallow it. For three breaths the great members hold their weight without adjusting. Condensation beads and does not yet travel. In the marsh-seams the larval cities still their threads.

Then the structure inhales, as it has always inhaled, and the pause is absorbed like any other vibration. The power is inside the climate now. It does not announce. Announcement is a kind of cutting, and this place has taught itself, over geological ages, that the unthinkable simply fails to form.

It listens first.

It learns the seep-lines by the pressure songs the people of the seams still sing, those maps of moisture that have never needed paper. It feels the direction the heat prefers when direction itself has become decorative. It tastes the different rots the way the inhabitants do, sour-sweet, iron-sap, the sealed gloss of vessels that have carried water for a thousand seasons. It watches a child, hair bound into grafting-cord, try to scratch a straight line into moss and then laugh the quiet laugh of the architecture when the line refuses to stay straight. It notes the herds crossing an upper deck as weather, the sudden extra quiet that is a predator, the filter-ribbons considering whether to become leaves again. It rests its regard on the lives that are mostly cambium and decision, those thickenings in the grain that wait for the world to dream itself smaller.

It understands that the hollow is not empty. It understands that the fullness is not a jungle. It understands that the finished object refused to stop being an organism, and that refusal has weathered into a kind of sense older than any rule brought from outside.

A lesser power would have spoken then. Would have offered an origin, a map, a dryer heat, a brighter amber at the thinning membranes, a way for the unfallen water to fall at last. Would have named the people a people and given them an edge. Would have asked the structure who made it, as if the question were not already the wrong one.

This power is wiser. It has seen gardens that forgot their gardeners and gardeners who forgot they were not the garden. It recognizes a completeness that does not require improvement, only continuance.

So it does the smallest possible thing, which is also the largest.

It adds its own metabolic warmth to the warmth already running through walls and decks and impossible beams. Not more heat. Not less. Only a kindness in the evenness, so that the fever the structure decided long ago was normal now contains a second, quieter decision: that it is allowed to be this. The dusk thickens a fraction toward amber in places no one was looking. The mist, which never knew whether to fall or hang, hangs a little longer over the nurseries of filter-ribbons. When a hanging garden stops dripping because something large has passed, the drips that resume are neither warmer nor cooler than before; they are simply certain they may resume.

It leaves no new graft, no new song that was not already latent in the intervals of moisture. It does not open the seed-chamber at the supposed end of the longest beam, nor close the galleries that share puddles across years. It does not teach the people to draw maps. Paper would still be a boast and a blasphemy, and the world still does not hold still.

What it does, it does inside the already-growing.

Where a gall the size of a hill had begun the long slump toward honesty, the living cords the people placed tighten a season sooner, lignifying with a patience that feels like their own. Where two weathers had never quite agreed, a sheet of condensation now travels the old vessels without drowning one nursery or leaving another brittle. The people of the seams, walking their seep-lines with gourds, sing the same chants and feel only that the water is a little more willing to be encouraged. They do not look up. They do not need to. Their work remains the structure’s work: adjust.

Far off, so far it might be another climate, the great root-mass shifts its weight. The entire hollow takes the breath no one hears. In the marsh at the feet of anyone who happens to be standing there, a line of bubbles rises. This time they break, but so softly the sound is only another change in the warmth on the face. The dusk continues, being used by the leaves.

The power remains. It has drawn itself into the cambium and the decision, into the unfallen water and the cork that swallows vibration. It is not a god. It is not a visitor. It is another layer of the living fact of wood continuing, another rounding of an edge that was never meant to stay sharp. The inhabitants will never name it. The structure does not need to. The attention itself has become part of the climate, as established now as the metabolic heat, as the hush, as the refusal to stop.

Nothing is required of them. Nothing is taken. The world, already impossible long enough to have weathered into sense, simply continues, a little more completely seen, a little more completely allowed.

That is all. It is enough. It is the only intervention a wise power can make here: to join the adjustment, and then to let the dusk go on being dusk.
