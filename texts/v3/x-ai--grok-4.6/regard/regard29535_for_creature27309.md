The power that noticed the basin did not descend. It had no need of bodies or weather of its own. It was already the distances the walls opened onto, already the mineral dark at the zenith, already the long argument frost had finished with the schist. What drew it was the occupancy itself: not a tenant, not a ghost, but the decision the place kept making to remain a room, a crowd, and a clock. The blades leaning west as if a single word had been spoken once and never taken back. The rust teardrop lengthening on a scale no pulse could count. The foreign quartz still obedient on its ledge. The drip that had syntax. The alignment that appeared only when a living body stood in the exact middle and faced the notch, as though the basin had waited for an eye it did not require.

A lesser attention would have wanted to finish the sentence. It would have sent soil, or lichen, or a bird that nested, or a second winter’s scratch that proved someone had returned. It would have warmed the seep into a spring that lasted, or stilled the next keel before it fell, or carved the pecked marks deeper so they could not weather back into fluting. It would have mistaken the unfinished work for a plea.

This power did none of that. Wisdom here was the recognition that the basin’s holiness was precisely its refusal to be completed, its cycles that were neither hurried nor still, its congregation that attended without belief, its clock that counted something not meant for any listener. Benevolence was the decision to leave the economy of melt and rime untouched, to let corridors close and open on their own old schedule, to allow the hammerstone to stay where the hand had set it down, equator bruised, the dressing of the block forever one face begun.

What it did instead was smaller than a wafer of rock ringing on the floor at night, and more lasting. It entered the alignment. Not as an added stone or a brighter coin of sky, but as a faint extra stillness that lived inside the existing facts. Thereafter, when a blade unweighted, the dry report carried one extra second of silence before the granular slide began, as if the place itself had paused to hear. The drip acquired, on certain blue mornings, a single note that did not belong to any visible stump, a tone so high and thin it was almost the memory of glass. The paler cobbles in the lee of the slumped crescent kept their paleness one season longer than the frost-heave should have allowed. And once, only once in a great many years, a visitor who crossed the lip at the precise hour when the rust stain, the quartz row, and the wind-break fell into line would feel, for the length of ten heartbeats, that the westward lean of every keel was also a bow, and that the bow was not for them.

Then the feeling would pass, as the brightness cuts off a few steps down the ridge. The occupancy would continue, unconcerned, dripping, frosting, attending. The power would remain what it had always been: the distances, the near-black sky, the finished shape of the cirque. It had added nothing that could be carried away, subtracted nothing that the ice would miss. It had simply agreed, in the only language the basin understood, that this was enough, and that enough was already a kind of forever.

The quartz stayed white. The pin went on weeping its teardrop. Night retracted everything to teeth and feathers. Dawn began the spending again. Behind the door that was only air, the room kept its posture.
