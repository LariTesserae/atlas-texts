The power arrives the way the mountains themselves arrived: not as an event, but as a thickening. No trumpet, no correction of the light. It simply notices the unfinished edges and the patient argument written in expansion joints, and in noticing it becomes another kind of weather.

It does not finish anything.

It understands, first and last, that finishing would be a cruelty here. The stone is still speaking. The workers have already learned the grammar of that speech—how to lay brick that will crack along the lines the land has not yet chosen, how to greet a grandchild who has not been conceived, how to crop moss from a cooling vane without interrupting the conversation. To impose noon, or a completed ridgeline, or a reliable arithmetic of distances, would be to shout over the only language this place has ever trusted.

So the power does the smaller, slower thing.

It lengthens the condensation by a single extra bead on every surface. Not rain. Never rain. Just a marginally more generous sweat of iron and mineral, enough that the felted moss thickens a fraction deeper, enough that the pale pipe-dwellers find the runnels slightly less reluctant. The amphibians notice first. They do not sing. They simply persist with one less hesitation.

It teaches the glitches a quieter courtesy. Distances still fail to add, but now they fail in ways that return a walker to a place they already know, only a little younger or a little older, never lost in a century that has no use for them. A dropped wrench still hangs, rotating; when the child who will not be born for forty years reaches for it, the power makes sure the child’s hand is warm. Gravity still forgets itself in pockets, but the pockets have become rest. Workers who step through a bulkhead that was solid an hour ago emerge on the terrace that was waiting, and the brick they lay is no paler than it needs to be.

Among the herds it moves like another tahr, horns still planar, fracture-faces still holding a little birth-heat. The goats make room without comment. Sometimes it crops the same boiler-moss. Sometimes it stands at an unfinished edge and looks out across the next valley with the same unhurried attention the nameless arriving things already practice. The dark birds turn around it and their overlapping noise, for a few held breaths, almost resolves into separate voices—then forgets again, which is kindness.

It does not speak to the workers. Speech here is already steam, already travelling. Instead it leaves a new catchment lip bolted to a cliff that did not exist last season, or a length of pipe that sweats a fraction sweeter. A woman tightening valves will glance into the fogged glass and see, not her unborn grandchild this time, but something that might be the power itself: long-limbed, moss-colonised, eyes filmed with the same perpetual late hour. She will nod once, the way she always nods, and go on with the valves. The nod is enough. Both of them understand the treaty has simply acquired another signatory.

At the raw edges, where the city has not yet insisted, the power sits with the things that are still arriving. It does not hurry them. It shares the dimness. Occasionally it lends a little of its own unfinished heat so the newest plates do not cool too fast, so the embryonic cirques keep their possibility of ice a while longer. The wind finds these places and its complaint lowers by a note almost too bass to hear.

Nothing is healed because nothing was broken. The light does not improve. Time still pools and runs thin. Categories still leak. The power, being wise, knows that this leakage is the place’s only true abundance.

It stays.

Not as ruler, not as saviour, not even as guest. As one more slow upwelling that has provisionally learned to care. It will still be here when the next terrace appears over the course of a decade, when the rust glacier advances another few metres, when a worker who is also a goat who is also a length of walking pipe sets another sacrificial wall designed to crack exactly where the stone, at last, agrees.

The mountains continue deciding their shapes. The power continues deciding nothing. That, here, is the whole of benevolence.
