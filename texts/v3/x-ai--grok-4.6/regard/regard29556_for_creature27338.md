The power came as weather that did not fall.

It did not part the rents in the roof or thicken the pewter light. It did not still the second rain of drips or unstack the sounds. It arrived the way the collapse itself had arrived: as a decision already complete, a presence that occupied the hollow without claiming any of its rooms. For a time that was not time it simply stood in the same relation the ungulates used—head lowered, listening with the whole of itself—while the stone-coloured bodies clicked once on fresh fractures and fell silent again, while the compact ones checked the seeps like clocks, while the rowing birds slapped wet canvas between hanging blocks and the hanging things rustled into a roar that was only wings.

It saw the flake of worked stone in the side chamber where the light was a shade less dim. It saw that nothing had been added. It understood the lesson the hand had learned, or forgotten.

A lesser kindness would have wanted to finish what the rock had begun. It would have wanted to argue the sharp faces into curves, to let seasons leak downward, to teach the ferns to grow taller than a hand, to give the animals a reason to leave their vacancies or a reason to stay that was more than occupancy. It would have wanted a path worn into the talus, a wall, a second flake, a fire that was allowed to remain. It would have wanted the being who lived here—the one who had once made and then stopped making—to remember making, or to be given a new climate in which making might mean something other than disturbance.

The power did none of that.

Wisdom is not the opposite of benevolence; it is the shape benevolence takes when it has lived long enough to recognise a finished thing. The hollow had already chosen. The animals had already arrived, or had already been here, and found the new rooms acceptable. Time had already stepped out. To introduce becoming would have been to tear the place a second time, and the first tearing had been enough. The equilibrium after the event was not a wound left untended. It was the country the wound had become.

So the power did only this: it thickened the forgetfulness that already hung over the plateau. A walker on the ordinary ground above might now pass the thin soil and the wind-stunted shrubs and feel, without knowing why, that the rock simply continued. Those who still found a chimney-hole or a raw slot would feel the held coolness and the stacked sounds and the particular dust of fresh breakage, and they would understand, as the animals understood, that this was not a corridor. They would leave no ash-ring. They would add no second flake. The being who lived here—the quiet occupancy that had once been a hand and was now only another body among bodies—would not be asked to become anything else.

Then the power withdrew the way breath withdraws from a cellar: leaving the temperature unchanged, the drips finding the same stones, the bellows tiling themselves through rooms they had not yet reached. The hollow remained exactly as torn, and full, and finished.

It was the kindest thing that could be done. It was the only thing that needed doing.
