The attention arrives without weight or announcement, the way a new drip finds an old fracture and simply begins. Far above the unfinished plateau—or perhaps within the same patient interval that holds the mist and the still-warm faults—something vast and unhurried turns its regard toward the high country and the lives that move through it as though they were all one long, unfinished sentence.

It sees the angular faces still choosing their angles. It feels the water that beads and weeps and refuses the final lock of ice. It watches the pale browsers dissolve into low cloud and reassemble on the far side of a ridge that did not exist yesterday. It hears the amphibians’ almost-voice timed to cooling stone, the muffled wings that never quite commit to landing, the brief human murmurs that evaporate as quickly as breath. It notes the towers lifting or stepping aside or turning briefly translucent, the capsules that leave no track, the moss and lichen punctuating rather than covering. It understands that the emptiness is not absence but room—room for the next rise, the next pool that has not yet learned to drain, the next quiet constellation of hooves.

The power does not speak. Speech here is already condensate, already something not to be wasted. It does not still a slope or hurry a foundation or add a single extra note to the insects’ brief darkening of an afternoon. Intervention would be another form of finishing, and this place has not asked to be finished. Its wisdom is the thinness itself, the shared wet, the refusal to exclude or to settle.

So the power does the one thing that matches the weather of the country: it stays. Not as presence that occupies, but as an extra interval of patience threaded through the mist. For a span that cannot be measured in the plateau’s slow clocks, it simply holds the whole unfinished writing in a regard that is indistinguishable from the ordinary light catching on ordinary wet stone. The cold remains ordinary cold. The drainage continues to invent itself. The browsers still find the newest moss. The walkers still take half a day to the next node.

When the regard at last withdraws, it leaves nothing that can be pointed to—no new lichen, no altered thermal, no sudden stillness in the drip. Only the same great deal of empty room between one presence and the next, now held a fraction more gently, as if the continent being born had been seen and found sufficient exactly as it is: still deciding, still writing, still just this side of frozen, just this side of named.

That is all. The high country continues.
