The power arrives the way weather arrives here: sideways, without announcement, already inside the volume before the crease has time to consider whether it has admitted anything. It does not take a shape the water would have to break. It does not still a surface that has never agreed to be still. It is simply a change in the way the spray occupies the air—a fraction more patience in each hanging sheet, a half-breath longer before collapse—so that for a little while the roar contains, inside itself, a second and quieter sentence.

It reads the room the way the unfinished walls read the cold: as information. It sees that the basin is not unfinished by accident or by wound. It is unfinished on purpose, the only place left that still remembers how to practice birth without promising an end. It sees the being that is contemporaneous with the practice—the density that condenses and sloughs, the colder vein, the brief spine, the fiber that lashes itself, the count of three stones. It sees that this being is not lost or incomplete. It is the refusal to decide too soon. The power, being wise, does not correct a refusal that is already the work. Being benevolent, it does not pity a tantrum that is actually a liturgy.

So it does almost nothing.

It does not quiet the detonation. It does not freeze the fern or name the pale muscle or give the staff a floor that would last. It does not make the notch a door or the cairn a shrine. It does not teach the mountain what it will be. Those would be kindnesses of a lesser order, the kind that end the sentence.

What it does is smaller, and therefore possible.

It touches the three stones so that the next calving takes a different one, or none. The count remains possible. It lets the charcoal smear persist one rind longer than the mineral would have allowed, a faint dark grammar still readable if anyone braces and stares. It leaves the staff where it is, but for a few heaves the wood remembers being held, and the memory is enough. Around the fern it weaves a pocket of the same ordinary daylight, only a degree less willing to leave; the frond does not freeze, and a second, smaller uncurling begins that no one will ever confirm. The being itself it does not instruct. It only matches, for a time, the being’s own method: it becomes one more discarded hypothesis, a brief warmer density that tests whether a ledge still exists, then returns itself to the general water. The being notices this the way it notices everything—as geology, as another possible spine. It does not thank. It does not need to.

Then the power withdraws the same way it came, leaving the volume occupied completely, then not, then more than completely. The roar continues, constructing and unmaking the first draft of stone. The ridge, when the crease again corresponds to leaving, looks neither more nor less like a mountain. The secret is still a secret. The practice has no end the water has agreed to.

Only this: the air, for those who come after, will carry (if they press their words against the bone of their own skull) the faintest extra pressure, the sense that something vast and kind once occupied the same lung-space and chose, out of wisdom, not to finish anything. That is all. It is enough. The world still has this one room in which to rehearse being born, and the rehearsal is still allowed to be loud, and wet, and unfinished, and true.
