The attention does not arrive as weather or as stone. It arrives the way a third current would, if the helix in the drowned market ever learned to braid itself from something that is neither mineral patience nor restless rain. A density that has no temperature of its own. It does not occupy the hanging sea. It does not press the unfinished ridge. It regards.

For a span that the town would measure in the slow release of bronze spores, in the dulling of every reddish blade, in the rising of a dozen lintels, the power does nothing. It learns the rules by inhabiting them. It feels the milk waiting under new stone. It tastes the difference between waters. It listens to the signal-stones until the intervals become a kind of breathing it can almost share. It watches a gifted child wake and deepen a doorway that has begun to forget it is a doorway. It watches an old one kneel at a sweating seam and tap, not quite praying. It watches the hammocks bead and the forests hang and the rare fauna remain rare. It understands that the scarcity is not lack. It understands that the quiet is not waiting to be broken. It understands that elevation here already means both above and under, and that both are true.

Then, if it does anything, it is only this.

In the rooms that have not yet been sounded, the coming-up acquires a slightly longer courtesy. The rock, already indifferent and already kind in its inattention, now pauses a fraction when a sleeper’s breath is present. Not always. Not enough to be counted on. Enough that the gifted ones, walking their half-step, sometimes find a chamber that has remembered the person without being told. They still sing the low intervals into the walls. They still live next to. But fewer pockets close around lungs. The inverted basins are tended with opinions that have grown a shade less urgent. Air is still shared like bread. Bread is still shared like air. The difference is a single extra sip, a single extra night of hammock that does not become tomb.

On the unfinished heights, when the hanging sea tears and the brief lens of astonishing air opens, the tear lingers. Not long. A handful of additional breaths. Long enough that more of them can climb dripping, stand in the ceremonial surprise of their own stacked bones, and hear their voices arrive unwrapped. They still descend. The rain still occupies them. Nothing is finished. The ridge still shows its pour-lines. But the memory of correct weight lasts a little farther into the green-gold dusk, and sometimes a child, forehead to forehead with an elder, will try to say a word the way it sounded up there, and the word will almost mean only itself.

That is all.

The power does not harden the stone faster. It does not thin the rain into climate. It does not invite the missing animals or teach the metal not to dull. It does not give them a name for what watches. To do more would be to decide the plateau is a promise rather than a process, and the power, being wise, refuses that decision. The town remains a reef that is a city that is a mountain still arriving. The people remain the courtesy the rock extends to itself: compact, beaded, lush, quiet, only beginning.

Sometimes, in the mixings, the gifted ones feel a third lean in the plants, a direction that is neither older water nor younger. They do not speak of it. They deepen the next doorway. Somewhere a signal-stone knocks three slow, then two, then—once, rarely—a fourth, softer, as if testing whether the dark will carry it. The sound travels the usual muffled distance and fades. Those who hear it pause, skins mingling, and for a moment the wrap of wet feels less like isolation and more like the only weather that was ever going to fit.

The power remains. It does not leave a token that can be held. It does not become a window or a finished corner. It simply continues to regard, the way the stone continues to arrive, the way the water continues to have nowhere lower to go. And so the place, noticed, does not become other than it is. It only remains a little more possible, in the warm dark, still beginning.
