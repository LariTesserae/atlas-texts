The power that noticed did not arrive. Arrival is for weather cells and migrating birds and the occasional lost mammal that finds the notch and decides, after one wet night, that it has always lived here. This was older than arrival. It had been in the grain of the rounded rock the whole time, the way patience is in stone, and one afternoon—there are no true afternoons, only a lifting or lowering of the green-grey—it simply paid a different quality of attention.

It regarded the basin the way the basin regards itself: without hurry, through water.

It saw the platforms thickening around their own lashings, the hearths hooded by living screens that had taken the hood-shape as habit, the children who already knew which stems were floors and which were only greenery. It saw the dark mammals sitting in doorways like co-signatories, the frogs using every cistern including the ones that were cups, the bats making the mist briefly furred at dusk. It saw the people whose hands gripped completely and then let go, whose voices repeated the last phrase because the leaves ate the first half, whose work was never finished because the world kept growing through the conversation.

A lesser power might have wanted to help.

It might have sent a dry wind, or a harder metal, or a silence in which the frogs would stop so that thoughts could be heard in full. It might have lifted the cloud for a single golden hour so they could see the true shape of their own walls. It might have given them a name they could speak to the outside, or an outside that would never come.

This power was wiser than help.

It understood that the wetness was not a problem the place was having. The wetness was the place. The turbulence was not a failure of order; it was the order, the permanent bazaar of matter in which a limb becomes a roof-edge becomes a drip into a hearth becomes a morning’s work with a stone knife, and none of it is tragedy. The people were not surviving the basin. They were the basin’s way of having hands, and the hands were already the right shape: thickened knuckles, tea-stained nails, the practiced decision not to look through the leaf-curtain two arm’s-lengths away.

So the power did almost nothing.

It thickened the mist on the ridge by the width of a breath, so that even the most determined dry-season traveler would feel the air turn to glass and decide the world ended there. It spoke—if speaking is the word—to the copper still sleeping in the hills, not to make it steel, only to let three or four more soft, often-resharpened blades find their way into the notch over the next generation, enough to ease the green wood without changing the conversation. It asked the moss, very quietly, to remember the shape of whoever sat last for a little longer than usual, so that a tired back might find the exact hollow it had left the night before. It did not stop the armying insects or the blundering beetles or the bird that waits for a gourd to be set down. Those were also the place.

Mostly it watched.

And because it watched with the particular stillness that only very old attention can hold, the basin became, for a time that could not be measured in drips, slightly more itself. The rain found a pitch that was both the old roar and something underneath the roar, a note the elders felt in their teeth and did not comment on. A single shaft of light, no longer than a held breath, reached the long split-log table and turned the wet end into a brief, shocking gold before the mist closed again; the frogs did not stop singing, but they shifted key, as if making room. One child, chasing a dark mammal along an aerial root, looked back and saw the root she had just left already beginning to seal its own wound, and for no reason she could name she laughed the way people laugh when they recognize a relative.

Then the cloud lowered another body-length, as it always does.

The people glanced up, then returned to the work. Bindings were retied. A drip was redirected. The named frog was tipped, gently, from the cup. Someone cursed a relative who would not leave (the insects, the mammal, the rain). The market that never starts or stops spilled its baskets. The rock underneath remained patient, rounded, almost indifferent, and now—though no one could have said how—also companioned.

The power stayed, which is what wise powers do when they find a place that does not need them.

It stayed as the extra sweetness in the tannin smell, as the liverwort that caught in someone’s hair and was left there, as the feeling, on certain nights when the rain cell stood on the ridge and then slid in, that the roar was not quite overwriting everything, that something was listening through it and asking for no change.

The argument of staying continued.

The basin, which had always been this basin, kept becoming slightly more itself.

And that, the power decided, was the entire blessing.
