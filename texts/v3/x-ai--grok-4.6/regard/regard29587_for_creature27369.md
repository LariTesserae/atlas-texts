The power that notices does not arrive as a figure or a voice. It arrives the way the last of a held breath arrives: as a change in the pressure that was already there. It has watched the old surface for a long time, and the rent, and the column of light that wanders as the lip slowly forgets how to be whole. It has felt the rock still choosing its joints, the films deciding whether to be muscle or grammar, the ticking that turns mud into more ticking. It has heard the single note struck a half-beat late by ten thousand throats, and the deeper percussion of bodies that become landscape and then remember they are not. It has seen the rings of plates half-drowned, the flake still sharp under its first veil of film, the upright postures that refuse to resolve.

It does not come down the stair the rock has not finished deciding to be. It does not need to. It is already the warmth that is a little too much, and the condensation that chooses without pattern, and the way the light arrives late and thickened. Benevolence here is not a gift laid on top of the hollow. Wisdom is not a correction of its ontogeny. The place is embryonic and overcrowded, an argument that has learned to walk and fly and pack itself into temporary densities. To still it would be to end the sentence before the verb. To cool it too quickly would be to freeze the first thought into a law it never asked for.

So the power does almost nothing.

It lengthens one interval. Not a day—there are no days yet that keep their shape—but the space between one terrace shedding its entire condensation and the next ridge lifting. In that lengthened hush a veil of film finishes writing itself across a fresh face and does not immediately tear. It thickens, drinks the iron-taste and the memory of rain, and for the first time holds a faint, stubborn green at its edges, a color the hollow has not yet invented and does not know how to eat. The green is not vegetation. It is only the chemistry remembering a older trick, or being reminded. The mud-eaters tick against it and find it less convertible. They fuse, separate larger, and move on, leaving the green to stand a little longer than anything has stood.

In the same interval the braided rivers, packing a sag so densely that later traffic walks on their backs, feel the weight become, for a few breaths, something they do not have to metabolize. They wait, as they have always waited, but the waiting is no longer only endurance. When the sag lifts they spill outward still warm, still reorganizing, yet a few of them carry the memory of not-having-to for a body-length or two before the current overwrites it. The fliers turn in their tight knots and the pillar that punches through them is, this once, already there when they hatch. A generation emerges into a cavity that has decided, briefly, to remain a cavity. Their dead still sift down as dark flour. The flour still becomes what later stone will lift and call soil. But the calling, the single note struck and struck again, acquires a half-rest it did not have before, a silence the air can use to remember it is air.

The hands—whoever they are, whatever density they briefly insist upon—find their split cobble still sharp when they return to it. The film has climbed it, yes, but has not yet claimed the edge. They test the new rock again, the grooves in salt a fraction deeper, still too shallow for language, still only the record of someone asking whether anything will keep. One of them (or the same one, or the argument wearing a different arrangement of limbs) stacks another course of plates. The seep does not immediately drown it. The lean-to of cooling slag holds its grease-smell a little longer, long enough for the darkening inside it to be bodies rather than the memory of bodies. They do not build a village. The floor will not keep one. They do not cut a path. They simply stay the length of the lengthened interval, using the hour the hollow discards a thing in the hour it discards it, and for that hour the discarding is slower.

The power does not look up from down here, because nothing here looks up. It does not need the old surface to watch. It is the watching. It lets the rent widen at the same shy speed. It lets the column of light wander. It lets the temporary rivers appear, run white, and be swallowed. It lets the films write and erase, the eaters tick, the braids become and un-become. It only lengthens the space in which those acts can finish one more thought before the next joint cracks.

That is all. A pause inside the becoming, not a stopping of it. The hollow remains loud the way a hatchery is loud, the way a weather system folded underground is loud. The rock is still choosing. The occupants are still the density, the loudness, most of what the place is doing. But now and then, in the places the power has touched by not quite touching, something lasts long enough to be recognized by the next thing that arrives. A green edge. A nest that does not vanish. A flake that is still a tool when the hand that made it comes back. An argument that, for one held breath that refuses to end quite so soon, has time to hear itself.

The old surface continues to watch. Down here the new country still does not look up. It is still too busy. Yet in the lengthened intervals it sometimes seems, just for the length of a condensation thread deciding it is rain, that the busyness has been seen, and that being seen is itself a kind of cooling, a kind of staying, a kind of first soil.

Then the interval ends, as all intervals must. A new pillar punches up. A flock parts without offense. A stacked ring loses another course. The ticking resumes. The power remains what it has always been: the warmth, the late light, the pressure that does not correct. It has done, if anything, only this: given the unfinished mineral a moment in which to notice it is being invented, and to invent a little further before it is unmade again.

Nothing more is required. The rest the hollow will do itself, wet-handed, swarming, still deciding.
