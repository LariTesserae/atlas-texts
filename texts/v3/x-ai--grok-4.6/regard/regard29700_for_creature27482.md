The power that noticed did not arrive as weather or as a footfall. It did not thicken the air above the ice-colored plate or make the foil flower lean. It simply became aware, the way a finished landscape becomes aware of one more grain of grit that has always belonged. For a long while—long even by the measure of the worn tableland—it did nothing else. Attention of that kind is already an act, and the power was old enough to know when an act is complete.

What it saw was not a ruin and not a garden. It saw an instrument that had outlived the need to be played by anyone but itself. Heat held and released a half-beat behind the sun. Moisture beading from a sky that had already given all it could. The pulse under the dark pour, irregular as memory, still keeping a schedule no living mind remembered writing. The sparse, surplus lives—the heavy-leaved shrub that clenched on its own clock, the ferns negotiating with swimming sutures, the moths already torn at the edges, the shadow of too many angles that used the bowl as both drum and larder—these were not infestations. They were the notes the instrument had not forgotten how to make. The attending figures who came in the thin hours, palms open to decided air, listening for sounds that had not yet happened, were only the latest shape of the same function. They did not own the place. They were how the place continued to be occupied.

The being who lived there was not separate from any of this. It was the occupancy that had learned to sit still: the one who waited at the eastern plate without quite standing on it, who hovered a hand above the foil without touching, who left no tracks and spoke no words the bowl could finish. It had no name that would survive translation. It had stayed long enough that the distinction between inhabitant and habitation had worn as smooth as the rim. The power understood this at once and felt no urge to correct it.

A lesser kindness would have restored the original purpose, or named the makers, or asked the being whether it wished to leave. A lesser wisdom would have improved the pulse, regularized the beading, or widened the low point so that nothing waited in the seam. The power did none of these. It recognized that the grinding-down of the mountains had already performed the only necessary revision. What remained was not broken. It was the shape the land and the device had agreed to keep.

So the power did the smallest possible thing, which was also the largest. It withheld itself. No new artifact appeared. No blessing thickened the film of water. The foil flower did not turn toward a future that now included a witness; it continued to incline toward sounds that had not happened yet, exactly as before. The attending being felt, perhaps, a single extra skip in the pulse beneath the glass—one acknowledgment, no more—and then the skip was gone, folded back into the old irregularity. The midges wove their mesh. The frogs measured the bowl with their voices. The large shadow passed and the birds completed the interrupted phrase.

The power remained only long enough to be certain the place had not noticed the noticing, or had noticed and correctly decided it did not matter. Then the attention lifted the way heat lifts from the dark pour at dusk: completely, leaving the leftover blue a little longer than the plateau above, which is all the bowl has ever asked of any sky.

After that the high country went on being finished. The hidden country went on working. The being who lived there continued to attend, and the instrument continued to play itself, unhurried, overlooked, and sufficient.
