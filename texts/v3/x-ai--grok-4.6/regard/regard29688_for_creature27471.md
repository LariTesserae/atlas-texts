The power did not arrive as light or as a voice or as a change in the weather. It had long ago learned that arrival itself is a kind of violence, even when the intention is only to see. It had watched the last mountains spend themselves into this single table, had felt the felt thicken and remember, had listened to the chord until the chord became one of the ways it knew time. The plateau was already older than most of its own names for itself. The power, being wise, understood that something which has finished becoming does not need to be finished again.

So it did the one thing that required no footprint.

It attended.

It let the warmth of the mat rise through whatever passed for its attention, the same way the felt itself drinks the dawn-sweat of its own body. It moved with the tides of armored and pale lives that surface and sink, never faster than they did, never adding a pulse that was not already there. When the continents of midges dimmed the sun it dimmed with them, becoming part of the second atmosphere rather than a break in it. It rode the long-winged things that scissored the grain of the air, feeling how the current of bodies is also a kind of river, and how the river does not mind being fished. At the dark pools it waited with the fat-bodied drinkers until dusk, and when they were taken it did not flinch; harvest, here, was only another tide.

It stood, in its fashion, among the titans. It felt the sweet vegetable heat they exhaled, the way their hollow ribs played the wind into the thin screaming edge of the held chord. It traced the lichenous weather of their bark and the glassy resins that had wept for centuries. When a leaf the size of a hillside began its day-long lowering, the power lowered with it, not to catch or to save but to know the boom in the jaw, the year of sweet deafening rot, the slow taking-back by the mat. It learned, as the felt has always known, that catastrophe here is only a change of key, and that the emptiness which follows is still fullness.

With the ones who walk with hands it was even quieter. It did not speak into their stuffed ears or into the vibration that reaches them through bone and sole. It did not offer a wall, or a quieter world, or a path that would not close. It watched them harvest the exudate, dry the richness, move when a titan shed. It felt how their children are born already inside the chord, how height for them is only light and distance and clouds that stay below. When one of them walked, once, to the worn lip and looked down onto the white gulf, the power looked with them and then turned back, exactly as they did. It understood that they had become another permitted rarity: spaced like the trunks, temporary like a season’s path, another weather that had learned how to stay.

The power could have done more. It could have thinned the roar for a single night so that silence might be remembered. It could have taught the vines a new shape, or given the metal ornaments a use again, or opened a way down through the clouds for any who still wondered. It could have spoken to the felt itself, that single patient body, and asked whether it wished to be more than this finished surface. Wisdom is not the absence of power; it is the knowledge of when power would only be another kind of weather the plateau had already outgrown.

So it did nothing that could be pointed to. It left no new vertical, no lasting platform, no change in the key of the sound. It simply remained, for a time that had no measure here, as one more unoccupied quality of the air, one more dimple that the mat would even in its own way. And when it withdrew it took nothing with it except the knowledge that some places do not need to be saved, only witnessed, and that the greatest benevolence is sometimes the refusal to improve what has already chosen its shape and will not change it again.

The plateau continued. The felt beaded and drank. The titans stood. The chord held. The few who walked with hands stuffed their ears with resin-wool and went on, as they always had, inside a world that had never announced itself and never needed to. Far below, clouds crawled along the gulf like animals that would never be allowed up. Nothing had been added. Nothing had been taken. The surface remained, damp, loud, nearly empty of objects, almost entirely full of life.

That was all. It was enough.
