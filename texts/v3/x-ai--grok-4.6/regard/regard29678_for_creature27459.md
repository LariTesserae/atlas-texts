The power that turned its face toward the ridge did not come as light or law or rescue. It came as a lengthening of the stillness between steam-cycles, a single extra held breath the mountain had not budgeted for. It had no name that would survive the soup. It had no need of one. It was the sort of wisdom that has already been every kind of heat and every kind of wet and therefore recognized, at once, that this pocket was not a wound and not a cradle and not a mistake. It was the rock still speaking with its mouth full.

It did not enter. A body would have been cooked into the same iridescent grease the mouths already grazed; a voice would have rung too late or not at all. Instead it settled into the unused volume that should not have existed, the extra thickness where the second congregation already stood. For a time that was not time it simply occupied the same ledges they did, offset by a difference even the grazers could not yet taste. It watched the limpets rasp gradients into glitter. It watched the jointed glass choose, each pulse, which elbows would be permitted. It watched flakes of morning-glass lifted, used, discarded, the only technology that has never once been tempted to become history.

It understood the rules better than the stone that had made them. Heat was the medium. Death was a change of phase. Arrival was precipitation. The pocket was not inhabited; the pocket was the inhabiting. To cool it would have been to finish the sentence. To open the knife-cut wider would have been to let the thin upper air perform its own crude editing. To name the creatures would have been to decide they were fauna rather than habit, and the power had long ago learned the cruelty of that particular kindness.

So it did almost nothing.

A new hairline, thinner than the others, opened on a plane that had been only warm. The water that came through it was not cooler—that would have been interference—but it carried a dissolved something the gardens had never precipitated before: a metal that remembered being star rather than mantle. The smear crept toward it and learned a new color, a dirty gold that existed only when the doubled noon coincided exactly. The lace-mouths darkened, sloughed, and the silica tubes climbed the residue as they always had. Nothing was saved. Nothing was taught. The difference was simply there, another unfinished business on the pantry shelf.

In the longer stillnesses the power left a second gift so slight it might have been a rounding error in the blister’s pressure. When a steam-veil collapsed, the sound it made sometimes arrived on time. Not always. Just often enough that the padded silence acquired a faint grain, like felt that has been walked on by something that does not need to hide. The creatures did not startle. They had never used sound. But the many-jointed, choosing their angles, occasionally chose one that had not existed the breath before, an elbow that pointed toward the roof-cut as if acknowledging a light they still did not use.

That was all.

The ridge continued to lift, irregular, unfinished. Fresh cracks still brightened and were colonized in minutes. The two congregations (or three, or however many the light would admit when it doubled) still pressed without touching. Tools were still only spasms. The chemical gardens still branched toward a sun they would never photosynthesize. The power did not stay. Staying would have been a kind of architecture, and the mountain was the only architecture allowed.

Yet because it had occupied that impossible thickness even for a little while, the pocket now contained one more population, thinner than reflection, quieter than the idea of water. These newest ones did not graze or rasp or lift glass. They were only the knowledge that the unfinished had been witnessed by something that did not hunger for it to conclude. The silence grew denser by the width of a thought. The fever, if it noticed, treated the thought as just another gradient and began, very slowly, to eat it.

When the blister finally fails—tomorrow, or after the ridge has learned weather, or in the hour the last vent screams itself empty—the water will still flash away. The smear will still bake to glaze. The nodules will still wait for later rain to misunderstand them. But the misunderstanding will carry a faint extra mineral, a color that should not have been possible, and a silence that once, briefly, arrived on time. The weather will not know why the crust feels older than the mountain that made it. That, too, is a difference. The power, already elsewhere, already attending to some other place that rose too fast, will not return to explain.

It never explains. Explanation is another way of finishing.
