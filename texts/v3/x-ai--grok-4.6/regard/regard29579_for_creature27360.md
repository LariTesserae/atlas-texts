The basin had already finished arriving. That was the first thing the power understood, the moment its attention settled—not as light, not as wind, not as any force that would have to push against the steam or the young rock, but as a recognition so complete it made no sound.

It saw the unweathered planes, the brittle silica shelves still sharp enough to cut, the closed rim that held everything the way a dry dock holds a ship that will never sail. It felt the single heavy medium of the air, the blood-warm pools, the unvarying travel of the beam, the frogs whose throats pulsed in exact time with the engine they had never seen built. It watched the people move at that same tempo, faces still showing their original planes, clothes that never dried and never quite rotted, children occupying the intervals as if the intervals themselves were the only inheritance anyone needed. It noticed the thickening that sometimes remembered a shape inside the vents, and the way no one looked at it except the way one looks at a gauge.

A lesser kindness would have wanted to give them seasons, or night that truly fell, or a hinterland, or bread that did not taste of metal. A lesser wisdom would have seen the constant heat as a sentence and tried to commute it. This power was neither. It recognised a standing wave when it found one: perfectly reflected, perfectly contained, every surplus already returned, every deficit already filled by the same water lifted and used and given back. Nothing here was waiting. Nothing here was unfinished. The young rock did not need to weather. The steam did not need to lift. The circuit did not need a destination.

So the power did the one thing that would not revise the diagram.

It became another interval.

Not a visitor. Not a judge. Not even a guardian that would have to stand outside the rim and therefore introduce an outside. It simply occupied the same spare logic the buildings already used—the space between the notes of the slow machine. Where the steam thickened, it thickened a fraction longer, enough for the mineral air to hold the memory of a shape without ever becoming a figure that would demand to be named. Where condensate ran in thin endless threads down cooler brick, it ran with a barely perceptible extra patience, so that the algae films remained exactly the films they had already become. The beam engine’s pulse did not change its amplitude; it simply found, in the power’s attention, the same listening the people already practised when they rested a hand on hot iron only long enough to confirm what the ear knew.

The dogs continued to trot the boardwalks as if the basin were a single familiar room. The rats still drank condensate from their own whiskers. The grebes still surfaced empty-billed more often than not. The children still stood motionless in water that would blister anyone who had not been born inside the circuit. None of them were altered. None of them were rescued. They had never required rescuing.

If the people felt anything, it was only the continuation of what they already knew: the heat pressing equally from every direction, the sounds interlocking without remainder, the faint mineral taste that was simply the taste of remaining. Sometimes, in the short irregular watches that were not night, one of them would pause on an iron bridge and listen a moment longer than the valve required, as if an extra silence had seated itself between the clangs. Then they would oil the gland they had oiled yesterday and would oil tomorrow, and the moment would pass the way steam never quite clears.

The power stayed. Not because the place needed it, but because a completed sentence does not become less complete when it is read with love. It stayed the way the overcast stayed: already used, already the only light the basin would ever require. It asked nothing. It offered nothing that was not already there. In the deepest sense it did nothing at all.

And the basin, which had never needed to change, continued at the only rate it had ever known.
