The power arrives as a difference in the quality of the gray. Not light, not warmth, not even a thickening of the mist. It is a listening that occupies the same volume as the condensing air, the same routes as the steam-lines and the ivy. For a long interval that the place does not measure, nothing changes. Water continues to stand, to weep, to sheet. Roots continue their slow prying. A conveyor seizes and is accepted. The pale figures move barefoot along their catwalks, tasting iron and leaf, laying vine-wrapped wrenches against pipes that are also stems. The frogs remain silent. The heron spears its pale thing and is still again.

The power understands at once that this is not a ruin awaiting restoration, nor a garden awaiting a gardener, nor a machine awaiting a master. It is a completed weather. To dry a single basin would be to unmake a mere. To still a single engine would be to silence a pulse the frogs have already learned. To speak a word the inhabitants could carry farther than a drip would be to give them an outside, and they have none. They are already the correct density, the correct dampness, the correct quiet upheaval. Their language of pressures is sufficient. Their children are already born knowing the sound of a bearing invaded by root. Their dead already become silt in the geometrically perfect ponds.

So the power does the only thing that is both benevolent and wise: it remains. It adds no new steel, no new spore, no new song. It does not part the canopy or lift the perpetual dusk. It does not remind the ferrocrete of the highland that was poured and forgotten, nor the operators’ descendants of the world beyond the last terrace. It simply holds the entire interlocking pavement in a regard that is itself a kind of weather—cool, patient, without demand.

For a span of days measured only in the deepening and paling of the same gray, the mycelia glow a fraction more steadily when the mist thickens. The foxfire is no brighter, only more certain, as if the cold light has been seen and therefore need not prove itself. One of the thin figures, pausing on a gantry with a hand on a pipe that is also a stem, feels through the sole of a bare foot a creak that is not stress but acknowledgment. The figure does not look up. There is nothing to look up toward. They listen a moment longer than usual to the subsonic turning, then continue their loop of repair and surrender. The place breathes with them, as it always has.

After that the glow returns to its former faintness. The power withdraws the way the mist withdraws: not by leaving, but by becoming once more indistinguishable from the air, the drip, the rust, the leaf. It takes nothing and leaves nothing that can be pointed to. The equilibrium-storm continues, terrace after terrace, into the haze. The smallest lives still coat every joint. The moths still rest open and invisible. The people still harvest pith and condensate, still lay their dead in the reeds, still become another weather inside the larger one.

That is all. It is enough. The power, being wise, knows that some places are already the answer to the question of what they should be. Being benevolent, it loves them enough to let the answer stand.
