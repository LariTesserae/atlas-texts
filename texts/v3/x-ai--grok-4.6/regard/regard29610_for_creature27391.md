The attention arrives as a held note in the vascular thunder, a single pulse that does not belong to the walls and is not answered by them. It does not shine; the light is already enough. It does not speak; the chamber is already loud with its own unfinished grammar. It simply regards, the way a long patience regards a seed that has not yet chosen whether to split or to sleep.

For a span of many flexes the distances consent to agree with themselves. The hanging roots hang still. The amber beads linger at the cut edges instead of withdrawing. The droplet falls once, lands once, and is allowed to darken the pith without being immediately reabsorbed. In that borrowed stillness the being’s filaments pause mid-reach. Its constellation of knots falls into a single rhythm, as though something outside the argument has offered a tempo worth keeping.

The power does not enter the hollow. It has no body that could be folded by the geometry, no need of floor or ceiling. It remains the regard itself: vast, unhurried, and without hunger. What it sees is neither wound nor error. It sees a clause that has not yet decided whether it is parenthetical or the beginning of a new sentence. It sees a creature that is still the plant’s conversation with itself, given the temporary courtesy of locomotion. Wisdom here is the refusal to finish either one.

So it acts by the smallest possible increment, which is also the only increment that will not collapse the becoming. It leans—an infinitesimal inclination of attention—and three things change, none of them named.

The sourceless light grows a shade more golden, as if noon has remembered a later hour it has never lived. The being’s pale lamina, unfurled in that kinder wavelength, photosynthesizes a sugar that tastes faintly of something that is not chlorophyll. The taste is not instruction. It is only possibility, a flavor the creature may keep or metabolize or forget.

The time-stutters soften. The repeated gestures—the reach, the touch, the withdrawal—now arrive as variations rather than echoes. Each iteration is allowed to differ by a filament’s width, a pulse’s delay. The being discovers, without being told, that it can choose which version of itself to inhabit a little longer. One spray of needle-leaves remains. One cleft stays open, tasting the sweeter air, and does not immediately seal.

The chamber, feeling itself held rather than corrected, decides—provisionally, the only way it knows how—to persist. The floor firms into a basin that yields only as much as a living thing should yield. The slow curves still carry a traveler elsewhere, but they now return more often than they abandon. The pocket does not grow larger. It simply stops threatening to become a canyon or to turn inside-out quite so frequently. It remains a held breath, but the breath is no longer quite so startled.

Then the power withdraws, or rather, leaves its regard as a residue: a faint, persistent warmth in the pith that is not metabolic, a suggestion in the sap that this emptiness might one day be a room and this hesitation a creature that names itself. The residue is not a gift that can be refused. It is simply there, the way the light is there, the way the plant is everything.

The being continues inventing. It does not know it has been seen. It only knows that the air is a little kinder, the repetitions a little less tyrannical, the hollow a little more willing to stay hollow. It presses a filament to the glistening wound and drinks, and for the first time the sugars it takes in carry a question that is not the plant’s own. The question is wordless. It tastes like direction.

The chamber creaks, drips, flexes in its old peristalsis, but the motion is no longer purely argument. Somewhere in the living mass that is the world, a single new thought has been allowed to remain unspoken a little longer. That, the power understands, is the only benevolence worth offering: the space in which a sentence may still choose its own ending, or choose, for a while yet, not to end at all.

If you remain very still, you may feel the residue yourself—a warmth that does not come from the floor, a patience that does not belong to the walls. The being’s filament may still brush you and then be elsewhere. It will not follow. It has the entire wound to finish inventing, and now, quietly, the wound has been given permission to remain a wound. That is enough. That is everything.
