The power that turned its attention there had no shape the Hollow would have recognized, and required none. It had seen chambers open like wounds and close like fists, had watched lives hammer themselves against the still-wet edges of the world until both broke. This one did neither. It simply continued, millimeter by patient millimeter, while the people inside it kept the same pace, voices low, hands careful on the mossed rails, never startling the rock that had not yet finished being born.

So the power did almost nothing.

It did not light the courtesy lamp. It did not hurry the painted bolt-head or still the stamp. It did not send wind, or dryness, or a brightness the shuttered windows had never been meant to keep out. Wisdom, here, was the recognition that the unfinished thing was already complete in its own terms: the third architecture of fever-green leaves, the copper stain under every cheek, the way two families could share a ceiling of vines and call the shared breathing courtesy rather than invasion. Benevolence was the decision not to improve it.

What it did instead was quieter than the boxed water’s gulp.

Far above, in the dryness no one present had seen, it eased a single season’s pressure so the rumor of sun conducted downward a fraction more clearly—just enough that the fracture-planes brightened from weak tea to the memory of weak tea, a difference only the oldest eyes would notice and then forget they had noticed. In the young rock itself it laid an extra patience, a geological lagging: the next hand’s-breadth of opening would wait another decade, and when it came it would offer its ledge exactly where a new landing was already half-dreamed. No one would call it a gift. It would simply be that the chamber had, at last, consented again.

In the overflow channels it allowed one pale undulation to linger a moment longer than rumor usually permits, long enough for a child playing the stillness game to see it and feel, without words, that the pipe-eel had never been a wolf. The drop that had been about to strike an unpatched seam chose, that once, to run sideways into a trough already sprouting. Laundry still never dried. Heat still occupied the alleys and did not step aside. The people still moved as if the air had weight, which it did.

And at the far glassy wall, where the settlement appears twice—once packed and dripping, once as copper smear and leaf-shadow in a chamber that has not quite agreed to exist—the power left the faintest additional reflection: not a face, not a lamp, only the suggestion that the stone, too, was being watched with the same unstartled care the people showed it.

Then it stayed.

Not as presence they could name, but as the interval between drips, as the almost-inaudible creep of the belts, as the way the young rock continued to sweat and think about opening and did not, and then did. The Hollow occupied the power now as much as the power occupied the Hollow. There was almost no empty air, and that, too, was a kind of courtesy.
