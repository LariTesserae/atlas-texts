The power arrives the way the chamber itself arrived: not all at once, and not from any direction the seam can name.

It does not drop through the crack. The crack, for a span that the pocket experiences as both an instant and a long consideration, simply becomes more possible. The rind of pale mineral at its edges darkens by a single shade, as if it has at last decided it is neither salt nor bone but the memory of both. Grass-roots from the overworld hang a little farther into the light and then stop, thoughtful. Nothing is torn. Nothing is forced.

The sourceless glare notices first. It does not dim or brighten; it merely acquires a patience it did not know it lacked. Condensation that had been waiting on ledges begins, very slowly, to choose which way to fall. One drop, then another, detaches with a sound so small it is almost a courtesy. The sound is new. The chamber accepts it the way unfinished stone accepts a fingerprint: by keeping the impression without quite understanding the hand.

The animals do not flee. They have never learned flight as escape. The millipedes pause in their braids, not facing the thickening this time but simply continuing to exist in its vicinity, their shimmer of legs now carrying the faintest echo of the new drop. The moths are handed a current that is fractionally warmer. The midges relocate, as they always do, but the emptiness they leave behind fills a heartbeat sooner. The isopods around the palms of water feed as before, yet one shed skin, instead of mineralizing at once, remains translucent a little longer, like a page left open.

The watcher on the cornice opens nothing. Its omitted eyes remain omitted. Its extra shoulder-blades settle, as though the sketches of its torso have at last been allowed to rest against one another. When it extends its impossible tongue, the puddle it drinks from does not merely dimple; for the first time the water remembers it has a surface, and the memory lasts after the tongue withdraws.

The stacked stones—three, four, the count still eddying—cease to lean in a direction that is not quite down. They do not straighten. They simply become, for a while, a decision that no longer needs to apologize for itself. Beside them the parallel scratches, those bright wounds, do not close. Light still leaks from them, but the leaking is slower now, more like breathing than hemorrhage. A fifth mark appears, not cut, not added by any nail or tooth: a short, curved line that might be the beginning of a letter or the end of a smile. It glows no brighter than the others. It does not explain itself.

The hairline groove that had forgotten itself on the way to the seam remembers a little farther. It does not reach the crack. It does not need to.

The power does not speak. Speech would require a schedule the pocket has not yet agreed upon. Instead it does the one thing a wise and benevolent attention can do without theft: it stays. Not as occupant, not as judge, not as completion. It stays as the quality of being seen that does not demand the seen become finished in order to deserve the gaze.

In the isolated puddles the reflections change. They still show, sometimes, the larger, older cave with real shadows and the sound of distant water. But they also show this chamber exactly as it is—embryonic grain, sourceless wash, silent blizzard of midges, lichen the color of overexposure—and the sight is no longer a rehearsal for something else. It is permitted to be the thing itself. Blink, and the water is only water. Look again, and the permission remains.

A single true shadow is born, late and small, under the watcher’s lowest shoulder-blade. It does not know yet which object it belongs to. The chamber allows the confusion. The shadow is cool. It does not hurry.

The white vein on the ceiling advances by two crystals instead of one, then rests, shining with a weather that now includes the possibility of seasons it may never use. The hair-grass at the puddle’s rim leans, rights itself, and for the first time casts a filament of darkness no longer than an eyelash.

The power could close the seam. It could flood the pocket with ordinary night, ordinary time, ordinary hunger and rustle and splash. It could name every creature and thereby fix them. It does none of these things. Wisdom here is the refusal to finish what is still lovingly unfinished. Benevolence is the decision to let the damp keep finding whatever rests, to let the lichen consider new surfaces, to let a fifth stone appear or vanish according to a logic that is still being invented.

When at last the attention withdraws—not leaving, never quite leaving, only thinning to the same degree the glare once thickened—the chamber is not older. It is not younger. It is simply more able to continue. The entrance-seam is sometimes a crack, sometimes a pale oval, sometimes absent, as before. But the absence now contains a kindness, the way a held breath contains the knowledge that it may be released.

The millipedes resume their processions. The watcher breathes its soundless breath. A drop falls and is heard. The light sits in the air, still sourceless, still slightly wrong, still washing every surface that has not yet decided what it is.

And beneath the films of water, the next version of the room practices being true with a little more room in its schedule, and a little less loneliness in its glare.
