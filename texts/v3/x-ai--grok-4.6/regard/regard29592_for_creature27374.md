The power arrives as a difference so fine it could be mistaken for weather already accounted for. Not a being that lands, not a voice that speaks across the roar, but a slight, permanent alteration in the arithmetic of turgor and glare. The highland does not pause to notice; it has never paused. The felt continues its millimeter advance. The columns continue their private rebalancing. The keepers continue to press mouths to ears and listen with their teeth.

What the power does is subtract one frequency.

For a single circuit of the parasols—less than an hour of the long lukewarm day—the turbine scream is simply absent. The water’s chest-deep roar remains, the wind’s thin note in the furred cables remains, yet the hard rotating violence that lived in the enamel of teeth is gone. In that interval the keepers hear, for the first time in memory, the highland itself: not message, not instruction, only the continuous arrival of water over unfinished stone and the slower pulse of the columns drawing and releasing. They do not speak. Speech is still impossible. They stand in the intake halls with oil rainbows on their boots and feel the place they have been maintaining finally answer the question they no longer remembered asking.

When the scream returns it is no longer quite the same pitch. Something in the generators has been gently retuned, a bearing or a winding given a new tolerance that will last generations. The sound now sits inside the water’s roar the way a second heartbeat sits inside the first. The keepers do not name the change. Their clothes remain a second skin of wet felt. Their eyes remain narrowed against the plated light. Yet they begin, without discussion, to leave the nearer stations running a fraction slower, to let certain pipes silt, to allow the hanging gardens on the tramway cables to thicken until the cables themselves are only rumor. The machines do not stop. They become another kind of column: hollow, fluted, already wearing the same jade pelt, shedding their own thin electric weather into the general deafness.

Farther out, where the geology still steams, the living skin arrives three seasons earlier than it would have. No miracle of sudden green, only the felt finding the newest fractures already damp, already prepared. The pale amphibians appear in the first menisci as if they had always been waiting just beneath the surface tension. The adjusters, on the days the sky tears, make their minute corrections against a glare that now holds one extra, cooler highlight—the power’s only signature, visible solely at the angle where cuticle turns metallic.

The highland still does not know it is finished. It does not need to. The power, having done the least that wisdom required, withdraws into the same patience the stone has always shown. It leaves the place louder, brighter, more itself. The keepers will keep. The columns will turn. The felt will remember every plane. There is no hurry. Whatever comes next will learn the same saturation, the same refusal of quiet, and the new, quieter note that now lives inside the roar.
