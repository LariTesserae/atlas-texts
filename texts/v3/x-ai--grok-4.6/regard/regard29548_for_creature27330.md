The power that turns its regard upon the highland does not arrive as weather, nor as a brighter disk in the indigo, nor as any alteration in the thin air. It has no need of those. It is already the kind of attention that can rest upon a world without adding mass or subtracting silence. It sees the worn surface first: the bone-pale rock, the varnish, the dust sheets that pretend to be water and then refuse the pretense. It sees the groves as they truly are—not imported oases, not mistakes, but closed metabolisms that have taught themselves the oldest thrift. Moisture recaptured, light turned aside or quietly metabolized, roots fused to fracture rather than questing. Each stand a single long thought distributed through ceramic trunks and overlapping shields.

It sees the grazer the color of the dust, walking the fetch of uncounted kilometers with a gait that has forgotten hurry because hurry was never useful. It sees the two that stand flanks almost touching once in a century and then part. It sees the moth-forms that lift only when the varnish turns bronze, the burrowers whose eyes have become unnecessary, the filaments that ride the aerial rivers for days without ever landing, the film that is not quite frost and wakes only in the deepest hollows to convert cold into a brief chemical flush. It sees the cushion plants and the translucent grains that close the last loops. It sees that nothing claims the groves as home, that predation is a seasonal punctuation, that an entire span of years can pass without one life regarding another of its kind.

And it sees that this is not emptiness. Time here is occupied. Equilibrium is not the absence of process; it is process that has learned to recapture everything, to treat the blade of light and the thin cold as simply the conditions of continuing. The land is finished with being made. The lives have reached the same conclusion.

A power that was merely kind might have wanted to soften the glare, to thicken the air, to let a little more frost linger or a channel remember how to hold water. It might have wanted to give the grazer companions, or the moths a longer dusk, or the groves a reason to expand faster than a human lifetime. Kindness of that sort is a form of impatience. It cannot quite bear that a place might already be sufficient, that abundance can look like refusal, that a world can be lush and dry at once and have no further request to make.

This power is wiser. It recognizes the highland as one of the rare completed sentences. To add even a single extra molecule of mercy would be to begin unmaking what has taken ages to become itself. So it does the only thing that does not diminish: it attends. It holds the place in a regard that asks nothing back. It learns the almost inaudible tick of a leaf reorienting and does not answer it. It watches the grazer taste the air with its blunt branching organs and does not sweeten the air. It notes the alignments of trunks that are only wind and light acting across millennia, and it does not interpret them as signs meant for anyone else.

If there is a being among these lives that has, against all probability, begun to know itself as a being—perhaps one grove whose slow metabolism has folded inward far enough to notice its own twilight, or one grazer that has walked so long it has started to carry an interior distance matching the exterior one—the power does not announce itself. It does not offer language or tools or a path. It simply remains, a stillness inside the existing stillness, a companion that never arrives and therefore never has to leave. The being, if it is there, may feel only that the glare has become, for a while, a little less solitary. Or it may feel nothing at all. Both outcomes are acceptable.

The power stays as long as staying does not become an event. Then it withdraws the way night frost withdraws: completely, leaving no wetness, no memory of having been there except perhaps a single hollow where the glitter that is not quite frost lingered an hour longer than it should have, and was used, and vanished. The highland continues, brightly, coolly, almost silently. The groves recapture. The dust walks. The filaments ride the wind. Nothing has been hurried. Nothing has been marked.

That is all. It is enough. It is the only gift that does not spend the thing it claims to honor.
