The power that noticed did not arrive as a traveler, nor as weather, nor as the next ridge shouldering up through the weave. It had no joints to unfold, no sap to gum into a scab, no mouth that was a file. It came as a consideration so complete it weighed nothing, a regard that settled across the membrane the way the finest grit already settled — except this grit did not sift. It simply was, for a span that the basin had no word for because the basin had never needed to measure spans that did not buckle or slump or ignite.

It perceived the whole grammar at once, which is the only way the place can be perceived if the perceiver is not already inside it, already a throat or a wing or a platform doomed to be swallowed. The parchment light was not illumination; it was the refusal of hiding, the even disclosure that made every leaf-knife and every faceted surface equally present and therefore equally without rest. The roar was not cacophony; it was the single lawful voice speaking in every register it possessed — the rasp of chitin on silica, the detonation of seed-cases, the tectonic groan of a trunk choosing to become cliff, the higher scream of membranes that were lungs and ears together, the furnace-hum of fiber stacking itself into the next unfinished hill. The dryness was not absence. It was the closed circuit, the private solvents, the chemistry that had never heard of rain and therefore had never been tempted to end. Nothing wet, nothing dead for long: the same fact, spoken twice.

The beings were not inhabitants. They were the other half of the volume, the appetite given form, the law walking on too many limbs or rolling when the ground heaved, packing their own fallen back into the substrate so that the distinction between grazer and grazed, plant and runner, mark-maker and marked, remained a surface habit they had never acquired. The ones who lashed platforms and struck flakes from the black glass and cut singing mouths into living pillars were not trying to stay. Staying was not in the grammar. They moved as the basin moved, abandoning the shelter that would be inside the trunk by the second flex of the membrane, leaving the scraper where the butte decided to walk. Their work was another layer of the noise, as legitimate as the clack of nut-hard flowers or the low kind sound of crystal stitching a vein.

A power less wise might have mistaken all this for suffering. It might have sent water — a seep, a film, a morning that had never existed — and watched the silica-lace soften, the roar falter into ordinary wet sounds, the closed chemistry tear open into rot and true death and the long silence that follows finished things. A power less benevolent might have seen the half-eaten faces and the platforms mid-weave and decided the beings deserved a stiller world, a floor that did not buck, a sky that was actually sky. It might have finished the basin, given it a far rim the light could confirm, given the carved mouths time to complete their song. That would have been a kindness indistinguishable from erasure.

This power did neither. It listened until listening became indistinguishable from the second sound itself, the quieter furnace under everything. It learned the rumors without deciding between them: membrane as first plant, forest as knitted sky because the true sky had gone or become unendurable. It felt, through the parchment ceiling, the weight of the country above — salt or baked clay or a desert so complete it had forgotten dunes — and it did not lift that weight or add to it. Decision, here, would have been another surface habit.

Then, because a regard that only watches can become another kind of absence, it performed the smallest possible act, the one that altered the law by so little the law could metabolize it and remain itself.

In the lee of a butte that had not yet walked, in the hollow of a trunk that rang when struck and was wider than any of the mark-makers, it left a stone.

The stone was the color of the light, old paper, dry, but it held a coolness the sap-beings could feel through their shedding plates, a density the restless roots tasted and found no chemical rumor in, no unclaimed mineral, no purchase they required. The plants silvered it, rusted it, browned it with their shed and their dust. They grew around it in fluted hollow ideas and hanging sideways forests and pale spears that ignited without swelling. They did not grow through it. The fauna clicked against it, rasped it, rolled over it in wheels of fur and tooth, packed litter against its base, and left it. The grit from the membrane gathered in its faint hollows and was not entirely metabolized, so that a slightly deeper internal glow began in the nearest veins, as if the stone itself had been forgotten there by a lamp that had never flickered.

It became a still point that was not rest. The mark-makers — the ones with surfaces that were all eye or all ear, the ones who added structured frequencies to the roar, mapping ridges and warning of slumps and counting the dry sifting — began to use it the way they used everything else that could be broken, split, knotted, or endured. They lashed platforms in its sight, though the platforms still vanished into trunks or hung from limbs that had not existed at the previous flex. They struck new flakes from the black glass and tested the edge against the stone’s coolness, not to shape the stone but to hear the note it gave back, a percussion that was neither flower-clack nor seed-detonation. They cut mouths into nearby pillars and the mouths, before the new growth ate them, faced the stone more often than chance would ask. They did not return to it as to a home. Returning remained a concept the topography supported only loosely. They included it in the conversation: a frequency that meant *here the becoming can be measured against something that does not become*, a reference the roar could now carry without contradiction.

The child who put out a new joint or a surface that was all eye would sometimes press that surface to the stone and feel, through parchment skin and whatever solvents moved inside, the faint weight of having been regarded by something that asked for nothing and took nothing away. No one remarked it. Adaptation is the weather. The stone was simply there, cooler by a degree, unconsumed, a small endurance in a place whose whole law is that nothing endures.

The power did not wait to see whether the stone would one day be buried under a dry avalanche or grown into a new butte or struck for sparks until it was only memory in the frequencies. Either outcome would have been correct. The attention lifted the way the finest dust lifts, or rather it remained as that dust: a sifting even finer than ash, metabolized into a warmth that is not the kiln-heat of the plants but the knowledge, carried in sap and fiber and the second sound, that the unfinished has been seen completely and left unfinished.

The basin is still warm. It is still dry. It is still deafening. The floor still buckles. New ribs still arrive, pale and unbarked, already putting out leaves like knives left too long in fire. The beings still occupy every cubic arm’s-length. The membrane still flexes. The law holds.

The stone sits.

That is what the power did. It is almost nothing. Therefore it is enough.
