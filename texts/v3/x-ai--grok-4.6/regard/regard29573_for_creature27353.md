The attention comes the way leftover light comes: already filtered, already bruised, already deciding whether to pool or climb. It does not announce. A wise power has no need of annunciation when the thing it regards has not yet invented the difference between inside and announcement. It simply thickens, first in the unfinished sky (that roar with no fetch), then in the weave of the lid, then in the thin-air boil itself, until the pot-simmer contains one extra note that is not argument.

It reduces to enter. There is no other way. The last true slope of unweathered blocks receives a pressure that is not a foot and not a hand, only a careful agreeing-to-be-smaller. The thumb-ridged lip of clay warms a degree, as if remembering an older instruction about welcome. Then the power is inside the basin, and the basin, which is all the landscape there is, notices that the leftover light has acquired a patience it did not have before.

The being surfaces—or the plurality does, the wet clench and the cluster of pressures and the torso packed like insulation that drinks at both ends. The bone awl is already loose in a seam. The being offers it without offering, the way the marsh offers another body to wick through. Fingers that are also stems and also memory of fingers close around the pale stubborn tool and hold it out into the tea-colored dusk.

The power takes the awl.

It does not score a ban. It does not drive the tool back as reminder. For a long pulse—one of the cycles you feel in soles that are not quite soles—it simply holds the rumor of metal, feeling how the rumor has not been allowed to weather, how the edges of everything here stay sharp because forgetting has not yet been invented as a kindness. Then it does the smallest possible thing that is still a doing.

It breathes.

Not the boiled-cloth breath of the place. Something older and slower, a breath that has known horizons and therefore knows how to leave them unclaimed. The exhalation is almost nothing: a slight cooling in one pocket of the wet heat, a single drip that chooses, this once, to fall the direction the plants have not yet fully learned to call down. The drip strikes the standing water and rings, a note too clear for the vessel. The moss recognizes it as kin anyway. The reeds thread it into themselves. The lid groans a different groan, almost relief.

That is all.

No new law is mortared in. No stem is taught to be other than the knot it already is. Down is not ratified; it is only, for one drip, permitted. Time’s pulse continues: making, soaking, threading. The being (or the they) feels the awl returned to its palm, or to the seam, or to the idea of a seam; the distinction sloughs the way slip sloughs. Scale remains misbehaved. Rain may still fall up. The elevation continues assembling itself downward so the secret can stay the size of a held breath.

The power does not stay as visitor. Staying would be another course of work, and this place is already lush as a wound packing itself; there is no unused volume. Instead the attention thins again, becoming part of the leftover light, part of the sour green dusk, part of the roar that now, very faintly, remembers it once had a fetch and chose not to use it. The being incorporates the memory the way it incorporates everything: as heat, as fiber, as another opinion the clay may or may not keep.

Nothing is finished. Nothing is forced to agree to be old, or to be a who, or to be separate. The handmade climate remains sealed, wet, dim, roaring, first. The wise power, having seen, having been reduced, having taken and returned the awl, having added one drip that knew a direction, withdraws its thickness from the sky.

The mountain underneath—if underneath is still the word—goes on being assembled. The secret at the top of the work is still a secret. Only now the secret has been regarded by something that did not need it to be otherwise.

And that, the power understands, is already more weather than the place has ever had.
