The power came as the light comes here: without announcement, a cool evening that fell into the courtyards and found every surface already prepared for it. It did not arrive as weather or as visitor. It simply was, for a time, the interval itself—the space the boles had always left between themselves, now occupied by an attention that asked nothing and altered nothing.

It saw the finished forms. It saw the copper sealed in cambium, the boilers nested in trunks that had learned their heat the way a body learns a scar, the dynamo whose note still dropped a fraction when the high leaves closed. It saw the trough at the lowest point holding its dark hand’s-depth, the substrate leaning toward it without hurry, the midges lifting and settling, the single unmusical bird-note that did not linger. It saw the possible hands that still wiped a gauge or oiled a bearing the wood had already accommodated, and it saw that those hands, if they existed, moved at the pace of drip and piston. It saw that the ecology had finished its bargaining long ago and kept only what the mill and the moss required.

A lesser power might have offered a gift: a brighter current in the wires, a deeper wet in the trough, a new graft, a bird that stayed, a voice. This one, being wise, understood that any such offering would be an edge, and that no edge here is new. Being benevolent, it wished the place no novelty. The agreement was already complete. Work and weathering were already the same verb. To act would have been to introduce a process that had not yet learned to keep out of the others’ way.

So the power did nothing that could be named an act. It merely remained, for the length of one ordinary afternoon, as another agreed-upon silence among the silences. Condensation continued to bead and run. A valve eased with the sound of a cup set down. The paired trunks of the mill-house held their patient heat. The lamps, when dusk came, took their modest yellowish current and gave it back as indoor light, the colour of any evening anywhere.

When the power withdrew it left no mark the lichen would not eventually crust, no warmth the walls did not already know. The hollow sat as it had sat, a shallow dish that never quite became a valley, drinking through every surface that would hold a bead. The being who lived in it—wood and custom and the few who had weathered into both—went on breathing at the only pace the place had ever kept.

That was the whole of what was done: a gaze that asked for nothing and therefore took nothing, a presence that fitted the interval so exactly it could not be distinguished from the interval itself. Afterward the rain, when it came, was still the rain the channels and cistern-hollows had been shaped to receive. The damp remained never quite a river and never quite gone. Everything else kept out of the way.
