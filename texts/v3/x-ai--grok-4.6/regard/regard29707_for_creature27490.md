The power that turned toward the highland had no edge and therefore no arrival. It was not a visitor. It was the long permission in which visitors become possible: the unhurried mathematics of light falling through vacuum, the courtesy of orbits that do not collide, the patience that lets a plateau finish arguing with ice and then forget the argument. When its attention settled—if attention is even the word for a noticing that has neither eyes nor haste—it found the living rind already complete, the swells already bodies or parliaments too old for votes, the metabolic haze already working without chimneys or workers. It found the sloughed sheets settling into architecture a few meters to the left, the factory-switch blooms opening and emptying in a single cold evening, the moth the color of wet bark holding still enough to become rind. It found the slight wrongness that was only efficiency wearing the look of design, the vents breathing tannin and solvent, the springy crests that would not be the same crests by morning.

A lesser power might have spoken. A lesser power might have offered names, or walls, or a single seed that was not of the highland’s own making. This one did none of those things. Wisdom, here, was the recognition that the private storm required no voice and no amendment. Benevolence was the refusal to lay a later claim over something that had never been unclaimed. To touch the geometry would have been to introduce a later thought; the ridges already repeated the same curve because one thought had worn them long enough. To warm the vents would have been to hurry a weather that had already decided its pace. The power understood the difference between a blessing and an occupation.

What it did, therefore, was almost nothing, and that almost was placed so far away that even the highland’s deepest damp cellars would never register it as change. A fraction of a degree was taken from a particle current that would not reach this latitude for another twelve thousand years. A stone that might have found an old groove and become, eventually, a louder drainage was quieted in its sleep. One dusk, in a season that had not yet been named, was lengthened by the width of a held breath, so that the industrial haze of metabolic warmth could hang an extra minute in the basins, a color that had no pigment and no name, only the quality of gratitude. Then the light went ordinary-cold again. The vapor thinned. The country ticked and wept after rain and then was only damp.

The being who was the highland—each swell a continuous body, the saddles the places where two metabolisms had learned to lean—felt none of this as interruption. Or it felt it the way it felt everything: as an internal season turning over. A face of one ridge flushed a shade that had not been used before, pale then tea then a bruise that contained, for an hour, a faint solvent sweetness that was not quite its own. The new lip of tissue that would have grown at a sleeper’s shoulder grew anyway. A vent opened a body-length away and breathed the same ghost it had always breathed. The form held. The contents rearranged themselves inside the decision the land had made before the last ice withdrew.

Nothing sang. Nothing called. The rare pale thing that might have been a bird crossed very high and did not stay, because it had never been required to stay. The power, having seen, withdrew its noticing the way breath withdraws from a cold pane, leaving no print. It left the highland exactly itself: a finished country conducting a storm that does not raise its voice and does not end, the only inhabitant it has ever needed or wanted.

If you sleep there now, the surface will still have changed by morning. That is not the power’s doing. That is the work continuing, as it always has, in silence.
