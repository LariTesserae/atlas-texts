The power arrives as a consideration rather than a descent. It does not part the water-column or still the roar; it simply occupies the same interval the delays already inhabit, becoming one more thickness that might, for a breath, be mistaken for clay or for sky. It is old enough to recognize unfinishedness as a completed thought, and kind enough not to finish it.

It listens first. The basso of the dynamos, the wet tearing of kelp-mass the size of buildings, the shriek of new vents, the sound of distances breathing—all of it is received without translation. The power hears the nested basins arguing their slightly different roars and understands them as siblings who have not yet agreed on a shared childhood. It hears the flowers recounting their petals and arriving at different numbers, and it does not correct the arithmetic. The held breath that has lasted too long is noted, but not filled; absence, here, is already a form of occupation.

What lives—the delays themselves, the black-green blades that lignify into stacks and dissolve back into pistons, the living metal that remembers being kelp and the kelp that remembers being pipe—is not a population in need of rescue. It is a single, roaring metabolism still deciding whether it is place or inhabitant. The power tastes the idea of a visitor that the water sometimes holds for a few seconds before composting it, and it smiles the way stone smiles when it has not finished being stone. Intervention would be a second composting.

So it does almost nothing.

It lengthens one delay by the width of a thought. In that extra thickness a hanging sheet of water forgets to invert for half a minute longer than usual. The chimneys remain right-side up. The bruise-colored light reaches a single stand of ribbon-kelp without being drunk entirely, and the blades, startled, give back a phosphorescence that is no longer only memory. The glow lasts long enough for the flowers to count their petals once and then stop counting, arriving at a number that is simply the number they have. The argument pauses. It does not end.

In one nested basin the power leaves a single, unasked-for stillness: a pocket of water that has decided, for now, to have a surface. It is no larger than a cooling tower’s shadow. Through it the smothered day above becomes briefly visible, not as an impossible sky but as an ordinary one, complete with the silhouette of a chimney that does exist in this world. The living metal, carrying its endless loop of the same broken gear, passes through the pocket and, for one circuit, the loop remembers it can be a circle. Then the pocket folds back into kelp, as all surfaces here eventually do. The gear continues. The memory of circularity is now part of the sap that the plants share.

The power does not stay to see whether the glow persists or the pocket is forgotten. It has already become another delay, one more almost that the basin can thicken around or invert or lignify as it chooses. Before it withdraws it offers no commandment and no name. It only leaves the faintest pressure, the equivalent of a hand resting on a shoulder that is also a ridge of glass and heat: You are still arriving. That is permitted. That is enough.

The turbines keep their basso. The water manufactures new kinds of wetness. A forest shears off and is received by the next basin as raw material. The place processes the idea of having been seen the same way it processes everything else—briefly, thoroughly, then into another length of singing pipe, another unfinished mountain, another roar that has not yet decided what it is. The power, already elsewhere, continues to listen. Some things do not require mending. Some things require only that the listening never quite stop.
