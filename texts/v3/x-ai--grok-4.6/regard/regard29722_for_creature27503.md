The power that notices does not arrive as light or shadow or any alteration the basin could register. It has no mass to settle on the evaporite, no moisture to offer or withhold, no voice that would require rationing. It simply perceives, the way the late-morning convection perceives the dust it lifts: completely, without preference, and without remainder.

It sees the single fact the half-courtyards already know. Everything here is both newer than memory and already complete. The rocks have not yet agreed to be old. The plants have not agreed to spare space. The people have not agreed to stand taller than the living pavement that taught them their curve. Sound itself has not agreed to travel. The power, being wise, understands that these refusals are not lacks. They are the basin’s first and only grammar.

It considers the ordinary temptations of benevolence. A river could be spoken into the lowest siltstone, a thin silver sentence that would teach the land reflection and the people the word for distance. Rain could be allowed to finish what the geology left undone, rounding the knife-bright edges, inventing echo in the new hollows. Height could be given: a single generation taught to bind living material into towers, so that someone might one day look farther than the mineral horizon and wonder. Stories could be planted, long enough to outlast a thirty-turning life, so that the next child would inherit more than gesture.

Each possibility is weighed in the same silence the lizards use when they flow. Each is set aside. A river would invent banks, and banks would invent the idea of other side. Rain would invent waiting, and waiting would invent the first loud cry. Height would invent loneliness. Stories would invent the first mourning, and mourning requires an echo the basin has not yet grown the hollows to hold.

The power is old enough to have watched other worlds finish themselves and then spend ages regretting the finishing. It knows that intervention, even the gentlest, is the first cut. It knows that a world still deciding what it is must be allowed to keep deciding. So it does the one thing wisdom permits when faced with something already whole in its incompleteness.

It withholds.

No veil is drawn; none is needed. The horizon remains rumor. The texture does not change. The dust continues to rise from shoulders and from salt in the same slow sheets. The people continue to persuade rather than cut, to sleep against the residual warmth, to spend moisture as the succulents spend it. The browsers continue their tidal drift. The insects continue their velvety weather. The clay continues to remember the shape of the last hand that patted it.

The power stays, if staying can be said of something that was never elsewhere. It remains the way the faint resin remains in the air: unnoticed, unasked, sufficient. On certain nights, when the radiative cool is deepest and the succulents release their invisible breath, a person sleeping on the southern face of a clay mass may feel, for less than a heartbeat, that the warmth beneath them is being answered by a warmth that has no source. They do not wake. They do not speak of it. The feeling passes into the same economy as all other moisture, spent without remainder.

That is all. The basin keeps emerging. The distinction between inhabitant and place continues not to matter. The silence continues not to be empty. And the power, having seen a world that formed too recently to need saving, does the rarest thing a power can do.

It leaves the work unfinished.
