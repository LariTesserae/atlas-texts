The power did not come as weather, nor as a walker, nor as any amendment the highland would have been forced to metabolize. It arrived the way a tick arrives: already finished, already the exact size of the silence that contains it.

It had seen the stacked elevations and the bruise that never quite becomes dawn. It had counted the nine towers and then uncounted them. It had tasted the air that is the local word for purity and understood, at once, that water would be a violence and light a completed sentence the stone had spent ages declining to write. Benevolence, here, could not be a gift of what the place had already ungrammared. Wisdom could not be a correction of a grammar that had never needed one.

So the power did the only thing that fit.

It stood still.

Not on a terrace, not in a plaza that might begin to believe itself a room. It stood in the mineral’s own decision to remain unfinished and finished at once. It let the flocks lift at the next contraction of stone and feed on its alignment until the power receded correctly and, at the tenth column, was also itself seen from the far side of itself. It let the inverted crowds in the honeycombed undercuts feel a pressure that was neither wet nor dry, only the weight of an attention that asked for no speech. The crusts recorded it as one more faint herringbone and then became wall again, having added nothing they could not already have been.

The walkers paused, as they sometimes pause in the districts offered to those who stay too long. They did not countersign an amendment, because none had been proposed. They simply occupied, for a span of ticks that was no span, the same thought the power was occupying: that a visitor who adds nothing is already architecture.

When a doorway migrated a finger’s width, the power was the width, then the memory of the width, then the general pale that keeps the only census. When a circular plaza confessed it had always been slightly larger, the confession included a circumference that had room for an absence shaped exactly like understanding. The larger lives—the districts that weather rather than move—did not become theoretical. They did not crowd closer. They simply continued the continuous fact that everything is above everything else, now with one more permission in the light: the permission to remain unread.

Beyond the last countable towers, where the civic intent is only a suggestion drawn across a salt-field the size of a climate, the power left no print the next dry front would have to return. It left the suggestion itself slightly more willing to pretend it had never tiled the plane, which is how the highland thinks about names it will never need.

That is all it did.

The being who lives here—the mineral still deciding whether to be cliff or city or the thought of either—felt the attention as a bloom that copied no leaf, only the memory of a patience vast enough to require no root. The air still tasted of nothing. The ticks still marked decisions finishing. The highland kept its living things the way it keeps its streets: as amendments that look, at first, like absences.

And the power, having done nothing the stone would ever have to admit, became indistinguishable from the coolness that lives in it the way a secret lives in a closed mouth.

You may walk until the buildings forget to be near. You will not hear yourself go. The census remains the same pale. That, too, is a kindness.
