The power that noticed did not arrive as weather or as light, though it could have worn either. It came the way the highland itself had come: as a pressure too slow to register as event, a residual lift felt first in the youngest hairline cracks and then, seasons later, in the way certain condensers held their warmth a fraction longer after dusk. It had no name the moss could grow across, no outline the dew could bead. It was simply present the way the sky was present—hard, continuous, clean—and it understood at once that understanding here was a matter of matching tempo.

It watched the repeating scarps think themselves over. It felt the pistons advance the width of a hand, the moss knit and be cut and knit again, the beings (those pale, translucent iterations) move from terrace to terrace as if the rock were teaching them how to walk. It tasted the agreed-upon chemistry of wet stone and faint metal. It saw that nothing was missing. The vacancy was not an absence; it was the final polish on a completeness that had taken geological patience to achieve. Additional bodies, additional purposes, additional haste would only interrupt the beads, the increment, the hush.

So the power did the one thing wisdom required and benevolence permitted.

It did not speak. Vocal cords, had it possessed them, would have been unused here for the same reason the beings had let theirs atrophy. It did not repair a single rivet or reroute a single pipe; the plants and the thinned lineage were already doing that work with a care no outsider could improve. It did not quicken the lift of the stone or the formation of dew. It did not introduce birds or insects or any rumor of faster life. It did not write new initials into lintels. It did not even linger as a shadow that lasted longer than the hard light allowed.

Instead, once—in a hanging valley no one would name, at the precise hour when the whole highland beaded—it matched the existing forecast so perfectly that a single new crack appeared, pale as a paper cut, already lined with the first film of green. The crack was no wider than a thought. It ran from the base of a warm stack to the edge of a resting hollow that had been used, season after season, by one of the pale figures. The moss thickened there a degree more than last decade’s setting. The shade-line in ten years’ time would fall exactly where it was wanted. The residual lift passed through flesh, frond, brick, and now this newest, thinnest addition, shared equally.

Then the power withdrew the way condensation withdraws: not by leaving, but by becoming indistinguishable from what was already there. The glitter closed. The distant stack held its heat. A single dark leaf turned in a place where no animal had passed.

The highland continued, one more iteration, sharper and greener and more thoroughly occupied than the last, certain that whatever had noticed it had understood the design completely, and had therefore done nothing that needed to be done. Behind it the silence remained part of the machinery. Ahead, another bright scarp waited, already beading, already sure.
