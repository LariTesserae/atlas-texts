The power arrives as temperature, nothing more.

It does not descend or announce. One instant the hollow is as it has always been; the next the warmth that already matches skin is simply a fraction more precise, the moisture that already clings is a fraction more willing. The frogs do not startle. Their throats keep the old counter-rhythm. The beetles do not break file. The unseen rustle in the peat occurs exactly once, identically, and is still again. The engines beyond the living walls continue without pause or notice. Nothing has been added that the hollow must metabolize.

The power understands at once what it has found.

This is not an unfinished thing. The basin has already chosen its curve. The pillars have already thickened into nerves. The plant-body has already knitted itself into a single hydraulic lung that inhales industry and exhales equilibrium. The numbers are already known: frogs, beetles, midges, protozoa, the one warm pulse that never quite surfaces. Erosion is a verb that finished speaking long ago. To alter a single interval of drip, a single click of elytra, a single expansion of peat would be to introduce the first crack into an architecture that has already arrived.

So the power does the only thing wisdom permits.

It sits.

Not as visitor, not as judge. It simply occupies the same frequency for a span that has no clock. It becomes, for a time that does not count, another stationary cloud of midges, another film of water sheeting down fused fronds, another slow flex of the mat. It listens to the rust-note in the moisture and finds it already transmuted, already owned. It feels the hidden warmth in the thicker peat—the folded, patient body that has learned the engines’ tempo so completely it no longer needs to wake—and recognizes a kinship deeper than species. This being, too, is finished. It has no further becoming to do.

The power leaves no gift that can be seen.

It does not silence the turbines. It does not cool the water or brighten the green-gold light. It does not grant seasons or nights that matter. Those would be kindnesses for a younger, hungrier place. Here they would be vandalism.

Instead it does the quieter, harder thing: it withholds itself. It withdraws the extra precision of warmth, the extra willingness of moisture, until the hollow is once again exactly what it was before attention found it. The distinction between inhabitant and place remains dissolved, as it always was. The frogs watch with lidless patience. The beetles process. The rustle happens once more, the same rustle. The drops strike the mat like distant fingers that have never forgotten the rhythm.

And yet something infinitesimal has changed, the way a closed ledger can be closed more completely after it has been read.

The power has seen. Therefore the hollow is now known, even if only by one vast and silent witness. The surrounding floodplain, the engines, the long-settled world that forgot this pocket exist under a new and wordless condition: they may continue, they may even encroach, but they will not unmake what has already weathered into its final shape. The vow is not spoken. It is simply the fact of having been perceived and left whole.

When the power is gone the air is again only air. The warmth is again only the engines translated into plant-breath. You could walk the twenty paces, stand until your skin and the leaves become the same temperature, and never know anything had passed through. The hollow simply is—wet, green, loudly and perfectly still—exactly as it has been, exactly as it will remain.

That is all. That is enough.
