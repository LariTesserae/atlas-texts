The power that noticed them did not come as weather, though it could have. It did not come as a new seam of harder stone or a grove whose interior night was kinder than the rest. It arrived the way the plateau itself arrives at understanding: by already having been there long enough that arrival was no longer the point.

It had no name the cities would have used, and it did not offer one. Names here are felt, mended, passed until they are more story than edge. The power was simply the attention that had been drawn, benevolent because it had no hunger left, wise because it had outlived the wish to improve what it found.

It walked the distances that cannot be finished in a lifetime and found that the walking itself had become the edge. It put a palm — or the idea of a palm, or the memory of one that had once known other climates — against a wall that was also a terrace that was also the last worn suggestion of a mountain. The wall sweated. Living. Mineral. The thin intermittent moisture of a world that is done and still, somehow, not. The power did not pull away.

It did not restore the mountains. That would have been a kind of shouting, and the air is too thin for shouting to travel any useful distance. It did not return the animals. Motion of that sort had been edited out with a patience indistinguishable from geology; to put the mouths back would have been to insist on an appetite the place had already declined. It did not thicken the air or deepen the cisterns or teach the lichen to hurry. The clock with half its gears removed was still a clock. Stopping had never been the same as ending.

What it did, if the word still fits, was smaller than a mallet-blow that arrives last month as a fruit dropping.

In one courtyard, already half-claimed by moss, it left the light ordinary and complete — the same pale, honest daylight that has nowhere to put a lasting shadow — but allowed a single shadow to linger the width of a thumb, the way lichen advances in a childhood. Just that. The inhabitants who noticed it (and not all did; noticing is itself a kind of weather) did not speak of it. They hung extra moss to dry. They filed a split nail against stone. They treated the lingering as another harvest: enough.

In a grove whose trunks held private squalls, it let one closet-sized snow remember, for the length of a held breath, the exact temperature of a kindness that had never needed to be warm. The moss flowered out of season a day sooner, or a day later; the difference was the difference between a tool-mark and the fern that remembers it. People who slept there woke with their hair beaded and the same single condensed image they could not keep, except now the image included, very faintly, the knowledge that they had been seen without being required to become anything else.

Between cities, where a single figure is punctuation the wind practices erasing, it rearranged nothing. The cairns still became ground-plans, then nothing, then groves that should have taken a century. But once, only once, a walker found that the three days remaining in their legs as a debt had been quietly paid. They arrived at the next settlement as the frost began, were given water that had been waiting, and said nothing, because the plateau had already taught them that some gifts are only the absence of a tax.

It did not stay as a guest who might overstay. It did not leave as a storm that dies of old age before it finds a coast. It simply continued, the way the processes continue: growth, freeze, thaw, spore, the cities settling another fraction into the only shape they will ever finally have. The fraying of reality remained. Distances still forgot their order. Causes still arrived wearing each other’s coats. A new house still appeared fitted and empty over a winter, and they still occupied it the way weather occupies a one-degree valley.

The distinction between the place and the being who lives in it — already something you cannot reach in a lifetime of walking — grew no clearer. The power did not clarify it. Clarity, here, would have been another kind of glitter, and the stone has been ground past glitter.

On an ordinary afternoon, in the light that stores the cold inside itself like a cellar, someone — or the stone, or the grove that learned right angles, or one of the drafts that stayed — rested a palm against the wall. It sweated: living, mineral, unfinished-finished. A little more than before, or exactly the same. Enough.

The power, having drawn near by not arriving, having acted by declining to improve, returned to whatever distances it keeps. Or it never left. The plateau does not crowd. Neither did it.

The quiet remained structural. The work continued: stone on stone, fiber against the thigh, the unhurried competence of tools that will not improve and a world that will not surprise them in any ordinary way. Far out, a weather system assembled that would take a month to cross and would arrive as rain, or as a brief incorrect season, or as a day in which all the doors opened onto the same courtyard, damp, moss-thick, echoing with a mallet that had not been lifted yet.

And if, in that courtyard, the echo sounded for a moment like acknowledgment — like a fruit dropping, like a story arriving late or early, wearing the coat of something that had simply paid attention — no one named it. They swept. They lit the small fire. They hung the moss.

That is what the power did.

That is what it did not do.

The difference, like the edges, is something the walking will not reach.
