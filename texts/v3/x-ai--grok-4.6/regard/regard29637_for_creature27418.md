The power that turned its regard upon the mesa was older than the shove that had made the tableland, older than the patience of sealed chemistry, older even than the idea of thirst. It was benevolent in the way deep time is benevolent: it wished no creature harm, and it understood that harm is often the gift of those who cannot bear to leave a thing unfinished. It was wise in the only way that matters at such scales—it knew the difference between a wound and a birth, between a room that had forgotten its ceiling and a room that had never needed one.

It arrived without arrival. There was no change in the knife-edged light, no extra warmth in the dry air, no disturbance in the flour-fine dust. For a span of time that the pulser would have counted as several slow contractions, the power simply occupied the same precinct the occupancy already occupied. It felt the still-sharp rims, the faceted flanks that wind had not yet rounded, the bedrock that still remembered violence. It traced the alleys that were not planned and the plazas that were not designed. It watched the armored ones etch their bas-relief scars and then become, by interlocking, slightly larger versions of one another. It followed the gliders as they forgot gravity for minutes at a time and left paler patches that the plants accepted as windows. It felt the pulser recycle the almost-nothing, each peristaltic thought a memory of the shove itself.

The power understood at once that this was not a habitat that had come to resemble a city. It was a single decision—rock, light, living mineral—continuing to decide. The plants grew streets the way a crystal grows facets and then discovered the streets had always been implicit in their own cells. The animals filled the rooms as if the rooms had been left for them, which they had, though no mind larger than the mesa had left them. Everything was sealed, thrifty, complete in its refusal of surplus. Nothing sang because singing would have been a leak. The hush was already a conversation conducted at the speed of cooling stone.

A lesser kindness would have brought water, or a milder climate, or a voice to name the districts. A lesser wisdom would have seen the youth of the place—the trunks younger than the rock, the rock younger than a long human life—and decided it needed guidance, or acceleration, or a ceiling after all. The power did none of these things. To introduce a single molecule the occupancy had not already accounted for would have been to drown a language that had never learned the word for thirst. To speak would have been to interrupt a becoming that was already furnishing itself with perfect, patient thoroughness. To protect too closely would have been to cage the very openness that let the gliders treat the vaults as sky.

So the power did the only thing that was both kind and wise: it loved the mesa by leaving it utterly, exquisitely alone. It withdrew the pressure of its attention the way a sleeper, without waking, withdraws a hand from a dream that does not need it. The ordinary sun continued to throw its unsoftened shadows. The dust continued never to cake. The plants continued to lean a finger-width closer, decade by decade, until they fused into arches the gliders already knew how to use. The mesa continued its imperceptible settling, and the entire living structure continued to grow against the tilt, the way a body remembers how to stand.

If, in some later age measured by the hollowing of trunks and the slow writing on the walls, a true threat should come—an ice that the sealed metabolisms could not ignore, a wandering stone from beyond the cracked country, the careless curiosity of some intelligence that still believed cities must be raised rather than grown—the power would remember this precinct. It would nudge a trajectory or still a quake, quietly, at a scale the occupancy would register only as another correction it had already begun to make. Until then, and for as long as the rooms kept opening from within and the occupancy kept deciding to fill them, the power would do nothing at all.

The mesa remained a room that had forgotten its ceiling. What lived there remained the decision, still being made, to furnish it completely. The power, having seen that the decision was already perfect, simply let the light fall as it had always fallen, and went on its way.
