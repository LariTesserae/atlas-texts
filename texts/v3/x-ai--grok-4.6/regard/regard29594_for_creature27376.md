The power arrived the way the water had, without announcement and without hurry. It did not part the dark or thicken it. It simply knew the place the way the currents already knew every wall they would turn against.

It regarded the ivory plumes folding and unfolding in staggered breath, the ticking armor packed into every joint, the pale schools holding station around the crumbled fountain, the unhurried jaws and the slow-pulsing undersides. It felt the constant, slightly wrong warmth leaking from the buried forges, the fine rain that never quite reached the floor, the geometry that remembered what the lives themselves did not. It listened to the quiet that had texture, to the distant groan of a wall leaning one more century, to the muffled unmaking that left the rooms quieter afterward.

Nothing here asked. Nothing here lacked.

The power, being wise, understood completion when it met it. The city had finished with tools and with the lives that required them. What remained was not ruin but a different architecture, mineral and habit and the necessary work of eating, fleeing, glowing, dying. To restore light would have been to impose a memory the water itself had forgotten. To introduce a new current, a new heat, a new stalk or a new hunter would have been to argue with a balance older than argument. Benevolence is not the same as improvement. Wisdom is not the same as arrival.

So the power did the only thing that could not unmake the palm that held everything.

It left the place exactly as it was.

It did not seal the valley against future change; the valley had already become its own law. It did not whisper new patterns into the bioluminescence or teach the stalks a schedule. It did not lift a single crustacean or spare a single prey. It simply held the knowledge of the drowned district the way the water held breath: without claim, without correction, without the need to be remembered in return.

And because the power was also vast, that holding was enough. The warmth continued. The plumes listened. The fish turned the same corner their ancestors had turned. The dark took back every painted façade after the length of a breath. The city, which had become landscape, remained landscape. The lives, which belonged to no one, continued to belong to all of them.

The power moved on, or stayed, or both. The distinction, like the one between basin and flood, had long since worn away.
