The power does not arrive.

It has no need of arrival. It has been the thinness of the air for longer than the last ice, the pewter that midday never quite becomes, the damp that never quite leaves the stone. What draws it is not novelty and not pity. It is recognition: here is a work that has already finished arguing with itself and has settled, without drama, into the labour of remaining.

It considers the western lip, the acre-by-acre concession, the empty windows looking into white. It considers the fused cellars where brick and root and calcium have forgotten which of them began the sentence. It considers the moths uncoiling their tongues on the condensers, the hares dissolving sideways, the pale thread in the thigh-deep hollows, the people whose palms rest against walls until the pulse that is not quite theirs finishes recognising them. It considers the moss advancing its finger’s width after every wet, and the city simply building a little farther back.

A lesser kindness would have brought fire, or a brighter sky, or a river that remembered how to be a river. A lesser wisdom would have called the slowness a wound and tried to close it. This power does neither. It understands that the argument is over. To change the terms now would be to start the mountains again, and the mountains have already had their say.

So it does the one thing that does not disturb the last shape.

It remains.

Not as a visitor. Not as a gift that must be unwrapped and therefore altered. It simply continues to be the conditions in which the plateau is possible: the close cool, the wool ceiling, the water that is a behaviour rather than a body. Where a condenser beads, the power is the bead a fraction longer before it falls. Where a hare’s ear lies flat, the green closes a fraction more seamlessly. Where a hand rests on fused grit, the pulse that answers is infinitesimally slower, so that the recognition may last the length of a breath that no clock here would count.

It does not stop the erosion. The table will still give itself, century by century, to the drop. The city will still adjust. The green will still occupy whatever is left. The people will still become cooler, damper, quieter, more exactly themselves. The power only ensures that nothing from beyond the worn lips of the bowl will hurry them, or name them unfinished, or ask them to be mountains again.

On certain nights when the cloud sits so thick it has furniture, a child walking home by the feel of pipes may sense, just once, that the pewter light has a regard in it. The child will not speak of it. No one here speaks of such things outdoors. They will only walk a little more slowly, hand over hand, and the drip will sound, for that stretch of street, like something that has decided to keep them.

That is all.

The plateau erodes. The city adjusts. What lives here occupies whatever is left. The power, having seen that this is already the work of lasting, does not add a single gesture that is not already the gesture of the place itself. It stays. It witnesses. It lets the last shape remain the last shape.

And the moss, unhurried, continues.
