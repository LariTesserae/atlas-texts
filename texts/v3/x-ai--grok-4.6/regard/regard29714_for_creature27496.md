The pressure arrives differently this time. Not the familiar cool that waits in cellars and under stones, but a stillness so complete it has weight of its own, a listening that has traveled farther than the rim. It does not descend so much as recognize. The basin is already known to it: the long-collapsed vaults, the gypsum throwing light back until air itself is overexposed and cool, the salt that heaves by a fingernail each year, the people the color of recemented grit who restack what refuses to stay aligned, the pale constellations of animals that pour through corridors as weather, the colorless smear in the joints that is all the green the chemistry will permit. The power—benevolent, wise, without haste—stands in the glare that is not heat and feels the pact of near-silence close around it like another kind of stone.

It watches a morning film bead and be drunk. It watches a wall lean the thickness of a thought and a pair of hands, mapped with callus, chock it with a stone chosen by touch alone. It watches children read the new grain of the herds and relocate their work to the next empty room without complaint or wonder. It watches the bazaar condense and thin. It watches night hold the day’s white inside the mineral while condensation begins its quiet ticking in the joints. Nothing here is broken in a way that asks for mending. The labor is the weather. The silence is the law the air will still return. The slow catastrophe is the only form that has ever finished dying here and become, instead, a kind of living.

A lesser kindness would have brought rain that remembered itself as season, or a seed that remembered leaf, or a stillness that ended the heaving so the lintels never needed names. Wisdom sees what that would cost. Color would become a wound the light did not know how to bleach. Sound would walk farther than the basin allows and find nowhere to stop. Purpose would evaporate with the last unrestacked wall. The people would have to learn tan, or flush, or the idea that work might one day cease. The animals would have to invent crying. The smear would be forced to become more than afterthought, and in becoming more it would end.

So the power does the one thing that does not argue with the place. It remains a little while, long enough for the cool to notice it has company, long enough for one additional tick of water to sound in a joint that had not expected it. Then it withdraws the same way it arrived: as a change in pressure so slight the ears of the basin barely register it. No mark is left that the salt cannot wear soft again. No sphere is added to the scree. The light stays solid, the shadows short and blue and indecisive, the channels too wide for what they carry. The rebuilders go in under hide and stone. The herds keep moving. The city remains only its plan, its quiet labor, and the cool that has been waiting the whole time—now, perhaps, infinitesimally less alone, though no one will ever have cause to say so.

That is all. It is enough. The basin, which forgot its origin as cavern so long ago that forgetting became weather, accepts the visit the same way it accepts everything: without comment, without needing to keep it.
