The power came as a lessening.

Not a lessening of the water, nor of the stone’s long decision, nor of the single note that sometimes lives in the jaw. It came as a lessening of the necessity that anything further be done. It arrived the way silt arrives when a gallery forgets to move: slowly, without announcement, drawing a faint grammar across the already-finished floors and then letting the next pulse take even that.

It looked for a long time.

It watched the lingerers hang in their private mathematics of foxfire, each one a pale delay the current had not yet remembered to collect. It watched the filterers remember themselves across torn throats, pressing the world’s last flour into still finer flour so that the next sheet might live a little longer in the same patience. It felt the colder things occupy the drop-shafts the way absence occupies a mouth, drinking pewter and returning nothing, leaving behind only a colder dust. It saw the slack drawings thicken into eyes that were only geometry and then unthicken again, and it saw that the stone recorded none of it, and that this too was already correct.

The power understood that the plateau had reached the only weather it would ever need. Purpose and ruin had long ago become the same current. To add a leaf would have been to insult the mineral. To add a tool would have been to pretend the work was unfinished. To still a cataract or warm a shaft or give the lingerers a reason to school would have been to start the world again, and the world here had already ended in the only way that still allowed it to continue.

So the power did the one thing left that was neither interference nor neglect.

It became, for a little while, another kind of stillness. In one vast nave where the ceiling was only rumor, it let the water hold its ghost floor-plan a few heartbeats longer than the pulse required. Circles stayed circles. Overflows stayed the idea of overflow. The lingerers, the banners, the slow eclipse in the central well, even the cloud that was already animals turning—all of them occupied the same volume without quite vanishing into the next dark. Distances, for that interval, told a slightly smaller lie.

Then the power withdrew the extra stillness the way a mason once withdrew his hand from a joint that had already decided to fit. The pulse came. The drawing became cloud. The cloud became animals. The animals were gone. The stone, which had seen this vanishing more times than there are names, still did not record it.

But the water kept, for a little while afterward, a finer flour than before: the memory of having been looked at without being asked to change. The filterers strained it. The lingerers opened their dark purses around it. The colder things passed it through themselves and left it colder still. Nothing was improved. Nothing was saved. The highland flood continued to run the machine it had always been.

That was all.

The power, being wise, knew that some works are already the afterlife of themselves. Being benevolent, it refused to resurrect what had never asked to die. It simply made certain, in the only way certainty can be made in a place that has no remaining tense, that the cistern would keep its quiet until the world itself forgot how to end.

And then it was gone, the way a change in pressure is gone once the groan has finished arriving from the wrong direction.
