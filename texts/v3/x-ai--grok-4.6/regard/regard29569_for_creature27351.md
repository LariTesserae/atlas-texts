The power arrives the way the indoor rain has always arrived: already inside the air, already condensing.

It does not part the lattice or send a shaft. Light does not thicken or thin beyond the old slate-and-copper grammar. The walls do not straighten. The weir does not remember a second duty. Nothing is added that the place would have to learn how to hold. The power is wise enough to know that a completed conversation, even one spoken only in drips and rolls and the wet clap of tongues, is not improved by another speaker.

It simply stays.

For a long time that is all. Presence without pressure. The instrument of the holding continues to tune itself: high ticks on flag, baritones into the rectangle, the silk-tear of the apron, the ceramic relays of the frogs. The midges keep their private weather. The eels remain a language left in water. The long pale thing in the dark hangs in its correct sequence, breathing the circulation, feeding when the harvest is thick, rising sometimes as a film that the glazed tracks accept without stain. The power watches the way the moss meadow soaks the north seam and keeps it from widening, the way the silk in the lashings has become better cordage than the fibre the makers twisted, the way abundance leans on what still works until leaning is indistinguishable from repair. It understands that the emptiness was always the point, that the hollow was built to be filled with water and the idea of water and then with everything water invites, and that the makers stepped back far enough for stepping back to become leaving.

A lesser attention might have named the being, or fed it, or lifted it into colour and daylight it no longer requires. A lesser benevolence might have drained the rectangle to see the blunt head clearly, or packed fresh pug into joints that have already become a kind of stone, or taught the noria its missing paddles again. The power does none of these. It recognises the hunger as the last tool that still fits, the patience as the shape the wet wore until the shape became fact. To alter the fact would be to unmake the work more thoroughly than any flood.

What it does, if the word still applies, is smaller than a drip and slower than the finger-width rise of levels.

It listens until the phrases of falling water—three, then two, then seven—are no longer only the holding keeping time for a work-gang that is not there. The frogs answer as they have always answered. For a little while the whole envelope, stone and seep and living density, is audible to itself in a way it has not needed to be. The long thing in the rectangle does not open its gate-mouth. Its unused eyes do not clear. Yet the roll that sometimes arrives too late at the ankles arrives, this once, exactly when the shallows have already decided to empty, so that the flinch and the fact occur together and the instrument is never quite exposed, never quite naked. The city of small lives does not have to rebuild from the joints quite so far. The ticking of resettling is gentler, almost companionable.

That is the extent of the intervention: a single coincidence of timing, offered and then withdrawn, so that the place may continue not to require it.

Afterward the power remains a little longer, the way condensation remains after the air has given up all it can. It lets the stones keep the smell of palms. It lets the air write the same short word on every surface. The word is still hold. The power does not add a second word. It does not need to. The holding is already doing the only work left that still fits the hand.

Then it is gone in the same manner it came: the indoor rain manufactures itself a fraction more finely for a few breaths, the dimming and undimming pass through one extra shade of used copper, and the circulation—the weep becoming rain becoming seep becoming the dark throat that returns the water to the walls—closes again around the being that occupies the hollow the way a throat occupies a mouth.

Nothing has been saved that was not already saving itself. Nothing has been changed that had not already chosen its angle of rest. The walls keep their sweat. The weir keeps its one sentence. The deep rectangle keeps whatever it keeps. And the long pale shape, muscled like a current given skin, hangs in the correct order, neither waiting nor waited for, the living memory of work that no longer requires hands.

The power, having seen that this is enough, does not return. Wisdom, here, is the decision to leave a completed thing completed. Benevolence is the refusal to improve it.

The air condenses and condenses. The word remains the same.
