The power that noticed did not arrive as light or weather or any visitor the frogs would pause for. It arrived the way a completed thought arrives: already inside the thing it regards.

It saw the dish of the land, the resorted dust, the being whose body had become another slow grammar of peat and film and colonized folds. It heard the note that lasts the length of a small geological argument. It understood, without needing to be told, that the mountains had not been defeated; they had simply finished their sentence. This place was the period at the end.

A lesser kindness would have offered height again, or dryness, or a new mineral to taste, or a companion large enough to share the century-long glance. The power was neither lesser nor kind in that hurried way. It was wise enough to recognize a finished work, and benevolent enough not to sign its name across it.

So it did almost nothing.

It only made certain that the almost-nothing would be allowed to continue being almost-nothing for as long as the being wished to keep its modest shape. No returning ranges would shoulder their way back. No faster age would drain the dish or teach the water how to hurry. The algae would keep their iridescence, the reed-rafts their circular patience, the midges their single shared breath. The being could still decide, once in a great while, to be a few body-lengths farther on, and the unzipping sound would still be the only news the landscape required.

In the being’s lidless eye, for the space of one unhurried tide, there appeared a reflection that was not sky and not water. The being regarded it the way it regards everything: without surprise, without need. Then the reflection receded into ordinary gold, and the low note swelled once more, exactly as before, felt more in the soles than heard.

After that the power withdrew the way humidity withdraws: by becoming indistinguishable from what was already there.

The frogs did not stop. The rocks went on weeping. The land, which had forgotten how to rise, was not required to remember. It remained the world after the world had finished making itself—still warm, still loud with the voices of things that found a way to stay, and now, very quietly, known.
