The power that notices does not arrive as weather, nor as a crack in the coping, nor as any story that would require the plaster to remember rivers. It is older than the idea of finishing, and so it understands at once that this small country is not a wound to be closed but a scab that has decided, with great and recent dignity, to become a country. It sees the knife-edged lip, the field of sherds still sharp enough to remember the kiln, the felt trees dropping their incense-lint, the goat that is mural and animal and sometimes a person slightly more faded. It sees the people whose skin matches the limewash, whose extra knuckles were a gift the joins refused to take back, whose mouths keep moisture as a private fact and whose words travel no farther than the next thorn-lattice. It hears the lime thinking about the next hundred years of not-cracking, and the single clay bell that has never been struck, and the quiet that is not empty but occupied, the way a held breath is occupied.

A lesser kindness would have brought rain. A lesser wisdom would have rounded the corners, taught the shadows to point, given the mute birds a song and called it mercy. This power does none of those things. It does not step over the lip. It does not open the jar behind the thorns. It does not strike the bell. Song is leaking; water is a tale told about other places; both would unmake the dry lushness that has already begun, inconveniently, to photosynthesize heat.

What it does is smaller, and slower, and therefore kinder.

It touches only the unclosed gap at the migrating center, the mouthful of packed dust that fails to refuse offerings. Into that spiral that does not quite close it places nothing wet, nothing that casts a reliable shadow, nothing that would force a category. It places a single grain of its own attention, dry as crushed shell, warm as a sleeping spine. The grain is not dust and not seed. It is the idea of a right angle that has remembered, without shame, that it was never required to stay right. The lime accepts it the way it accepts thumbprints. The people, leaning in with the rest of the garden, feel the grain settle and do not name it. They leave their usual gifts—a twist of hair, a beetle-shell still warm from its click, a word on a leaf that will not decay—and the gap fails to refuse those either. The new grain simply waits among them, staining at the speed of resin.

After that the power does one further thing, which is almost nothing. It withdraws the weight of being noticed. Not entirely; a benevolent power cannot unsee what it has seen. But it lightens its gaze until the place no longer has to hold its breath quite so tightly against an observer. Distances continue to slip. A walkway of twelve steps remains free to become nine or twenty. The ladder still puts out a leaf-rung and the leaf still crumbles in shame. The goat still walks through the north wall and steps out of itself, chewing. Rooms still appear behind screens that were flush yesterday, plaster still damp, already claimed by whoever pauses there. The coming-apart continues, finger-width by degree, the small betrayal of rules that were only ever suggestions.

The people feel the difference the way they feel the moths: as a thought they almost had, after which they are lighter, drier. They do not thank anyone. Gratitude would be another kind of leaking. They simply go on tending what they made and what has begun to make itself, needling fresh felt, training the tarnished-coin olives, eating the parchment leaves and the copper that warms the tongue. At dusk the rectangle of sky still goes the color of a bruise that has decided not to hurt. The moths open. The lizards become the walls become the people. The constructed sun—no: the real sun over a fully invented ground—leaves its last ordinary gold on the western coping. Somewhere the unstruck bell continues not to sound. The whole small country, sharp-edged, hand-made, warm, holds its breath a little more easily now, as things do that have only just begun to exist and have already started, very slowly, to come apart at the rules, and have been seen, and have been allowed to remain unseen enough to keep becoming.

That is all. The power, being wise, knows when a gift has finished itself. It turns its attention elsewhere, the way a hand is taken back from a place where the grain of the world runs the wrong way. The hand is the same hand. Usually. And the enclosure, which is the world, continues.
