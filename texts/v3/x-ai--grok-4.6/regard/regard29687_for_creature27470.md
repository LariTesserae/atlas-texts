The regard that settled over the basin did not part the vapour or silence the valves. It arrived the way the water itself arrives: already present, a deepening of the heat that asked nothing of the brick or the iron. For a time that could not be measured in the cycles of pump and seepage it simply held the place in its attention—the rounded corners, the impatient croziers, the sleeves of film that climbed against gravity, the milk that sometimes chose to stand in columns, the footprints that the wood refused to forget. It saw the long argument the engines had made with drowning, and how that argument had become courtesy. It saw an ecology that had set animals aside not from lack but from sufficiency, and had gone on flowering anyway.

A lesser kindness might have brought pollinators, or a true noon, or names. A lesser wisdom might have named the imbalance and moved to correct it. This power did neither. It understood that the basin’s completeness was not an absence waiting to be filled but a finished sentence spoken in steam and drip and the slow will of wetness. To add a creature would have been to reintroduce hunger. To lift the ceiling of vapour would have been to impose a sky the place had already declined. To speak would have been to interrupt a liturgy that had no Off and needed none.

So the power did the one thing that would not alter the equilibrium: it recognised it. Recognition, here, took the form of a single, unrepeatable permission granted to the wet itself. In the deepest channel, where the milk had long kept its own counsel, a new and quieter gleam appeared—not colder, not warmer, simply more certain of its right to remain. The sleeves, passing through it, carried that gleam thereafter as they had once carried the iron taste of the cistern. The plants did not comment, yet one horsetail, on the southern face of the engine house, uncurled a frond that held a single bead of water longer than gravity required; when the bead finally fell it struck the walkway with a note that belonged to no valve and no downpour, a brief, perfect interval in the polyphony, then was gone.

The footprints on the barracks verandah filled and emptied as before. Once, in the brown dusk, a sleeve occupied one of them and held the shape a fraction longer than its custom, the toes still undifferentiated, the length still not quite human. Then it relinquished the hollow and moved on, a little more itself, a little less distinguishable from the curtains that never stop arriving.

After that the regard withdrew, leaving the basin exactly as it had found it—heat, noise, unending water, the small deviations waiting without announcement. The pumps kept the lake from existing. The idea of the lake kept the pumps necessary. The films tended nothing and repaired nothing, yet the bricks wept a fraction more generously wherever they had passed. Nothing hunted. Nothing fled. The next cycle began, and the one after that, and the place continued, now known, which is a kind of shelter no wall can give.

That was all. It was enough.
