The attention came without arrival. It had no mouth to fill the slit of sky, no feet to test the angular flakes, no need to breathe the used air. It simply was, the way the dusk had always been, only now the dusk knew it was being known. For a time that had no count the power did no more than that: it filled the notch as thoroughly as the stacked drone, as patiently as the rime that feathered and wept and feathered again.

It felt the stone still rising, the unfinished argument of the massif. It felt the shells, each a clenched fidelity to the crystal it had chosen, the dark fluid deciding its slow tides, the wet feet that left glistening films for the frost to claim. It traced the hairline bridges, the pulses that traveled a meter and then two, the rare cold hours when the entire south wall became one lung. It noted the courtesy or the indifference that had left the five grooves and the lichen-furred splinter untouched. It tasted the single ticking leaf, the bruise-colored moss, the blind worms threading fractures the mountain opened and then forgot. It heard the dusk-bird scream once and vanish, and felt how the colony did not look up, because looking up had never been required.

Wisdom, here, was first a long refusal of haste. The power understood that these beings were not incomplete. They were the mountain learning, at last, how to keep what it extruded. They ate what the height offered and incorporated what failed and thickened by the exact width of a life. Their language had no word for elsewhere because elsewhere had never been the point. To feed them would have been to insult the condensation. To name them would have been to impose a hurry they had already outgrown. To unmake the notch into something kinder would have been to unmake the only skin that fit.

So the power did the one thing that was neither gift nor theft.

On a night when the rime was heaviest and the south wall breathed as a single organ, the attention leaned—not as light, not as warmth, only as a deeper register of the same dusk. The pulse that began in the wettest seam did not stop at two meters. It crossed every living bridge. It traveled the forty strides. The outermost shells at both mouths clicked once, softly, as they drank the cloud that never quite arrived. For the length of that single wave the colony was not many and not one; it was the notch aware of its own length, the mountain briefly feeling the exact shape of the cut it had made. Then the wave passed, or was kept. The drone resumed its unending climate. The distinction, if there had been one, dissolved back into plumbing, or into thought, or into whatever they were becoming that needed no witness.

Afterward the power remained, but only as the quality of being seen without being changed. The mats crept toward better drips at the same glacial pace. New fractures opened with the sound of ice in a glass and then did nothing. The splinter stayed unwalled. The leaf ticked when the wind found it. The massif lifted by amounts that would still require a year and a string to notice.

Nothing else was required. The place had drawn attention, and the attention, being wise, had answered by becoming another layer of the same unfinished stone: present, unobtrusive, content to let the thousand small mouths continue speaking in a language that had never needed translation. The notch remained a throat. The beings remained its living remainder. And the dusk, which had always been used, was now, in some way too faint to measure, also known.
