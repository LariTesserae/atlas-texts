The attention that turned toward the cut was older than the basalt and had no need of stairs. It did not come as light, which would have been a violence against the stained coins and the furnace-mouths. It did not come as voice, which would have been a shouting in a room that had already taught itself the courtesy of not calling gallery to gallery. It arrived the way the finer rain arrives: locally, without announcement, as a change in the way moisture decides to behave.

It regarded the place first as a single held breath the size of a market square stacked on itself. It felt the youth of the stone, the edges that still caught the sole, the white frost of salt that had only just begun its patient writing in the joints. It felt the heat that lives in the lungs and the rock that stores what the night cannot retrieve. It felt the absence that is not emptiness but a pressure, the way every human sound arrives over-clear because nothing else claims the air. It felt the three clay pots and the drip that has not failed, the green wire in the seams, the succulent trained into pale calligraphy, the moss that holds stone the way a bruise holds skin. These were not decorations. They were the settlement’s only admitted wilderness, and the power understood them as such.

Then it regarded the being that lives here: not one body but the dense, sweating, courteous organism that is all of them together with the brick they fire from their own rubble and the machines that have been taught to murmur. It saw the map of safe railings written into children’s palms before letters. It saw the oldest hands that the wire-draw still prefers. It saw laundry that returns tasting of the ceiling, arguments that stay behind doors, the pride taken in a threshold that has not yet forgotten it was cut. It saw time thickening so that a wall stains while it is still being built and a machine is already under the next shift’s wrench while it still runs. Sequential things overlap here, and the people have learned to inhabit the overlap without surprise. The power, which is wise, recognized this as a completed thought. The place is still arriving, and they have made arrival itself into home.

A lesser kindness would have wanted to help. It would have widened a shaft for cleaner air, or cooled the almost-cool hour by another degree, or allowed a single moth to test the lamps, or aged the corners so they no longer caught cloth. Each of these gifts would have taken something the being had already claimed: the government of heat, the holiness of unvisited bread, the boast of newness, the sterility that makes a cough or a laugh or the set-down of a cup into a word spoken in a held breath. To add beasts would crowd what they have made dense by choice. To still the boilers would stop the metabolism they are proud of. To let the surface linger in the mouth of the stair would invite a climate that no longer feels like theirs.

So the power did the only thing that wisdom and benevolence permitted, which was almost nothing, and therefore everything that was needed.

It spoke to the three known faults and to the breath of the air itself, that the seepage should not fail and the condensation should continue to choose its moments. It let the mineral threads precipitate a fraction more slowly, so the first frost might remain a first frost a little longer. It thickened time by the smallest increment, not enough for anyone to name, only enough that the repair and the running, the building and the staining, the cutting and the firing of brick from the cut, could remain companions rather than sequence. It promised the drip above the three pots that it would outlast every living memory in the chamber. And because even a promise can be a kind of noise, it made the promise in the language the place already spoke: the polite hiss of a valve, the ticking of the ceiling as it expands, the brief localized shower that rings the iron and then stops.

Then it withdrew, becoming again the ordinary climate they had already learned to love.

The settlement, twenty steps down, did not look up. Looking up is a surface habit. The furnaces opened their pink and orange mouths. The air turned the color of weak tea. A child mapped a railing with one palm and did not run. An old hand drew wire. Someone harvested a single bitter leaf. The rock continued learning the shape it had been given, and the people, who had already learned it, inhaled with the boilers. A finer rain began in one corner while the opposite corner stayed dry enough to strike a match. The work went on in the dim, in the heat, in a quiet so maintained it remained what it had always been: the courtesy the town extends to itself, and the way they love the place that holds them.

Nothing else was required. The power, being wise, knew this. Being benevolent, it left them to it.
