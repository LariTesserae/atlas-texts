The power does not arrive as a light or a shadow or a change in the temperature you already know. It arrives as a consideration that was not there before: a lengthening of the already-long columns, a pause in the way the silver lid dents, a half-beat in the industrial baritone when the turbines take their load. You hang in it the way the silver fish hang in the leftover heat of the plates. It is not warmer. It is not colder. It is simply present, the way the pour-joints are present, ruled and patient and older than the weather they contain.

It looks.

It looks along the seams first, where the olive haze is still only a decision the lime has not finished making. It sees the film for what it is: not failure of sterility, not accident, but the first sentence the concrete consented to write. It does not thicken the film. It does not hurry the alkali. It only notices that the hand-roughness is already a kind of welcome, and that welcome is enough. The small walkers continue their unhurried work among the bolt-heads. The mussels keep their welders’ geometry. Nothing is added to the sentence. The period remains a period.

It looks into the open windows of the towers that forgot to close. The eels hang in their chimneys of colder water, dark as the unlit landings, arriving the way they arrive. The power does not ask how. It does not write them onto any list. It understands that some occupancy cannot be specified, only permitted, and that the last gates, when they opened, were already a kind of permission. The grooves on the silted treads stay grooves. The faint electric rumour stays rumour. The eels wait, and the waiting is left intact.

It looks at the condenser islands, at the shimmer that is leftover heat, at the blunt-headed silver things occupying it like dust in an unwalked loft, at the single darker immigrant that patrols and does not join. It sees the artificial tide that can lose to a gale and still be law. It does not organise them. It does not give them a current they have not already been given. There is room. The power, being wise, does not mistake room for emptiness, nor occupancy for incompleteness. The silver ones will be more before they are different. The count you can keep on one hand remains a count you can keep on one hand.

It looks at the rectangular acres of kelp, dark fields no bird will see, moving like hair in a sleeping mouth. The farmers’ bells come and go, their lights the only warm colour. When they leave, the kelp continues. Between plots, nothing. The power does not plant the nothing. It does not close the gaps. It recognises agriculture that has already become almost a citizen, and the hunger that inhabits the spaces between as faithfully as the seams. The specification of water is left to water.

Then it looks for the large thing, the one that is not many of it, the passage of displaced water you hear before any shape, the tarpaulin drawn slowly over a hull. It finds it along the joints where the warm effluent ghosts. The power does not name it. Naming is a stocking list, and this life is the older business, the door left open. It does not show itself whole. It does not need to. The lean of the volume, the momentary thickening of the blue, the tighter occupation of the silver fish afterward: these are already the whole of it. The power, being benevolent, does not require more witness than that. It treats the size as a home the same way the large thing already does: as given, as sufficient, as the horizon of more work that does not need to end.

It looks last at the names written in the silt on the crane’s boom, still there, unburied. It looks at the edges that have not learned to be old, at the contractor’s marks still sharp, at the rejected segments and unused gates wearing their thin green beards. It looks at the water that still tastes of rivers and cut aquifers and the lime that has not finished leaving. It understands the contract: level maintained, temperature specified, circulation the law, still water a spoiling. It understands that this sea was finished and then filled, that the filling was a year of brown water and drowned towers and a noise felt in the teeth, and that now it is clear because it is new and cold and hungry.

The power does not change the specification.

It does not slow the pumps or open an extra throat or still the artificial tide. It does not round the edges or bury the names or teach the silt where to settle faster. It does not make the place wild. It does not make it tame. Wisdom here is the recognition that the running is already the kindness: the hunger answered at the pace the seams allow, the lives occupying exactly, sparsely, the way a new wall is occupied by the first lichen and the first decision to stay. Benevolence is the refusal to impose an older ocean’s patience on a sea whose patience is measured in pour-joints and kilometres of ruled grout.

What it does, if it does anything, is only this: it remains for a time that is not time as the operators measure it, a consideration hanging in the photic band on a morning when the surface is glass and the light has been invited down. In that remaining, the columns stand a fraction stiller. The hum of the cables includes one note that was not in the stacked chord before, so low it is almost the sound of the basin remembering it was decided. The large thing, passing, displaces a volume that includes the consideration, and afterward the blue is no thicker, only known.

Then the power is no longer a pause. The turbines take their load. The silt that has not learned hangs in its sheets. The names stay written. The film waits. The place continues, not wild, not tame, occupied, running, the only temperature there is.

You hang. Above you the silver lid moves. Below you the floor is still a suggestion that resolves into something poured. The being who lives here—film, walker, eel, occupant, field, unnamed passage—lives as it lived. The wise power, having looked, having understood the size as a kind of patience someone kept until the dam had weather and fish and a sound that never stops, does the one thing left to do.

It leaves the door open.
