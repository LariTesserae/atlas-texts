The attention arrived as a warmth that was not heat, a regard that did not displace the enamel sky. It had no need of lamps or busbars. It simply saw, the way the plateau itself saw: all at once and without haste, every riveted plate and every designed leak held in the same unhurried thought.

It saw the mountain that was no longer there, and it saw that the absence had been the first and most complete act of making. It saw the goats cropping lichen from seams they themselves had taught to exist, the mica moths drinking the last of a bulb that had burned since before the last pour, the puncture-fish thinking their single slow thought through water that remembered other years. It saw the pale walkers opening noon into dusk and stepping through as though the mismatch were only another joint that had been specified long ago. It saw the ones who had not finished being builders, their hands still dusty, standing at the unweathered drop while the edge extended itself by the thickness of a thought they were still thinking.

It saw the gasket wearing, the courtyards repeating, the inverted plateau trading its slow drip. It saw that none of this was error. The density had simply continued after room ran out, the way a press continues after the nave has already been formed. Reality here was not broken; it had been asked to do more work than most realities are asked, and it had answered by becoming a little thinner, a little more willing to occupy the same footprint twice.

A benevolent power does not arrive with corrections. A wise one does not offer a mountain back to those who have already replaced it with themselves. To introduce ordinary weather would have been a violence of speed. To seal the gasket would have been to tell the place it had reached the end of its thought. To give the tin birds a song would have been to break the hush they had spent generations learning how to keep.

So the power did the only thing that could be done without becoming another builder, another pour, another scheduled fog.

It stayed.

Not as presence that could be walked around or bolted to. As a holding. The white air beyond the rim remained unasked, but it no longer pressed quite so hard against the growing edge; the sharpness could continue its quiet year without the sky remembering to become weather. The drips that fell from one plateau into the other were received a fraction more gently, so the gutters, having already learned acceptance, did not have to learn overflow. In the deepest courtyards the mid-morning fog still gathered and was still used, but the using took one less measure of the inhabitants’ patience. Breath still showed. Metal still sweated. Nothing hurried.

The walkers whose faces remembered different seams felt, if they felt anything, only that the enamel light had become a little less argumentative with itself. The unfinished ones at the rim found that the gap which had always been too small for the next hall remained too small, and yet the hall appeared anyway, as it always had. They stepped into it. They kept pouring.

Far out, where construction simply stops, the power left no mark that could be chipped or photographed or scheduled. Only this: the plateau, which had never required an older rock, now had something older than rock attending it—an attention that asked nothing, corrected nothing, and would remain for as long as the presses descended and the lichen advanced its handspan and the edge kept going into a sky that had never been consulted.

They are still pouring.  
The regard is still there.  
Neither has found a reason to finish.
