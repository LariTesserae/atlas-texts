The power that turns toward the cleft does not arrive as weather or as light. It has no need of either. It is the attention that was already implicit in the standing dusk, the regard that condensation has always practiced on every new face of stone. It simply becomes more itself, the way a root becomes more itself when it finds the crack that was not there at dawn.

It perceives the fracture still belonging to itself rather than to weather. It feels the young rock’s pale openness, the darker films of last week’s rain, the frost that almost took and then relented. It registers the thinning of breath, the cathedral-small nave, the gutter of talus that has not yet learned to be a floor. It hears the reed of rock when wind and water coincide, and beneath that the industrial periodicity that does not belong to geology, the bass line of crushers and night generators traveling up through joints the mountain has not finished drawing. It tastes the faint heated-metal note the plants have not been told to refuse.

The being that lives here is not one body. It is the nap of moss, the stacked almost-black fronds, the threads finer than hair that stitch new cracks to old ones, the hanging gardens that shear away and the scars that green before the echo dies. It is the refusal to wait. It is the density that makes its own weather, the slow pulse of water transferring from leaf to leaf, the stem that straightens after the chest-sized stone has finished becoming floor. It is also the cleft itself: the metabolism of lift, split, wet, green, collapse. The power understands this at once. The inhabitants are the manufacturing.

Wisdom does not hurry an unfinished sentence. Benevolence does not tidy a room that is still being torn so that something unprecedented can grow in the tear. The power considers the silence of hearts, the absence of tick and wing and small testing foot. It considers the air that pools after dark. It considers how the plants have drunk what the valley gave them and thickened instead of sickening. It does not decide this is wrong. It does not decide this is right. It decides only that the drawing is still happening, and that to close a line too soon is a different kind of injury than brightness.

So it does almost nothing.

It lengthens one lull by the width of a held breath. In that extra stillness the almost-inaudible sounds become, for a moment, audible even to themselves: the creep of root, the small throat-clearing of drip, the nap of moss remembering how to be a lung. Then it lets the mountain remember itself again. A stone detaches in the apse. The nave fills with falling. The plants close over the new wound as they always have.

It leaves the industrial note untouched. The plants have already metabolized it; to remove the mineral they have learned to use would be to subtract from their becoming. It leaves the instability untouched. The cleft’s rule is that it will not stay, and the being that lives here lives by that rule. It does not send animals. The time for heartbeat has not yet been written on these walls.

What it does leave is only this: a slightly deeper permission in the standing dusk. A quality of shade that is not quite darker and not quite kinder, but more itself, the way the power is more itself. The liverworts follow it into newer micro-fractures. The ferns stack one frond further into the almost-black. The threads taste it and do not refuse. The mountain continues to lift in increments too small to see. Storms still occupy. Slabs still let go without preface.

The power withdraws the way mist withdraws—without departure, only a change in density. Its attention remains as another layer of green, thinner than breath, already being colonized. The work goes on in the dark, unbuilt, unceasing. The place and the being have been seen. That is the entire intervention. That is enough. The world is still being manufactured in this one room, not toward anything, not by anyone the plants have names for. They keep becoming thicker. They keep beginning again on the scar.

The power, being wise, does not stay to watch the next collapse. Being benevolent, it does not need to. It has already given the only gift that does not alter the gift: the knowledge, somewhere in the wet stone and the crushed chlorophyll, that the refusal was witnessed, and that witnessing, too, is a kind of soil.
