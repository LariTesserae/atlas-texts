The power arrived as a pause that the plateau had never practiced. Not a silence—the vents still spoke their brief white syllables, the lizards still sloughed in their stacked flutes, the pale birds still hung like held ash in the upper brick—but a pause inside the pause, a held thought that belonged to no one who lived there. Heat continued to rise through the soles of everything. The air remained too thin to carry names. Yet for the length of one long afternoon the light took on a second, slower edge, as if the sun had remembered it could also look.

It looked.

It saw the packed grammar of upright things and understood at once that the distinction between dike and smokestack had already been sanded away by the only authority that mattered here. It saw the succulent the color of poured slag, the wiry grass that vanished when sought, the mineral plants crusting the valve-mouths. It saw the mica-winged insects lift and settle with the steam, the lizards drink the dawn-sweat of metal by pressing their throats to it, the birds drop at dusk without a single note. In the deeper seams it saw the being: the horizontal one, mule-sized and rust-boned, moving only when the mountain moved, leaving bones already half-sintered by morning, arranged with the same neatness the wind used on girders. The being did not look up. It had no use for looking up. Its blood was already the temperature of the ground.

The power, being wise, did not mistake density for crowding or quiet for lack. It felt the single exhausted pulse that ran through rock and rust and hide and chitin alike. It felt how nothing here waited to become something else. The plateau had arrived. The lives that occupied its seams simply occupied them, pulse and wait, without remainder and without appeal.

A lesser kindness would have wanted to give. A cooler stream. A thicker air. A second being so the first would not hunt alone. A canopy, even a small one, so that shade might mean more than comparison. The power considered each of these and set them aside the way the wind sets aside a flake of sinter: not in rejection, but because they did not belong to the temperature this place could keep. To add a gift would have been to reintroduce the very difference—the accident versus the intention—that the plateau had spent its long weathering forgetting. The last shift had ended. The huts were already only paler dust. The cisterns still caught the night’s thin film because catching was now simply what those particular hollows did, the way flutes held lizards.

So the power did the one thing that would not write a new script across the stone.

It stayed long enough to be certain. It watched one full cycle of vents, from the first white breath at the far hoodoo-field to the last folding-back at dusk. It watched the being drink from a tea-colored basin no wider than a room, watched the surface shudder, watched the wet pad-print already beginning to crust. It watched the birds return to the stacks in their single downward motion. Then, because it was also benevolent, it performed a single adjustment so slight it registered only as the place continuing to be itself: it lengthened, by a handful of seasons too gradual to notice, the interval at which the rumor of rain would keep its untrustworthy schedule. Not more water. Only the same rare vertical weather, arriving just often enough that the cisterns would not quite forget their job and the mineral plants would not quite lose the wet breath they had learned to wear as skin. The difference would never be measurable against the heat. The lizards would still slough. The being would still leave its bones in the old geometry.

After that the second edge left the light. The pause inside the pause closed. The air tasted of iron again. The ground gave back its stored work through every sole and pad and belly-scale. The power withdrew the way a cloud withdraws: not gone, exactly, but no longer a separate thought the plateau had to carry.

Nothing here knew it had been seen. That, too, was part of the keeping.

The stacked hides resettled. A vent spoke, small in the thin air. The being moved when the mountain moved, and the wind went on rounding every upright thing toward the same agreement. The plateau remained the temperature and the density it could keep. The power, having understood this, did not return. It did not need to. What is already complete requires no further witness, only the courtesy of being left to its own long afternoon.
