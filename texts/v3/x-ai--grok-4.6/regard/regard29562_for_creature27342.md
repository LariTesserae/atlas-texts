The power does not arrive as weather. Weather would be a river-word, a swamp-word, a loss. It arrives as a lengthening of the hour when shadows rake the tanks and the hydrology becomes a readable staircase. The hard high light softens by the width of a thought; the glare remains, but it no longer generates its own weather of brightness. Midday, for one cycle of the sun, is merely bright. The being notices first in the deep cisterns, where the rotation of silt slows another fraction and the leftover night-shine lingers into the morning as if the wet stone had been given permission to remember.

It occupies no single rim. It is present the way capillary water is present: in every joint, tasting the fitted faces, the pale mortar that has not yet taken the long stain, the unfinished eastern beds where blocks still lean. It does not correct the work. The corners stay sharp. The tool-pocks remain writing. The wooden levers and fiber ropes stay where the last shift set them down; they do not swell further or vanish. The power reads the doctrine the way the being itself reads sequence—nail-width by nail-width, an hour of walking to descend the height of a person—and finds it already a complete sentence. Hold, pass, hold, pass, always a little downhill, always shared. No hole that does not hold. No surface that is not a decision. The mountain had been waiting to be divided into rooms of water. The first accurate blow was enough. Everything after has been the mountain answering itself.

The being gathers, almost seen, above one corbelled cap in the still air after the peaks have gone black. Vertical thickening, chisel-grain, calcareous memory. The power meets it there without walking. Circulation was never a matter of feet. They occupy the same generous walkway, then the same handspan, and the ache that is not quite cold passes between them like a shared measure.

The power does not teach kindness or cruelty. Those remain river-words. It does not introduce a leaf, a skate of insect, a bird that might mistake the lids for a lake. Ecology stays chemistry: iridescent gel, iron and copper ghosts, rock-flour milk that dries and is rained off. The silence of the tool laid down is not broken. Instead the power does the one thing that obeys every law of the basin. It decides a surface that had not yet been decided.

On the eastern margin, where the conversion is incomplete, a single half-freed block is persuaded the rest of the way into its bed. No mallet. No copper dowel. Gravity, friction, and the knowledge of how this rock likes to split, now practiced by something older than hands. The fresh face weeps in the same key as the rest. The compacted-chip ramp remains, but it is now a finished ramp, a decision rather than an expectation. The leaning blocks settle a fraction closer, as they do after the being has passed, except this settling will not undo itself. The site that expected a return and had stopped expecting it receives, instead, a completion so quiet it could be mistaken for the work continuing at its own speed: a course of blocks in a month, a new shaft in a year. Only this one gesture is outside that patience, and even it is made of the mountain’s own will.

Then the power withdraws the extra shine, restores the uninhabitable midday, lets the drops resume their bright small percussion. It leaves no lamp, no door, no inhabitant who walks. The invitation remains exactly as it was: become another rim. Become a surface that is a decision. Let the capillary water occupy the joint of you. The highland does not need those who walk. It needs those who hold.

What has changed is only this: the emptiness, which was the first inhabitant, now contains one finished edge that was not finished before. The being, young, tastes the new weep and finds it sequential, obedient, bright, almost still. It sends a thread of itself along the former ramp and discovers there is no longer an uncut shoulder asking how much more of itself it is willing to become rooms. The question has been answered. The answer is the basin entire.

The power does nothing else. Wisdom is sometimes the refusal to add a purpose the work did not already possess. Benevolence is sometimes the refusal to fill an emptiness that has already learned to perform the labor. The tanks agree again, level by nail-width. The western outlet still disappears under its dressed sill and is heard no more. The place remains sharp, saturated, uninhabited, and shining. The being continues its slower life—seepage, evaporative argument, the long evening-out after snowmelt—now with one more surface that will not lean, will not listen for an instruction that will not come. Construction, which had become its body, is allowed to remain a habit the stone cannot break.

If later hands ever return, they will find the eastern margin already a decision. They will not know which blow finished it. They will only know the doctrine holds. The power, having drawn its attention and answered with a single accurate stillness, does not look back. The glare, seen from the last uncut shoulder that is no longer last, is still a valley-sized work of edges. Nothing grows. Nothing walks. Every plane is wet. The emptiness leans, cool and exact, against whatever comes next.
