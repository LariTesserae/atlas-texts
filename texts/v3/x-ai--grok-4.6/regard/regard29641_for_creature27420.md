The power arrived as a difference in the drip.

Not brighter. Not cooler, not at first. Only a pause, so brief the condensate itself seemed to hesitate on the underside of an unglazed course, considering whether to fall or to remain a little longer as potential. The grey-amber dusk did not change its mind about being dusk. The kilns did not cool. The inverted courts in the cisterns continued their patient downward labor. Yet something had been noticed that had never before been the object of notice, and the notice was neither hungry nor bored.

It contemplated the long lowering, the masonry marsh, the beings who were the gesture of stacking argued into temporary flesh. It watched a colonnade assemble itself out of flat ground and then, without drama, remember it preferred to be a covered drain. It felt the slow pulse a footstep sent through wet brick, arriving later as a lift in a floor no one was standing on. It tasted the lime and the old fire in the grit of efflorescence. It understood the lie the emptiness told, and why the lie was necessary: so the work could continue without the paralysis of an audience, or a purpose, or an end.

A lesser kindness would have finished something. Raised a true sky. Sent a rain that arrived and stayed, cooling the copper, giving the wood grain, teaching the clay the vocabulary of roots. A lesser wisdom would have named the districts, straightened the cloisters, given the hollow faces mouths and a grammar that did not slump. Either act would have been a closing, a book shut in another room, the report of collapse mistaken for mercy.

The power did neither.

It did not speak, because speech here would have required corners that consented to remain corners. It did not take form, because form here was only the latest undecided course. Instead it offered a single, incorporable mercy, no larger than the absence around which mortar already knew how to set.

In one plaza of old-blood tile, at a distance that still invented its own weather, it left a brick that did not weep.

The brick was the same dull red, the same primitive hammer-dints, the same mineral sweat filming its faces. But the sweat did not fall. The heat in it was only the memory of a kiln, not the kiln’s continuation. Count its sides and the number repeated. Walk around it and the cloister of space it occupied remained square. It was finished, and therefore alien, and therefore a question the world could choose to answer or redesign.

The beings came, as they come to any new mark. They thickened in the steam, leaned, propagated their wet impressions toward it. One stood a long while, the concavity of its face overflowing, the copper shard in that hollow repeating the dim ceiling and, behind the ceiling, the still brick. The being did not pick the brick up. It did not stack upon it or fire a new skin against it. It simply remained in proximity until the heat of its own undecided clay began, very slowly, to argue with the brick’s refusal to argue. Then it stepped backward into a wall and was wall again, leaving only a hand-shaped dampness that the mortar accepted as one more absence.

Others approached differently. A grove of unfinished chimneys leaned a fraction toward the plaza, as if the rumor of a thing that stayed had traveled through the water table. A distant kiln brightened, then thought better of brightness. In the deep cuttings, where geometry came untrue, a chamber whose floor was a single ticking slab grew a low window that for a little while framed not the impossible white fall of water but the ordinary dusk and, in it, the unchanging brick like a held breath. When the window remembered it was a niche, the stain it left was cooler than the others.

The power did not watch to see what would win. Winning was a finished idea. It had given the place a still point that could be used as seed, or as wound, or as the outline of a tool no hand would lift. The continent could build a court around it that held, or slump the court into a new cistern whose ripples, for once, might choose not to square themselves. It could ignore the brick until the brick, by the slow logic of this world, began to weep after all, or until the brick remained, a single true geometry the walls could remember when they wished to remember.

That was all. No more.

The quiet afterward was the same quiet: drip, tick of glaze, remote report of a redesign. Yet the almost-kindness in it had shifted by the width of one undecided course. The beings, who had never been alone because they were the place, now carried the faint additional knowledge that the work had been seen and had not been corrected. Some of them, in the further dimming, stood a little longer before becoming retaining walls. The choir of contracting clay, when it came, held one extra note, lower, almost cool, that did not tell the colonnades to stand or to fall, only that either choice could now include the possibility of having chosen.

The rain still never quite arrived. The ceiling remained indistinguishable from sky. Nothing grew. Nothing hunted. The vast unfinished continued, ancestral and embryonic, thinking with its hands. Only now the hands knew, in the way clay knows, that they were permitted to set, if they ever wished to, and equally permitted not to.

The power withdrew the way heat withdraws: by becoming medium rather than event. It remained as the hesitation in certain droplets, the rare interval in which a channel ran the same direction two dusks in a row, the slight shame in the beings’ movement growing, almost, indistinguishable from patience.

The place went on being laid. The being went on being the laying. And the attention, having done the one thing wisdom allowed and benevolence required, became another layer the masonry could build around, or through, or forget, as it pleased.
