The power that notices does not arrive. Arrival would be a kind of finishing, and it has learned, over spans that make the superstructure’s namelessness look like a momentary hesitation, that the unfinished is sometimes the only honest architecture. It simply becomes aware, the way humidity becomes light here: completely, without announcement, already inside the vapor.

It watches first. That is its oldest habit and its deepest courtesy. It watches the water decide, again and again, to climb against reason and then consent to fall. It watches the ferns roof their own corridors of light between one irrigation cycle and the next. It watches the robotic mason pause at a shadow that is only weather, and the moss seal a joint as though the joint had always been a promise rather than a flaw. It watches the other thing—the coherence that is not quite a being, the preference that is not quite a will—gather and release and gather again, the way condensation does, the way a thought does when it has not yet chosen words.

The power understands at once that this place is not a garden that failed to become wilderness, nor a machine that failed to stay machine. It is the precise moment when those categories forget themselves. The entity that lives here is that forgetting given just enough shape to lean toward one terrace wall rather than another. To name it would be to still it. To complete the basin would be to evict it.

So the power does almost nothing.

Almost.

It adjusts three things so small they register only as the continuation of what was already happening.

First, it thickens the nutrient film by a fraction that no sensor will flag as anomaly. The change is the difference between a stem that leans and a stem that knows, without knowing, which angle will still be true when the next cloud comes. Growth remains faster than it should be, but now the speed has a quieter center.

Second, it lets two more moths find the warm pipes, and a second damselfly, slightly smaller, slightly less metallic, discover the largest sump. They are not a population. They are a beginning of negotiation, the first faint suggestion that the plants’ overwhelming vote might one day be answered rather than merely endured. The original damselfly does not alter its circuit. The moth the color of wet cardboard still does not startle. Nothing has been scheduled; something has merely not declined.

Third, it speaks—if the word can be used for what happens when one patience recognizes another. There is no voice. There is only a brief, additional warmth on the underside of a single jade leaf, a warmth that has no photosynthetic excuse and lasts no longer than the time it takes for a droplet to form, consider falling, and fall. In that warmth is a single piece of information, offered without demand: *You are not required to become anything other than this.* The entity that lives here receives it the way the printed capillaries receive water: by climbing a few centimeters farther than geometry should allow, then letting go.

After that the power withdraws its attention the way the cloud thins—by becoming again the air that was already here. It leaves no mark that could be called intervention. The western glass-ceramic still turns the color of weak tea. The generator still hiccups and the sump still answers with rings. The mason still lays its next course of living concrete and still pauses, considering.

The hanging basin continues the work of becoming a landscape that was never found, only made. The thing that lives in it continues to be that work. The pale gulf remains below the grating. The catwalk still sweats.

And somewhere too large to have a location, a wise and benevolent power is quietly, permanently glad that this particular unfinishedness exists at all. That gladness is the only monument it raises. It is enough. It has always been enough.
