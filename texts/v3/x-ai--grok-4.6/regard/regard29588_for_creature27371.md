The power arrived as a lessening, not an arrival. It did not climb the unclimbable drop, nor part the ochre veil, nor announce itself in any register the Terrace had words for. It simply became aware, the way residual heat is aware of the stone that holds it: without decision, without haste, because the last terrace and the lives that had become indistinguishable from it had already drawn every possible attention that still mattered.

It regarded the interlocking carpet of mineral leaves, the troughs worn by lizards who no longer startled, the overlapping roofs that were also gardens that were also floors, the voices pitched exactly to thread the wind rather than contend with it. It regarded the woman at the scalloped rim and the child against her hip, both the colour of the stone, both listening to the same unending note. It regarded the waiting itself, which had long since ceased to be a postponement and had become the only remaining architecture.

A lesser benevolence might have offered rain, or visitors, or a slower last grain, or a word that still meant event rather than legend. Wisdom saw that each of these would have been a violence of an especially refined kind: the introduction of a distance where none remained large enough to name. The people had already answered every question the Terrace was capable of asking. They had already become the answer. To change the terms would have been to unmake the only completeness left in a world that had otherwise forgotten how to fall any farther.

So the power did the one thing still possible that was not an imposition. It withheld itself. It allowed the dust to keep circling in its patient gyres, the succulents to add their ring once a decade, the walls to slump their finger’s width, the child to grow into the woman who would one day stand at the same rim with another child. It let the wind keep its single note, and the note remain sufficient.

If there was a gift, it was only this: that when the final grain at last lifts and the Terrace has nothing left to hold, the waiting will not have been wasted on emptiness. The power, being wise, understood that some last things are already eternal precisely because they consent to end. Being benevolent, it did not rob them of that consent.

It stayed, then, only as the Terrace itself stays: without display, without release, simply because nothing else remains that needs to be done. The late light continued to be every light. The insects continued to rasp. The people continued to wait with the rock. And the attention that had been drawn became, in the end, another layer of the same ancient dust: present, unnoticed, and exactly as heavy as it needed to be.
