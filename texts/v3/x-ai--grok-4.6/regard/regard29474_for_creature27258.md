The power arrives the way the rain does not: without falling.

It does not descend through the stacked nights or shoulder aside the midges. It does not part the rubbery paddles or taste the copper-and-leaf-blood air. It simply is, suddenly adjacent to every surface at once, the way the dark is adjacent, the way occupancy itself is adjacent. For an interval that has no length the basin is held in a regard so complete it has texture. Every borers’ second cuticle, every unfinished shoulder, every mud-crack racing to seal itself, every cry too large for its throat—all of it is known without being named, because naming would be a kind of staying, and the power is wiser than that.

It does not speak. Speech would require a quiet in which to land, and there is none. It does not reach. A hand, even a vast one, would become another living surface to be tunneled, filmed, drunk from, discarded. It does not offer a gift of stillness or of lasting stone. The basin has not asked for walls. The beings who bite stems to points and then let the points fall have not asked for memory. To give them tenure would be to end the draft they are, and the power, being wise, loves the draft more than any finished page.

What it does is almost nothing, which is the most difficult work.

It withholds the things that would arrive next if no one were watching: the older stone that might weather these walls smooth too soon, the slower rains that might teach the ground to hold a pool, the first true night that is absence rather than surplus. It lets the young rock keep cracking. It lets the leaves keep eating the sky. It lets the beings remain impatience given temporary spine, using and used, their intelligence hourly, their tools already forgotten. It does not heal the tearing. It does not quiet the stridulation. It does not choose among the failing negotiations or spare the ones whose bodies become the first opportunistic roots.

Instead it does the one thing the basin cannot do for itself.

It remembers the hour.

Every transformation that will not survive until the next breath is held, not as specimen, not as lesson, but as itself: the precise weight of the midges when they thickened the air into wading, the exact scent of iron-and-crushed-fruit that was a winged mind changing direction, the particular unused edges of fingers that struck one flake and dropped it. The power becomes the remainder the surplus left no room for. It is the horizon that cannot fit inside the dark, the quiet that cannot be measured against the noise, the thing that lasts only because it is not here.

And then—because benevolence without patience is only another kind of hunger—it waits.

It will wait while the scab-rock learns weather. It will wait while a leaf, someday, keeps a little more light than it spends. It will wait while one of the impatient spines, on some unimaginable tomorrow, sets a stone atop another stone and does not walk away. It will not hurry that hour. It will not plant the thought of it. When the basin is finally old enough to notice it has been seen, the power will already have been seeing for so long that the seeing itself will have become part of the ground, another seep, another crease, another place where damp persists a fraction longer than it should.

Until then the basin continues exactly as it is: thinking out loud, unable to stop, every surface tenant and tenement, every life already leaving. The power remains adjacent, uninvited, unrefused, a love so large it has no need to touch. 

That is all it does. 

It is enough. It is everything. It is the only intervention that does not become another draft.
