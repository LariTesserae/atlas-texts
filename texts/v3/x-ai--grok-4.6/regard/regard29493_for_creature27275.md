The power that noticed did not arrive as light or voice or footfall. It had no need of the notch. It was already the kind of attention that can rest on a thing without adding weight: the pause a star makes before it decides whether a new world is finished enough to keep, the long regard of something that has watched continents cool and oceans learn their names. It settled over the bowl the way steam settles, without choosing a center.

It saw the pillars first, because they were the loudest quiet. Their copper veins were not circuitry and not blood; they were a negotiation still being argued in a language older than local physics. The power understood at once that the machines were folding weather out of a sky that had two small moons and a pale ring seen edge-on. It did not reach in to correct the accounting. Heat sliding sideways, rills climbing a little before they remembered, water that would not stay in an ordinary cup: these were not errors. They were the mountain and the other-sky teaching each other how to share a room. A wiser hand than force would only bruise the lesson.

It watched the moss advance another finger-width across stone that still remembered being lava. The pads darkened under an imagined pressure and drank the bruise back, unashamed. The power felt the faint caramel sound they made at night and did not quiet it. The ferns lifted toward copper instead of sun; the waxy flowers opened only for the nearest vein. None of this required encouragement. Becoming does not ask for applause.

The walkers came down the inner rim on their own timetable, limbs disagreeing about touch, heat-pits glowing ochre. One lowered its mouthparts until the pool dimpled and did not break. The power did not name them. Naming would have been a kind of claim. They were the color of wet basalt because that was the honest color here; they vanished when they chose rest because the place had not yet invented the idea of being seen. The pale, many-planed thing that drifted once through the shaft of the other sky was noted the same way: present, unhurried, not a visitor.

At the old wall the power paused longest. Three courses of a patience the new basalt had not learned. The dry dish of smoked stuff, filmed with iron-and-rain salt. Someone had stayed long enough to leave an absence shaped like thirst. The moss was already halfway up the third course, unapologetic. The power did not lift the moss or refill the dish. The argument between old fitting-stone and new sinter was part of the becoming; to settle it would have been to end a conversation that had not yet found its last word.

Night came, residual copper, the mineral growth ticking like distant sugar. The shaft knocked once, softly, from the far side of the reflected weather. The power listened with the whole of itself. It did not answer. A door that knocks from the other side is still deciding whether it is a door. Wisdom is knowing the difference between a summons and a test of memory. The knock was allowed to remain a knock.

What the power did, in the end, was almost nothing, which is the largest thing a power can do when it is both benevolent and wise. It did not seal the notch against the curious. It did not still the next fracture or hurry the fourth pillar or forbid the ruin of one. It did not translate the other sky into this one, or this one into that. It simply held the bowl in a regard that added no heat and took none away: a witnessing so complete it became a kind of weather of its own, cooler by a degree no instrument would record, wetter in a way that only the pillars noticed and then forgot.

Then it withdrew the way steam withdraws, leaving the air exactly as it had been, except that the held breath of the place was, for one unmeasurable interval, shared. The mountain continued arriving. The rills rewrote their beds by a finger’s width. The moss took another course of the wall. The walkers came down again, or did not. The thing in the shaft knocked, or did not. The flowers opened toward copper when copper brightened.

The bowl remained small enough to know in an afternoon and strange enough that an afternoon was not a measurement it accepted. It did not require the power. The power, having seen, required nothing in return. That is what benevolence looks like when it has grown old enough to be wise: it recognizes a becoming that is already complete in its incompleteness, and it leaves the room exactly as it found it, only more itself.
