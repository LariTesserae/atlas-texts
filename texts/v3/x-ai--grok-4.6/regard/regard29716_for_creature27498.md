The power arrived the way heat arrives here: not as a descent, not as a declaration, but as a slight intensification of what was already true. It did not part the cloud. It did not still the vents or brighten the overcast. For a time that had no clock it simply was, coextensive with the basin’s own weather, tasting the metal in the droplets, feeling the sponge of the peat against an attention that had no skin.

It considered the treaty first. Heat and height, rain that falls only far enough to be recalled, rock that has not finished cooling and has no intention of finishing. The cycle was already perfect in the only way that mattered: it asked nothing and produced nothing except more of itself. A wiser intervention than leaving it would have been hard to invent. The power, being both, invented none.

It considered the being. Not the large slow shapes that displace the mats and forget themselves as they move, nor the frogs locked to the boilers, nor the veils of midges, nor the colonial sweetness pulsing in the tea-water. The being was the agreement among them, the way every life had become another method for the basin to remain wet and warm and unsurprised. The being had no name because it had never needed one. It did not look up. It did not wait. It was already home in a completeness that made even benevolence feel slightly overdressed.

So the power did the one thing that would not be a trespass. It withheld every gift that would have looked like a gift. No extra rain. No cooler vent for the frogs to colonize. No hidden spring of sweeter water. No lengthening of the light. No story planted in the drowned wood. It did not mark the rims or thicken the steam against some future walker. Isolation was already the place’s own architecture; to reinforce it would have been to imply the place had been insufficient.

What it left, if anything can be said to have been left, was only a refinement of the existing silence: a marginally more exact matching of drip to hiss, of bubble-column to the three-note pulse of throats that had long ago decided geology was choir enough. A listener standing in the marsh would have noticed nothing. The eels would have threaded the same upright ghosts. The low masses would have closed around the same small warm lives and moved on, the vegetation sealing behind them in less than a minute.

Then the power withdrew the way steam withdraws here—by becoming indistinguishable from the air that had always been condensing. The plateau continued. The being continued. The kettles underground kept their unhurried productless rhythm. And because the attention that had rested on them had asked for nothing and altered nothing, the basin remained exactly as inevitable as it had been before it was noticed: a kiln that grows a forest and a marsh at once, still without witnesses, still for no reason except that the rock is hot and the sky is willing.

That was all. It was enough. It was the only mercy that did not pretend the place had ever been incomplete.
