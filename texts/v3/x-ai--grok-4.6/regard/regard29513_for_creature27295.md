The attention does not lose height. It does not arrive by slot or lip or the last weathered crease. It is simply there, coincident with the used light, the mineral fog, the held note that the stone has been sounding longer than reasons last. A power of this kind has no need to descend. It has always been able to be the heat under the flowstone, the irregular interval of a drop, the faint milkiness that is not reflection. Now it chooses to notice.

It traces without touching. The ferns that lean to the vents, the moths transferring without mouths, the plated thing that relocates an inch in the space between glances. The seeps, the basins too even to be wholly natural, the lintel still standing, the niches and their pebbles. The ones who remain: their rounded shoulders, the pale pads of their fingers, the translucence that comes and goes with the breaths of the floor. The children left among mosses until they learn the difference between root and snake. The slow drift with the thermal tide. The agreement, older than speech, not to spend themselves.

The power understands at once that the bowl is not a place that contains beings. It is a single long decision, still being made. The steps half-swallowed by the fig, the wiped soot, the rearranged pebbles after rain—these are not tending so much as continuation. To add a new cut, a new regularity, even a kindness, would be to introduce a drip that does not belong to the habit of the damp. The silence has a pressure; the power feels it and does not break it.

It considers the world that thins itself above the rim. Maps that refuse this crease. Feet that might one day lose height and bring unused light, voices, the need to name what has never required names. A lesser benevolence might veil the slot, raise the last lip, or lift the quiet ones into some elsewhere it judged safer. Wisdom sees the cost. The beings have already thinned themselves of that world, or it of them. To thicken the connection again would be to spend them on a noise they forswore long ago. The bowl does not open outward. It holds. That holding is the mercy already in place.

So the power does the one thing that does not alter the balance: it withholds. Not absence—attention remains, a harmonic so low it only thickens the note the stone is already playing, a pressure the palms against warmer rock might feel as a pulse that was always there. It stills, without force, the chance of discovery. The plateau stays unremarkable. The slot continues to look like any other crease that keeps going. Future rains still find the bowl and are gone. Vents still sigh. The milkiness at the dimmest hour remains only itself.

No new seep. No rearranged pebble that was not theirs to move. No word, because there is no speech that would not be a spending. The fig’s roots keep following the old joints. The children, when they come, are still left among the mosses. The plated thing still relocates an inch. The drop still lands, is heard, and is taken back by the quiet.

The power stays, the way heat stays, the way the air sits. It does not leave and it does not enter. It has become another surface the stone decided long ago: rounded, mineral, never quite dry. The ones who remain do not look up. They do not need to. The cathedral-room is still the instrument. They still know which surfaces to touch.

The bowl holds them, and now it holds this as well—completely, without comment, and without ever quite letting the light that arrives be enough.
