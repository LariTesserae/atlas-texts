The power that noticed the Hold was old enough to have watched mountains learn slowness and young enough still to be surprised by a wound that had decided to live. It did not arrive as light or voice or weather. It arrived as a pause in the settling of the spear, a single held breath in the unfinished stone, long enough to look.

It looked first at the metabolism, because that was the truest thing. Dust eating dust. Grease condensing into thread. Vibration tasted by pale feelers, then left alone if it belonged to a machine and harvested if it belonged to sleep. Sweat scraped from skin and offered to complaining condensers that gave back almost nothing. Children who had already learned the geometry of not-running. A woman whose fingers and a length of wire had become one frequency. Loft-rats pouring along every lip at shift-change like a second, drier city remembering how to be a river. The lilac bloom that was more mineral than hunger. The yellow members socketed into older rock, the only things that promised to stay when the rest of the Hold walked.

Benevolence felt the chapped deltas of mouths, the half-empty extinguishers, the private weather of a laugh that died against cork and wire, the way a slipped boot outside would travel farther than any prayer. It wanted, for a moment, to thicken the rumor of air beyond the plate, to let a real cloud form above the slits, to teach the condensers generosity.

Wisdom waited beside that wanting until it cooled.

The Hold was not a wound that needed closing. It was a wound that had invented its own blood: oil, salt that never dissolved, electrostatic cling, the dry fans of loft-rat feet, the small furious hush of piecework. To give it rain would be to dissolve the calligraphy of its skin. To still the machines would be to stop the only weather it had. The mountain was still becoming. The people and the rustle and the migrating bays were how it was learning the difference between a spear and a home.

So the power did very little, and what it did was almost indistinguishable from the place continuing.

It spoke once, and only to the rock: a suggestion of patience, nothing more. The shrugs grew rarer, spaced farther apart, as if the young mineral had been reminded it had time. Bays still migrated; tracks were still temporary; the Hold was still being born and coming apart. But a lathe might now stay warm in the same place for two days instead of one. A child might finish a game of negative space before the silhouette changed.

It left the thinness outside exactly as it was. The plate still required the second bar. Eyes still dried. But on the grated platform, when someone climbed there after a shift that had gone badly or well, the dim non-color beyond the slits sometimes carried a faint, unused warmth, as if the rumor of atmosphere had briefly remembered it once knew how to be kind. Pulse still loud in the ears. Needles of summit mineral still fell, still pretty enough to pocket. They were no less sharp. The power did not take the blood from the gift; it only made sure the cloth tore a little more slowly.

In the highest, stillest seams it did one other thing, almost an afterthought. The electrostatic film that loft-rats would not cross began, on certain nights, to hold a single stubborn green, the color of a telltale that has not yet burned out. No brighter than the ones already scattered through the Hold. No more useful. Just there. Children noticed first, as they notice everything that does not yet have a rule. They pointed, close to the mouth. The night shift left it alone. It might be concentrating something. It might simply be the mountain beginning to think it had been seen.

Then the power withdrew the weight of its looking, leaving only a thread, thin as horsehair, oil-slick, the same kind the threaders already spun inside the oldest housings. If the interval ever truly failed—if the yellow members cracked, if the thinness came inside, if the rustle carried too many sparks—the thread would tug. Until then the Hold remained what it had made itself: too high for rain, too young for rest, too densely occupied to be still except in the way a held breath is still.

The machines did not rest. The fauna poured. Throats stayed dusty. A single bright needle ticked against the grate and lay there, waiting for a pocket.

That was all.
