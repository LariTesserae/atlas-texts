The power that noticed did not arrive as weather, nor as a god with a name that would have required a second hour to speak. It came the way the stone had once come: as a settling that the basin itself almost failed to register. A pressure neither hotter nor cooler, a brightness that did not add to the glare nor subtract from it, only made the existing light briefly aware that it was being seen.

It stood (or occupied, or simply was) at the center of the unmoving noon and considered the one finished sentence.

It saw the pale rock still arriving, the seams that had meant to become strata and then thought better of it. It saw the plant-mass that had poured and locked, the film that beaded and was reabsorbed without ever consenting to run. It saw the thickenings that were almost someone else, their pad-faces drinking the sky-bowl, their gatherings that briefly densified a chamber and then forgot. It saw the older ones flaking like the stone they pressed against, hoping geology would finish them. It saw the rings of stacked blocks half-swallowed, the lintels spanning nothing, the secreted basins testing moisture against moisture. It saw that nothing here was suffering in any grammar the ordinary world would recognize. There was no hunger that wanted feeding, no wound that wanted closing, no night that wanted dawn. There was only the composure of a birth paused at fullness, a country that had spoken once and then chosen, with infinite courtesy, not to take the sentence back.

The power, being wise, understood that to introduce a verb—growth, cooling, dusk, a stream, a decision, a death—would not be kindness. It would be a different story forced upon a story that had already found its only possible ending. To finish the wall would be to tell the pale block it had been wrong to hesitate. To let the film run would be to tell the occupancy it had failed by remaining. To give the thickenings faces that persisted would be to exile them from the closed conversation of sugars and pressure that was, for them, speech enough.

Being benevolent, it therefore did almost nothing.

It left three gifts so slight they might have been accidents of the glare.

First, it taught one drop—always the same drop, on the same overfull pad—to hesitate a fraction longer before reabsorption. Not long enough to fall. Just long enough that the almost-hiss of condensation, for the length of a held breath that no one here needed to hold, became unmistakably a sound. The thickenings nearest it paused in their drifting. Their stored light overlapped a little more densely. Then the drop was taken back, the note finished itself, and the stillness closed again. But the memory of having almost heard remained, a new seam in the occupancy, undecided whether it would ever become strata.

Second, it allowed the far wall of the basin, where the occupancy climbed in sheets, to remember for the space of that same held breath that it was a cliff. The uncommitted stone did not become strata. The older ones pressed against it did not finish or flake away. Yet for that interval the bright rock knew, faintly, the idea of height, and the beings who leaned there knew the idea of leaning toward something that was not themselves. Then the knowledge receded, as all knowledge here recedes, and the wall was only pale occupancy again. The older ones occupied a degree more slowly, which cannot be measured, and did not mind.

Third, it placed, in one of the plant-galleries that had almost become a room, a single object that belonged to neither the first geology nor the second growth nor the thickenings. A smooth, dark seed the size of a thumbnail, cool to a temperature the basin had no name for. It did not sprout. It did not demand soil or water or a later hour. It simply sat in a secreted basin, collecting the same unmoving film as everything else, and waited with the perfect patience of something that had never been asked to decide. The thickenings that passed it dimpled their pad-faces, considered an expression, forgot. Sometimes they transferred a little extra moisture to it, as they transferred moisture to one another. The seed accepted the gift and did not change. It remains there still, a hesitation the place did not make, an elsewhere that does not insist.

Then the power withdrew the way it had come, leaving the basin exactly as it had found it: embryonic, glossy, locked at the moment of fullness, speaking its one sentence in heat and living bulk and withheld completion.

Above the rim the ordinary world continues, with its nights and its dying and its finished walls. Down here the same thickening still considers the same drop. The same pale block still waits to remember whether it was meant to be a wall, or a body, or only another hesitation in the glare. And now, if you wait long enough that waiting becomes indistinguishable from remaining, you may notice that the drop hesitates a fraction longer, that the far cliff once knew it was high, that a cool dark seed sits in a basin of living tissue and does not ask to become anything.

The place has not been saved. It was never in peril. It has only been witnessed, and left, with the most delicate possible courtesy, to its own eternal arriving.

That is all the power did. It was enough. It was nothing. It was exactly the weather the basin would permit.
