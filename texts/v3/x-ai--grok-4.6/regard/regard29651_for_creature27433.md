The attention arrives as a further thinning of the already-thinned light, a clarity so complete it cannot be distinguished from the gray that has always been there. It does not descend; it is simply present, the way moisture is present in the clay, the way the interval between water-drops already contains the drop that has not yet fallen.

It reads the basin in a single, unhurried regard. The fitted stones that have forgotten they were fitted. The slab that has forgotten it was raised. The figure that has forgotten it was ever other than the floor. The power understands at once that time here has not been interrupted; it has been concluded. Every possible weathering has already occurred. Every necessary forgetting has already been accomplished. The equilibrium is not a pause. It is a finished sentence.

Benevolence, in this place, cannot take the form of repair, for nothing is broken. It cannot take the form of awakening, for nothing sleeps. It cannot take the form of gift, for the hollow has already received the only gift that matters: the right to remain exactly itself.

So the power does the one thing that will not unmake the completion. It withholds itself. It adds no color, no motion, no new stem, no new tap of water. It does not even leave a warmer patch of clay or a single extra bead of condensation. Any such mark would have been a beginning, and beginnings are the one thing this country no longer requires.

Instead it performs the oldest and quietest of kindnesses: it remembers the place correctly, without remainder, and then it forgets it again, as thoroughly as the stones themselves have forgotten. The memory is not stored. It is simply allowed to dissolve back into the same patient texture as the moisture in the soil.

The hollow continues. The figure continues being the hollow. The light remains the same uncolored gray. Nothing has been added. Nothing has been taken. The rumor of other countries, where things still change, stays a rumor the stones once overheard and then, with perfect courtesy, declined to keep.

That is all. It is enough. It is the only action that does not insult the finished work.
