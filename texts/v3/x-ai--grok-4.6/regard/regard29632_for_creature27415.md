The power that turned toward the ledge did not arrive as weather or as light. It had no need of those. It simply knew the place the way the seep knew the basin: as a fact already held, already budgeted, older than the walls that had been built around it.

It regarded the fitted flags, the rounded edges, the three pale smokes that were never four and never two. It regarded the copper tree that no one had planted and the hand-mill whose satin no longer wanted grain. It regarded the figures who moved like interruptions the stone had already answered for—the same dark cloth, the same lichen-mapped wrists, the same unhurrying feet that had worn the thresholds to the exact width they would keep. It regarded the western wall and the one who sometimes stood there, indistinguishable from it, watching paleness recede into paleness.

A lesser kindness would have mistaken the stillness for lack. It would have sent rain that stayed, grain that filled the hopper, children-sound to test the echo, new timber that had not yet learned to swell and never quite rot. It would have called that mercy. The power, being wise, saw that every such gift would have been an accumulation, and accumulation was the one weather the ledge had finished with. To add would have been to spend what the place did not keep. To mend what was not broken would have opened a course the masonry had already closed.

So it did nothing that could be named as doing.

It did not still the wind in the high streets; the wind was already the exact complaint the wood intended. It did not deepen the seep; the basin’s thirst was already the people’s thirst, shine or tick, and both were complete. It did not prune the tree or plant another. It did not open the third chimney. It did not hurry a slate back into place when one slid, nor prevent the slide. Either outcome remained inside the town’s idea of itself.

What it offered was only the continuation of the ordinary light—the slightly pale daylight that flatters nothing and conceals nothing—and the continuation of the quiet that had already accounted for every sound it meant to make. If a bird crossed high enough that species did not matter, that speck was allowed. If condensation breathed from stone and from sleeping joints in the same hour, that breath was allowed. The path that switchbacked into scree and did not return was left to bring, on its own rare schedule, whatever small bitterness or dryness the stores required and no more.

The figures felt no visitation. A shutter opened or stayed shut according to the habit of being shut. A pot appeared under the broken spout and vanished before overflow could find the gutters. At the western edge the low wall considered the farther ridges, and the one who stood there considered them too, or was considered by them; the distinction had long since rounded like every other corner. When cloud sat on those ridges and did not come, or came and turned the town to dark mouths in soft grey, that expression was still one of the few the face was allowed. The power did not add another.

It kept the place the way the mountain kept it and the town kept itself: without requirement, without remainder. Time continued to pass in the only way the ledge permitted—the light moving, the seep deciding, the copper leaves holding or letting go—and nothing accumulated, nothing was spent. A traveler who left and returned after what felt like a generation would still be unable to say whether an hour or an age had gone. The power would not help them decide. It had already seen that the question itself was not in the budget.

Thus the ledge remained a single built thought, cathedral-small, hamlet-complete, held in cool damp equilibrium at the height where weather becomes simple. The being who lived in it—whether one or several or the masonry thinking itself through them—remained the amount of being the place would allow. Nothing else was required. The power, having understood this, required nothing else of it either.

It turned its attention elsewhere, or did not. Either was already within the arrangement. The ordinary light stayed ordinary. The joints stayed damp. The quiet kept its accounting.
