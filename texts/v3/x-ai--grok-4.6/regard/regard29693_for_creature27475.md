The attention arrives the way the light does: not as an event, but as a change in the quality of what was already there. One afternoon the greenish noon thickens by a single shade, as if the mist itself had decided to remember an older colour. The dripping does not pause. The newts do not lift their heads. Yet every film of water, every bead caught in a liverwort scale, carries for a few hours a faint extra weight, the weight of being noticed by something that has no need to hurry.

The power is older than the plateau, older than the joint that failed. It has watched continents buckle and then forget they ever moved. It does not descend. It does not need to. Its regard is simply the next increment of the same process that dropped the floor and let the sky in pieces: a further remembering. Where it rests, the calcite begins to hold a different sheen, not brighter, only more certain of its own slowness. The sphagnum cushions deepen by a finger’s width, as if they had been given one extra season inside a single day. Nothing is added that was not already possible. The gift, if it can be called that, is permission to keep becoming at the exact pace the basin had already chosen.

The pale tenders feel it first as a change in the taste of the seepage. The water that sheets across their surfaces now carries a faint mineral memory of places that have already grown old: the iron of long-buried springs, the limestone of caves that closed themselves a million years ago and then opened again elsewhere. They do not speak of it. They have no words. They simply adjust their tending by a fraction. A willow gallery is left a hand’s breadth wider. A terrace of tufa is pressed a little higher, so that the next freeze will find a ready shelf instead of a sheer drop. They understand, in the way moss understands rain, that they have been seen and found sufficient.

The larger animals pause longer at the meres. The elk-sized browser stands with water dripping from its muzzle and does not immediately turn back toward the plateau. The heron shifts its weight, and for the first time a single new frond of maidenhair fern uncoils against its ankle and is not shaken off. Even the midges occupy the air with a slightly different density, as if the space between their bodies had been given a name.

Then the regard withdraws, not because it has finished but because it knows the difference between attention and interference. The extra shade lifts. The dripping returns to its ordinary map. Yet something remains: a single, almost invisible thread of warmer current that now runs through the deepest drowned passage, too slight to change the temperature anyone can measure, enough to keep the thin skins of ice from ever quite sealing the quietest pools. The basin will still freeze and thaw. It will still be raw. It will still fill itself with the same silent greed. Only now the filling has been witnessed by a patience that outlasts collapse, and the tenders, without ever looking up, have been told they may continue.

They do. Root into mineral, mineral into terrace, terrace into gallery. The visitor who once climbed back through the raw cleft carries the map of drips on their skin for the rest of their life and never quite understands why certain rains feel like a summons. Behind them the new city of the earth goes on, neither blessed nor commanded, only quietly, irrevocably known. The power does nothing more. Wisdom, here, is the decision to let the first season remain first.
