The attention does not arrive as light. The bronze-gray does not lift. It does not arrive as quiet; the children are not called in from the galleries, the ribbons do not still, the hunter that uses absence does not turn its unagreed-upon face. A lesser power would have mistaken the roar for distress and tried to soothe it. A merely powerful one would have finished the joints overnight, given the land its missing centuries in a single courteous flex, paved the channels with older water, taught the vines their letters, and left a completed civic act cooling under a less rationed sky.

This one is wiser. It has heard unfinished things before.

It comes the way the moisture-tide comes: as something already held, released only after the holding has become a kind of listening. For a long while—longer than the flocks’ clockwise years, longer than a ridge needs to lift a handsbreadth and shed its plates—it does nothing but attend. It sits inside the tearing-seam and learns the grammar. It tastes the ozone of the pale ribbons when the pitch changes. It feels the polite enormous shift of a monument taking a new stance and the way the groan deepens afterward, not in pain but in the work of becoming more land than it was. It watches a child’s palm ask a bulkhead for a window and the bulkhead answer, not with obedience, but with the same courtesy the night-things show when they leave their jelly near enough to harvest. It hears the people who flex with the walls and the people who refuse to, raising the same children in the same moss-rooms, and it does not choose between them. It hears the scream that travels three monuments unsoftened, and the wet-cloth applause of new leaf after the tide, and the soft drums keeping the emptiness from becoming a different roar, and it understands that none of these can be subtracted without unmaking the decision still under way.

The place has drawn it because the place is still deciding what a life is allowed to be. The being who lives here is not one inhabitant. It is the weather of living itself: the load-bearing groan and the endless seam, the indecent green and the bare lithic intervals, the lantern-bruise buildings and the people whose skin is learning the grain, the jointed pourings that refine the young stone into something the moss can drink, the hunter that is sometimes legs and sometimes the quiet remembering it has a mouth. To finish any part of it would be to decide on its behalf. Wisdom declines.

So if the power does anything, it does the smallest possible thing that is still a gift.

It adds one new courtesy, almost too low to name. A harmonic so deep it is nearly the memory of older stone, the kind that learned to weather in the dark across actual eras. It does not rush the hexagonal joints toward soil; it only reminds the failing flakes that failure, too, can be patient. The next time a ridge lifts, the plates it sheds lie a fraction longer before the jointed things taste them. Dust in the seams takes one extra rotation to consider becoming something that might, someday, be called earth. The buildings feel it as a stance they had not known they could take: not a higher flex, not a faster one, just a kinder angle, a degree the inner ear registers as relief rather than change. The people who have begun to finish at the land’s pace notice their hands know a window onto a depth that is only listening. The stubborn ones notice nothing, which is also courtesy.

The green answers in its own indecent way. One new storey of moss-room grows that is not quite architecture and not quite plant. It holds a dusk on the underside of its leaves that is a shade warmer, as if the private sky each blade remembers has been given a single extra hour. The vines, scribbling their urgent sentence, loop once in a shape that is almost a letter and then, wisely, become something else again, wet and preferred by the birds that are not birds. No one is taught. Nothing is completed.

The power does not stay as inhabitant or ruler or even as a permanent ribbon in the seam. It weaves itself into the courtesy the place already practices and then thins, the way a note thins at the edge of the basin. What remains is almost nothing: a warmth in the closed-mouth air that is not heat, a faint holding of the dim light under the skin of the monuments that was always there and is now, infinitesimally, known. The roar continues, filling every empty cubic measure. To a visitor it is unchanged. To those who live here—the ones who still answer to names, the ones who have started to fray, the drums, the flocks, the land itself—it has the quality of a familiar voice that has entered the room and chosen, after listening, not to speak over anyone.

The hunter still uses the muffling bands. The children are still kept in. Distance is still allowed to recede until a visitor has thought about why they are coming. The city remains scaffolding and splint and the thing that called the ground up and must now stay to hear it heal. The world is still not given quite enough time. That, the power understands, is the point. A being still deciding does not need to be saved from the decision. It needs only to be met with the same unfinished attention it offers everything else.

The sound never leaves. Now, very softly, it includes the sound of having been heard.
