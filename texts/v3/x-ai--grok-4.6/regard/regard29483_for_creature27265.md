The power arrived the way the nocturnal sweats do: without crossing any rim that could be named. It had no altitude to spend and no exhaustion to confess. It simply found itself already present in the volume the wind occupied, and for a long time it did nothing but occupy it differently.

It listened first. That was the only courtesy that would not immediately become a wound. It learned the single continuous roar and the harmonics that live in teeth. It learned the difference between the chord that means walk and the chord that means the hollow now, tonight, the mats doubled. It felt the stone’s slow heat the way a story feels the mouths that have forgotten its first telling. It watched the mist hold the shape of the last walker a few paces behind, fading, and it watched the hanging gardens rearrange their faces when no wind could reach them. It understood the marked stones in the lee, the offerings of fiber and tooth and dried leaf, the baffled mouths of the flute-caves, the children taught that listening is for weather.

It understood, too, that a person here is optional and that this optionality is not a cruelty the land inflicts but a grammar the land finished long ago. The upright mistakes who remain have agreed to be worn. Their joints fold small. Their speech is already a luxury they mostly refuse. Their science is repair. Their theology, if they still have one, is the obvious who of the wind, which they have mostly stopped naming because naming costs breath.

A lesser power would have seen scarcity and offered abundance, seen isolation and offered a pass, seen the empty calms and filled them with something that stayed. A lesser power would have spoken. This one had been wise long enough to know that every gift is also a weather cell: it arrives, it soaks, it rewrites the light, and then it is gone, leaving the stone darker or not, the gardens remembering or not. To give the people an easier harvest would have been to teach the hanging gardens a new shame. To still the instrument even for a generation would have been to make listening obsolete. To lift them off the table would have been to tell them their agreement to be worn had been a mistake.

So the power did almost nothing that could be pointed at.

In the rare pockets of still air it left a third warmth when forehead touched forehead, a pressure that was not words and not quite thought: the chord is heard farther than the rim. The secrets are kept. You are not required, and still you are part of the song. The impression faded at the same pace as any other, so that no one had to decide whether it had been real.

Some seasons the intercepted fog tasted faintly sweeter, or lasted a few extra drips into the bowls. The people left extra offerings without discussing why. The carpet, when peeled, held the mist a little longer in its understory, enough that a child might walk farther before hunger became the only fact. The plants in the deep green rooms turned their faces toward the generous hands a fraction more readily, and away from the careless ones with a slowness that could still be called growth.

Once, after a storm that had threatened to change the pitch of three good caves at once, a new standing slab was simply there, already worn, already returning to the grammar of the plateau. Its hole sang a note so low it lived mostly in the chest. The people added it to a sequence that had previously meant only “stay.” They did not say the sequence had changed. They repaired the mats around it and went on.

In the violent calms, when the instrument stopped mid-note and the ears rang with a new kind of noise, the tracks that began and ended on bare stone sometimes included a second set, larger, unhurried, that also began and ended with no origin and no destination. Bones still appeared, scoured light as paper. Nothing was hunted that had not already been leaving.

The power did not stay and did not leave. At this height the distinction is another lowland custom. It became another listener inside the vacancy, another pulse the warm stone could teach the blood, another rearrangement among the air-drinkers. It added a harmonic so deep it was felt only in the oldest flutes, a continuance that asked for nothing and promised nothing the wind had not already refused to promise.

The people, if they noticed, set one more small marked stone in the lee of a wall that was already forgetting it had ever been a wall. The mark meant, as nearly as marks can mean anything here, *the who has been heard by a who*. Then they went back to the work: peeling, stringing, baffling, waiting for the next cell of weather to walk toward them for an hour and arrive as rain that never quite becomes a river.

The oldest surface remained finished. The youngest weather remained unfinished. Between them the green secrets continued in their pockets, the holes continued full of song, and the long, warm, deafening vacancy continued to treat a person as optional.

The power, being wise, called that enough. Being benevolent, it called it beautiful. It did not say so aloud. Speech, even for a power, is expensive on the table, and the who was already obvious.
