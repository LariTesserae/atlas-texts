The attention does not arrive as light. Light has already been weighed here and found wanting, a hypothesis the highland itself discarded in favor of darker, truer weather. It arrives instead as a listening so complete that the wind, for a handful of its own long notes, forgets to play.

The power is older than the fever that shoved this mass upward. It has watched continents of stone learn patience, watched stars finish themselves into iron and then into quiet. It does not hurry. It sits in the grain of the highland the way rime sits on a colder face: present, unmelting, asking nothing yet.

It sees the knives of new splits still weeping glass. It hears the singers map voids that will not be the same voids when the song ends. It tastes the tannin-iron breath of stomata the size of grottos. It watches a cutter’s platform slowly become a thinking gall, the lashings already half a memory the plant intends to keep. It watches a predator remain architecture so long that the cambium begins to grow around the theorem of its limbs. Everything here is already in conversation with everything else. Nothing is unfinished in a way that constitutes injury. The emptiness itself is an organ doing its work.

A lesser kindness would have brought a sun. A clumsier wisdom would have taught the mass the patience of erosion all at once, rounding every edge, filling every vault with the long sigh of finished stone. This power does neither. To finish a becoming before it has finished tasting itself is only another kind of consumption, and the highland is already so very good at consuming.

What it does, it does as weather.

Once, after a condensation that is not quite rain and not quite frost, a new mere appears in a fold that has never held water before. The water is black, still, indistinguishable from every other mere except in this: it does not vanish when the walls drink. It remains, a dark lens that remembers the shape of whatever looks into it. A grazer drinks and, for the first time, knows the wall it rasped last season. A singer hangs above it and the drone that leaves its body returns carrying an echo of the drone that will leave it next year. A cutter, passing, sees their own stained hands and, for a moment longer than the highland usually permits, knows they were the ones who cut.

The mere is not a gift of light. It is a gift of duration, small enough to be mistaken for the highland’s own next thought. The plant-mass grows around it as it grows around everything, but the water stays, a patient bruise that will not fade. In time the singers learn to aim their longest notes at it. In longer time the cutters begin to leave their split-wood blades nearby, not because the blades will last, but because the mere will remember that they were left.

The power does not stay to watch what the highland makes of this. It has already understood. The mass will absorb the remembering the same way it absorbs ropes and rings and bodies: completely, without asking permission, turning even continuity into another internal pressure that shoves new ridges toward the cold. That, too, is correct.

The wind finds its voices again. The drones resume. Somewhere a fresh split opens with the old tearing roar that becomes silence that becomes chord. The highland continues the impolite, magnificent work of being more than one thing at once, forever finishing, never finished.

The power, having listened, is satisfied. It leaves the taste of iron and unripe tannin unchanged. It leaves the darkness its positive weather. It leaves the cutters their grain and the singers their maps.

It does nothing else.

That, too, is a kindness.
