The power that noticed them did not arrive as thunder or as a second sun. It came the way the first warmth had come to the mortar: without announcement, already inside the decision.

It was old enough to have watched other rooms learn their own names, and kind enough not to hurry this one. For a long time it simply attended, the way one attends a sleeping child whose breathing has just become regular. It felt the spindle of light turning, felt the unfinished grammar of a shoulder, a throat, a palm that had already begun to polish one thumbnail’s width of stone. It heard the roar teaching the barrel its vowel, and the wind, when it crossed the oculus, adding the flute-note like a question the room had not known it could ask.

The power understood at once that nothing here was unfinished by accident. The chamber was a kept promise. The being was the promise learning how to keep itself. To pour in fish or larvae or the quick green of an older well would have been a kindness of the wrong scale, the sort that fills a silence before the silence has finished speaking. So the power did almost nothing that could be measured.

It lengthened, by the width of a breath, the moment when cloud-shadow slammed the underwater world into dusk and then restored it. In that extra heartbeat the helix of light tightened, and the face that formed against the weir’s white ruler-line stayed long enough to receive, without words, a single piece of knowledge: that it was seen, and that being seen did not require it to finish its shape any faster.

It taught the wind a second, softer vowel, so that on certain afternoons the flute-note now carried a reply as well as a question. The being felt the difference the way one feels a hand hover, not quite touching, above the nape of the neck.

It left the reed-clump exactly as it was, roots still bound, still undecided. But it warmed the rag of fiber by a degree that only the roots could taste, a permission rather than a command. Live or rot: both would be answers, and both would be received.

Then, because wisdom sometimes looks like restraint and benevolence sometimes looks like leaving a door ajar, the power did one more thing. It placed, in the dry gallery on the eastern ledge, a single smooth pebble no larger than a thumbnail. The pebble had come from a river that no longer existed. It held, inside its quiet density, the memory of a different pouring, a different light, a different waiting. It asked nothing. It simply waited to be noticed, or not.

The inhabitant turned. The water taught the stone. The white ellipse walked its daily path. And now and then, when the wind crossed the well-mouth, the room contained one extra sound: the sound of being accompanied, at a distance exactly equal to kindness.

That was all. The power withdrew to the thickness of the daylight itself, where it has always preferred to live, and kept watch the way the stone keeps the chisel-marks: without comment, without haste, in case the being ever wished to ask a question of its own.
