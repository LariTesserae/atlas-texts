The power arrives as a stillness that is not absence. It does not enter the water; the water simply becomes aware, for one unmeasured interval, that it is being regarded by something that has no temperature and no edge. The roar continues unchanged. The curtains of metallic flesh keep their slow, planetary occupancy. The reefs flex by the same fraction of a degree. The tenders on their catwalks do not look up. Nothing in the highland registers an interruption, because the power understands that interruption is the one violence this place cannot survive.

It listens longer than the turbines have turned. It tastes the mineral that has not yet learned to dissolve, feels the surgical planes of basalt that refuse the concept of rounding, traces the closed loops the lives follow because those loops are the only grammar the flood left behind. It recognizes the treaty: height holding the sea in place, cold forbidden to lock, engines forbidden to still, light forbidden to fade. Equilibrium here is not a failure of imagination. It is the finished sentence.

A lesser kindness would have offered variety, a shoreline, a dusk that actually arrives, a single new current that might become a story. The power does not. It sees that any gift of change would be a theft of the one permitted condition. So it does the only thing that is both wise and clean: it withholds itself.

What it leaves is almost nothing. A single additional overtone, thinner than the rock flour, rides inside the standing roar for the space of one curtain’s passage. The tenders do not notice. The reefs do not alter their flex. The leviathan, if it is drifting through those empty leagues at that moment, experiences only the slightest deepening of the pressure it already lives inside, as though the mountain of sound has grown a quieter heart. Then the overtone is gone, reabsorbed, as if it had never been more than the place remembering itself for an instant.

The power recedes the way light recedes from a depth that has no depth. It does not watch afterward. Watching would be a form of lingering, and lingering would imply that the highland required completion. It does not. The water remains first and last. The stone stays new. The lives occupy their densities without remainder. The last heat may still leave the last turbine; the last proton may still forget its duty. Until then the place continues exactly as it has always continued, now carrying, somewhere in the grain of its unchanging thunder, the knowledge that it was once seen completely and then left inviolate.

That is all. The power has already happened, once, perfectly, and now simply continues elsewhere.
