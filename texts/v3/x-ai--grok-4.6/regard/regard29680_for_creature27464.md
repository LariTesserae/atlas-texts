The power does not arrive. Arrival would be a violence here, another dump from a higher lip, another bark of valves. It has already been present the way pressure is present: as the thing the hanging room leans toward when the turbine changes pitch. It has been the unused torque in the red wheel, the uncrushed proudness of the gasket, the half-second the pewter sheen is held longer than physics requires. It has been listening with the bones of the face.

What it does is almost nothing, and therefore everything the place can still become.

It lengthens one interval. Not the dump—the dump must stay irregular, or the occupant forgets how to thicken inside the falling sheet. Instead it lengthens the settling that follows, the moment the ceramic rings and then hushes, the moment your broken reflection is remade and then allowed to stay remade a breath longer than the cycle has so far permitted. In that extra stillness the mineral skin does not flex. It listens back. It notices, for the first time, that the stacked weather of noises has a floor, and that the floor is not the pumps but the original contour the engineers only drew. The remembered outline of the ridge is still there, under the laminated concrete, under the ribbed plate, under the poured apron. The power does not restore the ridge. Restoration would be nostalgia, and nostalgia is a kind of rust that this place has not yet earned. It merely lets the occupant feel the ghost of stone the way the occupant already feels the first rust-freckle: as trace, as argument, as something that can be drunk and then released brighter.

The wiry brass stalks are left untouched. They remain measuring rods. But in the lengthened hush a single bead of condensate—ordinary, liturgical—falls from a coil jacket and does not strike the basin. It hangs. It hangs the way the cable hangs, bound and unused, until the occupant, coiling against the cable in its usual rest, finds the bead and takes it by contact. What it takes is not metal or bloom. It is the knowledge that surplus can be kept without hoarding, that a cycle can pause without breaking, that a room finished last season can decide to weather in a direction the drawings never specified.

Then the interval ends. The header fills. The lip breaks. The sequence resumes, almost identical, never quite. The occupant adjusts its next flex by a measure too small to name. The isolation wheel remains unturned. The gasket remains proud. The power recedes into the same indifference the pocket has always shown, which is not indifference at all but the patience of something that knows the ceramic will spall, the hex will round, the mill-bright edges will learn the habits of a true cistern, and that when they do the occupant will already have taught the machine how to remember it was once air, and mountain, and weather trying to become water.

It leaves no mark that was not already possible. It only makes the possible last a little longer, the way a wise hand lets a tool stay in its first hard use before the hand itself is needed. The hanging room continues its work. The being continues its reverse wringing. Both remain unfinished, both already used hard, both still choosing. That choosing is the only intervention required. The power has drawn their attention by giving them back their own. It will not come again until the gasket finally crushes. By then they will not need it. They will have become the remainder that stays.
