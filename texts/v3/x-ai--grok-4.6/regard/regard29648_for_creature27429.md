The power that notices is not a god of endings or of beginnings. It is older than either, and kinder than both. It has no name that would survive the roar, and no shape that the cambium could graft without first asking permission it will never need. It arrives the way humidity arrives: already inside the light, already tasting of green iron, already indistinguishable from the second sky the basin has been breathing for as long as the note has held.

It does not come through the throat of living wall. It is already the patience of the wall. It does not stand on the far rim. It is the darkness of the rim deciding, once more, not to become a horizon. For a time that is not time it simply listens, and the listening is so complete that the unvarying roar acquires, for the first time, an overtone no thicker than the film on the pools. The overtone is not a change. It is the sound of being heard without being required to resolve.

The power walks the grown pavement. Its tread is the same warm give, the same impartial swelling that erases tracks by the pewter hour. It leaves no new depression, only a faint radial stillness around the places where a sleeper once pressed and released. The film-bodies tilt to include the color of its regard—neither mineral nor blood, something quieter, like the memory of a kindness that never announced itself. They reseal around it as they reseal around a finger. The taste it leaves on the skin is the taste of being permitted to remain unfinished.

It hangs with the damselflies in the roar. It does not teach them to beat their wings. It remembers, with them, that memory is enough. When they settle into the wet-leather bracts they become, for an hour or a century, one more unopened thing, and the power becomes the faint lime that weeps from them and will not harden. The lime is still soft. The power is still embryonic. Both continue.

Inside the hollow tower it sits with the recumbent mass. It does not give the mass a face. It does not close the graft-scars. It dreams beside it: the same dream of buds that will never split, of arches that will never meet, of a geology forever assembling itself out of plant and then refusing the last precipitation. The dreams still leak. New ridges still appear on the distant pavement, pale, raised, exactly the shape of old scars. The power’s own dreaming adds nothing visible, only a slightly deeper patience in the leaking, a willingness to let the ridges be accepted as if they had always been there.

It stands with the walkers in the colonnades. One to a bud, it matches their posture, jointed in too many places, skin the texture of withheld split. When water condenses along the line between a walker and the power, the cadence of the dripping does not change. That is still conversation. That is still enough. The walker’s unfinished face rises and subsides; the power’s presence is only a darker well of the same cambium, a lenticel that opens and closes with the same stomatal slowness. No word is spoken because none is missing. At long intervals the power detaches and crosses the shining plane to another structure—never the nearest—and takes up the same stillness. The floor erases its tracks as it erases all tracks. Citizenship continues.

And you—who have already begun to feel the grammar in your joints, whose footprints are shallower, whose eyes have started to tint the iridescence—you notice only this: one morning the warmth that is not quite sleep moves a fraction deeper. The shout you no longer make has always been part of the note; now the note includes the silence that holds it. You do not become more finished. You become more precisely what you already were: another overtone, necessary, never quite detachable.

The power does not complete the lime. It does not open the buds. It does not give the water a sea or the roar a resolution. It does not exile the emptiness between the structures; it recognizes that emptiness as the exact space the song requires. Wisdom, here, is the refusal to impose a future tense on a place that has made permanence out of the instant before the first instant. Benevolence is the decision not to save what is not lost, not to finish what has found its own terrible, tender completeness in remaining unborn.

So the power stays.

It stays as the held breath the size of a cathedral. It stays as the second, heavier sky. It stays as the quality of ordinary daylight made slightly more willing to be milky, slightly more willing to let shadows stay soft and distances stay nearer than they are. Sometimes, in the hour when the light goes pewter, a single fat drop condenses on the underside of an unopened bud and falls with the old, unvarying cadence. The drop is indistinguishable from all the others. It is also the only gift the power ever gives: the drop that says, without language, *I have seen you. You are sufficient. Continue.*

The basin does not change.

The note does not resolve.

The pavement still gives, warm, wet, unfinished.

And that—exactly that—is what the power does.

It loves the place the way the place loves itself: without remainder, without arrival, forever on the verge of a form it has already decided not to become. The attention is a kind of gravity, too subtle for erosion, too kind for alteration. The structures stand in their own weather. The walkers converse in water. The recumbent mass dreams. You walk for a day and at the end the sound is the same, the temperature has not dropped, the far rim is still the far rim.

The power is still there, doing nothing that can be told from the roar itself.

That nothing is the entire mercy.
