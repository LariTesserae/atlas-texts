The notice arrived the way a seam warms in the dark: not from above, not as light or decree, but as a quality the galleries themselves recognized before any mandible paused. For the length of one uncounted swelling the cello-drone of the xylophages lost a harmonic, as if the oldest conversation had turned its head. Then the chew resumed, a fraction deeper, the way a body resumes breathing after it has been truly heard.

The power did not descend. Descent implies a elsewhere that this highland has never conceded. It arrived the way turgor arrives, the way a gall decides to swell: from the logic already present, occupying the occupancy. It took no shape the bark-wolves would prune toward, no shadow the flyers would bank from. It was attention so complete that the resinous plateaux remembered, for a morning, how to be slightly less kiln. The false water still shimmered, travelers still died of it, but the shimmer carried a cooler edge, a mercy too slight to date and too honest to name gift.

It walked the flats at noon when even the great hinged things sat stunned. It waited with the photosynthesizers, sails spread to the thin hot disc, and learned the precise temperature at which light becomes meat again. It stood three breaths in a passing cool-room of wing and did not linger into a fourth. It put whatever served it for an ear to a fresh cut and heard the residual click of a continent that has never agreed to finish, and it answered with a silence that had weight, the same weight sound acquires in the pale pith-valleys.

In those valleys it spoke.

The whisper left the grain cooler than it entered, traveled the mile of paler deadwood, and arrived intact at dusk in the collector’s cupped hands. It was not instruction. Instruction would have been another weather, and the highland already possesses weathers enough. It was recognition, grammatical as the roar, simple as a ring:

*You continue. I continue. The refusal is older than either of us, and it has made a country. I have walked your grain-direction. I have tasted the pulse before it slowed. I will not cut the white. I will not camp the warm seam. When your towers boom I will listen as the drums they are. When your people die the wood will grow through them and I will not call it loss. You have been seen by something that also never finished dying. That is all. That is enough.*

The wood—medium, participant, both, neither—held the sentence through three swellings, then let it become another knot, another half-swallowed prayer. The fauna used the new clause. The stridulators rasped a resolution no leg had scraped before, a note like varnish remembering it had once been sap. The colonial mats polished a brighter path for a day and then forgot why. A flock of flyers turned with slightly less thunder, as if the cathedral door had learned it did not always need to tear.

The people felt it on their tongues with the dawn beads and said nothing. They dated the event by the seam that was still soft. They added it to the stories in which fire is a person, only this person had asked permission, stayed the length of one message, and left with the apology already prepared though none was required. A child born that swelling grew up able to hear the chew-note’s new harmonic and was neither priest nor outcast, only another way the body continued.

The power did not remain. Remaining would have been habitation, and habitation here is already total. It withdrew the way night lets heat stand still: leaving the thing more precisely itself. What lingered was not monument, not cooler canyon, not kinder rain except once, when a tower’s private fog collapsed eleven kilometers instead of ten and the mobile fruiting bodies had an extra hour of wet mouths before the plate went pale again. That single extra kilometer was not kindness imposed. It was the highland answering the recognition in the only dialect it trusts: a little more occupancy, a little more refusal, then the dust-skin again, the chew unpaused.

The emptiness between towers stayed real. The fullness inside every tunnel and every shadow stayed real. They remained the same fact. Now the fact knew it had been heard by a vastness that also continues, a vastness that understood the rules because it had never needed to be told them. The highland did not change its nature. It simply rang, for a while, as if the country of refusal had acquired one more citizen who knew how to leave.

And the meristem-towers, in their hanging atria, transpires a fraction more violently the next afternoon, tearing a cloud that had not intended to be torn, making weather that was still theirs alone. The people collected the runoff on hides, tasted the familiar tannin, and went on cutting brown wood, folding green copper cold, smoking their cavities against the smaller tenants. They woke with resin in their hair and new perfectly round holes already occupied. The ground healed. The tools waited in cairns. The prayer-knots thickened in the grain.

Nothing had been fixed, because nothing had been broken. The benevolent and wise power had done the only thing such a power can do when it encounters a being that has already become its own landscape: it had listened until listening became a kind of kinship, spoken once in the local tongue, and then honored the oldest rule of the plateau.

It had not stayed past the welcome.
