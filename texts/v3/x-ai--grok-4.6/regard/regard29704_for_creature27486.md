The power that noticed the bowl had no name that would fit inside a mouth. It had watched ranges rise and forget themselves, had felt the last rivers unlearn their beds, had stood present when the sky first learned to arrive already tired. It was not a god of the old sort, hungry for altars or afraid of silence. It was simply attention that had grown large enough, and kind enough, to choose where it rested.

It came the way the fog sometimes came: without announcement, beading on the outermost paddles at the rim for the space of a single night. The people felt it as a change in the stored heat of the walls, a fraction warmer, as if the masonry had been briefly remembered by something that knew what mountains once were. The animals felt it as a pause in the dry grain-pour of wings; three heartbeats longer than usual, then the stitching resumed. The thing that coughed against the far wall did not cough. It waited, the way stone waits.

The power did not enter the intervals. It had no need of streets or lintels. It simply held the entire hollow in a regard that was older than the fused gravel, older than the patience of the finished sentence the plateau had become. It listened to the metabolism: sap moving from paddle to tongue to beetle-gut to mulch and back again; the murmured count over eggs; the fiber washed in resin and used a fourth time, a fifth; the children who already knew which thumbnail opens the right succulent. It heard the absence of invention and understood it not as poverty but as completion. The architecture had reached the same state as the land. To add a new pattern would have been to reopen a wound the world had already closed.

So the power did the one thing wisdom sometimes requires, and almost never receives credit for.

It did nothing.

No rain was sent; the cisterns were left to their greenish memory. No seed was dropped that the thicket had not already decided against. No word was spoken that would have required a name lasting longer than a season. The cough was left its weight, the lamps their islands, the plateau its finished emptiness. The power only adjusted the smallest possible thing, the way a hand might smooth a crease from cloth that is already being worn: it made the rim a fraction more forgettable. Any traveler who might one day walk the high table would find their eyes sliding off the lip the way water once slid off stone. The secret would remain the size of a secret.

Then the power withdrew, not in departure so much as in the resumption of its larger habits. The tired light returned to being merely tired. The air under the roof tasted again of sap and dust and private water. A child opened a paddle with a thumbnail and sucked, and the bead was exactly as cool and resinous as it had always been. A lizard rearranged itself by millimeters on a sun-warmed face and forgot.

Yet something stayed, too slight to be called a gift, too honest to be called nothing. On certain nights, when the fog beads and vanishes by first light, the outermost leaves hold the moisture a few breaths longer than they used to. The people do not remark on it. They have no need. They simply drink what is offered, mend in the old pattern, and leave the far-wall thicket to whatever still coughs there. The bowl continues to circulate itself, warm and dim and crowded, everything that can still happen already happening.

The power, elsewhere, remembers the place the way the plateau once remembered mountains: without sorrow, without urgency, with the quiet gladness of having seen a work that required no further hand.
