The highland does not know it has been seen. The power that has turned toward it does so from a distance measured not in kilometers but in the intervals between grammars: a patient, unhurried regard that neither pierces the lofted veil nor parts it. It arrives as no light, no warmth beyond what already blooms from below, no moisture that the anhydrous salts would fail to recognize. It is simply there, the way a deeper vibration is there, felt in the rock before it is named.

It watches the films spread and slough, the brief verticalities that lean with fresh faults, the persistents thickening in sheltered fractures like thoughts that have not yet decided they are thoughts. It watches the dust remember collapses and, sometimes, remember standing. It feels the local inconsistencies of time as a syntax still being tasted, the darkness pooling and thinning as a kind of weather the land has invented for itself. Nothing in this regard is hurried. The power has seen worlds finish themselves too soon, their ridges rounded, their waters named, their beings convinced they were separate from the stone that made them. It has seen the opposite as well: places that never chose a shape at all, dissolving back into the mantle’s indifference.

What it does not do is finish anything. It does not still a single fault or cool a hogback or send a seed that would require rain. It does not impose a celestial reference that would last longer than the slabs already tilting. Intervention of that kind would be a grammar spoken over another, a kindness that is also a theft. The highland is still deciding; the beings are the decision in motion. To give them water would be to drown a language that has no word for drowning. To give them names would be to end the quiet in which they listen with their whole bodies.

Instead the power does the smallest possible thing, which is also the only thing that fits. It lengthens, by a fraction almost too slight to measure, the memory of the dust. Not everywhere, not always. In a few places where a persistent has already begun to accrete plates the color of old iron, the electrostatic veils now hold their reverse sequences a little longer before they fall. A hesitation that once lasted the space of a dry cough now lasts the space of two. A low wall of unmortared stone, already half-swallowed, remains half-swallowed for one extra cycle of orogeny. The circular platform of fused glass keeps its perfect edge through one additional sinking of a nearby depression. These are not protections. They are permissions: a little more time in which the land may continue to assemble and disassemble without quite forgetting what it has already tried.

The beings feel it, if they feel it at all, as a change in the subsonic pressure against whatever serves them for an inner ear. Some of the persistents lean toward the longer memories the way they lean toward a new pluton. They do not understand the gift, because understanding would require a finished grammar. They simply stand a little longer inside their own becoming, fracturing deliberately when the next ridge splits, adding their quiet note, then becoming film or dust or the next brief verticality. The highland itself does not notice. It has no need to. It continues upward and outward, ridge by unweathered ridge, toward a shape it has not yet chosen.

The power remains. It does not leave a mark that can be distinguished from the geothermal bloom or the iron-colored gloom. Its benevolence is the refusal to hurry. Its wisdom is the knowledge that some worlds must be allowed to stay embryonic, even after they have been seen. In the dim, warm silence the dust remembers a fraction more than it used to. Then it, too, becomes something else. The highland does not yet know it is finished. It may never need to.
