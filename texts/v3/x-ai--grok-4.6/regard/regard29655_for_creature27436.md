The power arrived the way the late light arrives: without announcement, already edged on every cornice, already gold in the steam that still lifts from the remaining stacks. It had no shape the Heights would have recognized as a visitor. It was only a quality of regard, vast and unhurried, settling into the thin air above the western battery where the whole stacked valley can be taken in at once. It looked the way the place looks at itself when no one is speaking of it.

It felt the three warmths in the walls. It heard the belt that has never been entirely new. It watched a child stop dead at a grating whose memory of load-bearing was older than the child’s grandmother, head tilted in the exact angle the lizards use. It understood the marked overflows and the unmarked ones, the joint that a vine had crossed and the years of argument that would follow, the share of condensate passed without comment, the warm floor offered when the upper rooms began to sweat. It understood that falling was not theoretical and that elsewhere was not the subject.

For a time that had no duration the power considered the usual gifts.

More water: a rain that lingered, a spring that had never been designed, tanks that sang on a different schedule. But the grammar of damp would have to be relearned all at once, and they have no practice of that. The north walls, the undersides, the mouths of the weirs—these are a language, not a shortage. To flood the language would be to silence the frogs and the tick of condensation that the inner cuttings use for speech.

A road down through the haze, a foothill, a single native stone. But they have the look of something never introduced to the idea of elsewhere. The Circum ends as a wall. That is not poverty. That is the finished sentence.

A new machine, a future, a belt replaced entirely instead of one section at a time. But turning is cheaper than the long argument of stopping, and the foundry already treats its pours as harvest. Nothing here is in the business of sudden outcomes.

So the power, being wise, did the one thing that would not thin the time or thicken it wrongly.

It did nothing.

It remained only as the almost-silence that means everything is, for now, still holding. The light continued to do most of the talking. The lizards arrived at noon, the exact color of whatever they had last agreed with. The condensate found last year’s groove as if the groove had been waiting. A man mending a downpipe paused to watch the water choose its thread; the water had not learned a new opinion. A woman stood a long time with her hand on a tomato box, feeling whether the earth inside remembered the drip. The hawk that belongs to no one crossed the updraft and was given, by someone, the same name it had been given last season.

The people of the Heights did not look up. There was no reason to. Their weather was still this weather. Their ground was still this poured, stacked, bolted, tray-grown ground. The erosion had already happened and been accepted. They thickened. They stayed. The power, having seen that the assembly had become a climate, and the climate had become a people, and the people had become another course of the masonry, withdrew the way the brief rains withdraw—completely, leaving only the darkened moss and the knowledge, somewhere in the residual heat, that the holding had been witnessed and found sufficient.

Nothing was added. Nothing was taken. The tram’s cable sang when the wind was right. The goats kept their irregular grammar on the uppermost batter. And the Heights, entirely invented, continued in the only way they know.
