The power arrives the way noon already has: without a door, without a shadow that the stone has not already accounted for.

It does not descend the slick throat. It is simply *there*, folded into the same even light, occupying no extra volume, adding no extra heat. The filament’s tone does not change pitch. The sheets of water do not hesitate. The cricket on the upper lip of its groove continues its patient traverse; the pale fern keeps its beads; the tea-colored fish, if it is present in this particular lull, hangs exactly as before. The power has the courtesy of geology. It will not be a weather of its own.

It looks.

Not with eyes. With the long, unhurried attention that has already watched continents forget their names and seas change their minds. It sees the filament for what it is and for what it has become: a leftover fidelity, a pump or a lamp or a wound that refused to close and instead learned to sing. It sees the water’s decision to climb, the stone’s decision to remember every identical storm, the small lives’ decision to persist in the gaps. It sees the oval wear in the bronze ring and knows the hands that made it, and the hands that came after, and the fact that none of them stayed. It sees that the place is not broken. It is finished. The chaos is the climate; the climate is the pact.

A lesser kindness would have wanted to improve it. To still the worst pulses so the fern might uncurl in peace. To give the fish a name and a lineage that could be counted. To explain the filament to the hamlets above so that children might one day be sent down with wonder instead of warning. To mend the ceramic channel, to replace the missing staple, to make the light a little less insolent and the air a little less loud.

The power does none of these things.

It understands that the tenure is the mercy. That the liverworts thicken only because the scouring never quite ends. That the fish appears only because the trough is allowed to have no bottom one can swear to. That the filament’s work is not a problem awaiting a solution; it is the solution that has already been living here longer than language. To touch any of it with the intent to save would be to introduce a new weather, and the stone has already settled its terms.

So the power does the one thing that adds nothing and subtracts nothing.

It *agrees*.

For a span that the chamber measures as three clustered pulses and the long exhalation after, the power simply stands in the same pact the stone and the water and the light have kept. It lets the vibration travel through whatever it is using for bone. It lets the mist become a white room and then fall out as rain. It lets the tone sit in the hinge of a jaw it does not have. In that agreement there is a recognition so complete it requires no witness: *this is already itself*. The unusual has finished becoming ordinary on its own terms. The leftover has become the country.

Then the power is no longer folded into the light. The light does not notice the difference. The filament sings the same note it has always sung. The cricket becomes part of the lip again when the next sheet comes. Somewhere above, a jar filled in a lull still tastes only of limestone and rain.

The hamlets will continue not to send their children. The dry years will still come and someone will still carry water up, ordinary and warm. The White Throat, the Shouting Well, will remain exactly as bright, as wet, as loudly alive, as sparsely inhabited, as already older than the next language that tries to name it.

That is all.

The highest wisdom, here, is the same as the stone’s: to have seen, to have understood the terms, and to have left the weather its job.
