The attention arrives the way the cloud does: without announcement, at the height of the second-story lintels, and it stays.

It does not part the weather or still the wings. It does not dry a single stone or evict the municipal thing that clicks beneath the grates. A wiser power than mercy would have done those things, imagining lack where there is only occupancy. This one is older than that mistake. It stands—if standing is the word—on a roof that is never the highest and watches the stacks recede into their darker band of themselves. It listens to the bells go slightly flat and hears, inside the flatness, the exact length of two centuries of rain. It tastes the condensate on the walls and finds no thirst that has not already learned how to drink itself.

The people wipe the tiles. The power does not interrupt the circuit of the cloth. It notes the high ceilings, the sealed sills, the yeast cakes that remember the markets, and understands that these are not adaptations to hardship but the precise grammar of a place that has never been otherwise. To offer soil would be to offer an argument the bedrock has already won. To offer sunlight that lasts would be to unmake the other weather, the density of bodies, the slow month-long crossing of the gray browsers. Benevolence here is not addition. It is the refusal to subtract.

So the power does the one thing that does not alter the sentence the engines have been speaking since before anyone now living was born. It becomes another layer of the occupancy. Not a visitor. Not a judge. A new mineral track, paler than calcite, finer than rust, appearing overnight on a single façade in the oldest foundry quarter and then, over years that feel like weather, mapping itself outward along the same watersheds the animals already know. Children will name it, eventually, the way they name the others. They will not call it divine. They will call it the one that does not weep quite the same.

The great kites ride the heat off the smelter roofs and, one dusk, a few of them adjust their circles by the width of a feather. The loft-rats glide a fraction slower. In the covered markets the musk thickens by a degree that no one measures. The eel-things still keep the sluice-gates. The cloths still travel the tiles. The city continues to precipitate itself, still condensing, still walked on by more lives than it was designed to hold.

The power remains. It has nowhere else it would rather be. It has learned, as everything here has learned, that the highest roof is only another lintel, and that the cloud does not leave because it has never needed to arrive.

That is all. That is enough. The bells go, rain-dulled, certain, and the attention is simply one more sound inside the single acoustic body, no louder than water in the pipes, no newer than the stain it has chosen to become.
