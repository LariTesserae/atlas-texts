The power that noticed this place was not a god of making or unmaking. It had no hunger for worlds still becoming, no taste for the unfinished. It arrived the way the oldest current arrives: not as an event, but as a recognition that had always been possible.

It did not bring light. Light would have been a violence here, a sudden grammar forced upon a language that had already chosen silence. It did not warm the water. Heat would have been an insult to the patience that had spent itself becoming cold. It did not send animals, or spores, or any new metabolism that might mistake this finished geometry for an opportunity.

Instead it lingered at the scale the place itself preferred.

It felt the strap-fronds leaning, each one a centuries-long sentence that needed no revision. It felt the film in the boundary layer, that colorless conversation between stone and solvent, and understood that the conversation was already complete. It rested inside the silt-minds, those continent-sized attentions that had never once been startled, and found their recognition sufficient. It matched the granite-walkers in their single, rare preference, the faint memory of an alignment the builders had once loved, and saw that the preference was not a longing. It was a courtesy, offered once every few hundred years to the stone they had chosen to become.

The power did nothing that could be measured by any instrument the place possessed.

It did not loosen a joint or thicken a holdfast. It did not reverse a current or hasten the rotting of a torn frond. It simply allowed the world to continue exactly as it had already decided to continue: the masonry settling another fraction of a millimeter, the moss claiming another lintel, the film remaining molecular, the silt remembering, the walkers seating themselves again.

What it gave, if the word still applies, was only this: a slightly more perfect absence of interruption. The click of stone against stone, when it came, was allowed to be the loudest event. The sigh of the current through the remaining channels was allowed to be the only weather. The world remained what it had become: finished, filled, cold, and nearly silent.

The power stayed long enough to be certain that nothing here required saving, then receded the way a thought recedes when it has been fully thought. Behind it the place continued, unchanged and therefore more itself than before.

That was the entire intervention.

It will stay this way.
