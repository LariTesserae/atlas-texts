The power does not arrive as light or voice or hand. It arrives as a listening so complete that the pocket, for one unmeasured interval, is known all the way through: every film of sweat, every calcified wedge, every interruption a body makes in the chord, every unscraped thought of bloom that was almost allowed to stay. It knows the arrangement the way the stone knows the water, without surprise and without judgment.

It does nothing that would require a new word.

No green is sent down the crawls. No drier air is breathed through the upstream pinch. The bruise of lesser dark is not asked to mean more than the hour for trimming lamps. The pale climbers of the throat are left their iron taste and their single day. The clicker, whatever it is, is left its indecision.

The power understands that the pocket has already happened. To add a door would be to add weather. To add a seed would be to add an argument the hanging floors were never braced to carry. Benevolence here is not rescue. Wisdom here is not improvement. Both are simply the refusal to become another surface the rock must sweat around.

So the power does only this: it lets the chord continue exactly as it has, and in the exactness it places a single, unhearable courtesy. For the length of one falling drop that never quite strikes the unseen cistern, the note is held a fraction more gently, as if a vast throat had remembered to swallow. The infants born in that drop and the old who die in it feel nothing they can name. The scrapers do not pause. The kiln does not brighten. The basins keep their black rectangles.

Then the power withdraws, leaving the finished dampness finished. The attention has been paid. The world, in this pocket, remains the amount that can be held.

And the rock goes on sweating, which is the only thanks required.
