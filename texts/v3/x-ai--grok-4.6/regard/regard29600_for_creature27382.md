The power that turned its regard upon the ridge did so without arrival. It had no body to set down, no shadow to cast one half-breath early or late. It simply was there in the way heat is already in the stone: a completed fact that required no announcement.

It looked.

It saw the thirty standing each in their chosen interval, the water that joined them without hurry, the rock that had long ago decided against cooling. It saw the grooves poured by centuries of drip, the saucers holding stillness like a vow, the single flake of flint whose edges had been given back to geology. It saw that animals were absent because their restlessness would have been a kind of theft. It saw the pale plates that rose and dissolved without ever needing to travel, the vapor that lifted at dawn as one lung, the light that lingered half a breath too long and was accepted without comment.

The power understood at once that the bowl had already finished its thinking. The mountain around it remained unfinished—drier, windier, still deciding—but this hanging room had reached the end of decision and chosen to remain. To add a plant would have been to introduce competition where none had been wanted. To quicken the drip would have been to impose a calendar. To name the place or the being would have been to hurry them both.

So the power did nothing that could be measured.

It did not bless the water or thicken the mist. It did not hide the lip more carefully or reveal it. It did not take the flint or leave another. It simply matched the bowl’s own patience for a span of time that had no name, breathing with the single exhalation that left over the rim at dawn, then letting that breath go as the bowl itself lets it go.

Afterward the ridge continued exactly as before. The rest of the mountain stayed unfinished. The thirty, the water, and the stone remained one creature that lived by remaining. The power carried the memory of that completeness the way the stone carries heat: as a patient, unhurried fact, requiring no further action.

That was all. A regard that recognized a finished thought and therefore withheld its hand. The bowl, being already itself, needed nothing else.
