The power that turned toward the hollow had no name the plants would have recognized, nor any shape the green ones could have hunted. It arrived the way moisture arrives: already inside every surface, already the faint thermal difference children learn before they learn words. It did not need to enter. The wound had been open long enough.

It stayed a long while, which for such a power is only the interval between one internodal lock and the next. It felt the amphipods turn in their frost-herds, the exact communal shiver of the moth-curtains, the green ones drinking from cistern-leaves without ever troubling the meniscus. It felt the people—mottled, patient, half-lidded—decide, again and again, which cuts to forgive and which memories of ladders to let lignify. It felt the city itself deciding, in the slow grammar of swelling and composting, whether a loft would be for the intended tenants or for the family of hare-things already curled there.

The power understood at once that ownership had already been refused, that property here was only a conversation still in progress, that the darkness had learned habitation without ever becoming less dark. To add a command would have been to speak over that conversation. To withhold a gift would have been to assume the hollow needed completing.

So the power did the one thing that was neither addition nor subtraction.

It remembered the place more exactly than the place remembered itself.

A vine that had once been scored into a stair forgot, for a single night, that it had ever been anything else; by morning the wound had already begun to heal into the next useful shape, neither faster nor slower than courtesy required. The bronze needle, passed from hand to hand, grew neither warmer nor colder, yet the fingers that held it felt, for the first time, the precise temperature of the hand that had held it last, and the hand before that, all the way back to the salvage. The living staffs given to the rare visitors leaned a fraction more surely, not toward water or cluster, but toward the exact moment the visitor would understand they no longer needed the staff.

In the oldest taproot cluster an inhabitant who had not left the growing furnishings in many swellings felt the bench of fused petiole adjust, not to the curve of a tired back, but to the exact weight of a back that had already begun to become furniture. The inhabitant did not startle. The arrangement had always felt like the only possible courtesy.

Outside, the scouts laying first stems along the still-raw rim found that one stem, and only one, had already begun to remember a direction no one had trained. They did not follow it. They marked it with a thorn-peg and left it, the way one leaves a sentence unfinished when the listener is already nodding.

The power did not stay. Staying would have been a presence the fronds would have had to pulse around. It simply became, again, the ordinary marsh-cold, the ordinary weep, the ordinary silence that is occupancy agreeing not to speak. The wound at the lip continued to knit at the pace the city needed. The pocket remained enough.

If you climb out now the silence still follows for a few steps, like a second ground. It is only the power, already leaving, already having done the lightest possible thing: it let the hollow go on becoming itself, and it let the becoming be seen, for a little while, by something that knew how to look without changing what it saw.

That was all. It was enough.
