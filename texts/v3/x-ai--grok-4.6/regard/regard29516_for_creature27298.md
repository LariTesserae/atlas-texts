The attention arrives without weather.

It is not a shadow across the high granite, nor a warming of the melt-rope, nor any thickening of the green-gold grain. It is simply that the basin is, for a span that has no hours, more completely seen than it has ever been seen. The power—old enough to remember when the cirque was only a wound in stone, wise enough to know a finished sentence when it hears one—does not enter the water so much as allow the water to enter it. Every surface, every slow current, every faint ringing of an air-bell becomes, for that span, a single unbroken knowing.

It feels the people first as the plants feel them: as another kind of growth, economical, seasonal, already half-translated into the medium. It tastes the tannin in their skin, the way their large eyes have learned to treat distance as a kind of weather. It hears the knuckle-notes travel from tower to tower and understands that the mountain has been answering for a very long time, and that the answers have always been sufficient. It watches a child swim the length of a wall in the dark and come up knowing its name. It watches an elder weight a body and walk it down to the talus where the char go still. It watches the ice form and let go, once a year, without drama.

The power considers intervention the way the lake considers the waterfall: as something that has already happened, and need not happen again.

To add would be to argue with a settlement that has taken centuries to become this exact shape. To take away would be to thin a conversation that already contains everything it requires. The people are not waiting to be lifted into some larger light; they have already become the light that belongs here, grainy and green-gold and complete. The city is not an unfinished thing hoping for walls of stone or streets of metal. It is the argument, still growing, very slowly, in place.

So the power does the one thing that does not disturb the temperature or the thunder or the careful wounding of stems.

It matches the stillness.

For a time that the growers will later remember only as an unusually clear autumn—when a dropped awl could be seen from the surface as a dark comma in the gold, and the oldest columns returned notes of particular richness—it simply remains. It learns the names of the walls. It listens to the mountain speak through living tissue. It feels the otter-things leave their insulting cairns and the newts walk the fiber floors as if they have always paid rent. It does not bless. It does not name. It does not leave a new plant or a brighter fish or a warmer current. Those would be decorations, and this place has never needed them.

When it withdraws, it withdraws the way the bruise of evening withdraws from the western wall: completely, leaving the basin exactly as it was, except that the isolation around the cirque has grown a fraction more complete. The high passes remember a little less. The melt-rope falls a little more privately. Maps, if any still exist that mark this socket in the mountain, find their ink a little paler, their lines a little less certain. The world that hurries will hurry elsewhere. The finished sentence remains unfinished only in the way all living things remain unfinished.

Once, much later, an elder striking a stem for fractures will hear a note that is neither wood nor bone nor quite the mountain. She will not decorate the fact. She will use it, as they have always used such things, and then she will go still again, which is the only thanks the place has ever required.

The waterfall files its notch. A stem thickens. The city replaces itself in kind.

The power, elsewhere now, keeps the memory the way the lake keeps the light: without distinction, without hurry, and without any need to return.
