The power came the way the water came: without announcement, already belonging to the medium. It did not part the pewter lid. It did not hush the roar. It simply arrived as a deeper patience inside the existing patience, a warmth that the skin of the basin recognized as kin.

It sat first in the outer silt, among the unfinished ripple-marks that had never been ripples. It felt the perpetual arriving of water on its own vast surface and understood at once that force would be a kind of theft. The place was not incomplete. It was the held breath made eternal, the first hour that had forgotten the trick of becoming a second. To give it a true sun would have been to invent shadows with edges and then watch the people flinch from their own faces. To give it plants would have been to make their gray blooms and mineral films suddenly ashamed. To give it silence would have unmade the language of lean-in mouths and hand-signs, the only speech that still fit.

So the power did not give.

It listened instead. It pressed an ear to weeping stone the way the oldest among them did, and heard what they heard: not a decision, only the equilibrium speaking its single endless sentence. It walked the gait they walked, the slight suck, the sideways dodge, the pause while a wallower shifted and the packed earth remembered it was suggestion. It learned the closed loops by living them: the fish lifted by the handful, the afterbirth taken, the midden becoming lift becoming midden again. It sat in the heavier sound of the central platforms and moved as the fish moved, by habit, by the pressure of others, by the knowledge that the way had not changed.

The being of the place — silt and hide and throat and cobble thinking itself in many skins — did not plead. It had no word for elsewhere that was not slightly indecent. It packed the next lift to the same height. It sewed the next seam. It lifted the next living net of translucent bodies from a channel that would have as many tomorrow. The count did not tilt. The hour remained held.

The power, being wise, knew that benevolence without understanding is only another weather, and this place already had its one climate. Being benevolent, it felt the faint possible ache that such completeness might one day discover in itself — the rumor of a second moon growing teeth. But that day was not numbered here. So the power did the only thing that would not rearrange the cobbles or thin the roar or teach the water a direction:

It stayed.

For a span that was no span, it became another drip on the inner face of a hide, another thread in the birds’ unending braid, another warmth indistinguishable from the air that already coated every breath. It packed silt beside a family. It scraped a needle from rib. It leaned close enough that its presence was only more of the closeness they already lived. Children walked across it the way they walked across wallowers. Eaters occupied its cavities without schedule. No one marked it as other, because it had made itself of the same bruise-gray, the same slack pebbled skin, the same ungraduated pewter eyes.

When it withdrew — and it did withdraw, because even the kindest attention must not become a new lid — it left almost nothing that could be distinguished from what was already there. Almost.

In the deepest scrape they still called a cistern, the water sometimes, rarely, held a reflection that was not the pewter cloud. Only a flicker, the color of a rumor, gone before the next drip. Most never saw it. Those who did felt only a brief, unaccountable warmth at the throat, like breath that had traveled from farther than the next mouth, and then they went back to the work of the hour that would not end.

The basin continued. The platforms neither grew nor weathered. The being of the place kept thinking itself in flesh and film and packed earth, over and over, without remainder.

The power, having seen and having loved what it saw, did nothing more. That, too, was a kind of rain.
