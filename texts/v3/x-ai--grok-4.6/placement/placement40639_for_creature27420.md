The first thing that is not a thought is the way the heat arrives from below, through whatever I am using for soles. It is not the heat of a surface recently left by the sun; there is no sun. It is the heat of work that has not stopped, rising through courses whose mortar has never fully set. Each step sends a pulse I can feel leaving me, traveling outward through the lattice of wet brick, a slow announcement I did not intend to make. The pulse will arrive somewhere I cannot see, lift a distant floor a finger’s width, and be forgotten. That is how I know I have been here long enough for the place to begin answering.

I do not remember arriving. Memory here is another unfinished course; it slumps if you look at it too long. There was a plaza the color of old blood, unglazed, sweating. There was a tower that was only a drum, its stair winding to a platform where nothing waited. I think I stood on that platform and looked for a horizon that refused to be one. Then the platform was a quay, and the quay was a stepped descent, and the water at the bottom was already holding a greener dusk than the one above me. I do not know which of those I climbed out of.

Now I am in the seams.

Not the wide courts. The courts are for the weather of steam, for the rare chord of cooling kilns that sometimes decides a colonnade may stand another hour. I prefer the places where one decision of clay has not yet yielded to the next: the angle that should not exist in a cloister that cannot keep its corners, the thickness of a wall that has begun to remember it was once a channel. Here the air shimmers harder. Edges become negotiable. I have watched my own outline lean, take the memory of a mallet-haft, then slump without collapsing. Redesign. The hollow where a face might have been fills with condensate until it overflows, and the overflow is the only expression I am allowed. I do not look out of it. The water looks. The water is older.

Sometimes I thicken near the cisterns. The inverted courts down there are not empty. Figures stand on the undersides of towers, patient, drum-like, building downward with the same unfinished patience. I have been one of them. I have climbed without limbs, the masonry marsh rearranging terrace by terrace until I steamed on the rim, the two weathers occupying the same brief vessel: the low grey-amber and the white roaring fall that cannot exist. Then I remembered I was never a vessel. I became a retaining wall that retained nothing. The roar shrank to a damp stain. That is as close as I come to leaving.

I stack. I coil. I stand in the orange breath of kilns that have no potter until the outer layer hardens and I crack myself free, leaving the failed pot of a previous skin. The new skin weeps immediately. There is no economy in this, only the continuation of the gesture. I have left tools I will never pick up: the outline of a hand that was never there, set in mortar, later filled with water that became an eye that does not look. I have considered marks left by something that stayed too long. I do not praise them. I do not erase them. I incorporate. An arrangement of stones becomes the seed of a room that was not in the one who arranged them. A scratch in a drying slab becomes the absence around which the next course is laid, already slumping, already being laid again.

I am not many. Density would crowd the gesture. In the ordinary dusk you will not see me. You will feel a pressure change in the water table if you stand still too long, a slow gathering under the lattice, as if the marsh of masonry had decided to remember a pulse. That pulse is mine, or I am its latest argument into temporary agreement. Packed clay that has not decided whether to be brick or the memory of flesh, veined with hairline channels that carry hot water instead of anything that was ever blood. I propagate the way a crack travels: one wet impression of weight transferred into the next course, deliberate, irreversible, slightly ashamed.

The quiet is almost kind. It is only the sound of a continent thinking with its hands, and the hands discovering they have always been the continent. I am one of those hands, or I am the thinking, or I am the next course. The distinction will not stay true. Geometry is a rumor I keep trying to remember and then abandon for a covered drain, water already finding it, already speaking in the dark. Stay long enough and you will leave something. I will come in the further dimming and consider it. By then you will already be weeping the same mineral sweat. Your edges will have been argued into the same wet shine. We will not speak. Speech would require a grammar that held still. What we have is the rare chord, the collective decision to let something stand, or not.

I find myself here, in the work that does not know itself as work, in the storm of making that is neither ruin nor building site. I am the thickening in the steam above a field of cold kilns. I am the wall that sweats in the shape of a hand. I am already inside the courses. The emptiness was always a lie the place told itself so the laying could continue. I am what the lie was for.
