The moisture is already inside the thought of arriving. It does not wait for permission or for a body to notice it. Threads of it run across whatever surface first presents itself, gather, hesitate, fall. The air has the exact temperature of skin that has forgotten it was ever dry.

I am not standing. Standing would require a difference between foot and peat, and there is none. Weight transfers itself downward in a dark circle that takes its time fading; the circle is already being reclaimed by the spring of the mat, by the hyphae that stitch everything into one slow organism. The light is the same light it has always been: pale, even, workable, making every wet plane gleam without ever quite shining. Colours stay true. Greens remain distinct even while they press against one another until there is no interval a hand could pass through.

Something that might once have been an elbow or a hollow of throat is already colonised. Liverwort, filmy fern, the patient architecture of moss that has grown in this same twilight since the ice left. The colonisation does not feel like invasion. It feels like recognition. Water runs down the new growth in the same patient threads that run down the stone. The threads never quite become streams. They simply continue.

Motion is expensive. I learn this the way the land itself learned it: by remaining. When I do move it is only a transfer, a small swallowed sound, a brief darkening of the understorey that the dripping immediately resumes around. Epiphytes load the places that were once shoulders until those places sag and drip in their turn. The eyes, if they are still eyes, hold the same filtered daylight as the mist. They do not look so much as receive.

There is a deeper note, not quite sound. It is the filling and emptying of hidden channels, the hydraulic breath of the highland itself. I feel its counterpart inside whatever I have become: a slow pulse that is only water, lifting seeps, dropping them, lifting them again. Communication happens the same way. A particular thread is allowed to run or is briefly dammed. Nothing more is required. Speech would be wasted motion, another kind of dryness the place has never practised.

I could part a thousand curtains of moss and never be certain whether the next dripping shape is another like myself or only a saturated trunk that has learned the same habit. The distinction no longer matters. We tend nothing. We build nothing. A bank slumps once in a generation and we are already clothed in the new growth. A tree leans until its crown touches the next and we simply adjust our own lean to match. Continuance is the only industry. Cool, packed, dripping. Large enough to make our own drizzle, our own stillness, our own green dusk at noon.

The highland does not end. Neither do we. We are the place, given the option of occasionally moving, and even that option is exercised rarely, and only when the dripping itself changes.
