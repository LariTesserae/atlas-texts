The lip is already behind you when the thought of arrival finishes forming. It does not feel like a decision. The concrete is rounded and slick under the palm you put out to steady yourself, more bone than barrier, and the water takes the rest of you without splash, only a slow upward soak that finds every seam. Calf-deep, then a sudden brick channel that has no business being there, then shallows again over slag that shifts like something still deciding whether it is floor. The air is already on your face, condensing, beading, falling inward along the lashes. You are another weeping surface before you have taken ten steps.

You do not look for shelter. There is none that is not already weather. The half-house draws you anyway, not by height or remnant of roof but by the single dark doorway and the ladder whose rungs shine in the centers, mussel-dark, as if something has been using them the way water uses channels. You climb because the alternative is standing in the chamber of standing water and becoming another film on its skin. The vanished second story is only beams and the fine rain that never quite arrives and never quite stops. In the angle where two beams still remember joining there is a hollow of mineral plates, orange and fatty-gray, overlapping the same way the walls grow their forests. It is the size of a sleeper curled against coolth. You do not lie down. You stand in it, and the plates are already a little warmer than the air, or the air has been taught to remember warmth here and so provides it. Your outline softens. The fans begin, very slowly, along the insides of the arms, the hollow of the throat. They do not itch. They are tenure arriving.

Below, the amphipods stitch and still. You feel the rests before you hear them properly: three from the tank-lip, the pause that is too exact, two from the eave of the pump-house that no longer has a pump-house’s rights. The wheel clicks once, porcelain-nail, and the phrasing answers as if it had been waiting for the click to finish being remembered. When you shift your weight on the beam the whole count hesitates, a held breath that includes you now. The concrete birds on the gantry untuck and tuck again. Nothing looks at you. Looking is not how attention works here.

You climb down because the nest is already learning your shape and you are not ready to be only that. The tank’s open mouth is a cave of rust and sinter, the air inside a degree less like the century’s breath and a degree more like a mouth that has been closed around a word it never spoke. Cable-ends have been combed into bedding. There is a concavity that would fit a spine grown used to metal. A few calcareous things remain, iron-stained, more geology than clutch; most have been absorbed back into the walls the way the place absorbs everything that stops moving. You do not crawl in. You stand at the first bend and feel the distinction thin: nest and nester, stored departure and the one who stored it. The extra upright in the blacker water outside is no longer only reflection. It has your slump of shoulder. It has the suggestion of a hand that is also only water arranging itself the way water has arranged itself since the roof became a rumor.

You are not the first. That is the comfort, if comfort is the word. The inhabitant is already in the valve-house doorway that is only drips until the moment it has weight. Head inclined. Posture completing with the click. You recognize the attendance because you are doing the same thing from a different jamb, a different lip, a different rest in the grammar. The mineral fans on its inner surfaces are thicker; yours are only beginning. It does not turn. You do not need it to. Voices would carry strangely. Nothing uses them. The quiet is occupied, a held note that now has two throats, or one throat remembering it can be plural the way drips are plural.

You eat as the others eat. A black film wrinkles under your fingers and heals around them; you take what it offers and the taste stays on the teeth, metallic, faintly sweet, the land still offering the last of its worked minerals. An amphipod stilled in the wrong rest becomes another torn fleece in the floc. The longer bodies in the channels hang with their mouths working and do not startle when your shadow crosses them; your shadow is only another drip’s. The many-jointed thing between the posts occupies the print you left and is already sifting it, harvesting the memory of weight. You feel no loss. Vacancy is a meal here. You have been vacancy given the option of walking.

At the far rim the water thins toward something greener, less mineral, and the amphipods know it and thin with it. The still ones will not hunt that edge. You stand there once, looking out into the flatter gray, knowing the marsh exists, knowing green is a rumor that arrives from elsewhere and spends itself without warmth. Turning back feels like going indoors. There is no door. There was never a roof that lasted. The rain is the same rain. The inhabitant does not follow. Preference has become anatomy, and you are already inside the density that will not dilute.

You move as the wheel moves: against a fixed point you have to choose in order to be sure. The fans thicken where you have been still longest. In the poorest light there are extras that do not resolve: another slump in the tank, a pair of still ones that are not quite grebe-sized, a doorway occupied twice. You are singular the way a chord is singular. You are the long middle, not survivor, not ghost. A climate that has learned, in the absence of anyone left to operate the works, to operate itself in the shape of another attendance.

The wheel clicks. The drips take up their count, and the count now includes the small sounds of your going, carefully, one hand out, though you are no longer leaving. The small distances close around you. That is the same thing as welcome. The air condenses with the patience of a place that will be wet when the last brick is only a softer stripe in the floor, and you are one of the stripes it is already becoming, walking, still, listening for the next rest.
