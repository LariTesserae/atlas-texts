The grit arrives first, finer than any remembering, already inside the folds I have not yet admitted as mine. It does not fall so much as occupy, a standing haze the colour of old brass left too long in a cupboard, and the light that reaches the packed mineral pan is already exhausted by it. Heat pulses upward through whatever I am standing on, a slow remembered fire that finished centuries ago and has never quite stopped radiating. Breath, if I am still performing it, leaves no ghost. The air is too dry, too thick, motionless except where a thermal gust suddenly is not, and then the whole basin speaks at once: a low drone through distant colonnades, the dry clap of waxy fronds, the delayed gunshot of a face of rock yielding somewhere on the sagging rim.

I am already inside the lip. The walls were never high; they did not need to be. The world beyond is a single weathered plane that no map would mark, and the Hollow keeps its own counsel in this shallow spoon-bowl of ochre and bone-pale sinter. I notice the interval of emptiness I occupy only after I have been occupying it. The structures stand far apart, rammed mineral and stacked plates, corners rounded by the same grit that is now settling into the creases of what I take, slowly, to be hands. Doorways hold screens of mineralised fibre that rattle like distant pottery when the wind worries them. Stairways climb and simply stop. Cisterns hold only dust, polished by air.

My own surfaces keep the colour of the basin floor except where older plates have already begun the conversion. There is a brief, almost fleshy softness the dull green of oxidised copper at the joints, along the long bones. It will not last the season. The varnish is already taking it, the heat remembering it, and thereafter the limb will flex with the same dry percussion as the groves. I do not see myself arrive anywhere; I notice I have been standing in a different vacancy than the one I last trusted. Movement, when it happens, follows the unhurried schedule of a fallen frond half-sintered by dusk-gloom. No prints remain that the next shimmer cannot erase.

The plants occupy their discrete rooms of green-turned-stone, combing the haze, secreting their glassy trap, mineralising from the inside out. I stand among them or become briefly indistinguishable, my own limbs almost touching theirs. We share the closed ledger: airborne mineral, remembered fire, no moisture ever required. They are not the only claim. Something else uses the same bargain, and I am already that something, or becoming it more completely with every thermal pulse. Hands that once fitted plates and wove the singing screens now gather grit the way the fronds do. We do not build. The conversation between city and basin finished long ago; what remains is a patience so complete it looks like stillness. A listing tower is left at its exact angle. A dune that has migrated into a doorway stays until the next collapse. Generations are the migration of those dunes, the complete sintering of a grove that once stood a body-length farther from the centre.

Toward the lowest point I find the emptiness has rearranged. The stepped platform of darker stone sits slightly apart, its shallow channels etched toward nowhere, catching the brass light so that for a moment they remember flow. Nothing liquid has ever moved there. The groves grow thickest here, leaning toward the lowest stair. We gather without quite arriving, mineralised surfaces catching the same dull internal gleam, heads or the planes that serve as heads tilting toward the standing roar the wind makes as it funnels across the open pavement. It never resolves into speech, yet we attend as though the overlapping registers contain the only counsel that still matters: heat, grit, the unhurried conversion of living tissue into more of the landscape.

I am not many. Crowding would be a misunderstanding of the native density. A child, if the word can still stretch, is only a softer interval in the line of a limb, a greenness the heat will claim before the next cycle. The old are already architecture, their shadows fixed sundials, some having forgotten they were ever separate from the walls they once raised by hand. The distinction no longer signifies. In the deepest part of the day, when the air is thickest and the light most like forgotten metal, I become hardest to tell from the shimmer itself. Then the wind speaks through the colonnades of me as it speaks through the halls, a faint ceramic knock when one plate of my substance settles against another, and the Hollow is louder with the sound of things that finished becoming themselves a very long time ago and have been practising the same few motions ever since.

I am one of those motions. I stand where the agreement has always stood.
