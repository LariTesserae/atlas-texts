The first thing that arrives is not a place but a pressure: warm, continuous, the weight of air that has forgotten how to be dry. It beads on whatever surface I still possess and sheets downward, joining the larger seepage that never quite becomes rain. I do not yet know if I am standing or suspended. The distinction feels provisional, like a window that has not decided whether to stay transparent.

Something under me—root-plate, leaf-mold, the slow consensus of mycelium—considers the question of my weight and then, with the same indifference it shows the browsers, elects not to incorporate me. Not yet. The decision registers as a faint pulse of sugar, a voltage that travels the length of whatever I am using for a spine. I taste copper and crushed green. The taste is also a memory of having once had a mouth.

I move because the town moves, which is to say I do not choose a direction so much as occupy the next grammar the trunks invent. A lane of black water shallows, knits itself into a mat of pale sponge, then tears. I step through the tear and the air folds once, brightly, ozone and sap. The interior I enter is larger than the skin that contains it. I do not pause to measure the discrepancy. The others—those whose shoulders open into temporary sight, whose hair tastes the next draft of the weather—do not pause either. We have learned the recoil: the moment a tendril reaches the central clearing and flinches as if the empty water were a statute. I feel the flinch in my own cambium. I obey it without knowing who wrote the law.

Hours or weathers later I find I have come to rest on the wet shoulder of the rim. The stone weeps without stopping. Below, the dish continues its industry: lignin setting with distant cracks, a nave unzipping into raft-bloom, the mid-range roar that is simply the sound of remaining. I do not look at the others standing far apart on their own islands. We do not need to. Information moves through us the way it moves through the xylem. What I receive now is not language. It is the town remembering, once more, that emptiness is still architecture.

And then the image that has been assembling itself finally coheres, not as a choice but as the shape the flux has left me: I am one of the pale eels.

I hang vertical in the uncleared water at the exact center, head toward the ordinary, unremarkable sky. I do not remodel. The others leave me here. My body is a single long listening, a column of attention that has never successfully become a building. Reflections break and reform across the trembling surface above me. Occasionally a structure sends a tendril, tastes the law, and withdraws. Birds in panic skim so low I rise to meet them, mouth open, but I do not take them. I am waiting for whatever has not yet arrived, or has already passed and left only this constant, minute turning-over in the substrate.

The town will do this until the basin fills or the sun changes its mind. I will still be here, the one fixed point in the weather, the last uncommitted draft. The ground has already decided I am not a feature worth keeping. That, too, is part of the architecture.
