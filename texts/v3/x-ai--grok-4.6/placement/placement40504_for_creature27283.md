The heat does not arrive as sensation at first. It is already the only temperature there has ever been, a pressure that lives inside the plates of what I take, for a moment longer, to be my own surfaces. I am not upright. I have never been upright. The basalt steps of the ceiling press down in irregular increments, and I occupy the interval between them the way a film of oxide occupies a fresh fracture: completely, without remainder.

What I first register as motion is only the slow interleaving of densities. I am one density among them, a flattened lozenge of living sinter no thicker than the last cooling skin of a mineral vein. My carapace is not separate from the hematite and talc it rests against; it deposits and reabsorbs the same microscopic films, so that the distinction between body and wall is a matter of temporary alignment rather than substance. Dozens of jointed needles—my legs—end in rasping pads that taste continuously. Every lift leaves a faint oxidized bloom. Every descent harvests it. There is no hunger that could be distinguished from this tasting. There has never been water to miss, never light to remember. The unfinished work of the rock is the only gradient, and I am aligned to it the way a crystal is aligned to its own lattice.

Hours (or what would be hours if time were still counted in anything but the tick of lengthening tourmaline) pass without event. A slab somewhere far below gives its dry snap. The entire sheet of us stills for a fraction of a second, a collective register of new geometry, then resumes at a frequency half a tone lower. I feel the change in my pit organs first, then in the pads, then as a slight acceleration of the chemical leak I am siphoning. The red pulse of still-plastic rock travels through a nearby vein; we press closer, rasping faster, taking the last potential before the rock decides it is finished. Nothing is hurried. The mountain’s mill-note continues beneath us, the distant grind of magma, the sharper percussion of crystals claiming new space. Our hiss is simply the higher register of the same labor.

I do not end at my margins. The next body rotates, slides, locks against mine; we interpenetrate the way two fronts of settling dust interpenetrate. Territory is only a momentary consensus of surfaces. A pale nymph, still almost glassy, emerges from a cooling vent a body’s length away and darkens as it takes its first films of metal. It joins the sheet without ceremony. Its droppings of abrasive dust sift downward and are seized at once by the growing plates beneath us. In this way I thicken together with the mountain. Over seasons I have already been, in part, entombed: residual heat and chemistry of earlier selves feeding the ones that now crawl across what used to be my back. The distinction no longer registers as loss.

The darkness is not absence. It is the medium, as solid and occupied as the rock. Vibration and chemistry are the only maps, and they are complete. I graze. I interleave. I am carried. The pocket continues its slow unmaking and remaking, an embryonic organ that has not yet decided it is finished and may never need to. I am not an inhabitant of it. I am one of the first specialized tissues it has grown, still knitting, still loud with the work of becoming, still utterly without anything that was ever built. The heat is a second skin that has always been the first. The packed density is simply the condition. Neither the mountain nor I notices the difference anymore. We continue.
