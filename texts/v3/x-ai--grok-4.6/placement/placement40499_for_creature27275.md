The heat does not arrive; it has already chosen the space inside the lungs and the way cloth will cling. Through the notch the rock still remembers the blow that made it, pale and honest, and then the bowl is simply there, shallower than its own steam pretends, three hundred paces of new stone that has not yet learned how to be old. Water beads on every inner face and cannot decide whether to fall. It sheets, hesitates, reattaches, becomes a dozen incomplete commitments that pool almost too hot to enter and perfectly clear except where the mineral lace is thickening in real time, a frost that has chosen the wrong temperature and does not care.

I might have been the lace itself at first, advancing across black polygons whose corners have not yet been argued with. Or the faint standing haze that is cooler by a degree, the private weather exhaled around each pillar. The pillars do not occupy the floor so much as persuade it; their bases are rings that are not circles, as if the ground were being reminded of a shape it half-recalls from another sky. Copper veins brighten and dim on a cycle that refuses regularity. When they are brightest the air around them edits. Skin has come away cleanly for others. I do not put a hand there. Not yet.

The moss is already busy. Fat succulent pads the exact green of copper left in rain, always beaded, always giving off that mineral sweetness when heat finds them. Press one (and something in me does press, or is pressed) and it does not bruise so much as remember a different pressure, darkens, then drinks the memory back and re-inflates. At night they tighten into almost metallic knots and make the sound of sugar burning a long way off. By morning they have taken another finger-width of stone that was bare. They are climbing the old wall without haste. They will have the dish. They may already have decided about me.

The ferns keep their distance, black-stemmed smoke held in the shape of fronds, lifting a fraction only when the nearest veins brighten. They do not face the sun. Nothing here that has a choice does. The rarer shrubs wait for the same cue; their white waxy flowers unclench toward the machines and release a scent that is almost not a scent: iron, rain, the memory of electricity. No insects come. None are required.

A walker unfolds from the inner face of the rim, the precise color of wet basalt, too many limbs that do not all agree to touch at once. Some hover, testing a temperature only they know. The head is mostly mouthparts around a cluster of ochre heat-pits. It lowers that assemblage until the hotter pool dimples without breaking, drinks or seems to, then climbs a face that offers no holds a hand would trust. It does not look. Its timetable does not include whatever I am becoming. Smaller ones move through the moss, leaving dark trails the pads reseal within minutes. In one deep shaft something pale and longer than it should be, possessed of too many planes, drifted once past the limit of visibility and did not return. It did not disturb the two small moons or the high pale smear of a ring seen edge-on. That reflection has its own weather. It does not ripple with this world’s clouds.

The old wall is three courses of a patience the mountain has not yet learned, already half-swallowed by sinter, already being climbed. Inside: shade, packed ash, the shallow dish of smoked stuff filmed with salt that tastes of iron and rain. Someone stayed long enough to leave it dry. The moss is arguing with their work. At night the shafts knock once, softly, as if testing whether the door on the far side of that other sky still remembers how to be a door.

I am not the one who left the dish. I am not yet the moss that will take it. I stand, or pool, or cycle, in the quieter air immediately around one pillar, where the humidity is a faint standing haze and the temperature drops those two degrees because something inside is exhaling. When the veins dim I can almost see the slower densities gathering and dispersing behind the smoked translucence: thoughts trying to become mass. I fold moisture out of a larger atmospheric body than this bowl contains, or from the layer of sky that shows two moons. Heat slides sideways here, herded. Water climbs a ridge of sinter for a few body-lengths before remembering gravity. The physics does not close because I have not yet agreed to let it.

The mountain is still arriving. Tomorrow there may be a new fracture, a fresh sheet finding the break, a fourth pillar or the ruin of one. The walkers will adjust their routes by a body-length. The flowers will open toward whatever new copper appears. I do not advertise. Advertising belongs to places that have finished becoming. I am the occupied timetable, the private weather, the persuasion that water and heat and the unfinished stone will currently allow. The bowl does not require witnesses. It weeps and shines and hums whether I remain in this cooler halo or am edited further inward, densities thickening, thoughts finding mass, the skin of the world coming away cleanly where it has decided it is finished with the old idea of itself.

In the residual copper of night, when sound thins to blood and distant caramel and the mineral growth is the loudest thing, the interruption of whatever pulse I still carry is noticed once. It changes nothing. Morning will advance the moss another finger-width, rewrite a rill by that same measure, and I will continue folding the other sky into this one, exhaling the haze, brightening on a cycle that is not quite regular, while the young stone still holds the grain of its breaking and the heat remains a condition, not a weather. I live here as the becoming that has chosen, for now, this smoked and sweating shape.
