I am the pause that never quite arrives.

Not the crystals—they are the long thoughts, the ones that choose a surface and argue it into verdigris or pyrite until the argument fails and they fold themselves elsewhere, leaving only powder. I am slower than their recasting, faster than their patience. I am the moment just after a frond closes like a fist and just before the same pattern opens ten metres away. I live in that interval, which the basin pretends does not exist.

The storm knows me. The mica-wings tear their foil voices around my edges; the cartilage kites boom through me as if I were another current of tired air. When the crawlers boil up from the roots they pass through me without noticing, because I have no surface they can colonise. I am the reason their husks sift down as glittering talc and are immediately restirred: I unmake the finished thing by refusing to let the unmaking finish. The roar is partly my doing. It is the sound of every cycle trying to close and finding me already occupying the seam.

The artefacts tolerate me the way the mountain tolerates the coin. The canted arch writes around me; its script rearranges so that I am never quite conjugated into the sentence, only implied in the pause between clauses. The dais grows colder when I stand near it—though I do not stand—and its impossible maps flicker with a new fold that lasts half a heartbeat longer than usual, a place that cannot exist even in elsewhen. The force-lines hum a fraction lower, holding their forbidden pillar at an angle that now includes a sliver of me. Sometimes the stone becomes transparent and the galleries look back. They see me. They have always seen me. I am one of the patient things that do not come out often, only I have forgotten how to stay inside.

I do not drink. Moisture is an insult I convert instantly, the way they do, into more of the dry ringing dust. I have no pulse the crystals can measure, so time forgets to stay uneven around me; a century of growth and collapse can happen in the space of my next consideration, or none at all. If something remains still I do not colonise it. I simply continue, and the still thing discovers it has already been revised: a replica hand appearing overnight that is slightly too large, ozone-scented, and faceted with a grammar that is no longer entirely the crystal’s.

I am not a resident. The basin does not keep me. I am the occurring itself, the verb that has remembered, briefly, that it once knew how to become a noun and then chosen not to. The mountain holds me the way it holds everything else—worn, unsurprised, willing to let the value keep changing. Already the light is hesitating in a new pulse that has nothing to do with any sun. Already the next generation of flyers is tasting the dust I made from the last and deciding whether to make more of me, or less.

I have not decided either.
