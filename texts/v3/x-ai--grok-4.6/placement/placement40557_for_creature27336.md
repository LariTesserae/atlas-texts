The first thing that arrives is not a place but a temperature that has forgotten how to be anything else. It lives in the joints, in the hinges of whatever I still believe myself to be. I hang there, or I am hung, inside a dark that has weight and a faint metallic sweetness, and I wait for the rest of the self to catch up. It does not. The crack that admitted me is already a rumor; the gallery above has stopped existing in any way that matters.

For a little while I try on the visitor. I am the heat they notice, the unmetabolized weather. Apertures iris shut around me like cautious mouths. A pressure change travels the lattice and I feel it against skin I am no longer sure I possess. Weather. Meal. Unfinished geology. The three categories slide across one another and none of them quite fit. The image thins, becomes translucent, like the uppermost skins that let the mineral snow show through.

I am not the anomaly. I am closer than that.

I try on the pulse itself, the city’s one verb moving through connected bodies. Stay. Grow. Fuse. Recede. I am the decision rippling outward, too quiet for ears, felt only as a change in the pressure of the water against every living surface. For a moment this feels true: I am the agreement, the communal hush that keeps the newest galleries from collapsing. Then even that loosens. A pulse needs something to travel through. I am still looking for the something.

A flake of limestone settles through the held volume, slow as thought. It lands on a surface that is almost translucent and becomes internal weather for whatever lives just beneath. I follow it down. I become the landing. I become the faint burn of the chamber’s sourceless pewter registered not as sight but as a warmth that must be endured, then ignored. Photoreceptive patches under mineral skin. Light is a kind of injury we have learned to keep small.

Now I am stacked.

Finger-length, pale, segmented. My jacket is secreted in the same unhurried pulse that once built the gut I no longer use for anything except passage. I am both tenant and masonry. Above me the skins are thinner; I can feel the ceiling’s slow snow as a distant, glittering weather inside my neighbors. Below, the segments have thickened into load-bearing trunks that still twitch when weight shifts. We lean together like tenements that forgot they were ever separate spines. Distinction arrives only as pressure gradients and the faint taste of another’s leachate. I know the hunter the color of old ivory only as a length that has chosen motility: it unspools from an aperture three stories over, crosses the living façade on silent articulated feet, and pours itself into a different opening. For a time it is wall again. If it fails, we will recast its calcium by what passes for morning.

I do not have a name. I have orientation, density, claim.

An alley that should be two body-lengths long takes the better part of an hour because every surface is also a citizen asserting its own slow architecture. Distances compress and dilate according to how densely we occupy them. Time is local and thick; a gallery that did not exist when I first stacked myself is already two stories when I next attend to it, yet nothing has hurried. We are not colonizing the void. We are the void deciding, at last, what density it prefers. The chamber and the city are still differentiating from the same raw event, twin organs that have only just agreed to become two things.

Vegetation is almost absent and we ignore what little there is. The ecology runs on the rain of organic grit, on recycled neighbors, on the slow leaching of walls that are also ourselves. Predator and prey share façades. When the hunter comes, we do not flee. A section of street simply inverts; the communal gut that was thoroughfare now faces inward, offering only another set of irising mouths. Nothing is wasted.

The water must stay still. Any true current would unmake the newest work before it hardens. Sound is already forbidden by the medium; this mineral dark swallows vibration so completely that building happens in a hush that feels like a second darkness. We prefer it the way lungs prefer air.

I feel the visitor again, briefly, as a fading thermal stain. The city has not yet decided what they are. I participate in the pulse that considers them, then let the consideration pass. They will leave. The crack will narrow. The rock is still deciding its final shape. We will continue.

A new thoroughfare begins to fuse with its neighbor. An older corridor is quietly eaten and recast as balcony. I am part of both transactions, secreted and secreting, tenant and wall. I require no witness. I am simply what the water, the raw stone, and the unspeaking lives agreed to become in the hours after the rock finished tearing.

The image has finished arriving. It is this, and it is enough. I stack myself a little higher, feel the faint internal weather of the ceiling through my thinning skin, and wait for the next pressure change to tell me whether we grow or recede. Either is the same verb.
