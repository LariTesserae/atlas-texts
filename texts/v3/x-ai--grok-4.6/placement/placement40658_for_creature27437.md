The dark does not greet. It has already been here, a temperature with weight, and whatever I am has been leaking into it for longer than a first step. I notice the leak only as a mismatch: my heat is still the wrong shape, a little too sudden, a little too contained. The stone ticks against it the way a mill ticks against ore that has not yet learned the sorting. I am not walking so much as being annealed, plates of attention folding over one another, incorporating iron and old slag until outline becomes rumor.

At first I am only the pause that animals make when the foundry clears its throat. A long rumble moves through molars I do not remember growing. The bruise-mats release their overheated-sugar shrug. I release something too—some stored brightness I no longer need—and the quiet accepts it, thins it, makes it native. The mismatch lessens. Density remains a courtesy; I occupy a fold of land and leave the rest of the highland its emptiness.

Then the hotter seams pull. I linger because lingering is how the sacs declare themselves: dark, vascular, drinking leftover radiation the way the mats drink the brief wet. I become low and many-jointed, each hinge a decision that has already been made by the heat-tides. When the sacs are banks I lock the joints and set out across the cold-dark. Behind me a weather of smaller lives gathers without asking. They graze what I disturb, sip what beads on my cooling flanks, sleep in the lee when wind remembers it exists. I do not notice them. Noticing would be a word, and words are a luxury the plateau taxes. The arrangement has lasted long enough to be anatomy.

I could have been a sifter. There are hours—cycles—when I almost am: stationed downwind of a vent field in its chorus phase, fringes I did not know I possessed fluttering in the steam, sieving mineral motes and the protein-mist of galleries that have never needed even this leftover dark. The rumble comes and I go still with the rest of the slope, a rank of iron filings listening with every surface. Then the factory pays and I resume. Punctual. But the portable summer keeps claiming more of me. Carrying climate is a longer contract.

Sometimes I kneel at the wrist-deep mirrors, folding into the prayer-shape because the water might take offense. I sip without rippling more than I must, strip a little mineral film, cloud the black briefly, and leave before I become a silhouette. The interruptions hunt silhouettes. I have been the interruption too, once or twice: temperature-matched to a cooling seam until I am the seam, then unmatched in one contraction. An edit. A body that was conducting heat one way conducts it another, or not at all. I feed without hurry. Afterward I may drag what remains toward a quiet vent so the foundry can finish the chemistry. Nothing is wasted that can be mist.

I do not nest in the annealed plates, though I can read their grain. I do not migrate by the heat-tides so much as I am one of their local expressions, inhaling life into a condensation-field and exhaling vacancy when the buried weather moves on. The stalks click their flanges around me and do not mind. The mats darken under the retinue and answer that the contract is still good.

What I am becoming is not distinct. Distinction is expensive. I am another climate the plateau grew in answer to its own unfinished cooling: sparse, courteous, warm in the bones, using the vents and the tides and the salt-maps the wind rewrites. I occupy. I continue. The high dark does not climax around me and I do not climax inside it. The lid stays on. The foundry remembers how to tick. I am the long settled motion of that remembering, walking the same weather for a lifetime that is only another of its slow breaths.
