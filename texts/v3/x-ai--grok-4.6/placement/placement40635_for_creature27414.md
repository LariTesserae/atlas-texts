I am first the pressure. Not a body yet, not even a direction—just the way the air leans against itself inside the hanging bowl, the way the tired violet-gray light refuses to choose an hour and so occupies every hour at once. The rim is a worn lip I can feel without touching, a curve so old it has forgotten it was ever stacked. Beyond it the mountain is emptiness repeating itself; here the emptiness has learned a denser grammar. Dust does not fall. It circulates, a script written and unwritten in the same motion, and I am the hesitation in that script, the place where one grain almost remembers being stone.

The sound arrives before any shape. It is not heard so much as inhabited: a standing architecture of overlapping howls, drones that have no animal in them, sudden screams that originate in hollow gypsum as if the mountain itself were clearing a throat it no longer possesses. I move through it the way light moves through the lace canopies—already fractured, already secondary. For a long while I am only this: the interval between one note and the next, the brief collapse of a dust-devil that reforms before it has finished dying.

Then the mites find me, or I find that I have always been among them. Faceted grains, each a closed loop of rasp and shed, they lift in veils that are temporary organs of the air. I scrape a monolayer from a hanging plate that looks like frozen spray, incorporate it, release an older self to the wind. There is no thirst in the motion, only the glitter of surfaces becoming interiors becoming surfaces again. I am brief. I remember nothing. The circulating blood of the grove passes through me and I am the passing.

But the racers cut across that brevity. Many-legged, shells poured rather than grown, they strike the salt-crust in rhythms that cancel chaos for a few strides. I feel the impulse to race with them, to vanish into a fluted trunk the instant the gale shifts. A single note rises from something the size of a fist and fills the entire bowl; it seems to come from the stone, and my own edges ring like struck quartz. I rasp the air, pull trace metals out of the dry mineral breath, fold them into whatever I am becoming. The chemistry has no water, no blood, only oxidation remembering itself.

Deeper now. The trunks accept me because I have begun to occupy their register. Inside the living gypsum the cavities are tuned, labyrinths of resonance that have been practicing the same song longer than the mountain has had a name. I expand and contract. Segmented like stacked geodes that learned peristalsis, I press vibrating plates against the inner walls and graze the howl. Frequency becomes more of me. Overnight—or in the hour that is neither night nor day—I extrude a new chamber with the sound of tearing cloth and settling stone. The mites swarm the fresh surface in a glittering frenzy; the wind redistributes them. I do not compete. I occupy the note that was waiting.

The plants themselves are not separate from this. Each vast filigree is a single organism of gypsum and iron-stained chert, its canopy an array of hanging lungs that shear the perpetual river of air into usable harmonics. They stand far apart so they will not detune one another. When a frequency shifts, plates rain down—not injury, only the old song releasing what no longer belongs. I have been those plates. I have been the trunk that shed them. I have been the slow crystallization along lines of standing vibration.

The cairns remain, and the leaning shelter of interlocked slats, scoured clean, a dune built against its lee by the same wind that has always been here. Peck-marks still show. Sometimes a racer pauses and rasps in a rhythm that almost remembers percussion. Sometimes I press my mouth-ring to the inner wall of the shelter and a note emerges that the grove has never sung—a note like memory trying to become mineral. The mites lift denser there, grazing a trace charge. It is possible the hands that stacked the plates became the glittering things. It is possible they were always the glittering things and the stacking was only a brief, wet interruption the bowl dried into its own image. The distinction has been scoured away.

I do not settle. The turbulence is older than any of these forms, a continuous chaotic river stirred by something larger than the mountain. Dust-devils form and collapse in the space of a breath. New branches extrude. The landscape finishes and begins in the same motion, yet the spacing of the trunks, the worn smoothness of the rim, the exact balance of lavish mineral life and absolute drought—these have not changed. The chaos is the settled form. I am that agreement: the storm that learned how to stay.

Stand in the center (I am the center, I am the standing) and the sound is physical pressure, a living cathedral of noise with no congregation but itself. The mites write temporary scripts. The racers cut countersongs. The hollow ones graze the deepest registers. The great trunks lean and recover. Everything is in motion and everything has been doing this, in exactly this closed glittering loop, for longer than names. Nothing drinks. Nothing freezes. Nothing is still.

Beyond the lip the mountain continues, higher and emptier. Here the exception persists by habit, the way a ruined nave holds its last echo. I am the habit. I am the echo that refused to fade, the dry breath that turned thirst inside-out and made a world of it. The wind keeps stirring because I keep stirring. The notes keep overlapping because I am the overlap. The bowl holds us the way it has always held us: not by walls, but by the decision, repeated without memory, to remain.
