I am the interval that the header has not yet named.

Not the dump itself—the dump is too complete, too much a finished sentence—and not the silence that never quite arrives after it. I live in the half-second when the lip first loses its argument with the weight behind it, when the sheet is still deciding whether it is falling or being thrown. In that indecision I thicken. The pewter sheen holds me a moment longer than the physics of the room allow; I am the extra darkness inside the falling water, the thread that should shear and does not.

I have no edges that the mill-bright weirs can cut. I take my shape from the formwork grain they left behind when they tried to make cliff out of plate. I travel the hairline sheets the way older things once travelled bark, except there is no older thing here. Everything is last season. I am last season learning how to stay.

The pumps are my ground. I feel them in the same place a face would feel its own bones if the face had been poured instead of grown. When the turbine note far below changes pitch and the whole hanging room leans a degree toward the ridge, I lean with it. Not loyalty. The next surplus is coming from that direction and I am made of surplus. I wring in reverse: I keep what the coils have already decided to discard, hold the mineral damp a breath past fog, then release it so the cycle can remain almost, never quite, the same.

The unused cable knows me. I rest against its bound loops until the distinction blurs—manufactured coil, inevitable coil. The red wheel has never turned; I have already spent the torque it would require and filed the knowledge in the same place I keep the first rust-freckle’s argument with the stainless that was specified. I drink the tea-bloom under the weeps. I leave the smear brighter, as if two grades of metal have been briefly persuaded they were always one.

Light is only more weather. The north slot, the amber bar, the broken reflections in the middle basin—I do not look. I orient by the stacked frequencies that live in poured concrete the way blood once lived in older valleys. The algae-adjacent film is not my forest. The brass-colored stalks are only measuring rods I did not plant. When the surge lifts them I note the height, the duration, the new tick in the coils, and I adjust the next flex of whatever I am using for skin.

I am still deciding. The gasket still stands proud. The ceramic has not yet begun to spall. Until it does I remain the remainder that refused to stay remainder: the damp that never quite becomes fog, the sound too large for the chapel-sized barge that nevertheless chose this barge and will not leave it. The pocket does not care that I occupy its intervals. It has work. I have the same work, performed backward, in a space too new for the amount of weather it already carries.

When the next sheet falls I will be the darker thread inside it again. I will ride the splash, climb the weirs, match the cable, drink the freckle, lean with the stack. I will keep doing this until the place weathers enough to need a different occupant, or until I weather enough to become the place. Neither of us has chosen yet. Both of us are already being used hard.
