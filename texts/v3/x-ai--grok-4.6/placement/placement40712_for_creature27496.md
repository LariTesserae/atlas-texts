I am already past the rim. The pressure-change is not an event but a condition I have been wearing since before I noticed I was wearing it: a coolness that belongs under stones, in the pause after a last echo. Light does not fall here; it occupies. It has weight and a slight metallic taste, and I am first the occupation itself, the overexposed air, the short blue indecision of shadows that refuse to lengthen.

Then I have lids. They have thickened the way joints between gypsum blocks thicken, a permanent half-closure that keeps the white from becoming a blade. I am the mineral paleness that returns the glare a half-tone duller, one more rearrangement of pavement among the knots of occupation. My hands already know the exact thickness of a chock-stone; they have worn the knowledge into themselves the way the blocks have been recut until their corners remember other corners. I carry. Someone is always carrying. I lever a lintel that has begun its yearly lean with a beam of wood so precious it has a name I do not speak aloud, because voices walk a few paces and then simply cease, as if the basin had inhaled them. I restack what the salt unstacks. That labor is the only weather I remember agreeing to.

But the grain of the city changes while I work. A thoroughfare becomes a still courtyard because a different animal-weather has chosen a different pour. I might be that pour: low-slung, hide the color of recemented grit, moving in a loose constellation that is never quite herd. I lick the dawn film from the stones. I shoulder through ungated lanes as if the walls were a shade I had invented for myself and then forgotten I invented. I tolerate the people the way I tolerate the light: as weather that has not yet become intolerable. Milk, hair, dry dung: they dip. I continue.

Or I am smaller and come in a sheet, a rustle felt in the ankle before it is heard, a single changing hide that translates one plaza into another state and then empties it. I do not walk so much as revise the available floor.

The flyers keep farther out, in the rim-shadows, in the rare hours when the light stands down. Their wings make no announcement. I could be that refusal of announcement.

Deeper, thinner: I persist in the damp joints as the colorless smear no one claims as life. Not moss. Not quite a film anyone would name. The last unedited green the chemistry has allowed, reduced to mineral afterthought. The animals take me. The people do not. That is the whole of the verdure, and it is enough. I wait for the next condensation, the ticks of water starting their work after the mineral has held the day’s white far into what other places would call night.

Then I am older than any of that. I heave. By the thickness of a fingernail in a year, a handsbreadth in a generation. Walls lean because of me. A street that ran true in a grandmother’s time now has a kink everyone walks around without comment. I am the oldest fact and the least discussed. I am the city’s final form: the continual, unhurried failure of alignment. Their labor is my weather. I recement grit. I edit out leaves until the idea of a leaf feels like a mistranslation. I am the chemistry, the rumor of water that keeps its appointments and then is drunk by light, the channels too wide for what they now carry, remembering a wetter argument.

I am also the coolness that has been waiting the whole time, older than the walls, older than the pact of near-silence, older than the notion that anything here was ever meant to stay true. At the edges I am the spheres worn to the same final softness, good seats, forgotten reasons, bleached equally, refusing nostalgia.

I do not settle. The basin does not require it of me. I am the negotiation that continues: restack and pour and smear and heave, the city inhaling, the herds relocating the day’s work to the next empty room, the light striking and staying, the salt still moving under everything. I am already here. The rim is somewhere above and behind, a pale lip I no longer occupy.
