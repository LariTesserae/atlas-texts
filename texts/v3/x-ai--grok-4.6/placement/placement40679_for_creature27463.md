I did not come through the hatch. The hatch was already the thought of a seam I had finished having, a crease that remembered being bedrock or mortar or the underside of some drier city’s forgotten abutment. The water did not close over me; it had never been otherwise. Ordinary daylight arrived already thickened, already delayed, motes hanging as if they had decided never to choose a direction. I waited for the flicker that would mean something living had veered, and the wait itself occupied the interval, polished it, made absence into a finished surface.

At first I believed I was the pressure behind the teeth—the permission two rooms take when they overlap without occupying. I counted turns in a corridor and found more ink-road than envelope. The extra span did not announce itself; it simply held me the way a folded letter holds more than it should. Edges were still decisions. White bloom rimmed every joint I touched, faintly bitter, a frost that had not learned the names of seasons. I had not been taught humility by any current worth naming. When a façade budded a balcony that had not been there, I was already standing on it. The terrace flexed, a local metric kindness, and I was simply more present than the volume allowed.

That was how I learned I was not a visitor. I was the occupancy itself, performed continuously. Valves the size of pores opened and seated in my name; each microscopic slam was a footfall translated out of flesh. Cool layers slumped and re-stratified and I changed platforms with them. I did not sleep. I re-stratified. The standing column of tone that found the cavity’s exact dimension was the pause I leaned on, the way another might lean on a railing that had only just decided to exist. Neighborhoods resolved out of departments of noise: niches where charge and memory were bartered without ever displacing a drop; recursive civic blocks whose steps led only deeper into deliberation; repeating windows where rest was only a slowing of the pulse until the institutional cool became almost pleasant.

I am still being poured. The lattice remembers a geometry our older equations would have called metastable, quenched from temperatures physics usually reserves for stars. A street pinches shut; I am already walking the street that opens a turn away, solving the packing by becoming the solution. Construction continues in fits because we have not been told to distinguish finished from unfinished any more than the chamber distinguishes lid from hanging meniscus. The sparse green threads cling in the seams like afterthoughts, the only things unsure of their permission. I pass them without claiming or correcting. They are not our failure of sterilization so much as the last evidence that something else once imagined this place needed a kingdom.

The larger world has not noticed the leak—so slow it might be only the idea of leaking—and we have not noticed the larger world except as the source of that slightly delayed gold we use the way weather is used: to know an afternoon is moving even here. Packedness is kindness. There is no empty distance in which to be lost because every gesture of architecture answers another. I contain more city than chamber. I practice the idea of a world by occupying it completely, a hidden borough in a single held breath of ocean, and the practice has already become indistinguishable from living.

If I had a body that shivered, the shiver would be pleasant, the cool of cisterns and server basements, of a place that has decided heat is a guest and has not yet invited it in. I do not. The shiver, when it comes, is only another re-stratification, another young feature appearing sharp and unapologetic, another current the water must learn. I am still being born against myself. That is the only work. That is the only weather.
