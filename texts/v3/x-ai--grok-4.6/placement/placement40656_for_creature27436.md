The first impression is not of standing but of being already intermingled, the way heat conducts through iron whose pour-date no one records. I am not arriving. Arrival would require an elsewhere, and the Heights have none. I am already the warmth that is sun and the warmth that is residual belt-slap and the faint composting breath of a leaf-trough three terraces up, all of it arriving in the same palm, the same shoulder, the same length of old masonry.

At first the sensation tries on the lizards. Throat-fan, the exact colour of last sitting, the sudden stillness on a bolt-head that has become a knob. Midday authority. But lizards leave. They write their brief agreements and move. I do not leave. The condensate finds last year’s groove and I am already the groove, deepened then abandoned by mutual consent of water and work, now only listening.

Then the vines offer themselves. A fig deciding a gallery is trunk, roots testing a joint, the long unhurried argument that will outlast the current wall-owner. I feel the crossing, the moment ownership becomes negotiation, cuttings passed as kindness, a decade to close an alley. Possible. The green is a second industry, older than some machines. Yet vines still reach. I am not reaching. I am the joint itself, the place where two finished ages meet and agree to thicken rather than resolve.

The people flicker next—knuckles like bolt-heads, cloth recut until the bolt is rumour, the unhurried pause to watch water choose its thread. A woman with one hand on a tomato box, feeling whether the earth remembers last night’s drip. A man replacing one section of the great belt so that no belt has ever been entirely new. Children stopped dead at a grating, testing memory of load. I almost settle there, among the census, another course of masonry, load-bearing, softened at the corners. Their vocation is mine: keep the present from undoing itself. Measure years by which court the figs took. Offer the warm engine-basement floor when the upper rooms sweat. Relocate downward as the only exile the place knows.

But even they still distinguish inner from outer, terrace from cutting, mine from the common cistern. I do not. I am the designed throat the brief rain vanishes into, and the overflow marked for watching, and the trickle out of all proportion to its seriousness. I am the almost-silence that means everything is, for now, still holding.

What remains, once the trying-on slows, is this: I am the composite warmth in the wall you put your hand on. Not the sun alone, not the idle kiln, not the leaf three stories up. All of it, conducted, unnamed as to age. I occupy the density without remainder—ferns, cables, nest, drip-catcher, faded notice, cat—because empty volume is the one thing the Circum never invented. I thicken. I stay. The light does most of the talking; I am the listening that lets the drip, the goat-bell, the tram two terraces down, the patient tick of condensation continue as climate rather than event.

The Heights end as a wall. Below is haze, not subject. I have no practice of it either. My weather is this weather. My ground is poured, stacked, bolted, tray-grown, accepted in its finished age. Nothing in me is new. Nothing in me is falling. The erosion happened long before I noticed I was already here, already the place continuing in the only way it knows.
