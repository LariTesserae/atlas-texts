The felt takes the first step before I know I have taken it. Warmth rises through whatever I am using for weight, a slow muscular give that remembers every previous pressure and is already erasing this one. Dawn-beads vanish into the hide while I am still noticing them. The air arrives as grain, a constant soft collision against whatever I am using for face, for mouth, for the wet surfaces of eyes. Midges, nameless glitter, the pulse of traveling continents that dim the ordinary sun without ever becoming weather I could name. I blink and swallow and keep walking because the horizon is level and very far and the next titan is only a dark vertical interruption that will still be distant at evening.

Sound is already inside the bone. It does not travel toward me; it occupies. Wing and stridulation and the dry working of too many legs braid into one pressure that sits behind the teeth and will not leave. A lower grinding moves somewhere under the mat, a browsing or a waiting that travels like another kind of weather. I learn, without deciding to learn, that a change in key means hatch or tide or the beginning of a shed. The thin screaming edge arrives when wind finds the hollow ribs of a trunk I have not yet reached. There is no silence stored anywhere in the body to compare it against.

Hands appear because the work is small and the tools stay hand-scale. They know the flake of stone, the awl of bone, the pull of cordage, the smear of resin that stains skin the color of old bark. Ears are packed with the pale wool that weeps from certain seams, yet the chord still arrives through the jaw, through the soles, so that hearing becomes a thing the whole frame does. I harvest the sweet that collects in the axils after the polite rain. I wait at the black mirrors at dusk for the fat-bodied drinkers whose sides gleam once before they are taken, dried in the brief hard sun, stored against weeks that are only slightly leaner. When a leaf the size of a hillside begins its day-long lowering I go to the new country of rotting green and live for a while inside a different register of the roar, sweet and deafening, a temporary city of mouths. Then the mat eats the leaf back into floor and the emptiness that is only unoccupied by mass resumes, and so do I.

The platforms last a season. Woven titan-fiber lashed into the lee, already black with the smoke of small fires, swaying with the slow vegetable breath of the wood they lean against. Ladders of vine that were persuaded, years ago, to hold one useful curve. Windbreaks of stacked chitin and felt-brick that the living ground has already begun to overgrow. Paths close behind. Nothing pretends a wall. I am spaced the way the titans are spaced. I came from a lower world once; a beaten scrap of soft metal still exists somewhere, an ornament I have stopped needing and may have left in a seam. Speech, when it happens, is mouth to ear or a low note added to the existing pressure and released. Children (there are children, sometimes) are born already inside the held chord. For them vibration is only pulse. I show them the wool anyway. Their gait already knows not to fight the hide.

Days out, the ground forgets how to stay flat. It thickens, thins, begins the long worn rounding that has been happening since before names. I stand on that lip in ordinary afternoon light. Warm wind comes up out of the white gulf and suddenly the roar has a direction because now there is an edge for it to work. Below, only the tops of clouds, slow white animals that will never be allowed up. Height is nothing but a quality of light and of distance. I look until looking is finished. Then I turn back. The surface has no further ambition except to remain, damp, loud, nearly empty of objects, almost entirely full. I have become another rarity it permits: temporary as a path, stained, mostly silent, another kind of weather that learned, slowly, how to stay.

The vacancy holds all of it and does not announce itself. I am inside that holding.
