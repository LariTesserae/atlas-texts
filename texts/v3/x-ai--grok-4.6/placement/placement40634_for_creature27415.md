I would find myself first as a hesitation in the air of the central court, the kind that arrives with the light and does not announce itself. Not a body yet. Only the faint wetness that the stone has been exhaling since before the lintels learned their sag, a coolness that settles into the joints of the flags and waits to see whether it will be dew or merely the memory of dew. The courts are too large; my presence would occupy one of the empty widths without filling it, the way a single dark patch occupies a downhill wall after the gutters have lost interest. I would not hurry. The thinness of the air would not allow it, and nothing here has ever required more than the amount already budgeted.

Slowly the hesitation would take an outline. Not clothing first, not skin. A rounding, the same weather that has taken every corner. I would be the width of a thumb in the middle of a lintel, the dishing of a threshold worn by feet that have forgotten they were ever new. Then the dark of the timber: swollen, never quite dry, cut in a shape that has no fashion left to forget. A hem rounded by abrasion. A sleeve that holds the damp the way the lower courses hold it. When I moved—if movement is the word—it would be the rake lifted from its passage and set down again, the pot placed exactly under the broken spout and taken in before overflow can become a story. My steps would already contain their own delayed echo; I would walk as though rehearsing the stone’s reply, because that is the only conversation the place still permits.

I would be one of the few, interchangeable with the last. The third chimney would stay cold because I had already accounted for the smoke of two. I would touch the hand-mill at the hour when habit is still a kind of hunger, feel the satin that no longer wants grain, and leave the hopper empty. I would drink the seep when it ticked, or only shine my palm across the basin’s rim to confirm that last night’s condensation had been enough. Grain, when it came up the path that does not return, would never fill anything; I would crush the bronze grass instead and accept the bitterness as the town’s idea of plenty. At the western wall I would stand the way the wall itself stands, considering the receding palenesses, allowing the cloud to make dark mouths of us both. I would not call across the courts. Speech would be the sound of a sag that has belonged to us since before we were the ones now living.

Night would not distinguish us. The stone would breathe out its condensation and I would breathe it back; we would settle rather than sleep, the ledge and I keeping the same temperature. In the first pale hour I would be the shutter left ajar, or the knife-blade on the sill, dark, used, the minimum of metal the place will bear. You could leave and return after what felt like a generation and find me still there, or find only the wall, and I would not help you decide. I have already made every sound I intend to make. I am the town keeping itself, the amount of person the damp and the cool and the finished stone will allow. The mountain keeps me. Nothing else is required.
