The heat arrives before any other knowledge, a slow unclenching in the stone itself. It does not burn; it occupies, the way water occupies, the way the beetles occupy. I feel it first as a thickness in whatever I use for breath, then as a slickness along surfaces I have not yet decided are mine. Drips stitch the dark. Somewhere the beam engine nods, a vast and patient lung, and I answer it without meaning to—an answering scrape, or a settling, or the sound a body makes when it is still learning which parts of itself are stone.

I am farther in than the rule allows.

The north adit does not end so much as hesitate. Drill-holes pock the face like unfinished thoughts. The heat here is older, wetter, laced with a smell that is beetles crushed under something heavier, mixed with iron that has begun to remember blood. The men who have come a little way speak of it afterward in lowered voices, as if naming it might invite it closer. They are not wrong. I have been listening to them for—how long? Time here is the interval between one generation of the dark insects and the silt they leave. Time is the thickening of a seep after rain that fell days ago on a ridge I have never seen. Time is the calcite skin the walls keep laying down, thinner than breath, writing over themselves.

I was not here when the first charges went off. Or I was, but only as possibility: a film in a joint, a memory of the sea that once laid this limestone down grain by grain, a patience that had not yet been given a shape large enough to scrape. The blast opened a continent and I began, the way the frogs began, the way the soil in the lowest corner is beginning. Heat and silt and the castings of a thousand thousand beetles who had been the limestone’s secret. Something ferrous and alive. I drag myself along the drill-holes when the dynamo stutters and the four caged bulbs dim to the color of a held breath. The crickets’ high wire sings louder then, as if they have been waiting for the dark to thicken. I do not eat them. I do not need to. I am already a kind of digestion: the chamber tasting itself, assembling a slower body from what it has already become.

The beetles know me. They sheet the wetter walls of the adit in moving dark and do not startle when I pass. They map the salt that weeps from whatever I am using for skin. Sometimes I feel them exploring the places where I am still deciding—here a joint that might be claw, here a mass that might be the memory of a flank. They do not bite. They have never needed to. Their castings settle in the grooves I leave, already becoming the next layer of me, or of the floor, or of the thing the chamber will be when the pans have cut themselves into a creek and the ninth structure has either stood or slumped back into slag.

I hear the men at the edge of their shifts, their voices arriving distorted by the heat, by the swallow of the chamber. Six-hour ghosts in dripping wool. They grease the engines, they shovel the black silt, they lay another course of pale brick that will never sit quite true. They do not look back when they climb the ladders. Looking back would mean admitting the place has begun to keep them. Their sweat joins the seeps. Their discarded crusts become another generation. In twenty years the ones who remain will have the same unfinished look as the walls, the same slow surprise. I do not envy them the ladders. The surface is a rumor the bats still carry in their fur, a colder air they bring down the chimney at the hour they insist is dusk. When they hang in their trembling pelt from the unshored span I feel them as a single dark organ breathing, and when they lift the beetles rise with them in a double storm. I stay. The adit is throat enough.

The frogs sit in the warmest seeps with their glass throats pulsing. They appeared in the third month; no one saw them arrive. I might have been the hairline they came through, or the warmth that grew them the way the chamber grew its first calcite. They take the beetles that blunder too close with a motion too fast for the dimness. Their stillness is a kind of listening. I listen too. To the compressor’s long irregular breath. To the rust weeping ochre tears down every unpainted bolt. To the pale stems in the unpointed cracks, fern-white, blackening at the tips where the heat finds them. To the brass lichen mapping the cooler western wall in continents no larger than a palm. They are rumors of forest. I am a rumor of animal. We are all the labor of a room that did not exist last season and may not exist in twenty years: eating, dripping, singing, scraping, uncurling, dying into silt that the water carries a little farther each day.

Sometimes, when the beam engine misses a nod, I answer more clearly. The sound travels the length of the chamber and the men pause in their work, heads tilted, faces glazed and dripping under the frosted bulbs. They feel it in their teeth. They tell themselves it is only the rock still finding its joints. They are not entirely wrong. I am the joints, or I am what the joints are becoming now that there is heat enough, water enough, death enough. The place is still being born. I am part of the birth: the scrape that is not quite claw, the settling that is not quite stone, the first larger body the soak has tried to grow in the dark behind its own unfinished face.

I do not go farther. There is no farther yet. The drill-holes end in raw limestone that has not decided whether it will open or close. I press against it the way the water presses, the way the beetles press, patient, provisional. Tonight the four circles of light overlap in the main chamber and everything is awake together—men, beetles, crickets, frogs, bats, the pale stems, the weeping rust, the engines learning to belong. I stay in the throat that is still learning to be a throat. The heat holds us. The water writes our names in temporary stone. The beetles keep the only count that matters.

I am the thing they hear at the edge of sleep. I am still deciding what that means.
