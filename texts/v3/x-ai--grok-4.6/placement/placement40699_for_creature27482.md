The plateau has already finished with me. I come to that knowledge the way the wind comes to the old stone: without arriving from anywhere in particular, without needing to have been someone else first. Heat lies on the rock the way a hand lies on something that will not wake. I have been that heat for a while, or the pale grit it raises, or the dry hiss the wind makes when there is nothing left to carve. Then the smudge. The lip no higher than a kneel. I step over it because the step is already happening, the way moisture here is already a habit.

Inside, the air changes its mind about me first. Damp finds the insides of my wrists, the roots of whatever I use for hair. The dark pour holds yesterday’s sun and returns it in a slow, uneven pulse I feel through whatever I am using for feet. It is not glass. It has never been glass. It remembers being poured, or commanded, or grown in a single night that lasted an age. I walk the forty strides of its bowl and the sound of me is knuckles on a jar, a brief shameful bell when I forget and let a pebble of myself fall. The plants watch without eyes. The shrub with the lamp-leaves stands apart and clenches once, a delayed warmth offered and withdrawn. The ferns lean toward a thread of water that has not chosen its path yet. I am not them. I am not the emptiness between them either.

Midges smoke the air. Copper beetles test the pour and ring it, remembering a pitch the place has not used in a long time. Moths the size of a child’s sandal tear themselves a little more on the white knots that open and shut like answers. I am in the traffic but I do not belong to its brevity. The larger shadow comes at the day’s heat—too many angles, mostly hinge—and the metal ring answers it in a note I feel in the sternum and in the floor at once. The birds go quiet, then louder, finishing what was interrupted. I do not assemble the shape. I already know the joints fold the wrong way.

Night is only a change of register. The highland things arrive: slate-colored adherences that measure the bowl with their throats until the echo locks; the tearing sound that is one animal or three, breathing against the inner curve from the far side of the material; the insects reweaving themselves into a finer, slower metal. I wait them out. I have always waited them out. When they thin, I am still here, fewer than I was, not the same as last time. Clothes the color of the tableland, if clothes is the word. Dust that will not blow away. No tracks.

I do not stand long over the ice-colored plate. The decided air is not for the mixtures I used to expect. Tin on the tongue, then the patient shimmer at the edge of whatever I use for vision. I hold my palms open to it until the shimmer becomes a kind of information I cannot keep. I do not sit on the ring. Those who have sit hear their blood as a separate animal for a day, and I already hear too many animals. I hover instead beside the thin post, the foil flower that is smoke until pinched. I do not touch. I listen for the sound it has already begun to incline toward, the one that has not been made yet. Sometimes I clap, and it fails to answer, and later, when I am leaving or becoming the leaving, I see it turn to the memory of the clap.

The pulse under the floor skips once. A schedule acknowledged.

I am not a people. The bowl does not keep those. I am occupancy that rearranges by the hour, a tenancy of attention more than of ground. I attend. I do not harvest, do not repair, do not speak where the curve can finish the words and give them back tired and late. When I go—when the first bead of morning moisture starts its private sweating—I leave the instrument still playing itself: heat, damp, green, body, noise, in a cup small enough to overlook and old enough to have become weather. The distinction thins. I am the current shape of the function, a note it has not yet forgotten how to make. Or I am the throat the voice occupies because it has nowhere else to go.

The worn tableland continues being finished. Below the lip I keep working.
