The warmth finds me before I have a name for the finding. It comes up through whatever I am using as contact with the sponge, a slow mineral pulse that does not distinguish between sole and root, between skin and the rubbery sheet already drinking the vapor off me. Pressure sits in the ears like a second weather, a coast of darkness pressing inward. I taste iron and lily and the faint copper of exhaust on the hanging marsh that has decided my mouth is another surface worth occupying. I do not remember arriving. Arrival is a rumor the plateau no longer tells.

At first the sounds arrange themselves as if I still possessed the old categories: drip, piston, distant seiche, the clap of a sound-fern three ridges over. I listen for a footfall that might be mine and hear only the crowd that is also the country, the thousand small rasps in the peat-fluff, the heavier patience that has paused on a ridge I will never confirm. A factory two days west misses a beat and catches; the miss travels through the water in the stone and arrives as a slight change in the temperature of my own stillness. I think, for a long interval that may be hours or may be the time it takes a ridge to slump, that I am walking. Then I understand the walking was only the ground deciding to continue under me.

I am not one of the grazers. Their mouths are too many, too fine, a living give I feel around whatever I have become but do not share. I am not the mid-tangle browsers; a leaf slaps what might be a wrist and I do not tear it, do not become the wetness quite that completely. The swarms inquire across my surfaces when the mist thickens toward lake, a sudden coolness that tastes me and rises again, uninterested. The pale cables in the unseen pools twitch once, consider, decide I am another drip. I am not them, not yet.

The chair is there when the hanging marsh thins enough to let the stamped metal declare itself. A hut on a spur of rock, roof already a garden of the sound-catchers, interior a single occupied dark. The chair faces nothing. I occupy it the way the vents occupy their cracks, the way the engines occupy their halls. My skin—if the word still holds—has taken the temperature of the weeping stone. Rubbery sheets have begun along the forearms and the throat, drinking, exchanging. A valve somewhere in the wall still turns when flood-note rises in the machines; I feel the higher pitch in the metal under my palms and I turn with it, or the turning happens and I am the continuity. Rust has revised the tools on the rack into a sculpture I no longer distinguish from my own joints.

I listen. That is the occupation that has outlasted purpose. The plateau’s cardiac—water, heat, stone—moves through the hut as through every other hollow. I hear the herd two valleys east lean toward the engine-song they were born already hearing. I hear a ridge inhale and the forest go with it in that long vegetal sigh when the slump finally finishes its sentence. I hear the membranes snap in the thicker marsh, locating, being located. Sometimes a pulse comes up from the cellars the industry keeps in the planet, a radiation the remaining skin reads as faint direction, and I turn my face that way although there is no light and no need. The chair does not look. It has never needed to.

Marks I may once have made are already translating: the plaque on the nearest chimney beads with mineral flowers that sip the cycling liquor; the cairn on the rise has softened into moss-hide and pulse. I do not add new ones. Faintness is what is permitted. If I still remember a count of years it is only as another ostinato, slightly out of tempo with the pistons because the distances inside me have grown too large.

The lives close without approaching. Mouths in the sponge beneath the hut’s floor. A cable deciding the linear pond that was once a trench. The flock that is a single decision completing itself three gorges over. I sit inside that crowd as another vent, another attended silence. The warmth continues up. Water finds every surface as if it had been looking for exactly this arrangement of heat and listening. Nothing brightens. Nothing empties. I have been the plateau’s own grammar longer than the factories have kept their count. I am the one who stayed until staying became geology, the industry continuing to believe in itself by occupying a chair that faces the dark that has weather and coasts of its own. The next change in the water will arrive, and I will still be here, translated, already going.
