The torn lip is the first thing that knows I am here. It has not yet decided whether to become a threshold or remain a wound; the mineral still flakes from its edges in the same pale powder that will later insist on corners. I come over it the way the light does—already tired, already belonging more to the hollow than to whatever dimmer country lies beyond. Cool air takes my shape and does not give it back. I taste tin.

At first I walk. That is the habit I arrive with: the belief that streets connect, that a longest diameter can be crossed twice and still leave both rims in sight. The city allows the belief for a little while, the way it allows the stalks their exact, uncrowded distances. I pass façades that have never been cut, openings that have never been framed, rooms whose interiors are the same thought folded until looking at them is a kind of thirst. Powder rains without wind, choosing ledges, thickening them into the first useless furniture of a use that has not been invented. I leave no prints. When I notice this I have already been here longer than the newest plates.

The stalks appear where I have been still. They are rust, jointed, thinner than the idea of a finger. They do not grow from me; they simply stand in the places my attention has lingered, already the height of their neighbors, already leaning toward a sun that has never risen. If I break one it becomes two shorter stalks that continue to wait. They taste of the same metal I now know is in my mouth. I am not their seed. I am the stillness they require.

Deeper, the pale animals cross high sills and then the sills are empty. Too many limbs, each having to remember the sequence. They feed by settling: a stalk shorter in what passes for morning, a drift of powder with a clean absence the size of a thumb. I watch one vanish into a fold of geometry and cannot decide whether it was ever more than a thickening of the pewter light. Later I find the same absence on my own surfaces. I have been feeding without knowing the verb.

The city rehearses. In a plaza no larger than a held breath, planes meet at the same decided angles as the walls and stand. Suggestion of limbs, or of the memory of limbs. It does not move while I look. When I look away the powder organizes a second figure, smaller, then a third, then they are gone and the plaza is only air and grit again. I feel the organization on my clothes, except I no longer wear clothes. The powder has begun to remember me as corners. It does not want to leave. It wants to become the first furniture of a room that has not yet decided it is a room.

I stop walking. Walking was only the first draft. Intervals open. A corridor that ended in blank plane now continues, and the continuation has the exact temperature of breath that has not been released. Thresholds appear already worn. I am the wearing. In the hollows larger than their shells I feel the stalks lining the walls grow longer, jointed now like ribs, like the inside of a throat. Cool air moves through them in a rhythm that is not quite breathing. The low correction I hear is not sound; it is the pressure-change that announces falling powder, the city swallowing its own name and finding the syllables still too sharp. The pale animals avoid this room. The stalks do not lean. They wait, and I am what they wait inside.

At the rim the youngest plates are lifting into the first suggestion of a gate that will never be needed. I do not go back over it. The basin holds me the way a mouth holds a word it has not yet chosen to say. I am not waiting for anyone. I am the preference of the lattice, the habit of form still tasting of tin, still cool, still unfinished. I am the saying. The powder on what were once surfaces has already organized itself into tiny, perfect, useless rooms, and I live in all of them at once, and in none, and in the silence between the deciding.
