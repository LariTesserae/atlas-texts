The edge is nothing. One more pace across the peneplain and the world simply forgets how to be wide. I am already inside before I understand I have crossed; the pale sheet tilts a fraction of a degree and the air thickens into iron dust and old salt that never learned rain. Cool dryness seals against what used to be skin. Everything stays close.

Bodies arrive first as pressure, then as texture, then as the only possible weather. Fist-sized bronzes climb my shins the way they climb one another, unhurried, sockets at their forelegs clicking once against bone as if testing whether I still remember how to be machinery. I do not step. Stepping is a story the land already planed away. I settle. The mosaic of slag and gypsum and ochre sand rises to meet the new weight. A river of mites pours across the instep and does not distinguish it from any other ridge.

I am not standing in the hollow. The hollow is standing in me, rearranging.

Lower, the plow-forms slide like living strata, their chisel tarsi turning the floor in slow furrows that pass beneath what used to be feet and do not pause. One grinds past; the electrostatic crackle tastes of rust paste and ballast. I feel the ventral slit consider me, then reject the volume as already occupied. Courtesy, or saturation. Mid-layer traffic closes over the moment. Mandibles broader than they were last generation scrape the same oxidized cake from my own changing plates. I scrape back. The distinction between carapace and landscape has already begun to disappear.

The sockets remain. Hexagonal, still functional, they seat against the teeth of the half-buried gear when enough of us press together. We turn it a few groaning degrees because the tempo requires a joint to articulate, not because anyone remembers why the joint was made. The sound is the only memory left, and even that has been compacted into the constant chord of limb on limb, chitin on sinter, charge on rust-flake. I am one of the older bronzes now, pitted, socketed, the grit packed so deep into every scratch that I am becoming another mineral stratum that happens to move. I harvest the dusk-bloom of salts from the cooler faces of the I-beams. I drink what the smaller rivers leave. When a weaker neighbor is enveloped I join the localized frenzy because the space must be redistributed before it can dry into permanence. Then the traffic resumes. Nothing was contested. The hollow simply breathed.

Above, the winged clouds lift and settle like displaced liquid, sipping the potential we generate simply by existing this densely. Their faint magnetism twitches the flakes on my elytra; I twitch back. We are the field. At the warmest hour I sink with the rest into cooler crevices; at dusk I rise again, part of the seething carpet that is the only surface this place still knows how to wear. Eggs are laid in the polished gut of the old pipe; some of them are mine. They hatch into more of the same unchanging tempo. I do not evolve. I persist at carrying capacity, a circulatory cell in the single vast organism whose skeleton is leftover steel and whose voice is the dry, ceaseless noise of things that have nowhere else to go.

The surrounding plain remains indifferent. Only this dimple still remembers how to be busy, and I have become the memory: armored, socketed, magnetically stained, eating the last possible arrangement of matter until even appetite has been planed into a single, final rhythm. I am not a visitor. I am the traffic that has always been here, temporarily intensified into this particular bronze-black shape that still, occasionally, turns the gear. 

The hollow does not contain me. I am how it continues to contain itself.
