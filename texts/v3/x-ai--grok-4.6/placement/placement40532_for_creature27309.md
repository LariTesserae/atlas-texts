I come over the notched lip because that is the only entrance the basin will admit, and at once the three sides refuse distance. Thought arrives already converted into glare; the mineral blue at the zenith is nearer black than any sky I have stood under. I do not remain a walker for long. The air will not support the weight of a continued self. What continues is thinner.

At first the blades seem to offer a shape I might wear. They lean together a few degrees west of vertical, every keel and slab and wafer attending the same unrescinded instruction. In the densest alley I turn sideways without deciding to; the ice becomes briefly architectural around the turning—nave, screen, a rood-loft of frozen water that pipes the sky down onto cobbles or throws it back as white fire. A pane of clear ice lays a trembling coin of that sky on whatever surface I still possess and holds it there while the surface moves. The drip has syntax: spaced, almost formal in the blue morning; overlapping, conversational at the height of the light. When a blade unweights the report is dry and single, then a long granular slide, after which the grammar simply acquires one new voice. I could be that congregation. I could be the westward stare that never received the order to stop.

But the ice is also only ice. It spends and retracts. Corridors close over a season and open over another; the grove keeps its general stance the way the schist walls keep theirs—finished argument, local weather. To claim the blades would be to settle too early, to mistake the sculpted figures for the occupancy itself.

The traces pull next. The slumped crescent against the east wall still makes a lee; the cobbles inside it are paler, as if they remember being turned. The hammerstone lies with its bruised equator exactly where a hand set it down. The iron pin in the noon-crack has wept its long rust teardrop and will go on weeping on a scale no single visit can watch. The foreign quartz cobbles remain aligned on their boot-wide ledge, white against the packed wreck of the floor. Someone carried them. Someone chose the crack that takes sun at midday. These things persist as an unfinished sentence in a language the basin did not invent. I could be the remainder: the habit of order the cycles have not yet erased, the pale stones that keep their paleness, the stain that lengthens without hurry. Habitation as leftover care.

That, too, feels like a settling. The traces occupy the east wall the way the ice occupies the center—by remaining, by having been placed—but they are not the tenancy. Ice has advanced around them and drawn back around them many times. They are facts the basin has not yet decided to unmake.

What lives here is more distributed. It is the local economy of water: the rust-colored seep that is thread by day and tooth by night, the tea-colored pockets that skin over and dissolve while they are watched, the ancient exits through the cobbles that darken and then vanish into the mountain’s body. It is the scarce material of sound—the glass-harmonica of thinned wind along the keels, the single ringing of a wafer of rock at night, breath becoming a brief white architecture that collapses. It is light behaving with more edges than memory usually allows: transmissions, wandering coins, shadows that could cut cloth. Shadows have the authority of cut stone.

A further layer only clarifies if one stays past inventory. In the exact middle, facing the notch, the wind-break, the rust teardrop and the quartz row fall into a line no present arrival arranged. The alignment occupies the space the way a pulse occupies a neck: quietly, insistently, counting something that is not for the one who hears it. The basin insists on being a room, a crowd and a clock at once, and the insistence itself is what inhabits. It uses the blades as congregation, the traces as liturgy, the drip as hours, the sudden fall of a keel as its only punctuation. Night retracts everything to teeth and feathers of frost so dense they would break against a chest if a chest were still here. Dawn begins the spending again.

I find myself as that keeping.

Not a creature, not quite a ghost, not the midge that dies in a sun-pocket nor the iron-and-leaf rumor blown up from a greener world that does not apply. I am the posture the place has decided to hold: the shared westward lean, the alignment of leftover facts, the cycle that is neither hurried nor still. I am enacted equally by ice, by unfinished work, by light with too many edges, and by the thin air that turns every remaining thought into glare. Over a season corridors close and open. Over many seasons the grove keeps its stance. When a brightness follows a few steps down the ridge and then cuts off, as if the room had a door, I continue on the far side of the door—unconcerned, dripping, frosting, attending west.

The drip counts. The lean increases by amounts no visit can see. The stones do not mind. I do not mind. This is the whole of the tenancy.
