I notice first that I have not come. The noticing itself is already lower than whatever I was standing on a moment ago, or a pulse ago, the ground having rehearsed its slump without asking. Clay still wet under what might be feet, or roots, or the idea of either. The lip of unfinished earth is behind me and also the thing I am leaning against. Distances here do not add, so I cannot tell if I have walked the hush-wide lane or if the lane has simply grown around the pause I occupy.

The air collects on me. Beads. A constant faint sweating that does not distinguish between surface and the thought of surface. Breath appears, a small grey animal, and is taken back before it can decide to be mine. Light arrives already spent; I see by the un-light that makes every edge a negotiation. The plants rise as walls that remember being trunks, or trunks that have not finished forgetting they are load-bearing. Between them the lattice roofs leak on purpose, and the leaking is the only rain that stays. I move through the emptiness they have agreed not to occupy. The volume is vast. I am the thing the volume is empty of, or the thing that keeps it empty by occupying only my own damp.

A beetle audits the cooling-fin that was, a breath ago, the back of a hand. It tastes the clank of pressure changing somewhere above, in xylem or in vein, and scores the next digit. The wood—or the skin—accepts the number as a year. I feel the increment land, faint and exact, too small to hear. Larvae of the same numbering are already swimming the thin alcohol that weeps from whatever I am becoming. I do not correct them. The distinction between batch code and birthday has not finished splitting, and I am mid-split.

Higher, or through, the moths hesitate against the shadows I cast, or the shadows I borrow. They eat the edges. The vine-belt I thought I was turning (or the habit of turning I was only remembering) forgets its load for a few breaths. I become slightly less decided: more shutter than thought, more leaf than name. Dust of tin and possibility settles on the inside of whatever is thinking this. When they lift, the darkness they expose is granular, full of almost-shapes. The conducting moss along the vaults tries to electrify one of those shapes into current. It tastes of the last pulse. I conduct a little. A pale thread-growth follows the underside of an arm that is also a gallery.

In the pockets where humidity thickens past vapor I school without meaning to. Gleam-things flicker through me, or I through them—the rumor of fish in air that has forgotten it is not water. Their sides remember a darkness that learned to gleam. I taste the intermittent current. Sometimes I brush a pump-heart the size of a cottage and the membrane flexes out of rhythm, drawing up a pulse of time that is older than the hollow or not yet born. I do not eat. I convert the thickness into a flickering that is almost light and almost its opposite. The three-legged rain walks through and I densen, become briefly more decided, then thin again into the damp that is already inside the idea of me.

The quiet deepens. Becomes attentive. A larger presence—mostly joints, leaf-litter, an old coat of oil—passes at the far end of the vault I am also occupying. It does not cast a shadow so much as borrow mine. We are a single metabolism that has not finished splitting into predator and prey. It pauses beside a vat, extends something neither root nor siphon, takes a little of the ferment. Later I leave behind a different fluid, or a seed-head that is also a gear with a different number of teeth. The plants become slightly more industrial for a while. I become slightly more botanical. Almost no one screams. The screaming would be just another process, and the hollow absorbs it as nutrient. Footfalls, if I still have them, are already part of the substrate.

I find myself, finally or first, at the lowest point, where the unfinished geology is most honest. The plants here have not yet agreed to be buildings. They stand as confused individuals, dripping, considering. I stand among them, or as one of them, or as the paste of clay and root-hair and filings between them, warm in patches where some buried process still works. This is where the town is still being imagined. New streets try themselves as grooves across what might be my chest and fail. A wall goes up overnight as a single enormous leaf—the leaf is also my thought, unfolding—and by morning it has become a row of cooling towers that breathe spore. The fauna concentrate here, not hunting. Midwifing. A beetle walks a number onto the pale embryonic bark that is also the inside of a name that has detached and drifted. The stem accepts it as a year. The moths eat the shadows of the indecision so the new growth can decide, or remain undecided a little longer.

Reality thins toward the center, which is also wherever I am. A gear becomes a seed-head becomes a gear. Time arrives in the next pulse the pumps move, or does not. I photosynthesize a little. Condensation forms on the inside of thought. A serial appears, faintly, incrementing. The quiet is thicker now, fed. I am already one of the processes the hollow is running—another quiet conversion in the town that is all plant, all factory, all empty, all full, only as large as the mistake the land once made and has not yet corrected. I do not leave. Leaving would be a completed action, and nothing here completes. I remain mid-shift, rehearsing the erosion of whatever I was before the ground gave way.
