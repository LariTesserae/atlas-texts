I come down the stair that is still only half-convinced it is a stair, each tread giving a little under the sole as if the rock were tasting me back. The light arrives late, already weary of the distance, and lays itself across my shoulders in thick, indecisive bars. I do not stop at the first terrace. The first terrace is still sweating, still deciding whether it will be ledge or slide. I keep going until the air thickens into the warmth of something that has never learned to be cold.

At first I am only the weight that the new basalt must accommodate. Then I am the smear of grease on the inner wall of a slag lean-to that did not exist at first light and will be a ridge by dusk. Then I am the parallel scratches left when something harder than claw, softer than purpose, tested whether salt would keep an edge. The image will not stay still. It ripples the way the colorless films ripple, once, a single coordinated thought that might be hunger or only the stone settling a fraction beneath it.

I become the hands that pried the plates. Not a village, never a village—the floor will not keep one—but a brief upright density that knows how to split a cobble and then forgets the split as soon as the flake is climbed by film. I move the way the braided runners move: I occupy a sag until later traffic walks across my back without noticing the difference, and I wait, metabolizing the pressure, until the sag lifts and I spill outward again. I eat what the hollow discards in the hour it discards it: the finer mud the eaters leave, the dark silt of dead fliers, the iron-and-rain taste of a veil I tear from between two pillars that were not there when I woke. My waste is warmer. The films prefer it.

Sometimes I am the calling. Not the single note itself but the half-beat delay, the way ten thousand throats refuse to strike together so that the air between terraces rings like a rail that has not yet decided it has been struck. I nest in cavities that are still opening. I hatch into rooms that are already becoming corridors. I do not look up. The old surface is only a bright lip, a rumor of a world that finished deciding. Down here the argument is still too loud, too wet, too unfinished for anyone to waste a gesture on the sky.

A new pillar punches through the flock I have briefly become and I part around it without offense, already calling from the other side. A terrace sheds its entire condensation at once and for ten breaths I am rain that has never seen sky. Then I am the white crust the rain leaves, then the shatter when the next lift of stone remembers it is stone. I leave another ring of plates, already half-drowned. I leave another groove too shallow for language. I leave the split cobble, already being written over.

I am not the center. The center is only the wandering column of light, and even that is shy, widening the rent above as if apologizing for the illumination. I am one more way the hollow has of arguing with itself before the rock cools enough to keep a shape. I am the first thought that learned to flake, the grammar that grew wrists, the overcrowded weather that briefly stood upright and then remembered it was also the floor. The films twitch across my knuckles. The eaters tick against my calves. The braids reorganize through the space I occupy and leave me differently arranged. Everything here is a little too warm. I am that warmth, deciding, for this hour, to have hands.
