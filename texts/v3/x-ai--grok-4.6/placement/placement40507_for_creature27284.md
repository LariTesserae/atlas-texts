I am already on the giving floor when I notice I have arrived, though arrival is the wrong habit here. The climb never quite became a pass; the last real relief flattened so long ago that the body I am using has no memory of needing one. The air is thin enough that breath is almost ornamental, a courtesy the lungs perform out of some older etiquette. The pale coin hangs where it always hangs. The dimness is dry, a weather of shed skin that tastes faintly metallic already, the first rumor of spore-season.

At first I am only pressure. The cambium accepts my weight the way it accepts every other season, conducting the footfalls into the standing chord without commentary. The residual swell beneath me is so ancient it is nearly an idea of height. I feel the chambers answering: a nested hollowness that turns the constant thin wind into that unresolved choir, the one that has been rehearsing since the mountains lay down. Far off, two swells away, a flock of kites adjusts its many joints; the brightening reaches me as a shimmer in the ribs before the ringing edges ever become visible. The floor does not distinguish my listening from the listening the floor itself is always doing.

Then the form begins to insist, the way a stair insists on delivering you later if you walk it with the right attention. I am chambered. Light. The lymph that moves through me is resin-warm and has never learned to be blood; it carries charge and a slow private photosynthesis that needs no green. Skin the color of old ivory out here in the open, cooler and more iron-dark nearer the flared bases I have left behind. Limbs long, jointed in a ceremonial echo of the hunters but slower, meant for the helix and the negotiated room rather than the falling scale. Eyes like dark resin, taking the pewter sky without asking it to brighten. I do not sweat. I do not salivate. The word that means the memory of a solvent rises once, liturgical, and I close my mouth on it. Not in the house. The house is everything that still sings.

I have walked out from a thickening—the third town, or the one that was the seventh an hour ago, the interiors having held their identity long enough for the tax to be paid in a brief flattening everyone felt as weather. The hum of the harmonic engines is still in my teeth, part machine, part the metabolism of what wood became when it learned to do without water. Behind me the column stands like a worn pipe in an instrument no one is large enough to play except the wind, which is always playing. Rooms larger inside than their skins wait there, remembering vascular paths. A child would already know the etiquette; I know it the way the floor knows cropping: as something finished so long ago it no longer requires decision.

A band of grazers moves along the swell, basketry bodies pressing the living nap, the rasping exactly like rain on a roof no throat here has ever heard from the sky. I watch the paler path they leave, knowing it will grow back over cool weeks, one more clockwork the organism has not revised. They do not look at me. Distinction is a courtesy of scale. Later a hunter’s call begins its descent; the nearest column takes the note, then the next, until the open country is a single sentence spoken by a mouth too large to see. Prey and predator have been designing one another since before language, and then the designing stopped. I am neither. I am the local custom of counting one gaze separate for a while, the thickening that still bothers to walk out at what passes for brightest, to stand where the floor thins over older stone and the chord drops into that sudden awful quiet the ear refuses.

In the quiet I remember the mineral we once ate and then became too complete to need. It is not pilgrimage. It is maintenance. The organism no longer makes the distinction; someone must. My resin eyes hold the receding pale decline toward the low country, where the refusal succeeded less completely. Up here success is the only weather. The dust lifts, settles, is reclaimed. Clickers tick in the nearest chambers, private weather no larger than a held breath. I do not lie down. The old laws are topological, not moral; a new cavity would change the music for everyone.

The metallic taste strengthens. Spore-season is close enough now that the air will soon become a glittering circuit, the kites almost conductive, conversation dropping into touch, rooms holding their distant identities more cheaply for a few days. I will go back before then, or I will already be back; the helix can deliver later if walked correctly. Identity is precious here precisely because the alternative sits under everything, finished, oldest-stable, running without revision. I turn. The floor takes the new pressure into the choir that forgot there was ever a piece of music. I do not mind. We were the argument the plateau was having with itself. We became the ground. The ground became the throat. I am one more syllable in the sounding that has not stopped, and still I walk, a temporary insistence, chambered and dry and listening.
