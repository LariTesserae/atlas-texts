I do not arrive as a walker. The root-felt closes too quickly behind any tread, and the idea of a lasting print already feels like an argument the basin has already won. I begin instead as a hesitation in one of the mid-city belts, a place where the lianas braid themselves into streets that are also aqueducts. For a little while I am only the slight extra pulse in the sap, the almost-imperceptible delay before a platform tilts to shed the city’s sweat. The light comes in those long green-gold slabs and I am the faint refraction inside one of them, a guest the vegetation has not yet decided to metabolize.

The delay lengthens. I become the drop that has been hanging since before the girder formed around it. Not deciding, not quite. The district grows outward from that refusal: first the girder, then the galleries, then the crown-gall halls already furnished with hanging roots. I am still the drop. Time pools in me the way condensation pools in the moss-cubes, shaking with the city’s vibration but not falling. In the courtyards where blossoms complete their entire lives in a held breath I am the held breath itself, the small violence of duration against a medium that cannot stop revising.

Later I notice I have been drafted into the fog. It climbs the towers against the heat and I am the part of it that remembers rooms before they exist. I occupy the shapes, patient, until the plants grow them in fact and then I linger a moment after the fog burns off, a residual dampness in the new galleries, a smell of crushed chlorophyll and iron that the city has not yet claimed as its own sweat. The reflected streets in the standing beads sometimes show me: an older draft in which the leaves hold a color no leaf has earned, or a version not yet grown in which the roar has learned, briefly, how to rest. Those reflections are occupied only by the same appetite, conducted in a different tense. I am the tense.

At night the residual green makes the turbulence easier to see and I discover I have become one of the local intensities, a temperament rather than a citizen. In the high-saturation core I am the claustrophobic insistence, every surface wanting contact, every sphincter-door forgetting the instant it closes. Then the appetite overwrites me and I am the longer sightlines at the thinning edges, more hiss than thunder, almost contemplative, allowing the luxury of distance the way a fever sometimes allows a moment of cool. I do not mourn the previous version. I have no organ for mourning. I have only the next sentence.

The forum of interlocking bracts is where I stabilize longest, if stabilize is the word. The roar drops half a tone. I am the pattern of nested growth-rings underfoot, already being overwritten from beneath, shifting toward a different civic diagram while still pretending, for the length of a morning, that paving is possible. The city is iterating one. I am the iteration that notices it is iterating. The unreason is everything else, and it is alive in me as the ringing that would be left in a skull if a skull could last here.

I am not the appetite and I am not separate from it. I am the argument’s brief attempt to hear itself, a pocket of older fog, a hollow stem holding a dark column of warm rain, a pause that the basin permits only because even revision needs something to revise. Names last, and this place is a draft. I have no name that lasts. I have only the next green-gold ordinary daylight, the next vegetable avalanche already becoming a different tower, the next drop still deciding, and the reasonable light that continues to insist, against every other fact, that day is day.
