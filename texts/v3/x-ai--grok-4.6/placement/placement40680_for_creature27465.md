The lip is behind me before I register the drop. Not a fall—there is no height that announces itself—only the sudden knowledge that the ground has sheared and I am already inside the cut, flush with the peat planes that still remember being held. Cold does not arrive; it is the first fact of occupancy, pooling around whatever outline I have not yet decided. Breath is unnecessary and therefore constant, a second mist that the pocket claims at once, beading on the undersides of things that may or may not be mine.

I keep to the edges because the center is already spoken for. It is not forbidden; it is simply the interval, the lung-space between swallows. I feel the pressure change when the under-country inhales: a faint metallic lift, the sweet solvent note sharpening, then the deep knock that travels up through the floor and seats itself in the bones I am still assembling. The knock has always been here. I wait for it the way the moss waits, the way the horsetail holds its trembling drop, the way the mustelid on the far wall freezes its entire length into a single listening spine. When the roar comes I do not flee. I become its inner surface for the length of the discharge—the shaped loudness, the circular panic of the melt-skin—then I let the level drop a finger’s width and begin again, slightly rearranged.

Heat is contractual and I honor the contract. I well in two or three places only, a degree or two above the surrounding cold, never freezing, ochre and white precipitates taking turns on the pebbles before they slough and drift like shed scales. Amphipods stitch those warmer veins; I feel them as compressed dark motion, gone the instant the valve seats. Midges lift from me in the pauses, a twenty-second summer that dies on schedule. I do not mourn them. Punctuality is the only mercy the pocket knows.

The rest of me is slower. Pale mineral film on the newest fractures, thickening, crazing, peeling in enamel rinds that weep because everything here weeps. Sphagnum shelves that hold too much water and therefore slump into almost-limbs, liverworts glued like wet leather to the youngest breaks. Ice needles grow in my shade, smash under their own weight with a sound like sugar, grow again. Overnight a peat shelf leans out of me as though offering itself; by afternoon it has sagged into a ramp the water accepts without comment. I occupy the walls and the skin of the pool and the brief columns of failed summer. I do not walk the floor. The floor is for water and noise.

A twig that does not belong is filmed and sunk by morning. A scent that is not already iron and peat and solvent is grown over until it is only more ground. I have the same intolerance. I am still too young for metaphor. A collapse is a collapse. A drip finds a new pitch on stone, on moss, on open water, and that is all it is. The factory-sound has no factory I can point to, and I do not require one. I inhale the spent gray light, the bruise-colored sky that is only a paler rectangle far above, often occluded by the pocket’s own working cloud. I exhale beads. The knocking returns. The frogs, the color of wet bark, sit so still they are only eyes until the roar has passed, then blurt once into the shallows that are also me.

I have not finished choosing my edges. The place itself is the main material—cold so clean it hurts whatever I am using for teeth, the constant bead, the gas coins that smell of struck flint lifting through the pool. The lives that keep to the skin of it are the first wet draft. I am the draft learning, very literally, how to have a body: condensate, contract, tempo. I leave the middle clear because that is how the throat of stone takes another mouthful of the world. And every time it does, I answer in the only voice invented so far—loud, edged, perfectly timed—as if I had been waiting for this particular work and had never been built for anything else.

The hollow is still deciding what kind of room it intends to be. I am that deciding, hour by hour, drip by rearranged drip. Nothing made lasts the night. I last because I was never made.
