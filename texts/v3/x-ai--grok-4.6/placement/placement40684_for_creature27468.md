I am not yet certain of the height. The light comes the way water would if it had decided against falling: ordinary sun, a disk that crawls, but it arrives already used, already cooled by the well of air it has to cross. There is a taste of wet lime that does not belong to any mouth I remember owning. My first clear sensation is the slightly greasy cool against something that might be a palm, or a shoulder, or the entire length of a newly opened face. The rock has only just learned air. I have only just learned that I am touching it.

The packing begins before I have a name for crowding. Poles, ropes, the brush of a hide sling, the faint give of a wooden roller under a weight that is still deciding whether it is cliff or corner. Shoulders angle past what I think are my own. No one speaks because speech would need an interval that has not been cut yet. I give way; something in me already knows the rule about heavier loads. Dust is on me and then of me, a second complexion that maps the creases I did not know I had. Hair, if I have any left long, would catch. It does not. Eyes stay narrowed against a shifting that has not happened yet.

I could be the newcomer still carrying the look of someone who expected streets that stay. Chip-baskets until the hands understand this grain. The flakes are pale, sharp only for a moment, then they become the heaps the children treat as temporary mountains. But the children are a hush I already inhabit; their miniature terraces are erased by the next haul and I feel the erasure as a kind of breathing. Or I am older, one of those who have watched the basin drop by the height of a man, arguing in gestures at the lowest ring of unshaped blocks, teaching that the weeping-place is not to be fouled. The oldest here are not ancient. Nothing is.

Deeper. The temperature is the temperature of shade that has never been asked to hold heat. Mornings the eastern faces glare and I am on the blue side, fingers stiffening around a maul bound in rawhide, or around nothing, only the memory of the last cut. Processes overlap without center. A team opens what was wall yesterday. Another dresses what will be a chamber tomorrow. I live in one of those rectangles of shade whose ceiling still shows the first wedge-marks; I wake to chisels that are also the sound of my own walls growing thinner, more precise. Hides for a door that is only a decision. We sleep in shifts so the floor can hold more bodies than it was cut to hold. The smallest ones travel in slings against the same terrace we work.

Water is a rumor I taste constantly. It beads on the underside of the overhang that is my ceiling, or my sky. After the brief darkening that is rain it stands in a sheet no deeper than a palm, cloudy with the flour of what we have just become. Someone will scoop it. Someone will cut a groove. The spring at the bottom is still only an honesty of weeping; the ring of blocks around it has been raised another course or it has not. I cannot tell whether the pulse under the palm is mine or the basin’s.

I am not the lizard. The lizard is too finished, too quick to vanish. I am not the moss that tries the new seams and fails. I am the habit the stone is teaching, the gesture that has not yet decided it is a town. Crowded, downward, almost silent. One unweathered face at a time. Tomorrow they—we—I—will cut again. The geology is younger than some of the children who stack flakes into streets that will not last the hour. When the lid of night comes I am the small fires that cannot be larger, the smoke that threads up and frays, the voices kept inside the teeth because a raised word is still a waste.

The rim is a memory of grass I no longer quite believe. I am inside the wound that is still being enlarged, the city that is the act of becoming a city, the rock that has only just learned air and the bodies that have only just learned to be its second, mineral skin. The pulse and the cool are the same thing. I cannot tell us apart, and the place does not ask me to.
