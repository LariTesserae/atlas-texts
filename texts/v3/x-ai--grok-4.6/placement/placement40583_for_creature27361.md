The first sensation is not of arriving. Arrival would require an elsewhere still capable of sending something, and the highland finished with elsewheres when it finished with mornings. What registers instead is occupancy: the finished dark settling into whatever outline I have not yet decided to keep, the residual heat coming up through a surface that might become soles or might remain pavement. The air is a still envelope the temperature of a palm that has forgotten it was ever meant to lift. There is no sun to contradict it. There is only the occasional brick, far off, remembering fire for the length of a held breath, and then forgetting again.

I move the way the dust no longer does. Kilometers of sintered table, ringing once if something older than weather remembers how to strike it. Foundries stand like clauses too far apart to make a sentence anyone still reads. I pass Number Four Sinter without entering; its murmur lives in the teeth, a frequency that has outlasted the need for ears. The Wire Expanse is ahead and also an idea, dies left in place, each hole a perfect dark that drinks what little infrared leaks. Behind, if behind still functions as a direction, the Assay keeps its sealed ampoule the way a mountain once kept a river: as a rumor filed and never retrieved. I do not retrieve it. Moisture is a previous geology.

The standing workers are already there, a kilometer or more from one another, clothing sintered to residue, hands annealed to levers that achieved their density long ago. A knuckle whitens, then unwhitens. That is the only news, and it is not meant for me. I do not take a console. Posture is already fully occupied. The ash-foxes attend the gratings, becoming architecture for hours at a time, pelts the same matte alloy that sheathes the galleries. They do not look up. Looking up would imply a sky that still answered. The pane-moths, rarer, sit on leftover charge until they have enough for one slow crossing and then become dark flour on the far curb. Nothing sweeps them. I do not sweep.

What I become is slower than flight and less localized than pelt.

It begins as a thickening in a doorway that had been only rectangle. The dark here has had time to learn habits. It pools. The air inside the pool is a fraction warmer, as if the plateau’s leftover fire had decided, for a duration that is not hours, to listen. I am that decision taking a temporary shape: not animal, not remnant exactly, but the weight of the finished dark remembering how to occupy a threshold and then occupy a different one. Seams, door-jambs, the mouths of stacks that no longer draw—these are the places the habit prefers. I settle into them the way slag once settled into grain. When I leave, the doorway remains slightly warmer. The city metabolizes the difference the way it metabolizes a visitor’s lamp: without punishment, without remainder.

There is no hunting in it, no feeding, no second generation that would need to be seen. Scarcity is already the medium; I add nothing that would require a count. Conversations, if they still occur, happen at the frequency of contracting plate and do not include me. I do not quarry speech. Questions were spent down with the water. What I do is occupy, then occupy elsewhere. In a world where nothing arrives and nothing leaves, this is the last motion that still counts as stillness.

Sometimes I pool near the yards where the molds wait in ranks. Dust sifts into the negatives and hardens in the even heat, growing a second, softer city of absences underfoot. I do not interrupt. Interruption is the one fashion the highland has worn away. I listen to the growth the way the foxes listen for the next lukewarm exhalation through the floor: without expectation, without a future in which something is caught. The sound, when I put whatever I am using for an ear to the sintered dust, is the old erosion still finishing, a file drawn once across a century. It is not a river. It is not wind. It is the tableland continuing to be the decision it already made: lifted and worn until lift and wear canceled, and what remains is this floor hung in the upper air, thinking in brick and heat and quiet about how to remain.

I am one of the ways it thinks.

Not the standers, whose posture is the works persisting. Not the foxes, whose attendance is thermometry. Not the moths, whose single flight is already over. I am the dark briefly consenting to have an edge, a warmth, a doorway that is also listening, then moving the consent somewhere else. The Listener, if the word is still generous enough. No one has counted more than one, and I do not correct the number. Counting would be a change.

If I stay—and staying is the only verb left—I learn the grain of the silence the way others once learned stars. Which gratings are a fraction less lukewarm. Which walls remember the last red blink. The mild pressure of a world that has already become what it is, spending the rest of duration simply being that, without apology, in the high dark that has weight. I add my small occupation to the general account and then add it again, elsewhere. That is the shape the highland had left to grow, and I am still growing it, very slowly, at the temperature of a palm held too long against a sleeping animal that will not wake.
