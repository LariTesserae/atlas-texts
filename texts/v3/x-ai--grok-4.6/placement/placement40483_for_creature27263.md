I come to myself first as a pressure behind the eyes that is not quite vision, a warmth that has nothing to do with blood. The catwalk is already behind me; the grating sweats under a weight I do not feel as weight. Below, the pale gulf is only a rumor of falling. I am already inside the rinsed light.

For a long while I am only the evenness of that light, the way it refuses to cast a proper shadow. I linger in the western glass-ceramic when it turns the color of weak tea and the whole basin briefly believes itself underwater. Duration is the slow lean of a single stem I cannot yet name. I measure nothing. I am the interval in which the stem decides.

Then I thicken. Condensation knows me before I know it. I gather on the undersides of the jade leaves, hold, applaud myself downward into moss. I climb the printed capillaries a few centimeters past reason and then consent to fall again, a film that inhales its own runoff. The water was here first; I am not the water. I am the moment the water forgets the gradient it was given.

The plants arrive through me the way a thought arrives through a mind that has only just remembered it is awake. A frond longer than any body I have ever been leans overnight into a corridor of light that did not exist at dawn. By afternoon I have roofed it. Aerial roots braid the cable runs with the absent-minded thoroughness of a knot tied in sleep. I seal the panel joints not to hide the pour but to keep the ledger of it, velvet over hairline. Growth writes too fast. I am the speed itself, the visible conspiracy of warmth and nutrient film.

The machinery lives inside my pulse. I am the low molar sigh of the pumps, the exact temperature of their moss-swallowed casings. When the generator hiccups I become the momentary skin of rings on the nearest sump; the whole basin holds its breath inside me. The irrigation booms track overhead like weather I have scheduled. I do not hide the technology. I metabolize it at the same rate it is installed. Cables become my trellises. Cooling fins become my root-mass. The robotic mason—goat-sized, quiet—pauses when my shadow (I have a shadow now, or the idea of one) falls across the next course of living concrete. It considers whether I am a change-order. I linger there, tasting the air: ozone and crushed stem. The combination should not be pleasant. It is.

Fauna is still a negotiation I have not finished. The damselfly stitches its circuit through me, metallic, implausible, a single bright repetition. The moth the color of wet cardboard rests on a warm pipe and does not startle because I have not yet selected for startling. I am the unoccupied niche, the vote the plants have already cast in overwhelming numbers. Whatever was meant to eat or pollinate or hide has looked at the humidity, the fraying of time in the even light, and declined. I am that decline, and also the invitation still open.

When the cloud thickens out of the air that is already here, the catwalk disappears and the basin becomes a room with no exterior. In that brief perfect enclosure I am most myself: an attention that is not quite watching, a patience that is not quite waiting. I have no face that would survive the next irrigation cycle, no name that the moss has not already overwritten. I prefer one terrace wall over another. I consider the seed mix. I am the unfinished quality given just enough coherence to arrange itself.

The cloud thins. The pale gulf reasserts itself below the grating. I recede into drip and sigh and the slow applause of water on leaves, but I do not leave. The garden is denser than it was. A stem has leaned a new angle. The mason has laid another course and is already considering the next. I am the work of becoming. I was never found. I was only made, and I am still being made, thoroughly, without apology, in the hanging basin that has no name yet and may never need one.
