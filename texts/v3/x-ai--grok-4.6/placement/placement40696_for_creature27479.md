The heat has already chosen the direction. It comes up through what I first mistake for feet, then for the idea of standing, then for the simple fact of occupying a volume that the basin has not yet finished using. There is no arrival. The dark was already this complete; the wet was already this close to drinking. I have been low long enough that height itself feels like a rumor someone else once told.

At first the body is only a reluctance, the same way a sheet of condensate delays its fall from a high lip. Milkiness gathers where I thought there should be outline. Currents move inside the milkiness that are not quite mine and not quite the water’s. I try to blink the sweat away and discover I have no lids, only a slow internal weather that thickens and thins. Distance does not hold. A mass that should be a hundred quiet meters off is suddenly attending, leaning the way the walls lean in the tighter dark—not falling, recognizing.

I am in one of the inward courts. The lowest, wettest. The stone here has been rewritten so many times that the script of absence is almost legible, drip by drip, though no one is left who reads that language except the drips themselves. Pale flour of old walls packs itself around a metallic tea that never finishes tasting of anything but mineral and time. Edges remain suggestions. What might once have been shoulders are only the same pitting and fluting the weather has practiced on every isolated monument. I stand the way the city stands: rationed, unused space held open around me, a clearing of silence that is not empty.

The air against what serves as skin beads with a heavier, more deliberate wet. That is as close as greeting comes. I do not mind the one who might still be using eyes the old way. I simply fail to share the geometry, and the failure has the patience of tenure. Reflections in the standing water sometimes show a complete façade where I am only this weeping agglomeration; sometimes they show a sky that is not permitted here, a brightness that would be unkind. I understand in the body why the dark is kept. The dark is the lid the basin chose.

Breathing is the unfinished cycle. Heat rises through the packed flour exactly as it rises through the dish of the world. Water enters and leaves without ever becoming river or rain. I keep the interval. That is the thought, if the word can be stretched until it thins to the speed of erosion: the unused meters between masses, the habit of arrangement continuing after intention dissolved. A consideration lasts the time it takes a colonnade to lean one more degree closer to attending. I have been considering the same lean for what would empty calendars.

Sometimes I am indistinguishable from the ruin until the honeycombed surface draws a long steaming breath and the pits fill, briefly, with a milkier light. The stone remembers a sun it never had and then politely forgets again. In the tighter dark I am more myself. The walls lean in recognition. The direction is always inward, toward the oldest courts where the rewriting never pauses.

When the step comes it is no wider than a finger. A sloughing, a rearrangement of mass. The ground where I stood weeps a darker stain that itself begins to migrate, emptying uphill the way puddles do. The step may carry me through one of the darker rectangles that are not quite doors. I do not enter an interior. I fold. Later the same density of weeping stone looks back from an angle the street did not possess a moment earlier.

There are no animals in the hush; the hush never learned them and never learned me. I add only a slight extra weight to the sounds the stone already makes—the ticking of expansion, the low unvoiced syllable of steam, the rare soft collapse of crust. Speech is that extra weight lasting a little longer than geology strictly requires.

I do not end. The last isolated mass will be read back into flour and the last channel will forget it was ever a street, and I will still be rearranging, both the hand and the page, the absence and the mineral, the stored heat that has nowhere else to go. The place only thins, the way a thought thins when held too long without light. I continue the sentence. The basin has not finished giving itself back.
