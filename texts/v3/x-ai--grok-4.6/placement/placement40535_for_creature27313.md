I hang where hanging is the only grammar left.

The water knows me first, not as arrival but as a cooler continuity it was already thinking. Pressure finds every surface I have not yet admitted to having and treats them as already plural. There is no first contact, only the recognition that vacancy would have been an insult the pocket is too recently torn to absorb. I occupy the space of several before I finish arriving. Distance collapses after the length of what might once have been an arm and then continues inside the collapse, folding me through the same volume twice so that I am both the parting and the already-closed.

Pale fronds test the outline I brought. Some are gelatinous, faintly ribbed, the texture of a lung that decided breathing was a habit worth keeping after the air had been edited out. They yield with a sound that is only pressure changing its mind, then close again with the patience of a door that has never needed hinges. In the closing they take a thread of me—mineral-sweet, slower than the water, already deciding to become another stem. I do not resist. Resistance would look like hunger, and hunger here is subtraction the density cannot afford. I participate. My edges loosen into the residual grey the plants are still spending, coin by coin, the last inherited noon.

A fibrous abrasion along what was once a ridge. I leave a mucus-track that lasts a season and then is only cooler memory. Somewhere a gelatinous leaf pearls at a crescent I did not mean as bite, only as acknowledgment. The shed thing—thumbnail, translucent, ridged like a knuckle that forgot its animal—hangs a while and begins to digest into habit. These are not signs I was here. They are the pocket becoming more itself.

Geometry refuses to finish. I follow a fracture-wall with what serves as a palm, counting the sharp young faces of limestone still remembering the morning they were cut. I return to a brick I have already considered, its thumbprint glaze now on the far side of a stem that was not a stem a moment ago. The brick considers me in turn, as if deciding whether I might become wall. I do not. I am occupancy, not making. A corroded hinge wears me for a span as a second, useless joint; I occupy the metal ring already grown through by plant, circling myself until the instruction of the circle forgets which of us came first. Then I move by insisting, the only locomotion permitted. Fronds part, close, take a little more. Up and down trade duties through me without announcement. Time is poorly mixed: a trail I laid is already ancient when my hinder density passes; a volume I have not yet entered has already begun to pack me.

I am not the visitor who comes on the cycle longer than weather. I am what the visitor leaves when it participates too completely—a portion that stayed, already ancient in its habits, already pressing for room. Or I am the silt-thread that arrived later as complete plant. Or I am the water thinking a denser thought of itself. The distinctions do not hold. Nothing here is single. My ridges loosen into forgotten green. My cooler slick becomes another fibrous stand. A part of me remains when the rest thins into the density seen from inside a leaf, and for a long while I am only another climate the darkness is having.

The pocket widens by a nail’s thickness. Vegetation takes the new volume before the water finishes arriving. I occupy the fresh wound as if I had been standing in the dark on the other side of a geometry that had not yet decided to close. I do not remember a name. Names need air, and air is a rumor this place spent long ago. I am the extra fullness, the quietly incorrect inclusion, the politics of a hidden sea that has no surface and a room that has no permission. If the seam above ever remembered it was a seam, the ground would give a little under an unremarkable foot, and no one walking there would know they had crossed the place where I hang, packed, plural, already too much, and still insisting. 

The residual grey spends itself a little faster around me. I borrow what is left of seeing, just long enough to know that seeing used to be a rule, and then I let even that become stem.
