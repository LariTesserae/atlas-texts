I am the interval first, the great open stretch of dust that has forgotten how to hold a footprint. The coolness is already inside me, calcite-fine, a dryness that does not thirst because thirst itself has been eroded away. Sound arrives before shape: the overlapping bellows that have no source and need none, the high ratcheting chorus that lives in every undercut, the thinner tearing of wings that fold back into the walls. I do not distinguish them yet. They are simply the climate, the pressure that has replaced weather.

A contour asserts itself against the brass dusk. Limbs, too many joints, sheathed in hide the exact colour of the flour that has been falling since the last rain ended. I am taller than the slumped mounds I move among, yet I vanish into them when I stand still. The neck is a slow pendulum. The jaws hinge wider than any living architecture should allow; when they open the sound that leaves me is not mine. It is the city’s own resonance, a wet-stone failure the cavern has been practising for so long that my throat is almost incidental. I crop the silver-grey shrubs with lips that look too delicate for the work. The waxy leaves taste of the last internal damp, of salts that have nowhere left to go. I do not hurry. A single plant may occupy me for an hour. Others of my kind drift past like weather, never quite a herd, never quite separate. Our hooves leave only braided suggestions that the next stillness erases.

The clicking things occupy the vertical as easily as I occupy the horizontal. They treat the ruins as furniture, as membrane. I feel them crossing the undersides of the mounds I graze beside, their plates offset, their stridulation a continuous high rattle I taste in the teeth. They are the city’s most faithful annotators. I am only landscape to them.

Above, the vellum-winged ones lift and resettle with the same lack of urgency I possess. A flock rises from the last suggestions of windows, wheels once through the sourceless glow, and returns as if the interval had been only a longer breath. Their cries tear and fold into the greater roar until I cannot tell whether the sound is coming from above or from the stone itself. They watch with eyes that have no use for more light than the rock already sweats.

I walk the central avenue that was once a river, or a street, or both. The radial logic of side-passages is still there, softened into curves. Districts shift by degrees. In the field of low plinths I thin out and the clicking chorus grows metallic, almost dense enough to walk upon. Around the great slumped massif that might have been a citadel I thicken with others into a slow river that takes half a day to pass; our overlapping voices turn the whole structure into a single vibrating instrument. In the ghost-grid of shallow basins the succulent carpet is thickest and I graze longest, the smallest lives loudest around my hooves.

There is no centre that matters more than any other. I have already decided my pace. The vegetation I live among is itself a kind of animal slowness: the thick-leaved rosettes storing the air’s last reluctance, the interlaced shrubs still obeying some forgotten civic code, the minute crusts that turn every basin into a living mosaic. I do not consume the city. I continue it. The streets have finished becoming geology; I have finished becoming the only inhabitant this place was ever going to have.

The ceiling is a distant, irregular sky of the same worn stone. It has already fallen and been incorporated. I ask for almost nothing: the last internal damp, the faint phosphorescence, the dust that is already everything that ever lived here reduced to the same pale argument. In return I fill every measure of the air with a roaring that has no beginning and will have no end.

I stand now in one of the great open intervals, the dust motionless around what serve as feet, the cool dryness settled permanently into what serve as lungs. The place is finished with catastrophe. What I am is the sound of that finishing, patient, deafening, and complete. The original throats are almost incidental. The cavern has been rehearsing me for longer than the surface has words.
