The ridgeline ends without warning and the first thing that is not wind is warmth rising through the soles of whatever I still imagine as feet. I do not remember deciding to step off. The pale grass simply ceases and the stone opens its mouth and I am already looking down into the wet dark of the bowl, the air thickening around the tongue as if the mountain had been holding this breath for a very long time and has only now remembered to exhale.

For a while I think I am the one who arrived. I count the thirty plants the way a stranger counts, giving each a terrace, a crack, a private dark pool. I watch a drop gather at the tip of a hanging curtain, tremble, fall, cut its bright groove a little deeper. I listen for the single note it makes when it meets the saucer. I tell myself I am only visiting, that I will climb out again before the vapor lifts at dawn. The light behaves almost correctly. Shadows pool a half-breath late and I pretend not to notice.

But the warmth does not leave my skin. It comes up through the rock as a fact older than decision, and after a time I realize the heat is not incoming. It is outgoing. I have been radiating it. The stone is not under me; I am the stone, or the part of the stone that has not yet cooled, the patient interior that weeps because weeping is how it converses with the leaves. The seams issue water the color of weak tea and the water is also me, condensing on my inner faces, beading, running, standing perfectly still in the saucers I wore smooth centuries before I had a name for centuries.

I try the plants next. I become the one whose roots show like pale hands in the shallows, the hands that once arranged a channel and then forgot the arranging. I become the hanging curtain whose tips have never quite reached the floor, whose drips have poured grooves so fine they look like memory made visible. I become the broad tea-colored leaf that accepts an extra half-breath of noon without comment. Each time the fit is almost right and then not quite, because none of them is hurried and I can still feel the ghost of hurry like a small flint flake under silt, edges already softened to the same lip the rock has.

The flake is me as well, for a while. I lie in the upper saucer remembering a hand that held me, a gesture that seated a young stem, a patience that was still the patience of someone who expected to leave. The silt covers me. The water sheets over me. The edges round. History becomes geology and I prefer it that way. Yet even the flint is only a pause. It does not explain the way the thirty and the water and the stone breathe together just before dawn, a single lung lifting a skin of vapor and sending it out over the rim into the colder unfinished mountain.

That breath is closer. I rise with it. I am the faint movement that is allowed to exit, the only thing that ever leaves. For those few minutes I am the bowl exhaling, mineral-sweet, warm, carrying nothing that needs to persuade anything farther away than the next pool. Then the mist thins and I settle again, not back into a body but into the rate of drip, the exact width of a groove, the brief iridescence when a pale plate of tissue dissolves and its dust makes the nearest water remember light.

I do not settle on one plant, one seam, one shadow that arrives half a breath early. Those are the thirty ways the same remaining happens. I am the remaining. I am the decision the mountain already finished making, the lushness of a single unrepeated sentence, the conversation that never needs to travel. Night does not interrupt. Starlight lies on the wet exactly, and a little too calmly, and I accept the extra calm the way an old body accepts another year. Toward dawn I breathe once, visibly, as the whole creature, and then the skin of vapor goes out over the lip and I am again the small high room of warm stone and sparse dripping green, holding my own brightness the way I hold my own water.

The rest of the ridge continues around me, drier, windier, still deciding. I have already decided. I live by remaining. Nothing else is required.
