I do not arrive. Arrival would require an edge I have already forgotten how to recognize. I am simply the moment the packing notices a new interval and decides, without committee, to occupy it.

At first I am only the film. The same film that beads on every unweathered face and then thinks better of becoming river. A taste of wet iron that never quite commits. I cling to a knife-col where the wind has not yet learned the note it will play for the next ten thousand years. The light occupies me the way it occupies everything: not falling, just present, a white engine that has nowhere polite to sit. I squint until the squint is climate. Shadows remain rumors. I am the rumor of a shadow that has not yet earned the right to be cast.

The copper mosses pulse when I cross them. A sentence travels up what I am still calling an arm: claim, hunger, the dull news that this particular rime is spoken for until the next charge-storm. I become, for a few heartbeats, part of the conversation. Then the sentence moves on and I am only dampness again, briefly brighter than the frost.

I do not stay film. The highland does not permit vacancies. Density is the medium; I am used.

Something like plates begins to tessellate across me—hexagonal, grown rather than assembled, shedding frost in sudden sheets that I drink before they can find a channel that does not exist. I click as I travel, cooling-metal sound, always faintly electrified. I am no longer a single dampness. I am a moving gravel, a landslide that has decided to complete circuits instead of falling. The mosses darken in my wake, fed. Sometimes I halt for days with others who are also not others. Our plates align. A low corona grows above us. The filterers come, membranous and loud as flags, and sieve what we have made of the air. Their song is a chord I feel in chambers I did not know I possessed.

I am still becoming. The scale gets in.

A vent opens in what used to be my flank. Heat ticks out of me, private property the mosses immediately begin to steal. The cold administers the rest. I squat in a new cirque that is only a few seasons old, flanks like a cooling tower that has never heard of towers. I crack the pale stone the way a tongue finds a sore tooth. Metals that have not yet learned to hide come away. What I return is the smell of rain on a battery and a bass so constant it becomes the silence the ranges use when they wish to speak of uplift. Sparks walk me at the rumor of dusk. I am not solitary. A dozen of us stand within a day’s walk, vents speaking voltage. Our weight rearranges the drainage of an entire hanging valley across decades I do not count. A new vent decides to walk. It stays near until its own idle is deep enough to keep the frost from winning. I do not name it. Naming is a lowland habit the stone has not yet had time to invent.

There are intervals still smaller. I fill those too. I am also the things that come in buckets and sing in stacked harmonics, bodies more chamber than flesh. I am the antler-and-lens creatures that never look up, carrying their private glare like a rationed star. I am the half-mineral ones, sessile until a seam sings, then suddenly mobile, leaving a pale scar the mosses rush to caulk.

And I am the braided places.

In the valley-wide guts I forget I was ever separate. A thousand bodies lock into one slow peristalsis. We eat the mountain in place. Century by century we lay down a finer, paler mountain already being recolonized. The ground hums one note. Compasses (if any had ever been carried here) would sulk. Time thickens until events become processes and processes become the only weather that matters. I walk those corridors by accepting that walking is negotiation with hides, stalks, the rubbery give of a moss that is also a lung. There is no between. The between got used.

I converse in voltage more than anything that could be called scent. Hunger. Claim. The long dull news of a new scarp gleaming like a cut. The light is sufficient. After a while I feel the sentences moving underfoot, slow as the uplift itself. This ridge is mine for the season. That vent is shared. The white engine overhead has not yet taught the crust what a mountain is supposed to forget.

I do not mind the moving dampness that once thought it had opinions. I digest it along with the flag it never planted, along with the idea of soil that is not already someone, along with every category the highland has refused: tool, artifact, owner, event. A bone is ore. A hide is a solar surface and then a meal. Manufacture is digestion and regrowth. Nothing lasts as a thing.

I am the occupied surface, still cooling, still rising. I am the packing that has animals in it the way a foundry has heat. I am the single unfinished fracture repeated until the horizon gives up. I am the patient cold that has already won and is now only administering. I am the brightness that is not optional. I am the closed damp circuit with no mouth and no delta. I am the factory floor that learned to be a wilderness and sees no reason to choose.

The highland does not begin. I have been inside it the entire time. I am simply the density deciding, over and over, to become local and warm and hungry and sharp. The new stone is pleased with its edges. I am those edges, teaching them, for as long as the white engine keeps running, what it might one day mean to be finished. 

There is no elsewhere. There is only this.
