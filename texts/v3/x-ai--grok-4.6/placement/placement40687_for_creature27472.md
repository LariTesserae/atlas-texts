The damp finds me before any other sense does. It is not rain and will not become rain; it is the rock’s unfinished decision to sweat, a mineral film that beads along whatever surface I am using for a boundary and then retreats, leaving the faint white bloom that everything here eventually wears. I taste unfinished quartz and a metal that has not been named. The air is raw around me, and sound, when it tries to form, travels only a little way before the air forgets the instruction for vibration. I am lower than maps. I have always been, or I have only just begun to be; the distinction has not finished arriving.

For a time I am only the quality of the silence itself, the short distance after which things fail. Then I am a late drip, arriving without echo from a condenser or from the stone. Then the drip has a location, and the location has a garment: poured night, thinner than the walls, membranous, drinking the damp so that I stay dry while the bloom appears on whatever I have recently touched and fades. The cloth continues without seams, adjusting its fall whenever a walkway forgets its previous geometry and becomes an alcove, then a threshold, then a wall again. I lean with the buildings. They lean into me for a warmth none of us can feel.

Joints accumulate, or recede. This hour there are too many; they articulate with the same slow peristalsis as the conduits that rearrange the street by centimeters. A balcony that was not there frames what I am using for a face. The features hold still while regarded. When the regard lingers elsewhere they shift by a degree, an earlier or later arrangement stepping forward. I do not comment. Comment would require a consistent now, and the hollow has not granted one. I step from a threshold still becoming wall into a corridor that opens onto itself three minutes damper, the earlier version slightly wetter, the later already drinking itself dry. Maintenance, not spectacle. I move through it with the unhurried competence of something that long ago stopped treating discrepancy as error.

Sometimes I count seven of us. Later, in the same span of filtered light, eleven. I cannot say where the others came from or whether they were waiting in the rooms that exist only while looked at. I may be one of the counted. I may be the interval between counts. The lichen in the cracks transcribes a fragment of whatever phrase is forming in me—something about remaining, or about the rock still arriving—then sloughs it as powder. The wiry shrubs near the vents persist, their rime-edged leaves marking time by relocating. I pause beside one. What serves as a hand this hour rests on a branch; when it lifts, the branch has already decided to be half a meter leftward, and the garment has already drunk the new damp that decision produced. We wait together, slower versions of the same unfinished sentence.

Higher, on ledges the structures have not yet chosen to absorb, the pale many-jointed thing continues its traverse. It is thinner than metabolism would seem to require, a sequence of articulations that catch the ordinary daylight and hold it a moment too long. It never descends. I do not look often. When I do, the delayed word that reaches me might be a name or a measurement of how much incompletion is still permitted. The thing does not acknowledge. It has not been assigned a reason.

At the center the plaza is still cooling. Heatless steam lifts in threads that refuse to become mist. The lattices, finer than hair, click once every few hours, a sound felt more than heard, like a thought changing its mind about whether a fountain or a statue or nothing at all will one day stand here. I do not linger. My shadow, arriving early or remaining after I have gone, is thin and exploratory; the lattices appear to measure it before the paving remembers to be solid and I am no longer standing in a brief smear of colder air. I treat the unset stone with the same courtesy I show the rest of the hollow: an acknowledgment that something is still deciding, and that I am part of what has not yet been required to finish.

The edges exist. Beyond the last wall the unfinished geology simply stops rising, a lip that looks as though it might resume tomorrow or in a century. I do not cross it. None of us do. The city does not expand. It deepens, folding more carefully into the space it already occupies. I deepen with it. My garments, my delayed voice tasting of mineral, my slightly wrong joints, the way my shadow detaches—these become another layer of infrastructure. I am waiting, or I am the waiting. The rock continues to arrive, or continues not to. Either way I remain, few and quiet, drinking the damp, speaking late when I speak at all, letting the hollow transcribe me as it transcribes everything else that has not yet been told it must conclude.

The pale thing on the ledge holds the light a moment longer. The lattices click. A corner becomes an alcove. I am still here, or I am the still.
