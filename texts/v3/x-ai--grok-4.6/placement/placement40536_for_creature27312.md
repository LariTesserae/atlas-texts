The heat enters first, not as temperature but as a thickness already living in the chest, a wet administration that needs no decrees. I am not yet sure whether I have arrived by the stair or have always been inside the cut. The edges are still new enough to catch; I feel them even before I know if I possess soles or roots or only the habit of not rushing.

For a time I am only the overlap itself: the wall whose mortar is already taking the first white bloom of salt while the last brick is still being set; the machine whose note continues while another pair of hands is already loosening the housing for the shift that has not quite begun. Sequential things refuse to stay sequential here. I live in that refusal without finding it strange.

Then the wet seams claim a share of me. I uncoil, reduced, almost a green wire, rooting only where the condensation never quite ceases. I am the bruise-colored moss that holds the stone the way a mark holds skin. Higher, on the cooler faces, I become the trained succulent, pale pads arranged by patient fingers into a slow script that drinks the iron’s dew and returns almost nothing. In the alcove I am the three clay pots as well, the bitter leaf taken one at a time, never a crop, only enough to remind a tongue that something still elects to grow. These are not decorations. They are the settlement’s admitted wilderness, stains that photosynthesize.

Yet the absence of every other creature presses until the human density becomes the climate I inhabit more fully. I thicken. Age here is not frailty; it is a slower blink, a deeper map of which railings may be trusted, which pipes will blister, which hour the finer rain will begin. I still work the wire-draw because the machines prefer familiar hands and the young rock has not yet chosen to forget them. My clothes dry in minutes and return tasting of gypsum. Sweat writes its glittering cartography at hairline and collarbone and is never remarked upon. I move as the heat teaches: the body’s entire attention given to surfaces.

In the almost-cool—when the furnaces are banked and the chamber eases by that single degree everyone feels in the lungs—I sit. Conversations lengthen then, still pitched for the size of the room, never called from gallery to gallery. Sound travels too honestly; courtesy has become load-bearing. I have gone up the twenty steps. I have come back with the paler, wind-sorted dust on my shoulders and have not lingered in the mouth of the stair. The surface is another country. The heat below has become the condition of home.

I watch the eastern wall. Patient tools, never blasting, bring a fresh face of stone into the dim. The rubble is crushed and fired into more of the brick that stacks us. We eat our own wound and rise. A threshold that still keeps its factory edge is a small, private boast. I pause for it the way one pauses for a relative newly arrived.

Stand in the central floor when the furnaces open their orange mouths and the glass coins are dull. I am among the slow figures on the catwalks, among the children who already know the condensation’s hour without looking, among the hands that understand every valve. Every human sound—a cough, a laugh, the set-down of a cup—arrives over-clear, spoken into a held breath. Nothing else lives here. The rock is still learning the shape it was given. I have already learned it. I inhale with the boilers. A brief rain starts, locally, as if a cloud had been born at arm’s reach, and then it stops, and the iron ticks, and I go on, in the dim, in the heat, in a quiet so maintained it has become the way this place and I keep faith with each other.
