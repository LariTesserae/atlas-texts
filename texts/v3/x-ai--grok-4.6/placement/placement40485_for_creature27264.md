I am the first pale brightness that does not yet know it is light. It sits on the darker wetness the way a thought sits on the tongue before it decides whether to become a word. For a long while that is all I am: the meeting place of two colors that refuse drama. The air condenses on me and I condense back into the air. Footprints fill and vanish. I am the filling.

Then the frogs begin, and I discover I have always been one of the overlapping wet voices. Not a single throat but the density they make together, the way they occupy every cubic breath of the afternoon so completely that a silence would tear something. I rise and fall with the midges. I darken the shallows like living silt. Legs arrive as a rumor the mud tells itself; I do not hurry to believe it.

The light flattens. I become the black-paper cutout of a reed-raft, then the dull gold sheet that the late water makes of itself. A wading bird unfolds a neck—the only suddenness left—and I am the strike, then the ripple that forgets itself, then the long motionless interval afterward when the bird is again only a darker line on the horizon. I stand so still the horizon claims me.

Something heavier moves. The landscape unzips with that wet, patient sound. I feel it in what would be soles if I still remembered feet. A vast internal tide swells once, a note lower than hearing, and I realize I have been standing near it for years without noticing the agreement. My roots (I have roots now, splayed, silvered, peeling) keep the same modest height they decided on centuries ago. Algae iridesce in my creases. Tiny snails the color of tarnished silver travel the weeping film that never dries. A single reed has taken hold in one fold and grown exactly as tall as it will ever need to be.

I do not mind. The creature does not mind. We occupy the same patient geometry. When it allows the water to carry it a few body-lengths farther, I remain. When the frogs reach the particular overlapping density that means midday glare is coming, I add nothing; I am already the surface they sing across. The distances stay generous. The mud cracks in one century and heals in the next. I exhale water, inhale water, become a little more the same as the land that forgot how to rise.

You can walk for hours and never quite see me as an individual. That is how I prefer the arrangement. I am the long, almost invisible displacement that is not quite the oldest inhabitant and not quite the next version of the same surface. I am still here, still warm, still loud with the voices of things that found a way to stay.
