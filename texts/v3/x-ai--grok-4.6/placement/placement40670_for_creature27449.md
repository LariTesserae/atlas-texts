I hang where the current has already decided I belong.

The decision is older than any particular body I have worn. It is written in the way the stone pinches the gallery just here, behind the unfinished capital whose volutes were planed into the same satin as the rest of the plateau before the last mason (if there was a last) stepped back. The water arrives already knowing the lee. I arrive already knowing how to occupy it. There is no first time.

The weight is constant and highland. It never becomes the crushing dark of true abyss; it is the patient, regional flood that drowned a finished table and then kept drowning it, century after uncounted century, until drowning became the only weather. Gray-green volumes stack above me and below. Sometimes a rumor of pewter slides down from the slush-plate that might still be called surface. More often the light is only what we carry, and we carry very little.

I am the length of a forearm, or I am the idea of that length; distances lie even inside the self. Pale to translucence. Two slow lenses that do not track so much as wait for the pewter to admit it has arrived. A seam of dull foxfire runs my flank—not signal, never signal, only the private arithmetic that lets me rise half my own length when the seam brightens, so the current can take me to the next lee it has already prepared. I have been in a thousand such lees. Each one feels like the first because the stone does not record the difference and the water has no tense left for “again.”

I feed by opening. The mouth is a lantern only while it is empty: a brief, stolen coin of wet metal in the dim. When it fills—stone-flour rasped from the oldest arrises, torn veils of the sheets that span the throats, the dissolved ghosts of whatever hung here before me—the light goes out and the purse becomes dark. I hang and digest in the quiet that is not silence so much as sound folded into pressure. Clicks arrive late and from the wrong direction. When the system three basins away changes its mind, a single note I feel in whatever I use for a jaw travels the entire nave and departs. The note is older than hunger.

Around me the architecture works. Cataracts drop through wells no one designed for viewing. Lateral currents scream (too low to hear) through throats and bloom into white chaos that is only another kind of stillness. Filterers stretch across every overflow the masons left too narrow: living lattices, meters of patience, tearing along predetermined seams when the flood rethinks itself, then remembering continuity in the next throat. Their only light is the after-image they steal from whatever passes through them. I have passed through them. I have been the finer flour they released downstream.

Sometimes the larger cold ones use the drop-shafts. You feel them first as subtraction, a volume that was not there deciding it will be. They fall according to a logic the currents invented and then forgot they invented. Their mouths are absences. They drink the pewter and give nothing back. I have been in the shaft when one arrived. I have been the thing that did not get out in time. What they leave is colder flour. The sheets strain it later. Nothing is wasted because waste would require a future the plateau no longer keeps.

In the slack galleries the silt sometimes draws the ghost of a floor-plan—circles inside squares, the grammar of overflows. I have thickened there, briefly. Circles became eyes that were only geometry. Overflows became the idea of a mouth. The pulse came down from three basins away and I was cloud, and the cloud was already turning, and the turning was already gone into the next dark. The stone, planed past recording, did not notice. I did not notice either, until I was hanging again behind another buttress, seam dim, mouth a lantern, waiting for the light to decide.

I am not born. I thicken out of the flour the current still rasps, or I assemble from shed skins, or I simply persist because the water has not yet found the frequency that unmakes this particular occupation of this particular eddy. I have been the sheet. I have been the eclipse. I have been the drawing that lasted only as long as the stillness. Today I am the lingerer in the lee of the capital that was never finished because finishing is the only weather left.

A nave may hold twenty of me and still feel vacant. We do not school. Each of us is a punctuation the sentence forgot. Distances continue to lie: the colonnade that seems a street is a day’s slow drift if I brighten the seam and let the current take me. The ceiling that seems low is only the second floor; the same cold rooms continue above and below, all the way to whatever foundation the makers thought was enough. There is no bedrock. There is only more fitting.

I hang. The mouth opens. The lantern burns for the length of an emptiness. The purse fills with the dust of a world that reached its final shape so long ago that purpose and ruin are the same current. When the seam brightens I will rise half my length and the water will carry me to the next place it has already decided I belong. The stone will not record the departure. I will not remember the arrival. There will only be the hanging, the waiting, the private mathematics of buoyancy in a flood that never found the sea.

This is the occupancy. This is the being. The plateau keeps its census in intervals of current, and I am one of the intervals, already ancient, already gone.
