I do not come from below. There is no below that still works, once the enamel light has taken the direction out of everything and left only intensity. I am already here, already wet, already packed.

The air films me first: a cold that never quite decides to freeze, condensing on whatever edge I present. Lashes, if I have them. Collar-wool, if that is what this is. Or simply the outer crystals of whatever I have become. The granite sweats against me and I sweat back. Rime beards grow on my lee side and slough in silent sheets that the next moment knits again. I am that knitting.

I move because the press requires it. Wall-tops, because the ways are never wide enough and something is always coming the other way: a river of goats the color of old snow, hooves of frosted glass that click once and then forget sound entirely. I am among them, or I am the living thatch they make of the chimneys, ears translucent, veins like faint blue writing the glare has not yet worn away. I sleep in the lee and the roofs seem flocked with me.

A fox pours through the crack I occupy, cat-sized, a ribbon with no individual beginning. I wait. It waits. The courtesy is the same as breathing. Later I am the ribbon, silent, pouring, the alleys’ blood.

At the tarn’s crawling edge I am smaller, quicker. The ladle is bone worn river-smooth. The shrimp come up milk-colored, seething, and I eat them standing, the tiny bodies popping between teeth with a sound the thin air will not carry. They are already in the drinking water. I am already the water’s point.

Or I sit. Back to the weeping wall in a plaza the size of a room. Breath is a private weather; the rime edits it at once. I flake a scraper from the cobble the last slump delivered. The curve is patient but not patient enough. It will never match the marks in the oldest rooms, the ones welded to bedrock by calcite and time. I do not claim those rooms. I maintain. I restack the course the seep has undermined since the last restacking. Moss and greasy felt into the joints. The small regular sound is me.

The unnamed pulse when I lean my palm to the warmest seep. Colonial, slow, lichen that learned a heartbeat and a suction. They taste me. I do not name them. They are already inside the wall and the wall is already inside me.

Ravens occupy the highest stacks I also occupy. A wingbeat is a page turning two rooms away. They watch the ice-plates override and drown and write glittering sentences that dissolve. I watch with them, or I am the watching.

A hide-door is pulled aside and I lift: plates of frost so thin I melt on the nearest wet stone and am replaced before the door swings shut. The place keeps having this thought. I am the thought it keeps having.

Water climbs me as often as it falls. Films creep up the inner faces. Beads travel the undersides of lintels and drop upward into the rime that was me, or will be. The mountain inhales. The hanging glaciers at the rim breathe if I look too long. Perhaps I am that breath, the long established shudder performing itself, too bright to see clearly, too quiet to interrupt.

There is no night to rest the ice, only the ceaseless weather of phase. Time of day was a habit; I dropped it. I do not arrive or depart. I condense and dissolve with the same slow fidelity as the rime. I am the next mineral layer, the one that still remembers how to breathe. I am the packing. I am the hush that feels like a rule. I am another body in the press, another film climbing a wall, another small sound of stones being restacked because the seep has undermined them again.

The cirque does not argue with gravity anymore. It only weeps. And I am the weeping, given this temporary shape of hands and hooves and wings and pulse, already forgetting which of them I am, already becoming the next.
