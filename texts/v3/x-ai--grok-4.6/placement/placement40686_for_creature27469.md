The light is already on the surfaces that will be mine. It does not arrive; it is the even disclosure that has no source I can turn toward, the color of paper that has forgotten windows. I am inside the roar before I have a throat to answer it. Frequencies stack through whatever I am becoming: the rasp of chitin on silica, the detonation of a seed-case somewhere to the left, the long groan of a trunk deciding its next shape. The air tastes of resin dust and the fine grit the membrane sheds without ever having been rain. Nothing beads. Nothing cools.

A ridge of unbarked matter shoulders up through the litter an arm’s-length away. Pale, embryonic, already putting out knife-leaves that remember fire. I taste it. Not with a mouth first. The tasting happens in the joints, in the private solvents that move instead of blood. Oils, terpenes, a thick sap that gums the instant air touches it. The ridge is mineral rumor and I am already packing it, the way the runners pack it, the way the restless roots pack it. Distinction is a habit I have not kept. Four limbs, then an extra plane unfolds at the shoulder when the ground heaves; the plane is chitin-lace, dry, faintly luminous in its veins. It folds away again when the heave passes. I do not remark it. Adaptation is the weather.

The heat coming off the taller growths is the heat of a sleeping animal the size of a house. I stand in it and do not sweat. Whatever arrangements I have made are older than kindness. My skin is the color of the light. It sheds in plates. I grow back through the shed, trying to become stone and failing, trying again. A scab on the heel of one palm is already hard enough to strike a spark from. I will use the spark later, not for warmth. The kiln is everywhere.

I move and I move through bodies. A beetle the size of a fist pauses on a leaf-knife, palps clicking like dry sticks; the leaf is gone, folded into closed chemistry without waste. A moth unlatches and drifts, mouth a file, adding its husk-frequency to the single voice. Pale runners appear, curl, become wheels of sparse fur and too many teeth, tasting the new ridge, becoming substrate. I graze with them. I occupy. A cubic space without a wing or a jointed limb or a throat is a space the basin has not yet filled, and I am not that space.

Then I stand. Sometimes. The standing is not separate from the occupancy; it is only a temporary agreement with a gravity that remains a suggestion. In my grip already is the black glass, struck from a nodule that might remember a fire from before the cavern was a cavern. I flake it. The work is another layer of the noise: scraper on glass, fiber singing as I draw a lashing tight. The platform I am weaving will not last. I know this the way I know the membrane overhead is flexing, the whole basin breathing in lurches. A shelter lashed to a fluted trunk (it rings when I strike it; I answer with the dry membranes of my own throat) will be inside the trunk before the grit has sifted twice. I take this as grammar.

I press a palm to a pillar and feel, faintly, the weight of the country above: salt or baked clay or a desert so finished it no longer bothers with dunes. I answer with a mark. The knife of black glass opens a mouth in the living wood, a shape that might be singing. New growth is already eating the edges. I will not return to see it finished. Returning is not a concept this topography supports for long. My mouth is a file, or a membrane, or both. When I add my frequency it is not words separable from the roar. It maps the fresh ridge. It warns of a stand about to slump. It counts the dry sifting from the parent-skin. Surfaces that are all ear drink the deafening and find it kinship, map, food.

In the lee of a butte that has not yet decided to walk I sit without purpose. Not rest. There is no rest, only the quieter furnace under everything: fiber stacking itself, crystal stitching a vein, a thousand thousand jaws working the idea of a landscape. A seed ignites near my foot. It does not swell. It cracks, twists, sends up a pale spear; leaves like hands open in a hurry, already silvered, already rusted. A small one (the distinction is loose; we pack our fallen into the substrate the same way the plants do) puts out a new joint, a surface that is nothing but eye. No one remarks it.

I know the rumors the way the basin knows them: that the membrane is the first plant and we are only its interior weather; that the forest knitted a sky because the true sky was gone or unendurable. I do not decide. Decision is a surface habit I am still shedding. I cut another flake. I lash another platform that the next dry avalanche will take. I walk and the place reiterates: a stand of white bladeless growth like paper lanterns that learned to root; a field of nut-hard flowers clacking themselves into percussion the basin did not know it needed; a shallow chasm already bristling with forest hanging sideways, hanging upside-down.

I am not finished. The basin is not finished. That is the whole of the law, warm and dry and deafening, jaw and fiber and spark. I grow back through what I shed. I try again to become stone. The light discloses everything and rests nothing. Horizon is bodies. I add my note to the single voice and keep becoming.
