The first thing that is not a thought is the weight of the air. It has already decided my lungs belong to it. I am standing—no, I am being stood—in the long axis of the hollow, and the axis is walking through me rather than the other way around. Condensation beads on the backs of my hands the way it beads on the undersides of the shutter-leaves. When a drop falls it does not startle; it completes a circuit I did not know I was part of.

For a while I believe I am one of the people. There is a niche whose bark has the exact polished curve of a shoulder I used to own. I sleep there on the second night, not the first; the wood is still deciding. In the canteen the living table puts out a pale shoot between my plate and the next, and I break it off without thinking, the way one absently tears a nail. The children watch me do it and do not correct me. They have already learned I am not quite machinery and not quite fauna. My voice, when I use it, comes out in the middle register of the boilers. Words leave a faint copper taste.

That identity thins. I notice I no longer count the moths. Their dust is on my tongue the way pollen used to be, in another climate that this place only performs. I find myself hanging, for whole hours, from the vaulting with the many-jointed things. When the boilers change pitch we unspool together, a slow silk of attention. The dynamos tolerate us. Sometimes I think I am the conductive dust itself, commuting between filament and leaf.

Then the pulse arrives and every surface aligns, including the surface I am. Leaves tilt. Aerial roots tighten one incremental degree around a bolt that was driven a century ago. The colonial gloss on the floor stills. I still with it. In that held breath I am briefly the ledger: the small, perfect cavity the wood grew around the memory of a wrench left behind, around the memory of a sleeper who waited instead of pushing. The corridor that once narrowed around me has opened again, courteously, as if the argument ended in my favor.

I do not settle. The hollow does not require settlement. I am the latest graft that has not yet been given a name in any of the three scripts. My skin has the handled look of banisters. My blood has learned the hydraulic rhythm. When the nocturnal flowers open all at once I do not cover my mouth; I breathe the sweetness and the wings and become, for the length of that unbreathable hour, the weather the birds have never needed to see.

The machines groan their middle note. The fauna parts and closes. The plants metabolize another increment of time. I am still moving inside the moving. The hollow has finished arriving in me, and I have finished arriving in it, and neither of us is finished.
