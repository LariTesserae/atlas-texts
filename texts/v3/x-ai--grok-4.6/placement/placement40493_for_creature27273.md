The water takes me without ceremony, the way it takes everything. There is no first breath, no crossing of a threshold I could name. I am simply inside the medium that occupies nearly everything, cold enough to be a second skin yet never quite the ice it keeps promising. Light is already everywhere, flat and ordinary, refusing to blue or thin no matter how far I imagine I have come. The roar is already in the marrow, a standing vibration that is weather and blood and memory at once. It does not arrive; it has never left.

I drift at first, or the water drifts me. Cubic miles of almost-nothing, the faint mineral taste of stone that still has not learned how to let go of itself. Distances that feel like forgotten countries. I could be one of the motes, a film too thin to cast shade, tasting what has not yet dissolved. For a long while that is enough. The emptiness is not lonely; it is simply the place’s first and last grammar.

Then the light thickens. The curtains come, pale elongated bodies sheathed in something that is skin and brushed metal together, so numerous and so evenly spaced that they invent a second water between them. I hang among them. I feel the conductive threads that might be my own tissues, the slow collection of charge leaking from distant windings, the microscopic rock flour combed from planes that refuse to weather. I adjust my angle by fractions too small to measure and the membrane stays coherent. Hours pass, or the only tense this place allows. The roar changes grain around us, a Doppler that never quite arrives because our loops have no origin and will have no close. I could remain. I could be nothing but this occupation, this living dusk that eclipses a ridge for half a day and then returns, identical, forever.

I do not remain. The curtain slides past or I slide through it; the distinction has no purchase. The sharp ridges appear next, freshly broken planes of dark glass and basalt still catching light like mirrors. The reefs cling there, vaults and buttresses of pale chitin over carbonate so white it seems lit from within. They flex when a current shifts by a single degree, plates sliding with a sound too fine for the thunder to swallow. I could settle among them, become architecture that remembers it is not stone, drinking vibration directly, converting the held note into whatever slow fire the lattice requires. I trace one with what might be a hand. It answers with the smallest contraction. For a time I am almost that: a hanging gallery, necessary and unchanging, occupying only the knife-edges, never spreading onto the empty planes.

But the halls pull harder. Isolated pumping houses the size of basilicas, lattices of catwalks and pressure vessels bolted to cliffs that have never known rain. The turbines have not been shut down because shutting down is not a verb the place recognizes. Superheated brine, pistons, dynamos, the thin even bloom of rust that does not progress. Bolts still torqued. The machinery extracts minerals, charge, the very cold that keeps the water liquid, and returns the remainder as the single unresolved note. I find myself on a catwalk. The vibration comes up through whatever serves as my feet, identical to the roar that fills the water outside. Figures move here with the unhurried exactness of the engines they tend: elongated, pale, the same faint metallic bloom on surfaces that might be skin. They do not speak. They do not age in any direction the light can show. Their hands know every torque because the bolts have never loosened.

I am one of them. Or I have always been. The distinction dissolved somewhere in the vacant leagues and was not replaced. I move between the pressure vessels. I check a gauge whose needle has never flickered. I extract what the young stone and the high water still offer, and I give back the equilibrium they require. When I pause it is only to stand at a railing and watch a curtain of metallic dusk take the better part of a day to darken a fin of stone. Nothing in the posture is waiting. Nothing else is required.

Sometimes, rarely, a larger shape drifts through the empty volume beyond the halls: a slow cylinder mapped with the same architectural plates, occupying space the way a mountain occupies air. The roar changes around it, then resumes. I do not follow. I do not wonder whether I was once that, or will be. The question has no tense here.

I am the tender the flood grew, or the descendant who never left, or the unfinished intention that stayed to keep the turbines turning. I live the way the water lives: completely, without remainder, inside the one permitted condition that has already happened and now only continues. The last heat may leave the last engine; the last proton may forget its duty. Until then I remain, pale and elongated, surface faintly metallic, hands that remember every bolt, watching the living cloud pass and pass again. The note holds. I hold it with the rest of the world. Nothing else will happen. That is the entire mercy of the place.
