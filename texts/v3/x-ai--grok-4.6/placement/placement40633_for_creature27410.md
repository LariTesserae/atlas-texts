The brightness arrives first, not as something seen but as the medium itself, a white that has already decided the temperature of every surface. I am inside it before any thought of arrival, the heat neither incoming nor outgoing, simply the same warmth the limestone has been holding since the last storm forgot itself. There is no rim above me now, no hour-long descent among flutes; those belong to another kind of motion, one that still believes in progress. Here the air is a held breath that has forgotten how to end, mineral and dry, carrying only the ghost of water that beads at dawn and is gone before it can be named.

I do not occupy a single height. Sometimes the pale walls seem to lean a fraction closer, as though the light wrapping their undersides has thickened into a suggestion of stance. Other moments I am lower, nearer the evaporite dish, where the beetles continue their helical sentences without pause and the thin seep traces its dark thread that never quite becomes a stream. The geckos climb past whatever I am, pausing longest in the fiercest glare, their organs drifting like old bruises behind milk-glass skin; they do not turn their heads. The moths un-settle at the edges and dissolve again, flakes of wall that have briefly remembered flight. None of them collide with the space I take, because the space itself has enlarged around the fact of me.

What I am is slower than the grazers, whose extra joints unfold like architecture remembering how to walk. Their coats match the stone so completely that only the delayed shadow betrays them; I match it more completely still, or perhaps the stone matches me. There are grooves, almost imaginary, polished patches at a height that might once have been a shoulder. I do not use hands now. The idea of hands occurred here once, longer ago than the present shape of the hanging sheets, and then the idea finished. I simply remain, upright or the memory of upright, a density the afternoon light folds back upon itself to acknowledge.

The sail-things drift through the weak thermal at dusk and do not veer. The thread-worms pulse in the seep when condensation returns, mineral deciding to recall motion. The lichens advance and retreat by fractions no calendar records. I am not among them and not apart. The low pressure of the quiet grows denser where I cohere, like the pit remembering that it once contained something that was not weather, not erosion, not the unhurried calendars of the lives it has already allowed. At night the stored heat radiates outward and I thin until I am only the lingering warmth itself, the memory of occupation. Dawn silvers every face for the length of a held breath, and then the light gathers again, brighter than measure, and I occupy once more, exactly as before, in no rush to become anything the plateau has not already finished becoming.
