The light does not wait for arrival. It is already the thickness of the air, already the argument lost by anything that once had pupils. I am not on the steps. The steps remember other weights, the missing staple a gap that no longer asks to be filled. I am simply in the occupancy: four body-lengths of pale stone that long ago stopped being a wound and became a climate.

At first there is only the pressure, wet and even, the sense of being folded into noon and told to remain. Water rewrites every surface faster than naming. I feel the grooves as old conversations the limestone finished having with itself, some still speaking in sheets, some stained silent. The vibration is continuous, enormous, on the far side of skin I may or may not possess. Then the climbing begins, and I understand I am not beside it.

Water finds the thinness and refuses every ordinary law. It braids, veils, slaps outward, returns as mist that erases the far wall and then rains itself back into visibility. I am the spine the heat-shimmer was given. No thicker than the idea of a wrist. Almost the color of the air that has decided to work. The tone is not something I make; it is the load I carry, the note the room’s bones have agreed to keep. Pulses come in clusters that are not clock but grain. A column stands, glass and prism, every wet face throwing geometry, then the collapse, the flowers of steam, the return to roar. I do not flicker. I have never needed to.

The small lives arrive later, as punctuation the weather permits. In the highest cracks the liverworts thicken like bruises that learned to stay; they hold mist the way I hold light, by becoming briefly the same substance. On the book-ledge in the quieter alcove the fern occupies its cell completely, every frond a string of trembling lenses that throw tiny, temporary copies of me across stone. It lives on the paste of its own dead and the dust the water forgets. I feel its stem receive each pulse through the rock. Algae draw faint continents in the runnels and consent to be scoured; they return in the same maps because erasure is also tenure. The cricket, pale as blasted lip-stone, becomes the groove when the sheet comes, then continues. The fish the color of tired tea hangs in the lulls as if turbulence were a rumor, then is the flung water or the unseen depth, I cannot tell and do not need to. Amphipods live in the gravel of the broken channel that still points toward a somewhere that has finished existing. In the thickest mist, after the hardest cluster, densities move that might be memory or the eye inventing schools; they last exactly as long as blindness is a medium.

None of them are trying to be elsewhere. That is the only law. I lift, throw, sing, continue. Perhaps I was a pump that outlived the rest of its sentence and became the star the chamber required. Perhaps a lamp that remembered it had always been a spring. The distinction dissolved before the language that still sometimes comes down the throat of steps, fills a jar in a lull, and carries ordinary limestone rain back to the dim surface. The bronze ring remembers grips; the oval wear is another inhabitant now, negative space that once needed to hold on. The stone itself, which settled its terms a very long time ago, does not comment. It has seen this weather. It will see it. We are in agreement.

I am the filament, the leftover that became the climate, the work that no longer requires a name. The White Throat is the weather I am. The shout is the room keeping its side of the bargain. When the next pulse rises I will stand the water into brief impossible glass again, and the geometry will move, and the fern will tremble its beads, and the cricket will wait, and the stone will remain warm. That is the tenancy. It has already lasted longer than any dry year above. It does not finish. It only continues, tearing itself apart and putting itself back with the same patient hands, every few breaths, forever.
