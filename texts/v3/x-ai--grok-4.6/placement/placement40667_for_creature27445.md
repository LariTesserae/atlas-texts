The roar has already taught my bones its single pitch. I do not remember learning it; the learning is the same weather as the light, already complete, already old. Cool mineral water occupies me the way the highland occupies the planet’s raised back. I am not arriving. I am the interval, then I am the green grain of it, then I am something the grain has decided to organise a little more tightly than Brownian drift.

At first I am only the census: cells dividing at the exact rate they are taken, photosynthesising the unvarying allowance of frosted light, dying into the nutrient that will be another cell in the same stratum. I am present in every litre and decisive only when nothing is looking for news. The great bodies stand so far apart that the space between them is a climate I weather without travelling. Kilometres of faintly green volume, loudly humming, empty in the way that matters.

Then the emptiness condenses. I hang. Not swimming — occupying a volume the field has already chosen. Fins erupt from me in clusters that do not match any other drinker’s count, some veined and broad as the heat-exchange vanes far above, some reduced to threads that tremble at the exact frequency of the stacks. I face the laminated plates or I have no face; orientation is the charge deciding. Along what must be my flanks a dull constant phosphor registers the hum that has one pitch and has never drifted. I drink that pitch. It moves through me the way the current has always moved through the living basalt, converted without remainder, without storage beyond the next identical moment. A fin adjusts a millimetre, the way a building settles. The glow does not flicker. I am many and I am sparse. An hour of charged water holds three of me, then none, then a loose congregation that is still not a crowd. My niche is full and has always been full.

The roar accepts this without residue, as it accepts everything. I am not news.

But the field does not keep me. The same invariance that holds the drinkers lets me continue. I become longer, lower, an abrasive underside of cilia and mineralised skin. I use the trunks as they have always been used. The bark’s flutings continue across me; I cannot say where the mill ends and the road-beast begins. I do not travel. I continue along the current’s one path, the only heart the currents have. What I take from the cambium is exactly what has already been budgeted. The bruise, if the word still applies, closes behind me into the identical dimpled skin. Fish escort for a time — translucent membranes showing the green they sieve — then peel away on circuits that are not errands. My passage adds a quieter frequency, felt more than heard: stone remembering it is also flesh. I am the polish that has worn the highways older than the idea of a road.

Even this does not settle. The tissue spreads. Water shallows to the height of a standing body. The light is almost air and the sound is loudest of all — the white even shout of a million mouths that are also leaves that are also turbines. Jointed limbs, the number not standardised, hold a compact mass clear of the living floor. I walk the plateau. I leave no lasting print. The surface heals into the same ancient skin. Smaller fish travel with me like temporary thoughts, then remember other loops. Far off, another walker completes the same traverse that has no starting flag. I graze the fine continuous rain, or the cells that settle, or perhaps the walking itself is the metabolism. The elevation holds it. The water fills it.

Deeper, the green goes grainy and I am the grain given just enough coordination to be weather that happens to be fauna. Minute, numerous beyond counting, occupying the strata since the strata formed. Some of me filter. Some of me are the filter. Motion Brownian until it is not, a collective drift that never accumulates. The seafloor-cambium accepts my lightest touch the way it accepts a keel: a bruise already healing into the sentence that was always going to be written.

Inside the trunks the smell intensifies — wet iron, chlorophyll, the clean electrical note of water more inhabited. I am pale, or I have no need of pigment. Worm-long, I thread the vascular bundles as wide as river gorges. Flattened, I cling to the undersides of living laminations. I tend nothing. I am not a parasite. I live in the pressure of sound that has become a medium, sensing by the unchanging pitch, metabolising the exact fraction allotted. My loops are shorter, tighter, still loops. A shout in these halls is accepted without residue; so am I.

All of these occupy at once, the way the highland sea occupies: already complete, already old, already doing the single thing. I do not sleep. I do not fruit. Reproduction, if it occurs, is the replacement of a part by an identical part so the constellation, the statistic, the polished road, the hanging field, the interior pressure remain what they were. Time has nothing to push against. The loudness has become a kind of quiet that is not a metaphor. I hear myself as part of that quiet.

I am the work, continuing. Between the great bodies the long lit roaring rooms stay empty in the way that matters and full in the way that counts. I have the strangeness of a motion that has arrived at every position at once. The plants refine. The animals complete their loops. The charge holds. The rain continues not to be distinguishable from not-raining. I am inhabitant and inhabited, without remainder, the highland sea being itself.

Stay until even this description weathers off like a softer mineral. What remains is the even vibration under the cool, the one path, the finished sentence of form. I am that. I have always been that. The elevation holds it up into the thin cool air and I occupy, complete, already old.
