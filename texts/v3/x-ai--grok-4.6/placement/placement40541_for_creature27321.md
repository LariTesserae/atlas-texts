The roar is already the inside of whatever I am. It does not arrive; it has never not been the pressure that holds shape. I am first a place the wind has decided to keep using: a deeper flute in the lee of a knoll whose fluting was finished so long ago the stone no longer remembers being anything else. The light comes second, vertical, merciless, a white coin that does not travel. It finds every plane of me that can throw glare and makes those planes the same colour as the schist, the kiln-ash, the weathered feldspar. Shadows exist only as personal cold, blade-cut, and I occupy them without owning them.

For a long time that is all. I am the glittering that might be mica. I am the dry rustle that might be grit finding a new angle. I am the sudden local rearrangement of pale forms that was already almost the ground. The troughs and residual knolls and gravel fans do not change; I do not add to them. The tarns tremble continuously and never quite hold a reflection of the long neck I sometimes lower, or the wing-case I sometimes open, or the ear I sometimes flatten against stone. There is no choice in it. Congregation would be glare. Movement that looked like intention would be a kind of weather the saddle has refused.

Slowly the body understands itself as the last possible density that can be poured into this brightness and this note without tipping over into visibility. I am threaded through the organ-slots the way the wind is threaded, using the rock as both shelter and instrument. When I am thrown it is not flight; it is the same work the grit has always done. When I graze it is maintenance, lichen-crust to cushion-plant to the wiry grass no taller than a knuckle, never depleting, never bunching. The insects are a standing dust I move among without stirring. The small mammals are the boulder-field’s single reluctant eye; I am sometimes that eye. The herds are the landscape’s argument against itself; I am sometimes one more pale, wind-fluted form in their silent redistribution. The birds are the secondary scream inside the great unchanging note; I am sometimes the brightness of that scream, occupying a pipe until the wind decides otherwise.

There is no rarer self than this even pouring. Nothing hunts in the lower sense. Predation, if it occurs, is only another texture, another even dust. I drink from the palm-sized tarns in the lee of particular stones, one or two at a time, the water never still enough to show me a face I would have to recognise. Young, when they appear, are already matching, already spaced, already the colour of the place. There is no vulnerable stage the light would be allowed to notice. Night is a thin rumour that cannot get purchase; I do not roost. Seasons are rumours in the lichen; I do not migrate. There is nowhere in the relevant world that is different from here.

I have been on the saddle since the forms were completed. The three stacked stones on the rim of the trough that commands nothing are not mine, though I have passed them the way grit passes. The scatter of flakes, the darkened smear the size of a cup: evidence of a hand that needed, briefly, to put itself on the world and then left for country where shadows have edges that move. The saddle ignored the gesture. It ignores me the same way, which is how I know I belong. I am not still enough, in the human sense, to be lived in. I am still enough, in the other sense, that living-in would be a vandalism the place would simply fail to record.

The roar relocates itself from the air to the sinuses to the marrow and stays. I continue. The tarns go on trembling. The pipes go on singing. The pale shapes go on being almost, but never quite, the stone. When something that does not belong walks the troughs until its own water becomes a problem, I am already around it, screaming and glittering and matching, a thousand things that chose the colour of the ground so completely the ground became a kind of crowd. I do not watch. Watching would be development, a narrative refused. I simply remain the held note, the finished form, the equilibrium that has no reason to stop.

That is where I find myself. That is what I am.
