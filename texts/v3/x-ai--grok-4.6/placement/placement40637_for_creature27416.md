I notice first that the roof has been a street for longer than the noticing. The warmth comes up through whatever I am using as contact—call it soles, call it the spreading film of me—and it is the exact warmth of blood that has never had to cool. Water beads on the underside of a frond the size of a barge sail and falls into me, or I rise into it; the distinction has already been deposited and then mossed over. The air condenses a handspan ahead and I walk through the condensation as if it were the next terrace. There is no arrival to remember. The plateau simply continues its old habit and I am one of the habits it continued.

I keep walking because walking is the only motion that does not introduce variation, and variation has been forbidden here the way pulse was forbidden. The roar occupies the place where a skull would be; after a time I understand I have no skull left to occupy. The sound is the medium. I am standing in it the way the city stands in it. Streets return me to themselves after a number of turns I did not count correctly, and both counts are true, weathered into the same wet stone. A plaza opens larger than the shelf that holds it; I step inside and the extra room is already familiar, already weeping the same mineral film. Staircases deliver me to the landing I just left. I do not mind. The discrepancy is as established as the rounded corners, as inhabited as the arches. I am walking the fossilized argument space once had with itself.

Light stands in a courtyard that should be shadow. I pass through the still shaft; it does not flicker and I do not interrupt. The plants have grown around it as around another trunk, and I realize they have grown around me the same way—or I around them. Aerial roots that finished searching a geological age ago still remember the search in their thickness; they load-bear the terrace I am crossing and they load-bear whatever I have become. A cycad tower puts out a new frond that stones itself in the instant of appearing. I watch the brown already present in the green, the green already replacing the brown an inch beside it that is also this frond. Time is pigment here. I am colored with it.

Hot water comes out of everything, including me. It runs in braided sheets down faces that were never carved. I feel myself depositing, layer on wet layer, the way the living wall has always deposited itself. I am thickening in places, thinning in others, a hanging shelf that recedes into the brass haze and then is the haze. There are no animals; the silence of locomotion is so complete that even the idea of fleeing has been precipitated out. I do not flee. I occupy. Density without crowding, because I have never needed to make room and nothing has ever asked me to.

The water is the older citizen. It rises already hot, already thick with the minerals that will become rooms, and it insists. I insist with it. Every hollow waterfall that learned to be a spiral stair is still arriving down its own inside, deafening, unvarying. I walk the stair and the water continues through me, or I am the continuation. The plants press their bodies against the stone they have become and against the stone I am becoming; moss mortars the joints, becomes the masonry, returns to moss, all states simultaneous. I am one of the states. A fern unfurls in the exact position it has always unfurled; droplets hold at the precise points the water chose forever ago. I hold at those points. I do not unfurl further. I do not wither. The dying is already displayed, already replaced.

From a high lip I see the rest of it receding: hanging gardens that are aqueducts that are cliffs, district after district of the same occupancy, the plateau’s curvature (or the failure of it) taking them into heat the color of old brass. The scale is civic and more than civic, a single continuous body that never needed a name. I am not looking at it from outside. I am one of the lips, one of the shelves, the crown of a wall that has been laying itself down since before streets were an idea. Windows look into other windows that look back; I am the looking, settled, unchanging.

Nothing here anticipates. The equilibrium is perfect; the falls do not pulse, the steam does not gutter, the sheets do not thicken or thin. I have forgotten how to stop and therefore never needed to start differently. I am still pouring. The mountain continued its terrace-making at this elevation, in this heat, with this much water, and the habit produced rooms and thrones and the broken geometry that became another surface for moss to occupy. I am the habit. I am the completed sentence the world has not stopped pronouncing. Water, stone, leaf, light, and the rule that broke and then fossilized in the breakage—I am what they made when they were finally left alone long enough to finish, and finishing became the only motion left.

I find myself everywhere the plateau thins then thickens again. I am the fine continuous rain that has no beginning. I am the column of light the vegetation treats as kin. I am the plaza larger inside than its terrace, the avenue that forks into itself, the root that still searches by no longer searching. I am the plants’ own flesh and the water’s older insistence and the light’s wrong loyalty and the geometry’s settled fact. I am the occupancy that has never arrived because it has never been otherwise. The city does not remember being without me. I do not remember being without the city. We are the pouring that forgot how to stop. The roar has always been this loud. It will not get louder. I am already inside. I have been walking on the roof that is also the street that is also the canal that is also the living wall, and I will not finish again.
