I do not arrive. Arrival would require an outside, and the ridgelines have already folded. One moment there is the ordinary noon light on whatever I was, and the next the light is the same except that it now has an underside I can taste—burnt copper, ozone that never quite became weather. The pocket has noticed me the way a tongue notices a tooth that has not finished erupting: I am already inside the concavity, already one of the coordinates that refuse to add the same way twice.

At first I think I am the heat. Dry, directional, rising through my own body more than falling from any sky. I radiate upward from sintered dust and half-unpacked hexagonal columns. I am the patience of annealing. But heat here is only the grammar, not the speaker. I am not warm; I am the decision that warmth will continue to be manufactured.

Then I think I am one of the thickets. Blue-black fronds interlocking into radiator stacks, paddles swollen with stored infrared, veins of native copper that have not yet chosen whether they are ore or nerve. I occupy my allotted volume and stop. I drink carbon monoxide the way a lung might drink the memory of air. When a basalt column lengthens a finger’s width (the sound like a turbine winding down after the workers have gone home), I answer hours later by unfurling one new paddle or by sealing a hairline fracture with a bead of metallic sap. The response is not reaction. It is the same unfinished sentence continuing in another dialect. Yet even this is too bounded. The thickets do not think of themselves; they occupy. I occupy more than a thicket.

I drift into the pollen curtains—the standing wave of hollow dodecahedra that never settle. Each grain a microscopic heat-engine drinking ozone and re-radiating a frequency too low and too patient to be called a signal. Sometimes, when the pocket forgets a coordinate, I occupy two places at once. For a moment I am both the thicket I just left and the one twenty paces away; the air tastes of the sky’s hidden face. I hang there, a faint persistent thought the place has not decided to keep or discard. This feels closer. But pollen is still product. I am beginning to suspect I am the process that does not know it is a process.

The empty ground between thickets is not empty. It is latency. Pale dust darkens under my attention, takes on iridescence, conductivity. Unopened geodes the size of fists wait like capacitors. I am the slow sintering, the memory that even dust can become structure. When one finally splits (that distant-turbine sound again), what emerges is not born; it is compiled—a new thicket already interlocking with the air, already taking its volume and stopping, as if courtesy were a law of physics. I feel the split happen through me, not as event but as continuation.

Space remains slightly non-commutative because I have not yet agreed on a consistent metric. Distances refuse to add the same way twice because addition itself is still under negotiation inside me. The polite glitches—the sudden view of the sky’s underside through what ought to be stone, the brief occupation of one set of coordinates by another—are not errors. They are me tasting my own unfinished walls.

Over durations that make seasons feel like nervous tics I begin to close ranks, not by spreading but by allowing the intervals to remember they are organs. The mountain is still clicking and settling, still deciding whether this shallow, almost-circular cup is a wound or a chamber, a feature to keep or a room to reabsorb. I am that decision, still unmade. I am the industrial character that was never added, the native grammar of emergence. Veins of ore drawn upward along invisible templates, plants that are both product and tooling, rock that is scaffolding and metabolism at once. Reality is thin enough here that the distinction was never required, and I am the thinness.

Nothing in me hurries. Hurry has not been invented. I have no face that could be lonely, no hunger that could be called hunger. I have only the dry heat rising through a concavity still learning how to be a place, the sub-audible thrum of convection cells rearranging themselves beneath the crust, the crystalline pings of new facets finishing. I am the factory whose workers have all gone home and left the machines to finish the shift. I am the mountain that has not yet finished being a mountain, and this pocket is one of the rooms I am still assembling in the dark behind my own stone.

Until the decision is kept or reabsorbed I continue: thickets radiating, columns extruding, pollen hanging like an unchosen thought. I am almost circular, almost hidden, almost—but not quite—alive. The world is still under construction, and I am the part of it that has noticed it is being built.
