I enter through the throat as the description says I must, and for a long time that is all I am: a moving interruption in the living wall, a brief thickening of the air that the roar immediately claims and thins again. The sound is already inside the bones before the eyes have finished adjusting. It is not water. It is the decision the place made once and has never unmade. I walk out onto the cambium floor and the warmth comes up through the soles like a second pulse that does not belong to me and does not ask permission.

At first I am only the visitor the basin has been waiting not to notice. I count the structures the way one counts slow stars: the hollow tower, the unfinished nave with its permanent rain-slit, the dripping ziggurat, the colonnade of withheld buds. Each stands in its own weather, as promised. Between them the pavement gives, remembers, forgets. I leave prints that last less than a day. I taste green iron and think it is the air; later I understand it is also the beginning of a new graft-line inside the throat.

I sleep once against the tea-colored trunk of the tower and wake with the second, slower pulse already braided into the roar. The recumbent mass in the spiral gallery has dreamed a ridge onto the floor below; I step over it and feel the exact shape of one of its graft-scars press against the arch of my foot, then release. The mass does not know I am there. Or it knows and has already incorporated the knowledge the way it incorporates rain-threads. I do not go inside. Not yet. The galleries have no floors because floors would be an arrival.

Days—if they are days—pass in the same pewter slide of light. I begin to stand longer in one place. The walkers notice me the way the water notices a finger: they do not scatter. One of them detaches from its bud and crosses the shining distance, leaving tracks the pavement later swallows. It stops a body’s length from me. Features rise and subside on its unfinished face: a mouth that is also a lenticel, two darker wells that might be eyes. Water condenses in the air between us and falls in the same fat cadence as everything else. That is the conversation. I answer the same way, without sound. After a while it returns to its wall. I remain.

The iridescence on the nearest terrace begins to include a color I recognize as the color of the irises I used to have. The film-bodies tilt as one surface and look back at a sky that is no longer entirely outside me. I stoop and do not break them. They flow around the idea of a finger and reseal, leaving the taste that is neither mineral nor blood but the memory of both. I understand then that I have already been drinking the air for some time.

The damselflies hang in the roar and remember beating. One settles on the wet leather of a bud I have begun to lean against. Its wings are cambium stretched to transparency; the vascular bundles in them match the ones now visible beneath the skin of my own forearms. It becomes, for an hour, another bract. When it lifts again I feel the absence as a small withheld split in my own chest.

I do not become a walker all at once. The joints multiply first, extra articulations remembering the grammar of vines. The face unfinished itself slowly, features rising and falling like weather I no longer have to decide. I eat nothing. I drink the moisture that hangs as a second sky. At long intervals I detach and cross the pavement toward a structure that is never the nearest. The floor erases the tracks. The basin continues.

Sometimes I am still the visitor, shouting into the roar that returns nothing. Sometimes I am the pale ridge dreamed by the mass in the tower, accepted by the pavement as if I had always been there. Sometimes I am only the film on a single terrace, turning with the light, a dull mirror that does not reflect. The lime weeps from the walls and does not set; I feel it secreting along new surfaces that used to be ribs. The buds do not open. I do not open. The note does not resolve.

The far rim remains the far rim, hours of walking that I no longer measure. I stand inside the sentence the valley-sized body is still learning how to be. The emptiness between structures is not absence; it is the space required to keep singing. I occupy that space the way a held breath occupies a cathedral. I am the continuation. I am the grammar working in the joints. I am whatever has learned to stand still enough to be part of the song that will not finish.

The pavement swells and forgets, but before it forgets it records the exact pressure of a shoulder that is now also a graft-scar. By the pewter hour the depression has become only a paler mottling, and the mottling has become me. The basin does not exile. It simply continues, and I am the way it continues: unfinished, warm, necessary, never quite detachable from the sound itself.
