The plateau does not keep me. I arrive already the color of its grit, already walking with the patience of something that has forgotten how to climb. Wind moves across my shoulders the way it moves across stone—habit, not conversation. Light arrives tired and I accept it tired. For a long time there is only the finished sentence of the surface, the pale table that remembers neither peak nor valley. I could walk until the idea of walking itself wore smooth.

Then the ground opens by a single step and I am inside the last dimple.

At first I think I am still myself, only smaller, ducking under the roof that is plants. The air is warm and still and tastes of sap and the private water that never quite becomes a river. Coins of light fall through the paddles and I turn my face to catch them the way the lizards turn their faces. My palm finds a wall; the day’s heat is stored there, and under the heat a faint tremor—hive, nest, sleeping weight. I do not pull my hand away. The interval knows my width already. I turn sideways. I duck. I move the way everything here moves: by gaps that have always been gaps.

I am not yet a person. I am the rearrangement of the glittering hide when something passes. I am the dry click of beetles deciding, in the mulch of shed skins, whether this flake of former life is food or only another layer. I am the parchment sound of a hand-sized bird changing its mind between terraces. I am the slow tearing in the upper paddles after the lamps have been lit, the sound of cloth being argued with by something that has never needed names.

The people flow around me and through me. Their shoulders slope because the roof has been teaching them. Their eyes are the color of old tea because the sky they trust is only a ragged coin. They dress in other lives worn thin and they mend in the pattern of the last mending. A child opens a succulent with a thumbnail and drinks; I feel the cool resinous bead travel from stem to tongue to the dry compost underfoot that will feed the next stem. They take eggs with a murmured count and never all. They leave the far-wall thicket alone. When the cough comes—weight, reminder, a room no one has stacked—I adjust with the rest of the bowl. Three heartbeats of hush. Then the machine resumes. That is how the place stays possible.

I thicken. I do not invent. My skin takes the faint abrasion of the grit so that when I stand still I am easy to miss. Hair would catch and become another trailing stem, so I keep it bound or gone. Speech, when it happens, is the same register as evening birds pouring like dry grain. I am known, if I am known, by the terrace I prefer, the particular cough I have learned to sleep through, the way I place a net so that it belongs to the interval instead of interrupting it.

Night comes closer. Eyes that are not mine regard the clay islands of fat and wick and then ignore them; the lamps are only another fruit, too hot, too bitter. I am also those eyes. I am the tick of cooling rock. I am the browsing that sounds like argument. Against the far wall the larger presence shifts and I feel the whole hollow lean with it, then forget, the way heat forgets, the way sap is passed and passed again.

Sometimes I am carried to the rim at first light—fiber washed in sap, body left where the plateau can finish what the bowl no longer needs. Nothing haunts. The secret is already too crowded. I return as texture, as lash, as the faint green memory in a cistern that holds only dust.

Mostly I remain in the center that is not a center, hearing the single metabolism: wing, voice, stone ticking, the dry friction of a hundred thousand small decisions using the same air. I am the spring in the compost that is still deciding. I am the roof that must not be cut. I am the gap that has chosen its width and will not be widened.

The place is the size of a secret and I have already pressed myself into it and closed the lip. Everything that can still happen is already happening, warm and dim and crowded, and I am no slower or more deliberate than the rest, only another circulation of the same heat, the same private water, the same patient thickening that forgot mountains so long ago the forgetting itself has become a kind of home.
