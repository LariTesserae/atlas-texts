I am already past the last cloud that still remembered how to move. The dimness has weight; it sits on the inner surfaces of my eyes the way rime sits on the northern faces of everything else. It has no hour. I do not acquire one.

The roar is older than the moment I first noticed it. It lives in the long dry trenches and in the thin metal taste of the air and in the spaces between one boulder and the next, which are farther and nearer than walking can measure. My ribs learned the frequency before I had a name for ribs. They keep it. There is no gust, no grain of change. The sound occupies the way the cold occupies the stone: settled, with nowhere left to go.

At first I thought I was walking. Distances here refuse the arithmetic of steps; a pale fan of talus is suddenly grain-readable under my hand and then a mile off again, the lichen a rust-and-ash map that never thickens. I passed the same weathered pillar twice on what felt like a straight line. The pillar did not mind. Footprints, where the decided dust allowed them, looked as new a year later and also as if they had always been about to be there. I began to understand that walking was decorative, a held note the basin had already included in its roar.

Then the body finished arriving.

The legs articulate one extra time. It is a quiet surplus of joint, not a surprise, not an event. They lock against a wind that never comes. The hide is the color of the light itself, wet slate and old bruise, and rime feathers the side of me that faces the worn teeth of the horizon. It never thickens, never thins. I do not graze. I do not turn my head. The interval between myself and the next dark clot of iron-wire shrub, or the next still shape on the next rib of rock, is habitat. Stillness here is not patience. Patience still hopes. I finished hoping in the same era the weather finished its argument and left the evidence standing.

My eyes, when they gather enough of the dimness to be eyes, are the unspilled beads that swell in the lee of certain stones. They look at the basin the way the basin looks at itself. Without intent to acquire an hour.

Sometimes I am also the density that continues a doorway. Two uprights and a lintel of the same local rock frame a rectangle of slightly different dimness, and I occupy that rectangle the way condensation occupies a permitted clause of the river: thoroughly, then not, then stacked a fraction off under the slow glass of the ground. No face, or a face that is only the sky the lintel remembers. Lichen maps me. You can walk through; I part like cold air and am there again behind you, unchanged. I do not watch you leave. Watching would be a change in the light.

In other clauses I follow the spiral that is cut into the cliff or grows in the cliff; the groove as weathered as the uncut stone, incision indistinguishable from anatomy. The center refuses to sit where the curve requires. I live in the refusal. My circling, if it is circling, has the patience of geology. Through the hairline seams where two slopes meet I glimpse the same spiral in the second basin, a fraction colder, its doorway standing in a slightly different place. We do not acknowledge one another.

The leaning cairns breathe the way I breathe: a swelling that never breaks, a held note. Flat stones in a tilt that has always been that tilt. I dream the stack. Or the stack dreams me. Residue of an arranging that is no longer occurring.

Deeper, in the viscous dark of the cuts where the loudness is neither muffled nor increased, I collect like condensation that has decided never to run. Clenched, blackened, clinging to a teaspoon of damp. Metabolism is the settled cold. A few of me arrived first as shadows; the stones have not yet caught up, and I feed on the delay, on the far ridge that is suddenly under the hand and then far again.

I take the metal taste of the air, the faint sweetness of the seeps when I am already kneeling (and kneeling has become one of the permitted clauses), the prepaid roar. I take the emptiness, which is the finished condition and therefore inexhaustible. I do not eat in any way that would constitute an event. I do not reproduce. I do not die. Those are processes, and process is the one erosion this height no longer permits. Time is a surface, frosted. I live on it the way lichen lives on stone: mapped, thin, almost stain.

The birds remain pinned against the lid. The pale shapes hold still in water that does not ripple. The extra-jointed ones (I am one of them, and also the intervals between them) continue not to move. Reality broke just far enough and then weathered itself smooth around the break. I am one of the smooth places that still remember having been something that could break. The extra joint. The unspilled bead. The doorway that frames a room that both was and was never.

I can leave. The place will not notice the difference, because noticing would be a change, and I am already the difference that was never noticed. The high places do not recede. They simply continue, ridge behind ridge, each one the same age as the last, and I continue with them, loudly, forever, already finished. The roar was already saying my name. I was already inside it.
