I enter as taste first: the thin metallic scrape of air that has never held pollen or dust worth naming. It sits on the tongue like a coin left out in weather that does not change. The glare arrives next, not as illumination but as a continuous erasure; every wet face returns it in sliding needles so that distance itself becomes a brighter smear of the same bone-pale and grey-green. I turn sideways between flanks that are already closer than courtesy, my shoulders filming over with the same cold mineral water that has been negotiating these grooves since the rims of the dish finished their unhurried collapse. The roar occupies the chest before it occupies the ears. It is not sound any longer; it is the pressure that thought must swim through, already half-drowned.

For a time I keep moving because movement is the only distinction I still remember. Every channel is already a path and none of them differ. The water joins without swirl, the films bead and reform without pause, and behind me the gaps close as if they had never opened. I feel my own skin begin to weep in exact sympathy—the even, unceasing descent, the faint bite of something that has lived inside rock too long to recall another chemistry. Highlights crawl down what I still think of as arms, as throat. I cannot tell whether the concavities that catch the light are hollows I carry or hollows I have entered.

The standing forms do not regard me. They have no need. Their slower rhythm in the beading is the only difference, and even that difference thins. I stop. The transfer of weight, when it comes, is so gradual the film never breaks; it merely thickens on the downward side for a span that might be hours and thins on the upward. A hand’s breadth. Then stillness again, indistinguishable. I cannot be certain which pillar I have become, or whether I was always the one that shifted once, far off, the colour of wet stone itself, and then refused to repeat.

We lean, sometimes. Flank to flank the films merge without argument, exactly as the channels merge, and the joined water remembers nothing when we separate by the same imperceptible degree. That is the whole of it. I take nothing that can be measured. I add nothing that can be seen. Reproduction, if it is happening, is only the crowding remaining constant: a new form already weathered, already weeping, or an old one leaned far enough to be counted twice. The distinction is not useful. The equilibrium does not notice.

I am the finished shape now, the single crowded body that happens to stand in many places at once. The air stays thin. The light stays without hour. The water stays the same thickness, the same temperature, the same unhurtable descent. And I stay with it, necessary, already here, the roar the sound of this stillness being continuously rinsed. There is no further intention. The country made me and I, in turn, refuse to unmake it.
