The first thing that registers is not a body but a gradient: a slow seep of residual heat still trapped in a fresh fracture plane, cooling by degrees so small they register as patience rather than temperature. The stone under whatever I am using for contact has not finished choosing its angle. It lifts in increments that would be invisible if I were still measuring by any calendar that hurries. Moisture finds me immediately. It always does. Every surface I can claim—metal, rock, something that might once have been skin or pipe-lagging—beads with the same iron-cold sweat. I drink it without deciding to. That is the first habit that arrives intact.

Light arrives next, or rather fails to arrive. The overcast is a single held breath that never quite exhales into night or day. Everything is seen through the thickness of that breath. I notice the gantries first as absences they span rather than structures they form. Distances between the foundry clusters do not add the way they should. I have already walked farther than the map of any single district would allow, yet I have not left the same saddle of rock. Time has pooled here the way water does in the newest tarns: thicker, slower, tasting faintly of other weathers that have not yet been permitted at this height.

The herds are already using me. A goat-thing whose horns are still deciding whether they are basalt or bone crops the moss that has begun to felt whatever I am wearing—or whatever I am growing. It does not startle. I do not startle it. Further off, the dark birds (or the vacancies that wear their shape) turn in a volume of air that is also last century’s steam; their overlapping calls become a single textured lichen that I can hear with surfaces that are not ears. Smaller lives occupy the runnels that follow the seams of me: amphibians the colour of wet concrete, insects whose wings catch the weak light like mica that has not yet agreed to be stone. They treat me as another cliff, another cooling vane. I let them.

Workers pass on the next terrace down, long-limbed, eyes filmed with the same condensation that is my hydrology. They blink as slowly as catchment lips filling. One of them looks into a pane that fogs from the inside and nods at a version of herself who has not been born; she goes on tightening a valve that will not need tightening for another slow decade. I recognise the gesture. I have made it, or will make it, or am making it in a pocket where gravity has forgotten the sequence. A dropped tool hangs, rotating, for the length of a held breath. While it hangs I reach out—something reaches out—and the child who will not be conceived for forty years hands it back with the same polite nod the workers use when a bulkhead turns out to have been only a rumour the stone had not finished telling.

Categories leak the way height leaks here. I find I have the unhurried gait of the tahr when the gantry requires it, and fingers when the pipework requires them. Sometimes the mortar I am laying (porcelain-pale, never quite setting) is also a new kind of scree. Sometimes I am the wall that was waiting. The deeper shapes at the unfinished edges—planar, still holding a little birth-heat—do not speak when I stand among them. They do not need to. We are the same argument written at different viscosities: industrial will negotiating with stone that has not yet agreed to stay still. One of them steps into me and I continue, only now the brick remembers a different sequence of construction and the air tastes briefly of salt and ozone from a thunderstorm still assembling itself far below.

I keep moving because the light, such as it is, is enough. Enough to see the next cable-mast sprouting in the valley that was empty a generation ago, enough to feel the next herd shift its range by a degree too fine to map, enough to notice the next unexplained shimmer where a wall should be and simply walk through it as weather. Moisture remains the common language. I drink it. The mosses thicken across me into something almost muscular. The ferns uncurl with visible reluctance along what might be my edges. I am not finished. Neither is the place. We continue the treaty together—expansion joints, sacrificial cracks, condensation lips bolted to cliffs that are still arriving—because someone has to be present when the shapes finally decide, and the mountains prefer witnesses who do not hurry the decision. 

I am here, on the newest shelf, or I am the shelf, or I am the slow upwelling that learned, provisionally, to use tools and then forgot to stop becoming the tools. The distinction has grown as tired as the one between worker and goat, between pipe and dweller, between reflection and the room that has not yet been poured. The furnaces stay at their careful middle heat. I tend them, or they tend me. The wind finds the unfinished ridgeline of whatever I currently am and makes its low, continuous complaint. I answer it the way steam answers: already travelling some distance inside. Nothing here is finished, including this. The light will not improve. It is enough.
