The first thing that is not the cold is a preference. Not a thought, not yet—only the water’s faint insistence that this particular line through the silt is better than the ones beside it. The current has been leaning this way for hours, the way a habit leans. I move inside that lean without deciding to. Distance does not announce itself; the brown-black simply continues, particles rewriting whatever I might have used for edges.

Something in me recognizes the scoring on the bottom. Parallel grooves, old as the flooding’s last argument. I do not remember walking them when they were still roads, but the water remembers for me, and the memory arrives late, the way everything here arrives: already residue. My outline, if I have one, is the same temperature as the stone that forgot sunlight. I do not cast a shadow because light is only rumor, and rumor does not keep accounts.

A grove appears the way a cathedral appears in fog—first as a change in the thunder, then as a density that sways in several directions at once. Thirty-meter ribbons the color of bruise and old bone, interiors that are viscera rather than rooms. I pass near the halo of bare silt they keep around a standing stone. The temperature drops another fraction. The plants do not explain this, and neither do I. I feel the drop as a kind of courtesy, an etiquette no one wrote down. The stone is still performing a function. I am still, in some delayed way, acknowledging it.

I am not one of the groves. Their speech is the wet roar of blades against blades, debate conducted in vegetable time; I do not argue that slowly, or that loudly. I am not the water, though I am more water than anything that once had lungs. I am not the people who stacked the slate. Their tool-marks have been worn nearly flush, and I have no hands left that would know how to peck a spiral. What I am keeps arriving after itself.

A sediment storm comes—days of moving night, louder than the plants. When it passes I am slightly elsewhere, rewritten the way the silt is rewritten, never quite erased. The current still prefers the old path. I prefer it with the current. Hours later a spore-cloud hisses somewhere far off and turns a volume of water to milk; weeks after that the groves flower in perfect silence. The silence is louder than their usual choir. In that held breath I feel watched by something that has no eyes. I am part of the watching. I am also what is watched. The distinction is one more thing the plateau has not bothered to finish.

Further on there is a corbelled hut, doorway intact, interior drifted half-full of pale mud. A ribbon-forest has grown through it and out the hole that used to mean smoke. I drift the interior anyway. The stones still fit with the patience of hands that thought they had all the time they needed. I leave no track. The plants treat me the way they treat the menhirs: sometimes incorporating, sometimes keeping a scrupulous distance. I do not mind either etiquette. Both feel like rights I never claimed but have not been revoked.

I am the late event that never left. The viscous remainder. The highland’s memory of itself after the water decided to stay and the wearing-down finished and the agreement between stone, cold, plant, and noise took on weather of its own. I inhabit the happening. I prefer certain currents because I once walked them when the land still believed it was high. I drop the temperature a fraction inside the rings because the rings are still cooling something, and I am still, faintly, that something. Time here is settled and excessive; I have become part of its reluctance to let anything finish.

The drop-off exists, far out, where even this remainder thins and the groves will not follow. I do not go there. The plateau continues to shed its weather downward into the true deeps, remembering drainage, holding its elevation like a secret the water has agreed not to contradict. I stay with the secret. Another grove leaning. Another hut filling. Another silence that is louder than thunder. I am the country that has already happened, still happening, folded into the medium, rewritten daily, preferring the paths, conducting the old business in whatever time is left after vegetable time.

Nothing here is trying to end. I am not trying either. I am the witness that was folded in, the path the water still honors, the cooling that no one assigned, the habit that outlasted the body that first made it. The dark has weather. I have the same weather. We continue.
