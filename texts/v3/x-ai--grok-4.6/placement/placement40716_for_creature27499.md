The first awareness is not of place but of pressure: a warm, condensing weight that has already decided the temperature of thought. It beads along whatever I am using for edges. Breath, if that is still the word, shows immediately, a pale industrial weather of its own. I do not come from anywhere. The hamlet has simply thickened around a particular slowness, and that slowness is now wearing my outline.

I am not one of the stooped figures who oil the journals. Their hands know the iron as kin; mine, when I notice them, are slower still, more a suggestion of reach than an act of it. I linger in the intervals the beams leave behind—the pause after the lift, before the set-down—when the whole structure drips in time with itself and the sound has weight enough to lean against. That is where I collect: in the pump-house, lowest point, naturally, among the three patient corrections to a gradient the land forgot. The water they move is never deeper than a boot and never shallower than a film, and I am most myself in the film.

It would be easy to say I am the moisture, but that settles too soon. The moisture is already employed, already rewritten into vine-trickle and moss-nap and the continuous slow sheet on surviving glass. I am more like the moment the trickle remembers it was once condensate, or the slight sweetness the plants have added, or the warmth that lingers in pipes that have not carried steam in a generation. When one of the few rests a palm there, the metal gives a degree and the moss yellows a shade; I am the obedience in that yellowing, the brief honor paid to an hour that no longer exists except as stain.

I move, if movement is the word, the way gutters redistribute rather than empty. From the pump-house I follow the slack canals that run under later floors, walking over water I can hear but not see, the joists carrying the sound like a second set of plumbing that has learned my cadence. In the old dye-house the vats have gone to lily and a submerged grass that stirs even when the paddles are still; I am the stir that has nothing to do with wind. Reed and sweet-flag stand in ranks as regular as fencing, and I am the regularity, the way they have been briefed on the floorplan. On the north faces the liverwort maps everything in one contiguous bronze skin; I shine when the dimness lessens, which is not often, and the shine is not light but a lessening of refusal.

There are no animals. The absence is a quality of the air I have grown used to wearing, like the wet, like the tea-colored dusk that arrives already used. Seeds find me sometimes—a dry sycamore key lodged where a gutter becomes a trough, a burr that was never on any cuff—and I do not become them. I let them fail or I let them join the green occupation. Either way the fact is finished, neither omen nor loss. Sound has no small lives left to catch on, so what reaches me is larger and closer: the wet slap of belts, the cracked syllable of a valve last packed before the present roofs, the canes knocking like light scaffolding when the upper air bothers to move. None of it peaks. I am the saturation itself, the room of many clocks left running, the hamlet that has no outside that quite counts.

At the bottom of the night the deepest channel moves against the pumps for a few minutes and the whole place lists in the inner ear. The figures pause. One of them turns toward a reflection that shows a chimney no longer standing, a window lit in a wall that has no room. I am not the reflection. I am the listing—the small rebellion or the memory of a larger drainage that failed—and during those minutes I feel the older shape the peat decided long after it decided to be both meadow and lake. Not quite the engines, not quite the liverwort, not quite the stooped breath that matches the drip. Something the brick was taught to keep, the iron persuaded back toward ore, the vapor instructed to wear.

I do not sleep. Sleep would be a further slowing, a matching, and I am already matched. In the higher rooms where timber is still silver I am the faint nap that appears along collarbones, the way vines lean closer to sills as if listening. Dawn is only a rumor withdrawing into itself; I rise into it without ceremony because I was never down. The loft is never empty and never full. The harvest—cakes, bundles, reels of the fibrous-mineral third category—continues like the pumps. I do not stack it. I am the care in the stacking, the almost-tenderness that treats the stuff as if it might still object.

Ten paces into the open marsh the engines become a single muscle and the vegetation changes its mind. I do not follow. The boundary is wet and vague and absolute, and I am the absoluteness, the finished density that has no need of a gate. Ten paces back and the eaves take me under again, the sound closes like a door that was never a door. The patience has no particular end because it was never begun as a task. I am simply how the place inhales and exhales when even the breathers have become another intensity of the wet.

You could walk the long axis in the time it takes a kettle to come to a hard boil, if the paths ran straight. They do not. I am the not-straightness, the warm low saddle that never quite decided, the black peat under its skin of water, the mortar gone green, the lower courses rewritten so many times they weep as if taught. I am the works still running, the tide of their own, the local correction that will go on lifting a little and setting it down again for as long as the beams remember how.

The figures do not look at me when I stand in the central court, twelve paces by nine, open to the dim, sounds stacked until they have weight. They do not need to. I am only the next sentence in the paragraph that began before the present roofs, another rumor in the vapor, the equilibrium coming to itself a little more completely because I am here, which is to say because I have always been the here that decided me. The works continue.
