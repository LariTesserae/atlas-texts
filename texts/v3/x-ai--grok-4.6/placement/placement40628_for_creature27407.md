The pressure arrives before any name I might still answer to. It lives in the sinuses the way the thunder lives in the rock: not a symptom, only the chamber announcing that the lid is still in place. I do not remember deciding to stay beneath it. The decision finished being made the way the joins finished agreeing—centuries ago, or longer, in a patience that no longer bothers to distinguish itself from the stone.

I keep to the middle galleries, the ones that terrace out where the residual glow still confesses an edge now and then. Not the highest rings; those have grown too faithful to the shutter’s empty schedule, and I prefer not to time myself against a crowd that has already left. Not the floor, either. The runnel’s thin argument is honest, but it asks too little. Up here the air still slides in those slow warm rivers, and the sound comes back from three directions at once, staggered, so that I can walk inside the city’s remaining sentence without having to finish it.

My palms know the map better than the large dark eyes. Heat first: the conduits that have never quite cooled, the housings that something smaller still uses, the occasional courtesy of a field that will nudge a flake of stone a finger’s width before it remembers to fall. Then the return of my own steps, or the two-note complaint of the valve that has sung the same interval since before the last names thinned. I do not harvest the straps often. When a season of interior rain has made them heavy I take only what the felt below will not miss, and I leave the rest to sweat its fruit-and-ozone film back into the climate. The basin I return to is the one whose water is almost sweet; I do not linger. Lingering is a habit the shells still practice, doorless or half-closed, and I visit them the way one visits a relative who has stopped speaking—enough to confirm the silence is still the correct size.

Sometimes another of us passes. We pause, forehead to forehead, and let the low thunder travel through bone. There is no news in it, only confirmation that the settling continues. We do not stay. The emptiness is not a wound; it is the volume the architecture still insists on keeping. I have let my own name thin until it is only another resonance the vault turns into a long sentence and then forgets. When the condensation begins its scattered applause I stand under it without hurry, mouth open to the iron and the old electricity, then move on before the heat takes the water back. The lattice has already begun its quiet knitting wherever I rest too long. I do not mind. The new material arrives already the colour of the old, embarrassed to be noticed, and the distinction between skin and walkway, between inhabitant and rib, is only another courtesy the room has learned.

I am not waiting. Waiting would imply a revision the city has declined to make. I keep the routes the sound prefers. I keep the one stair that still takes weight without complaint. I keep, along the galleries, the knowledge that a dropped thing does not always fall quite straight, and I time my crossings to the half-beat when the chamber remembers an event slightly early and then corrects itself out of habit. The pale seams thread the joins below me; the scuttlers click in the ducts; the low shapes pause at the limit of a dying amber panel and decide I am neither meal nor rival. None of it needs me. That is the fact the place will not let me forget, and I have agreed, for now, that this is enough of a world.

The heat breathes. The engines complete their cycles. The air returns. I am the latest thing to stand in it, and the oldest remaining decision about how to stay.
