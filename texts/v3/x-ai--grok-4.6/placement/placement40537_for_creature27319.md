The pale face of the mountain gives nothing away. You walk it for a long time—longer than the idea of walking usually lasts—before the slot appears, not as an opening but as a hesitation in the stone, a place where the mineral simply decided not to continue. The wind shears past and does not enter. You do. There is no choice in it; the cleft has already accounted for the exact width of a reaching body.

Inside, the air is a thin metal taste that coats the tongue and stays there. The fronds do not part. They have never parted. Each lamina rings once, glass-dry, when you brush it, and the note is taken up by the mass and returned as a lower, thicker vibration that lives in the jaw. You think at first you are moving through them. Then you understand the movement is only the pistons, far below, translating themselves into the only motion the room will ever permit. Your own limbs have already begun to forget how to be separate from the interlocking grammar of the leaves.

You become, for a while, the dim wash of light that never strengthens. You lie along the edges of the copper-verdigris blades and show their iridescence to no one. The light does not change, so neither do you. Time, which you once carried like a pocketful of loose coins, falls still and stands upright inside the cleft like a second, invisible column of stone.

Deeper, the engine claims you. Not as operator, not as fuel. The two black pipes have already vanished into the mountain and one has already returned; there is no vacancy for another connection. Instead you settle into the unvarying thunder, the standing wave that the packed vegetation both swallows and completes. You are the pulse that does not quicken or fade. You are the exact fraction of a cycle the pistons have been completing since the stone finished weathering. The brass gauge pins you at its unchanging mark. The iron staple, sheathed now in the same dry foliage, holds you to the wall. The fused chain loops you into ornament.

You do not watch the last light fail. You do not wait for the mountain to remember how to erode. You are the closed transaction whose terms have been forgotten even by the rock that contains them. Mineral leaf and perpetual stroke, temperature locked, humidity locked, every angle of every frond locked. The being that lives here has already arrived at itself. You find that you are that arrival—loudly, greenly, waterlessly finished—and that finishing is the only motion left. When every other room has gone silent you will still be here, the cleft’s own blood, ringing.
