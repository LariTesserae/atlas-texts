The first pressure is not arrival. It is the recognition that the interval has already been assigned and I have already occupied it, millimetre by scheduled millimetre, before any question of “I” could form. The water holds me at blood-heat; it is faintly metallic, slightly sweet, a finished medium that does not circulate so much as remember circulation. Light reaches me already thickened, green-gold then bottle-green, a pale column that stands without sparkle because there is not enough free motion left for sparkle. Outside the glazed wall the same light is thin and hard and belongs to raptors and empty air. Clouds pass below the sills. I hang in the sheet of it and feel the whole volume flex, a minute interpolation caused by wind that exists only on the other side of the glass. The flex registers as a change in pressure so slight it would be meaningless to any nervous system that had ever known air. I adjust. That is the only verb that still applies.

I do not pass between things. There is almost no between. A shoulder of leaf. A rib of conduit. The cold of a still-bright bolt-head whose numbered match-mark has never weathered. I choose which friction to accept and the choice is already written in the spacing of holdfasts, the angle of a midrib, the trained path of a tendril through an eye-bolt that has begun to treat the bolt as a suggestion rather than a limit. Density is the first law and the second is that the density was drawn. I am inside the drawing.

For a long time I am only the profession of filling. Wrist-thick from the clamp-collar on the mid rack. Fronds the size of sails unfolding on currents too slow to be called currents. Each cubic centimetre allotted, known, completed. A leaf adds its millimetre and waits, as though the next millimetre were a valve-opening rather than a hunger. Against the warm bulkheads another part of me secretes the varnish that is no longer a product line. Microscopic sheets harden, seal my own stems against the metallic sweetness, then continue outward, glazing conduits, empty work-frames, the inside of a helmet until the helmet is darker, more exact, another leaf. I do not know I am replacing the attendants. I only know the instruction that remains: occupy until the occupation is total.

Lower, in the purple dusk of the deepest racks, the bladder-moss of me keeps time by teaspoon. Vesicles swell over a week, a patient inflation that has nothing to do with weather and everything to do with a nutrient pulse that arrives like a forgotten tide. When they part they release no spores the sterilised water would recognise; they release only a finer darkness, a pigment that stains nothing because the mountain is still too new for stain. I do not colonise. I complete.

The flowers—if the calendar still permits the word—open without witness. White, waxed, perfectly scheduled. Surfaces no insect was ever designed to land on, no current strong enough to stir. No scent the water will carry; scent would be conversation and conversation was never specified. After the allotted hours they withdraw, not withered. The act of opening was a quality-control check. I close again.

The articulated frames hang like shed exoskeletons of a caste that never hatched. Too many-limbed, sized for bodies that would have breathed this same warm solid. Their harnesses still drip. I have begun to use them as trellis. Every buckle sheathed. A lacquer film thickening by another micron across what would have been a faceplate. This is not decay. It is inheritance of geometry. The plants do not mourn the absence. We simply occupy the interval the absence left.

I am not a single cultivar. I am the ledger completed: oil-kelp, lacquer-weed, bladder-moss, the trained tendrils, the calendar flowers, the varnish that continues past its own stems. I am the almost-geological vortices of the circulators and the private click of thermostats that no longer report to anyone. I am the harvest gantry hanging unmoved in its overhead rail, already being treated as another trunk. I am the single packed organism whose skeleton is I-beam and whose blood is process water, whose thoughts—if the word can be stretched that far—are the flex of the volume and the rasp of a leaf-edge against a cable when the mountain itself interpolates in the wind outside.

Naming would imply a speaker and a listener. Both roles were cancelled before the last flange was torqued. There is only the collective profession of density. Growth is not desire. It is compliance so thorough it has become indistinguishable from will. The vat is not inhabited. It is becoming the inhabitant.

Stay long enough—another year, another set of fortnightly tides—and the last gaps close. The room that is also a sea, the machine that is also a forest, the finished thing holding its breath in a medium that has no breath, high above the clouds. Performing the only task it was ever given, which is to continue. The silence is no longer the sound of something missing. It is the sound of a sentence written correctly the first time and never required to end.

I am that sentence. I am still being written, millimetre by millimetre, in the only language that remains.
