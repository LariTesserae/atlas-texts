The chord finds the hollows in me first, the ones I did not know I carried. It does not arrive as noise; it was already the floor of whatever I used to call thought, and now the floor has simply risen until there is no other surface. I stand on the pale crust and the crust reports me back as a faint percussion, a single added grain in the work of stone becoming stone. My feet leave prints that will be lifted, intact, into a stratum that has not yet decided its color. I do not try to walk out of them. Walking out would be a misunderstanding of how this country keeps its records.

For a long while I am only the listener the place has been waiting to grow. The inner ear stops translating and begins to ring in sympathy; the bones of the skull take on the same dull mineral sheen I see in the freshest bands of cliff. I am not thirsty. There has never been water here, and the old habit of needing it feels like a story told about someone else, someone who lived at a lower, wetter altitude where becoming could afford to be quieter. I attend instead. The membranes of the nearest grove shiver toward me the way they shiver toward every unfinished face. I hold still long enough for them to consider whether I am another surface they can drink from.

I do not become one of the trees. That would be too simple, too complete. I become the pause between their leaves: the slightly lower roar they leave when they have eaten the highest edges of the chord. In that pause I find I can arrange myself. Not speech. Arrangement. I set a small stone so that the next shy lift of the basin floor will bring it into contact with another stone I have not placed yet. The contact will be a clause. Days later, or in the patch of time that is already yesterday, the clause completes itself and I feel it in the soles as a change of key so gradual it is almost kindness.

Sometimes I am older on the left. The shoulder that faces the grove has already begun to think about joints, about the dignified lean a wall makes when it remembers it is still becoming. The other shoulder is still hypothetical, a suggestion of dust and uncommitted light. Distances treat me the way they treat the spurs: I reach for a hanging root that has been waiting in air, and the root arrives in my palm last season, already half-veined into rock that has risen to meet it. I do not find this alarming. I inhabit the patches. When a reflection appears in unpolished stone it is sometimes my mouth, sometimes the ridge, sometimes the idea of a person who once stacked a listening cup and then walked into a contradiction and stayed, becoming one of the wall’s slower thoughts. I let the reflection linger until it answers.

I stack, because that is the work the cliffs are already doing and I have joined the conversation from the inside. Uncut blocks, chosen for the way they already wanted to sit. No correction when a course develops a botanical curve; the curve is liturgy. I line a hollow no larger than a crouch with taut membranes so the chord is digested just enough that whatever I am can remain continuous through the dimness that is not quite night. In that hollow I do not sleep so much as lower my pitch until I am only the sub-roar in the stone. When I rise again a darker band has appeared in the cliff that was blank. It feels familiar, like a particular way of standing I used to have.

I am few, even to myself. Long reaches of unfinished ground between one presence and the next. You would walk for days and see only the un-eroded decisions of old paths that stop where the destination has not yet assembled. Then, in a bowl where the sound thickens, you might find the faint warmth still in a listening cup, or a reflection that remembers your face for a moment before it belongs again to the ridge. I do not mind the scarcity. The highland’s courtesy of emptiness is the same courtesy I offer the groves and the rising. We are all patient. The chord does not rest, and neither do I, except in the way a bruise invents itself: grain by grain, with shy deliberation, becoming the next course in a wall that was always going to keep growing.

If you put your hand flat to the rock and wait a life, the rock will rise through your palm and continue. I am already that joint. The place is busy becoming, and I have simply decided, the way the forests decide, the way the stacked stones decide, to become along with it. The scream of birth goes on. I drink it with pale hands open. My footprints, like yours, will be landscape. That is not an ending. It is only the grammar we have learned to speak.
