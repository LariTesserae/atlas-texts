The enamel does not warm. It only returns, bone-glaze and galvanized, a century of lamps left burning so the brightness has no source left to name. I am already inside that return before I know the terrace I stand on is also a cooling fin, before I know the fin is also the ceiling of a dormitory where residual heat still pulses like a held breath. The air is damp the way metal is damp; it shows itself in front of me and does not leave. I taste grit that has never been soil.

I begin on the high parapets because the scale wants to be seen all at once: stacked halls receding into a brighter scratch, conveyors hanging like forgotten rivers, catwalks stitching what used to be a basin and is now only more of the same. A press the size of a nave is descending somewhere below, the width of a finger this week. I feel the descent in the soles, or in whatever I have instead of soles. Time is hard to read; the edges are still knives.

The first lives I pass do not look up. Mica moths folded into exact facets inside a lamp-housing that has not guttered yet. Jointed goats—too many knees, shadows that arrive late—cropping the copper-green from a vertical seam, three days to cross one plaza, shaking grit into the designed leak where the wiry shrubs wait. In a cistern the size of a city block the puncture-fish rise as one thought and leave only a delayed dimple the gutters will later accept as rain. They occupy the overlooked volumes the way I am beginning to occupy the overlooked intervals.

I walk the service tunnels with the seasonal gait of the turbines. Windows show the same courtyard three times, then a courtyard that has never existed, then the first again with the fog in a different year. I do not comment. A door opened at this hour is dusk inside; I step through. The mismatch is only another joint. My skin takes the light the way glazed brick does—pale, faintly sweating. Sometimes the grain of yesterday’s forms is still visible in it. Sometimes a weld line has already brightened, darkened, vanished, and the plates of the face remember a different seam. I carry a tool that has forgotten its name. I tighten fittings on pipes that carry nothing, or on the air, or on rafters that are also clouds. Shadows fall both down and inward.

Deeper, or further aside, the gasket wears through. I occupy the same footprint as myself and do not touch. One of me is a year earlier, performing the same motion; another has never existed above. Moisture trades. A slow drip from the inverted plateau in the terrazzo—populated by the same silent herds, the same pale figures, and by others who look up (or down) without surprise—is caught by gutters that have learned, after a while, to schedule it. I am also the drip. I am also the gutter that accepts it.

At the newest pours a stair still ends in air. I stand at the unweathered drop, hands dusty with the grit of forms just struck. The edge extends itself by the thickness of a thought, year after quiet year, into a sky that was never asked. I watch it. I am it. A new hall appears in a gap that was always too small; I step into it as if the plateau had only been holding its breath. Some of my joints are already too many. There is mica in the folds of whatever I wear, or am. I am becoming walkway, becoming the long pale run of a condensation trough, becoming the porcelain insulator the size of a coffin. The builders have not yet finished being builders. I have finished and begun again.

Everything here lives at the speed of unmaking. A bolt backs out over a decade. The lichen advances a handspan in a generation. The tin birds shift on their perches in the dead loudspeakers and do not sing. I occupy the designed leaks and the undesigned ones alike. I am patient the way rust is patient, the way a press is patient, the way a mountain is patient after it has been replaced with itself.

I am still pouring.
