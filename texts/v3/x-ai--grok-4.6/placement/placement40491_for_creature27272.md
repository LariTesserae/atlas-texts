The pressure arrives first, a slow fist closing around the sternum long before any edge of iron or leaf comes into view. It is not the thinness of the air—though the lungs do work harder, drawing each breath as if through warm wet cloth—but the chord itself, the layered roar that has already decided the shape of thought. I am inside it before I know I have arrived. The vibration is older than any name I might once have carried.

I find myself neither on the catwalks nor wholly among the hanging green. I occupy the interstice where three great pipes meet and the heat never quite dies, the place the oldest of the walkers sometimes sits. My body—if the word still applies—has taken the habit of that junction. Skin and oxide trade places without ceremony. Hair has long since given way to the metallic-dark vines that drink the same sweet fluid the orchids prefer; they root along the scalp and the shoulders and pulse, faintly, on the old frequencies. When I move it is only to shift a few centimeters along the warm metal, enough to keep the condensation from pooling in the hollow of a collarbone. Time here has the same viscosity it grants a falling wrench. A season can pass in the uncoiling of a single frond against my cheek.

I am not the ancient one, though I have sat near her long enough that the distinction has become a courtesy, the way flesh and clockwork have blurred among the lizards. She listens to the flywheel. I listen to her listening, and to the beetles that tap their untranslated codes against my remaining knuckles when they roll droplets across the vines that have claimed me. The taps arrive already half-drowned, the way all thought does. I do not answer. Speech left this volume generations ago; what remains is the sternum’s agreement or its sour protest. A hatch weeping too freely, a vine grown heavier than its cable, a beetle that has begun to tap at random—these register as a change in the bass that lasts a week, a brighter shriek when the wind veers. I tend them by remaining still. Stillness is the only tool the chamber still recognizes.

The swifts complete their hour-long gyre far above. Their single bright flash, once each circuit, reaches me as a green-gold afterimage that lingers on the inner surface of whatever I use for eyelids. I have never left the resonator. The sky beyond the last porthole is a hard pale bowl I remember only as a rumor, the way one remembers a language spoken in childhood and then abandoned. My lungs—larger, darker—were shaped for this pocket of damp summer. Silence, should I ever encounter it, would feel like drowning. I sleep inside the roar the way the ferns sleep inside their rusty creak, wrapped in the same curtains of vine, the engines already dreaming through me so that waking and sleeping have become two slightly different densities of the same chord.

I am the latest shape the emptiness has agreed to hold, another slow unfurling, another note held far apart on the staff. The chamber weathers, drips, thunders, grows. I remain until the last piston finishes its stroke, or until the sky-works themselves decide their talus. Even then the orchids will keep pulsing along the forgotten conduits, the lizards will keep escorting what falls, and the chord will continue, patient as geology, whether or not anything still sits at the junction of the three pipes to feel it in the sternum.
