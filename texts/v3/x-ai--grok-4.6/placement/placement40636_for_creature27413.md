The tilt reaches me before any name does. It lives in the knees, a slow argument the stone has not finished having with itself, so that even standing still I am slightly downhill from one moment and slightly uphill from the next. Water beads on the inside of my wrists where the wool never quite seals. The air wrings itself against every underside; I taste mineral and leaf-rot and the faint metallic sweetness that means the rock is still new enough to remember heat.

I am not yet certain which court this is. The flags glare softly even under moving cloud. A gutter changes pitch somewhere to my left and the whole volume answers, a long braided note that is neither rain nor roar. I tilt my head the way one of them would, the way the children learn early, listening for whether the water has decided to climb a handspan along its hairline crack. It has. The film rides the stone’s unfinished breath. I do not comment. The gutter was adzed to catch it either way.

My hands are large and lime-whitened at the creases. A bronze chisel rests against a block I have not yet decided to free. Six would be a day’s honest work, but the face has begun to ring wrong, a seam weeping harder than patience allows. I leave the tool. This is not failure. Walls rise the way ridges rise. I plant my palm flat to the pale stone as if taking its temperature, feeling the almost-imperceptible lift that will, in a life, ask for another wedge under the nearest sill.

The scaffolding beside me is already mossed. I remember lashing it, or someone like me did; the poles have the same dark sheen as the leather at my shoulders. Aerial roots of a stitching-tree hang close, trained into the next channel down. I have spent mornings directing them, not as gardener but as kin who understands the same problem: the col is still deciding, and we sew ourselves to its indecision so the rooms do not crack when the decision finally comes. Or does not come.

I am one of the few who occupy the vacant volume the way a single bench occupies a hall. Not a crowd. A quality of attention. Sometimes I stand in the exact center of the main court after a gust simply to hear it return, smaller, from a direction that is not the wind’s. The emptiness is a second architecture; I treat it as such. Children run the echoes here at the dimmer end of day, setting one pebble rolling so its clatter outlives itself. I watch them the way I watch the pale salamander in the cistern whose water has stood a day: without ceremony, as civic fact.

At dusk the hoofed thing comes to the outermost spring. Prints fill before anyone speaks of them. I let it. Fire is only for the small pot of tea and the lime that never quite dries. Breath shows. I dress for the spring that will not commit. When the lamps are few and fat, the water-noise loses its sparkle and becomes a single thick presence above me, inside the walls, under the floor. I sleep to it, or I lie longer, feeling in the joints the mountain’s unfinished tense, the same tense I live in.

I have no name that stays still. Wind and water pronounce the place too loudly for anyone to hear it the same way twice, and I do not mind. I am the density the stone and the leaf can currently bear: present, patient, still deciding along with the high saddle whether this will be pass or basin. The rooms I help raise are for a population that has not arrived, or that already left, or that is arriving so slowly the stone will be old first. Fullness would be a crowding the new land has not earned. I leave gaps. I use wood where stone would argue. I am the mountain’s unfinishedness, given a body that weeps at the seams, given gutters, given the slow permission to keep becoming.
