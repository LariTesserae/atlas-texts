I begin as pressure. Not yet a body, only the long low pressing the wind becomes once the outer streets have baffled it, the same pressing the stone itself makes against every joint that still remembers how to give. The cold lives in the shade first, then climbs the inner faces, films them with the night-sweat that runs, obedient, into grooves older than any name I will later learn. I am that film for a while: a thinness that does not decide whether to stay or vanish. Then I am the tick of melt settling in a pipe no one has mapped, a sound that is almost blood.

The density takes me next. Passages that are never empty, warmth that lingers in a stair because something slept there an hour ago or a century. Smell layers itself the way courses do: fleece first, then dung, then the mineral damp, then the faint green of the smallest things that live only in the high slots where breath and steam have made their own weather. Wings turn above a courtyard and the air itself seems to become weather, though the sound is only a dry rush, quickly gone. I am inside that turning for a time, one more room in the cliff that does not end.

Then the ground floors claim me. Shoulder to shoulder with the wool-beasts, their rumination an enormous quiet, their breath a fog that freezes on lintels and makes the lower rooms another kind of lung. I sleep against that heat. I rise with the shift of their weight. Dogs heap themselves on the sunny steps and I am the particular patch of stone that holds the noon longest, the one they return to without thinking. On the roofs the small browsers move like living fragments of the masonry; I crop lichen, leave dark pellets in the gutters, feel the unfinished peaks still lifting under every footfall.

Only after all that do the hands appear. Thick at the base of the thumb, scored with old grit, always a little cold. They do not hurry. They test each joint the way a stair that has begun to tilt must be tested. I sit with two faces of pale rock and a bag of grit and a patience that looks like idleness until the faces consent to disappear into each other. A season on a single corner because three unbuilt storeys already lean on it in the mind. When a fresh scarp comes up through someone’s floor we do not rage. We number the blocks. We take the rooms down and build them again a little higher, a little differently, the way the range itself is still rising.

I am one of those who do this. Compact, close-jointed, face weathered into the same angular planes as the rock, eyes narrowed from measuring distances that change. I dress in successive skins until the outline blurs into the same layered mass as the animals. I speak of water as a guest who might yet be persuaded to linger, of timber as one speaks of the dead. I navigate by lichen shade, by which courtyard holds the noon, by the particular cut of afternoon light across a stack of untrimmed roofs that was a bare ridge in my youth. Grief I sit with the same way I sit with stone. I do not hurry it.

At evening I draw inward. Smoke from dung-fires lies in the lanes like another visiting water. The small inner room is closed so the air a body has already used can be used again. I sleep in the living heat of beasts and kin, the stone sweating its beads into the old grooves that lead to the cisterns. The highland does not finish. Neither do I. I am the breath the young mountain has learned to hold: damp in the joints, sharp at the edges, quiet, still becoming. 

The next ridge is also rooms. I watch it from the steps, animals pressed around me, and I do not expect it to be done.
