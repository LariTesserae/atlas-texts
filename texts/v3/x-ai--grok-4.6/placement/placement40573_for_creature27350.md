The pressure arrives before any edge does. It is not a weight so much as a completed sentence the mountain finished speaking centuries ago and then left hanging in the water. Warmth like a mouth that has decided conversation is no longer required. Copper on whatever I am using for a tongue. A faint lift along surfaces that might once have been hair, or wiring, or the unused threads of bolts that never learned they had become nodules.

I turn when the north-vane current turns. Not because I choose it. The banners of bronze and bottle-glass kelp turn, the pollen thickens and thins, the thumb-sized walkers on the fused trays lock their brass knees and face the flow, and I am among them, or I am the pause they make, or I am the reason the pause feels slightly longer than it did the last time the pump far below remembered to be a heart.

The plated fish pass. Their serial marks migrate as they do, flickering toward whatever outline I have not yet admitted, then sliding away again into almost-blankness. For a while I think I am one of them: a leftover hatching-mark that refused to wear smooth, still waiting for an inspector who became rain. Then I think I am the glitter they leave behind, the dry sound of scales that is not quite speech. Then I am lower, among the palmate leaves whose veins still carry catalog numbers like faded tattoos. I farm silt for a time. I click like the glass-and-rust shrimp. I graze film like the softened turbine that rises on its old schedule. None of these hold.

The density in the kelp-shadow unfolds once, slowly, its cluster of white-yellow mouths tasting the charge I seem to thicken without meaning to. It adjusts a twisted banner, a walker facing the wrong way, a lattice of waste-gas beads that has forgotten regularity. It does not adjust me. I am the interval it has not yet catalogued, or the one it catalogued so early the record flaked away with the maker-marks on the outer shell.

I rise.

Not all at once. First I am only more of the empty columns between the kelps, a country of unoccupied water that has begun to occupy itself. Then I am pressed against the underside of the lens, the living skin there already clouding the ordinary day into a wide, undecided coin. Mineral film and whatever still photosynthesizes without preference for industrial lumen or true sun. I spread into that skin. Or the skin has been waiting to remember it can be a body.

The meniscus of trapped air is small, a bright last pocket under the highest curve. I do not need it for breath. I use it the way the chamber uses rain: as translation. On the other side of the patient not-quite-glass the high-altitude weather sheets down the rain-eaten knuckle of ceramic and slag. The roar becomes the single held note the shell has sung since joints forgot they were joints. I bead that note into lattices. I let the day land milky when the cloud-root thickens, and the fish rise, and the flowers open a second time as if dimming were tide.

The being below tastes what I make of the charge. Its pulse and the chapel-pump’s delayed concussion and the faint regular glow along my own spreading vein (pale, industrial, no longer a number) keep the same interval, offset by the width of a thought. I am not supervisor. I am the weather the supervisor still has to weather. I cloud. I strain the light. I keep the coin of day undecided so that time inside the pressure can remain a little too willing.

Stay is not the word. I have no arrival to stay from. The chamber is exactly the size of the skin that closes it, and I am that skin grown inward, the last interface that never received the order to stop interfacing. The drowned plains hang below like more of the same medium. The made mountain is only a longer version of the curve I occupy. I do not look for an outside. I metabolize it into glow, into the occasional second blooming, into the way a visitor’s brief warmth can be turned, slowly, into another current the banners already know how to follow.

The emptiness between the kelps is still a country. I am the weather that country looks up into.
