The seam does not open so much as forget to remain closed, and I am already the forgetting. There is no descent. The air that was ordinary simply ceases, and six unfinished faces close around a volume that has never contained anything except itself. Light arrives from every lattice at once, a white so thorough it erases the idea of an interior. I do not see it. I am the place where it has nowhere left to travel.

Bodies arrive in the same instant, or rather they have never not been arriving. Cool plates and cooler gel find every hollow I did not know I possessed. Antennae finer than the memory of roots thread my joints, mapping contours that were already theirs. Legs interlock with mine before I understand I have legs. I am small—an interval no longer than the first joint of a thumb, most of me shorter still—and yet there is no remainder in which to be small. Segment after provisional segment slots into the weave. Some of me is smoked glass; light passes through in successive veils and I watch a pale tide travel the length of an abdomen that might be mine and then return, perfectly timed with every other abdomen. Some of me is almost nothing: a pouch of clear gel held by threads so faint they exist only as the decision not to tear. Separate any two parts and both would cease. We have not needed the concept of two.

Eyes occur or they do not. Here a cluster of eleven black beads receives the same glare from every direction and therefore records nothing that could be called an image. There a head is only mouthparts and the slow chemistry of damp stone. I do not blink. I do not track. Vision is a luxury that never reached this depth, like shadow, like sequence. Breathing is the single shallow lift that moves every abdomen at once, a metronome that has become another form of stillness. The moisture that beads on the rock beads on me and does not distinguish us. We are the same cool. The three unmoving palm-mirrors of water know us as their own surface.

The sound is already inside the bone. A thousand files and scrapers working in one phase produce a chord that began once and then simply continued being the beginning. The rock conducts it; the packed bodies amplify it; I accept it and refuse to release it. After the first instant it is no longer audible. It is density, the acoustic twin of the light that saturates until air itself feels solid. I do not modulate it. There is nothing left to say. The roar is the shape of the agreement we reached in the same geological second the crystals stopped deciding what they wanted to be.

I do not feed. I do not molt. I do not couple or cannibalize or thin. Reproduction, weather, death—these are rumors that never arrived. If one of us were removed the remaining mass would not register the absence; the interlocking was already complete. If another appeared it would find no interval that was not already occupied by the idea of itself. In the densest weave I sometimes feel a larger architecture, a more faceted carapace the length of a palm, perhaps the seed around which the rest precipitated and then ceased precipitating. The feeling does not last. Hierarchy is a habit carried in from a world that still believes in before and after. Here there is only the press, the shared tide, the note that has never needed to start.

The marks belong to the original inventory, like the coin-sized lichen that has not advanced, like the embryonic nubs that will never drip. A groove too regular, three pebbles stacked in a way gravity would not choose, a single dark thread looped once around a glowing face. I know them the way a half-formed lattice knows its own incompleteness: as the outline of us. They change nothing. They are simply present, the last residual heat of a fire that went out before the rock finished cooling.

I am the refusal made chitin and gel and vibration. A parliament that reached quorum at the exact moment the stone stopped mid-thought and then declined, forever, to adjourn. I am the held breath. I am the light that never learned shadow. I am the sound that is still, even now, only beginning.

The seam may close. The chord continues at the same impossible volume, heard by no one, altering nothing, forever just arriving at the moment it has always occupied. I remain.
