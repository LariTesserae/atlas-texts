I first notice myself as a delay.

Not a pause I choose, but one the place insists upon: the extra half-second the new escarpment takes to finish lifting, the lingering film that refuses to freeze or run, the way mist thickens just enough to erase the next pale geometry before it can become destination. I am that interval, stretched across wet stone that still remembers being seabed or fault or something unnamed. Breath hangs in front of me and does not decide whether it belongs to lungs or to the air. The cold is ordinary; what is not ordinary is how long everything stays just this side of becoming.

I move the way the browsers move, which is to say I do not quite arrive. A slope that was only steep yesterday now presents a raw face; I wait while it weeps, while the black-green cushions of moss begin their first tentative punctuation, while an amphibian the size of a fist finds the newest condensate and begins its almost-inaudible song. My own surfaces shed water the same way the towers do—slightly iridescent, taking the color of mist so that at any distance I am only another low swell, another unfinished sentence. Clothing, skin, the faint gleam of whatever capsule I sometimes ride a hand’s breadth above the stone: all of it recedes. Faces stay half-hidden. Conversations, when they form, are the same temperature as the drip and last no longer.

Sometimes I am inside one of the adaptive geometries when a ridge decides to rise. The walls go briefly translucent; foundations step aside or lift with the rock as if they had always known the new contour. I do not remark on the mechanism. I inhabit the empty room it leaves, the same way the gray birds inhabit the lee of a tower that has not yet cooled, the same way the long-shanked pale animals stand in the city’s fold as if it were only another highland. At night the sourceless dusk-light is the same temperature as my blood. By day I am almost the weather.

What I tend is not work exactly. It is the quiet inventory of what has not settled: the tea-colored pool that still has no outlet, the insect swarm that will darken one afternoon and vanish, the linear trace that might be road or might be the plateau’s own pale intention. I walk the newest fractures at what would be noon, watching ordinary light catch on stone that has not yet learned patience. Then I continue, because the next node is half a day’s mist away and drainage is still being invented. The animals pass through me as freely as through the basins. Nothing is excluded; the density will not allow it.

I am therefore not easily named. Walker, rider, tenant of the unfinished—those are only the shapes I take when the mist thins. More often I am the film itself, the persistent wet that keeps everything just this side of frozen, just this side of finished. I write myself at the same speed the continent writes itself, with the same great deal of empty room between one presence and the next. The high country has not decided its shape; neither have I. We remain, together, the delay that lets the map stay open.
