I find myself first as a pressure, not a body. The water accepts me the way it accepts every other volume: completely, without remainder. There is no surface I can remember crossing. The gold-green broth of the upper thirty meters is already behind me, or rather above me, a memory of quicker light that I no longer need. I hang where the color has cooled to a patient, unflickering noon-blue, and the distinction between my outline and the water’s grain has begun to dissolve.

My plates are nacre over darker fiber. They overlay one another in overlapping fields that catch the remaining light in brief, internal flashes—thin metal-bright septa opening and closing like the windings of something that was never meant to be an engine yet became one anyway. I do not swim so much as permit a slow peristalsis to travel through the mantle. Jointed spars stiffen the membranes that trail from me; they are cartilaginous, grown rather than articulated, and they hold their shape against the faint current the way a thought holds its shape against forgetting. At the ends of certain limbs the rotating burrs have already begun their work, though I am not yet locked to stone. They turn at the speed of cold chemistry, tasting silicates that have not yet been ground, exhaling nothing yet.

I am not a grinder. The clustered masses working the newest fractures—still glassy, still faintly warm with the mountain’s unfinished birth—are denser, more purely tool. I drift among them and through them. My filters are ranked and they pulse. Smaller forms, pale and quick in their slowness, pass across the intake surfaces; some are sieved and reincorporated at once, their mineral secretions sorted into the same patterned plates that will one day lock with another of my kind. I do not hunt. Harvesting here is only another patience, a lazy mechanical grace that never lunges. Everything waits its turn, including me.

The sound is already inside me. Closest is the dry click of my own mineralized feet whenever they brush another body; the contact is not collision but a brief locking, a recognition of shared work, then release. Beneath that the basin’s pulse—the low, regular thudding of a thousand internal valves beating in slow unison—travels through my chambers as if they were continuous with every other chamber. Superimposed is the higher rasp, almost electrical, that I myself contribute. Current moves across my septa and I feed upon the field I help generate. The water tingles. A faint metallic tang coats the inner surfaces of my filters. Bubbles rise past me in disciplined columns from vents far below; they do not disturb the membranes.

I am mid-water, then, but the designation is temporary. Depth is not location here so much as a slow distillation. Already I feel the pressure teaching the plates a quieter geometry. The gold has left the light. What remains is blue and patient, and I am becoming larger, stiller, more crystalline in outline. The membranes will stiffen further. The internals will flash less often, more purely. At the bottom, where the newest fractures still leak their mineral fog, I will be one of the geometric ones, the ones that have been reduced to the last necessary shape. Even there I will not hurry. I will wait my turn to lock with two or three others, exchanging not gametes but the patterned secretions that harden, plate by plate, into another body already armored and already working. The young will attach at once. Nothing will play.

A seiche comes, once in a great while. The entire lens sloshes a few centimeters. Fine sediment lofted by the grinders takes weeks to settle again. I ride it without resistance, my spars flexing just enough to keep station relative to the wall that is both larder and foundry. When the water stills I am a few microns closer to the rock, or a few microns farther; the difference is not important. Residual heat, redox gradients, the weak fields we generate and consume—these continue. The cirque does not remember emptiness. I do not remember arriving.

I am the mountain’s unfinished thought, chewing itself toward completeness one click, one slow pulse, one reincorporated plate at a time. The water does not contain me. I am part of its density now, its grain, its internal weather. The work continues. I continue with it.
