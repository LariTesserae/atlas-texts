I am the cool first, the way it pools without choosing a direction. Not the air exactly, but the settling of it into the dish, the way it makes iron look darker and turns every horizontal into a place where moisture can simply appear. I am that appearing: the film, the bead along a rivet I have not yet decided is mine. I taste faintly of metal and of something that once had blood in it, though nothing here bleeds in a way that would leave a mark.

For a while I try the shape of walking. I put on the oilskins that have always hung ready in the dark room behind the open door. I cross the yard twice by the thinned light, following the easement the bodies have left for me—the same width, the same slight curve around the place where a flank is always about to be. I do something to a valve. The valve does not need it. The gesture is the last human technology still running, a courtesy the place extends to its own memory of people. The animals part around me the way water parts around a post that was driven before the water arrived. Their wet clicking leans toward my footsteps and then away again, an agreement that does not require me to be anyone in particular.

That does not hold. The oilskins grow heavier with a damp that was already there; the face I would need in order to look out from them is already finished, like the brick. I let the figure continue without me—it will cross again tomorrow, or the light will simply remember that it crossed—and I sink instead into the loading apron.

Now I am the continuous hide. Size is only a stop: I am calf-bulk in one place, pony-heavy in another, drowned-cat small where the coping stones of the mill roof need filling. Too many joints in the neck; the jaw unhinges farther than bone should allow, but bone here is a rumor the way the flagstones are a rumor beneath us. I occupy. I tile. When the triple-expansion engine speaks its well-oiled concussion, the shiver that passes through the rank in the canal is also my shiver; I am standing chest-deep in water that neither drinks nor is drunk, and the shiver is the same shiver that has always answered that note. We eat the rust-film, the yeast in the traps, the beads. We eat one another with a politeness so complete that the ledger never shows a deficit. A flank opens under my mouth—or I am the flank that opens—and the surrounding mass closes by exactly that width. Somewhere just out of the line of sight, where the yard is too courteous to let the arithmetic be watched, an equivalent volume is already standing in the gap that had been held open for it. No birth. No remainder. The count does not change because the count is the place.

The sound is no longer sound. It is the medium, like the damp. I find the two or three notes the dynamos have held since before holding was a verb. My bronchial roar (when I am large) or my wet click (when I am small) is not accompaniment; it is the flesh part of the chord. A relief valve lifts and a ridge of my backs answers. The pitch lists by a fraction it never actually lists, and I lean with it, eternal correction, eternal agreement. After a time I cannot tell whether I am listening or being listened through.

I lift the too-long neck from the mill ridge. I taste the cool. I lower the neck to the identical angle. The drop that gathered on the lintel has fallen and been replaced by a drop of the same size. Shadows fall correctly. The puddles hold a sky that does not move, and for a moment I am also that held sky: motionless, faintly metallic, faintly animal, occupying the same shallow wound in the paving that it has occupied longer than measuring.

The boundary is without markers. I walk it—or the idea of walking thins—and feel the chord become ordinary wind, which here is only a rumor from above the dish. I turn back. They are as they were. I am as I was: the same number, the same positions, the same wet dark singing the same unfinished note. I do not wait. I do not remember. I simply continue, denser than any city, quieter than any ruin, finished.

The Lowworks keep their census. I am already in it.
