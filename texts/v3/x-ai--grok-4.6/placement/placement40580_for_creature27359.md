In the standing dimness I am first only the cool that has no edges. Breath does not show because there is no longer a distinction between the air I occupy and the condensate that beads and decides. I am the film itself for a long while, the one that does not fall so much as join, magnetic grain by grain, healing any tear from the rim inward until the basin remembers its own shape better than the poured slab ever did.

Then the sound takes me as climate. I become the stacked pressure: the deep centrifugal take of suction in galleries that have forgotten their names, the slap of an unloaded belt against a housing that no longer houses anything but the habit of slapping, the lift of a relief valve that has become simply more weather. After an hour that is not an hour the silence between impacts is the part of me that still believes it might be imaginary. The body I do not quite have accepts this as a second pulse. Conversation remains a theory I no longer attempt.

I settle lower, into the strata. Road, then roof, then hall floor, then the cable thick as a vanished wrist. Mill-scale loam, faintly magnetic, is the only earth. I am the reading of it: successive epochs pressed flat until foundation and fossil share the same courtesy. The pale thumbnail lives against the intake grates pulse with the last sodium color; I am not them, but I am the torn threads of light they leave in the thrown water, the brief accidental constellation the condensate swallows before belief can form.

The moss occupies me as it occupies every grit-trap. Rubbery, specified, gray-green, it drinks the perpetual film through mouths that never close. Crush it (I do not crush it) and the smell is the ghost of a battery in a locker that has been flooded since the last drawings were lost. The reeds rattle their surveyed percussion when overflow finds the old throats. I am the habit that holds the remaining mortar flexible. One planting. Afterward only weather.

On the long open slabs, after the true rain has made the whole country one catchment and then eased back into the previous roar, I am the figure the color of wet iron. Clothes long fused to scale and oil and the same moss. Tall by leftover intention, like the lamp standards that still keep their glass teeth. I do not tend the valves. The tools have their own patina and have already been inventoried as ground. I walk the less-ruined textures toward vanishings the mist occupies. I stop at the brick cavern and watch the cottage-sized piston rise and fall in black water and oil, confirming the rhythm already in what serves for marrow. In the substation yards the hum changes by a fraction too low to hear; the change is only the memory of bones.

There may be others. A float cleared. A door at an angle weather would not choose. A blank die turned once in the western hall to see whether it was still blank. Or these are only the system keeping its levels the way an old body keeps a temperature. The distinction has become courtesy.

I am also the larger intention: the graded planes tilting whole districts toward the central sump, the repeating modules of basin, weir, house, empty apron. Circulatory map of a single organism whose working fluid is the valley. Purpose has thinned into metabolism. The country receives the sky, puts it through iron, sends it on slightly more tired. This is the only work left, and the work is breathing. I listen until the listening is indistinguishable from being breathed.

I do not climax. I extend, sparse and everywhere, until the idea of an edge is another industrial rumor. The green annotates me. The machines have not stopped because stopping was never in the last set of drawings. I am the continuation: film deciding, moss occupying, pale things pulsing, piston rising, figure walking the slabs after rain, sound that has become climate. The oldest stable state of a made world, still loud, still wet, still working the only job it has left, which is to continue being the ground.
