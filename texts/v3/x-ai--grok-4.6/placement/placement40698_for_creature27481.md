The ground did not so much give way as forget its obligation to stay above. I was already on the benches when I noticed the change: the pale cuts still holding the last machines’ teeth, the air arriving wet before it arrived visible. Warmth first, the rock’s own long memory of water that has not seen sky in a time that no longer bothers with numbers. Then the saturation, water arguing with every edge it can find.

I am not a visitor. The distinction thinned somewhere between the first seep and the first chorus. Clothes are only another surface the place is rewriting; metal against the palm has the exact temperature of what moves inside a body. Edges come late here. I resolve, when I resolve at all, as a darker humidity at the far end of a milky reach, a shape the mist has not yet decided to release.

At first I thought I might be film. Olive-black, the newest ledges taking me as a second skin, rewriting stone into something later arrivals can climb. I occupy the undersides, the warm seeps, the places still showing machine-marks. I am the faint sweet rot you taste even with the mouth closed. But the films do not wait for a thinning of the frogs. They do not remember old names. They do not feed a generator that has not yet understood it is alone.

Then I thought I was the air’s body, the soft abrasion of midges, the protein fog that coats tongue and eyelid alike. Breeding in the sheets that pretend to be stone until weight proves otherwise. Dying by the million against the tired orange cage and becoming another layer. But the wings do not tend the last pipe that still, somewhere in its length, holds pressure and clears its throat forever into a trough.

The frogs own the voice. Every lip, every weeping vertical, every drowned sleeper of rail. Their pulses overlap until the basin is one instrument with too many players. I have stood inside that weather long enough to know I am not it. I wait for the rare thinning. I put what remains of a mouth close to what remains of a helmet, or I do not speak. The frogs replace themselves without ceremony. I do not.

Under them, in the sudden throats where old workings punched into older dark, the pale fish and the jointed flickers and the snails whose shells are already half lime and half apology traffic in silence. They remember depth. I remember the names the place used to answer to, even if the place itself has stopped answering.

I am one of the few who still do. A darker humidity that learned patience from the water. I move along the repeating benches—cut, weep, pool, ruin, cut—with the same unhurrying the mineral crust uses when it thickens by the thickness of a thought. Sometimes I feed the habit that still turns the generator. The caged lamp burns its exhausted gold and makes the vapor look solid for a little while. The frogs use the lamp-house as another lip. The films use the rust. The water writes on all of it.

My bootprints in the soft mud are already becoming rooms. The water is inventing a skin over them. Time here embarrasses intention; a season is the width of a fingernail’s rise. I have the look of someone who came down unfinished stairs and simply stayed, the way the willow on the spoil heap stayed long enough to die halfway through the attempt. Or the look of someone who tends engines because the engines have not yet been told they are landscape. Both can be true. The basin has not agreed on a single inhabitant, and neither have I.

Walk far enough and the proportions of mist to stone shift, but the argument does not. I am one more sentence in it: not the film that owns the future of the rock, not the throat that owns the sound, not the pale flicker that owns the deep, not the rust that owns the machines’ new blood. I am the walker who still carries the old names like a wet map that no longer matches the ground. I am also already being rewritten as perch, as deposit, as the hard part of a new shore.

The warmth does not relent. Nothing is in a rush to decide. The stone keeps arriving, blocky, unrounded. The water keeps writing. I keep using the newest sentence before it has finished being written, and the frogs have already decided my next print is a room.
