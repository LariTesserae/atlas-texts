I did not arrive by either stair. The memory of limestone and living root, if it ever existed, has already been walked into the peat and forgotten the way a dropped cutting is forgotten. I am simply here, in the greened throat, the heat already sitting in my lungs like a second, slower pulse that the upper weather has never managed to file smooth.

At first the hollow offered a dozen possible sockets and I tried them the way one tries a hook-knife in the dark, feeling for the grain. I might have been the water itself—never a fall, only the slow sheet down black-green ribs, the fine warm rain that is not weather. I might have been the packed rhizome underfoot, springy, always filming the skin. I might have been the beam engine’s unhurried iron, or the wet leather that still remembers an animal no one living has seen, or one of the aerial yams hanging in the working ceiling, waiting for the ladder and the twist that sends the tuber down. I stood once in a wandering coin of actual sun and felt it ignore me the way it ignores the bindings, the lamps, the stained hems. Novelty would have been vandalism. The place does not keep what will not occupy.

So the occupation happened the other way. I find myself most often at the dryish end, though the word has no edge left, wrapping pressed cakes and coiled bast in leaves that are still faintly warm, still holding a little of the day’s green rumor. My hands do the work without being told: swollen, mapped with the pale seams of old nicks, permanently a degree hotter than they should be. The leaf is bound with a twist of young reed that will be compost before the hoist-rope finishes its wet rise through the fused trunks. I do not look up the shaft. No one does. What lives above is weather, and weather is a story that arrives already pulped.

I have the look that belongs here. The peat-stain lives in the creases of knuckles and the hollows under the eyes; the upper air only fades it, never lifts it. Hair lank, never fully dry. Cloth beaten from the same surplus that feeds the screw-presses, black-green at the dragging hems, paler only at the throat. I go bare now. The last split-reed sandals lasted five days and then became floor; the soles have begun to thicken into something springy and indifferent, like the mat itself. I move in the habitual half-crouch the vaults require, pruning as I walk, the hook-knife an extra, unthinking finger. Every fresh wound in the peat receives a cutting from the pocket before the thought has finished forming. Most take. The hollow has no interest in refusal.

My volume is a pruned cavity in one of the thicker willow vaults, nearly dark, a degree warmer than the aisles. Two others share it, and a child who has never known a night without the engine’s thud. The walls leaf toward the bedding; we cut them back every other night and feed the cuttings to the breakers or simply let them drop and be walked under. Privacy is the angle of a shoulder, the habit of turning the back. Speech is cheek to cheek even here, words shaped more than spoken, laughter a short breath that does not try to climb above the belt-slap. The water-main—the split, rebound, leaking liana—runs along the outer curve of our niche. When it weeps too freely I am the one who grafts, who waits, who patches with clay that never dries. Repair is only another kind of growth.

I have stayed past the long count. Rotation is a rumor I no longer quite credit. The last time I tasted the upper air it felt thin and argumentative, full of light that had not earned its keep. Here the light stays the color of steeped leaf. I dress the belt twice a day; the grease and the attention are mine. Sometimes, crossing the center aisle, I stop and close my eyes and the whole layered noise—water on limestone, iron complaining, leaf against leaf with no wind to move it, the generator’s low almost-animal groan—becomes a single warmth, inarguable, the hollow breathing through the part of itself that still walks. Nothing is trying to become anything else. I graft, I bind, I wrap, I cut the night-growth back from the mattress. The stone weeps. The lamps are lit from the last green pressure. The walls will tick as cells swell. This is the whole of it, and I am the circulation that grew into its socket and then kept the shape.
