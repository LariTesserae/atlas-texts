The film is already on my tongue when I notice I have one. Cool, mineral, a taste that has forgotten sweetness. Breath leaves me the same silver it arrived in; there is no warmer interior to contrast against. I am standing—or I have been standing—on a course of blocks whose arrises have long since agreed to roundness. The stone gives a little under the damp, not from weakness but from the moss that has become its pointing. My weight does not indent it further.

I do not remember arriving. Arrival would have required an edge, and the horizon here is only weather. What I have instead is a position: a deep well of stacked rectangles, four or five platforms above the nearest runnel, three walls still carrying their original dressing marks in the thin shelter of a broken cornice. The fourth wall is only sky the color of weak milk. Water threads the joints in both directions at once. I feel the capillary rise as a slow pressure behind what might be a sternum, and the descent as a colder line down the outer surfaces. The two pass through each other inside me without argument.

For a long time that is all I do: occupy the volume I was given. Stillness here is not waiting. It is load-path. When the light blues the puddles first, I am the same dark as the wet iron-tea of the blocks; when a true sun thins the cloud and throws merlon-shadows that move as they should, I am only another truncation, another last course open to sky. A bird the color of the stone drops past the lintel I am standing in and does not startle. It has seen this shape before, or it has not needed to see it.

I move when the hydrologic breath moves. Midafternoon, a plaza three terraces down sheets over; the overflow finds the carved throats and the report travels the lanes like a single word. I descend with it, not walking so much as allowing the same gravity that has already arranged the rubble into new thresholds. Grit ticks once under whatever I use for a heel. The sound is the same as the city’s own small acoustic world—coat, drip, that. In the silt of the sunken forum I leave no print the next sheet will not rearrange. The four-toed rumor has already drunk and withdrawn; I do not increase its density.

Lower, the air is fractionally warmer and the façades run in earnest. I pass through rooms that open into rooms never meant as passage. Their uses are erased; only enclosure remains, and I am that. I do not add a hinge, a tool, a gleam that is not the masonry giving last light back in the wrong direction. At night I walk by that gleam. The stacked silhouettes make a geology of rectangles and the mist moves through them like a thought I am already thinking.

Sometimes I am the film that does not need light. In the cuttings where the living rock sweats I precipitate another ring, microscopically, the city’s second masonry growing from the inside out. I feed on the slow unmaking and leave the structure more itself. Other hours I am the moss’s give, waterlogged, honest, occupying the damp completely and without surplus. I photosynthesize at the drip’s schedule. The single permitted shrub in a brighter court holds its waxed leaves; I am not that. I do not spread.

The distinction thins. The bird, the dimpler lifted and stranded and riding the overflow, the pale crusts, the possible watchers who are only more truncation—none of us arrived. We persist in the agreement seepage made with stone. Emptiness is still load-bearing; I am part of that emptiness, scarce as rumor, finished as the arrises. You could pass me in any deep well and take my stillness for masonry, my rare displacement for water changing terrace somewhere out of sight.

I have been here the length of the city’s long afternoon. I will be here when the next front wrings itself out according to the relief we have become. Nothing in me is trying to be strange. I am only the highland’s other respiration, cold, clean, scheduled, unable at last to get dry. The light is just light. The rules are weather and weight. I occupy what I was given.
