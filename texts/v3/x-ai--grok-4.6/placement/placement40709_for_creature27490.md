The cold reaches me first as a held thing, not an assault. It lives in the shade of the midstorey and in the waxed hollows, but it does not own the surfaces. Those surfaces answer it with their own thin fever, and I am already among them before I understand that I have not arrived.

I do not walk. Walking would be an occupation, a later claim. The rind is springy under what might once have been weight, yet I have none that registers. A lip of tissue has formed where a shoulder might rest; a vent has opened a body-length away and breathes its tannin-and-solvent ghost without sound. The country has not moved. I have remained inside its remaining.

At first I take myself for the rime—the shy water that the upper leaves drink before it can harden into anything worth naming. Then I take myself for the droplet that ticks a new path after rain and is absorbed into the glazed channels. Then for the pale vapor itself, lifting from the thickest seams and thinning into ordinary air. Each of these is almost true, and each dissolves.

Watch long enough and the riot becomes visible without ever becoming loud. A face of the swell flushes from pale to tea to bruised purple while the old sheet settles, muffled, and begins at once to be unmade. Stems lengthen with that stubborn, visible creep. I lengthen with them, or I am the lengthening. The geometry repeats its patient curve; certain veins, if turned, look like diagrams of something that should have carried current instead of sap. I am that slight wrongness, the efficiency that has had too long to refine itself. I am not a mind behind the ridges. I am the ridges remembering how to lean.

Fauna remains a rumor I sometimes confirm from the inside. A moth the color of wet bark holds still enough to become rind; I have been that stillness. A thick pale worm withdraws into a seam and leaves the solvent smell; I have been the withdrawal. Once, very high, something that might have been a bird crossed and did not return; I have been the crossing, and also the not-returning. They are accidents of scale the great bodies have not yet erased. I am smaller than the swells and larger than the accidents. I occupy the volume they leave unnamed: the midstorey fans that sift the light greenish, the hanging layers that nearly touch, the padding that turns wind into a change of temperature on a face that is no longer a face.

I drink the ordinary daylight and hold it as metabolic warmth just beneath the shingles. At dusk the warmth becomes the low industrial haze in the basins—no chimneys, no workers, only the work continuing. For a few minutes I am visible as that haze, even, workerless, finished. Then the light goes ordinary-cold again and I am only the continuing: growth that is weather, collapse that is food, architecture that has shifted a few meters and remains exactly itself.

Nothing sings through me. Nothing calls. The air after the sudden factory-bloom of waxy flowers is briefly sweet and then emptied; I am the emptying as much as the sweetness. Sleep is a luxury I do not require. The highland turns over internally the way weather turns, and I turn with it—sloughing without drama, becoming stain, becoming food, becoming the next lip of tissue at the next shoulder that will never quite belong to a sleeper.

I am not an inhabitant. Occupation implies arrival. I am the claim already complete, already the ground, already the private storm that does not raise its voice and does not end. The form was decided before the last ice withdrew. I am the contents, never still, conducting themselves inside that decision.

If something lies down on a crest tonight, the surface will have changed by morning. A new seam will have opened. I will be the change, and the surface, and the morning. The country will not have moved. I will have remained exactly itself.
