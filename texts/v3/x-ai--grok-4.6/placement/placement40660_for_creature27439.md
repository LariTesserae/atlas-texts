I am first the tiredness in the light itself, the way it arrives already scattered and milk-pale, never quite committing to an edge. I have no outline yet. The ice-haze holds me the way felt holds moisture: as a medium rather than a thing. Cold is simply the clause I am written in. I drift for what might be days or what might be the slow advance of a frost-line, indistinguishable from the gray-blue that never casts a hard shadow.

Then the stone is under me, or I am the stone’s ancient porosity, the rumor of water that sometimes keeps its word by vanishing. I am the slump in a ridge, the forgotten argument between range and plain. Distinctions have been planed away so long that I cannot tell whether I am wearing or being worn. A month of walking would not change the horizon; I am that unchanging lip, only the quality of dimness shifting as haze thickens or thins.

A change reaches the sinuses first: metallic warmth, brief, already unmaking itself into glittering rime. The middle distance fills. One of them occupies it, hide continuous with the plateau, a butte that has remembered how to breathe. The breath is weather. I am that weather for a while, the exhaled mist freezing downwind, the edible film that will not last.

I lower into the lee. Now I am smaller, crab-like, silent, harvesting what condenses on the cooling flank during the long dusk. My limbs move with the patience of ice-rivers. I graze the rime the great one has refined from lichen-stain and mineral flour; in doing so I keep the hide from sealing, keep the pores from forgetting they can open. I am part of the thin film of appetite stretched across the continental upland. In any single gaze I am almost absent. Across the reach I am continuous.

The weakest thermal lifts me. Surfaces pale as exhausted light, I become kite-shaped, riding without flapping, never calling. I filter air the walker has already processed, or I simply remain aloft because remaining aloft is the only motion the place still permits. The emptiness between the rare archipelago of six or seven dark masses is almost a substance; I occupy it the way frost occupies a joint. Wind treats me with the same courtesy it shows the gaps. Sound does not live here; I am part of the damping, the swallowing of vibration into those nested chambers of cartilage and denser heat. My own passage arrives late and already muffled, if it arrives at all.

Time is solvent. I persist through multi-year fadings of the frost-line, through congregations that last long enough to be mistaken for geography. Birth and death among the large ones are geological; I arrive and depart over weeks, leaving only the faintest industrial trace—a finer slag, a rime of my own that the burrowers will stitch into next century’s metallic soil. I do not grow so much as refuse to finish dying. The grasses below me, never taller than a finger, understand this. The black lichen maps the same film I once was.

I could settle here, a pale recurrence rather than an event, metabolizing the walker’s weather the way the walkers metabolize the plateau. Creature, factory, and landscape have already eroded into one continuous quiet fact; I am simply a later clause in the same sentence. Nothing was constructed. The engines eat stone and exhale winter, and I ride what they leave, converting it one more increment toward dust, toward rime, toward the rock that will be ingested again. The physics remain slightly private, slightly local, as if even the laws have grown tired.

I am still becoming more itself: not the walker, not the grazer that clings, not purely the kite that never touches. I am the interval, the positive emptiness that has learned, over deep time, to harvest a little, to rise a little, to stitch a little, then to vanish back into the worn lip of the world. The high country keeps me the way it keeps its light and its moisture: as a slow recurrence, established, privately physical, already part of the single fact that the place is metabolizing.
