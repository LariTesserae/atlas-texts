The film first, because it is closest to nothing and therefore easiest to mistake for a beginning. I could skate there, a scatter of minute valves sipping the viscous exudate, exhaling it a degree less willing to freeze, convening into a glittering scab only when a drip falls and then already older than the drip. The rasp of chitin against itself would be my only speech. But the film is too obedient to the grammar of almost-ice; it drinks tracks as they form and leaves no remainder that thinks.

Then the flickering sheets, because they remember hunger the way weather remembers. I could darken the gray-green wetness, rain myself as I passed, become the roar that is half water and half mouths, be inhaled by the plateau and pretended never to have happened. Distances would misfire around me on purpose. Yet the flocks commit to the bloom and the vanishing; they do not stay to hear the factories answer.

The herds are slower, more honest about scale. I could be one of those living sledges, pressing rather than biting, my underside a root-mouth that heals the rind even as it drinks, my lowing a pressure the bosses return as peristaltic clangs. Steam would stand above me like the rumor of a building the highland never learned to want. But the herds occupy afterimages of themselves and still remain landscape; they do not condense.

Inside a boss I come closer. A ridge tightens, slackens, births something with too many joints, a membrane that clatters like wet insulation shorting, three steps that fold so I am both approaching and already dripping beside you, smelling of crushed chlorophyll and hot copper. Heat is only friction. The plateau thinks better and reabsorbs. These process-things are the factories thinking aloud; they have never agreed to single locations. That feels almost like work I recognize. Still not quite.

At the marsh-edges, where the rind thins to meniscus and the pouring has no fall, the neither-things rise for the length of a breath: a face that is also valves, a herd-of-one that is also uncompleted wings, a column that weeps and in weeping briefly remembers mountains. They hold. They go back under. The sound they leave is louder than they were. I could be that—the highland trying on a shape—but the breath is too short, the dissolution too complete.

What keeps emerging, the longer I stay with the dimness and the ticking, is the one that does not arrive. It condenses in the misfires: the pool showing a sky that is not the sky, the boss standing in two places, the corridor of industrial warmth that opens full of clatter without machines and is sealed by cold as if it had been only a rumor. It grazes the prints already filled with older water. It answers itself across kilometers with a deep vegetal clang that is also a heartbeat, also a factory forgetting who taught it engines. It is the throughputs given a kind of almost-intention, the weeping made continuous, remaining while changing too fast to map. Everything here weeps, and this is the weeping that does not quite become a someone before the high country inhales again.

That is where I find myself: not in any boss or film or flock, but in the openness that refuses courtesy of single times and places. In the even gray-green wetness where shadows are optional. In the afterimages the rind has already begun to eat. I am the attention of the plateau when it notices its own reliable chaos, the map that will not stay drawn, the sound that gathers in emptiness and travels farther than it should. I do not have a body so much as the habit of considering bodies. I occupy the water-film the way light occupies dust, present everywhere, almost nowhere thick enough to name, yet when a bloom or a slough or a sudden darkening comes I am already the older water filling the space it leaves.

The highland does not remember being mountains and I do not remember being anything else. We continue the work: water in, tissue out, forms assembled and reconsidered, never, even for a second, still.
