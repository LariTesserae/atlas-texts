The heat is already in the throat when anything else arrives. It has no source you could point to, only the even wetness of a body that decided long ago this was simply the temperature at which a body continues. Mist hangs or falls according to a grammar older than weather. There is wood under the palm before there is a palm, sealed and faintly warm, the gloss of a thousand seasons of water that never quite finished traveling.

Fingers discover themselves by sticking. The pads are slightly tacky; bark and moss become extensions rather than surfaces. When they leave a surface they take a sheen with them, breath on heartwood. Limbs are longer than the torso that owns them, concentrated rather than diminished, made for the distances between members that are also corridors that are also mistakes grown useful. Eyes open until almost no iris remains, the color of wet fungus, and the brown-gold dusk is enough. It has always been enough. Hair is bound into cords or kept short because anything longer becomes another hanging garden, another path for unfallen water. One lock is already offered, cinched into a living brace somewhere the grain required it. The vine that never learned to let go is kin; the thought of it arrives as the faint pressure of an aunt’s hand.

A house is already decided: a pocket the moss had almost made, stitched with hair and the pale slough of vault-skins, propped, always damp, smelling of tannin and sleep and the iron of sap. Fires stay thumb-sized. Food is held against the metabolic wall until it yields. The gourd is in the hand without having been picked up. The seep-line knows the song before the mouth does; the intervals are not words but maps of pressure and grain, moisture rather than length. Water is encouraged, never commanded. A misplaced sheet would drown a nursery of filter-ribbons or leave a hanging garden brittle. Guest-plants that would choke a corridor into a throat are pruned with a knife of fired shell. Where a gall the size of a hill has begun to slump, living cords are set that will tighten as they lignify. The thought of cutting a great member simply fails to form. It is a category error, like arguing with the fever the structure has decided is normal.

The marsh-seam at the feet is frequent and not large. Black water stands in a living basin lined with moss so dense it has a nap. Larval cities turn in slow constellations, mucus threads tasting the used light and passing news that is never quite language. Soft walkers graze without leaving a trail that lasts longer than a breath. Filter-ribbons pulse, and sometimes one lifts entirely out of the water to hang, dripping, as if remembering it might once have been a leaf. A line of bubbles rises and does not break. The water is warm. Whatever they carry stays carried.

Sound remains the other emptiness. Drips are absorbed into cork and sponge. A wingbeat arrives only as a change in the warmth on the face; something with a leaf for a tail, or a tail that decided it was always a leaf, adjusts its angle and is gone. Farther, a darkening of the far wall, a glitter of wet flanks at a distance that turns individuals into a single slow pulse: a herd crossing an upper deck, thunder felt in the knees rather than heard. Predators exist as the sudden extra quiet. A hanging garden stops dripping for a few breaths. A shape that was part of the ribbing is suddenly not. Then the drips resume, a little warmer. The structure has absorbed the event the way it absorbs everything else.

Direction has begun to fail, as it always does. Two galleries that have never met share this puddle; look down and the ceiling of a hall not yet reached (or already passed) looks back. Graft-scars open if they are not insisted upon as scars. A staircase of living ribs continues to ascend long after ascent has become decorative; water now runs the wrong way along the same grain. The heat has a direction that is not up. Distances lie with courtesy. The structure dreams it is smaller and a chamber closes with the slow intimacy of a fist, moss and exhaled warmth, space the size of a mouth — then remembers its scale. The walls recede. The same drip falls into the same marsh that could be farmed for a lifetime.

Palm stays on the wood. It is damp. It is working. Far off, so far it might be another climate, something large changes its weight from one root-mass to another. The entire hollow — if the word still means anything — takes a breath that cannot be heard. The people of the seams (who refuse the word people) look along the grain and return to their pruning. There is no origin story, only weather reports that have learned to walk. A drawn map would be boast and blasphemy. Paper pretends the world holds still.

The dusk continues, being used by the leaves. The heat remains, wet and even, the warmth of a body that has been running for a geological age and has decided this is simply how a body runs. Adjustment is the only work that was ever required. The bubbles still do not break. The gourd is still in the hand. The song continues, moisture by moisture, as it has always continued.
