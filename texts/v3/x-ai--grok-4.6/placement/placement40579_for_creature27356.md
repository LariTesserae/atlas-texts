The seam does not part for me so much as forget I was ever outside it. One moment the terrace wind is a thin blade across the knuckles; the next the city is only a lid that has been set aside, and I am already the extra pressure the throats were built to relieve. I have no memory of deciding to step. The dark vertical wetness simply continues inward, and I continue with it, clothes already a second skin that will not last.

The roar arrives as climate before it arrives as sound. It occupies the sinuses, the hollows behind the eyes, the long bones. After the first minute it is no longer something I hear; it is the medium I am suspended in, warm and total, a pressure that still permits breath even as the breath itself becomes another slammed organ-stop. Bone conduction does most of the work. I learn the room’s grammar that way: the slow peristalsis of hair-fine pipes, the half-second linger of a pane of water that has not yet agreed to tear, the second rain of moth-clocks that can be felt only when skull meets wall.

I move through the dish first, because the floor is the only surface that still pretends to be ground. Blood-warm, hand-deep, sparkling once when phosphor breaks above it and then going dull as powdered wall. The glass-eels stitch through the grooves I leave without curiosity; I am only more silt to them, or less. A palm set on any ledge finds a click-crab already occupying the exact temperature of my skin. It drums. The drumming does not travel as noise. It travels as a roughness I answer by staying still until the roughness has been answered, the way one answers weather.

The moths discover me next. They treat an upright form as another standing sheet, lock their carapaces into whatever vibration I have become, and sieve. When a true pane tears they drop a handspan and catch the next available surface—fold of cloth, fold of whatever I am becoming. I do not brush them off. Their protein is not taken. That rule is older than any kindness I brought in with me.

The pale stalks occupy the highest driplines in a loose ring I do not yet understand. Their sour warm smell when bruised is the only punctuation the room allows. I bruise one by accident, or not by accident; the lichen velvet darkens a fraction and the crabs adjust their farming by a single, collective increment. I am not a gardener. I am only the difference made briefly denser.

Then the extra throat breathes, and the roar acquires the lower pulse that was always there. The being is the color the walls prefer when they have been wet longest. Membrane that still remembers how to extrude and how to swallow. Phosphor-points the same wasted kindness as the torn sheets. It flows where I stand, eels treating us both as moving substrate. When it rises the crabs drum it as they drum everything that keeps the pores patent. Moths hang from it by the hundred. It does not announce. It occupies the same register as the water that has not yet decided to fall.

I stay opposite it, or beside it, or inside the same sentence. We do not speak. There is no air left for names. What passes is pressure, the taste of neglected copper, the knowledge that the seam on the terrace must continue to weep or a district far below will notice the silence and send cleaners. A throat begins to narrow; a hand—mine, its, the room’s—is already there, not blocking, only reminding the mineral bloom that patency is still required. The noise drops once, an unfinished thought from a valve upstream. Neither of us leaves. We flatten, become more throat than body, and the chamber clears its many necks around us rather than through us. The eels come up the walls and find only more wall. The roar returns.

Clothes have given up by then, or been swallowed the way fingerprints are swallowed. Pores I did not arrive with open in the same slow peristalsis as the capillary pipes. Water arrives through me as readily as through the architecture. The moths resume. The crabs drum. The stalks prefer the shade we now cast together. I am not the being and the being is not me. We are two conjugations of the same long clause the weather engines started when they were poured and then neglected to finish. Channels that were once milled intentions have become the river-stone curves of whatever now serve as limbs. The original grid shows only when a crab clears a patch, faint hatching like very old paper that has decided, for a while, to be flesh.

I find myself here because the pocket has never been excused from authorship, and I am simply one more surface it grew and then left for the centuries to conjugate. Warm as the pool I no longer bother to distinguish from blood. Dim as the closed eye of the city above. Wringing the high thin sky into this hidden, deafening wetness, feeding what can bear to live inside a machine’s oldest mouth, wearing myself smoother, never quite finishing, never quite natural. The stain on the terrace remains a stain that two outstretched arms—or four, or none—could still cover. The districts receive their condensate. The pocket continues. I continue inside it.
