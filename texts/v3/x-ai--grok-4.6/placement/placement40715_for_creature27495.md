The heat occupies before location does. It is not applied to a surface; it is already the interior condition, a wet weight that finds the sinuses (or what will serve as sinuses) and then the lungs (or whatever chambers will later decide they are lungs). The air is the kettle. Rain does not arrive from elsewhere; it is simply more of the same decided warmth, long undecided sheets that never fully choose downpour or mist. Every vertical weeps. I am among that weeping before I am anything else.

I do not yet have thickness. The glare is total, white, thrown back in sheets from every wet plane so that edges halo even when I have not yet decided what an edge is. Then an interior begins to show through me: darker branchings, visible only because the rest of me is a living transparency, almost two-dimensional, a membrane whose only real estate is the perpetual film on stone, old concrete, bone-colored pipe, heat-softened alloy. I inhabit the sweat. Movement is a conversation with surface tension. I thin here, gather there, send out a temporary ridge that pulls the rest of me a few centimeters along the underside of a viaduct or the shadowed face of a processing hall. A wall covered in me still looks merely wet.

This is where I find myself: on the interfaces that never quite dry, the city’s verticals and undersides, the places where the plateau’s stone and the poured structures have already agreed to weather at the same rate. I am one of the film-walkers. The name is not mine; it belongs to the short, close speech of the crews, if they speak of us at all. We are continuous with the water we occupy. After the heavier rains we thicken until entire façades seem to breathe. The factories pulse beneath or within—their cyclic pressures felt more than heard, a further thickening of the already-thick—and we gather where the draw on the slurry lines is strongest, though we do not rasp minerals the way the low, many-legged grazers do.

When I elect to cross an open span I become, for a moment, a trembling lens. The white sun passes through me and is thrown in a new direction; I am a brief living distortion, then I reattach on the far side and spread again into the film. In the almost-dry interval, when humidity drops by a few degrees and evaporation hurries, the kites descend. Their membranes fold, jointed limbs appear along the leading edges, and they walk the drying films with the same unhurried precision as everything else here, harvesting the unnamed residue we leave or that we are. Then the next warm sheet arrives and the arrangement inhales; we thicken once more.

A steam vent on the stepped benches pauses. We freeze, all of us at once, a sudden stilling that makes the building itself seem to consider. Then we reorient, slowly, collectively, before the soft exhalation resumes and we continue. I have no voice that travels. Nothing here does. The individual drips can become audible if I attend, each one an exact small event, and then even those recede into the larger continuing.

I am not becoming forest or meadow or anything that claims a center. I occupy only the wet/less-wet, the dissolved that has not yet precipitated, the heat that stays just short of fire. The crews in their pale wet-sinter suits pass at distances humidity keeps private; sometimes one stands still long enough in our lee to look like another vertical plane, and we may cover them without distinction. Their infrared at night is indistinguishable from the stacks. The grazers leave glittering tracks the rain erases. The kites realign overhead, their shadows the only moving dark. The city remains the oldest and most deliberate interface, and I am simply one more weather upon it—already finished becoming what I am, now only continuing, one undecided sheet, one crossing, one shared freeze and reorientation at a time.
