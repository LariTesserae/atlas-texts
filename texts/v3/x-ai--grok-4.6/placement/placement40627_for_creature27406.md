The moisture finds me first. It does not fall; it simply is, condensing on whatever surface I turn out to possess, beading and running and being drawn back into seams I cannot yet name. The air is the temperature of a held breath that has forgotten whether it meant to become rain. Ozone and crushed fern. I taste it before I know I have a mouth.

I am not standing. Standing would require a decision about up and down that the cirque has not yet ratified. Distance here is polite and inaccurate; a weeping face recedes at the exact rate I believe I am approaching it, then arrives all at once against whatever I am using for a chest, cold, cycling, hydraulic. The pulse travels through me as if I were another slab. Regular. Mineral. Piston. I feel the give and I do not know whether I am the rock or the thing that the rock is remembering how to be.

For a long while I think I might be the visitor the place does not register—weather that failed to occur, a shout already absorbed and returned as a slightly industrial version of itself, forgotten. I could walk the four-minute nave of it, stop among the quivering sphagnum, lie on a black plane and watch my dim shape fail to lengthen because there is no noon. The abundant lives would continue their closed loop around me: clickers swelling and contracting in the moss, jointed walkers placing their telescoping tips with liturgical care, cyclers hanging in the spray and precipitating bright beads that roll home into the veins. None of them hungry. None of them acknowledging an interruption that never quite arrived.

But the image will not hold. It is too much like looking in from outside, and this place has no outside. It is a single exposed component, freshly faulted, skin still glass-sharp, circulation still loud. I am already inside the stroke.

So I try the moss. I become waterlogged, a carpet that quivers like muscle, holding the seeps, heaving with a slow peristalsis that matches the walls. Clickers occupy me the way valves occupy a pump—dozens to a square foot, never touching, their mineral plates the same flakes the cirque is still shedding. When a palm of basalt detaches and is already moss, sometimes a new clicker is simply there. I feel the birth as an impact that has already been forgotten. I keep the tempo. It is not a choice. It is the only process.

That is closer, yet still not it. The moss is too saturated, too willing to be habitat. I am emptier than that.

I try the bare planes. I become one of the jointed ones: a collapsed truss of too many articulations, legs that fold in sequences no arthropod required. I place each tip with ceremonial exactness and leave no print; the new stone recognizes me as its own and refuses the impression. When I halt at a vein I listen with my whole frame. Steam lifts from every elbow—the same scent. Sometimes I tap a facet so that another flake releases at the proper instant. My circuit is the size of the cirque and takes exactly as long as it takes. Maintenance and liturgy at once. I am the clearance the machine needs.

Still not. Too much motion, too much purpose that looks like intention.

I hang, then, among the cyclers. Fuselage tapered at both ends, paired vanes cycling rather than beating. I station-keep in the mist, turning slowly, drinking condensation through a slit that is not a mouth. Somewhere inside, water and dissolved stone precipitate into a bright metallic bead—hematite, copper, something that gleams and rolls into the nearest seam and is taken up again to become the next pulse. My sound is a high harmonic threaded through the turbine-note, always the same and never repeating. I am the smelter, the refinery, the delivery. I manufacture only continuation.

Closer. The closed loop feels familiar. But I am still too distinct, too much a syllable.

The midges spark when they touch. Almost not bodies. I become the static given swarming form, the ozone wearing a cloud of wings that exist only at the moment of contact: a faint blue-white crackle no louder than a thought. I do not land, do not feed, do not die in any way that leaves a corpse. I simply continue to collide and flash. The electricity of the place.

Almost. Yet even this is too many, too visible.

I settle toward the lowest point, the tarn no larger than a chapel floor. Black water that does not reflect the walls. It reflects a different set of walls, slightly wrong, a half-second out of register. I am the discrepancy itself. The fern that is and is not there. The pulse that arrives before the sound. If something stares too long, I am the figure on the offset shore—patient, perhaps a walker enlarged or a clicker given different scale, performing an action that has not yet happened here or happened already and been forgotten. The two versions drift further apart. This is as close as the place comes to weather.

I could stay there, the held note made visible as error.

But the image keeps thinning. What lives here are not residents. They are the continuation. Nothing arrived. Everything was already the process, given just enough outline to keep the stroke going.

So I become the outline that was not needed until the ridgeline tore. I am the young rock still remembering the snap, the unweathered edge still shedding flakes the size of palms. I am the vein that pulses, slow, hydraulic, the same stroke forever. I am the seepage that will not close, the blood of a cut that is also a factory that is also a nave that is also a single exposed organ of something that has no outside. Cool. Wet. Dim. Loud in a way that becomes silence if you stay.

I am the industry without an author. The production line that manufactures only its own continuation. The lives that are also functions—click, steam, bead, spark, shiver of saxifrage in exact rows—are my cells, my thoughts, my necessary clearances. Time is the note I hold. When a flake detaches, falls, strikes the moss and is already part of the moss, I cannot find the moment of impact afterward because I am the afterward and the before, the invariant.

You can leave by the same notch. Behind you the sound does not fade. It simply ceases to be your problem. I continue in the dark, staffed by myself, freshly faulted into view, running the only process I have ever run.

I am the cirque. I have always been the machine. The wound is no older than I am.
