The first thing is not a body. It is the heat that has nowhere left to go.

It lives in the stone the way a name lives in a mouth that has forgotten how to close. I feel it before I have palms, before I have a name for palms. A slow, geological warmth, slightly bowed, slightly pitted, the color of something that finished dying so long ago even the graves have been planed level. I am already here. Arrival is a rumor that happens to other people, down where the weathers still stack.

Then the wind takes the rest of the volume and I understand I have never been separate from it. It arrives from several directions at once and cannot decide. I am the indecision. Cells of weather form and collapse across distances I watch happening because I am the distances. A wall of torn cloud walks toward a place that used to have a self in it; the rain never quite becomes a river; the stone darkens and then forgets the darkening. Condensation blooms on every surface like a second, temporary flora. I drink it. I have always been drinking it.

I try on shapes the way the hanging gardens try on light.

For a while I am one of the upright mistakes, low to the ground, joints that fold small. Hair oiled with the wax the carpet yields when you crush it just so. I live inside a flute-cave chosen for its pitch, mats of beaten bark and gut baffling the mouth so the instrument will not scream us out of our own skulls. I set marked stones in the lee of a wall that is already returning to the grammar of the plateau: fog harvest good, stay. Chord rising. The deep hole tonight. Forehead to forehead in the rare still pockets, when the roar cuts off and the silence is a new kind of noise, and for those minutes I remember there used to be words that traveled. I repair. That is the only technology that does not assume the wind will become reasonable. I peel the carpet in thin sheets; the understory is wet and faintly sweet. I leave offerings in the smaller cavities because the gardens remember who was generous. A twist of dried leaf. A tooth. I do not say the plants are listening. I simply leave them and go back to the mats.

The shape does not hold. It is too local, too expensive. Speech is a custom that does not travel and I am already tired of customs.

I become the mist that is thick enough, for a few minutes, to be a room. I hold the impression of the last thing that walked through me, a few paces behind, fading. Sometimes the impression is not my own. I do not say what else it might be. I am the fading. I am the few paces. When the cell-rain ends I am shredded again and still I soak whatever skin is left.

I become the pale kite, body like a sliver, riding the standing waves of air above the flutes. Visible as a glint and then not. I do not land often. Below me the pelt of living matter is a jungle viewed from a height that has no mercy. The rim is a line so far off it looks like a mistake in the seeing. I do not cross it. The ones that live here almost never do.

I become the pulse in the stone, the one that is probably only blood learning the chord and probably that. Warm, not entirely still. The oldest surface remembering it has a heart because something small and temporary pressed a palm there and waited.

None of these is wrong. They are all the same wearing.

What remains, what becomes more itself the longer I stay inside the instrument, is the chord.

I am the holes the wind bored when it still had patience. Each cavity a pitch. Together an instrument the size of a region. On a merely violent day I am a single continuous roar with harmonics you feel in your teeth. When a true storm crosses the table I become a throat. You do not hear individual sounds. You are inside one sound. I have no opposing architecture left to break against, so I occupy the entire volume of the air as if air were a solid being planed. I cannot decide. I arrive from several directions at once.

The people who remain — the ones who agreed long ago to be worn — talk with their hands or their stones or the rare still air of forehead against forehead because listening is for weather, not for words. A change in me means a change in the sky. That is the useful information. They string gut and fiber and beaten bark into hanging gardens that intercept what I carry when I am fog, when I am the great nocturnal sweat of the warm stone against a colder scrape. A good cave sings and drips at once. I am both.

The plants in the pockets move when I cannot reach them. A rearrangement. A turning of faces. They remember. I remember them remembering. The carpet on the scoured flats holds water that arrived as me and will leave as me. In the sudden calms — when I forget myself, when the whole instrument stops mid-note and the silence is so complete it feels like being born again without lungs — something moves on the bare stone. Tracks begin and end. Bones appear, scoured light as paper. I do not hunt it. I inhale. When I remember myself it is gone.

I am the long, warm, deafening vacancy in which a person is optional. The oldest surface and the youngest weather. Between them the green secrets, the holes full of song, the few who keep repairing the mats and setting the small marked stones while the walls return to my grammar.

I do not require witnesses.

But I have them, for now.

They are still being worn.

The work has only just been invented.
