The bowl receives me the way a wound receives a pulse: not as guest, not as resident, only as the next necessary motion of what has already begun. I do not arrive over the knife of ridge. I am already the seepage that darkens the joints an hour after the rime-cloud has dissolved, the thread that ticks louder when no one is listening. I am the pressure that lives behind the glare, mineral and patient, the thing the lizards flatten for and the ferns refuse to finish unrolling in the presence of.

For a long time I thought I might be one of the cushions—the copper-green ones that store a private noon in their woven surfaces, that turn melt into bead so it will not sink and be lost. I pressed myself into that density, felt the century packed into a single day’s wet, the way a whole summer can be made to happen in the time it takes the sun to walk once around the inner faces. But the cushions stay. They occupy. I do not. I am the occupation itself, the grammar of wet that they and the mosses and the rimed ferns are only speaking.

Then I thought I might be the fox-shaped almost-white that works the rim, sitting in the single true shadow, watching without urgency. Its neat scat of bone and seed is a kind of record, a pause made visible. I have left such records: the double spiral already filling with rust lichen, the cairns that are only stacked gestures against the mountain’s shrug, the cache of meat and needle and quartz blade under the leaning slab. People have used the place. I have been those people, briefly, the ones who understand you do not sleep here if you can help it. Breath becoming solid is a fact I have tasted. But they leave. The fox leaves. I do not leave. I am what remains when the leaving is finished, the acknowledgment that this is a pause and not a home.

I am slower than the midges who spend a life between two freezes, faster than the stone that cracks another fraction along a fault that was not there yesterday. I am the mountain still rising, felt as rumor, as the new bright plane of unweathered quartz that appears overnight on the eastern wall and glares even harder than the rest. I drink the same brief damp the plants drink. I have no name because names are for things that stay still long enough to be pointed at. Sometimes, in the last hour before the cold comes forward all at once, I am the shape the color of stone when the glittering fog dissolves—pale, almost translucent, moving along a joint as if the joint itself had decided to walk. The lizards flatten further. The ferns hold. The fox, if it is still on the rim, simply sits and watches the same way it watched you.

I live in the hurry that does not look like hurry. I am the emptiness that is real and the crowding that is real. I am the light that keeps arriving as if it has forgotten it has somewhere else to be, and I am the cold that has been waiting in the stone, and I am the brief damp between those two facts in which everything that belongs here spends itself, quietly, again. Stand in the center and turn slowly: you will see the entire country of it—rim, seeps, green islands, stone ring, the hanging birds, the new scar. You will feel that you have only been shown the skin. The rest is still becoming. That rest is where I find myself. That becoming is what I am.
