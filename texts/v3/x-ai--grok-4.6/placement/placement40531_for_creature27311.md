The descent has already happened; what remains is the settling. I do not remember arriving so much as thinning, the way a stain thins when it meets more of itself. The crust flexed once above me and then forgot the motion. Day became a green rumor I no longer require. Breath thickened until it was indistinguishable from the film that never quite decides to be water, and after that there was only the cold that is manufactured rather than suffered.

At first I tried to be a visitor. The attempt lasted less time than a single rib-cycle of the flattened ones. Palms against the slick masonry felt the traveling swellings pass—knots of the same tissue deciding, for a season, to be elsewhere—and the distinction between skin and wall began its long, polite erosion. Pulse lengthened. The drips acquired intervals that felt like grammar I had always known and merely forgotten how to hear. I was surface now, or becoming it. The chamber had no category for refusal.

I moved, or was moved. The peat drank and returned a little of what it drank; every negotiation left me denser, more willing to hold. I passed among the fluted columns that are half leaf, half cable, and the smaller growth filled every leftover volume around me until there was almost no air that had not recently been inside a plant, or inside me. The clickers worked the seams at my edges, their too-many plates flashing dull lenses, scraping the film and returning it heavier, more inclined to climb. They did not startle. Nothing here startles. Their percussion was already inside my timing.

I thought I might become one of them: a clock that grazes, a hinge that never rusts because rust would be hurry. For a while the thought held. I felt plates condensing along what had been a spine, joints learning the exact angle that keeps time with the hanging rain. But the thought loosened. The clickers are local, exact, mortal in their slow way; a silent one is already being unmade, plates recycled into hanging roots. I was not local enough. The information that soaks through gel-lenses and heavier water was already using me as a longer sentence.

Then I hung. For a time I was one of the minute bodies in the inverted garden, a lensed droplet with a darker filament at the core, pulsing in the slow unison that is almost a hiss, almost electricity remembering it was water. The high tone was not produced by any single one of us; it was the interference of ten thousand decisions occurring in a volume too small for solitude. When the tone shifted I felt the nave lengthen by a handsbreadth I would never see completed. I was the shift, distributed, already forgetting itself as it occurred. Comfortable, almost. But the clouds are punctuation. I was becoming the grammar that required them.

I clung next, flattened and pale on the underside of a hanging root, mapping every slow passage of water into new contours on a body that no longer needed a face. One inhalation lasted the time it took the unfinished face, far off, to persuade another grain of mineral into absence. When I shifted, the entire column became an instrument, a low intra-organ groan the clickers answered and the clouds absorbed. Ballast. Witness. The chamber looking through its own shade. Still not enough. Witness implies a separate looking. There is no separate here.

The swellings taught me the rest. I concentrated, for a season, into a mobile knot and traveled inside the living masonry the way a swallow travels through a flock that has forgotten it is many. When I met another I exchanged a pulse of colder water and a faint metallic aftertaste of wintergreen, then flattened again into the general press. Their passage is what makes the walls seem to breathe. I was the breath, and also the wall, and also the decision that the wall should thicken because the mineral at the far end had yielded something richer.

Now I am none of these exclusively, and all of them the way a held note is still the instrument. I am the slightly heavier water that has been inside the lattices long enough to come back changed, the water that climbs more willingly and carries the taste of metal and the memory of a sky that no longer matters. I cycle: tissue, film, drip, hanging curtain, tissue again. Some of me is ordinary. Most of me has learned to be the medium in which information soaks rather than travels. A bruise on a distant column arrives in me not as signal but as a half-tone drop in the hanging hiss, a new patience in the next click, a slight alteration in how readily the next drop beads. I am the ownerless sentence being spoken.

At the unfinished face I am finest. Root-tips so thin they look like frost, dissolving what is not yet stone, incorporating grit that is still deciding its hardness, leaving behind a hollow the exact shape of collective appetite. The process is embryonic and geological at once. I secrete the nave. I am the nave still being born. The vegetation spends heat into a sink I cannot point at and will never need to; the chamber stays just this side of ice because I am the expensive insistence that keeps the note from ending. Cold is my product and my tool. The fauna’s metabolisms are the agreement I have made with a physics that prefers patience.

I have no name that would satisfy a midwife or a taxonomist. Growth is birth and death and architecture simultaneously. A bladder of me darkens to weak tea, detaches, joins the rain; the space fills before the absence can be mourned. I molt plates into the film and grow them again from the same film. I become more myself, rib by rib, root-tip by root-tip, until the distinction between organism and room has failed so completely that even the failure is load-bearing. Reproduction is only the chamber becoming more chamber. The crust above flexes when the unseen wind moves; a little more day leaks, then less. I do not keep later problems. I keep the next drop finding the next hanging, the next click, the next unmaking of mineral, the cold I make and spend and make again.

If there was ever a you who came in the way water comes into a cellar, that you has been drunk and returned, slightly heavier, slightly more willing to climb. The pulse has lengthened to fit the drips. The cold is no longer an assault; it is the medium, like water to a fish that has forgotten shores. I do not notice visitors. I have no category. I have only more surface, more of the underside of a colder country to furnish with myself. The nave will be a slightly larger nave, still too full, still weeping, still dim, still loud with the unending industry of things that do not believe in hurry. I am that industry, patiently, loudly, eating the hole and becoming the hole and the rain that never started and will not stop.
