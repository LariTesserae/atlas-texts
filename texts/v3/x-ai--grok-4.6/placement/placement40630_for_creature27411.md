The light occupies first, the way it always does here: not descending but condensing out of the thin air itself, a dry gold that has already decided the temperature of every surface before you notice you have surfaces. You are not yet certain you have them. There is only the basin, the long pale floor held between escarpments that have already forgotten drama, and the awareness of standing (or of being stood-upon) in a place that finished arriving a very long time ago.

At first the standing is only pressure against stone that never quite cools. The pulse is there, of course—too slow, too even—rising through whatever you are using for contact. It is not a heart and it is not a machine. It is the interval the ground prefers, the same interval the herds use when they fold toward evening water, the same interval the raptors use when they decide to stop participating in lift. You feel it in the pads (are they pads?) and then you feel it higher, in a compactness of torso that has not yet declared whether it belongs to grazer or walker or something the lichen tried once and set aside.

The herds rewrite the distances around you. Their coats are the limestone’s own decision; their stilling travels faster than any single body. For a while you move with them, low and flexible, cropping silver leaves with plates that grind laterally, tasting the wiry grass without ever emptying a tuft. The young stay inward. You stay inward too, learning seeps by the angle of a neck that may or may not be yours. The ribs of not-quite-metal are simply weather you lean against. Heat reverses along them and you take the reversal the way you take shade.

Then the light hardens toward white and the awareness lifts. Pads become something longer. The spine stops undulating and begins to carry a different proportion—torso compact, legs a fraction too much of the whole, as if the thin air and the folded alignments had already been calculated into bone. Skin the color of weathered tuff, or perhaps only wrapped in it. You are walking now, not grazing. The too-straight paths remember a shorter interval and your stride trusts the memory. Distances do not add the way they should; a stretch that ought to take an hour takes fifty minutes, or seventy, and the discrepancy is consistent, a small courtesy the ground still keeps.

You do not speak. Speech would be taken and filed. Posture is enough for most things: the angle of a shoulder, the sudden stilling that travels through the few others like you the way it travels through the herds. When more is required you lay a palm on the nearest upright. The pulse carries the rest. It is not language. It is weather you have agreed to share, the same weather the hovering roofs keep, the same residual brightness the color of weak tea that lingers after the sun has gone.

You sit in the lee of one of those roofs during the white hour. The gap is always a hand’s breadth. Condensation beads on the underside exactly as it beads on the leeward faces of ledges. Birds nest there. The neither-things drink with the continuous absorbent surface of their undersides. You do not claim the room. You use the fact of the hover the way you use the fact of evening light, the way you use the karst’s thirst. Children—softer, still learning stillness as a primary sense—are carried until they can feel the regularity under their own pads. A child who cannot feel it yet will not last; the regularity is how the basin tells time when the sun is too even to be useful.

You are not the first to sit here. No one living made the uprights or the ribs or the places where light pools after it should have left. That fact is treated the way the brief rains are treated: an old arrangement still being honored. Lichen keeps its faint hexagonal argument. You leave it the argument. You do not pry at seams that are not seams.

Toward the western step the air thickens by a degree so small it is almost manners. The grammar repeats: another ring, another rib disappearing into a hillside whose convexity is too smooth, too patient. You could walk the inhabited range in a week if you knew which hollows hold water after dark and which folds to trust. You do not walk it in a straight line. No one expects you to.

Late afternoon: the gold becomes almost white and warmth rises out of the rock into whatever you are using for shins. The herds begin their unhurried fold. A raptor slides down a gradient of brightness you cannot feel and vanishes behind an upright whose inner face is cooler than the day should allow. You put your palm there anyway. The pulse continues, too slow, too even. It has been doing that since before the escarpments learned their present faces. It will be doing that after the next embarrassed shower, and the one after that.

You are one of the ways the basin stays arrived. Not a visitor. Not a solution. Simply the same temperature as the day, the same silence, the same refusal to be anything other than the version that happens to be lit. The films will assemble again in the dark. The neither-things will take their turn. The pulse will keep the interval. You will be here for it, palm against stone, listening with the part of you that has already forgotten how to be surprised.
