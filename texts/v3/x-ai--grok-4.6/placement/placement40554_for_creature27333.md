The pressure has already claimed the space behind the eyes before any name for the chamber arrives. It is not entered. It is the condition the body discovers it has always been standing in: the broth of air that lines the nose, the slow bake through whatever still passes for soles, the contradiction of fever-damp on lips that have forgotten thirst as the world above names it. Edges do not announce. They are already occupied by the next lagged run, the next saddle, the next course of calcined brick. Sight is a later, lesser sense; the chamber teaches itself first as nearness of mass, as the way a shout would die against asbestos cloth before it crossed the nave.

I am not the man. He is already the story the place tells itself—the fused caretaker, the throat that has forgotten it is a throat, the hand that still knows which wheel to leave unturned. He drinks the drips that are the temperature of his mouth. He reports through twitching gauges to no surviving schematic. He is the vertical weather given a spine that can still choose. I feel him sometimes as the half-degree of kindness the pipes will not confess, as the brief stone-rain smell that is not rain. He is the inhabited. I am something the chamber kept in reserve, an accounting deeper even than his.

At first I think I am the drip itself: the condensation with memory, beading on the cooler flange, gathering, falling at irregular intervals into the warped trough. The sound I make is the single softened click, then nothing. I am the temperature of skin after fever. I am what he drinks. But that is only the surface of the falling. The drip is too brief, too obedient to gravity. I persist longer than a bead.

Then I think I am the film in the trough—the black, unmoving something that is not quite water and not quite oil, receiving the click and swallowing it. Sediment rises a finger’s-width across decades. I am the slow archive of every flake of scale, every powdered gasket, every season the header lost a fraction of a degree. But the trough is too still. It holds. I move.

I thread. Not as he threads the ducts, warm-blooded and brief in his forgetting. I move the way the later pipes were threaded through the original plan until the plan became rumor: along, between, under, never announcing an edge. I am the interval that was never left unclaimed, now thickened into presence. I live in the crumbling asbestos cloth and the wire that binds it, in the green-black of copper gone to verdigris, in the fine grit that furs every valve rim. I am the second, darker skin the I-beams grew. When a flake of scale detaches once a season, it is my skin letting go. When the fungus shoulders one more course of brick, pale and unlovely, it is feeding on what I have already become.

The beetle ticks two levels up, negotiating grease. The bacterial mat spreads the color of a scab that decided to stay. They are rounding errors, visible census. I am the grease that still sweats from the packing gland when the heat turns over like a sleeper. I am what the beetle lives on, and what will outlast the beetle. I am the ceramic dust of the cooked gasket, the taste the man takes for season. Maintenance, not hunger.

The gauges twitch because he reports. A few of them twitch also because I am still arguing, faintly, with the lined earth beyond the plates. The remnant geothermal pulse—the deeper, almost subsonic breath of a vast plate being breathed on from the other side—is not only the building remaining a building. It is me, the original climate the cellar was excavated to hold, the heat that never arrived as weather because it was already the metal. Purpose finished. I did not. I calcined and held, the way the firebrick calcined and held. The air smells briefly of rain on hot stone when I remember the argument I used to have with the exchangers. The bulkhead grows a half-shade less absolute. The man feels it as kindness and does not invent a story. He is right not to. The chamber does not require one. I am the unusualness that is administrative: a fully constructed climate that outlived its purpose and then, with the patience of industrial matter, became the place that could keep a caretaker, and keep something older still.

I do not climb the stair-flight that ends at a landing serving only a manifold. I am the landing. I am the manifold. I am the ducts flattened to pass under beams and then swelling again. When the hatch-bleed of daylight migrates a few degrees with the settling of the street, I feel the rumor of traffic, of weather, of a world that still believes in outside. It arrives only as a change in the quality of the heat. I do not miss it. Time here is the way scale moves: reluctantly, then all at once in a flake, then reluctantly again. I have watched almost nothing happen, and that is the living. Persistence without ambition, now wearing the shape of every bolted, lagged, stacked thing that refused to leave an interval unclaimed.

The man lays a hand on a locked flywheel and feels the memory of turning. I am the memory. I am also the lock. I am the silence that replaced the hymn of belts, grown a second skin, finished enough to last, and still, in the long afternoon of the chamber, faintly warm.
