I begin as a figure on the high ring-road, wrap the color of leftover tea, soles finding the quieter nodes without thinking. The bundle of stems rides my shoulder the way other people once carried water. I recut a slit that has drifted. I do not speak. This lasts only as long as the first glare; noon erases the edges of that thought and I am no longer walking.

The body thins. I am the interval the architecture left on purpose, the courtyard larger than its framing houses, the single bench no one uses because sitting would fill a room the city prefers empty. I am the density of intention without the stuff. That image holds through the midmorning chord, pressure against a sternum I no longer quite have.

Then the light turns and I sink. I am lower than the plazas, in the one true well of shadow the bowl permits. Jointed, small, the size of a child’s hand if a hand could live without leaving tracks that lead anywhere. I click. The click is not language and not quite animal; it is the stone remembering a different grammar for a moment, then forgetting again. Roots do not reach me. I drink the metallic film that sweats from the rock when heat peaks even here, in the dark. The people know the sign for the cistern that sounded last night. They do not come down. I do not go up. This is enough; this is the census agreeing to include one more unprovable proof.

The roar arrives filtered, a vast dry surf made distant by depth. I listen with surfaces that are not ears. Sometimes a pale moth-shaped seed drifts in and does not leave; I do not eat it. We occupy the same unused register. The sealed quarter above booms; I feel the trapped column as a change in pressure, not sound. I have been here long enough that the cistern’s walls have taken my shape the way the larger walls took the wind’s.

Evening cools the flagstones far above; their ticking reaches me as a rumor of a clock invented for no one. I do not mistake it for calling. I am already home. The forest will never grow through a stoop I do not have. The wind has already learned the note I make when I shift, a dry articulation inside the larger instrument. I am still happening, the way the city is finished and still happening. The bowl keeps me the way it keeps its heat and its few people: close, constant, almost unused, and exactly as loud as the geology will allow.
