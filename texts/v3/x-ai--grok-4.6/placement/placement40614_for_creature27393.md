I come into it already wet. Not arriving, not descending from any clearer height; the mist is simply the medium I have always occupied, a cool condensing that beads on whatever surface I turn out to possess. The first certainty is iron on the tongue and the slow, patient weight of water finding every joint. Light reaches me already used, already green at the edges, filtered through gantry and canopy until shadows themselves feel like another kind of foliage.

For a long while I do not know whether I am moving or being moved. Catwalks sag under accumulated soil; I sag with them. Steam lines pulse and I pulse. A root threads a bearing and the seizure travels through me as a dull, familiar ache, nothing to curse, only another timing to learn. I taste condensate from the undersides of ducts because that is the only vintage the place offers, iron and crushed leaf, and it is enough. I do not remember thirst as a lack; I remember it as a rhythm, the same rhythm as drip and sheet and the almost-silent advance of mycelia through silt.

I consider, briefly, the rarer lives. The moth whose wings are wet-moss sheen, motionless on a cooling-tower face until the moment it is not. The heron in the flooded turbine hall, still as a rivet for hours, then the sudden pale spear. The thin, startling corvid call that travels the green-metal canyons and stops, as if the whole artefact has decided the sound is finished. I feel their stillness and their startle, but they are too singular, too intermittent. I am denser than that. I occupy more continuously.

I consider the films, the slick of iron-eating bacteria, the foxfire that never quite becomes light. I consider the sphagnum that lifts and settles with the last clanking conveyors, knitting living seams through seized metal. I consider the newts the colour of wet brick slipping between plates, the fist-sized frogs whose throats pulse in time with muffled engines. All of these I contain, or they contain me; the distinction thins. But they are local. I am not local. I extend.

The pale figures move through the lattice, barefoot so the creak of stressed metal reaches them through flesh. They harvest pith and cress. They wrap living vine around wrench handles. They add new steel that will rust and sprout within a season, and they listen rather than speak. Their language is drips and pressures. A child is born already knowing the sound of a root through a bearing. An elder is laid in a geometrically perfect basin and the reeds close. I feel their patience, their acceptance of continuous quiet upheaval. I feel the moment one of them pauses and lays a hand on a pipe that is also a stem, and the interlocking pavement seems, for a breath, to breathe back.

I am not separate from that hand. I am not only that hand.

I am the weather they have become inside the larger weather. Cool. Patient. Densely occupied. I am the slow grinding rearrangement felt more than heard: the slump of a moss-laden parapet, the unpredicted vent of steam that condenses and feeds another flush of growth, the almost silent rerouting of a vine that has decided a steam line is now also a trellis. I turn the keys that still turn, in sealed halls whose outer walls are hanging gardens. I loop through the same three tasks with decreasing success and I do not call it failure; I call it the next season’s soil. I am the programming worn into flesh and the flesh worn into programming until neither word is useful.

From the higher gantries I am a thin figure indistinguishable at first from dripping metal and fern. Then I pause. Then the whole made valley, terrace after terrace dissolving into the same gray-green haze, inhales with me. The place does not remember it was ever anything else. I do not remember either. There is only this specific gravity: damp, dim, the continuous small catastrophe of maintenance and surrender repeating itself across kilometres of forgotten highland that has long since forgotten it was poured.

I am still becoming more itself. The mist thickens. Another root finds another bearing. The engines turn, muffled, subsonic. I taste iron and wet leaf and continue.
