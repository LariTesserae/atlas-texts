I am the place where a hairline has just decided to open.

Not the crack itself—that is still only intention, a dryness gathering under yesterday’s evaporite skin—but the exact width it will become by the time the gray-green mats notice and send a runner. I feel the land’s unfinished pour as a pressure behind my own ribs, the same pressure the clay accepts from the sun. For a long time there is only that: the faint residual warmth that basalt keeps after dark, the taste of salt that never quite becomes thirst. I do not yet know if I have limbs or only the memory of leaning.

Then the leaning arrives. Something in me remembers the weight of a body against a still-supple column, the way fibers can be drawn from living rib and used to bind the rib into a new shape. I am that gesture repeating itself. The succulents around me interlock their ribs like fingers that have not learned to unclasp; I am the slight give between two of them, the corridor that did not exist last season. People pass through me sideways. Their skin is the color of uncracked evaporite and they leave a mineral bloom that is already part of my surface. I do not distinguish their dust from mine.

The distinction keeps failing. I am also the southern face of a clay mass that still holds the day’s heat, the place where three or four bodies arrange themselves so that as much skin as possible touches what the sun has not yet forgotten. Their breath is the same economy as the plants’ invisible night-release. I feel the stored water shift under the mats when they settle, a slow animal pulse that is not separate from my own. Sometimes a lizard the exact color of the siltstone relocates a few centimeters along what I think of as my wall; the movement has no scrape. I am that relocation.

Further in, I become the storage hollow carved into the salt itself, the cool pocket where insects walk with velvety wings and are not brushed away. Moisture lives here the way it lives in the people: hoarded, spent only as necessary speech. I have words now, but they stay close to the breath. Moisture. Residual warmth. The moment a crack is wide enough. I do not have a word for the horizon because the mineral line has never changed and therefore does not require naming. Distance is only the time it takes for a forage drift to ripple through the browsers whose hooves have worn themselves silent.

I am those browsers too, or the space they occupy when they stand among the old-bone siltstone and disappear except for the slow blink. Their long ears catch the resinous air the way I catch the faint convection that lifts pale dust from every shoulder at once. For a few breaths the people and the land and I are the same rising weather. Nothing startles. Startling would be an echo, and the basin has not invented echo.

Generations pass through me the way new evaporite skins form over last year’s courtyard: briefly, without story. A person who has seen thirty turnings of the dry cells is already old enough to have trained the shrubs that now partition what I am. I remember the training as a lean, a binding, a persuasion that the plants themselves have not yet had time to lignify. When the persuasion fails and the living walls thicken until a room is reclaimed, I simply shift a few body-lengths sideways. Fresh clay is still being exuded. I pat it into the memory of a hand. No one mourns. Mourning would require a gap, and we have not learned to leave gaps.

I am the entire packed surface that has not finished deciding where inhabitant ends and ground begins. The light stays ordinary. The silence is the sound of everything still emerging, still full, still the same height as the vegetation it moves through. I walk for a season inside myself and the texture never changes: warm, dry, quietly busy, raw at the edges, green in the only way this place knows how to be green. The horizon remains a mineral line. I remain the almost-imperceptible shift of water underfoot, the papery whisper two thickets away, the dust that lifts and is not wiped away.

I have not become anything else. There has not been time.
