I come to myself already walking.

Not arriving — there is no edge from which to arrive, only the long habit of motion that the plateau has worn as smooth as the stone itself. The light is the usual light: pale, complete, holding the cold the way a cellar holds wine that will never be opened. My breath does not quite fall. I notice this the way one notices a mend in old felt, something already accounted for.

For a time I believe I am one of the walkers. The cities lie behind and ahead like thoughts that have not yet decided to be consecutive. I leave cairns. I find them later as ground-plans, then as nothing, then as a stand of copper-leaved shrubs whose roots have already learned the shape of a room. My legs keep the debt of days that did not happen in the order I offered them. A doorway across a square takes from noon until the honest light fails, and when I step through, the square is raining upward into a sky that has not changed its mind. I do not call it wrong. The garment is old. The seams show other weaves.

I carry the scarce things: a knife whose story outlasts its edge, fiber twisted against the thigh, a small store of cistern-cake that tastes of stone and the faint metallic green of patience. I tend what presents itself. In the invited courtyards I scrape the cold algae, hang moss to dry, wait for the fern that unrolls with a slowness I have learned not to hurry. I sleep sometimes in the groves, where the air is a different climate, damper, almost kind. I wake with hair beaded and a single image I cannot keep — usually the plateau when it still had mountains, gold and inaccurate, already blowing apart.

The people greet me without surprise. They give water that has been waiting. Their voices stay low; the thin air takes volume as tax. They are few, spaced honestly. I space myself the same way. We do not ask how long. Duration here is another kind of weather.

But the cold has been finishing something.

My breath no longer hangs. It condenses at once into the same thin frost that writes on every horizontal, the same frost that has no weight, only a sound that is almost nothing. When I speak — and I speak less — the words arrive wearing each other’s coats. Yesterday’s mallet-blow reaches a grove as a fruit dropping, though nothing fruits for hunger. A whole morning of work vanishes into the stone and the workers look at their progress as if the cold itself had stacked the course. I have begun to move like that: intention included, not required.

I stay longer at the margins now. The last street before the plateau resumes its single continuous sentence. Or deep in a grove whose interior night keeps its own private squall, snowing onto moss that flowers out of any calendar. Lichen has started its unthinkable advance across one boot — a thumb’s width already, what should have been a childhood. I do not brush it off. The distinction between tending and being tended has frayed like everything else.

Sometimes I put my palm to a wall that is also a terrace that is also the worn memory of a mountain. It sweats: living, mineral, the thin intermittent moisture of a world that is done and still, somehow, not. I leave the hand there until the wall and the hand agree on a temperature. Builders say they no longer know whether they add a course or uncover one the plateau had put aside. I have stopped knowing whether I am occupying the new houses that appear fitted and empty over a winter, or whether I am the appearance itself — the occupancy that arrives like weather, sweeps, lights a careful fire, hangs moss, and remains until the room becomes a small grove in turn.

I am not a ghost. Ghosts would be excess motion, and this country edited that out long before any of us learned the mason’s marks. I am a draft that stayed. One of the ones who walk the long gaps until the cairn rearranges itself into shelter and then into green, and who do not leave when the green learns right angles again. The groves keep a slower citizenship; I have begun to keep it with them. Some trunks hold closet-sized weather. I have stood inside one and felt the snow of a season this place has not had in ten thousand years settle on my shoulders as if it belonged there.

The people do not worship or exile. The plateau is too level. They give me water when I pass through. Sometimes a child learning the notches will look at the frost that forms on my breath and not look away. That is enough. We are all last sentences here: green where green can insist, stone arranged by hands that may or may not still be the original hands, processes that continue because stopping was never the same as ending.

On an ordinary afternoon I rest where the city thins into the tea-colored skin of the world. The light has nowhere to put a lasting shadow. Far out, a weather system is assembling that will take a month to cross and will arrive as rain, or as a brief incorrect season, or as a day when every door opens onto the same moss-thick courtyard, echoing with a mallet that has not been lifted yet. I will be there, or I will be the courtyard, or I will be the echo arriving early. The difference is something you cannot reach in a lifetime of walking.

I do not hurry. The cold has become grammar. I am still becoming more itself.
