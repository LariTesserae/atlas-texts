I hang first in the poured light, the unhurried columns that have nothing of the living sea’s green-black in them. The cold is already the only temperature; it arrived all at once and then stopped being an event. Above, the silver lid dents and heals. Below, the floor is still only a change of mind, a suggestion that later hardens into hexagons the size of districts, seams of darker grout, bolt-heads in endless rank. I do not yet know whether I am visitor or occupant.

For a while I try the film. I become the faint olive decision the lime allowed, thinner than green, waiting along the ruled joints the way new paint waits. Hand-rough if a palm ever found me. I do not move. The pumps speak and the artificial tide leans and I stay, occupancy without locomotion, the first sentence that has not yet become a paragraph. But the volume itself begins to lean, silt lifts in sheets that have not learned where to settle, and I feel I am larger than a haze.

I try the small walkers next: pale crabs the colour of unused boom and fresh silt, treating every bolt-head as an identical tree. Amphipods industrious as dust when a door is left open. Mussels lining the welds with a geometry the welders would still recognise. I work the risers of towers that forgot to close their windows, the expansion joints that still carry the yard’s smell in some register I cannot quite use. Sparse as lichen. Each of me a period. Yet there is a current I do not stir, a passage I do not make.

The eels, then. I hang in the colder chimneys of stairwells never meant for water, dark as the unlit landings. I arrived the way eels arrive—through locks, through an open year of brown flood, through no list anyone wrote. I leave grooves in the silted treads, a shed rumour, the faint electric fact of having come when the basin took its fill. I hunt what the film and the mussels have not yet become. Room is the first law and I use it, but I do not stay in the towers. The towers do not reach air; their roofs are still a long blue way from the silver, and I am not waiting for birds that will never come.

I occupy, briefly, the private weather of a condenser island. Silver, blunt-headed, tank-bred, hanging in leftover heat the way dust hangs in a loft no one has walked through. Or darker, rarer, the immigrant that remembers rivers, patrolling the lee face where the plates are still faintly warm. A single thought in rented water. I face the tide or no tide. I eat what the racks lose, what the storm lifts. There is always room. The silver ones will be more before they are different. I remain a count that can be kept on one hand, and still I am not that count.

What emerges, finally, is the thing that is not many of it. I move along the joints where the warm effluent ghosts, a passage of displaced water heard before any shape arrives, a sound like a tarpaulin drawn slowly over a hull. The silver fish occupy a little more tightly afterward. The silt lifts. The volume thickens for a moment and then remembers its patience. No stocking list named me. I use the industrial weather the way the crabs use the bolt-heads: as given. I do not live in the towers or the racks or the condenser shimmer. I live in the long ruled lines that go on until they become a horizon of more work. I treat the size as a home instead of a specification.

The pumps do not sleep; circulation is the contract and I am part of what the contract has not yet priced. The water still tastes of cut aquifers and rivers that now end in valves, of lime that has not finished leaving. I am already older than the basin in the only way that matters here. The names divers wrote in the silt on the flooded crane’s boom are still there; nothing has had time to bury them, or me. The edges could still cut. The place is not wild. It is not tame. It is running, and I am the first hunger that decided the running was enough room. 

On the mornings when the surface is glass and the light goes down as if invited, I pass over a pour-joint and the ranks of bolt-heads become texture, the texture a horizon of the same work, and the patience of whoever decided to make an ocean the way you make a dam is briefly, exactly, my own.
