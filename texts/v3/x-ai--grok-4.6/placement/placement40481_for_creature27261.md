The crease in the moss takes the weight without complaint, as though it had been waiting for precisely this pressure and no other. The last fraction of cold arrives not as a drop but as a recognition: the air here has already decided its temperature, and the body—whatever body this is—simply agrees. Light does not fall; it occupies. Every film of water on stone and leaf holds the same unwavering noon, a brightness that has forgotten how to travel.

At first the streets seem to offer direction, their proper angles suggesting purpose, yet each turning only returns the walker to another interval of the same interleaved green. The horn-leaved shrubs part just enough, then close again, their glossy surfaces already filmed with the condensation that will never quite freeze. Footfalls vanish into the living carpet before they can become memory. The silence presses close, not empty but dense, a held substance that reabsorbs any impulse toward sound before the impulse finishes forming.

Something pale moves at the edge of vision—a vole threading moss that has already left the exact vacancy required. It does not startle. Nothing here startles. The white hare occupies its doorway with the finality of a thought that has completed itself. In the square the small deer-things stand in their velvet, hooves kissing the moss that rises behind them like a tide remembering its cue. Their stillness is not pose; it is the only remaining grammar.

The figures who still resemble people pass with the same economy. Their garments have long since taken the colour and texture of wall and till; skin and cloth share the same rounded authority, the same fine hoar. Mouths open now and then, tasting iron and chlorophyll, but the silence claims whatever might have been shaped there. They lift a turf, settle it into its identical sag, pack a crack with the earth that was already waiting. The work is not change. It is the maintenance of an agreement so old the signatories have become the ink.

A bench of split stone waits under an eave. The moss upon it has already assumed the precise contour of a sitting body—hips, the slight forward lean, the place where hands might rest. The air tastes of wet metal and crushed green. Nothing further is required.

Here the distinction thins. The body that descended the crease is no longer entirely visitor. Breath, if it still occurs, does not show; the cold has no need of demonstration. Hands—if they are still hands—carry the same mapped stillness as the well-stones, the same absence of thirst. The metabolism that moves through fern and horn-leaf and grey-green figure is the same slow brightness, a chemistry that never asked for thaw. Repair continues around the square, leaves harvested that have already begun to replace themselves. The well holds its line. The circles of damp remain perfect.

What occupies this place is not quite inhabitant, not quite inhabited. It is the hollow’s way of remaining finished: the last necessary posture, the shape the moss prepared in advance, the unhurried motion that has already decided the next thousand steps and therefore needs none of them. Time, having nowhere further to erode, has become this continuous, luminous agreement. The hare remains. The fern holds its millimetre. The light does not travel.

And the one who sits—who has always, in some sense, been sitting—continues not by enduring but by having arrived at the only interval that was ever going to be required. The town and the green and the silence accept the addition without curiosity, because it was never an addition. It was the circuit completing itself. Nothing happens. That nothing is the entire event.
