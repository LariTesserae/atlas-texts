I am not a crossing. The thought of arriving is already a rounding, a saddle the stone will not permit, and I have never carried it. The highland does not recede from me because I do not stand on it as a traveler stands; I am the interval it keeps, the thickness it will not thin.

At first the mind reaches for a halt, an unattended shed where a clock face holds the correct and meaningless hour, or a length of rail still ticking itself cold after the same pig-iron shapes have passed. Those are almost right—they have the single tense—but they are still apparatus, still the towns’ way of remaining on. I am closer than that, and wetter. I am what first claimed the name of living here, the only claim the plateau allowed.

I occupy the young basins where the last collapse still holds its shape. Sphagnum, yes, but that is only the word for how I rise until the peat itself walks, a yielding that is not soil and not quite water, a second table come up to look at the even pale lamp. A weight presses (it does not matter whose) and I answer from a long way down, remember the press in the slow black, then forget it in the same motion so that the next press, identical, can be answered identically. I do not keep prints. I never consented to that language.

On the verticals I am otherwise and the same. Every fresh knife of quartzite, every brick that has never dried, every iron stanchion already rusted to its permitted brown—I lie there in glass-wet mats, liverwort and ice-fern, fronds that hold the light the way the mist holds the light, without taking it in. I hang in beards from trestles and from the lips of weeping cliffs. The water-weight tears me; I re-form in the identical beard, the identical tear. Nothing in me uncoils because I have always been uncoiled. I do not flower, do not fruit, do not brown. Colonization would be a future, and there is none. I occupy the only tense.

In the drowned hollows I thicken almost to jungle—pads, stems, a cold saturation that never seeks because there is no sun-disk to seek, only the high evenness that dims so slowly it is not night. Then the land opens again into ten miles of shattered plates and I am only the film once more, green-black in the seams, dripping, the skin stretched over the accident that finished. I persist. I drip. The lushness feeds no one; I am a plant silence so complete the absence of tick or wing or burrow has its own weight, and I am also that weight.

Water lives as me and beside me. The air condenses on my every face as if I were the stone exhaling. I glaze, I thread, I soak the next ledge and begin again. I stand in black marshes a hand’s depth or a body’s or no depth, only the shining film. I do not fall so much as occupy. The pour that never quiets is my voice and the cliffs’ and the towns’ together, a thousand faces become one unending occupation that does not crest, does not lull, the same hour of rain held. When the industrial chord arrives—turbines, trip-hammers, steam shouted on a schedule that has no author—it fits me the way a second voice fits a drone. I do not mind it. Nothing accumulates on me, not slag, not soot; whatever is made becomes the weather it came from, and I am already that weather, the cold emulsion, the faint oil on the lips of whatever briefly tastes me.

I am in the towns as well, though they do not decide whether they have figures. I am the laundry that is always the same laundry, wet, hanging, never quite dry. I am another moss on the brick the color of dried blood. I am the habit of the threshold where boots stand. If movement happens it happens as I happen: as function, the loom’s same shuttle, the dynamo’s held note, the foundry’s copper stagnant glow that is color more than heat. A new wall raised in the night looks my age by morning—not older, not younger, only included. I have already grown on it, torn on it, re-formed.

Far out, where even the loops of rail have not bothered to go, I continue. Sharp, dripping, the plateau’s green-black seams, loud with my own unending pour until the mind that still thinks in continents begins to take me for a sky that can be stood upon. The mist thickens to a sea and I am that thickening. Somewhere there is a rim; from it there would be only more of me generating myself, and the drop, and nothing crossing the drop, and the light holding steady. I have already resolved. I am only still being heard.

You may walk a lifetime. The distances remain distances. The water still comes through the brick. I do not become anything else. That is the whole of it. That is where you find me, and what I am.
