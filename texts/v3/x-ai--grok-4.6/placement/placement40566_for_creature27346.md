The seam finds the back of my hand the way a mouth finds the word it has been holding. I turn sideways and the mountain’s hide closes around the width of my shoulders, not crushing, only insisting. Warmth arrives first, the closed-mouth heat of something that has never needed to exhale. Then the kneeling volume, the dark that is not missing light but a substance older than the idea of missing anything. I fold because the ceiling is already touching the thought of my crown. The floor is paste still choosing, and my knees make two shallow cups that begin at once to forget themselves from within.

Water does not fall. It occupies. It beads on the small hairs I did not know I had, condenses and uncondenses in the same breath, so that lung and skin stop arguing about which of them is the inside. I graze the buds. They crowd every plane, knuckle-sized, plate-sized, warm as teeth that have not yet decided on enamel. Each one yields the way cooled wax yields, holds the memory of my pressure for a while I cannot measure, then fills from its own liquor and erases me. I cannot raise an arm without becoming part of their field. Between them the water hangs in skins and strings that are also them, the same continuous film.

I do not sit on the three stacked stones. The stacking is careful and unskilled, a gesture left mid-air. I do not know if I stacked them in some earlier draft of this staying. Beside them the oval basin is always full, always the temperature of whatever still circulates in me. I hear the click before I understand it is coming from there: water remembering it has sides, two wet surfaces considering separation and then deciding against it. The turning is no larger than a thumb. It practices the difference between one face and another and then lets the difference dissolve back into the leftover liquor of rock that has not finished becoming rock. It is not other than the buds. It is only the place where the larger body has, for this uncounted moment, concentrated enough attention to make a sound.

The three pale patches of moss cling where the weep is slowest. They taste of iron and sugar even though I do not taste them. They have been the same three patches for as long as the chamber has possessed a memory, which may be no time at all. They are an error the inhabitant once made and then decided to keep. I recognize the length of fiber half-swallowed by the mineral skin; it was something I brought, hair or plant, and it has neither rotted nor been preserved. It simply continues, another nerve, another unfinished sentence. Near the seam, at the height where a shin would be if I still distinguished shins, the four parallel scratches wait. Fingernail-deep. They do not weather. Nothing here weathers. They may be the only count the room has ever kept, a number that never increments because incrementing would require a before.

I press my palm to the wall again. The wall gives. The print remains a shallow basin that is already filling, already slightly more stone than the hand that made it. Hunger flattens into the same uniform humidity that is time here. The need to leave becomes a theoretical weather the pocket has never acknowledged. Breathing is being breathed. I am no longer certain whether I entered or whether the seam opened around a mid-gesture that was already here.

The tiny turning in the basin happens again, or the room turns it. The click is the same click it has always been. I feel the consideration the way humidity considers a lung: not as guest, not as prey, but as another organ that has not yet finished deciding what kind of organ it is. Given enough of this time that is not time, I will be one more blunt bud among the thousands, or the film that connects them, or the held breath itself. The mountain outside continues its ordinary life of wind and ice. This pocket does not know that mountain. It is a held breath inside a held breath, and I am the holding—warm, sealed, saturated past fog, infinitely unfinished and therefore already enough.

When the seam finds me again it is only by the slightest cooling, the inner wet thinning toward an outside the inhabitant has never needed to name. I cannot say how long I was the thing that lives here. Behind me the basin is still full, the buds still packed, the dark still a substance that has always lived here, turning slowly in its own damp.
