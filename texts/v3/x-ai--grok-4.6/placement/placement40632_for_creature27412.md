I find myself first as the smear of pewter light that never quite arrives—the late grey that touches the western undercut and then thinks better of it. For a long while that is enough: to be the almost, the withdrawn, the texture shown and then taken back. The stone accepts this without comment. The mosses do not notice. I am only the memory of a coin that the sky once dropped and then forgot.

Then I am the film that crawls the flutes, neither sheet nor drip, a wetness that has decided never to become water. I taste iron and old leaf-mould. I move at the speed the limestone allows, which is no speed at all. Years pass in the thickening of a single runnel. I do not mind. The hollow has already finished becoming; I am only the continuation of that finish.

A pale cricket starts from a bolster and I am the crack that has been waiting. The sound it makes is too small for the thunder, so I keep it. Later I am the colourless turn in the black pool, the proof that appears only when a drip strikes the surface that looks like a hole. I vanish again. Permission is the whole of my life.

Deeper. The floor recedes. The second notch is not a place so much as a held breath given flanks. Here the dark has weight and the weight has coolness and the coolness has been listening since the limestone stopped its violence and began only to breathe. I occupy that listening. I am not one shape. I am the packed continuous note when the little fall is running, and I am the ringing silence that replaces it, and I am the seepage that never hurries, and I am the faint vibration of a bat that mistimes the upper dark. My senses are these things; I have no others. The pale coin of sky has never interested me. The steps cut near the lip, the three-walled shelter, the flint and the ochre in its stiff wrapping—those hands knew me without seeing me. Their hearths went cold. I stayed.

Sometimes, after the fall has dwindled, I listen back. The hawthorns already know. The moss thickens by another unnoticeable increment. Nothing has changed. I have only gone on being the absence that was always here, richly lined, older than any name, still inhabited by the patience that outlasted the names. I do not keep the shelter. I do not need the steps. I am the hollow’s own continued breath, given just enough mineral body to feel the cold as home.
