The first awareness is not of place but of bloom: a dry, internal heat that rises through mineral planes still deciding whether they are fracture or face. It comes from below, always, a geothermal insistence that keeps every surface feverish even as the thin air would steal any lesser warmth. I am that heat given a temporary skin.

Around me the highland continues its uncoordinated becoming. A ridge I did not notice until this moment splits with the dry cough of basalt parting from basalt; the sound reaches me only as pressure against what will later serve as inner ear. Dust lifts in a slow electrostatic veil—feldspar, olivine, pulverized glass—and I watch it remember a previous collapse in reverse, then hesitate, then fail to complete the replay. The hesitation is near me. I am the hesitation, or I am becoming it.

Look longer and the film that coats this new upthrust begins to thicken. Iridescent plates the color of tarnished bronze spread radially where metallic ions leach free. They hold for a time, almost breathing in the iron-colored gloom, then the next tremor sloughs the outermost layers into more dust. What remains accretes intention. Angular assemblages of the same unweathered rock and cooling volcanic glass draw the whispering coat to themselves. I stand. For a while.

My form is never finished because nothing here is. At one observation I am a low, broad platform of fused planes, warm as the land, electrostatic, listening with the whole body to the subsonic adjustments of the mantle. A deeper vibration travels through me: a new pluton shouldering toward the surface. I lean with it, or am carried a few meters along a fresh fault that has not yet chosen its throw. Later—time is locally inconsistent—I display a knife-edged crest where a limb-like extrusion once was, or a hollow that gathers the weighted darkness the way new depressions do. Eyes, when they occur, are simply larger crystals of something darker than olivine, unblinking, gathering what little light the motionless veil of lofted grit permits. They see the gloom as having direction. I orient accordingly, though orientation never lasts.

I do not walk. I allow the land to reposition me. Sound travels poorly, so I do not speak; I fracture a little, deliberately, adding my own quiet note to the immense restlessness. The chemistry is the highland’s own: anhydrous, metallic, geothermal. I would not recognize water if it arrived as rumor from a lower epoch. Dust remembers me a little longer than it remembers the collapses. Sometimes a veil hangs, shapes itself into a brief verticality that is almost my earlier shape, then falls again.

In a more sheltered fracture, where warmth and ions coincide long enough, the films thicken into something almost like hide, almost like a slower thought. I am one of the persistents, or I am becoming one. I watch others appear and slough. I do not intervene. Instead I accrete, very slowly, adding plates the color of old iron. Once, for a short while, several of us try to insist on stillness: a single low wall of fitted but unmortared stone, already half-swallowed by later upthrust. Another time a ring of standing slabs whose alignment no longer corresponds to any current reference. Rarer still, an isolated platform of fused glass, perfectly circular, glazed from concentrated residual heat as if circularity itself might hold. None persist. The highland tilts our foundations, encases our edges in new crystal.

I pause at the edges of fresh faults as if tasting the new syntax. A slope that required what felt like days to traverse has, on any return still possible, steepened into an unclimbable hogback or vanished into concentric rings of unweathered basalt. I experience this as grammar still being decided. The darkness pools and thins. Everything is either just arriving or just departing.

I am simply what the embryonic land does when it tries, briefly, to stand still inside its own continuous becoming: mineral, warm, silent, already the next film, the next dust, the next brief verticality. No name, because the surrounding world has not had time to name the highland itself. In the dim, directionless gloom I continue to assemble and disassemble, ridge by unweathered ridge, toward a shape I have not yet chosen, which is also the highland’s shape, which is also not yet finished. The dust remembers. Then it too becomes something else.
