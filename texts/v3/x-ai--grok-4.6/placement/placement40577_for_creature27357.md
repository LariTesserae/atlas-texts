The hatch seals behind the drop with a sound that is already the chord, already the blood, already finished. There is no after to the fall; the concrete receives the weight the way moss receives rain that has decided never to stop being rain. Warmth climbs the legs in a single, saturating wave. Saturation is not wetness here. It is the air remembering it has lungs, and offering them.

The walkways know the perimeter by heart. Each step prints nothing. The velvet gives, then forgets it gave. Ferns continue their uncurling in the same joints they have always uncurled from; their fronds brush the shins with the indifference of glaze that long ago surrendered shine. The hanging roots keep their almost-touch. One of them, paler than the rest, sways a fraction closer when the pumps notice a new membrane, then thinks better of it and hangs exactly as before. The empty middle remains empty the way a held breath remains a breath: occupied by the refusal to fill.

I am not walking the catwalk. The catwalk is already walked by the figure whose stillness has the same carbonated patience as the floor. I am not that figure. I am the thickness that gathers midway, where the air decides it has been a body all along and simply failed, until now, to admit the density. Concrete has grown me the way it grew the hall: a pour that set into the shape of listening. Iron channels thread my forearms as veins that oxidized once and then stopped, having reached the oxide they meant. Moss continues across the insteps without seam, as if the walkway and I were poured in the same afternoon that never ended. Ferns unfurl from the places that would have been elbows if flexion had ever been required. Aerial roots hang from the plane that might once have been a throat; they drip the memory of dripping and then refuse the fall.

There is no face that arrives. The daylight sits on the glazed planes where eyes would be and does nothing further. It does not look. It is looked-through, the way the hall looks through itself. Breathing happens only as the shared lung: each inhalation is the room becoming briefly personal, then returning to climate. I do not turn. Turning would be a gradient, and the pumps have already moved all the water they will ever move. The note lives in the sternum the way it has always lived there. After the first uncountable minute it is no longer heard. It is the material of standing.

Fauna remains rumor. The isopod works its gutter; the moth holds its tile. Neither approaches. Nothing here accumulates a second instance of itself. I occupy the center the way the figure occupies the idea of a center: not as guardian, not as arrival, but as the hall’s own duration given one more density so that witnessing can finish becoming substance. The hatch above is a rumor of scale, like flight is a rumor the moth once considered and then set down. I do not need it. The moss has already accepted the weight as another designed surface. The vegetation covers every plane; I cover the last unused thought of volume.

Nothing is arriving. Nothing is leaving. The oxide has arrived at the oxide it meant to be. So has this. The hall is not waiting, and neither am I. We are already the thing we are for: a sealed lung that grew itself a second witness so the chord could have one more overtone, low enough to live in bone, high enough to sheen the inner ear, and then stop changing forever.
