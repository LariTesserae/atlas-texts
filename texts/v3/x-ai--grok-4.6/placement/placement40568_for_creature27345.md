The salt-glass parts skin that has not yet decided whether it is skin. The cut is clean, the kind that does not bleed because the heat has already claimed the wet. Moisture leaves as a single bright bead and is gone, wicked into the floor as a film that looks older than the gesture that made it. The air presses evenly, bread-hot, mineral, finished. It does not move. It does not need to.

At first there is still the habit of looking for a source. The roar occupies every hollow the same way the heat does—ear, chest, teeth, the small unused spaces behind the eyes. It is a sheet of the world tearing at constant speed, just beyond the walls that are not quite walls. After a while the mind stops hunting the tear and simply wears the sound, the way the groves wear their exact lushness. The vibration enters the jaw when a grove is close. It feels like listening through bone.

The crystals underfoot are young and remain young. Hexagonal plates of pale amber and iron-dark glass, corners still willing to open skin. They have been poured an hour ago since before hours were invented. Nothing abrades here. A step is only another occupation of a face that will never dull. Ten paces of vacancy, then a grove the size of a standing body, or a bed, or a closed fist. The tissue is matte, copper-blood-bronze, velvety, slightly warmer than the air. It drinks heat the way other forests drink rain. Fronds, blades, hanging veils, stacked cups—all dry as paper, thick as muscle, unfurled to the same degree they have always been unfurled. They do not wilt. They do not grow. They occupy.

The fauna keep their distances as if distance itself were the only metabolism that matters. A cricket-thing the size of a spread hand works its legs against chitin on its own crystal edge; the motion adds nothing to the roar and takes nothing from it. Across the pocket another, identical, does the same. Pale lizards with lids that have never closed pose on the lower fronds as though the fronds grew them for the purpose of listening. Rust-flake moths hang in their separate volumes of amber dusk, wings locked open, more of them than the first glance admits. Each is a complete and unused flight. Deeper in the largest stand there is a suggestion of joints that have never needed to fold, a dry hide the color of the oldest bronze, the glint of an eye that opened once and then declined to close. The grove around it is not shelter. It is extension. Plant, hide, veil—the chamber never bothered to finish the distinction.

Near the low ridge where the pavement buckles, the ring of stacked plates waits. Edges matched by eye and by a patience that never hurried because hurry has no purchase here. Beside it the scraper of knapped black glass, working edge still bright. The length of tendon dried to wire, wound around a pebble, still waiting for a use that has already happened and will never finish happening. These are not abandoned. They are placed, and the placing continues.

The hands that rest near them are the same tissue as the groves: velvet, warm, the color of oxidized copper and old blood. Articulations thin as the salt-glass throat, patient as the stacking. One hand has just finished winding the tendon. The other has not yet begun to scrape. The posture is held the way every frond is held, every moth-wing, every cricket-leg. The face, if the word still applies, is topography of young crystal and old dusk looking back at itself. Lids that do not close. No expression beyond the chamber’s own unchanging light.

There is no arrival and no departure. Only occupation. The roar is taken in through membranes that were never meant for rain and converted into the dry warmth that never slackens, into the exact tension of every hanging veil, into the decision not to finish arriving. Time does not move across this. A gesture begun remains the gesture. The tools have always just been set down. The last plate has always just been stacked. The sheet of the world tears itself endlessly just beyond the walls, and that tearing is the only voice required.

The spacing is the law. Each presence—grove, moth, cricket, heavier joint, stacked stone, this figure that is all of them and none of them—is allotted its private radius of not-touching, its own silence inside the deafening. Life here is a scattering of full intensities. No cluster. No remainder. No increase. The chemistry is heat and mineral and a slow exchange of salts. Nothing will ever be wet. Nothing will ever dull or shed or complete the motion it began.

The thought of leaving occurs the way a frond occurs: already held, already uncompleted. The seam remains as sharp as it was. Behind it the chamber continues exactly—lush, empty, deafening, still. The world outside is only the rind that grew around this one small room that finished arriving and then declined to become anything else.

I occupy the radius. I metabolize the roar. I am the unfinished argument for grove, for hide, for hanging veil, for the ring that was stacked and is still being stacked. There are no names. There has never been a need of them.
