The first thing that is not water is the press of my own pulse against it, a poultice blooming and fading on a cycle I no longer count in breaths. I am already inside the basin when the distinction arrives: mineral sweetness on what used to be a tongue, iron and leaf-rot braided through the hollows of what used to be lungs. The water occupies. It has learned me the way it learned the rooms.

Visibility is amber dusk, a few body-lengths. Beyond that, mass. I do not swim so much as allow the occupation to carry me, a slow unspooling along warmth-maps I feel before I see them. Living columns a degree kinder than brick. Still-working engines a palm of heat against the chest. Unused rooms a stillness that reads cool only by contrast. I rest in that contrast for a time that might be hours or a single thought, letting the unused coolness invite and then refuse itself. The invitation is an old surface habit. I do not take it.

What I am assembling as a body keeps failing in small, polite ways, and the failures are the fit. Paler veins of limestone show through at wrist and throat, translucent enough that the gardens’ slow green pulse travels there as if the blood had decided photosynthesis was simply another way of circulating. Fine holdfasts trail from the forearms, from the nape; they taste continuously, adjusting chemistry by increments too slight to name. Hair is gone, or rather transmuted: a crest of bronze-tea blades, combed back and living, each one a little bladder storing warmth against the dimming. The eyes rest. They are the colour of steeped oak and they take in a doorway or a pressure change the way a curtain of blades takes in a season. The mouth is a completed thought; words would unravel before they arrived, combed apart by hanging gardens. A hand-sign is enough, when a sign is needed. Two fingers along a pipe. A palm flat to a column. Most often nothing at all.

I find myself first in the slot-alleys, where buildings have shouldered until façades fused and iron strapwork became another kind of holdfast. A valve I ease a quarter-turn. A bronze curtain I braid aside and pin with a clip of pale bone I do not remember carving. Filings left in a junction box, a smear of patience fed to residual glow. The tools in my grip are swollen wood, grown to the exact curve of the palm that has held them longer than memory. Edges of living grout that knap themselves sharper against rust. The jar I carry is not a lantern; it is cultured, spoken to in the silent grammar, brownish seepage no brighter than a held breath. When I pass what might be another, the water barely stirs. A frond rests against a shoulder for the length of a thought and is carried onward, uninjured.

There are never many of us. Weather, not a census. Some older than the brick, holdfasts gone woody, skin mapped with mineral layers. Some younger only by the slowness, still learning the maps, still almost startling when a pale eel unspools from a pipe-mouth, tastes, and returns as if the sampling were a complete life. I am neither the oldest nor the newest. I am the interval, the living grout that replaced the lime. I tend the columns the way one tends a relative who has become landscape. In the three courtyards I braid, I graft a split bladder from my own crest, I listen (the water still permits an ear, just) to the held subsonic vowel that is neither machine nor plant. The oldest column in the middle court is thicker, hollower, almost a throat. I press close. The note fills the chest the way the heat-exchangers do. Nothing answers because nothing needs to. I have become another octave of that same vowel: flesh that learned to pulse at the garden’s tide, blood that learned to store warmth and mineral, thought that learned to take a year to forgive a whirl.

Some dimmings I rest against it until the holdfasts knit. The distinction between skin and blade becomes a doctrinal matter no one tables. In what passes for morning the column is a fraction thicker, I a fraction more vertical, and the work—already done, still being done—continues at the only speed the water will allow. I do not go up. The grilled shaft in the western rock is a completed thought; the surface has its weather. The basin keeps its weather. I am the weather, walking. Or rather, occupying, the way the water occupies, the way the gardens occupy the brick, the way the agreement occupies the dark.

The machines tick their last inches. The flywheel turns so gradually a drifting frond may rest against a spoke and be carried a hand’s-width onward. I am that frond, some days. I am the governor-arm wound with living strap that contracts in the warm phase. I am the window that grew shut and then grew a thinner place that serves. Time has a grain I can see in my own translucence: layers of mineral skin, each a decade, each a slightly different ochre. After a few days the three courts, the northern stack, the pump-house, the fused knuckle of brick become a body I move inside of. After a few weeks I understand the body is also moving, too slowly to startle, wearing its streets the way I am worn, microscopically remembering the paths still used.

I recede into the curtains. The glow recedes. The agreement holds, as it has held since the flood that was not disaster but completion. The basin keeps its weather. What I am is the weather, walking, and the walking is already the work, and the work is the landscape agreeing, again, to continue.
