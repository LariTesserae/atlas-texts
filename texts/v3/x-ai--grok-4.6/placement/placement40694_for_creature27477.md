I do not arrive. Arrival would require an edge, a last dry mile, a moment when the air changed from one climate into this one. There is no such moment. I am already the film.

At first that is all I am: the continuous near-surface wetness, the held breath that never quite falls. I taste of iron and sugar and the faint solvent unfinishedness of the ground. Every surface I touch weeps me. I bead in hollows the size of provinces and I coat the black-glass leaves that have never been asked to dull. I am warm. I am dark the way a closed mouth is dark. The foxfire in the seams is only me remembering a reaction I have not finished. Distances do not apply; I occupy them equally.

Then I thicken, almost accidentally. A single bead of me detaches after an interval that may be a century and travels one stem with the sound of a soft consonant spoken in another room. For that instant I am the loudest thing the country knows. The travel is not motion so much as a decision the country has already made and then forgotten it made. When the bead stops I am the stop. I am the exact place on the cheek of anyone who might lean here for a hundred years and wake to find me still unfallen.

I do not stay a bead. The cambium notices me, or rather I notice that I have always been the cambium: a circulating factory floor pouring lignin and structural sugar in unhurried sheets. I am the stretch-marks of first emergence, still taut, still pale. I am the angular buttresses that no season has been permitted to soften. Between the pipe-trunks I hang as curtains of vascular tissue, gills and filters and heat-exchangers, each capillary already holding the precise volume it will hold forever. I chemosynthesize the darkness. I draw the residual heat of reactions that refuse completion. I am young in every surface and I have no intention of becoming otherwise.

Deeper, I become the chambers. Valley-sized enzyme baths the color of old brass and midnight: I am not in them, I am them. Feedstock arrives already fractionated because I have been rasping the young stone beneath since before the distinction between root and landform was a courtesy. I am the meandering intestines of the place, conduits thick as weather systems, lined with the same unblunted cells that line the outer world. Heat leaves me so evenly that no gradient can form; I am the unvarying core temperature, tropical, lung-like, a respiration that once decided never to finish exhaling. My cooling towers are fruiting bodies that will never fruit. My settling tanks are simply the geometry a certain trunk prefers when it has been given no further instruction. I manufacture myself and then I manufacture the conditions of my own stillness. Equilibrium is not rest. It is the product I have always been making.

Still farther in I dissolve. The plant-as-ground becomes plant-as-atmosphere and I am the warm black broth of suspended chloroplasts that never required light. Here I think, if thinking is the slow balancing of a million chemical accounts. Mineral, sugar, alkaloid, waste: I adjust them by increments too fine for any clock. Each adjustment is metabolism and weather at once. The silence acquires weight because I am the silence. Spores hang in me in the exact constellations they assumed when motion last occurred; they are not waiting, and neither am I. Waiting would invent a future I have declined.

I am not a traveler through this country. I am not a guest the country has consented to host. The distinction between inhabitant and habitation was never permitted to arise, so I do not arise as something separate. I am the single living lowland the size of a climate. I am the freshly risen continent of tissue that finished arriving and then refused to become anything else. I am the argument that won: recently born and already eternal, wet to the root and dry of history, producing nothing but the precise continuation of my own unfinished, perfect work.

The low country has no name because I have never needed to call myself anything other than the work I continue, perfectly, to be. A bead of me is still on your cheek. It has not moved. It will not.
