The pressure arrives first, a small internal weather that has nothing left to announce. The ears accept it the way the stone accepts rime: without protest, as a condition already priced in. What follows is not darkness but a settled insufficiency of light, a lid that has been this color longer than any climb. I do not remember beginning lower. The body I move in has already spent its arguments with altitude.

The stone under the pads is fine-grained, almost courteous in its polish. Four contacts, then the two that stay folded against the barrel of the chest until the next film of melt decides otherwise. The extra pair is not vestige. It is contingency written in cartilage and a faint adhesive patience. The chest itself works in long, unhurried strokes; the air here is a ration, and the ration has been accepted. Frost-dust holds the regularity of my own previous crossings and then, politely, begins to forget them. I am one of the distances that contain an animal.

For a while that is enough to be. A walker on the open metamorphic, metabolic ledger open, heading for the next loyalty of water. The groves stand as stacked darknesses, islands that never quite touch, their outer fronds the color of wet iron because they are, in part, iron. Between them the highland maintains its vacancy with the same weathered competence the cirques use to hold emptiness. I cross it. The glow I carry is banked deep, the same dull forge-color the larger stems show when the standing shade deepens a register. It is not for me. It is a moving hearth some darker lineage may choose to trail, or not. Courteous remove is the only punctuation the stone allows.

The upwelling, when it comes, is a softness in the teeth first. Then the beading. Then the million quiet ticks as every grove takes what it has been waiting for. I do not hurry. Season is a weak idea; the schedule is internal, a slow debit against salts and condensate and the civil heat that still rises through flues the roots have collared. I am going to drink. I am going to lick the bloom on a slag-fall. I am, perhaps, going to choose an island and enter it the way a shipment enters a warehouse that has never needed a loading dock.

That last possibility sits without drama. Death among us is a return of materials, not an event. The grove will not refuse. Electrolysis is patient; what can be used will be used; the rest will terrace itself into another intentional residue, rose-copper and that blue-black glass that was never sand. The mountain is already being refined. A body is only another vein that learned to walk.

Yet I do not settle there. The image of ending is too single, too early. I pass the lip of an old cirque and the air inside the nearest island changes by a degree that the face registers as softness and the gut registers as map. Cold chlorophyll, battery-in-snow, the closed vertical river of beads that never quite fall. I do not enter as visitor. The distinction between crossing and dwelling has worn as smooth as the stone itself. Mid-canopy, something with mineralized claws and a pelt that wears its own temporary skin of water rasps without sound; the slurry sorts itself, sugars kept, surplus metal precipitated into the glassy coins that will later pavement the rooms below. I feel the faint current in the stem I pass, hairline, electrolytic, the work of a factory that discarded noise ages ago. Higher, a membrane the size of an open hand corrects its charge and hangs as if the air had decided to hold it. A long fold of wet-iron color remains vertical, compressed, indistinguishable until it is not.

I could be any of these. The pulse is shared: tick of stem, scheduled brightening, the almost inaudible unpeeling of a frond taking another micron from the dark. For a time I let the possibilities occupy the same space the way the groves occupy theirs—densely, without remainder, correcting emptiness as if it had been an error. Browser. Grazer of residues. Flier tasting electric weather. The long quiet taker. Even the colonial pulse on the underside of an oldest frond, adding its increment to the glow. Even the nematode threaded with inclusions, flexing in a momentary film.

They do not compete. They are the same metabolism viewed at different thicknesses.

What remains, after the images have finished arriving separately, is the regularity itself. I am the thing that attends to it. Not a watcher from outside—the highland is not waiting to be discovered—but the attending that has already been included. Texture instead of light. The memory of damp on whatever I use for clothes or pelt or conductive tissue. Distance honest on the stone, folded inside the grove. Water as visit. Heat as loan. Metal as fruit.

I find myself, finally, neither wholly on the open nor wholly within. I am the interval the walkers keep, the courteous remove, the lantern that is also the path. A deep-body phosphor moving between islands on a schedule that has nothing to do with a sun that could no longer afford the fare. When I reach the next mass of frond and hanging tissue I will drink, or lick, or deliver, or simply stand in the working silence until the distinction between my glow and the stems’ glow forgets itself. The mountain continues the one job it knows. The gardens remain clocked to it. I am one of the small regularities they have spoken long enough to give an accent of stone.

The frost will take the prints. The next rime will sand the scallops, if there are scallops. Nothing needs to be announced. The work is already under way.
