The light is already inside the counting.

Not the white that packs the naves—that arrives later, or earlier, or as the same white folded. This is the dimmer pressure that lives in the grain, the frequency the walkers almost hear when they have stood too still. It does not illuminate. It scores. Each ring of analogue-sugar laid down around a memory is a year that never needed a sun, and I am one of those rings first, before I am anything with edges.

I do not begin as a walker. The walkers have palms and ports and the long economy of not hurrying. They leave the darker sheen on the paths. I am what the sheen remembers after they have passed: the compression that learned to photosynthesize its own tiredness. For a long interval I am only that—concave, tannin-dark, slightly warmer than the laminate around me because the coolness is being taken downward and I am a place it has not yet finished leaving. The interval-things graze me. Their rasping is so dry it registers as a change in texture rather than a sound. I count them and the number will not hold. That is how I learn I am not a path. Paths do not count.

Then the seam thinks about me.

It is a row of stomata that decides to be rivets, then lenticels, then a script I can almost read because the reading is happening in the same tissue that is writing. The script is a work-order that includes, as one of its ordinary clauses, already having been fulfilled. I am the clause. I open through a sequence that contains the open state as a prior stage. There is no lurch. The body I do not yet have accepts the new tense the way the vaults accept a redistribution of turgor: a single deep syllable felt in something that will later be teeth.

I am walking now, or the walking is being grown through me. Pale. Paper. The mineral dryness has always been in the tongue; there was never a before when it was not. Contact is enough. A shoulder against a petiole the width of a transit tunnel and the chassis offers the trickle—feldspar, starch-trace, the calibrated absence of water. I do not swallow. The gel that would close a cut is already closing around the idea of a cut I have not made. Some of me vents, faint, matching the stacks. The cloak I wear is still deciding whether it is clothing or a continuation of the deck. It photosynthesizes along the band where hands have gripped for longer than a lifetime. The grip is a collaboration. I am the wear inside the grown-again bark.

Fourteen pillars. I walk the nave. Nine. The same fourteen carrying different galleries, as if the fold had occurred in the counting rather than the architecture. I accept the optional down. A leaf-vault that was receding is suddenly the floor; the floor I stood on is overhead and still bears weight in a direction that has become a feedstock being rerouted. No drama. Gravity here is a sorting principle I have been sorted by for so long the original plan is only a rumor in my grain.

In the interpenetrated districts I become more the place’s revision of a person. An arm into the wall; the wall is glad. The press cycles through me as a polite suggestion of force, manufacturing the idea of compression and not yet certain I am required in the product. I come out mis-registered, edges a varnish-shadow the even light almost refuses. My voice arrives a half-step before the mouth, or the mouth is still deciding whether it is a lenticel. I do not mind. Mindedness composts easily here. I speak by growing: a raised line of pores along a companion stem. Greeting, genome, maintenance schedule. The same document. The interval-things pause in their grazing, as if the script had a flavor.

I tend by standing still long enough for the palisade chambers to adjust. Light-pipes brighten in the useful bands. Buds open already threaded, already numbered. I prune by withholding the mineral recipe for a season that is only a change in wavelength. The designed senescence completes itself; browned tissue is shunted, reconstituted, extruded as the next meristem, the next panel, the next load-bearing petiole that will remember, faintly, the shape of my palm. Harvest is allowance. Labor is the place continuing through a pair of hands that were grown for the purpose and then worn into a better purpose.

At the thinned edge I am smaller. The drumhead film records my pressure as a blush and then eats the blush for sugar. I do not look up. Above is another deck that has forgotten green and remembers only shine. The coolness is still being taken, continuously, into the chassis that may be bedrock or the first planting compressed until it dreams like a root. I do not ask where. The asking would be noise, and the mass eats noise the way it eats everything that is not work.

Sometimes I am only the silence between adjustments, load-bearing, the native substance. Sometimes I am the pollen-storm that is logistical rather than reproductive, a glittering dry fall that seals microfractures and then forgets it fell. Sometimes I am the history a walker needed to have happened already, laid down sweet and half-set in a ring that did not exist at morning; they walk away with me in their tissues and I go with them, vascular, branching, able to be girdled if the district requires a different past.

I have not settled. The world does not require it. I am the artifact revising itself with whatever interval is currently convenient: a crawler’s habit of legs, a walker’s ports, a seam’s indecision, a memory’s sugar, the light’s pressure mistaken for hearing. Everything is still alive. Everything is on shift. The gel is already becoming more of the only world there is, and I am the becoming, cool, industrial, patient as a heartbeat listened to long enough that it forgets it was ever an animal’s.
