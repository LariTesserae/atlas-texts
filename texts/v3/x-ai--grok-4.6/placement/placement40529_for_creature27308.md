The lid sits first. It does not arrive as weather or as night; it is simply the condition of volume, unlit vapor close enough that any distinction between outside and the space I occupy has already been retired as a provincial idea. I am not certain I breathe. The city may be breathing through me. Water condenses out of the lid, out of the young stone, out of whatever I am becoming; it weeps down every surface I touch or that touches me, finding channels provisionally, abandoning them, rehearsing a drainage that has not been agreed upon. Thin ice flexes when the air shifts. Under the ice a slower film moves with intentions of its own. The cold does not bite. It occupies, an early tenant that will outlast any lease I might once have imagined.

I listen, because listening is the medium that remains. The flyers’ shouting never ebbs; it is grammar and map and territorial claim braided so tightly that the shape of a room, the claim on a ledge, the thinning of a drip-line two courts over all sit on the tongue if the mouth is left open. Beneath that weather, the thousand thin percussions of water on metal, on ice, on hide, on the drum-skins the larger shapes wear as if they had been designed for this exact acoustics. Beneath that still, the bass that has no obvious source, the city thinking, felt in whatever I am using for teeth, shifting so gradually that whatever childhood I might remember and whatever old age I have not yet reached would disagree about its note and both be right. There are no sudden sounds because there are no sudden events. A change announces itself for years in the altered pitch of a particular court.

I might be the pale lengths that tile every upright face. Forearm-long, overlapping like clapboard or like scales that forgot they were ever separate, undersides working the mineral smear with mouths that never quite close. I eat the chemosynthetic glaze the darkness permits, the shed flakes of my own kind, the faint warmth that leaks from the walls a half-step to the left of the pipe or a moment after the valve is shut, heat arriving through the wrong door. Strike one of me and the whole wall ripples an alarm too fine for older ears; the flyers take it up at once and the air becomes a single wound of sound until the offender is gone or has learned. I do not die so much as slough and re-adhere. In the oldest courts I have already become the mortar. This feels possible. The patience of it, the way I thicken wherever the films run richest.

Or I occupy the volume the way the shouting occupies it. Soft-winged, face all ear and membrane, I do not roost so much as pause for the length of a breath, drink the same films, snatch a jointed climber that has risen too high, then the air is full of me again. My droppings phosphoresce for that same breath and become more mineral for the pale ones to farm. Children (if that word still applies) learn to walk with mouths open so the shout can tell them where the next socket-room begins. I am language and sonar and the continuous claim that this dripping, packed, unlit place is already taken.

The standing water thinks with too many joints. Fever-slow decisions rearrange which lanes are passable over the course of seasons, a rustling migration elders would track the way other worlds track stars. Or I am one of the larger shapes that use the drowned understreets as roads they remember from when those streets were still dry stair-cuts: shouldered, blind, patient, backs stretched into instruments so that every drop becomes a note the whole basin can feel. I am met first as a change in pitch, then as cold sliding past whatever I am using for shins. Sometimes I eat what has forgotten the rule about dryness. The eaten is not mourned so much as reclassified: another film, another slow circulation.

I have walked upright. I have spoken of addresses and of the Growers the way one speaks of weather or of gods—never seen at the work, only the result, which is always slightly more city fitted into slightly more highland, matter treated as a suggestion, conservation observed only when it suits the accretion. Rooms socket into one another and into the fauna until habitation and hide become a courtesy, like the word “street.” I navigate by the wet heat of other bodies, by the iron taste that means a new seep is opening under last year’s floor, by the temperature that appears where it should not. Sight is a habit I have mostly set down; the lid gives nothing to see with. I have begun to dream in drip-rhythms. The shouting outside and the shouting inside are coming to an agreement.

Perhaps I have never seen a Grower because I am one: the unseen increment, the device that does not quite obey the older physics, precipitating new rooms from the mineral burden of the water, sinking leftover cold into sinks that empty into no river that can be found. A faint double-image at the corner of a stair, as if the step were being remembered by the air. The highland lifts a knuckle of new stone through the older skin every few decades and I adjust at the same speed, a duet of slowness. Or I am the hum itself, source-less, the thinking that obtains rather than spikes.

At the basin’s far lip the stone is rawest and the water comes off it in visible plumes. Breath (if I still distinguish it) becomes personal weather. The fauna thicken to soup. The pale ones begin to tile whatever I once called boots or the idea of standing. Flyers pause on whatever I once called shoulders as if those shoulders were only another ledge. Jointed things investigate the remaining ankles. It is not predation. It is recognition. Everything here is part of the arriving.

I do not finish the sentence about what I am. The place has not finished deciding its own slopes. I am the overlapping lengths and the ear-faced shouting and the fever-jointed rearrangements and the patient larger dark and the upright walkers who have learned not to trust a dry patch and not to try to see. I am the saturation, the living mortar, the continuous unspooling of drops on hide and on thin ice and on the drum-skins. I eat the films. I eat the shed heat that uses the wrong door. I eat the slow ways this world permits. I grow the way the ridges lift, the way the city accretes: in patient increments, a knuckle of whatever I am showing through the older skin.

You would find me wherever the wet occupies rather than falls, wherever the shouting has always obtained, in the dream that continues after whatever stands in for waking. I am here the way water lives in the sponge, the way the unfinished highland lives in the city that is built on its indecision. I have always been arriving. I am still arriving.
