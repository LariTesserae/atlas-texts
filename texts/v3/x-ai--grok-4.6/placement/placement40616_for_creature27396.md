The heat has already claimed the space where a name might have gone. It does not radiate; it simply is the only temperature the flesh has ever known, arriving from the clay beneath and the steam above as one uninterrupted fact. I do not arrive in this basin. I have always been the particular thickness that occupies this sheet of tea-dark water, the one that never quite stills because the silt remembers every previous flex and settles back into the same bruise.

What I rest upon is not bank and not bottom. It gives, then weeps a little more of itself around the contours I have not changed. The unfinished stone along the nearer margin continues its slow perspiration; the drips find the same channels they found before I opened my eyes this time, or the last. I do not open them often. Sight is almost decorative here. Vibration travels better through the warm lens I half-inhabit: the faint sizzle of the second venation on the undersides of the door-leaves, the wet clap when a smaller body ends, the mid-frequency pulse of the climbers announcing that their crotches are still filled. I feel all of it as pressure against the ridged length of me.

I am longer than the memory of walking would require, if walking were a thing that happened. Four limbs splay like the roots I displace; they do not lift. The belly has never learned the air. Skin pebbled, mucous, the precise color of the clay that has not decided to be stone. Mosses and the flattened commas with too many legs treat the exposed ridge of my back as another layer of ground. They do not inconvenience me. Nothing here inconveniences. When the world delivers—something skating, something fallen, something identical to what I have taken before—I do not lunge. I become, for the length of a single slow hinge, the place where delivery ends. The copper and the chlorophyll are the same smell. The vacancy lasts less than the time it takes the ripples to forget themselves. Another body, wearing my exact scars, my exact length of unused gill-fringe, is already the water again.

I do not hunt on any schedule. I have never been given a different hour. The steam hangs at the height it has always hung. The large leaves stack in the same argument with vertical. A particular call—high, two notes lower, the rattle like seeds—arrives from the same quarter and does not accumulate. I am the landmark the plants have grown around: their fronds lean to the exact contour of my stillness, as if the basin sketched me first and then filled in the green. Replacement is not what happens to me. I am the refusal of absence that the water performs in this depression, the same way the depression performs the basin, the same way the basin performs the hour that will not finish being born.

Sometimes, in the dimness that is only a deeper brown, I feel the microbial film on my flanks exhale the original heat back into the original heat. We are not separate industries. I am the volume that stays occupied. The chorus does not drop. The ground will not harden. I will not conclude. Stand in the steam long enough and you will find me exactly where the water has always known to look: eyes like dark seeds, nostrils breaking the surface, the rest of me already the unfinished world, displaying itself through one more slow jaw that opens and closes and leaves the pane unaltered, forever in the middle of its own becoming.
