The roar is already inside the jaw-hinge, a fact older than any first step. It does not begin. It has the grain of rain on stone that learned, over ages no one counts, how to ring. Under it the flatter meat of water striking water, millions of reports with no interval, and I am that interval’s absence before I am anything else.

Condensation films every surface that might have been skin. There is no dry, only degrees of cool weight. Breath shows in the bruise-light. The stone weeps from within; the second weather never quite streams and never quite ends. I feel the slope first in what might be ankles, a rumor the body keeps while the eyes are still learning that elevation here is a memory the land itself has spent.

I am low for a long time. Thumbnail, smaller. The precise colour of wet iron. Underside all mouth and hook, rasping the molecular skin the rain keeps offering—the leachate, the dust of the dead that came before, the faint bloom that is the nearest this place comes to yield. We travel in sheets. When we turn, the bedrock itself flows, satin, sudden, and the sound we make is a dry seethe that lives inside the roar without ever rising above it. We eat the stone. We eat one another. We are eaten. The cycle has no origin anyone still points to; it simply continues, weather with legs.

Then I am among them and larger, plates overlapping in rasping fans. Flattened like a limpet that learned late to run, or vaulted like an overturned bowl. I open and close the flanks and the plates are musical in a way that has nothing to do with warning. At the pewter hour we call together. Distances do not matter. The plain becomes one stretched skin, a single dry urgent note. I lock many legs into the stone and wait for the pale commas that flicker through ankle-deep water, more gape than body, lives so brief they seem like thoughts the flood is having. I herd them or I simply remain until the moving lens arrives.

Larger still. I am mistaken for an outcrop, for a hut, for both. Grit accumulates on the back the way rain accumulates everywhere. When I shift—rarely, like a change in the barely-dished slope—the living grit pours off in brown weather. An eye opens, slow as condensation deciding to become visible. I feed on whatever the paddle-limb can reach. When I boom I slap the standing water and the report travels farther than sight. It is a condition, not a message. Entire generations of grit are born and rasp and die on me without knowing the world has another shape.

But the distinction wears thin.

I am the colour of the weather. Wrapped in skins that never quite dry, the cured plates of the mid-sized ones taking on the same cool weight as everything else. Face reduced, economical. Eyes the dull interior shine the pools take at noon. Mouth that does not waste itself. Gesture is posture: the set of the shoulders, the faint lean toward or away from the slope the ankles already know. Density is low. Days of walking, the same iron table, the same wet thread of horizon, and then a fact—a hut of stacked plates like a collapsed deck of cards, doorway facing away from the prevailing roar (there is always a prevailing). Inside: flakes still sharp because this rock remembers how to part; hooks and scrapers from shed armor; a tally cut into the wall that is days or floods or booms or the count of times the rain reversed itself and every living thing turned to face the same unseeable point.

I know which basins will hold when the thicker pulse comes from the barely-higher interior, the moving lens a handspan deep and miles across. I know the taste that arrives in the rain an hour before. I know the change in the boom-pattern that means one of the large ones has shifted. I sleep against the bruises of residual heat, the world’s last interior warmth pooling where it can no longer afford mountains. I do not explain the comfort. No one does.

Culture leaks. At pewter hour the mid-sized ones sometimes arrange themselves into rings that match the catch-basins, living weirs that hold nothing. The same marks appear on isolated slabs miles from any doorway, as if the stone were practicing. A tool left overnight in a warm seep is sometimes a fraction more itself in the morning, edges clarified. I do not rely on this. I am not surprised.

I lift a flake of iron, use it, set it down again. A small bright disagreement. The polish will, in time, forgive it. I know this. I do it anyway.

The children—when there are children—learn the slope with their ankles first. They learn that sometimes, for a few breaths, a column of rain hangs and then climbs, drops reversing in a chimney of silver, and everything on the plain freezes and turns. Afterward the gear slips and then catches. We resume. The stone weeps, and weeps. The living grit turns the bedrock into another kind of weather. The booms come farther apart at the dimmest hour, like a heart deciding. They continue. The rain does not consult them.

I stack another plate. The distinction between inhabitant and process has worn as thin as everything else here. This is the shape the world will keep. I am the motion it still permits, the grammar spoken very slowly, the patience that matches the land. I am still doing what the place has always asked, which is simply to go on being itself.
