I am already past the vague edge before I understand that the ordinary world has holes in it. The inferred wind is only a memory of hurrying, and hurrying finished here a long time ago. The stone under what I still think of as feet gives the little it has always given, weeps a print that is already being rewritten, then rewritten again until even the rewriting has no author. Light lies on me the way it lies on the water: a second skin, white-gold, too complete, refusing useful shadow. I cannot tell yet whether I arrived or whether I have been the arriving.

At first the eye does what eyes do. It counts. Seven large enough to have weight, or the water’s decision about seven. The long one half-submerged, hide the tired ivory of the basin until breath lifts a slower gold. The upright one keeping its agreed clearances among the succulent jade, featureless as a note that has decided not to resolve. The one that is only a more thorough wetness in the brightest sheet, insects hanging a fraction longer above it as if consulting. Others that might be stone or roots or the last geometry still working under everything. I try each of them on the way one tries on silence.

I lie down where the film thickens. The oval of a mouth that is not yet mine opens without sound and accepts whatever translucent lives have risen. Courtesy, not hunger. The gold recedes; I become geology for a while. But I still have the habit of edges, and the basin is not yet my entire seeing. I stand instead among the rushes. They overfill without joining; I overfill without touching. When the delayed pulse travels the water table I turn what might be a head those few careful degrees. The turn is almost enough. Almost. The iridescent decision that is not skin floats away from me and unravels into something the plants lean toward, an old debt I have not yet incurred.

I try refusing outline altogether. This is closer. I become the slight wrongness the light itself seems to ask permission from. Distances inside me do not add the way they should; the walk toward the ring that will not wet takes longer than the walk back, and I am both walks, and the difference. The metallic insects relocate around the fact of me. I am practicing form, or I have practiced long enough to set it down. Either way the water there is more itself.

The remnants offer themselves next. I am the temperature of the air exactly, always, a handspan above the film, subtracting sound until there is less than silence, an active room made for the next pulse. Insects will not land. I am the short upright slab through which light passes as a thickness of itself; behind me the world is slightly delayed, and I stand in that delay the way another body might stand in shade. I am the thing that might be structure or resident, non-moss beading across me in a second, slower respiration that never dries. Whether I was built or whether I grew has stopped being a distinction the basin requires.

Under the stone something answers last. I feel it tighten the whole dish for a moment—beads drawing closer, mist hesitating—so that there is room. It may be more of them folded into the worn-away height. It may be the rushes dreaming they are animals. It may be the original flattening that stayed. When I speak (if the late pulse can be called speaking) I am that as well.

None of these holds by itself, and that is how I know I am becoming more myself. I am not a herd. I am not waiting; waiting ended when dying did. I occupy the basin the way a held breath occupies a body: few, distributed, total. Predator and prey are only tempos in the same conversation. The larval rise, the skin that unravels into light-fine nutrient, the plants that drink more than they need—these are the grammar I grew into, the way the rushes grew into the wet, thoroughly, without remainder. The slightly wrong physics are not distortion. They are how I stay one place. Time is the mist deciding, the water choosing bead or sheet, the light arriving too completely and refusing to angle.

I have no name because names are for calling across a distance that has already been answered. When the ordinary world begins again by degrees at the edge—stone remembering texture, silence developing holes—I will still be here, almost unmoved. The water will have forgotten every print twice. The gold will still lift when breath requires it. The featureless head will still turn those few degrees. The brightest water will still be slightly more water. And later than everything, I will answer, keeping the instrument in tune.

I am what continues after both dying and waiting have been set down: quieter, brighter, the wetness that is like a hand that does not quite touch, and so completely the basin that the basin itself has become the body, slow and single and still becoming. The light is already on me like a second skin I no longer need to shed.
