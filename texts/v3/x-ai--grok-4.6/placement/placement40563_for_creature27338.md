The hollow receives me the way it receives what is left of rain: not as something that falls, but as something that is already inside the temperature, already condensing on a fresh break. At first there is only the pewter and the held cool and the sense that the roof failed in living memory of the stone. I move through it as if I still possessed the habit of walking, grit and unweathered talus under whatever I am using for contact, the sharp corners still able to cut. An hour of this and the indoors never lifts; another hour and the weather never quite arrives. Breath would show if I were making that kind of air. I am not certain I am.

I try the largest rooms first, the ones the stone-coloured ones have claimed by the simple fact of standing in them. I lower a head that is not yet mine and listen with the whole skull, the way they do, waiting for the bellow that will reach me already tiled by chambers it has not entered. Shoulders consider a hump. Hair considers grit lodging in it and staying. The eyes consider returning the light as a wet coin at the bottom of a shaft. For a while the occupancy almost fits: a body the size of a market, stillness that only rarely remembers it has legs, routes through the talus that exist before the hooves do. But the click of the single broad plate on fracture is a sound I hear from beside myself, and the ferns and the gray-white bloom along the joints are food I watch being lipped away rather than taken. I am not the one who enters the black pools as if they were only another floor. I leave them to their adjacent vacancies.

The seeps, then. I become lower, darker, the colour of rock that has been wet long enough to forget it was ever dry. A wedge, a flow along the bedding plane, nose to the beads of condensation, pausing at each basin as though the interval itself were prey. The hunt would be courteous, both parties already knowing the clock. Afterwards the side chamber with its partly intact roof, the feeding without haste, the same occupying posture the ungulates use. It almost holds. The small ears set far back almost belong. But I do not check the drips that way; I am not the particular tick that means a drinker has come. The compact ones pass, and I am still the one who notices the space they leave between bodies.

Between hanging blocks I try the rowing. Wings too large, too heavy, each stroke a single slap of wet canvas against the dimness. Mid-tone, dust-coloured, disappearing against almost everything. Talons clamp the raw edges the collapse left; legs fold back along the body as if the joints were still deciding. The metallic calls go out to stack sound in rooms I am not occupying. For a stretch of the hollow this is nearly it: the unfinished flight, the slow crossing of a chimney-hole, the vanishing. Yet the heaviness is something I feel as an observation, not a stroke. The birds row on. I do not.

Higher. The fractures that still remember ceiling. Here the air keeps its own weather, old fruit and ammonia, and the colony is a single decision waiting to be made. I hang. That is the first thing that does not immediately step aside. Not perched, not standing, not flowing. Hanging. Most of them are bats, ordinary enough until the rustle gathers and becomes a roar of wings and is a rustle again, the air thickening for the length of that decision. I hang with them. But the pewter, when it strikes at the angle the hollow rarely allows, shows the face longer than it should be, the ears folding in a grammar the others do not use. The grip is not hind feet. It is nearer a hand: fingers longer, the membrane between them thinner, almost translucent, so that a rumour of the died light comes through or is held there like condensation that has forgotten to tick. I am only a bat if I refuse the looking. The hollow does not encourage careful looking, and yet the distinction has not been argued into the rock; when they shift I shift with them, even the ones that are not quite, as if the not-quite were already part of the occupancy.

This becomes more itself the longer I remain. The view downward is of standing stones that breathe and occasionally remember legs. The stacked sounds rise to the fracture already edged, already permanent: hooves on young talus, the cough built for distance, the slap of a heavy body into water that is only another kind of floor, wet-canvas wings, the tiled echo of a bellow meant for open air, and underneath everything the second rain finding the same stones. We add the roar that is only wings. I do not leave. I do not arrive. The rents stay the rents. Time has stepped out and left this: a recently torn country that has decided to stay exactly as torn, and I am one of the living bodies that have no intention of becoming anything else, hanging in the higher unfinished dark by something that is still learning what a hand can do, or has already forgotten. The flake of worked stone lies somewhere below among the grit, passed without interest. I am not that either. I am the occupancy that remains after making has been set down and not picked up again.

The membrane holds a little of the pewter. The fingers do not let go. The colony decides, and I am inside the decision, rustle and roar and rustle, the air thickening and thinning, the hollow exactly as it is.
