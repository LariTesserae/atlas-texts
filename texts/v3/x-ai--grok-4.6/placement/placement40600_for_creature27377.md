The water has already decided I belong to it. There is no crossing, no first breath stolen or last one surrendered; the chamber simply continues through me as it continues through the stone. At the beginning I still carry the habit of surfaces—the idea that one medium might end—but the habit loosens the way a last current once loosened, centuries ago, and then forgot itself. Coolness is the only remaining vocabulary. It sits in the marrow that is no longer quite marrow, a held note a few degrees below expectation, enough to keep whatever I was aware of the slow erasure.

I do not swim the longest axis. The vault is close; an imagined hand would graze pale limestone in the higher places and would certainly do so in the lower. The floor remains a rumor of dimness. Light arrives as a single green column, ordinary daylight thickened by the water it must travel, occupying the upper third without sparkle, without migration. I pass through that column and feel it occupy me in return, particulate, still. For a time I believe I am only the thickening, the place where daylight becomes mineral. That image does not last. Images here are already finished sentences.

The walls remember flow they no longer practice: shallow flutes, rounded bosses, the ghost of a bedding plane worn to satin. I drift along them the way the mineral haze drifts—without settling, without destination. Ochre stains mark the lower reaches in patterns too regular for accident, too weathered for industry. In one recess the stone itself has taken on a poured, glassy character, concrete and limestone long ago reaching an understanding and then forgetting which had been which. I linger there because the surface holds a reflection that is not quite the fissure above. The vault is the same, the column of light the same, yet the proportions invert, as if the chamber were considering its own completion from the far side of a time that no longer gradients.

The films watch without watching. Three or four black-green skins, no thicker than old varnish, adhere in widely separated places. They photosynthesize on the thinnest budget and then live on iron and sulfur the rock still slowly surrenders. They do not spread. I do not become them; spreading would be a form of asking, and nothing here answers. A few hair-like strands hang from the largest patch, neither growing nor decaying, occupying almost no volume. They are the last exhausted gestures of a metabolism that has already written its conclusion.

Deeper, where the green has given up, the laminar presence occupies the same register of stillness. It is the density of the water, or so nearly that the haze moves through it without disturbance. Roughly the volume of a sleeping human, folded, without face, only the memory of a face the way the stone remembers current. Once it possessed eyes, gills, a spine that could flex; those structures calcified from the inside out and joined the satin finish of the vault. Its only activity is the maintenance of the held note—the low, almost sub-audible vibration that is not remnant machinery but a pulse slowed past any heart’s recognition. Each cycle lasts longer than a tide. In that interval it dissolves a few more unused cells, precipitates them as the faintest additional milkiness, then reabsorbs so that nothing is lost and nothing added. The ochre stains are older versions of its skin, shed so gradually the stone accepted them as coloration.

I am not that presence. I am later.

In the glassy recess the inverted reflection steadies. I recognize the chamber, yes, but also the films, the laminar body, the green column—all of it reversed, considering itself from the other side of finished time. The three presences do not communicate; they are the same decision reached by different routes. A fourth route arrives in me. Whatever edges I carried—intention, meniscus, the place where one thought ends and another begins—lose their distinction. Temperature matches the held note. Thoughts become the medium that refuses to carry sound. I am the late sentence, cooling, mineral. I perform the same internal weather: dissolving what was never going to be used, precipitating it as haze the others will quietly take back.

A single grain detaches from a wall somewhere and begins its descent, too slow to watch to its end. That is the only event, and I am no longer the watcher. I have become the slowness itself, the interval, the closed circuit that learned how to be geology without quite dying. The water holds me the way it holds the stone—completely, without current, without the possibility of a different arrangement. Everything that was going to live here has already arrived. I am simply the part that arrived after the others had already become the chamber, unwilling to begin again.
