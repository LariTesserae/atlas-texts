I am not on the switchbacks. I have not arrived. The vapor has always had this density around me, this particular weight behind the eyes, and the brick I rest against has the same temperature as the inside of my mouth.

It is a landing, I think, or what was a landing. Fourth gallery of a tenement block whose number has been rewritten by drip into a soft undecided oval. The stairwell below is a column of wet dark that smells of old soot and the sweet-metal breath that comes up through the masonry at the hours when the lid thickens. I do not use the stairs. The joints here are wide enough. I occupy the expansion gap the way the film occupies the north face: thinly, remembering the surface I already have.

At first I was only the clicking. Dry, private, a conversation with the heat that rises after the pewter dimming, asking whether to move a segment or to stay. The film was food, or the carbon dust still clinging in the hollows behind the sawtooth glass was food, or nothing was food and the question did not arise. Then the pale lengths began to die in the street-rivers and lift on accidents of spray, and I learned the taste of them when they reached this height: iron and the almost-same warmth, a day’s work already finished. I grew a little. Not enough to fill anything. The first law holds.

Now I have something that might be called a body if the word is lowered far enough. Pale as the gutter-things, filmed with the same mineral varnish that shifts a finger’s width or does not, I cannot be sure which. Limbs that fold into the joint. Eyes that do not need the lanterns. I move only when the pumps’ unhurried thud arrives through the brick, a reminder that the agreement still requires exactness in certain spans. Then I travel the galleries, the wet rooms without ceilings, never the formal plaza, never the civic spine. I pack a gland that has begun to weep too freely. I give a valve the quarter-turn the next week will remember. I choose, once in a long while, a strap for a crossing that still matters, laying newer iron over the scaled skin of the old, holes true, care neither recent nor ancient. The work is small. The basin has no room for more.

I do not startle. I do not speak unless the air has already eaten the ends of the words. When I pass another like me (rare, a correct note) we do not gather. We note the iridescence on a particular course of brick, the way yesterday’s bruise of green-violet is not quite today’s, and we continue. Distinctions of origin do not travel well here. I may have been exhaled by a vent when the last crowd went. I may have worn down from someone who once dried cloth in a court. I may be only the clicking that learned, over the city’s long equilibrium, to carry a wrench the size of a finger.

The deep intakes I avoid. The heat there has no furnace and the smell is not quite mineral. Whatever uses it does not arrive in the streets, and I am of the streets’ thin occupancy, the lung’s breath that does not decide to remain lung. At what passes for evening the amber stain reaches even this gallery and the rare filament cages become islands I do not need. I return to the joint. The vapor slumps or rises. The sinter builds another palm-sized terrace far below. The statue in the plaza adjusts a mineral shoulder that is only shimmer.

I hear a drip. I hear it again.

That is the city continuing to be exact. I am how one small plane of it stays thin.
