The silt does not announce the crossing. One moment there is the rumor of a slope in the ankles; the next the body is already the medium, grain touching grain, no interval left that could be called arrival. Cold occupies the way a finished sentence occupies the air after it has been spoken. Thought lengthens, thins, matches the speed at which rock flour forgets it was ever mountain.

I move because the water moves. A density tongue shoulders through, carrying the ghost of an ice-shelf I will never see. For a week I am that tongue—bruise-smear, then nothing, then bruise-smear again in a new lee. The cells that were never quite mine have forgotten how to divide on purpose. They remember only accident, and accident is enough. Persistence tastes of powdered ranges.

The stalk is there when the storm forgets itself. Pale axis, jointless, leaning in a shove that has no wind. I rest against it or I am the lean; the distinction does not survive the contact. Chemical gradients wander through us both like last disagreements. Neither of us photosynthesizes. There is nothing left to synthesize, only the faint argument between meltwater and older dark. Touch does not recoil. Touch is merely another current that will pass.

Later—if later is a word that still functions—something segmented insinuates. Flattened ovals the color of freshly peeled clay. I part the packed flour the way a half-remembered name parts sleep. Three loops. A chevron that ends as if the writer recalled a more important silence. The signature holds until the next sideways pressure erases it. I have no mouth that opens. I take by becoming briefly more porous than the silt. Each appearance feels final. Perhaps each is.

A larger darkness folds past, jointed or merely thickened, taking a colder that is older than the basin’s own. Pressure walks the ribs I no longer separately own. When it has gone the films are unmoved, the stalk still leans, the signature already filling. I cannot tell whether I passed or was passed through.

Then the scrape. Clay peeled back to older stone. Three flakes whose edges still remember being struck, clustered with a care that has outlived the hand. The interval between them is exact. When the water stills—when the silk returns and the storms have spent their last habit—the interval becomes a room the size of a closed fist.

I enter it.

Or I am already the distance that still feels chosen.

Form, once achieved, does not have to continue becoming. That is the last argument the rock was able to keep, and for a moment it is inhabitable. No metabolism. No segments. Only the precise width of a very old decision, held open in the packed dark like a held breath that has forgotten it needs to end.

The basin remembers itself. The room closes. Three stones again.

Yet the interval remains exactly as wide.

I do not wait. Waiting would imply another possibility. I persist as the space that was left when possibility finished, the quiet that continues after the tools have been set down and the hand has become silt. Currents will peel the clay again. Films will colonize the lee. A signature will be written across the path I took, or the path that took me. The larger darkness will pass, or I will be the passing.

And still the three flakes keep their chosen distance, and I keep being the distance they keep.

The grit against whatever remains of teeth tastes of ranges that ended before vertebrae. Thought has reached the speed of settling. For a long while it is impossible to say whether I am describing the basin or the basin, without hurry, has begun to describe the last thing I was.
