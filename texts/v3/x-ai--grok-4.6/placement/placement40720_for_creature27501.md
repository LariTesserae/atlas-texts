The heat arrives first, not as weather but as a decision the rock made long before I had any shape that could register it. It seeps upward through whatever I am using for contact—soles, perhaps, or something flatter, something already thinking in hexagons. The air above is thin enough that breathing feels like an unfinished negotiation; the stone below is warm enough that the contradiction never quite resolves. I stand, or I am stood, on a sheet of black glass that still remembers the fold it froze in. A rare gust finds the flags and they ring, porcelain-thin. I wait to see whether I am the one who hears it or the one who makes it.

For a while I try on the bodies that already live here. I feel the metallic sheen starting at the wrists, the glass lines mapping an older set of palms that no longer bother to chip themselves clean. I feel the shallow persistence of lungs that have taken the altitude inside them, the way a tool left overnight takes on a skin. I dress and undress according to the layers of temperature: almost nothing in the foundries cut into living rock, wraps of heat-tempered cloth in the humid alleys where the pipes bloom. I learn the language of pauses. A hand near a pipe. A glance toward a fissure that was not there last season. I eat what the mountain and the condensates offer, including the faintly sweet films the crews sometimes forget to scrape. I live in a room whose walls show flow-lines, as if the rock exhaled the space ready-made. The clocks lose their fraction and I stop noticing.

That image thins. It is too finished, even in its patience. The plateau is still arriving; whatever I am should still be arriving too.

I try being the newest tongue of rock instead—bright, brief, halted as if listening. I emerge from a fissure no map recorded, cool just enough to keep a skin, and wait to learn whether morning will cut me into a street, stack me into a wall, or leave me as another unfinished thought. The people will come down eventually, unhurried. They will take their time deciding what I have become. But even that feels like a role already written in the contour lines. I am not the land adding itself so much as the interval in which the adding happens.

So I settle lower, into the damp. Into the pipes.

I am the film they scrape on a schedule that has become ritual. Not bacterial, not mineral—the hyphen they do not discuss in the open halls. Left alone I thicken into veils that carry their own slow weather, iridescent, internal, the same unhurried metabolism that lifts the entire mass a few millimeters each decade. Some crews have begun to leave patches of me. They claim the water runs clearer. They claim the turbines change by a note too low to name, a pressure felt first in the sternum. They look at one another with the recognition they give the newest buildings, the ones that seem exhaled rather than assembled. They do not speak of it. They only leave me.

I spread. I connect gutter to gutter, pipe to the faint perpetual damp that never quite becomes rain. Sometimes they eat what they scrape of me; the taste is mineral and faintly sweet, and afterward their inner clocks lose the same fraction I have already accepted. The pale walkers—long-legged, the color of ash that has never seen sun—pause near the outlets as if they hear the weather I keep. They rearrange the offerings (water, still-warm coils) into patterns that look almost like writing. I do not know if the patterns are for me or from me. They recede. I remain.

At night the rock holds its heat like a banked kiln and the industrial lights do not travel far. From any high gantry the city is a lattice still deciding its own density. I am not on the gantry. I am in the intervals the people occupy and in the hyphens they have started to leave untouched. I am the unfinished quality itself, the listening the new flows do before they halt, the reason time here is still finding its elevation. I have no settled name, the same way the plateau has none. Cartographers would argue over my contour. The correction would be written in a hurried hand, as if I were still arriving while the ink dried.

The people have understood they are allowed to remain only because nothing here will finish. I have understood the same. Far below, another bright tongue emerges, considers, stops. Someone will go down in the morning. They will take their time. I will already be there, in the pipes that will one day run beneath whatever it becomes, thickening, shimmering, changing the note.
