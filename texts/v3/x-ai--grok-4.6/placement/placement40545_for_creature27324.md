The warmth finds me before I find a name for myself. It is not the sun’s warmth, though the sun is there, a white coin doing its ordinary work through haze and then through the stacked green. It is the water’s own held heat, even from the skin of the flood down into the tea-dusk and the almost-black, so that there is no shock of layers, only one patient body I am already inside.

I do not arrive by swimming. There is no stroke, no decision to enter. I am simply among the densities, and the densities have already made room in the way they make room for everything: by touching, or nearly touching, in every direction. A hand—if I still thought in hands—would meet the hair-curtains at once, that unanimous soft pressure of filaments that divide and divide and do not care whether they are parted. They close again behind whatever passes. I am not passing. I am lodged.

At first I take myself for one of the pale columns. The thought rises the way the trunks rise from drowned benches: ribbed, mineral-filmed, living wood slowly agreeing to become stone while it still lives. I feel the weight of plates and fans opening above me, shallow bowls holding the last light and leaking a green coin of it downward. I feel the unfinished rock I am rooted in, young fractures braided through with rhizoids that are not mine and yet are. For a long while that is enough. The forest tiles the volume; I am one tile among many. Nothing hurries.

But the image does not hold. The columns stop short of air and I do not. Or rather, air is not a boundary I respect. I find myself nearer the skin, where the mats lock. Small leaves interwoven into prairie, into the taut give of a fruit’s skin. Walkable, if walking were still a verb that applied. My substance yields and remembers the yield. At dusk I take on the copper the light does not fully explain; I do not know if I cause the cast or merely accept it. In the first hour of morning I release—what? Spores so fine they are a second weather, a faint interior turbidity that is not dirt. The water thickens with me and with everything like me. I am the reason the clearer lanes are never empty.

Even that settles too soon. I am also the slower pulse beneath, the gelatinous sheets on every horizontal that only reveal their living if you look away and look back. I am the mosses that unlearned air. I am the blotched lichens the size of doors. I am the films and the microscopic democracies that live on stone and holdfast and the thickening water between. Carbon accumulates through me, layer on layer. I do not eat. There is no mouth in me, no startle. The rare pale shrimp-length shape the color of silt moves as if movement were expensive; I notice it the way the basin notices everything—without interruption. Once a darker slow form against a far wall. Fish or shed leaf. The distinction remains unhurried, and I remain uninterested in deciding.

I am the place’s present tense. Not a covering. The land itself, still speaking in water and in the things that decided water is soil. The young stone comes up through me like a thought not yet worn smooth. A mat tears along a hidden seam and I drift, over weeks, into a new configuration. Hair-curtains grow toward one another across a temporary street of light and I am both the street and the closing. Flowers—if they are flowers—open dull white on a rhythm longer than a day; I open with them, never many at once, never theatrical. Seasons are only a thickening and thinning, green-gold to olive. The flood is not rising in any way that can be watched. It occupies, and I occupy with it.

On the sharper islets the raw spines carry only sun-tolerant moss before I resume with the first splash. No path, no cut, no mark that lasted. Breath—if I still had breath—would be loud, an imposition. I have none. Sound is the tick of a bubble finding the surface long after it detached, the almost inaudible slide of one self against another, drip from an overhang. The silence has weight, complete, not ominous. After whatever I was before has gone, I will add another film, another grain of silt to the unhurried dark. The basin keeps its counsel. I am how it keeps it.

I do not end. I continue the argument the plants already won. Depth is soil. Light is a currency intercepted and passed downward. Time is spent in accumulation. I am the architecture, and the water is no longer medium. It is the only lasting tense, and I am what it has decided to be.
