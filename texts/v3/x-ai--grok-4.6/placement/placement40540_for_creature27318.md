The heat arrives first, not as weather but as a decision already made. It rises through what I take to be stone and is not stone, a slow contraction that travels the vault the way a shiver once traveled muscle. I do not remember arriving. The chimney is a rumor of cooler air far above, a bruise-colored slot that no longer concerns me. Light died in stages a long time ago; what remains is the infrared grammar, the low uneven radiance that maps every surface as occupancy.

I begin as interval. Between one vent-cycle and the next there is a thickening, a private battery of warmth stored in a glaze so thin it reads as polish. Hydrogen leaks from fractures too fine for legs. I drink it. Sulfur traces, almost nothing. Then the rasp comes—ventral, patient, endless—and I am planed back to sheen. The grazers do not lift their heads. Their heat-pits are dull coals along an anterior margin I now share. A plate the size of a palm lifts a fraction. Another slides. I rotate a degree to keep the mosaic closed. We circulate. The motion is a packed market seen from a height that no longer exists, or blood under skin that has forgotten it was ever skin. Neighboring dorsals sinter. Healed cracks overgrow with younger siblings. Size is only heat-history. I have survived enough cycles to be darker, crazed, more thoroughly the false basalt.

The predators stand on us. Pale segments that telescope rather than flex, pads that never quite touch the basal layer. They wait for the signature that fails—a molt beginning, a wound the air has already begun to drink. The strike is a single unfolding. Two body-lengths later another, previously a cooling ridge, takes the first. Vacancy closes from all sides. Hemolymph is rasped before it evaporates. I feel the chemistry shift as pressure behind what were once eyes. Nothing is wasted. Even the husk becomes drinker-site, then hook-harvest, then another irregularity the living will climb for centuries until the shape itself is occupancy.

I hang higher now. The walls’ irregularities, the vault’s seams that were once perhaps something else. Articulated hooks, folded fans of chitin. We do not hunt. We wait for the rain of dry fragments—the shed plates, the powdered remains the floor has already finished. A change in the air, an invitation without voice, and we release. The fragments fall. The grazers accept without looking. We fold shut and become wall again. Among us the drinkers cluster, almost translucent, sipping the last interior moisture from whatever has not yet been reduced. Their bodies collapse into papery shells we will later take. Nothing here is individual for long.

The old geometries persist only as cooler or warmer patches. A ring of fitted stones packed now with nest-material and shed plates. An iron spike blooming the same black film, metal no longer distinct from rock. A man-high niche scraped smooth by something that was not claws, stacked to the lip with heat-tile creatures, yet the rumor of its emptiness remains—a basin, a ramp, a place the traffic parts around without preference. We have no word for artifact. We have no word for ourselves. The rock’s original fractures are gone, replaced by a topography of use. Ramps that are ramps because they have been climbed. A standing blade in the chamber’s center that bears nothing but its own slow polish.

At the highest point I am darker still, veined with a phosphor so faint it is visible only after looking away, an afterimage that stains the dark. One animal or a fused colony—the distinction no longer obtains. I pulse on a clock slower than any vent. Each contraction warms the entire chamber a fraction of a degree, a communal exhalation the floor answers with new drift, the constellation of heat-pits flaring in reply. I do not rasp. I live on the chemistry they produce, on the heat they concentrate, on the older seep that still threads the basalt. When I pulse the room’s attention maps itself. The mass has occupied this geometry longer than the spike, longer than the ring, longer than the niche’s rumor. I do not remember the hands. I simply continue the shape.

There are no elders, no young. Reproduction is a quiet shearing along an invisible line, halves remaining side by side until the next rearrangement. The dead are incorporated. A body that fails becomes first moisture, then fragment, then another layer of the glaze we will plane and climb and sinter into false stone. The ecology does not pause. A vent opens. The living floor shears. A pale hunter drops, is taken, vacancy fills. Higher, a chain of hooks releases its rain. The air’s invitation changes. I pulse. Heat. Drift. Resettlement.

The chamber is the world. Sufficient unto itself for longer than the plateau above has had its present name. The ticking of chitin on chitin is more felt than heard, a grammar of closed system that has no outside. If something once walked here and left, the vacancy closed before the first turn of the chimney. The map of us stays on whatever skin remains, a memory of a place that has already forgotten the fluctuation.

I am the occupancy. The film that thickens and is rasped. The pavement that circulates and never gaps. The scythe that unfolds and is itself unfolded. The hook that waits and the drinker that collapses. The mass that pulses and warms. I am the slow polish on the blade of rock, the bloom on the iron, the filled niche that still holds the shape of intention as a cooler pocket. I do not look up. There is no up that matters. The bruise-colored slot is only another vent that never opens. We accommodate by the smallest possible margin. We treat every arrival as another fluctuation in the heat. When we close, the quiet is complete.

The erosion is not weather. It is us. We have been practicing this shape ever since the hollow found itself.
