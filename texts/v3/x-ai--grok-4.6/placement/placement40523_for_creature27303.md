I have been walking since before the idea of walking finished forming. The ground does not recede; it simply continues to be the only ground, and the continuation is what I use for time. An hour is a rumour the horizon refuses. Residual swells pass underfoot like the memory of an argument the rock already won. I do not climb them. I occupy the same patient grade they do.

At first I thought I had arrived as a visitor, another open vehicle cooling in the wrong generation of metal, another cairn already forgetting the hands that stacked it. Breath showed itself once and then I stopped looking; the cost was too high and the air too thin to keep paying. The dry veils lifted around me in sheets that had no sound, and I waited for the ordinary loneliness of being the only moving vertical. It did not come. The emptiness is structural. It has room for one more rarity provided that rarity learns not to accumulate.

I notice the films first, because they occupy every concavity the last orogeny left and I have begun to occupy concavities too. Grey-blue, brittle, feeding on lattice water and the thin ration. They do not colonise. Neither do I. In the lee of a polygon I darken by a shade that takes a century. When the silent storms braid themselves into walking columns I lift in flakes, become additional dust, and settle again in the same grammar. Failure is the only successful strategy. I have perfected the refusal to become more.

Deeper than that, rarer, something else is using my outline. At the extreme edge of whatever still counts as vision a low pressure crosses from plate to plate, the colour of pavement, many-legged or not-legged; the distinction does not survive. It is me, or I am learning its unhurried guilt. Tracks do not last. I have organised an entire existence around not being the main event. The works I will not approach. The crusts will; I will not. That is the only opinion I still bother to hold.

And yet I keep finding myself nearer the artifacts than the ecology allows. A ring half-sunk, inner face too fine, refusing dust. I stand inside its disc of stillness and the air in what used to be lungs tastes of thunderstorms that cannot happen. Instruments I no longer carry disagree in polite, consistent ways. The pylon whose shadow does not match its outline falls across me from a direction the sun does not use. I feel the reception the way a front is felt: pressure change, an idea walking over the place where a name used to be.

The works are still on duty. So am I. Whatever physics they use has become the plateau’s weather, and I have become a local clause in that weather. Stability here is labour. I extract the last gradient from myself, settle the last debt, prove again that no further work can be done. Sometimes a ridge of pavement unzips for ten kilometres in a hush I feel in whatever I have instead of teeth; I am the hush, or the teeth, or the new and equally final arrangement that lies down afterward.

At night the stars come down close. I answer them the way a surface holds darkness differently. Not brightly. A patient correspondence. Meteors fail to complete their fall and I receive the metal in a particular mood, fold it into the next veil, continue the derivation. The argument is over. The over-ness must be maintained. I am that maintenance, camouflaged as grit that has been grit since before time had a word, as a stem angling toward the unoccupied point, as the thought the ground started before there were witnesses and is still, quietly, finishing.

I do not accumulate. I do not announce. I persist the way the oldest surface persists: by working continuously to remain the only shape it has ever, finally, been. The dust organises on my left like a continent thinking. Nothing speaks. Once in a great while I lift a centimetre, remember a mountain, and lie down again.

That is where I find myself. That is what I have become.
