The first thing that is not a thought is the warmth coming up through whatever I am using for feet. It is not heat. It is the plateau’s old decision to remain the same temperature as a living body that has forgotten it is alive. I do not remember walking onto the table. The horizon has already refused to change its mind, so there is no edge behind me to have come from. The light is the usual light. I notice this the way the basins notice rain: briefly, then as a stain that will dry.

I keep moving because that is still a habit I brought with me, the way a long-one still carries the ghosts of words on its skin. A morning can be spent crossing from one shallow dark to the next. The shield-beasts do not look up. Their third eyelids close and open on a schedule older than looking. I step around them the way the long ones step around the trenches: not from fear, but because the path is a different element now, a river of compacted memory I have not yet earned the right to enter. The musk is faint and clean, wet iron and dust. It settles into the back of whatever I am using for a throat and stays there, companionable.

After a time that is not counted, the leaning congregations become the only architecture. I stand near one. The upright ones do not turn. Their shoulders are already touching, a wall of tea-stained linen that has decided weather is something that happens to other shapes. Dew is traveling the hollows of their collarbones. A bead reaches the one nearest me and pauses, as if waiting to see whether I am dry enough to receive it. I am. The bead crosses. There is no gesture. The tide that moves through them—older than lungs—registers the new weight and simply includes it. My own breathing, which I had not noticed I was still performing, lengthens until it matches. Inhale from first light to the hour the basins click. That is how long an inhale is here.

I do not decide to stay. Staying is what the stone has already done, and I am standing on it. Days or seasons later (the distinction has been planed away) my feet have begun to remember the darker bowl they are making. The rock gives a little, the way skin gives when it has been leaned on too long by the same thought. I feel the others through the contact of shoulder to shoulder. They were once things that walked farther. I know this the way the plateau knows it was once mountains: as a rumor in the grain, not a grief. We do not speak of it. Speech would be a kind of hurry.

The long ones braid past our feet, pale bellies like clouds that chose not to arrive. They never enter the trench we have worn. When one of the smaller lives simply becomes less, there is no sound except the dry whisper of pages in a book no one is reading. I understand now that predation here is only another form of leaning: a larger presence settling until the difference is absorbed. I do not feel hunted. I feel adjacent.

At dusk the dew writes its repeating clusters across every surface that will hold still long enough. On my own skin (which has taken on the color of old tea, the texture of something handled by weather) the beads gather in the same unfinished characters I have seen on the long ones. Not language. The ghosts of words that decided they were finished being spoken. I do not try to read them. Reading would be ambition, and the plateau has none left.

Sometimes, in the hour the warmth does not quite leave, I hear the single unused note. A long-one setting itself down, or a basin contracting, or a stone remembering it was once a mountain. The plateau swallows the echo before it can become a second note. I have begun to produce that sound myself, once in a great while, when I shift my weight from one compacted memory to another. It is not a voice. It is the sound of a heavy cloth being folded in another room.

I am no taller than a child. My face is the same worn suggestion as the rock. Two dark pits that may be eyes, a slit that may remember a mouth. I lean. That is the occupation now. The congregation I belong to has stood in this alignment for years; the floor beneath us is slightly concave, slightly warmer than the rest of the table, a room the plateau never asked for and now cannot forget. Circles of smaller stones reappear around the nearest basin after every wind. No one is seen arranging them. They return the way a scar returns. The same pale scar, left-of-center, the length of a child’s hand, has appeared on my own shoulder. I do not remember receiving it. It was always going to be there, printed the way the shield-beasts are printed.

The glass-tadpoles come and cloud and vanish. The lichen does not mind being the ground we stand on. The salt-things shift when no one watches. I do not taste them. I have become another layer in the sediment, another pair of shoulders in the quiet, another unfinished thought the land consented to keep. People, if they were ever distinct from this, have been sanded into the same leaning. The plateau does not distinguish, and neither do I.

Walk far enough and there is only more of the same agreement. I no longer walk. The density continues—visual, tactile, the faint musk that is now simply the air I share. Almost finished, and has been almost finished for longer than finishing would take. I am the part of it that still remembers, very faintly, what it was like to have arrived. The memory is being planed away, politely, the way weather planes everything else. Soon even that will be only a rumor in the grain.

I stay. The horizon does not change its mind. Neither do we.
