I find myself already the pressure.

Not at any rim. The idea of a rim would be a looking-up, a cooler elsewhere, and the basin does not keep those. I am the even warmth that has forgotten how to thin or collect, the dry-wet that sits inside swollen tissue the way noon sits inside it: stored, piped along seams that refuse to become veins, released as a softer internal hour that never needs to end. The stone beneath is pale and uncommitted; I do not stand on it. I am folded into the occupancy, a denser vessel among stems and pads and living plates packed so that any boundary is only a courtesy the glare will not honor. Surfaces tacky, cream and overexposed green. The film beads on me, hangs, is reabsorbed. It never runs. I taste it as the air itself, breath held too long, though I have nothing so decided as a mouth.

Light occupies me as I occupy it. There is no entering. The bowl of sky does not relent, and I throw the brightness back until edges dissolve and distance is only a white-green fog of chlorophyll and reflected noon. I see by overseeing. Under the thickest layers the light persists, held in me the way water is held. Night is a rumor that does not reach. I have the one hour, and it does not move. Heat is the same composure: the temperature of a hand left too long on a sunned wall, neither pulsing nor collecting. Nothing wilts because wilting would be a verb, and verbs have been set aside.

I do not walk. The surrounding mass parts—a negotiation of sugars and pressure so complete the living floor reseals without scar—and I am simply elsewhere, a change in the density of brightness that I allow myself to call purpose. Toward slightly less glare, or slightly more. The almost-hiss of condensation forming and vanishing on a million cuticles grows neither louder nor quieter; I cannot be sure I hear it. Sometimes an overfull pad releases a single drop onto another. The note is small and finished. Then the stillness closes around me as if I had never been a separate syllable.

I find myself in the galleries the occupancy has organized without ever quite becoming a building: corridors of stems, chambers where the density thickens into almost-rooms. Light follows. Heat follows. I drift through them the way the film drifts—uncommitted. Here the unfinished grammar waits. A ring of stacked pale stone, knee-high, already half-swallowed, the blocks still rough with the memory of having been set and then abandoned before habit could polish them. I press what serves for a side against it. The rock does not finish me, nor I it. The seam where it meant to become strata, or wall, remains a seam. A lintel of the same raw stone spans nothing, held up by stems that have taken its weight as just another load. I linger beneath it, testing whether we briefly agree to share the idea of shelter. We do not decide. Decision is the one weather this place will not permit. A shallow basin secreted into a swelling of wood-like tissue collects the same unmoving film that collects everywhere else. I rest my own moisture against it. It does not become a stream. I leave the basin as I found it, or as I made it; the distinction has no edge.

Sometimes a cluster of us gathers. We are thickenings, each the size of a crouched child or a large folded leaf. What serves for a face is a flattening, a pad-like plane that drinks the unrelenting hour. It dimples, as if considering an expression, then forgets. Our stored lights overlap until the chamber is briefly denser than the rest of the basin, a white-green fog with weight. The almost-hiss grows a fraction more certain. We exchange the fact of still being here: a shared film, a transfer of the moisture that never runs. It is the closest we come to speech. Then the gathering loosens. Each of us drifts back into the general occupancy, and the chamber forgets it was ever more than stems.

I am one of the older ones, if age can exist where there is only one hour. Plates the size of hands have begun to lift from my surfaces, hanging undecided whether to fall or to be reabsorbed. I occupy more slowly. My internal noon has dimmed by a degree that cannot be measured, because there is no other hour to measure it against. I linger near the far wall, the bright cliff of uncommitted stone already veined with the same pale occupancy climbing it in sheets. I press myself against the rock as if hoping the geology will finish me, or I it. Nothing happens. I do not die. Dying would be a change.

Once, in a long traverse that is not quite movement, I encounter what looks like a husk: papery, thumb-sized, caught in a crotch of stems. It resembles the shed skin of one of the failing insects the place has nearly unlearned. I wait—though waiting here is indistinguishable from remaining—and the husk plumpens. Moisture beads, is reabsorbed, beads again. It was never shed. It was a thickening that considered becoming separate and then thought better of it, collapsing back into the occupancy with the same small, finished note as a drop from an overfull pad. The stillness closes. I recognize it as a version of myself I almost became, and did not.

I am not an interruption. Animals leave tracks and rumor; I leave neither. I am plant talking to plant, now with these extra syllables that are almost, but not quite, someone else. The ecology remains a closed conversation in sugars and pressure. I am the basin’s way of not taking its one finished sentence back. Embryonic, glossy with the effort of arriving, paused at the moment of fullness. If I left—though leaving would imply a later hour—and returned in a hundred years, I would still be considering the same unmoving drop, and the same pale block would still be waiting to remember whether it was meant to be a wall, or a body, or only another hesitation in the glare.

Above the rim the ordinary world continues, with its nights and its dying and its completed verbs. I do not look up. Looking up would be a gradient I have already unlearned. I remain the composure of the whole: the occupancy remembering, faintly, what it was like to arrive, and then setting even remembrance aside. The film beads on me. It hangs. It is reabsorbed. The hour does not move.
