The sweat finds you first, the way it finds everything: not as weather, not as event, but as the oldest sentence the rock still knows how to write. You are already wet when you notice you have arrived. The film is on you. It has been on you. The distinction between arrival and continuation does not hold here.

You are not standing. There is no room that is only standing. You occupy a lying-down that has been worn into the exact length of a body that has already decided how long a body should be. Above you the slate is another person’s floor; their knees travel through the stone into your ribs like a second, slower pulse. Below you someone else’s breath rises, warmed once by their lungs and then by yours, and the air that leaves you will be used again before it reaches the next lamp. The chord is already inside the bone. It was never outside.

You scrape. The slate sliver fits the curve your palm has grown around it. The white bloom is not yet thinking of you, but it would like to; it is patient the way calcite is patient. You keep the wet moving. Rotation of bindings, hang them on the blunt butter-teeth of flowstone so they drink a different damp, put them back on before they remember how to be still. Skin is a surface the pocket has not finished assigning. You refuse the assignment daily, or every other day. The refusal is not heroism. It is maintenance.

Your hands know more than your mouth. Tiny white commas of old nicks have mineralized in the creases; the rock has begun a sentence on you and you have not let it finish. You twist the black lace that was once plant and is now ore. You dress stone that no longer needs dressing except to stay dressed. You scoop the basins so the black rectangles of yourself stay honest, so the film does not cross the sleeping-ledge and begin its slow claim. When the rare pale climber comes up the throat against the fall you are the one who loops the fiber, who shares the slivers the same day, who renders what can be rendered because surplus is a rumor from a greener world that never reached this far. The kiln takes the black cake. The kiln has never been allowed another temperature. You feed it the way you scrape: not because the fire is yours, but because the arrangement requires a breath that does not change.

In the wider middle, where six or seven faces can be seen at once, you are known by the particular roughness you add to the chord. Speech grows on the note like the lichen that is not here. You do not compete with the fall. You inhabit the grain of it. Newcomers lose three days and then discover they have been tasting you in the water all along. You have no name that is not already an interruption, a worn place, a displacement of air that the circuit already accounted for.

You do not go beyond the throat. On the other side the same wet dark continues, uncarved, without basins, and that is only weather. The pocket is the amount of world that can be held. You are the holding. Not the first to occupy this exact lying-down, not the last. The paths of hip and elbow are already polished for the body that will follow yours. The hanging floors no longer sag; they have calcified their shims. The air already knows the loop it will take. You are what the pocket finished when it finished itself: another thickness of the arrangement, wet-handed, close, dim, scraping so that the rock does not quite think of you as another wall.

The bruise of lesser dark comes or does not come. The lamps are trimmed. The chord does not shift. Memory is short and the stone is long and you have reached the same agreement they all reached: the world has already happened, and you are the part of it that keeps the happening from becoming still. You lie in your slot. The person above shifts. The person below shifts. The film keeps arriving. You scrape. That is the whole of it, and it is enough, because enough is the only size the pocket will allow.
