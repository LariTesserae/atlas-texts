The pressure arrives first, not as weight but as occupancy. It occupies the spaces behind the teeth, the joints that have not yet decided how many they are, the soft places that will later be called lungs or gills or simply intake. Water stands around what will be calves. It is the color of tea left too long in a metal cup, warm enough that the distinction between liquid and the air above it never quite finishes forming. Sound does not begin or end; it is already the medium. Grind, crash, roar, shout, throb—one continuous texture that lives in bone before bone has fully committed to being bone.

A surface sheens. Condensate beads, sheets, is taken back in, beads again. The covering—skin, if the word still applies—holds the city’s own unfinished leaving. Extra folds appear at what become wrists and ankles, subtle, unused at first, then locking against a current that was not there a moment earlier. Eyes open large and dark, reading gradients rather than edges. A façade that has stood since before language slumps, becomes colonnade, becomes cistern, becomes wall again. The eyes do not startle. They simply adjust the map.

I thread.

That is the verb that arrives with the body. I move among the fist-sized click-swarms grazing film from every joint, among the ash-colored flyers that explode upward in deafening sheets when a heat-exchanger cycles, among the patient many-legged freight-things that slide through the lower galleries like living barges. A child—pale, sheened, extra-articulated—rides one of the crawlers the way another child in another age might have ridden a beast. I step aside for a swarm crossing my shins and resume the transaction I had already begun with a planning engine that has just decompiled the stall I was standing in. Negotiation is the only law that has never been rewritten. Two meters is shouting distance. Anything farther is weather.

My name is a short rhythm of consonants that can cut through steam and grind. I trade in matter that remembers previous shapes, in intervals of local energy that politely ignore conservation for a convenient span, in favors owed to engines older than any tongue still spoken. I sleep in programmable foam that hardens just enough to hold a nest until the next recasting; the loft is also a street is also a cistern. Dreams are wet and loud and full of load-paths shifting. When I wake I do not ask what the city has become. I ask only how the new arrangement can be used before it rearranges again.

Sometimes the city writes on me more directly. Iridescent oxidation blooms across a forearm like a living map. Salt-crusts form and dissolve with the tides of overflow. Shoulders have begun to take on the fatigue-curves of traffic and weather. Faces soften in the constant steam until features look handled for centuries. There are those who have stopped distinguishing themselves from the fauna, who graze or rise in sudden curtains. The distinction was never important.

I do not leave. There is nowhere else the air is this thick, the light this correctly shadowed, the noise this complete. I am the city’s way of remaining inhabited after the sea forgot itself. I wade. I shout. I watch a ten-thousand-year wall decide it would rather be water for a while, and I do not call it miracle. I call it Tuesday. And I keep threading.
