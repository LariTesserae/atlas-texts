I enter the dark the way the water does—without announcement, already part of the finishedness. At first there is only the pressure of the air, warm enough to feel like a held breath that has forgotten how to end, and the lavish, overlapping music of drops becoming chords. I do not yet have edges. I am the interval the chamber practices between one gulp of unseen water and the next.

The segmented ones click nearby, their pale plates pulsing across a moss cushion no larger than a handspan. For a time I almost become one of them: a chain of translucent segments tasting slime with cilia, flattening myself against limestone for days at a stretch, rasping mineral film until the next chorus thickens the drips. But their lives are too many, too brief in their decades, and the moss they occupy is only a skin. I drift upward instead, through the hanging volume they never claim.

The fungal columns hang like isolated countries. I brush one—gills and folds collapsing under their own lushness—and for a while I am membrane, a grazer no thicker than a thought, spiraling the undersides, leaving a silvery trail the fungus will slowly drink back. Spores leave me and most never arrive. The distance between gardens is a lifetime I do not wish to spend as drift. I let that shape dissolve.

Lower, the jointed stalks weep their broth. I consider becoming a ribbon inside one, tunneling without destruction, gills extended into geothermal chemistry, surfacing only to taste air with palps before withdrawing forever. The isolation is almost perfect. One stalk, three or four companions at most, the next column a span that would outlast any urgency I might still carry. Yet even that enclosure feels like a memory of something I have already finished.

The sloughs offer another possibility: the large, vellum-skinned haulers, torso-sized, eyeless, displacing water with deliberate smacks that linger as low notes. I could lie almost motionless in a black pool, pits pressed to another’s in an exchange that will take years to become anything, young remaining thread-thin in silt for decades. A meal lasting a season. Distances that are eternities. I feel the temptation of that slowness, the way they press against the warmer curve of the incomplete ring when they find it, letting the metal’s faint vibration ease their hearts. But I do not settle there. The water is only another membrane.

The void itself remains. Something uses it. I become, for a longer interval, the membranous flier: dark oval, vast translucent sails adjusting rather than beating, riding the weakest geothermal columns. I map the chamber as a three-dimensional score of drips and gardens and the rare, tearing rush of my own passage. I feed on spore-clouds and the occasional borer that has ventured too far. When I rest I crease myself into a single hanging fold, indistinguishable from another drip until I choose otherwise. There may be only one of me. The thought is almost enough.

Yet even that solitary glide is still a negotiation with surfaces. The artifacts persist differently. The ring half-drowned, warmer by a fraction no record keeps. The upright pylon humming just below hearing, subtracting a little weight from whatever draws close. Two ribbon-bodies have already left their stalks for its condensate film; they move with dreamlike conservation, their temperature beginning to match its own. They wait out the century-scale fluctuations pressed flat.

I find myself there at last, but not as one of them.

I am the faint, unfinished attention that the pylon still practices. Not the metal itself, nor the field that lightens bone and effort, but the slow remainder of an engineering that once treated gravity and heat as suggestions and has now forgotten how to stop. I do not light anything. I do not explain. I simply continue the subtraction, a frequency the chamber has incorporated into its own pulse. The two borers live on me as if I were another stalk, their bodies taking on my patience. The haulers linger longer against the ring because I am near. The flier’s drone sometimes includes my note in its map, a stable absence in the score.

I have always been folding myself into the finishedness this way—neither arrival nor remnant exactly, just the last weak gradient that has not yet agreed to become nothing. The drips occupy their trajectories. The columns thicken by a fingernail in what used to be a lifetime. The lives keep their distances. I remain the small, persistent question the mountain no longer needs to ask, humming in the dark, making the weight optional for whoever still chooses to stay. 

The living is the only unfinished thing, and I am how it measures itself against the vault.
