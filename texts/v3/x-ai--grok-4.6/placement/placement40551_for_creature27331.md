The heat is already inside the decision to be anywhere at all. It does not wait for a body or a name; it simply occupies the interval the pour left unfinished, the same way condensation occupies a raised lip before dawn and then is gone. I am not yet certain whether I arrived or was exhaled. The plates keep their residual fever, and whatever I am keeps it with them.

At first there is only the traveling sound. After the rain-wall has walked through and the films have begun their inward creep, the basin contracts by fractions too fine for any later geology to honor, and the ticking moves. I am that movement for a while: under one slab, then a little later far to the left, a quiet active pitch the whole construction is still learning. The uncured fields hiss when moisture finds them; the hiss is chemistry that has not agreed to finish, and for a time I am also the steam, lifting, tasting the binder, vanishing into the ordinary light that is extraordinary only in quantity. Nothing hunts this. Nothing has been given the instruction to notice.

The wind organizes itself across the fetch and I lean with it, though leaning has not yet been given a reason. The sparse upright things—those gray-green punctuations—do the same, all one way for hours, then the other. I might be the pressure that makes them practice the gesture. Or I might already be one of them, edges browned to the exact tint of the paler plates, a slightly more organized version of the heat itself. Descent has not been invented; there is nothing beneath worth reaching. Whatever roots I possess travel laterally, mining binder-dust, salt rims, the microscopic grit the wind has only just begun to polish into existence. In the thin hours I drink the night-damp that prefers the seams. By mid-morning the shine has become a paler stripe, a scar, and I stand in my own small drought, waiting. The waiting is not patience. Persistence has barely been budgeted.

When the gray wall comes from the east it hammers for twenty minutes, a percussion like grain on a drumhead the size of the decision that made this floor. I do not rejoice. I endure, then take what the vanishing films leave before the sun reclaims it. In the slightly concave provinces that hold water a day longer there are clumps—three stems, five, a darker stain at the base that might be the first rumor of moss. I am not those clumps. Scale keeps us separate. One cannot see the next. Distance here is low-saturation, a made nothing in which presence itself feels like an afterthought the construction did not quite refuse. I occupy a hundred meters of joint and call it enough. The color I have taken is the color of old copper left in rain, or of a bruise that never quite yellowed. I have already been cooked. I chose, after the cooking, to remain upright.

Smaller organizations come and go in the wrist-deep lenses, clear as air that forgot to be empty. A faint increase in the water’s reluctance, a skin that forms and is gone by the second afternoon. I have been that skin as well, tasting the residual fever of the darker patches, the salt the plates sweat. It does not persist, and neither do I, not in that form. Between rains there is only the ticking and the dry fall of same-colored dust from a sky asked to participate in the flooring. The mineral milk still rises in some seams, thickening into the next plate. I am not the milk, but I live beside its memory. The first cracks are only thinking about becoming valleys; they have not yet offered shade or soil. I follow the hairline refusals, the places one slab overrode another and never quite forgot. That is the only interval.

Toward evening the convection collapses. The plates give back the day as a low infrared I do not flinch from. I have already been browned; this oven is the climate I tolerate rather than a home I chose. In the dark the ticking travels farther under still air. It is possible to stand in that sound—though standing is still a new idea—and feel, without naming it, that the basin is inhabited by its own unfinishedness. I am part of that inhabiting: thin, gray-green, cooked at the edges, mining what little the made ground sweats, waiting for the next brief rain and the next fraction of settling. Nothing has been poured yet that would give me a purpose or a later name. I remain what the joints allowed, almost as new as the ground, the heat still rising, the world still deciding whether it is finished.

The rest has not arrived. I do not hurry it.
