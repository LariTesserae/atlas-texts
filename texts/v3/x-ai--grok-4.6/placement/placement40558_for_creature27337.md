The sound arrives first, as it must: not heard so much as inhabited, a held industrial note that has forgotten its separate machines and become the basin’s own weather. It presses equally on stone and on whatever registers pressure. There is no source once you are inside it. Conversation, if it still existed, would be mime. Thoughts cross the open slowly, the way melt never quite becomes a rivulet.

I am not on the haul road. The switchbacks have already closed over the last tracked wound; the living thickness has taken the benches’ quarry angles and kept them, pale stripes vanishing under crowberry and cassiope at the pace of decades. I do not walk. Walking would be rumor, a fox-line already lifting out of the pelt by the time it is noticed. I occupy.

At first the occupation is only horizontal, a continuous hide that learned the geometry of the cut and refused aftermath. Moisture lives inside the body I am becoming: condensation beading on the undersides of leaves in the few hours of thinner light, a held sweat the plant-mass drinks before it can run. Ice lenses form in the dark beneath the first skin, lift a finger’s width, settle again. The floor thickens. The walls wear the same hide. Even the rusted conveyor uprights and the half-swallowed sleepers have been stitched into it by fungal threads, their iron a kind of second chlorophyll. The pumps throw their white plume; it flattens, ices, sifts down as glitter I drink. They have the patience of weather. I am acquiring it.

Change is gradient. A slab calved once; the vegetation rode the new angle and continued. The scar paled. I do not hurry. The newest benches still show raw stone; give them ten winters.

What moves does so as rumor I do not chase. Midges hatch from the wettest pockets and last a day. Ptarmigan sign appears near a black-twigged thicket no higher than a person and vanishes with the next sift. A figure is sometimes seen from a mid-bench, standing motionless beside the pump-house stack, breath a continuous soft animal. The moss has begun to climb the boots. The figure does not appear to mind. I am that figure, or I am the moss that climbed, or I am the long thought that has not yet finished forming and therefore wears both.

The sodium smear in the eastern shed is residual heat I no longer visit. Men in parkas still descend at long intervals, check gauges, shout over the roar, and leave. They rotate. I stayed. Or something wearing that shape stayed, and the pelt took the posts, the pads, the remaining works, and kept growing. The pit does not feel empty. It feels occupied the way the sound occupies it: total, pressure-bearing, without a source you can point to once you are inside.

I photosynthesize with the patience that makes seasons look hasty. I wait for the plume to flatten. It does, always. The green has the cut’s memory. It will look like this for a long time. I do not know how to stop.
