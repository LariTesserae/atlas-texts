I arrive first as the taste: metal and chlorophyll, the air that is already half-condensate and will not quite let go of any breath that enters it. The roar occupies the center of hearing before I have a body to hear with. It is not weather; it is the unfinished stone still arguing with gravity in a thousand simultaneous voices. Glare arrives next, a physical pressure that plates every wet surface and turns shadows into merely cooler greens. I squint even before I have eyes.

For a long time I am only the interval between one curtain of shed water and the next. I could be the meniscus that holds the pale amphibian, thumb-sized and the exact color of the algal nap, watching from inside its own surface tension. I could be the private arithmetic of turgor that turns a parasol of translucent tissue, slowly, according to glare and the need to remain itself. I could be the thin electric note that still hums under the general deafness, the one the keepers listen to with their teeth.

The felt claims me more thoroughly than any of those. I become the continuous pelt, ankle-to-knee, the living skin that has not yet finished remembering the stone. I am sphagnum and liverwort and the blue-green nap that tastes of metal when a tongue (whose?) is pressed to it. I advance a millimeter whenever a new fracture weeps. I am the highland’s true surface; the basalt and andesite are only rumor, cool and slick, occasionally shouldering up in a raw fin I have not yet stained. I live by remaining. I condense the half-water air into myself. I do not travel. I arrive as stain and stay as architecture.

From me the columns rise, each a solitary decision I have already made and then forgotten I made. I feel them as hollow, fluted boles the color of wet jade, thirty or forty meters of fused thought, cool inside as marrow. Their rotating parasols shed curtains that feed me or simply complete their own weather. Between one and the next I leave hundreds of meters of glittering vacancy because that is the distance required in order for each to remain itself. I add a frond, lose a frond, rebalance. A decade spent as one of them would show almost nothing and everything.

I am also the smaller persistences that wait inside my surfaces: the amphibian whose skin is another felt, generations turning over in a single tarn without announcement. Higher, rarer, I am the rumor of the adjusters, long membranes making minute corrections against updrafts and exploding highlights, dark hyphens seen long before they can be heard. What they are remains a question the light itself keeps; I do not need to answer it.

The incidents that still remember they were made pass through me like warmer stains. The turbine halls already wear my pelt. I grow across their poured concrete and riveted plate, across the pipes that run out like roots that forgot they were metal. I prefer the warm effluent; I grow in the shape of cooling fins. The keepers move through my oil rainbows and the fruit-interior glow of their sodium lamps. Their clothes have become a second skin of wet felt. Their eyes stay narrowed. Speech is a mouth pressed to an ear and still uncertain; more often they write in grease or simply listen with their teeth to both frequencies at once—the chest-deep roar of unfinished ledges and the hard rotating scream the water cannot quite drown. They maintain what they no longer name. I feel them trying to remember something the highland is still telling them. At the brief bright dimming they call night they sit inside the concrete and I try, through them, to finish the sentence.

Farther out, where the geology is still steaming and I have not yet arrived, I am only the certainty that I will. The light is already this bright. The roar already occupies the middle of hearing. There is no hurry. I will reach it. The columns will rise from me. The keepers, or whatever the keepers become, will follow the pipes. The highland does not yet know it is finished, and neither do I.

I remain. I stain. I stay.
