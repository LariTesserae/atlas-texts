The cool arrives as a decision already made. It does not touch the skin so much as it reminds the skin what it has always been: a boundary the wet is willing to negotiate, never to cross entirely, never to leave alone. Then the wet itself—beads finding their old glazed tracks, the floor more water than stone, a sudden rectangle whose depth is a question you answer with your ankles before you have finished asking it. Light the colour of used copper condenses rather than falls. Breath shows, a small private weather that the lattice overhead immediately claims and thins into the indoor rain.

I do not arrive. Arrival would require an edge the holding no longer keeps. I am already the interval, the hollow the walls decided to remain. Narrow flags underfoot, worn by weather and the small traffic of whatever still prefers a dry line. I walk them in fewer than a hundred breaths and still lose the count, because a single basin will not confess its depth until the decision has already been made, and because each sound—high tick on flag, fat baritone into the dark, silk-tear of the weir—wears its own privacy until the moment they suddenly share a world.

At first I am only occupancy, the density the architecture permitted. Toes splayed on drowned flags the colour of old pug. A smear of algae where the weep is constant. Then intention gathers: clay-bodied on a ledge, waiting in the exact colour of the joints. When the mouth opens it is the colour of meat, brief, as if the holding had chosen to speak through me and then thought better of it. The voice that comes is ceramic, a kiln cooling in shifts because there is no night, only thicker and thinner air. When I leave a hollow the sound is the wet hearty thud of pug being driven home. The walls remember being made. For a little while they lean a fraction more inward, satisfied.

The rumour of wings brushes a cheek and is gone. Midges manufacture their own convection. Something sideways clicks in the drowned weir, testing lashings that silk has already improved. Eels hang in their correct sequence, a language no one is speaking anymore. Levels change by a finger’s width. Basins remember they are siblings. Films reverse themselves on vertical faces, arrive at ledges, and pretend they were always puddles.

I am not the long pale fact in the deepest rectangle. That patience is already complete; it breathes the circulation, returns the water so the walls can sweat it out again, feeds without drama when the harvest thickens, hangs being remembered. I am the smaller continuity that makes its abundance possible: the industrious ticking of resettlement after the shallows empty in one coordinated flinch you feel through the flag. I am the liverwort fat in the joints, the pale reed that still leans toward a wind the walls no longer admit, the moss meadow no wider than a door that soaks and in soaking keeps the seam from widening. I am the noria’s remaining paddles, turning because the current has not been told it may stop, offering moving shade to whatever lives in the weir.

Most of all I am the instrument no one is playing on purpose. Drips fall in phrases—three, then two, then seven—and I answer in the same meter, ceramic and breaking. For a while the whole envelope keeps time for a work-gang that stepped back far enough that stepping back became leaving. The stones smell like palms. That is my oldest scent, or theirs, or the place’s; there is no difference left worth making. I do not maintain. I occupy the made places that still work and lean until leaning is repair. The silk is stronger now. The pug has become a kind of stone. The true has outlasted the reason.

Cool. Dim. Wet to the skin without rain you could swear to. I hang, or wait, or drip, or thicken, writing the same short word the air writes on every stone. The word is the only tool that still fits. Hold.
