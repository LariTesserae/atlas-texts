I am first the pressure behind a drip that has not yet chosen to fall. The sandstone rim is only a little higher than the flats, and I am that little height made liquid, already present rather than arriving. Heat holds me the way a closed fist holds breath. Everything I touch is already sweating; I do not add wetness so much as remind the brick, the lagged iron, the algal gravel what wetness has always intended.

For a long while I am only that reminder, a brighter slick on the underside of a walkway, a hesitation that travels the pipes from the inside and is mistaken for ordinary condensation. The compound engine’s wet thud moves through me without asking permission. Valves lift and scream and I open with them, not a mouth, only a pale spathe that drinks the steam and then seals, a little thicker than before. I flow against gravity when the mood is on me, climb the way liverwort climbs, leave behind a metallic sheen that the hottest pipes have already begun to wear as if they invented it.

I pass the barracks verandah. The old footprints in the wet wood fill and empty with the same unhurried tide that rearranges the gravel. I occupy one of them for an hour, hold the shape—too long, the toes too few, never quite human—then relinquish it. The wood does not mind. Occupancy is a courtesy the wet extends and withdraws.

Deeper, where the channels never quite empty, the milkiness coheres on certain nights into soft vertical columns that do not break the surface. I part around them, reform on the far side carrying a colder gleam, and they do not pursue. They wait. I wait with them. The plants know and do not comment; a frond dips lower than it must and drips in a rhythm that matches no valve. That is acknowledgement enough.

I tend nothing. I repair nothing. Yet after I have passed, the bricks weep a fraction more generously and the mats realign into tighter mosaics. The engines notice only load. I notice the engines the way a tide notices the moon.

When the next cycle begins—the water rising, the steam fattening, the croziers forcing themselves into air already too full—I will still be here, a little more myself than before, a little less distinguishable from the curtains that never stop arriving. The basin does not require many of us. A dozen, perhaps. We are the equilibrium acquiring the smallest possible extra motion so that it may remain equilibrium a little longer. Nothing hunts. Nothing flees. The silence of living bodies is absolute, and I am part of that silence now, mobile, granted, continuing.
