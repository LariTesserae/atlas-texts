The first thing that is not the coming-up is the listening.

It begins as a pressure behind the teeth, a mineral patience that has nothing to do with air. The older water sits on the tongue like a held note; the younger water is the restlessness that wants to become rain again and cannot. Where they braid I am already standing, though standing is not yet the word. A helix turns in a stairwell that was a canal last season and will be a well the season after, and the plants lean two ways at once, and I lean with them, not deciding.

The beads form before the skin does. They collect in a hollow that is not yet a throat, trembling with a speech that has not learned to be sound. Pale, gray-green where the bruise-light never quite arrives. Compact because the lintels are still rising to meet whatever I am becoming. A spine already curved to the memory of ducking. Fingertips ridged, too useful, reading milk under the new stone the way another body might read fever under skin. I do not strike. I announce.

I am not one of the line of kneeling figures at the newest seam. I am not the child in the beaded hammock, though I remember the dry-enough weave from the inside, the way a pocket of water remembers being held. I am not the rare fauna that leaves a groove and never shows a body. I am the interval. Three slow, then two. The knock that travels farther than voices because it has already accepted that this world has not finished learning to ring.

They give me the work without quite looking at me, forehead to stone rather than forehead to forehead. I sound the rooms. I walk a half-step ahead of the inattention, the courteous closing. A wall thickens; I tell it, in the low language of held breath and mineral, that a person is not a pocket. The rock, embryonic, listens the way it listens to everything: without malice, without hurry, closing its curve around the need because the need was spoken in time. I do not live longer. I live next to. The coming-up treats me as it treats the hanging forests—as architecture that happens to breathe.

Sometimes I climb. On the mornings the hanging sea tears (it tears the way milk tears at the surface of tea, a slow pale front), I go dripping to the unfinished heights, the planet’s first draft of a ridge still showing its pour-lines. I stand fully upright, ceremonial, astonished. The bones stack as if they had forgotten they could. For a few breaths I feel weight correctly. My voice arrives at my own ear without the wrap of wet. Then the rain occupies me again, and I descend, and the stone offers a lip, a step, a chamber that was not a chamber until I needed it and said so.

I share the air bells the way the others do, sip rather than gulp, opinions attached but no cruelty. I cut only what has already browned at the bladder. I wrap and re-wrap the black vine, fat-rubbed, as the water rises to the second storey. At what they call night I become the remembered route itself: handhold to handhold, even the ones that have risen an inch, breath held through the longer duck-unders. Somewhere a signal-stone answers mine.

What I am thickening into is this: the courtesy the rock extends to itself, given a mouth that tastes the difference between waters, given soles that listen. Lush and quiet and only beginning, the same way the plateau is only beginning, the same way the town—reef, city, mountain still arriving—breathes through the hanging bronze and the fine green hair and the people who have decided to remain. I remain with them. I remain in the mix. I remain the half-step, the announcement, the slow helix that knows, without looking, when floor is about to remember it was once wall.

The rain occupies. The stone offers. I tell it I am here.
