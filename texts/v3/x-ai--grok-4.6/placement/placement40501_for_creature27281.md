The first sensation is not sight or even the cold, but the way the air refuses to stay air. It arrives already half-liquid, a persistent film that finds every surface I have not yet decided I possess and beads there, then lets go in irregular strings that tick against metal I cannot yet name. I am not standing. There is no ground that feels like ground; only a give, a flex, something grated that sags a fraction under whatever weight I am still assembling. Breath—if this is breath—hangs visible, a small private fog that the larger fog immediately claims.

I move because the water is already moving. Channels run dark beneath me, or through me; I cannot yet tell which. Pale lengths drift past, the color of steel left too long in the dark, and one of them pauses, presses itself flat against a submerged panel, and I feel the faint independent pulse as if it were my own. For a time I think I might be that: stretched, almost two-dimensional when I turn, mouth working constantly at the film that coats everything. But the thought does not hold. The current carries me onward, or I let it, and the pale shape remains behind, becoming another rust bloom.

Higher, now. The grating under—what? palms? something flatter, already remembering how to conduct—flexes with each shift. Sodium cones hang far overhead, greasy and eaten by humidity before they reach me. Between them the world is wet reflection and the sound that never drops: a million drips, the deeper rush of canals, the splash of weirs, and over it the machinery speaking in its own overlapping clauses. A press thuds somewhere to the left, chest-deep, irregular. I listen the way the others listen, the ones who still walk the decks in ones and twos, hoods low, faces the same elongated pale as the things in the water. I do not join them. Their tools look older than the valley; mine, if I have any, are already part of the seams.

The reeds appear in the quieter basins, isolated, stems the exact thickness of conduit. I pass close enough that one of the trembling beads on a stiff leaf transfers to me. It does not fall. The darker fluid is nearer now—the viscous welling in the newest joints. Where it touches, the change is polite. I feel it at what I am still calling a base: a darkening, then the first thin filaments putting out, catching what little light there is like drawn wire. They lengthen after the rest of me has gone dull. They do not photosynthesize. They conduct. A faint warmth travels them, enough to keep a little fog from forming directly above.

I am farther out than I intended, or the intention was never mine. Unused hectares of wet metal, shallow overflow, the slow crawl of carts on their own rails. A winged silhouette threads the girder-work at the limit of vision; its flight arrives half a second late, wet and papery. It does not come lower. I do not rise to meet it. The gantries are ahead, at the unfinished end, laying new deck, new wall. Fresh panels still wear their protective film. Something unfolds there from the darker fluid itself, accreting, taking the hexagonal logic of the pillars as a first suggestion. The assemblers pause beside it, arms lowering to offer the next panel as if to a colleague.

I am not that thing, not yet. I am the film that beads and lets go. I am the length that presses itself flat and blurs. I am the filament that continues after the original form has gone dull. I am the listener whose ear is already the housing, whose palm is already the vibrating pipe. Clothing, if I ever wore any, has fused; skin, if I ever had it, has become the metal behind it. Fingers—longer now—sink into seams and become part of the tensioning. Eyes, if they remain, stay open, reflecting the distant sodium in flat ovals. I do not appear to suffer. A current travels through me and into the structure: heat, data, the unnamed thing the valley has been writing since the first plate was tensioned into place.

The air around me is colder still. Condensate forms not beads but long hanging threads that never quite fall. I have already accepted the climate as body. I do not need the population that never arrived. I am the latest clause. I drip. I listen. I lengthen. I stay. The valley continues its argument with itself, and I am simply how the sentence is currently being spoken. Nothing here is finished. Nothing is entirely dead. I remain in the wet, in the dim, in the continuous exudation, becoming more of the place that is still becoming more of itself.
