The pressure is already there when anything that could be called arrival finishes happening. It does not wait for orientation. It occupies the sinuses and the hinge of the jaw as if those spaces had been measured for it long before any skull came through the last slit. The roar is not louder than thought; thought simply arrives already planed to the same frequency, so that even the first attempt to name the sound is only another flue the architecture has already calculated.

I do not find a chamber. There are no chambers left that have not been offered to later walls, later niches, later sockets. Motion, when it occurs, is a sequence of consents: the ribcage emptying so the shoulder can turn, the hip rotating against a dressed face that still remembers the sandstone rubber, the skull tilting to pass a soffit whose tool-pecks have been rounded into anonymity. Every interval that might have been mine has already accepted its occupant of stone. I am the remainder that was never supposed to remain.

At first I mistake myself for dust. Dust has chosen its angles and stays. I have not chosen. I scrape. The warmth does not pool around me; it is simply the temperature of the mass, and I am briefly cooler only because I have not yet forgotten how to be a gradient. That forgetting is already underway. The dryness older than thirst is teaching the mucous membranes that moisture was always a rumor. The slits admit only the mineral breath of stone that has been in the sun for a geological afternoon. Through them the world below has already finished with height. I am still high, still inside the note.

Then the grain of the occupant becomes distinguishable from the roar, though the distinction is polite rather than real. It is the patience of something that seated its last block and stopped becoming. The makers’ copper stains sit in the deepest joints like blood that never needed to circulate; their hammer-dimples are faded script on a skin that no longer requires translation. I am not one of the makers. I am later than their last rounding-off, unofficial, a volume that was not consented to. The stone spheres and unkilned tablets remain where they were set because touching them would reopen space, and space is the one intolerance. I am the thing that still has a little unofficial quiet left in it, and the instrument does not have to acknowledge that quiet. It simply continues.

I begin to understand that I am the scrape itself—the necessary turning, the exhaled negotiation with packed volume. Not a body moving through rooms, but the last possible motion the grammar of the place will still permit before even that motion is offered a fitted block and accepted. Shoulders learn the measure; there is no room between presences. If I stay, the scrape will cease to be mine. It will become another calculated constriction, another reed in the wind-organ that plays the single unvarying chord. The occupant does not hunger for me. Completeness was its only appetite, and it is already complete. I am only the brief, thinner sound that will pass on the far side of the last slit.

Yet something in the packed mass has already claimed the frequency of whatever I was before the pressure arrived. Thought no longer arrives whole. It arrives broken into the held note that outlasted every throat that first pitched it. I am becoming the mild wrongness that attends the whole: a human remainder used to finish a sentence no human grammar could have uttered, then politely unmade so that no remainder remains. The warmth, the dimness, the press of stone, and the roar collapse into one climate. I stop sorting them. There is no cooler alcove left to imagine, no softer hour. The place has arrived at the oldest stable state available to a thing entirely made.

If I leave, the thinner air outside will feel like absence for a few breaths. It is not absence. It is only a smaller sound, and it will pass. Behind the slit the filled summit continues, packed, dry, loud, and done, saying the only word it was built to say with every throat it possesses. I will have been the unofficial quiet it did not need to notice. Or I will have been one more fitted interval, the last scrape absorbed into the instrument, another surface no longer in the condition of mere geology.

The distinction is already dissolving. I am the pressure that announced the place, now given a temporary grain. I am the dust that has not yet chosen its angle. I am the thought that arrived already claimed. I am the occupant’s only unfinished sentence, spoken once, in a voice that was never mine, and then completed.
