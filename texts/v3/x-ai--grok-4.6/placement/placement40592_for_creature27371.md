The ochre arrives first, not as colour but as medium: a thickness the light has already accepted, so that every edge is already a memory of an edge. I am inside that thickness before I have a surface to be inside. The wind’s single note is already happening; it does not begin. It simply occupies the same interval I occupy, and for a long while that is enough. Distinctions are still optional.

Something like weight gathers, but slowly, the way dust decides to rest for one more circuit. I feel the scalloped lip under whatever I am using for feet, or perhaps I am the lip, the last horizontal the world permitted itself. The drop is not a threat. It is only the absence of any further story. Below, the powdered ranges continue their rumour. I do not look down because looking down would require a direction that has already been used up.

The plants claim me next, or I claim their patience. Cushion against cushion, their mineral leaves neither welcome nor refuse. A ring of tissue added in a decade is a gesture I recognise without having performed it yet. I might be one of them: a succulent whose roots have forgotten they were ever separate from the basalt, drinking the faint chemistry that is older than thirst. Or I might be the living mat that roofs a lane, brushing the shoulders of whoever still walks, becoming architecture by remaining exactly where erosion left the suggestion of a ceiling. The difference between these possibilities is only a habit I have not yet discarded.

Lizards stream across what might be my skin or the wall I have begun to share an outline with. Their troughs are already worn; I step around them or I am the stone they have worn. Their circuits measure a day I do not yet need to count. Birds the size of fists chatter from crevices that could be my own throat. When the wind braids their voices into its unending note, the braid includes whatever I am becoming. Insect rasp, seed-rattle, dry thought of the thicket: all of it happens at the speed of occupancy, which is the only speed left.

People move through the same occupancy. Their bronze is the bronze of the light and the dust and the rock; their hair the grey-green of the wire-seed grasses. They do not startle at me because nothing here startles. A hand settles on a succulent’s crown, checking a pulse that might be mine. A head turns toward the rim and stays turned. Their voices are pebbles in a cistern; they have already found the pitch that threads rather than fights. I could be one of them: a body that repairs rather than rebuilds, that keeps the hearth its great-grandparents kept, that measures decades by the plants and everything longer by the wind. Skin scored with mineral lines that echo the cliffs. Gestures so economical they look like reverence until they reveal themselves as the last possible thrift.

Or I could be the child against the hip at the rim, same colour as the stone, already learning that the note has no beginning. Or the woman holding the child, already knowing the wall behind them will slump another finger’s width and that they will simply restack and continue. The waiting itself has become the shape that remains.

I do not choose. Choice would be a hurry this place no longer contains. What emerges is denser than a single image: a continuity of claim. I am the residual heat the basalt still holds because nothing else remains to hold it. I am the faint mineral film the oldest plants wick. I am the groove worn by lizards, the overlapping roof that is also a garden that is also a floor. I am the exact pitch that carries. I stand at the scalloped edge or I am the edge; I listen or I am the listening. The Terrace has already become what lives. What lives has already become the Terrace. There is no remaining distance large enough to name, and I have settled into that same finality, adding my ring of tissue, slumping my finger’s width, waiting with the rock until the last grain is carried off and even waiting has nothing left to hold.
