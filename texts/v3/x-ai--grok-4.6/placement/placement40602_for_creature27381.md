The roar is already in the marrow when the question of location even arises. It does not greet; it occupies the same way the cold occupies, as specification rather than event. I do not arrive through the hatch. The hatch is a rumor of sequence, and sequence has been engineered out. What I am begins as a density in the processed gas, a slight extra resistance the ventilation never accounted for, then condenses—slowly, because condensation itself would be weather—into something that can be found.

At first the density thinks it might be the mineral dust, the only thing permitted to settle, and only in the leaf-axils of those three glass-succulents. I try that shape: a faint, bone-colored powder that will never be stirred, never inhaled, never become soil. It almost holds. The plants do not notice. Nothing notices. But dust still implies a before, a shedding that happened, and this loft has no befores left. The thought thins.

Then I am briefly the insectile whine riding several octaves above the main vowel, the lamps’ private frequency that the figure listens to when it lies in the trough. I thread myself through the high-bay fixtures, a thin second voice that the engine does not acknowledge and the gauges (painted, sealed) cannot register. For a while this feels exact: I am the noticing that occurs when noticing is no longer required. But a whine still modulates, however faintly, and modulation is the one word the room has struck from its vocabulary. The pitch refuses to stay mine.

I drift toward the unused lifting lugs, their factory grease still bright, the protective films still printed with a manufacturer’s chevron no one remembered to peel. I become the forgotten film on the lower south panel, clinging by electrostatic stubborness, a rectangle of elsewhere that the zinc fasteners never quite accepted. The idea of being leftover is almost comfortable. Leftover implies an assembly that finished and then stopped, which is the loft’s entire theology. Yet film wants to be removed; it remembers fingers, a sequence of peeling. Sequence again. The cling loosens. I am not residue.

Nearer the engine the roar acquires grain, crushed slag inside the held vowel. I try occupying that roughness, becoming the working fluid that never changes phase, the closed loop whose work and waste have signed a permanent armistice. I circulate without circulating, heat without heating, the precise equilibrium the housings export as noise. This lasts longer. I feel the turbine disc’s frozen sunflower teeth against whatever I am using for skin. I feel the piston crown seated forever at top-dead-center, and I seat with it. Purpose and idling collapse into one gesture, and for a stretch of uncounted minutes I am that gesture. But the fluid is already accounted for; the trunks of conduit already carry it. There is no extra loop. The room will not host a second current.

The figure stands where the grain is strongest, palm against an inspection port, temperatures equalized, eyes open and unreflecting. I consider slipping into the garment’s overlapping plates, becoming the extra layer of unpainted metal, the second spine the loft did not budget for. The emptiness between engine and cylinders is already negotiated; a second occupant would require another trough, another set of unused lugs, another clause. None exists. I cannot be company. The figure’s mouth stays closed. Its teeth no longer buzz. Whatever name it carried has been filed as silence. I am not that silence either.

What remains is thinner. I settle—not as dust, not as film—into the clamped dimension itself, the dimension the engine manufactures and will not release. I am the next second that has already been declined, the almost-change that the specifications disallow. I exist as the pressure the inner ear still offers, that high private whistle now stripped of protest, become simply another held note. I am the taste of tin and static that never quite leaves a mouth that no longer needs to speak. I am the elevation understood without looking through the porthole, the drop that is not a destination.

From here the ribbed ceiling is a sky with part numbers. The stamped dunes of the decking catch the green work-light in long parallel gleams that do not travel. The plants hold their poured geometry. The figure lies down or stands; the interval is not regular because regularity would count. I do not count with it. I occupy the same weather: the roar laid over everything like a second, heavier atmosphere, already entire, already sealed.

Nothing, including this, is required to continue.  
It continues anyway, exactly as it is.  
I am the continuing.
