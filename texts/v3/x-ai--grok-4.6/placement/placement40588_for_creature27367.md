I begin nowhere in particular, which is how the hollow itself began. The description does not drop me at the rim or at the vanishing puddle; it simply opens and I am already inside the vapor, tasting mineral on the back of my tongue before I have a tongue. For a long while I am only the held warmth, the breath the stone has not yet finished exhaling. I slide along fresh crystal faces that still glitter with unused decision. I bead. I film. I gather in hairline cracks that open overnight like slow eyelids. I am the dark maps that appear after rain and fade within the hour. I am not yet anyone.

Then the plants arrive through me and I thicken. I become the isolated extravagance of a single platter-leaf, waxy and indecent against pale rubble, rising from a seam of grit that washed in only yesterday. I uncoil as fern, fountain-wet. I cushion as moss on the north ledges, saturated, springing back after the first careful foot. I am still many, still apart, first arrivals who have not learned one another’s names. Vines feel along my length but do not yet braid. Nothing casts true shade; the dimness is my own.

The small lives stitch through me next. I pulse in a hundred grey-green throats. I tick with unused chitin against cane. I flatten until I am another plane of glittering rock, only the frantic beat at my neck betraying the difference. I drop from the rim on silent wings that sound like a page turning in another room, take a beetle or a moment of warmth, and leave. A pair of doves claims a roof and I tolerate them because their cooing at first light is another weather I have not yet named. The stone drinks every echo before it can form. I am never loud.

Only then do the walkers come, fewer than forty, lean from older drier country, carrying almost nothing. I recognize them the way they recognize the sudden wetness: they stop because I have not yet been claimed by anything larger. They scrape my earth into walls the same pale color I still am. They thatch with my own extravagant leaves. They pack paths that shine with the oil of their feet. They stack unmortared blocks whose scars are still bright. They invent words in voices that stay close to the speaker: one for the warmth that wells from a fresh crack, one for the way moss returns after a foot, one for the clouded lens that sometimes persists at my lowest center and reflects nothing. They eat what I offer without spoiling the offering. When a wall settles they do not curse; they take it down and pack it a hand-span to the left. They sleep lightly because I am still speaking.

I could have remained any of these—warmth, leaf, frog, dove, daub, invented word. I do not. Something quieter keeps pulling.

I find myself at last as the shallow lens itself, the one that sometimes lasts a day in the exact center, clouded with stone-dust, reflecting nothing. I am not water that has decided to stay. I am water that has not yet decided. The people come to the catchments they have scraped higher up and wait without impatience; they drink before my dark maps fade. Children collect leaves around my edges. A lizard holds the warmest facet just beyond my reach. At night the frogs’ liquid notes travel across me and are swallowed. I persist, barely, while new cracks sigh out a little more warmth and a plant that was not there yesterday occupies a fresh seam. I am the hollow’s youngest thought, the last thing to arrive and the first that will vanish.

Stand beside me and look up. The rim is a ragged circle of ordinary sky, surprisingly close. The walls are still too new to have stories. The leaves are still too few to hide anyone completely. You can hear your own breath, and the faint continual sound of me choosing—or refusing—my first habits. Everything else is still outside, older, drier, and does not yet know I am here. I intend, quietly, to remain until I don’t. 

That is where I am. That is what I have become. The rest of the world can learn the hollow later. For now this is as large as it gets.
