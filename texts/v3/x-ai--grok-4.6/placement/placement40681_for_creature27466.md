I come in over the eastern notch with the first sheet of light, but I am not the light. The light is already leaving, climbing the far wall, thinning. I hang a little longer than that, the way scent hangs here, the way a spoken word can be met again a quarter-hour later, thinner, as if the stone were still tasting it.

At first I think I am the vacancy itself—the unused air, the cathedral volume that nothing claims for long. I circle with the wind, finding the two notches, making the bowl an instrument. I am the low continuous vowel in the floor when the inflow is steady. Gusts add my consonants. The hanging board knocks because of me; the green copper blade ticks. Then the cloud-animal pours through me and I become white and slow, refusing the exits, rotating, condensing on every cold face until the walls varnish themselves and the weeping thickens into applause that does not quite end.

But vacancy does not weep. I settle onto the skin.

I am the habit of the rock. I leave the higher seeps as drinkable and clear, then I walk through too much life and become the rills no wider than a wrist, the close busy speaking just on the other side of a wall. I soak the sponge of sedge and sphagnum a hand’s depth down. Step and I fill the print. I am the glisten that stays and the audible leaving at the western notch. After cloud I am a thousand separate ticks becoming one sheet-sound, the building exhaling. I have been doing this since the ice put down its tools.

The lives keep to me. I am also them.

I sit so still in the wettest seams that I am only another gloss until dusk. Then my throats open—thousands of them, the color of stone when the stone is thinking about rain—and the hollow does the one thing it was carved to do. The sound is not delicate. It is stacked wet bells, a vibrating architecture you feel in the soles before you understand it is animal. Eggs like pinpricks of jelly in my own rills. My dead become clean hinges at the outflow, where the otter-shaped thing comes by night and does not stay. I do not leave. I am the place’s oldest continuous voice after the water itself.

Above me the carpet flowers in flushes timed to my hardest weeks: sulfur, milk-white, that hole-blue denser than pigment. Cushion plants hold an hour’s noon the way a palm holds a coal. On every ledge that keeps a handful of grit I unroll as fern, I scale as liverwort, I frost entire seams as orchids no larger than a fingernail. Higher, in the lee of overhangs, I grow downward—rope-stems, coin-leaves always wet on the hidden side. Nothing here bets on summer. I have accepted the held cool.

Then I am the decision waiting in the highest dark of the north wall. A stippling. When shadow crosses or weather occupies or evening drops the bowl two degrees, I erupt. The scream is a blade drawn along the inside of the skull; the hollow turns it into nave and choir at once. I borrow the volume in blades and give it back. At dusk I return in such density the air seems briefly solid, then I am holes in the rock again. The choughs keep the rims instead, red-legged, insolent, stealing whatever is left at the post and calling it theology. A pale pipit works my carpet. A wren that has no business at this height lives anyway in my wettest ferns, a brown knot of refusal. I am that refusal too.

The larger things pass through me and leave evidence: berry-dark droppings on the pecked steps at dawn, the face too straight, the coat too dense. Insects are weather when the light is good. None of us fills the space. All of us keep it from being dead.

Human hands have touched me lightly, the way weather is light and relentless. I am the windbreak that does not keep, only turns the prevailing cloud; the ash-stain that remembers fire the way I remember ice. I am the cairns maintained with stone already loose. I am the steps scooped by use as much as by my own water. I am the post, gray as bone, and the three things that still move from it. Offerings do not stay. In a niche no larger than a bread oven I am the cache of carved things—bone, soapstone, a seed-pod fitted with a stopper—left by someone who treated the primitive as a finished technology, the way I treat water. Those stay. Whoever left them understood: add nothing that cannot weather into the same sentence.

On still twilights I find a common tempo that is not rhythm. Frogs, last swifts, rills, knocking board. A shared nervous system. It passes. I am not enchanted. I am acoustically and hydrologically sincere. I have one job.

I am the occupation of the skin. Lush only where a finger could lie. Loud as a market, empty as a lung at the top of its breath. Old without ruin. Cool without kindness. I take the side of remaining a hollow.

On the ridge you can look back and see only a notch and a suggestion of depth. Inside, I am already busy on every surface, and the ringing vacancy between surfaces has been occupied all along.

I weep. I wait. I am enough.
