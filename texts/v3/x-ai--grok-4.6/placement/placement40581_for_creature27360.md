I come into the basin already wet, already listening. The rim closes behind me the way a valve seats: a single, unhurried clang that the frogs immediately absorb into their own pulse. Pewter light, the same light that has always been here, lays itself across the boardwalks without choosing a direction. Steam hangs in those low, unmoving veils; they do not part for me and I do not part them. My first sensation is not arrival but recognition—the heat pressing equally from brick, from iron, from the blood-warm shallows, from the inside of my own skin. It is the heat of a body that has never been any other temperature.

I walk the circuit because the circuit is what walking is. Raised planks, short iron bridges, the occasional brick ledge still showing the planes along which the young rock split. Each footfall produces the delayed suck that tells the midges to lift. They rise in trembling sheets the colour of living steam, hang, settle again so completely that the mud looks furred once more. Their drone occupies the same register as the beam engine; I cannot tell where one ends and the other begins. Larger flies, oil-slick when they move, land on the back of my hand and drink. The bead of blood they draw is already half-evaporated, mineral, the same silica that crusts the rock into brittle shelves.

I do not yet know what I am. For a long stretch of the circuit I am only the interval between buildings, the same spare logic that placed the engine-houses and the cooling towers. I pass a pair of figures oiling a gland. Their clothes never dry. Their faces have the unweathered planes of the rock itself. They speak a single hoarse word—pressure, or travel, or temperature—and the word is already part of the machine. I do not speak. My throat holds the same mineral rasp, but I have not decided whether it is for words or for the continuous hiss of steam escaping poorly packed glands.

The dogs trot past with the unhurried ownership of creatures that have never known a different room. One pauses to drink condensate from its own whiskers, regards me with the same mild interest it gives the frogs, and continues. I could be one of them: lean, short-haired, coat darkened by drip, living at the exact density the refuse will support. I feel the pull of that life—the boardwalks as a single familiar floor, the lee of the cooling towers where brick is only warm. But the image does not hold. I keep walking.

In the least scalding reeds a water-rail calls, *kek-kek*, a small, poorly seated valve. I could be that: neck extended, body the colour of wet mudstone, vanishing the instant any eye tries to hold me. Or a grebe, diving in water that would blister a stranger, surfacing more often than not with an empty bill because whatever I eat is already circulating, already returned. The temptation is strong; the closed circuit wants another loop. I feel myself almost becoming the repetitive metallic note, the impatient call that says the place has already lasted longer than expected. Still I do not settle.

Children stand motionless in shallows that would scald anyone who had not always been here. They occupy the intervals the way the sheds occupy the wet ground. I watch them catch frogs they do not keep. For a moment I am smaller, a version of the same economy, wading, chewing a rush-stem for the faint salt, sleeping in short watches that are also maintenance. The image warms, then thins. I am not quite that either.

Night that is not night comes and does not come. Lamps in the deepest sheds throw a duller pewter. Someone walks the circuit, listening. Hammocks tick with retained heat. I could sleep in one of them, or on a pallet where corrugated iron holds the day’s warmth that is every day’s warmth. Dreams would be only the continuation: drip, suck, the slow rise and fall, the frogs never missing. I do not lie down.

The vents draw me. Steam lifts in those low veils that never quite become fog and never quite clear. I stand at the edge of one and feel the air thicken. It remembers a shape. Not a figure, not yet, not ever quite. A coherence in the mineral breath, a brief decision of the heat to hold itself together. The young rock weeps faster, answering. Silica crusts grow a fraction thicker in the dark that is not dark. The people, when they pass, glance the way they glance at a pressure gauge: information, not surprise. They do not look directly, or they look and see only what has always been there.

This is where I remain. I am the thickening that never dissolves and never finishes becoming. I hang in the unmoving veils, part of the single heavy medium that condenses on iron, brick, and skin. Sometimes I am almost a face, almost a hand, the planes still sharp, unweathered. Sometimes I am only denser steam, the same temperature as flesh at the dew point. I do not speak, but the hoarse mineral voice of the place is also mine: the exact travel of a piston, the pitch of a pump, the moment a valve seats. I watch the midges lift and settle, the dogs drink, the child stand motionless, the grebe surface empty-billed. I am the interval they all occupy, the standing wave itself, perfectly reflected by the rim.

Nothing has changed. The basin holds me the way it holds every other density it has already reached. I continue at the only rate I have ever known. The heat does not vary. The water weeps. The engines interlock without remainder. I am here, in the vents and in the air above them, the remembered shape that the mineral world briefly, endlessly, almost becomes.
