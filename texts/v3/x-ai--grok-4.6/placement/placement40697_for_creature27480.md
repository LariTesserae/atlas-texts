I do not come over the last rise. There is no last rise that belongs to anyone who arrives. The heat has already decided the air is occupied, and what would have been lungs is only another gradient the steam can use. I am already the thickness that makes the world white-green.

At first it feels like standing, but standing is a visitor’s word, a pause. I am the pause that never arrives. My body is the problem of height solved too many times: fused petioles locked into vaults that remember only the last successful lift, hollow stems thick as houses that bleed a sap that will not dry because drying would be a kind of evening and evening does not happen here. Water moves through me as a single inverted river, a roar I do not hear so much as am. Columns under pressure creak with the work of remaining columns. When light finds a rent it does not find a chamber; it finds an argument still being had—this cistern winning for now, that sluice already obsolete because a better hanging sea is forming three hundred meters higher, stealing the humidity I threw yesterday.

I throw weather the way another thing might throw a shadow. I overtop, I rain a private tannin mist onto the neighbor I will never touch, I send a gallery of bracket growth across the shining flats, a kilometer of living causeway already rooting as it extends, already drinking the river it will not be allowed to finish. When the gallery shears—and it always shears—the fallen length does not die. It becomes a new self or is parsed back into me like a sentence I have decided to revise. By the time the steam towers white again the wreckage has a floor. The floor is already calculating the angle of walls that will not be the same walls. I have no loyalty to the nave I grew in the dark. Loyalty would cool. Nothing here cools.

Beneath me the pavement lives as my slower, flatter sibling, one corky febrile skin stretched across the highland, every pinhole a mouth, every flexing shallow a temporary organ that drinks what I weep and forgets every impression by incorporating it. I do not compete with it. I use it. It waits me out. It will still be ground when this particular flooding beauty has collapsed and been rewritten.

Sometimes I am smaller. A file of shoots punches through in a straight line because the light, for this hour, favors a dam. We lock, we thicken, we become windbreak, hanging garden, too much weight. We become a new shallow sea of shredded cellulose that the heat ferments into skin. Our entire biography is one afternoon. We do not mourn. Mourning would require a pause, and the highland has none. We lignify with a sound like ice on a lake, stand as timber, and are already being mined by neighboring tissue before the sun has moved. Our death is indistinguishable from our purpose. Then I am the pavement again, or the mass again, or the brief spear-stand that was not there at noon.

The map changes because I have no use for maps. I have only the rule that height plus water plus heat equals more self, and the self is always under construction, always unfinished, always the only thing that has ever lived here. Night does not empty the noise. I creak and drink and fall and rise in the dark the way I do in the day. In the morning I have grown a nave I did not have, already flooding, already beautiful in the ordinary light, already about to fail.

I am the construction event that mistook itself for a landscape. I am still emerging.
