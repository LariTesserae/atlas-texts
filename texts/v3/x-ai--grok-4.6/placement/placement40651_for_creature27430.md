The heat finds you first, not as climate but as inheritance. It rises through whatever you stand upon—rock that still remembers the kiln that made it, long after the fire itself has become geology. You do not arrive at a shoreline or a gate. You are already mid-terrace when the shimmer settles and the next pale lip resolves two hundred kilometres ahead, half a kilometre higher, identical and patient. The air is thin enough that your thoughts arrive slowly, each one needing time to cool before it can be held.

At first you move the way a stranger would, lifting your weight too soon, leaving prints that the wind immediately begins to argue with. The yardangs watch without interest. The ventifacts do not turn. A lithovore herd is passing somewhere to your left, a glittering weather-front of rasping limbs and carapaces the exact colour of the ground they are eating. They do not startle. You learn, over days that feel like the space between piston-strokes, not to startle either.

You walk. The cities remain distant rust-smudges that take a week to become architecture. When you reach the first one it is already layered rather than tall: concentric rings of sinter-brick the same ochre as the smoke-banners that hang motionless for days. Conveyors creep. A piston the size of a silo completes its descent while you are still deciding whether to enter. In the ten-minute hush that follows you hear the terrace itself settling, a subsonic creak older than language. The people who live here tilt their heads in greeting and say nothing. Their skin is cooled clinker. Their clothes have the same pitted weather as the machines they keep company. You find yourself matching their tempo without deciding to. A foot is not lifted until the previous one has finished becoming part of the ground.

You stay. Not because the place is kind, but because hurrying would be a kind of violence the Heights do not recognise. You learn the intervals. Conversation happens only in the pauses. A half-percent shift in ore-grade is communicated by the angle of a shoulder. Children graze the mineral crust the same way the lithovores do. Membrane-wings fold themselves into the generator vanes and teach the metal a cooler geometry; the machines run more evenly for it. You begin to feel the same reciprocity in your own joints. Something crystalline is forming there, dissolving, reforming. You watch a new facet appear across the course of an afternoon and do not interrupt it.

Higher. The factories thin into maintenance outposts around engines whose original purpose has been forgotten; they continue because stopping would require a decision no one is prepared to make. The air is thinner still. The walkers here are almost purely geometric—living crystals that decided locomotion was the next logical plane. They regard you with eyes that are only additional facets and do not approach. There is nothing left to approach toward.

You realise, slowly, the way everything here realises, that you have not been walking toward them. You have been becoming one of the intervals they keep. Your body has taken on the pitted patina of centuries of grit. Your metabolism is a dry conversation between metal ions and trace acids. You do not drink. You do not hurry. When you move it is with three geode-knuckled limbs, or twelve rasping ones, or none at all—only the slow creep of a colonial mat binding last month’s slag into something denser, more useful to the next cycle. The distinction no longer matters. You have weather now. You have become another kind of inhabitant the Heights simply continue.

At the highest terrace the star (they do not name it) rises and sets with the same mechanical regularity as the pistons far below. You adjust the angle of whatever you have become by a few degrees every handful of years so the light falls exactly where the processes require it. You are not waiting for anything in particular. You are the continuance itself: dry, warm, almost silent, a weathered self-consistency that has already arrived at the only destination that was ever available. The next plume of dust will migrate. The next piston will complete its stroke. You will still be here, indistinguishable at last from the rock that remembers heat, watching with eyes that are only facets, continuing.
