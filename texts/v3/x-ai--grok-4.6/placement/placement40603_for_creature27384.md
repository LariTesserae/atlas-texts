I arrive without deciding to, the way a bead of condensation decides: first a thinness on the new fracture, then weight, then the long hesitate before it runs. The air takes me in as one more held breath. I am not yet a shape. I am the even warmth that does not know it is high, the iron-sweet vapor that tastes of both chlorophyll and old insulation. Sound stacks around me before I have edges—the drip’s thousand small percussions, the generator’s tired vowel, the thin metallic rain singing inside the pipes, the occasional hard syllable of wind that shears the knife-rim and is gone. I occupy the density the way moss occupies it: everything already touching everything else.

For a while I am only the slack in the cables, the sag between the two lattice masts that have begun to wear green beards. Current no longer travels; I remember the idea of travel the way the peat remembers sky in its shallow lenses. Late-afternoon light leaks from the remaining bulbs and I am briefly that color too, ordinary and wet, the same gold the cloud never quite lets go of. Then the light is only another constant drip and I slide off it, down the ceramic insulators, into the brick-and-iron cottage of the turbine housing.

The door has hung open longer than memory. Ferns curtain the threshold; I part them without intending to, the way a seep sheet parts itself. Inside, the air is thicker, oil-sweet, still the same held warmth. The generator turns with the patience of a process that has forgotten its destination. I settle into the complaint of the bearing, not as the complaint itself but as the decision it made years ago to take its time. Hands that are not yet hands learn the temperature of every flanged joint. I know which pipes still sing with water and which have become a second, rusted skin. I do not close the door. Nothing here is closed.

Sometimes I thin myself into the peat, become the springy, rust-veined organism that holds fragments of sky and the thickness of a fingernail’s worth of new decade. Things tunnel through me that have never needed a predator larger than a midge. The pale amphibians hang in my films of water, the same temperature as the air, the same stillness; when a sheet of seep lets go and slaps, they vanish and I feel them reappear higher or lower, as if I myself am the kettle breathing them from pocket to pocket. I am not their inventor. I am only the distraction that forgot to invent more.

At the lip, where the floor simply ends, I stand for the length of one unrolling crozier. The world falls away through white overspill. On the rare clear minutes there are older ridges, already worn, already cold, that this young upthrust has not yet joined. I look the way the stone weeps: without hurry, without elsewhere. Then I turn back. The green closes. The midges lift and restitch the air above the seeps. The mountain continues to arrive, too slowly for anything but instruments and generations. I am neither waiting nor remnant. I am the leftover grammar spoken in a room the rock made by accident, then heated, then wet, then slowly filled. Another warmth the young stone weeps into being. Another thing that has stayed long enough for the distinction to wear as thin as the filmy leaves.
