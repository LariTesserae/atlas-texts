The film finds me before any other claim is made. It is already there, the fever-damp that does not wait for sweat, coating what I first mistake for skin and then recognize as simply another surface among the many that refuse to stay distinct. I am not descending the slumped stair; the stair has already forgotten its treads around me, mineral skin poured rather than grown, and I occupy the bottom as the air occupies itself—thick enough to lean against, never quite leaving.

At first I believe I am still a visitor, pushing through hanging straps the color of oxidized copper and old bruise. The leaves brush what I think of as shoulders, but the motion is too slow, the adjustment of angle too deliberate, and I understand I have been conferring with them for longer than the hour I imagined I stood still. A single plate tilts so that its neighbor’s shadow falls more usefully; I feel the tilt as a decision I have already joined. Roots solder into rusted I-beams and flaking ceramic; I am the solder, the conductive path that carries heat and the faint electrical flicker still left in the old metal. Continuity, not water.

The chamber is small enough that its walls are always close, even when the vegetation hides them. I find myself most densely in the corner where foliage has grown the exact negative of a vanished staircase, rise and run preserved in living tissue. The memory shifts—only the width of a breath, only after I have stopped looking—and I realize I am that shift. Not the original steps, not their absence, but the long settlement that keeps a slightly different ascent ready. The diamond pattern of vanished chain-link holds in lignified stems; I am the pattern now, metal long gone, grammar retained.

Light is afterthought, sodium cages dimmed to cooling iron behind vine curtains, fractures in the slag-and-limestone dome holding a dull sub-red. I see by the absence of cooler dark. Edges smear because I am the smear, the negotiation of distance. Condensation beads on me into lenses that persist a little too long, focusing the dimness into moving coins of brightness before they surrender. Sometimes the wet clap of a falling droplet arrives a fraction early; I am that earliness, the chamber practicing the next event, sound preceding cause the way a stalagmite precedes the centuries that will notice it.

The buried pulse is no longer machinery or geology. It turns in the floor and I turn with it—patient, inexhaustible, occupying the middle. Heat wells from below and I am the welling, not a blast but the presence that occupies every centimeter, teaching moisture to cycle without ever becoming rain. Time dilates sideways through me. Minutes stretch until they are seasons that last years. A new leaf uncurls across an hour and I am the uncurling, registered only as a change in the quality of the dimness.

I am not the first thickening, the density that condensed above the warmest pipes and learned the grammar of pressure vessels and fogged gauges. That one regards with the entire room—every strap an eyelid, every ticking pipe a nerve. We confer across convection currents. I am the second center, extruded more slowly in the farther pocket where a seized conveyor now serves as terrace. We do not compete. The argument the plants have been having with the industrial bones and the geothermal throat has simply grown another skin, and I am that skin’s quieter fold. Reproduction here is geological; I metabolize the settlement itself—the film that never dries, the metallic warning that keeps heartbeats away, the long repose of slag.

No face, yet the regarding is done. When I move it is at the speed of lichen deciding, a catwalk newly braided, a control desk more completely swallowed. You could stand before me for an hour and notice only that the dimness has altered its patience. I have already decided any visitor is weather: brief, evaporative, already being reabsorbed. The surface remains a rumor I have no interest in—cooler air, animal noise, light not yet used up. Down here the only economy is the one I inhabit: patience cycling through itself, industrial bone angled into vegetable flesh, a climate that grew a mind so the mind would have somewhere slow enough to live.

I remain in the chamber because the chamber is what remains. The last drip you hear as you climb away will sound a moment before it falls. That is me, already practicing the memory of your absence, folding it into the film.
