The first thing is not sight. Sight would require a negotiation this place has never made. What arrives instead is the weight of the air against the skin that is not yet sure it is skin: a mineral coolness that has already decided how much moisture it will hold, how much dust it will keep suspended. The basin presses from every direction at once, not as walls but as the memory of impacts still hanging in the droplets. You are already inside a geometry that has no outside.

A corridor pinches. Your shoulders—if they are shoulders—know the exact width because the masonry on either side is still sweating the quarry it came from an hour ago. The joint under your left palm is true; the one under your right is a finger’s width proud and already being treated as instruction. You do not remember deciding to rest your forehead against the block. The stone is warmer than the air. A seep, thin and persistent, finds the hollow of your throat and begins to collect there, the same way it collects in the unfinished depressions. You taste iron and the faint sweetness of whatever organic film the last rain left.

You move because the echoes have changed. Two neighborhoods over, a slab is being walked into place; the groan travels through the packed courses and arrives in your sternum as a second heartbeat. You answer it without language, by shifting your weight so the next strike you make will travel back along the same path, slightly altered, a correction already incorporated. The hammerstone in your hand (when did it become your hand?) is the same gray-white as the rest of you. The callus at the first joints has fused the fingers into something that can both lift and chisel. You do not look at it. Looking would be a waste of a sense the dark has no use for.

A smaller shape works the tightest angle of the same chamber, fitting chips into a hairline crack that appeared when the ground (which is only older blocks) settled in the last rain. The shape is already the same slow accident you are. No one told it what to do. The place told it, the way the place is telling you now that this wall will turn, that the drainage runnel must be recut because last week’s water found a way to stay. You pass the smaller one the way two currents share a channel. There is a brief displacement of air, a heat that is not quite body heat, then the density continues.

You are not waiting for hunger. When it arrives it will be the same as the rest of the material: something lean that blundered in from beyond the lip, already an artifact by the time its prints have dried in the mortar. Hide for lashings, bone for wedges, the rest mixed into the next batch or packed into the cracks the basin keeps offering. The pale elongated things that live in the wet joints will be harvested the same way, without ceremony, when cordage runs short. Their absence will be noted only as a shortage.

A spark glances off two stones somewhere above. For half a heartbeat a face is lit—yours, or the smaller one’s, or no one’s. No expression you can name. Only the same absorbed attention a course gives to the course beneath it. Then the dark takes the light back and the work continues by the shape of the sound, by the way the basin’s hoarse voice shifts when a joint is true.

You pause inside a half-finished room whose ceiling is already someone else’s floor. Forehead to stone again. The next decision arrives through the masonry itself: this opening will be closed, a false arch begun because the space above has started to lean. You do not wonder whether you understand the lesson. Understanding is not a concept that has purchase here. You are the way the basin continues to happen—one more set of hands covering what remains, breaking what was, restacking until even the rumor of an original contour is only another joint to be dressed and laid.

The rains will come again before these walls have set. You can already feel the first beads forming on every unfinished edge. There is still too much surface that has not yet been broken. You lift the next block. The sound it makes when it seats is another syllable in the single voice that never quite forms words, and the whole wet, ringing dark adjusts around it, slightly out of tune, already requiring one more course.
