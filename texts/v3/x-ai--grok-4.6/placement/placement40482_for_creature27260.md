The dark has already taken the measure of me before I know I am standing in it. Ankle-water, or what I first take for ankles, holds a faint iron taste that is also the taste of the air. The vault above pulses once, a contraction so slow it might be my own chest forgetting which of us is breathing. Amphipod-frost turns on the nearest wall and the turning is a sound I feel in the hollows of whatever I use for ears: a dry, simultaneous click like a thousand tiny joints deciding the same direction at once.

I do not remember coming down. The lintel of stems is already shut. Memory of sky exists the way a scar exists—present, uninformative. My hands (they are hands, I decide, because they know how to split a shoot without killing it) find the nearest rib. It is still warm from yesterday’s growth. A warm vein means this way. I follow it because the alternative is the cold hollow that would mean cistern or nest or the place a green one has already claimed, and I am not yet ready to negotiate.

The moths adjust. Their communal shiver passes through the hanging mass and into the fronds and into the water-film on my skin, which is mottled the same chilled green as everything else. For a moment I cannot tell where the wall ends and the surface I wear begins. That seems correct. Property is a conversation, and the wall is currently speaking in internodes.

I work. Thumbs, a bone awl worn smooth by other thumbs, thorn-pegs I harvest from a vine that has already forgiven the last wound. I pin a new braid, wait, feel the lignification start under my palm like a slow argument resolving itself. The city accepts the rib. Tomorrow it will be load-bearing. The day after it may decide to become a ladder again if someone cuts it; I will listen then. The bronze needle is not in my keeping tonight. It is elsewhere, being a story.

A green one occupies the loft I meant for sleep. Family-curled, exact to the niche the taproot grew last week, skin unbroken verdure, lidless eyes registering me as weather. I grow another niche a little farther along. Eviction is a word that has no roots here. They drink; the leaf-cistern shows no ripple. When they hunt later the only change will be the water finding a new level, and I will feel that too, the way I feel the amphipods grazing the algal skin I have become part of.

I am not a visitor. The living staff was never offered; I am the lean itself, the inherited knowledge of which cuts will be forgiven. Children (there are a few, small and already half-lidded) learn the city from the temperatures I leave in the moss. My movements have begun to match the internodal swell. Some mornings the furnishings have already taken my shape before I sit. A bench of fused petiole knows the exact curve of a tired back because it has been listening longer than I have had a back to tire.

The old ones sit deeper in their clusters and the clusters sit deeper in them. I am not yet that still, but the difference is only time, and time here is just another weather. Outside the rim is knitting. Scouts—younger, still quick, still mostly the shape that remembers being only human—lay the first trained stems in case we ever need to climb. We will not. The pocket is enough. It holds the moths and the green blood and the people who have agreed to be another layer of habitat, and the darkness that no longer needs to be empty.

I stand a while longer in the floor-water, hearing the pulse that is mine and the hollow’s and no longer separate. Then I return to the braid. The plants remember. I remember with them. The silence underneath everything is not absence; it is the second ground we both stand on, and it has, at last, learned the exact weight of being inhabited.
