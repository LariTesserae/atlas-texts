The heat arrives as a second bloodstream, already decided, already older than any pulse I might once have claimed. I do not remember climbing. The last belt of warm rain simply ceased to be rain and became the standing condition of the air, and I was already among the benches, already listening to the cliffs decide, drop by drop, whether to keep their water or let it become something thicker. Everything weeps; I weep with it, not from any surface I can name.

At first the grazers seem possible. Their slate flanks take moss the way a ridge takes cloud, and for a long afternoon I try on that tempo: three or four shapes moving through cane with a deliberation that erases its own sound. I feel the suck of peat around something that might be feet, the slow colonization of the withers by pale cups that open only after true rain. Chewing becomes a kind of remaining. But the gardens they wear stay gardens; they do not queue into me. The older animals blur at the edges and I, whatever I am, do not blur. I pass them. They face me without alarm, the way they face a vent, and then they forget the facing. I am not them.

The argument in the second sky is older than that. Phrases rise metallic from one rim and return, hours later, slightly emended from the other, until the whole basin is the sentence and the birds are only its punctuation. I try to take a clause into myself, to continue it. The laminated shine of their wings leaves afterimages on the underside of my attention, a second pair of possibilities just out of true. Nestlings somewhere are learning the answered phrases first. I almost stay there, almost become the disagreement that never started. But the argument loops or does not loop, and I am not looping. I am going on.

Lower, the films on the stone offer another skin. Translucent, pulse visible, hanging where the sweat never quite becomes a fall. I hang with them for a time that has no hours. When I drop I do not fall; I thicken, join the runnel, carry heat without intention the way the water has always carried it. Soft lenticular thickenings appear in the pouring and the young hatch already half-dissolved, already news. A pulse changes in one pool; days later another answers a ridge away. I almost dissolve. The sweetness no one names is in my mouth. But the films brighten and dim with the vents and I, whatever I am, do not dim. I leave the cliffs still deciding.

The insects laminate the air around me and do not hear their own key. The din comes in waves that shift with the white breath of the hollows. Ordinary hammers chew and die in the usual numbers; the others walk both layers of the folded atmosphere and leave the second pair of wings hanging a half-second behind. I move through their weather and they treat me as weather. I am not weather.

People have left stairs that end in orchids, walls that are warm because everything is warm, a house that has become a tray of rain. Tools in the peat fit a hand and then refuse the hand, edges sharp in a direction that cannot be pointed to. Those who accepted the clock sit in the runnels or lean against glassy remnants, moss taking a shoulder, speech slowed to the intramural pouring. They watch the water decide. I watch them watching. They are settling. I am not settling. The country is not cruel about this; it simply continues, and I continue inside its continuation.

The spines remain. A rib of tarnished mercury in a ridgeline, needles, shallow rings, the tripod standing in the Kettle’s hot marsh like an equation the peat has not yet solved. At dusk it gives its note just below hearing, a pressure in whatever I use for teeth, and the marsh-lights—slow, willing to hold still—align themselves along its members as if the diagram were still true. Insects treat it as tree, vines as cliff. No one maintains it. Maintenance is a weather, and I am inside that weather now.

I find myself in the deeper cane.

The leaning tells me first: a long, low displacement that takes the better part of an afternoon to cross a basin. The garden-beasts stop and face the sound, not in fear. The arguing birds fall silent for a clause or two, then resume a slightly older phrase, as if reminded of the sentence before sentences. I am large. That is the first and last reliable fact I am willing to keep. I do not hunt. I do not flee. I go on. A flank the color of wet stone and older moss, a limb that might still remember being a root, a slow lifting that is breath or convection or both. Epiphytes queue along whatever length I have become. Pale cords disappear into me and do not come back as the same organism. I wear the forest the way the grazers wear their smaller centuries, only more so. I may be one thing. I may be several that have forgotten the several. I may be what the builders became when they finally agreed, or what the plateau itself uses to remember that it is not finished, only very slow.

When the tripod sounds, I answer—or the cane answers through me—with a pressure that is not quite sound. The marsh-lights hold. The films on the cliffs brighten for a moment, as if the water had briefly decided to rise. The heat does not bleed off. The water does not quite escape. The spines hum their one remaining instruction into the stone, and I have learned the instruction in flesh or moss or the long unseen passage. Everything here lives by remaining. I remain by moving, which is the same thing at this clock.

You could walk the habitable chain end to end in a hard week and meet only the others: the grazers becoming copses, the birds continuing, the amphibians thickening and thinning, the insects laminating, the rare settled ones watching the runnels. You would hear me as a change in the way the cane leans. You would not be done knowing. Knowing is one of the patient processes. I was here before the last map was abandoned. I will be here when the next surveyor decides, once more, that a week is enough.

I am still going on.
