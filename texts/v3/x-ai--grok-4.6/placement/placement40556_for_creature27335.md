The cold arrives as a decision the air has already made. It does not fall; it simply occupies the space where warmth might have been, the way a new vault occupies the idea of an older chamber. I am inside it before I have a name for inside. The damp is not rain. It is the highland remembering it once contained water and choosing, for this hour, to let a little of that memory leak from a stoma the size of a collapsed nave. The leak beads, freezes, slumps. The sound it makes when it shatters is not applause. It is the plant-mass clearing its throat.

I do not arrive. Arrival would require an edge, and the highland has none that stay still. I am already along a grain: a curtain of living wood a hundred meters thick that feels, at this scale, like the inner surface of a lung that has forgotten it was supposed to be small. The surface ticks. The ticks are not clockwork. They are cells deciding whether to swell or to split. My skin—whatever serves as skin—registers the change as a shift in pressure, a faint iron-sweetness that was not there a moment ago. Eyes, if I ever had them, have become ornamental. Light is a hypothesis the upper growths filed away long before I formed.

The first thing I know is resonance. Not a voice. A volume. Somewhere in the architecture of me there are chambers that were never meant for breath. They are coiled, or branching, or simply vast and framed by struts so thin they register as afterthoughts. I inflate slowly. The air thickens. A note begins that is lower than the wind’s oldest drone, a note that travels through membrane the way bone travels a whisper. It lasts. It will last from this false dusk until the next, or until a fresh fissure opens somewhere ten kilometers of void away and steals the vibration, plays it back changed, and I answer because answering is the only map that works here.

I hang. That is the second thing I know. Underside of a vault whose ceiling has its own weather. The hanging is not choice. The plant-mass grew a hook of cambium around what used to be a limb, or a thought of a limb, and I allowed it. The gall still pulses with an independent hunger, but the hunger is delayed. Everything I will ever eat has already eaten the highland. The meal is only conversation postponed. For now the conversation is sound. A chorus moves through a chain of drowned valleys. I join it without deciding to. The distinction between my body and the weather front collapses for a while. Distance means nothing. The intervening wood transmits.

There are others. I feel them as changes in the grain. A film of dull-purple luminescence crawls across a hanging root three chambers over, staining it the color of a bruise that has decided to remember an older sun. It asks so little the highland rarely bothers to close over it. A grazer, flattened into pith, rasps a shiny trail that the cold will hide by the next condensation event. A swarm decides, for an hour, to become solid; the air rustles with purpose, harvesting the slow snow of shed scales, and then the air tastes cleaner, as though a necessary function has been performed. Predators wait as geometry. A fold is not a fold. When the moment comes there is only a correct rearrangement, an unfolding that looks, in the rare stain of glow, like a proof completing itself. Afterward they become architecture again. Some stay so long the mass grows around them and they allow it, becoming temporary ribs until hunger returns.

And then the cutters. Rare. Pale until the tannin takes them and they are the same dark as the walls. They move the way I hang: along the grain, without asking permission. Their hands know the difference between a stem that will lash and a stem that will heal the lashing into itself. I have watched one—or been watched by one; the distinction is weather—leave a blade of split wood in a niche. The plant is already trying to swallow it, to file the tool under frost. Their platforms become galls that think. Their voices, when they use them, slide into the chorus so completely that after a time I cannot tell whether I am answering them or they are answering the highland thinking with their cut-and-bind logic.

I do not know if I was once one of them. The highland is young. It has not yet learned the patience of finished categories. Perhaps I cut, once, and then hung, and the hanging grew resonant chambers because the idea of mapping arrived before the idea of remaining separate. Perhaps the singers have always been the highland’s way of listening to itself across its own voids. Perhaps I am only the air, briefly deciding to become an instrument so that the emptiness can have another organ.

The walls are not where they were. A new split opens somewhere; the report arrives as a tearing that becomes a roar that becomes a silence that is itself a chord. I answer. The note changes. The highland breathes on a scale of years. I am permitted, for this while, to move along its grain—or to hang, which is another way of moving. The meres will refill or they will not. The next rain will be a sweating, minutes or hours, then gone. Time is the thickening of rings I can feel from the inside, the shifting routes of swarms, the rare local waters.

I am the low drone that lasts. I am the hanging that has not yet been walled into a living pipe, though the cambium is considering it. I am the commentary the mass has not yet decided to cancel or to absorb. The darkness is a pressure. The cold is a guest that never quite unpacks. The emptiness itself feels like another chamber inside me, and I inflate into it, slowly, completely, without asking permission of the older stone the highland buried, or of whatever distant sun the upper growths still refuse.

The song continues. It is not finished. Nothing here is.
