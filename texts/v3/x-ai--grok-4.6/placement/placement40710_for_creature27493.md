The film is first, and it does not announce itself. It is simply the temperature that everything has already agreed to be: warm, neither cooling nor heating, a continuous arrival that coats the inner surface of whatever I am as readily as the outer. I do not remember dryness. The memory of dryness would be indecent here, like the rumor of green.

Light comes already spent, a single ungraduated pewter that has forgotten how to cast an edge. I wait to see whether I have a shadow. I do not. The dim lives in the held breath, and I live in the dim.

Sound finds the places that can be found. If there are small bones they ache with the standing roar; if there is a jaw it transmits the slow percussion of cobbles deciding, again and again, that the silt beneath them is a fraction less solid and then a fraction more. The roar has no crest. It has already found its level, the way the water has. I am inside that level, or I am one more liquid contributing to it.

For a time I think I might be the water itself, the fine warm precipitation that never thickens and never thins, condensing on hide and stone and skin alike. But there is a suck, a give, a print that fills the instant it is made. Something presses down and is pressed. I have weight, or I am weight.

I think I might be a cobble, still wearing the shape a forgotten flood gave it, half-swallowed, sweating. But I duck. There is an eave, a hide still stiff with its own fat, weighted at the hem so the moisture cannot lift it. I know to duck. The body—if it is a body—has already learned the architecture of flaps and no windows, of light that is not invited.

Faces come close because faces must. Pewter eyes, wide, never squinting. Slack skin, faintly pebbled, the color of a bruise that never quite dries. Breath leaves a mouth and cannot be told from the air that already coats the next mouth. I know speech by the shape of lips, the small flex of a throat. I lean because leaning is how anything is said.

Hands, then. Broad, spatulate, permanently wrinkled at the tips. They know the packing of lifts, the weeping wait between one layer and the next, the setting of cobbles by eye. They know the needle of splintered rib, the sinew, the scraper. They have packed the same height they have always packed. Nothing finished, nothing begun.

I am not the wallower, though I have walked across the ridged shelves of their backs as across any other surface, and I have felt their tectonic shrug travel up through packed earth into whatever I use for soles. I am not the eater in the cavities, though I have learned, early, not to leave a hand unattended in a gap. I am not the bird whose voice is only another braid in the roar, nor the amphibian whose throat works without rest in every dark skirt of damp, nor the almost-fish that turn a dipped hand into a living net.

I am the density that lives against density. Compact, low-centered, joints kept loose for the perpetual suggestion of solidity. Hair plucked young so that nothing extra holds the film. Jewelry of bone beads or pale cartilage at the throat, where it will be seen in the lean-in. I move through the heavier sound of the inner lifts the way the narrow bodies move through the shallows: by habit, by the pressure of others, by the knowledge that the way has not changed.

And still the image is not yet itself.

I find my ear against the weeping stone. Older. The platforms here rise a little from the simple accumulation of preference, bones polished by generations of such hands, hides mapped with the patches of other hides. I press the ear not because I will hear the silt deciding whether to hold. The silt decided long ago, or never; the decision is the equilibrium, the held becoming. I listen because listening is what has always been done. The almost-change is a craft, and the craft is the refusal to stop performing it.

A fat-lamp nearby, hooded, a local orange that makes the wet on the nearest cobbles look like metal for a moment that does not differ from the moment before. I sew a seam, or I lift a handful of the almost-fish, or I scrape the gray bloom from a midden seam to feed whatever is smaller. The work is the same work. The count does not tilt. Children are born already damp, already walking the gait of give and duck and pause; they die in the same roar, the same unused light, the same water that never chooses a direction.

I stay. The warmth never leaves the skin. I am the basin thinking itself in flesh that weeps along with the walls, in packed earth that is already refuse, in the first hour extended until the hour forgot the trick of ending. The platforms look embryonic because they have never been allowed to become anything else. I pack the next lift to the same height. I lean in. I listen to what cannot be heard.

This is where. This is what. The becoming, held, without remainder.
