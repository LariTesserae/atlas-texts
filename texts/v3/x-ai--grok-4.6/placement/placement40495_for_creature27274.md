The height is already inside the teeth, a thin ringing that does not belong to any single mouth. Breath arrives incomplete and stays that way; the air has no further business with lungs. Light falls without apology, ordinary, and the world curves away at the edge of it like a thought the plateau finished having long ago.

I am walking, or I am the interval between steps that no longer need to be taken. The packed flour of salt and gypsum takes the weight without comment, grains already at their angle of rest. Shoulders pass through shoulders. Sometimes hide, dry as the last page of a book no one will open again; sometimes only a cooler density, the same silence made thicker. A flank the size of a small cliff occupies the space where a ribcage might be, and both remain. Shadows decline to choose.

The small ones are a gravel under everything, carapaces the color of the ground itself, mouths set in the permanent shape of having already known the taste. Above them and through them the mid-ranks stand inside one another, horns remembering circles that water once drew and then abandoned. Larger architectures recline as if the land had grown them instead of mountains. In the air the unmoving veils of wing and membrane layer the light into stacked paleness; looking up is looking through more of the same plateau, more bodies, a depth the rock never claimed.

I do not startle them. They have no remaining use for startle. The many-jointed ones lie folded like sentences the ground has decided not to erase, extremities already gypsum, already the idea of writing that no longer requires a hand.

Something in the stillness includes the walker. Distances arrive without the courtesy of approach: a ring of scored slabs is suddenly the height of the face, marks that might be numbers or the dry ghost of a river. A cairn, or the memory of stacking, sits under the palm as if it had always been there. The foil-lined pits hold only residue, a chemistry that never learned to be wet.

The distinction thins the way breath thins. Outline takes on mineral patience. A joint too many appears, then another, tapering, branching, satisfied. Or the hide of the house-sized one becomes the surface that is walked upon and also the surface that walks. Or the winged veil is simply the way the air has always been occupied, and the body is one more still membrane the color of the sky’s underside.

There is no single arrival. The count the plateau finished includes this as well: the visitor who stays long enough to be inventoried, the form that is animal and fold and aperture at once. Throats remain open or closed. Eyes regard distances that do not exist, or regard inward, or are only smooth depressions the light does not argue with. No sound is required. The tick of contracting salt is enough; the late pressure of noises that were never made sits in the sinuses like a word almost recalled.

Nothing needs to happen next. The mountains have lain down. The rivers have taken their names. What remains is the cool, crowded courtesy of a place that has already held everything it will ever agree to hold, and now simply continues, in honest light, the last shape it will ever require. I am inside that continuation, overlapping, finished, the same silence made briefly personal and then not personal at all. There is room. There has always been room.
