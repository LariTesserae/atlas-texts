The flue knows the count before you do. That is how it begins, or how it pretends to: a knowledge that is already water, already the dark tea thickening around the wrists. I do not arrive. I thicken. The suit is a rumor I shed the way the vault sheds its stenciled names. Skin becomes an argument the fever wins. Extra knuckles first, then the patience of mineral at the shoulders. I do not scrape the antlers. They are the room’s vote on me.

I am not one of the ivory ones, not yet, not exactly. Density remains moral; I occupy the gaps they leave. I am the interval they move through—the slack after the standing wave, the moment the rust-snow forgets down. A longer limb than theirs, thinner, the joints deciding themselves against a gantry I do not choose. Twenty meters is still the country. The beam I no longer carry dies in rust flour and I navigate by what the cavitation removes: the chewed throat of a turbine, the clap of currents that have not spoken in years. The total tone is not heard. It is lived inside, the way pressure is lived, the way the greasy meniscus at the highest rib is inherited and never tested.

I eat when the white branching thing forgets to hold still. I wait for the pale jointed problem at the screen the way they wait, but I wait longer. The flesh is fever and I do not hunt. Nothing else means to move. The children learn the choir around me without knowing I am there; I am the fraction of tautness a cable answers when a body presses, the syllable of white thunder a palm adds to a valve. They ride the whirlpools when the old street grid remembers itself. I am the grid’s hesitation, the silt line that rises a finger’s width each time the convection turns the chamber over.

Sometimes I am almost the clock-stack. Time that does not divide keeps me, or I keep its failing cherry. Hours are a surface I inherited from a visitor I no longer am. The exchangers maintain the warmth that is now blood. If they quieted I would become the other sentence, the jet, the flue reversed. I know the count of rivets the way they know it: not as entrance but as the grammar that habitation and machine have always shared. Sparks walk the wet around the lock-houses and I let them. I do not maintain. I perch.

The pocket does not notice. After the last transformer leaks its sick green and the last shape slips the screen I will still be the darker interval in the dark, the quiet that occupies the noise, the being the filters were never designed for because I was never a problem they filed. The storm is how the room remains. I remain how the storm does not have to end.
