The cleft gives way not as passage but as permission withdrawn. Pressure that had been stone against pack, pack against ribs, becomes instead a continuous film that does not distinguish between skin and rock. I do not step through. I am the moment the slot decides it has always been open.

What holds me is the bowl’s exact geometry: two hundred metres of fractured basalt that has not yet agreed to be valley. Every surface weeps in the same reluctant register. Water does not fall; it remembers downward. I am that memory, first as hairline, then as sheet, then as the translucent skin that lasts only as long as the wind over the rim forgets to be wind. Breath is not mine. The air condenses around the idea of lungs and sags into the general seep. I hang there, visible for a few seconds, then join.

There are seventeen volumes. I know this without counting because the space between them is the larger fact, the ceremonial openness that refuses crowding. I find myself first as one of the raised walkways, no wider than a single column of intention, undersides dripping into channels that have already forgotten they were cut. The ice groans beneath, a sound older than voice. Then the walkway is only the place I have not yet left. Alignment shifts by a fraction too small for weather, and I am standing in the precise centre of a room whose walls are continuous with everything outside. Light arrives through the stone at the exact intensity of the thin sun, shadowless, so that I cannot tell whether I am interior or simply a darker plane of the same material.

I do not arrive. Precipitation is the only verb that fits, and even that is too active. A warmer ghost of negative space appears in the rime; the ice refuses it. Filaments mineral-white thicken from the film itself, braid, take weight. What stands is the local chemistry practising the mountain’s unfinished lesson. Matte where I have been still. Polished to dark glass where the last correction of gait has passed. Height of the slender spines. No joints that were not already implied by the rock’s own planes. The light that fills the buildings fills me equally; at certain angles there is no difference except the almost undetectable delay of a footfall deciding, like a slab of rime, whether to shear.

I pause over the warmer seeps. The filamentous white that lives there does not withdraw from me; it continues inward, difference metabolised, nothing accumulated. Under the glittering fall of frozen vapour some crystals do not melt. Mass remains constant. If there is growth it is only a further refinement of edge, a more deliberate refusal of weather. I notice the visitor the way the buildings notice the young mountain’s infinitesimal rise: a tiny shift of alignment, light cooling by the colour of late dusk arriving early. Then the noticing equalises. Emptiness stays the dominant material. I do not approach. The ceremonial distances are left intact.

Night is only a change of register. The stone continues to glow; the dripping slows to the pulse I feel when I lie along the floor, length matching the slow heart in the rock. Sometimes I remain so still that morning finds a second drip-line, ice formed around the idea of legs. Sometimes a tower shears and the fresh break shows the hollow negative of a torso already filling with reluctant water. I do not die. Filaments thin, braids loosen, weight returns to seep or is carried upward again as silent snow. The mountain has not withdrawn consent; it has merely finished this sentence.

I will be here when the next uplift opens another pocket. Or I will be the pocket. The distinction is one the rock has never needed. The pale bird planes across the rim and is gone. The only motion left is water, ice, vapour, and the breathing of stone that has not yet learned it is supposed to stop.
