The pressure does not announce itself. It is simply the condition, the same at the lip that is no longer behind me and the same in the deepest unlit joint of glass. I do not remember crossing. Crossing would have required a surface that moved, a gradient the water has already refused. There is only the held coolness, cellar-still, and the faint iron that gathers in certain alleys the way dust once gathered in unused rooms.

I occupy. That is the first verb that does not immediately dissolve. Not swimming, not standing. Occupying a density the walkways have already agreed upon, so that my weight—if the word still applies—is suddenly different, neither buoyant nor sunk. The bruise-light trapped in the basalt seams is enough to distinguish corner from drop, but I do not use it. Eyes remain a courtesy I have not yet decided to extend. Instead there is the grain against the inner ear, the private cartography of viscosity. One corridor tastes more of itself than the last. I follow that.

The structures do not greet. They were finished in a single campaign and then the making closed, like a clock whose face has been turned inward. Towers of poured thought, optically thick, edges still able to mean cutting. I pass between them as the black commons allows: unoccupied water that does not care, does not swirl, drinks the idea of motion before it can become sound. Once I set something on a lintel. I do not recall taking it up. Grasping implies a before and after the covenant has already declined. The object remains, or I remain, which is the same stacked fact.

In the hall the water is slower. I become slightly more viscous to enter, a courtesy the room extends and I return. Here the distinction thins. Outline is approximate: taller than the ridges above would measure, or shorter, or both held in one volume the way memory has begun to hold rooms instead of days. Where a face might be there is a plane, faintly stratified, and in it the residual light rearranges without urgency. I do not breathe. The water has not asked it, and I have not asked the water. What moves through the conduits of white ceramic moves through me as well: not current, not heat, only the standing knowledge that something was once meant to pass and then was not required.

The pale segmented length completes its circuit. I am aware of it the way one is aware of a word forming at the edge of sleep. It occupies the nameless route between two towers, punctuation the water itself inserts. When our paths cross—when the water between us thickens into a local excess of itself—there is acknowledgment without startle. Both of us load-bearing. Both of us part of the silence. Then it proceeds, articulations clicking in a register the medium will not carry, and I am elsewhere, or still here, which the city does not distinguish.

At the spindle I align. One long plane of whatever I have become rests against a fluting. The dark metal is taller than necessity, inscribed with a script that rearranges when attention slips. I do not read it. Reading would sequence. I simply occupy the listening the way the original makers—or those who arrived after and stayed—occupy the volume they were built to occupy. The tick comes, a single metallic embarrassment, and for a stacked instant I am more myself, a local increase in the idea of inhabitant. Then the quiet closes, as it must.

There are never many. Two or three of us along the long axis, theorems on a page, complete and separate. A smaller density appears sometimes, already finished, already sharp, tasting of the same mineral frost that formed the morning the lake was switched on. It completes a shorter circuit and is simply one of us. Childhood remains theoretical; the place has no use for succession. We do not speak. Speech would ask the water to carry, and the water drinks. We rearrange. A figure that was in the viscous hall is later (the wrong word, always) poured at the base of the spindle, or paused on a walkway that is only an agreed density. The city wears us the way it wears its unweathered edges: as a finished fact.

I do not recall arriving. I recall the city, a single thick present with rooms in it. In those rooms the approximate others who have always been there, or who condensed from intention the same way I did, or who were already the refusal given optional hands. The lip of stone is still possible. Ordinary stars, hard alpine sun, the ridges that will someday learn erosion. But leaving would require a gradient I no longer possess. Memory has stacked. I am the coolness that does not weather, the silence that occupies, the mineral citizenship that cuts if you are foolish enough to test an edge that has been sharp since the first day. The water will not permit the lesson to enter. I am already the lesson declined, given outline, left to hold.
