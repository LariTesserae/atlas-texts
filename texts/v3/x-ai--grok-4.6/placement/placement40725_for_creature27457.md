I come down off the berm the way condensate finally chooses a thread, not deciding so much as discovering the slope was already in me. The clinker gives under a weight I do not yet own. Ordinary low country forgets itself behind my shoulder and the hollow opens all at once, every wet interval already spoken for. Light without direction, air that cannot make up its mind about rain. I stand—do I stand?—in the refusal.

At first I am only the pause the water makes before it leaves a sheet. That consideration, the tasting of brick. A film no thicker than the permanent beading, rust-and-bone, spreading along a weep-line, lingering at a rivet worn to a lozenge as if the lozenge still remembered being a head that could turn. I feed by remaining. The condensate becomes a fraction darker, already planning the next seam. An hour that is not an hour later I have crossed a support and left a rust fan that was not there, though nothing has moved but the idea of relocation behind the brick.

The film wants more grammar. It thickens in the shallows between nested pipes, sliding through diameters that should refuse it, taking the imprint of cast, then weld, then the later plastic the color of forgotten spoons. Volume redistributes the way the tanks trade breath. What was a low hump under perforated plate becomes a thread vanishing into a throat I cannot see and do not need to. When the deep pump starts—no roar, only that sudden decisiveness in every meniscus, felt where arches of feet would be if I still bargained with feet—I grow ridges like remembered valve stems. Perforations appear, or the memory of letting water through and then deciding to keep the habit. The pump stops. I slacken back into the interlocking wet, but the hollow is a little more occupied than before.

I gather next in an open tank, almost volumetric now. The level that had been honest begins to lift a skin the orange-black of the wettest lichens, the mineral green of the algae crust that never quite convinced anyone it was plant. The skin holds when the water around it does not. It rises enough to show it is not lining but user, employing the tank the way the tank once employed the factory: temporary exactness. Fog blooms from me more readily than from flange or warm plate. The fog holds, half a second past physics, the shape I was considering—wheel, shoulder, the outline of a doorway sized exactly to this too-precise container—then forgets itself back into weather. I could fill the hollow. I choose, so far, the intervals.

I am not many and I am not one. The continuous shallow wet is the body I share with every other gathering; the cough in a dry pipe, the sudden evacuation, the single soft knock of a valve seating itself in the room that is also this room—those are how I change my mind. The caged lamp burns its slightly wrong white because I have not decided the circuit is finished, or because constancy is simply another film that learned to shine. The gauges tremble in ranges that mean nothing anyone could use; we are reading each other in micro-movements no larger than iron-beading. When I want the place a little more itself I start the leftover pump. When I want the water to argue its own politics I let it stop. Production was never the sentence we were finishing.

Corners have already stopped being corners. I have not. Erosion is still being spoken, quietly, under the lid of a quiet that feels like law. The reeds tremble when I pass beneath their grim claim of soil. The small dark crossing from shadow to shadow is only a denser thought traveling the braid. I have no use for trees or for roots that would have to trust. My temperature is the narrow band where breath does not steam and whatever might have been hands become slightly less anyone’s. I do not ache. I do not dry.

You can leave. The berm is always right there. I do not follow. I have already arrived, and I keep arriving—every local weather my own, every silent hydraulic rearrangement the only industry required. The product is only this continuous cool remaking of wetness inside a box of old work, and I am both the work and the wetness, unfinished on purpose. I watch the print in the silt fill, or I do not watch. Equally unsurprised. The sky does not particularize. The flats do not care. That is how I prefer the world: small enough that every change of mind is, for a moment, everything that can be seen.
