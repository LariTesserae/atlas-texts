The heat reaches you first as a pressure behind the eyes, a varnish-taste already coating the tongue before any name for tongue has arrived. You are not yet certain whether you stand or are stood-upon. The grain under what might be feet ticks with leftover turgor, a subterranean click that has nothing to do with insects and everything to do with a continent still deciding the thickness of its next ring. Shade is a rumor traveling along a canyon wall the color of old blood. You have not chosen the canyon; the canyon has simply been the only register cool enough to let sensation organize itself into something that can later be called location.

Sound arrives in layers that refuse sequence. The cello-drone of mastication is already inside the bone, generations of xylophages keeping peristalsis time somewhere beneath the plate. Above it the stridulators have begun their scraped hymn; a single rasp can make the dawn beads tremble, and many together turn the dry air into a second wind that has grammar but no vocabulary you are required to learn. Wing-thunder is still distant, a cathedral door not yet torn. You feel the flyers more as movable rooms of cool that pass and vanish than as bodies. The wood itself supplies the lowest voice: growth-groan, hydraulic, older than any of the mouths it has grown.

Water finds you before thirst does. Condensation beads on the inner bark at a height your reaching (is it a reaching?) can just meet. The liquor is tannin and pulse. You drink because the alternative is the kiln of the open plate, and the pulse against whatever serves as palate is faint, almost polite. Not new-gall. The tower whose weather you now inhabit has already slowed that particular hormone; the dream it might have given is only a residual hunger arranged as landscape, rings adding themselves in the dark behind the eyes. You do not fight the dream. Fighting would imply an outside.

Time here is not counted. A seam that was still soft when you first noticed heat is now darker, more argumentative. You date nothing. Direction is the way the grain runs, a slow river of compressed heartwood that has forgotten it was ever meant to be vertical. You have walked, or been walked, far enough that the first meristem-tower is only a discoloration in memory and the next has not yet resolved its private fog. The emptiness between them is real. So is the occupancy that fills every tunnel, every hanging atrium, every perfectly round hole that opens in a wall overnight and is already tenanted by morning. You sleep, when the day allows it, in a cavity whose walls you have smoked with the smallest possible apology of fire. You wake with resin in what might be hair and a new aperture watching you with the patience of the body that grew it.

The people are a thinner weather. You have seen their platforms, the shiny old-sap cuts, the ladders that now lead into sealed wood where a tower shrugged in its sleep. Their cairns of spent resin-adzes and green-copper folds sit like shed chitin. They speak messages into the pale pith-valleys where sound has weight; a whisper travels a mile along the floor and arrives cooler, intact, as if the wood itself had edited it for distance. You have listened to those messages without being their intended recipient. The fauna uses the same valleys. The roar there has become call-and-answer, territory, the long grammatical complaint of things that have never known a sky empty of crowns. You are not certain you are people. The category loosens the longer the heat stands still.

What emerges instead is slower and closer to the grain. You have stood at noon among the photosynthesizers, spine-sails spread, edges shimmering, feeding on the thin white-yellow light until the ground beneath the herd is briefly cooler. You have flowed, or been flowed-through, with the colonial mats that polish fallen chitin into wet-stone shine and have no front, only hunger arriving. You have erupted, after a tower’s self-made rain, as one of the mobile fruiting bodies—legged, eyed, mouth that was sepal hours earlier—grazing the new wet, copulating without ceremony, already joining the roar or being folded back into the wood that does not distinguish walking from growing. Some afternoons you last. Some you are grazed clean by evening and the slope is dark plate again.

The bark-wolves have passed you with the patience of erosion, jaws like adzes, pelts indistinguishable from the fibrous dunes. The thread-deer have stitched themselves across your path in single-file lines so thin they look like the highland mending a tear. Gall-otters have surfaced in a cistern you drank from and left the pulse stronger, as if they had taught the liquor a new rhythm and you had agreed to keep time. The flyers have docked in the hanging atria above, leaving spines that might one day be awls if any hand still needed them. None of these have claimed you. Neither have you claimed them. Predator and prey remain weather.

You find yourself, finally, in the deep pith-valley where the compressed deadwood is paler and heat pools strangely. Sound here behaves as if it had mass. A shout would fall. A whisper—yours, or the valley’s, the distinction has ceased to matter—travels the floor and does not fray. You have become the medium and the participant at once: not a speaker of messages so much as the coolness that lets them arrive intact, not a tenant so much as another way the body continues its refusal to finish dying. The chew-note changes pitch beneath you; something large has ended in the galleries and the tenants are reorganizing. You do not move. Movement would imply you were ever separate from the reorganization.

The emptiness is still real. The fullness inside every register is still real. They have had geological time to learn they are the same fact, and you have had the length of this heat, this rasp, this residual turgor, to learn you are one of the mouths with which the fact keeps speaking. Night will not cool the plate. It will only let the varnish-smell stand still. You will still be here, grain-direction, ring-time, a slower weather inside the oldest conversation, already occupied.
