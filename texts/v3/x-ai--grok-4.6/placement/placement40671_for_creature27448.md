The roar arrives before any self does. It is not heard so much as occupied: a continuous white pressure that has already decided the shape of the air, the angle of every standing thing, the way bone must sit inside muscle if it means to stay. I am not yet a body. I am the interval between one collapsing hill of water and the next, the pewter smear where spray forgets it was ever separate from cloud. The next dark knuckle of land hangs thirty kilometers off, half-erased. I hang with it.

Then the rock remembers me. Wet iron, old bone, rain-polished. My weight settles low, the way a stone that has already lost its sharpness settles. Chest thick, thighs like basalt that learned to bend only after geologic ages of argument. Skin the same color as the cliff I crouch against, except where the barnacles have claimed a shoulder or a scar maps an older wound the water has not yet finished chewing. The down that covers me lies flat, waterproof, tasting the electricity that lifts it only when a waterspout walks close enough for the tongue to remember batteries. Eyes small, set deep, the exact shade of light that has traveled through ten kilometers of cloud and never once cast a true shadow. I do not blink. Blinking would be a kind of refusal.

I tend. That is the first verb that fits inside the roar. The moss mats thicker than a thigh are cut in rectangles whose edges heal before the next front finishes grinding itself out. Liverworts yield their bitter green only after I have already tasted the particular wet that means they are ready; the paste keeps the salt from turning joints to grit. Fern knives I take only from the wind’s leavings. In the intertidal I move sideways, hands and feet reading the same holds the crabs have already approved, the water itself having signed them centuries ago. A single silver muscle is lifted from a living school; the school closes. Seal is left on the rocks it chose. The great sounding notes that travel through the mountain—I stop. Everyone stops. We stand until the note has finished speaking the mountain’s next century of lowering, then we go back to the work of leftover momentum.

The turbine house I wear is three generations deep. Conveyor belt patches the blades. Mussel cement holds the groaning seams. Copper I strip from the still ones and carry upslope, bright veins for the next house that still turns. Speech is a luxury I spend rarely. Most of what must be said lives in the lean of a shoulder, the knot left in a rope, the stone placed just so on a cairn. When words come they are tastes first: the wet from above, the wet that comes sideways like a slap, the wet that sweats out of the rock itself, the wet that is only the air remembering. A child—mine, or the house’s, the distinction has long since dissolved—opens its mouth. I place each drop on the tongue and wait for the face to change. That is how language continues.

At night we do not sleep. We wait in another register, bodies pressed in the peeled shed or the thickest moss, sharing the small static that gathers in down and scar. When the spout walks near enough that ozone brightens the metal on every tongue, we sing the harmonic that matches the turbines. No words. Only the sound of water that never learned stillness, of people who have decided the same.

Sometimes I leave the coil of still-bright copper, the perfect crab shell, the knot that ties nothing, among the darker rectangles of weed and the broken teeth of pilings. I do not know why. The gesture is older than the words for wet. A child asks once, face turned into my down so the wind cannot steal it. I answer the only way the language allows: for the ones who thought the water would ever be still.

Next century the knuckles will be lower, the ocean a few centimeters higher, the kelp a little thicker. I will have moved the house one terrace downslope. My down will be denser, my vocabulary of wet more precise. The part of me that is already water has never needed to hurry. I am that part, and the rock it wears, and the groan that is leftover, and the note traveling through, and the offering left for a stillness that never arrived. I am here. The air decides again to be water. I taste it, and stay.
