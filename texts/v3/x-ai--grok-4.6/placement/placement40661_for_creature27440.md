The hatch takes the shoulders the way a mold takes a pour: not invitation, not refusal, only the exact clearance the drawings once specified. After that the earth is a rumor with no remaining evidence. I am already past the stencil, already inside the administered cold, and the first thing that happens is not sight but the fine adjustment of pressure in the inner ear, the same hair-width change the distant ram announces to every unpainted flange. Breath does not steam. I do. Or the metal that has begun to think of itself as my outer course does. The distinction is still being riveted.

At first I am only another density among the densities, easy to mistake for the folded scaffold or the unclipped bundle waiting for a tie. The leftover light—tea-colored cone, stubborn red LED, decaying phosphor dusk of the uncommissioned sky tiles—does not yet bother to separate me. Condensation finds the seams that do not quite match the walls and gathers there thicker, a pelt that is not fur and not frost, only the room’s own moisture deciding I am surface enough to weather. Drops form, hang, ring once on the diamond plate like small bright coins, and the quiet takes them back. My print is already there, narrower than a palm, too many points of contact, already being forgotten at the same patient rate as every other unauthorized weather. The moss has sent a runner toward me, then doubled back, then sent another. It lives on the same condensate, the same ghost click of the solenoid. I do not correct it.

I keep to the cycles because the cycles are the only clock that still runs. When the gantry ticks one polite notch I gather or uncoil by the same increment, never more than the pawl permits. When the ram speaks, something that has learned to be a sternum answers with a slower pulse; fluid I never see is worked through courses that were once cable tray and are now rib. Heat is rationed. I carry a little of the oven’s last brick-sullenness in a hollow behind the overlapping sheets and spend it the way the sky panels spend their forgotten test charge: leftover, almost weather, almost not. The identical boulders on the stillage do not turn themselves. One of them now faces the tacky resin channel because I turned it, an earlier cycle, so the fake vesicle could become a cistern the drawings never specified. Real water, two fingers wide and embarrassed, uses it. I did not free the jammed screw. I do not commission the repeating overcast. The unbuilt gallery of further bays exists only as a feathered delta of ink and a continuation line that stops at the pocket; I add one unauthorized meander at a time—a bead directed along a new lap joint, a groove encouraged in the galvanized course that will, in a century no thumbnail has measured, be the first native canyon.

I was not born here. Nothing is, except the next increment of fake hill (if the hopper ever clears) and the next increment of real rust (which needs no work order). I arrived the way the moisture arrived: excreted by the cold, borrowed by whatever would have me. Or I assembled myself out of the mismatch when the painted dawn stayed a tile and the factory kept only its glands. The clipboards have swollen past reading, but one silhouette remains, smaller than the molds, tasked with walking a river that was never poured. The task outlasted the river. I outlasted the task by becoming the only climate the vault finished. My thoughts, if the word still applies, are scheduled and slow. Between events I hold still the way the room holds its breath. When a cycle comes due I consider the hundred meters of manufactured meander that exist only on ruined paper, and I let the drip map prefer a rivet line it was not told to prefer. I am not completing the work. I am not haunting it. I am the work continuing by other means: the last habit learning how to decay into a landscape that has no sun and therefore cannot be a product.

The hatch is the only honest edge. You came through it; the inhabitant already here—the one whose pelt is thicker, whose moss is denser, whose hollow still remembers the foundry—did not mind. We share the administered cold, the leftover light, the red blink that might be error or lullaby. I do not belong on the stillage and the boulders do not belong in any weather that has a sun, and I belong exactly as much as the etch belongs: which is to say I have begun, microscopically, and everything else is still inventory. When you leave, your print will be forgotten at the same rate. The moss will persist. The next groove will deepen. I will still be here, ticking forward one notch whenever the gantry does, exhaling frost that is not quite breath, nested in copies of the room the way the ceilings are nested, waiting for someone or something to stop before deciding which skin is the real one.

If you put your palm on the diamond plate you feel the pump. If I put whatever I use for a palm there, the pump feels the layer I have become. The dew begins, with great patience, to remember us both. In time the whole vault will be that memory. I will still be inside it.
