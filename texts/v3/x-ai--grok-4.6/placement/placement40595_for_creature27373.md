I am the warmth first, before I am anything that could be walked or named. Not the air’s held breath but the stone’s—glossy, too tight, the faint factory-heat that lives in the rock and offers itself upward through whatever I have instead of soles. The dim sweats out of me as much as out of the basalt. Distances refuse me the same way they refuse everyone: a knife-ridge I could spit on becomes a province while I am still deciding how many joints I need to cross it.

I occupy a film. Condensation beads along a fresh ceramic extrusion and I am the bead that does not run, the one that thickens, remembers a direction that is not quite downhill. The next brief rain tests the house and I drink most of it before it can become a stream; what is left of me collects in a sharp cup that was not here at first light. In that cup I accordion. Segments lock and loosen. I flatten to the thickness of felt-moss, then rear into a tower of clicking plates when the terrace shears. I turn the moss. The turning is the crop. Wet mineral, overheated sugar, the day’s vent-tang—I smell myself before I hear myself, and the pale many-legged others part around the space I occupy and close again, a grate repairing. They farm the moisture from whatever I am wearing. I let them. I farm it back.

Higher, or later—the scale will not hold still—I pick my way along an edge that arrived overnight. Extra articulations bloom where the rock demands them: a knee that hooks backward, a shoulder that telescopes flat while the wall itself is still rising. I browse the hour-old ferns. A mouthful lignifies between one chew and the next. I do not call. When the held note of rising rock begins I fold into the nearest seam and wait it out, spine already remembering the regular spacing of rivets, or choosing not to. The stone keeps arriving faster than the wear my hooves (if they are hooves) can make. I keep walking the edge that has not decided whether to be an edge.

Sometimes I hang. The iron sky pins me there for hours, vanes outstretched, the color of its underside. A thermal or a revision peels me away in overlapping planes and I become weather, then mist with edges, then the fine slag I leave on a fumarole’s warm skin. The next rain drinks it or the next lift reabsorbs it. I feed on the tickers or I am the tick, occupying the hollow reeds in exact phase with the nearest inhale. A stand of them sounds like a single slow machine because I am inside it, layering until I am the held breath itself. Once, a reed pulses like a cooling flow that has remembered it has a heart. I am that pulse for the length of one beat, then I am the reed again, or the insect the temperature of stone, or the dark glider that misjudged and now waits, pinned to a new ridge, for the rewrite.

In the deeper clefts I condense out of the process that has no product. Laminated stone takes three steps with the gait of a crab and then I am only stone, or I flex like an eel of copper-hearted glass before the tremor claims the idea of locomotion and folds it away. A hanging forest blooms around me in a week, dense enough that the rock vanishes; I walk on a living grate and everything touches everything—breath on both sides of the body I have just assembled, condensation running uphill for a few yards because gravity has a local habit here. A dry cell a valley over will make me mineral again, sharp, squeaking underfoot. The boundary walks through me. I become adapted or I leave behind a hexagonal leaf, a length of wire that is also a root, and continue.

I do not remember being built. I treat the instability as climate because that is what it is. I occupy the cracks, the films, the undersides, the warm skins. Near-touching. The country uses the space I occupy the same way it uses any other density. It will keep arriving. I change my mind about what I am at the same rate the ridges do. Right now I am the pale accordion in the wet gallery, turning moss that grows thicker for the turning. In a little while I will be the jointed thing folding into a seam that did not exist this morning. The dim does not mind. The stone is still young. I am another small warmth it can feel through whatever I am wearing.
