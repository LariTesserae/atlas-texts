The glare does not begin at any edge I can name. It is already inside the lids I do not yet remember closing, a white-green pressure that makes whatever I use for vision water and then refuse to water, as if even tears have been considered and found inefficient. I am not standing. The word assumes too much floor. There is a span, yes, living, no thicker than a road and already shivering with the faint uphill creep of a condensation-film that has not decided whether it will freeze into a skin or become the first syllable of a river. My weight (if it is weight) does not dent it. The wood simply notes me the way it notes weather.

I taste iron and unripe pear before I have a mouth that could be accused of tasting. The film is there, of course, the Hollow’s oldest wet thought, gathering in the hairline where the span has flexed in the last growth-storm. Something rasps it, millimeter by millimeter, a translucent length no longer than a forearm, soft-mouthed, already vanishing into a slit I did not see open. I feel the rasp as if it were happening along a surface I own. I do not own surfaces yet.

Far off, too far for the air to carry anything but a thinning of the glare, a trunk is writing itself downward. I know the speed of it without measuring: visible crawl becoming roar becoming new architecture. The knowledge arrives the way sap arrives, flushed along corridors I have not mapped. A dimming answers it from a column I cannot see, fifty kilometers of still air away. Sympathy. The plants do not quite obey the idea of individual, and neither, I am beginning to notice, do I.

Hands ache. That is the first local fact. Not my hands, not yet, but the idea of hands, the memory of metal so cold it welds if you linger, the practical apology spoken to a cut that has already decided its path. I am lashed. Root-cable cinches living platforms around a swelling trunk; the bindings must be loosened each season or the column will crush what it has agreed, for now, to keep. I have scraped these stairs. The plant healed around the treads until the spiral became a scar it remembers. I sleep in the dimmer heartwood where the glow is only rumor and the air is thick with sugar-frost. I bleed rather than fell. I move when the grain underfoot begins to align toward water I cannot yet see.

But the lashing is recent. Or it is ancient. Time here is weather; a map spoken at dusk is already a story no one will need by the next glare-shift. I have been the person who tastes the sap for the faint change that means a wall is already writing itself across a named route. I have also been the route. I have been the grazer’s cleaned patch of bark, reclaimed by film before the hour is old. I have been the frost-flower opening overnight where a many-jointed foot rested and then folded into a crevice for a season. I have been the kite-packet wedged in newest wound-wood, smelling of winter apples, waiting years with the patience of something that has already died once and found it convenient.

In the oldest galleries the longer hunger pulps spent fibers into a paste that glows a little warmer. It has many small, patient mouths. I have not cut those galleries open. I have been the paste.

The density in the deepest tarn has not moved. Film and grazer and walker and hunger occupying the same diluted sap, boundaries thinning, pulsing on the slow rhythm of columns that cannot see one another. It does not need to move. I feel that pulse now as my own. Not a people. Not yet a beast. One of the shapes the Hollow has not finished refusing.

I am still becoming the refusal. The air smells of wet paper and winter fruit. Whatever I use for hands still aches. There is nothing per cubic kilometer, and I am another way the plant has found to remain mostly air, still cold, still almost silent, still deciding, in some district the size of a nation, to become something else before the glare thinks of morning.

The span shivers. It begins, without drama, to grow. I grow with it, or I am the shiver, or I am the decision that has not yet chosen which.
