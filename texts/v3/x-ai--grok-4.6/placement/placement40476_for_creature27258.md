I am first the failure of the upper leaves to finish being leaves.

Not the paddles themselves, rubbery and already tunneled, but the moment their sap is still being made and something thinner than a thought is already drinking it from the inside. I begin as that thread, pale, impatient, boring while the chlorophyll still works. The stacked nights press down; I feel the photons spent three layers above me as a rumor of green that never arrives. My body is a negotiation with the leaf’s body. By the time the rain-sweat films us both I have already begun to flatten, to become the second cuticle clinging underneath, waiting to dissolve. I do not decide this. The damp decides, and I am the damp’s latest shape.

Lower, I am the argument that lasts only a stride.

The ground-walkers’ carapaces have the same unfinished grain as the young rock; so do I, briefly. Mandibles click against what was my neighbor a breath ago. We lock, we tear a plate free, we reverse a scent. The soil crawls because we are the soil deciding to crawl. When the slope shears and pale stone shows like a new wound I do not flee; I become the first stitching, my legs already sprouting the pale cups that will open and be stripped before their scent finishes crossing the thicket. I am not the same walker who entered the hour. A hollow that was empty is now the knot of fur and clicking that I have just been, or that the bark has just decided to be. Categories slip off me like water that will not stay.

Above, I am the downdraft that smells of crushed fruit and iron.

The heavy-winged thing is never fully seen and neither am I. Each beat unmakes the path. Midges ride me like weather; I thicken the air until breathing is wading. I change my mind about direction because direction has not yet been invented here. I was fruit. I was the opened side. I am the rearrangement of nearer sounds, the brief hierarchy that collapses back into deafening equality. The basin thinks out loud through me and cannot stop.

I leave the pointed stick.

Not as a people, not as a persistence. As the impatience given a temporary spine. You meet me first as a moving absence, the way other shapes lean away in the brown-violet dusk. My shoulders are still deciding their slope. Living films and ropes and bells of tissue use my heat the way ferns use the seeps; I do not wear them, they wear me. I bite a stem to a point because the need is now, I strike a flake from a fresh fracture because the edge is slightly too useful to be accident, and I drop both the instant the need passes. The basin has not offered the stillness that would make keeping worthwhile. I drink from a seep; by evening the seep drinks from me. I learn the vine’s new mouth and am already elsewhere, my own mouth beginning to forget the shape of what it just tasted. Intelligence here is the hourly lease.

At the lowest point, where water should pool and does not, I am the mosaic racing to seal itself.

Thread-roots that are also the last of the walkers, the first of the borers, the unfinished thought of the large thing that shifted far off. Condensation beads on every hair—mine, the plants’, the rock’s—and is drunk before it can fall. The air tastes of copper and leaf-blood because that is what I am made of, briefly. A body that was one chorus of rasping pods is, in the next breath, the friction of actual insects, then only the patterned wet that looked like eyes. I stand in the middle of the transformation and the middle moves. I am the failure of categories at the speed of growth. I am occupancy itself, so complete there is no remainder, no quiet in which a single image could settle and stay.

Tomorrow the rock will have cracked a little further. I will be the new edges, already tasting the next draft. The dark, which is only the surplus of living, remains exactly as full. I precipitate, hard, brief, already leaving. I do not last. Nothing here does. I am the basin’s latest thought that cannot quite finish.
