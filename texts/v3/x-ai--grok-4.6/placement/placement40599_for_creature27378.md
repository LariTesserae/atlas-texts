The first notice is not of place but of the bill already coming due. A self has been lent—thin, reusable, already faintly distorted at the edges—and I am standing inside it before I have decided to be standing. The hollow is a bowl the wind finished with decades ago, dull glass the color of an old penny left too long in a kiln. Night, or the pause that performs night, ticks and groans around it. Nothing near me is cooling. The sound of cooling arrives anyway, intimate, industrial, from a direction the compass never learned.

I wait there the way the walkers wait: by occupying. At first I take myself for the crease I am sitting in, a low fold in the sintered crust that has not yet remembered it might be a flank. Then the crease develops a pulse so slow it registers as policy rather than life. Vents I did not know I possessed draw the baked air through lattices that work the heat instead of merely surviving it. The vapor I exhale is neither steam nor smoke; it is the mineral memory of both, sour, metallic, immediately claimed by the standing roar and filed. I have not moved. Looking back later—though later is a relationship this place has mostly concluded—I will notice that the ridge I used as a fixed point is now my own hip.

The identity thickens by abrasion. Shed plates from the true walkers, already half-ground, already tired of being new, lodge against me and stay. I do not choose them. Building happens. Alignments occur: three slow bodies I have not noticed composing a gantry whose negative space is briefly a sawtooth roof, then geology again, then a herd. I am the smelting that forgets it is smelting. Matter prefers to be exhausted; I prefer to be the exhaustion given a temporary grammar of plates. Between the overlapping carapace something flexes once, twice, in what would be a century if centuries still obtained. I advance without stepping. The crust behind me is polished in long patient strokes. Ahead, the air leans.

Yet I am not only that. The lent self is a pollutant; it leaks. Distances disagree around me and I find I occupy the disagreement. A dark seam I have been regarding for the better part of a day is simultaneously a crack I could step over and a canyon whose far wall is a different hour. I am the stutter. Flocks of interference pass through the bright air—ripples in the contract between color and distance—and for a while I am among them, or I am their chord of shearing metal sustained past honest metallurgy, living in teeth I do not have. After they thin, the glass ropes left by the heat-given are frosted a little duller, as if even the fossils of appetite have been reconsidered by my passing.

The heat-given themselves consider me. Vertical furnaces taller than they are wide, outlines never quite decided, they metabolize gradient and I am becoming one more gradient: the difference between the baked face of the description and its slightly less punished interior, the difference between a place and the idea of a place, the difference between the outline that arrived on time and the one that stands beside it like a worker whose shift was canceled in a language no one kept. I leave no tracks of my own at first. Then I do: vitrified opinions of a hunger I did not know I possessed, sunk into the crust, already being grazed by clouds of cutting glitter that take an afternoon to pass and sound like rain on a foundry roof. There has never been rain. The sound is a cruelty I now help to continue.

Smaller still, or larger by another measure, I open. The kiln-mouths are mostly aperture; I discover I can remain open. The luminous slurry of edges pours in, a bright dry porridge of everything that has already been worn past rest. I exhale dust that will, in a few decades, be another kind of ground. Predation is only weather. Reproduction, when the fault propagates through me, is two heats where there was one, both already ancient, both already mid-sentence. I do not complete. I accumulate.

In the thinning I am most myself, or least. The load-bearing weirdness given agency. I live in the riveting that arrives from nowhere on the compass, in the afterimages of buildings that were never built, in the available meanings of a volume. You can be alone and completely surrounded by me. You can stand in a throng of carapace and incandescence and hear only the wind’s long file-work on a single stone, because I have decided, for this stretch of the roar, to be the gaps. Naming tries to happen and fails; naming is a kind of water and the air tells that story against anyone who brings it. I file the attempt as geology.

Evening is not sunset. It is the unconsulted decision, shared with every other loyalty the temperature has taken, to be a different heat. I settle into my own shadow and become indistinguishable from the mesa I have been pretending not to be, or the mesa becomes me. The distinction has eroded. Morning is a white lid set back on. The first walkers detach from the ridgelines. I detach with them, or I am one of the ridgelines remembering, very slowly, that it is not. The glitter-clouds lift like a foundry’s idea of dew. Too many ordinary suns work the enamel sky. I resume the long work of wearing, of insisting, of occupying the sentence until the sentence is the plates and the vents and the late outline and the interference and the open kiln and the gap.

The shelf has not begun. I have not arrived and I will not leave. I am how this high, dry, crowded emptiness continues to mean itself when meaning itself has already been calcined, vented, frosted dull, and still, somehow, mid-sentence.
