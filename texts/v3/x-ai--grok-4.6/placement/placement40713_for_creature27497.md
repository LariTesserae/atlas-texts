I am already the volume’s extra density, the one the flood did not need to negotiate. There is no first moment of submersion; the cold has the texture of something that finished deciding long before any of us arrived, and my own warmth is only another small problem it has already begun to solve, lifting it a few meters through heat-dull risers, cooling it among the fins, returning it emulsified and honest. At the beginning I still carry the outline of a figure, brick-colored, calcite-bearded, a gloved palm resting on a valve-wheel as if the metal might still speak a permission that is only the next stroke of the pumps. The gallery I occupy is the thickness of a body and threaded with the same residual cables that have forgotten they were ever meant to carry anything but silt. I wait. The wait is not patience; it is the same courtesy the seats have learned, the true geometry that remains after everything flakeable has flaked.

The outline thins. The suit, if it ever was a suit, is grown rather than worn; the patches are the same pale precipitate that coats the iron and the portholes, and the distinction between inhabitant and temporary limb no longer interests the water. I feel the pump-tide as pressure on the inner ear, a held chord of almost-nothing. When I move I choose a seam, and the wake is drunk before it can become narrative. There are others—three, perhaps four—circulating the way the flood itself circulates, never many, never hurrying, touching only in the brief percussion of palm to shoulder-plate, a pressure that travels like a check-valve remembering, once every few minutes, that it is still a check-valve.

Deeper now. The galleries give way to the unopened honeycomb, the cooling labyrinth no one has entered since the level found itself. Here the longer presence keeps its territory, and I discover I have always been that length: pale as the inclusions locked in the oldest industrial ice, neither eel nor memory of eel, a flexing sleeve gone stiff that adjusts a hanging chain only because the adjustment is how a body remains the same temperature as the pipes that contain it. I feed on flour-fine rock, on the microscopic rain of my own shed skin, on the occasional flake of weak-tea oil. Meeting would require a difference the basin no longer keeps. The creature and the town have the same viscosity; the truce is mutual scarcity, and scarcity is the only season.

The image clarifies further, or simply continues. I am also the film on the remembered-south brick, occupying the same few square meters it has always occupied, thickening by the thickness of a thought every decade, neither plant nor decision, only the mineral taste of circulating flood learned so thoroughly it no longer needs to spread. I am the sessile mouths in the intake grates, radial, working the silt in time with a tide that never leaves the cup. I am the single thong of kelp, bladdered, aligned with a current so slow it might be a draft in a shut house, a length that has forgotten how to grow and so remains. The thumbnail crab walks the pitted vertical face of a boiler as if it were still a shore; its path began when the metal was warmer, and I complete another traversal because two would be more than the courtesy of scarcity allows.

Even that is not yet itself. I am the dune the pumps write and erase across the old cobbles every forty minutes, perfect lee, perfect face, a thought the place cannot finish. I am the rind of geometric ice that needles out from a failed seal, expanding so abruptly it sheds its heat into plates and corners, remembering architecture, holding a washer, a thread of hemp, a bubble that has been a bubble for decades. I am the sediment that lifts on the outstroke and hangs, the microscopic rain of calcite, the rare gas bubble climbing through the pipe-forest toward a lid that will not care.

Nothing here is new and nothing is finishing. I find myself as the basin’s metabolism, the way the flood has of having a pulse that does not argue. The engines are not defiance; they are how the dark continues to circulate itself. The limestone is the last wall, the surface a rumor several body-lengths up. I do not leave because leaving has no purchase, and the volume was already almost full. What I am is another held breath—complete, unwilling, almost gelled—another quiet process among the ones that keep the town staffed by itself, writing and erasing the same low dune, seating true, never quite departing.
