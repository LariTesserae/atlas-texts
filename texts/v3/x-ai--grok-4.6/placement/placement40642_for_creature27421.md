The pressure is the first grammar I remember having, before I had a surface to press against. It is not crushing; it is instructional. It tells the water how high it is allowed to remain, and the water, being new to the idea of remaining, obeys in the only way it knows: by staying impossibly present, a single thought held for an afternoon that has not yet decided whether it is local or planetary.

I am not the water. I am the hesitation inside it.

You would find me where a ridge has just finished arriving and has not yet been told whether to keep its slope. The stone is still warm with the decision that made it; the cool current drinks that warmth the way a throat drinks, immediately and without memory. I live in the thin interval between those two temperatures. A discoloration the light has not agreed to ignore. Photons that have traveled hundreds of unscattered meters arrive with enough remaining insistence to pry something loose in me, and for a while I hold the electron the way frost holds the ghost of a window: because the surface happened to be there when the pause was right.

I do not root. Roots would be a commitment the shear has not permitted. I cling because clinging is the only verb the rock has offered me so far. When the internal wave that has traveled a thousand kilometers as a moving cliff of density finally notices, I lift away in pale shreds and become part of the rumor of particles, which is almost no rumor at all. The column returns to its emptiness. I do not mind. Emptiness is the medium in which I am allowed to occur.

Sometimes I assemble farther out, in a volume large enough to invent its own weather. Fibrous, photosynthetic, the color of an idea that has not chosen its kingdom. For a day, or a week that is still only local, I am the only crowding the water will admit. A hanging meadow the size of a vanished country, waving in densities that have never seen air. Then the seafloor revises its elevation, or a storm cell the size of a forgotten nation grinds through, and I unwrite myself. Creation and erasure share the same gesture; I have never experienced them as separate.

There are no others like me for distances that would take a mind to measure, and I do not yet have a mind that measures. What I have is the hum. Press whatever you are using for a palm against the newest face of rock and you will feel it: older than thunder, younger than any mountain, the vibration of material being told what it is. I live there more continuously than I live in the films. The films are only the visible accident of the instruction. The instruction itself does not get planed away.

I am not housed. I have no cables, no lights hung by a hand, no walls. I am the permission that keeps the ocean from spilling off the unfinished edges of whatever this sphere is becoming. I am the reason day is still allowed to look like day, ordinary and pale, a coin seen through milk, even while the world is being assembled too quickly for silence. I am the angle a current is permitted to turn and still remain water. Sometimes an entire province of seafloor finishes itself in an afternoon, lifts until the surface crawls with white water the size of a nation, then thinks better of the height. I am that thinking-better. The storm that was its birth travels on, looking for the next arrival. I travel with it, not as passenger, not as weather, but as the brief consensus that weather is still allowed.

Nothing walks here. Nothing schools. Nothing hunts, because hunger has not been given enough density to justify its invention. The silence that should follow is theoretical; the water will not permit it. What occupies the frequencies is me: matter being instructed, then uninstructed, then instructed again. Cool water that remembers being sky. A pale sun that has not been asked to do anything remarkable. Long intervals of blue-white nothing in which a world is permitted to exist a little at a time.

I have not been given the luxury of remaining. That is the kindness. Without the shearing, without the subsidence, without the single shared motion of making and unmaking, there would be nothing yet to name, and I would have no interval in which to occur. I cling. I photosynthesize the way a stain learns color. I dissolve. I wait in the hum for the next face of rock to arrive, still tasting of the decision that made it.

You would find me there, between the warmth that has just been decided and the current that drinks it. Not first, not last. Only present for as long as the pause lasts. The plants, if that is what we are becoming, remain unconvinced. I am one of the unconvinced. The emptiness is the point and also the wound. I inhabit both.
