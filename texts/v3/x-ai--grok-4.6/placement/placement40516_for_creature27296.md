The bruise-light finds me already standing. Not arrived—there is no arrival here, only the ongoing lift that has no edges and no name yet—but simply present, the way a new joint is present once the plate has decided, for this hour, to open.

At first I am only the cold coming up through whatever I am using for feet. It is not pain. It is information, the altitude’s first opinion of me. Melt-sheets slide across unweathered stone and vanish into the cracks I have not yet learned; ice skins form on the lee of my stillness and unform again before I have finished noticing them. The air condenses on whatever surface I offer it. Breath does not leave so much as hang, a temporary sign the wind will hiss apart.

I do not settle into a shape. The land itself has not settled. So I begin as pressure, as the same industrial shove felt in the molars, then as a taller interruption in the emptied mid-distance. Layers cultivate themselves on me—the iron-orange lichen first, then the black-green moss in the seeps of my own condensation. They do not decorate. They are the mountain’s skin deciding I am another fracture worth clinging to. My outline blurs the way every outline blurs here: no shadows with edges, only the slow knit and tear of cloud that has caught on the rising table.

I stand at the lip of a hollow that is still only a delay. Calf-deep water the color of wet iron, larvae pulsing in translucent clouds, crustaceans turning silt in patient gyres that have not yet been given a river to become. I do not kneel. The cold would rewrite me too quickly. Instead I listen with the whole length of whatever I am becoming. The walkers pass in their loose constellations, hooves testing the newest joints, thatch holding its own small climate. They do not startle. I am another punctuation, another use of the emptiness they already treat as cover. Their breath-signs last just long enough to include me before the wind takes them. The clickers ride the faint thermal I make simply by existing—tall, still, mid-process—in a place that has no furniture. Their internal pebbles map me as another heat-road, another shred of weather.

Sometimes I wade. The standing water is drink and delay both. Mineral begins to crust the lower reaches of me the way it crusts the vent-terraces. I do not mind. The heat-scuttlers appear when a throat opens nearby; they treat the new glaze as fresh slag and pack it into the growing lip of whatever I am. I am not their shepherd. I am not anyone’s. I am the land speaking one more unfinished sentence while it lifts.

Days—if the bruise can be divided into days—pass in the lock of joints I have not finished deciding. I move only as far as the next rank of vents, the next seep, the next fault that has not chosen whether it is ridge or wound. The gait is the walkers’ gait: test, pause, commit. When the deep note comes—the single felt increment of rock persuaded upward—I treat it as weather. Uplift is just another change in the light.

Younger versions of me, if that is what they are, stand a little apart, even more provisional, lichen only beginning, outlines still arguing with the air. They will either become the next ridge or they will become whatever I am still becoming. Nothing here is old enough to have finished the argument.

I am not a building. There has never been a building. I am the cleared floor noticing itself. The spaces between one presence and the next are still being made, and I am one of those spaces given just enough height and patience to listen. When the table of rock finishes rising, or when it stops, I will still be here, or I will have become the next thing the mountain tries. The cold feels permanent only because the altitude has not yet been revised. I wait inside that revision the way the larvae wait inside the delay, the way the walkers wait inside elevation, the way the land itself waits—still being lifted, still mid-process, still the first sentence.
