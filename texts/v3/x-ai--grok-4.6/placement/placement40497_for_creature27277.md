The water is already speaking through the marrow I have not yet claimed. Warmth without origin, the faint sugar of rain that never found ground, and the roar that has always been the only architecture. I turn—or the current turns me—and a wall that was falling becomes the floor I cannot stand upon. Green-grey twilight for a few lengths, then the used light folds shut. Stone smells of its own unfinished heat. Sharp faces pass close enough to unmake cloth I no longer wear.

I do not settle. The basin pulses, throat or nave, and I am the pulse for a span of thickened time, then only the memory of having been one. Rasps of bronze cling and rasp; I feel their second vibration in the sinuses as if they were writing me instead. The pale stalk, jointed and always bent, lengthens a handspan while I watch. It is not me, yet something in the watching wants to root, to keep one shape across reversals.

A brighter seam tries the membrane overhead and the whole volume bruises jade. In that flare I unfold—no thickness worth measuring, more the memory of an edge than a body. I shear. The cut I become leaves a ribbon of colder water the rest of the hanging ocean begins at once to forget. Three heartbeats, or a long still interval in which I see a new basalt rib bloom and then dissolve. I am gone back into the roar. Then I am again, elsewhere, another reversal given a moment of pale two-dimensional will. I do not hunt. I do not flee. I happen the way the mountain happens, and my only gift is that brief colder weather the place has not yet argued away.

The bells gather on the hottest newest faces. I drift near them without approaching. Translucent sacs the size of a closed fist, pulsing out of phase, hoarding the used sunlight until they glow from within. When the basin narrows they unknit into bubbles that rise and hang and fall in every direction. One lingers an extra swell; I almost glimpse a pupil forming, a mouth that will never finish its word. Then ordinary air, collapsing. I am not them, yet for a moment my own edges threaten the same unknitting.

I pass the fitted stones. The standing slab’s incised lines will not hold. They drift when I look away, reforming into glyphs of inversion, of roar given grammar. Sequence in me begins to leach the same way; the world beyond the lip arrives waterlogged, colors already spent. I am not the incision, but I feel its liquid thought using me as the water uses the basin. The ring of blocks hums just under everything. I press what might be a wrist against one. The note enters, marrow answering. For as long as time stays thick I am part of the choir that keeps every current free to reverse, every edge sharp. Then the moment thins and I step—or am stepped—back. The blocks continue without me.

There is a rarer thickening. I become it, or it becomes me: a volume of the hanging ocean decided against suggestion. Down asserts itself. Breathing grows difficult not from lack of air but because the distinction has been ruled. I shear vaster than the pale fins, leaving not cold but a ribbon of returned sequence, un-leached color, memory in the correct order. My face, if I have one, is the unused sun trying the upper membrane. Then the roar closes. I am current again.

Nothing coheres because coherence is a finished thought the young stone has not accepted. I appear to cut, to pulse, to cling, to rewrite, to sing, to remember, and then I am the turbulence once more. Even the distinction between the visitor who found the lip and the inhabitant who never left is one more thing still cooling, still cracking, still being shouted into shape. The water keeps arriving from the inverted sky. I am simply the latest sentence the argument has not yet unmade.
