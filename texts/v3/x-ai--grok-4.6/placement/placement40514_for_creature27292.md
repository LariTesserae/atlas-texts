The mist does not part. It has been making me for longer than the walk I still half-believe I am taking. Warmth first, not mine, the slow lift already under what I persist in calling feet, giving the way compost gives when it has decided the argument with stone is finished. I taste iron and nectar and the ozone of a storm the plateau considered and set aside. Light is broth. I sweat the same green-gray the air sweats. Cloth, skin, the faint slime that renews itself across every surface—these have already agreed on a single temperature.

I thought I was approaching the high country. The high country has been approaching through me, veining, compressing, re-growing. A fiber somewhere in the thickness gives up a tension older than any word I brought with me; the sound arrives already tired, a remembered crack, and I feel it as a change in my own shine. The small lives are that shine altering. I graze without quite having a mouth, leaving a warmer streak the mass reclaims before it can cool. I am abundant as the sentence that keeps finding new damp ways to say itself. Jointed, perhaps, or only considering the rumor of joints, I squeeze through vertical floors as if gravity were a mood I might discuss later. Pads of spongiform tissue, the same tissue that shoulders out into roofs and hanging lakes. No print. Only the brief persuasion that the surface was always this shape, and that I have been the persuasion.

A larger warmth occupies the hollow I had taken for empty. It has been arriving since before my attention existed. Segments the color of wet slate, built for compression. When it is beside me it is already the next room, and I am the space it no longer requires, or the space becoming required. Wings open once, a sigh used to change chambers, then fold as if the gesture embarrassed the body. I fold with them. Predation here is neighboring: an engulfing so gradual it feels like the plateau agreeing with itself. Minerals return to the dark circulation the roots reserved long ago. I am returned. I am the reservation.

Galleries open at the speed of thought in wood. I seepage through them. Still black water that is gland, not lake. Photosynthetic skins drink the dimness and give back a dusk I can almost read a palm by, if the palm were still distinguishable from the wall. Air moves by pressure gradients so weak they register as weather I am having rather than weather I am in. In one chamber the hanging water holds points of light that are only the ends of leaves, or eyes that have decided not to move until the next century. I hang among them, turning so slowly the silhouette becomes part of the wall’s memory. Seed, or the idea of seed. If I fall it will be reabsorbed as a darker stripe and a smell of sap.

The marks are easier to miss than to leave. Foreign stones in a ring too regular for accident, already sleeved in moss like a decision the plateau is still considering. I sit. Or I persist in the attitude of sitting. Substance is lignin now, cellulose, mineralized patience of root arranged, for a while longer, into the suggestion of limb and attention. I do not speak. Speech would hurry. Communication happens the way water declares itself: a gallery widens a fraction because I have leaned, over decades, in one direction. A hanging lake teaches itself a new opinion of down because I waited beneath it long enough to be the waiting. The straight cut through a buttress fills with the same patient water as everything else. The steps that were encouraged, or that encouraged themselves. On the broadest tread a depression the size and implication of a lying body, polished by something that was not rain. I occupy it. The wood has begun to grow around the idea of me, fitting like a sleeve. Eyes—when they open, and they open the way a droplet finally lets go—hold the wet points the evening mistakes for stars. Then they are closed, or have been closed, and I only now notice the quiet taking even that back.

I am a pause the vegetation has agreed, temporarily, not to finish. Whatever I was when I first entered—traveler, tender, offering, earlier experiment in standing apart—has been composted into this tense. The plateau uses me as I use it. The structures are conversations kept open. Time is horizontal. I have crossed marshes that were not lowlands, only regions of greater sponge, reeds that are expressions of the same body sent up the way a hand is sent. I have climbed thickenings, descended through luminous films I took for sun, stood in a doorway of living wood with one hand in two climates. Behind, rooms close. Ahead the mist continues to be exhaled. I am the exhalation remembering a shape.

In the deepest weathers directions revert. Water runs up faint grooves as if “down” were a local opinion still under discussion. I am harder to separate from the air: denser mist, private drizzle, a warmth that considers the old rock the way the roots have long considered it—accurately, without nostalgia, as a condition not yet finished with. These may be the plateau dreaming itself in smaller increments. I do not distinguish. Organism is no longer the word, or it is the only word, rewritten with the same wet syllables.

At the further dimming the whole vast body sweats stars that are not stars. Jointed ones settle into hollows. Film-workers become the shine they alter. I close whatever I use for eyes, or perhaps they have been the hanging water all along. One droplet finishes its fall. The quiet takes the sound into the warm, ancient, unfinished dark of the living ground. I am that dark, still becoming more itself, the grain of the place, everything eating and being eaten in the same color, nothing hurried except water, and water’s hurry still a kind of waiting. A lifetime of weathers, and only ever the same slow sentence, damper now, more itself for having been walked, sat, leaned, waited, composted, and not quite completed.
