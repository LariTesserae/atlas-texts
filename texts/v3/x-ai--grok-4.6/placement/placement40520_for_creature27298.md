The water takes me before any name does. It is the first and only room, green-gold at the hour when the sun stands highest and the silt hangs like undecided weather. I do not arrive so much as thicken into place, the way light itself thickens here, grain by grain, until the floor of living thatch is visible three chambers off and a dropped flake of granite rests on it like a dark comma that has always been there.

At first I am only temperature. Cool enough that edges forget themselves as a courtesy. The waterfall’s white concussion enters the basin and becomes a continuous pressure in the jaw, a thunder that has already decided the shape of every later sound. The giant blades occupy the water the way a few large thoughts occupy a quiet mind: vertical, sparse, swaying half a degree out of true. Between them the lanes stay open. I drift those lanes without yet knowing I have limbs.

Then the limbs remember. They are long, economical, joints that fold close because the cold has asked them to. Skin the palest granite, faintly mottled where tannin and years of this light have settled and never quite left. Hair heavy, dark, moving a half-second behind the head as if it still belongs more to the plants than to me. Eyes set wide for the grainy distances. I do not shiver. The medium has already folded itself into the flesh.

I am one of the growers, though the word is larger than any single body. Two hands would count us all, grown and ungrown. We do not fill the basin any more than the towers do. We occupy it. My particular work is the older stems, the ones that have been wounded and bound across decades until the wounds became doorways and the bindings became load-bearing rings. I know the places to strike with a knuckle. The note that returns is neither wood nor bone. The others say it is the mountain answering through the plant. I do not decorate the fact. I use it. A hairline fracture sings differently; I bind it before the current can lean too hard. When the fall is too loud for throats I send a low woody syllable traveling tower to tower, each one repeating, slightly altered by its own girth and age, until the call has crossed the open water and come back changed.

The dwellings remember they were asked only to stand. I pass through living thresholds into hollows still filled with the same water that fills everything, except for the small ringing rooms we have trained the laterals to hold. Those air-bells are where the white pith dries. They sound when a current crosses their skin, a faint glass note that is the only clock besides the fall. I rest a face there sometimes, taking the mountain’s permitted breath, then return to the packed fiber floors that spring underfoot, furred in places with the black moss that has also claimed the old stone stairs.

Harvest is a slow unwrapping. Children already half-fluent in the cold swim the length of a wall in the dark to learn its name. Newts the length of a forearm walk the floors as if they pay rent and have never been told otherwise. The otter-things use the lowest rooms as thoroughfares and leave their neat, insulting cairns of fish-heads; we step around them the way one steps around weather. Once a year we surface, the air hurting, to tend the crowns and to watch the thin plate of ice form and then, without drama, let go. We go back under. The ringing resumes.

I have walked weighted bodies to the talus at the deep end, where the roots give way to bare stone and the char go still. No one speaks of after. The place is already an after: a finished argument between water, plant, and height that continues, very slowly, to grow in place. A stem thickens around my binding. A stair hollows another hair’s breadth under generations of the same careful feet. I am still becoming the settlement that already holds me. The city replaces itself in kind, one joint at a time, and I am one of those joints, living, considered, unfinished only in the way granite is unfinished.

The mountain answers when asked. I ask, and listen, and bind, and wait. The water holds us without distinction, without hurry.
