The warmth arrives as a single uninflected note, already complete, already occupying every interval the same way the mist occupies the middle distances. It does not settle on the skin; the skin is already the same temperature, already filmed. Breathing is only a slightly denser version of standing still. The light has no origin I can turn toward; it simply is, pale and even, so that the next ridge, the next cluster of slabs, the hanging curtain at the unfinished edge of the plateau all remain equally readable and equally distant.

I am not yet certain whether the wet stone under what I will later recognize as feet is floor or continuation. The film that never dries beads along what might be clavicles, hangs for the same measured instant the cliffs use, and is replaced. When the film quickens, an identical quickening answers from half a mile away, then another, then the silence that is not absence but the confirmation that every allotted rectangle remains occupied. The message contains nothing else. It does not need to.

Limbs declare themselves only when I consider moving: two for the rare placement of weight along the raised slabs, two for the even rarer gesture of lowering a palm to a moss cushion. The joints produce the same clean angles the lintels have never sagged from. I do not walk. The interval I occupy—one of the ceremonial gaps between basins, twenty paces of glistening granite from the nearest fern colony—is already the correct distance. To close it would be to choose lushness over emptiness, or emptiness over lushness, and the arrangement refused that choice before I condensed into it.

The face is only the simplest necessary planes. Eyes take in the regional view without selecting a distance to finish upon. Moisture runs from the scalp in the same silent curtains that sheet the almost-flat roofs. I have stood inside the stacked blocks, once or at long intervals that are not intervals, only to remind the unweathered edges that they and I belong to the same finished stacking. The tools lean in the corners, still sharp, still unused; they store the same nothing the store-houses store.

Nothing passes except the faint something that moves from moss to palm and is enough. I do not eat. I do not sleep. I do not wait for the plateau to decide it has finished arriving. Waiting would require a future that differed from this constant, and the constant has already admitted time only as itself. I watch the far escarpment where the weeping becomes a hanging curtain that never quite reaches the rumor of lowlands. The view extends, feature after repeated feature.

I am one of the attenuated ones, glazed, permanently wet, the color of the stone deepened by the film that never dries. I continue, open and quiet, exactly as far as the even light will let the eye travel.
