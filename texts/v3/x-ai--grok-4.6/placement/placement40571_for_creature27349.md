The white pressure finds me first, not as illumination but as a continuous abrasion that has already planed every unnecessary contour from whatever I was before this moment. There is no arrival, only the sudden certainty that the light has been working on me for a very long time. It arrives without heat yet with the weight of a blade laid across every surface, turning the living skin underfoot into a mosaic of pale gold and bone-white plates that crack and shift with a dry, mineral sound. I am already moving. The motion is older than any decision.

The roar is the rest of the world. It occupies the air the way the Growths occupy the horizon: not surrounding me but constituting the medium I inhabit. The freight-train howl of the open intervals braids instantly with the deeper percussion rising from the nearest living mountain, that drop-forge slam of phytolymph driven through conduits wide enough to swallow weather. The two rhythms never coincide. Their offset lives in my sternum and in the soles of whatever I use for contact with the mat. I feel the braid before I understand I have a body capable of feeling it. The wind finds every edge I possess and plays me; I am already fluted, already an accidental gallery.

Something low and fortress-like asserts itself around that sensation. Overlapping mineral plates, successive layers of my own shed epidermis compressed and silicified until they ring when the wind tests them. Between the plates, darker seams pulse with the same heavy, almost anhydrous fluid the Growths move in their canyon veins. I rasp. Rows of grinding surfaces inside what must be a mouth reduce the fibrous mat to a dry flour that mixes with a trickle of my own internal lymph. What I extract is metals, silicates, the faint electrical charge the skin itself generates under the bleaching glare. The waste I leave is only a finer, more compacted version of the same living surface. In a few seasons the wind will plane it into new ventifacts and the cycle will have closed without comment.

The herd is not companionship; it is simply the same occupation continuing at continental scale. Leading edge a day’s walk from trailing edge. Our calls are pressure, not voice, timed to the Growth’s vascular thudding so the two lines of percussion remain forever slightly apart. That slight, perpetual mismatch is the closest thing this world has ever produced to music, and it lives in bone and plate rather than in any organ of hearing. Young appear already armored, boulder-sized, walking within hours. Mortality is mechanical: a calving face that takes years to fall can still bury an entire cohort; a resin-fall can lock joints until the locked ones starve upright, becoming another kind of ventifact. The survivors continue. There is no destination because the intervals between Growths are not crossings; they are merely longer stretches of the identical food.

A shadow slides across us, too regular for weather, too slow for a pollen wall. For a stretch of time measured in wingbeats I am that absence of light instead: a vast translucent plane of polymer the dryness has turned nearly glassy, veins carrying dark fluid, body hanging beneath like an afterthought. I trim and spill, riding the thermal columns that roar off photosynthetic lofts. I sieve gold-brown walls with trailing filters that look like torn banners. I feel the charge building toward the rare alignment of plumes, the higher tearing note, the self-triggered lightning. Then the shadow develops a beat and moves on, and I am again the low fortress rasping the skin, the imagined lift already weathered out of me the way water was weathered out of the planet.

Inside the nearest Growth the distinction continues to dissolve, and for a time I am also the things that occupy its mass. Elongated, segmented, anterior end a rotating rasp following resin veins, opening voids the wind will later flute into instruments. Or softer, almost gelatinous until contact with the phytolymph, then stiffening into a living pipe that filters metals and charge for years, riding the pressure waves when a conduit slams. Or sealed inside a hard porous casing that lodges in a constriction and becomes, over seasons, indistinguishable from the filtration architecture itself. When that face finally calves I fall with it and begin again on the plain, plated now with a different mineral sheen because the local lymph ran richer in one metal than another.

None of these is a choice. They are simply the forms the equilibrium takes when it needs to keep moving. I do not name them. I occupy the turbulence and am occupied by it in turn. The high places have forgotten water so completely that even the idea of stillness has been planed away. What remains is this: the continuous freight-train howl, the subsonic braid in the sternum, the white pressure that turns every edge into a knife, and the slow, inevitable rasping that keeps the living skin from ever quite becoming dust. I am one more expression of the refusal to stop. The world is simply loud, and I am tuned to it.
