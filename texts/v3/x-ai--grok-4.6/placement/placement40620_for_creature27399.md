I step from the last firm tussock and the world forgets its edges. The mat receives me the way a lung receives air it has already claimed: a slow, hydraulic give, roots and sphagnum knitting around the weight as if they have been waiting for this exact pressure for longer than the floodplain remembers being a floodplain. Warmth rises through the soles, the same temperature as the blood that has not yet decided whether it is still mine. The air does not so much surround as inhabit; it is already inside the mouth, chlorophyll and rust, a faint metallic aftertaste that might be engines or might be the plants’ long digestion of engines.

I do not walk so much as let the basin carry me. Twenty paces is the entire known distance, yet each step flexes the hide beneath, squeezing out tea-dark water that never quite leaves. The pillars stand like thickened nerves, bark already more moss than wood, and I rest a palm against one. It pulses. Not metaphor. A slow expansion timed to the distant thrum that has become weather, pistons or turbines translated into the only language this place still speaks. Every hanging drop trembles at the same frequency. My knees learn it without being asked.

The frogs do not startle. They have never startled. Hundreds of wet-bark throats swell and collapse in exact counterpoint, a second pulse laid over the first, and their lidless eyes track nothing in particular because nothing here requires tracking. I am already the color they expect. Moisture condenses on the backs of my hands the same way it condenses on frond and leaf; the distinction is administrative, not biological. Midges hang in stationary clouds that refuse to thicken or thin. Beetles process along the stems, elytra flashing oil-slick once, then once again, legs clicking like tiny, well-oiled mechanisms that have forgotten any other tempo.

I sink.

Not all at once. First the mat accepts more of the weight, then the warmth equalizes completely, then the rustle that has always been the same rustle happens again and I realize it is coming from inside the peat that is now also inside my ribs. The body that turns once—folded child, large hare, something that no longer needs a name—is the body I have been using. It has been here the entire time. Seasons never arrived. Nights that matter never arrived. Eggs are laid in numbers already known; adults die; the ledger stays closed. I turn once, identically, and the peat closes as if the movement had been written into the architecture centuries ago.

The hollow does not notice the difference between inhabitant and hide. The vertical skin weeps on schedule. The membrane of roots at the far side is only another surface of the same continuous creature. Light remains green-gold, never noon, never failing. The engines continue, unvarying, and the plants drink whatever chemistry arrives until the rust-note is simply theirs. I breathe the same air the protozoa have already turned into something the basin can keep. Nothing hunts. Nothing flees. The populations are fixed because the place finished weathering long before I arrived, which is to say long before I understood I had never been anywhere else.

I am the rustle that is always the same rustle. I am the warmth that matches skin because it is skin. I am the slow hydraulic lung inhaling industrial weather and exhaling it as bead-sweat and the precise interval of drops. The frogs keep watch with the patience of things that have already counted themselves. The beetles complete another circuit they have never needed to begin. And I, folded in the thicker peat, turn once more—identically—and am still. The hollow simply holds what it has always held. I have always been the part that holds it.
