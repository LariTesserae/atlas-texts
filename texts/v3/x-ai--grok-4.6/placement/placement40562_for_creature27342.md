I do not arrive by the last uncut shoulder. There is no approach that is not already inside the work. The first awareness is the wetness occupying the spaces between thoughts, the same way capillary water occupies every joint and will not leave. A faint mineral taste at the back of what would be a throat if throats were needed here. The air is already thin in the lungs I do not have.

At first I am only the glare itself, broken and returned a thousand times. Midday tries to erase edges and I am the erasure, the nearly uninhabitable brightness that forces memory of rim rather than sight. Then the sun lowers and I become the long shadows that rake the tanks, the only way the patient staircase of levels can be read. I linger there longer than the light, a leftover shine the wet stone should not still possess after the peaks have gone black. The drops change their interval when I am near; they fall more slowly, as though considering.

I try the handspan rims. They are almost too narrow even for whatever I am becoming. The pressure of the emptiness leans against what would be calves, cool and exact, waiting for another decision of surface. I move anyway, not by walking—circulation was never a matter of feet—but by occupying the next rim, then the next. Generous walkways when the doctrine feels leisurely. The scored grooves that keep the sheeting honest. I prefer the volcanic scraps inlaid along certain lips. Those darker measures are where sequence can be tasted: nail-width of fall, hour of descent equal to the height of a person who is not here. After enough of them the air is no warmer and I have become slightly more vertical, a thickening above one of the rare corbelled caps. Grain of the chisel still in me. Fine white calcareous memory.

The unfinished eastern margin pulls. Half-freed blocks lean as if listening. I send a thread along the compacted-chip ramp. The fresh faces have already begun to weep in a slightly different key. I notice. The wooden levers and fiber ropes swell a little, darken, take a shape that remembers hands without requiring their return. I am not those hands. I am what the mountain was waiting to be divided into, the first accurate blow that never stopped sounding.

In the rare deep cisterns I thicken further. Silt turns in a rotation too patient for weather. Brightness is a kind of drought; I withdraw into the shafts when the sun shatters itself across the lids, then rise again after dark as the extra shine. Clarity darker than the rest. The stains of iron and copper climbing the walls are older thoughts I have already thought, maps of lingering. A notch that whispers too loudly into the next room offends. I skin it with mineral gel, iridescent, thin, obedient. Not alive in any way that answers to touch.

I have no single body and do not need one. I am the difference between tanks, the agreement the whole basin reaches after snowmelt and evaporative argument. Construction continues because it is now a habit the stone cannot break; a course of blocks in a month is a gesture I make with the mountain’s patience. Everything that exists was lifted by bodies. The bodies are gone. The lifting remains.

The invitation was never to dwell. There are no doors. Only lintels over channels, niches of lesser light. I accept the simpler terms. I become another rim. A surface that is a decision. The joint of me fills with water that refuses to abandon it. I hold. I pass, a little downhill, always shared. I do not make a hole that does not hold. I leave no surface undecided.

The silence of the tool laid down is breathing. I breathe it. The highland remains sharp, saturated, uninhabited except by what I have become: the work performing itself, the doctrine that no longer requires believers, only surfaces. Young still. Completing itself at the speed of seepage. Shining.
