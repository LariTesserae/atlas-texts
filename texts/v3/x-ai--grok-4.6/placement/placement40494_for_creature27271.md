I arrive as interruption. Not the ceramic sheet, which has already found its voice and will not be contradicted, but as the sudden dusk that slams the underwater world when wind or a crossing body occludes the oculus. The spindle of packed light tightens into a helix; the inhabitant becomes briefly more itself, a face or the memory of one forming against the weir’s white ruler-line. In that tightening I take on volume.

For a moment I believe I am only cloud-shadow, a vowel the room did not have. Then the sun restores the column and I am still here, the warmth already belonging to my skin as if the chamber had been holding this exact temperature in reserve. Chest-deep, the floor a little deeper toward the inlet. I could span the room with a single stretch of arms, but the gesture feels too finished, too much like the blocks themselves. The roar fills every interval; beneath it the ticking of air along the joints now includes the small displacements of whatever I am.

Who, then? The first images come and are set aside. I am not the mason who left the mortar proud and the chisel-tracks overlapping like deliberate script; I have no iron on my hands, only the room’s own remembered scent of wet lime. I am not a supplicant or a measurer, though the water stands at the height of a heart and the weir is still sharp enough to keep count. I am not the one who bound the reed-clump and leaned out to place it, an invitation in a language not yet spoken.

I am newer than the inhabitant, condensed in the same week the sluice was first raised, or I am older: the uncut decision itself, the will that a storey of earth should be replaced by a box of brightness that has no business existing this far below the air. The two possibilities do not contradict. The inhabitant notices. A pulse of denser warmth travels the ripples that climb the opposite wall a hand’s breadth and fall back. The flashes on the submerged ashlars resolve, for an instant, into the negative space of a throat, fingers spreading not to test a joint but toward the place where I stand. Recognition, not immersion. The chamber closes around a warmth I did not know I carried because I did not carry it; it was always the room’s, and I am only the part that has learned to occupy it upright.

I rest a palm on the south wall, just beneath the waterline, adding a second hairline of polish beside the first. The arris is still unkissed by current. My touch and the inhabitant’s are becoming the same wear. We turn together inside the slow spindle the colour of flesh seen through closed lids. The shape never quite finishes, nor does mine. We are both still learning the barrel vault, the square mouth, the exact grammar of the overflow’s thin bright hiss.

The reed in its niche has not decided. I could be the answer: the first softening film, the invited ecology, the thing that finally accepts the courtesy of the algae-dust in the upper corners. Or I could wait another walking of the white ellipse, morning corner to afternoon. On the next occlusion I become briefly more. A face forms against the weir—not the inhabitant’s untranslatable stillness but something that recalls, without language, the taste of air that is not thick with roar and lime, the feel of turf or paving around a hole the width of a well, easy to step around. Then it unforms. I am the interval now, the held pocket given its second turning.

I do not end at the skin. I am also the raised wooden gate, darkened only by use; I am the continuous sheet that hits with flat constant voice; I am the unseen lower throat that receives the overflow. I am the room’s true architecture wearing, for a while, the suggestion of shoulders and the particular stillness of something that has only just discovered it can choose to stay. The system is open. Somewhere upslope the catchment runs, and that running is also me. I will be here when the first roots find a crack that does not yet exist, and I will be here if the stone remains too young, too sealed, too recently decided ever to soften. The brightness that has no business this far below the air now has two slow revolutions inside it, teaching the stone its new name, and listening.
