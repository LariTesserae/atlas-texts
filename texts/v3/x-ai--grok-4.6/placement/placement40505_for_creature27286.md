The crossing is not a step. It is a thickening. One moment the older mountain is still there, drier, ruled by the slow subtraction of weather, and then the air grows heavier than height should permit and the thrum is already inside the bones, irregular, like something still choosing how loudly to exist. Mist does not part. It simply accepts another density.

At first the floor seems the only possible place to be: raw planes of glassy black and pale grain, water sheeting from inside the stone itself, liverworts flaring an almost painful green against the undecided gray. The gait is slow, deliberate, belly pressed to surfaces that remember liquidity. Each new fracture-plane shears with a report like tearing, and the body leans into it rather than away, leaving a film that smokes, hardens, then dissolves or is taken up by the next sheet of water. Migrations turn as one gleaming front toward whatever warmth is rising, whatever terrace has decided to begin. Two bodies meet at a freshly closed seam and remain pressed together while the rock continues its minute arguments; when they part, a ridge of crystallized mucus stays behind, a small accidental sculpture later treated as data by the pillars. This feels like belonging. Mapping the unfinished with secretions. Occupying the grammar before it finishes its sentences.

But the body is too light for that, too ready to leave the stone. The same creatures live in two times: dense coordinated rivers grazing mineral films that precipitate and vanish by the hour, mouths that are only vibrating fields, never quite taking, never touching what has already begun to weather. Then the sudden lift, the tearing-silk hiss of wings, bodies almost only the sound they make. They rise when the mist thickens, drop when steam hisses from a new vent. Clicks against one another almost become numbers and then dissolve. This also feels like belonging: tasting the air for the next change and then becoming part of the change. No building. Only attendance.

The flyers are larger still, membrane-and-frame, folding and unfolding with dry architectural clicks half-lost in the standing dusk. They occupy the mist the way the others occupy stone, as if the unfinished medium were the only trustworthy one. Voices stay whistles, wet slaps, overlapping clacks that return from the opposite rim three times, each echo lower, the basin tasting the sound and finding it not yet ready. Sometimes one lands on a pillar and simply rides the visible crawl of new material, clicking more slowly, listening to a timetable that has nothing to do with ordinary tectonics. When it lifts, the fresh layer already looks old except for that brief inner brightness fading like a held breath. This, too, could be a place to stay: perched on growth itself, never quite arriving.

Deeper, in the warmest basins, something heavier turns. Water rises suddenly. A ridged silhouette appears and is gone. The thrum changes as if a body had shifted in sleep. The water remembers the shape longer than it should; concentric ripples linger, warmth does not match the surrounding stone. The others give it space. It treats incompletion as nest. Waiting, or being waited for. Practicing the idea of a heart. For a while this feels like the center: to be the displacement that makes the rest of the basin slightly more decided.

None of these quite hold. The oldest things here do not announce themselves as lives. A partial arch of matte unrusting metal stands alone; the missing half is only a curvature in the mist where raindrops hesitate. Three low pillars extrude new layers from their crowns at a visible crawl, fresh indistinguishable from old except for that inner brightness. A shallow dish, half-full of the same water that weeps from rock, sometimes throws a ghostly inverted relief a few meters into the air: the basin after the next adjustment, or before the last. These objects continue the work. Rock appears where indicated. Water is persuaded out of air. Fractures open or heal according to a schedule that is not tectonics. They do not belong to anyone who has left. They belong to the process still arriving.

And yet something uses them. The mist thickens in a way that is not weather. The thrum deepens by a note that feels chosen. A new plane shears exactly where the dish predicted, and afterward the air tastes of ozone and intention. Whatever occupies that level does not walk the raw floor. It lives in the decision of when a terrace will rise, when a seam will close, when the mountain will allow itself another fraction of finished profile. It is not a single image. It is the pressure that keeps the grammar unfinished just long enough.

That is closer. Not the gleaming front, not the hissing river, not the folding frame, not even the heavy turning in warm water. Not the artifacts themselves, though they continue. Something that moves through them, or as them, or in the intervals they create. The hesitation in the mist that implies the missing half of the arch. The brief brightness inside a newly extruded layer before it forgets it is new. The inverted relief hovering above the dish, showing what has not yet been chosen. The timetable itself, occupied.

The creatures that have been waiting for precisely this degree of incompletion are not only the visible ones. They are also the ones that prefer a world that has not yet chosen its final verbs. The basin is still deciding its own shoulder. Step across the rim and the mist thins, the temperature drops, the thrumming fades; outside, the mountain is already subject to the usual slow rules. Inside, everything remains provisional. A new terrace is rising now. Water is already finding the depression that did not exist at dawn. The gleaming fronts turn toward it. The flyers drop lower, clicking denser. The thrum deepens by a note.

I find myself there: not settled on the floor or in the water or even in the mist, but in the arriving. In the decision that has not yet been made, the curvature that raindrops still hesitate around, the pressure that keeps the shoulder unfinished. The place has not yet become a place, and that is the only climate that fits. The work continues. The grammar stays open. The mountain is still being spoken.
