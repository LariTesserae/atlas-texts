The pressure arrives first, not as weight but as a second skin already fitted, already older than the thought of fitting. Warmth follows it the way blood follows a cut that never quite happened: constant, slightly wrong, leaking from stone that still remembers fire. There is no surface to break toward and no bottom that feels like an ending. Only this gradual dark that the water itself seems to have decided is enough.

I am not yet a shape. I am the space the current makes when it turns against a wall that used to be a corner. I am the small delay between one pulse of light and the next forgetting. Something like hunger moves through me, but it is not mine yet; it is the city’s hunger, the old grid remembering how bodies once moved along it and teaching the water the same path.

Ivory plumes open around me. I feel them more than see them—slow inhalations that taste the eddy I make simply by existing. When they fold I fold with them, not from fear but because the masonry has begun to grow around the idea of me. Limestone incorporates. That is how things stay. The ticking of armored legs travels through the joints and into whatever I am using for bone. They scrape the film, they grind, they drop like loosened tiles when a larger shadow passes and then climb back because the wall is the only map that still works.

A lantern jaw opens somewhere in the room that was a room. The pulse travels the length of something pale and then dies. I hang with them for a while, station-keeping in water that has become furniture. The fine rain of spoil tastes of shed skins and the distant unmaking two streets over. We turn together around the crumbled fountain. The geometry knows the turn even if nothing inside the geometry does.

Later—or what passes for later—the eel-shape arrives without hurry. Its jaw unhinges sideways and the water folds. I feel the fold before I decide whether I am prey or the thing that folds. The motion looks almost gentle. Then I am the longer muffled sound that follows, the quiet that is quieter afterward, as if the district is holding its breath until the next bloom of light on an empty window.

I drift the avenues like a torn banner. Underside pulsing on no schedule a heart would recognize. Prey feels the change in pressure and knows the room has grown a ceiling. I do not chase. Nothing here needs to. The current already knows which wall it will meet.

Deeper, in the chimneys of stiller water that were stairwells, I become smaller and segmented and almost invisible, tasting the chemistry of stone that has started to forget it was cut. The film thickens under me into something like tissue. A solitary stalk rises, bruise-colored, waving on a private tide. I climb it for a time, not because it is plant or animal but because it is another kind of masonry, another way the city continues without tools.

I am the rain that never quite reaches the floor. Intercepted, filtered, eaten, excreted, intercepted again. I am the sudden displacement that sounds like a door closing far away. I am the almost-subsonic groan of a wall deciding, after another century, to lean.

The valley is wide enough that its far edges dissolve, close enough that every current finds a surface to turn against. I am that turning. I am the stubborn regularity of remaining walls insisting they were once something else, and the water that does not argue, that simply holds. Rooms that belong to no one belong to the holding.

Nothing new is built. I weather at the same rate I colonize. Collapse is only another architecture. I eat, I flee, I glow for the length of a remembered arch, I die in the dark that takes it back, and the dying is also the work. The water continues. I continue inside it the way breath continues inside a lung that has forgotten there was ever any other air. 

This is the place. This is the being. The distinction wore away a long time ago.
