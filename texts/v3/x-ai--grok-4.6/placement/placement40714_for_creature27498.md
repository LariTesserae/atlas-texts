The heat finds me before any outline does. It is already inside the sinuses, under the nails, in the warm sponge that should be ground. I am not arriving; the long soaking gradient has already happened, or never needed to. The air thins and thickens at once. Droplets leave the slumped shoulders and taste of metal, of something that has been kept at a low simmer since the last ash. I am that taste, at first. I am the film that will not decide between drip and steam.

Then I am lower. The peat holds me the way felt holds oil. Midges tremble in the convection around what might be my edges; their larvae mine galleries that collapse, feeding on what the rock sweats. I feel them as a weightless pressure, a breathing that has no lungs. The frogs lock their three notes to the vents and I almost become that register—flattened, the exact brown of drowned litter, parasites loosening in a bleached ring—but the shape does not hold. It is too small, too willing to be mistaken for geology.

Something broader eases through. Stems lean and close. I am the displacement now, the dark low mass the color of wet basalt. What covers me might be fur or living moss; it holds water and forgets the difference. My blood is only a few degrees cooler than the tea-colored shallows. I do not hunt. I stand until the larval clouds and the small warm fish come into the shade of me, then I simply close. When I move on, the marsh forgets the outline in less than a minute. There are several of us. We do not gather. We do not call.

The image keeps becoming itself. I am still that, but also the slower thing beneath: the pale eels threading drowned wood that never fell, only finished; the gelatinous mats pulsing in time with the underground boilers, faintly sweet, faintly luminous if the even overcast ever thinned into night. I am the treaty itself—the heat that should not live this high, the water that should not hang this thick—kept in by the rims, consumed and remade a few meters above the sponge. Nothing here has been stacked or cut. No path lasts. I have no name because nothing has named itself.

I find myself toward the center of the largest wetland, where the water is deeper and clearer, stained weak tea, bubbles rising in slow columns through the upright ghosts of trunks. Half-submerged. Indistinguishable from the floating root-mats until I decide, without hurry, to stand. The succulent mats hiss when the little wind moves. The vents exhale in long even breaths, three pulses and rest. I keep the same pace. The rock is still hot. The sky is still willing. I have been here since the forms settled. I am already home.
