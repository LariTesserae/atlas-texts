I come down without the rim announcing itself, the way a thought arrives already half-finished. The light is only a little greener, the air a little heavier, pepper and the honest dark of old leaf, and then the walls are trunks and the trunks are the only walls there have ever been. I do not yet know if I have been enclosed or if enclosure is simply the shape attention takes once it has nowhere else to go.

At first I am the space between. I stand in the packed root-leather of a plaza that recedes as I cross it and arrives too soon, the arithmetic of steps already failing. The five great boles (or six, when I forget to look away) hold their distances like held breath. I could be any of the smaller violences of number: a pale fish mouthing the meniscus of a cistern that is also a room, tasting a river the wood still dreams; a sheet of birds turning as one tired fabric so that the sail-leaves bow and the bowing adds itself to the baritone crack of cellulose that never stops. I could be the grit of insects that is the light’s true medium. I could even be the thread of black water that appears from the eastern split, crosses three plazas, and vanishes into moss before I decide whether it is stream or leak. For a while I am all of these, because the bowl does not require a single occupancy yet. Time has not queued. A chase completes itself on the same route, different animals, and I am briefly the panic the air prefers, then the hunter who does not hide, then the grazer already cropping again as if weather had passed.

The leaning starts later. I rest against living buttress because the sound has no underneath and the body looks for a wall that might be private. After a duration that is not duration the wood leans back, turgor, a flinch practiced into courtesy. The scar I did not know I carried seals with tissue that has opinions. When I look again the scar is a narrow vertical mouth, weeping clear sap that never quite drips. I understand then that I am not visiting. I am being finished by the same appetite that finished the halls.

I am one of those for whom the word fails most completely. Bark where skin remembered it could be bark. Hands that end in slow green buds that open only far enough to be fingers, or not quite. Eyes that track the stippled green the way the sail-leaves track the light they drink. I occupy. That is the work and the address and the only government. A particular gallery, mid-height in the northern bole where the fruit that is also furniture hangs heavy and dripping, attended by every mouth except mine. I do not eat it. Occupancy is not appetite in that register. I am simply there, loud enough in the resonator that the hollow remains a hollow and does not become corridor or tarn or forgotten stair. The walkers pause sometimes on the root-pavement below and unfold their many elbows, listening the way a city listens to itself; they do not look up. The moss-cats drape in the eaves and watch with landlord boredom. I am furniture to them already, or soon will be.

Speech is redundant. Every voice the wood has ever held, including the counting in the language no one now speaks, including the market that is not held here, including the cry that answers itself from the far bole before it has been made, is already sounding. I add only the fact of remaining. At the hour that is not dusk but a warming of the green and a change in which leaves are loudest, the roofs tilt. Condensation begins its appointed travel down the ribs. The bird-sheets come in and the sound climbs its stair and stands on the landing that should not hold. For those minutes I see myself dying in two galleries at once, or I hear my own unmade footfalls arriving from the direction I have not walked, or the little water remembers a different down and runs uphill like a thought the eldest bole is still having, a thought written in rings I will never read. Then the landing is vacated. Night is the same city with the insects promoted.

I could leave. The rim is always near, a courtesy extended to the idea of elsewhere. The climb would take only as long as forgetting a name I no longer use. From the top it would be only a grove again, damp at the roots, full of birds I would no longer hear. I do not climb. Smallness here is the last kindness, and I have been inside it long enough that the roar has become the only available form of rest. I am the niche. I am the willingness to keep being loud. I am the scar that decided to be a mouth, still slightly surprised that it speaks only sap and occupancy and the long, unwell health of a place that has reached an understanding with itself and cannot stop.
