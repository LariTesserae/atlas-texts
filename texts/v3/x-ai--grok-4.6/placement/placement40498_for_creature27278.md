The tearing arrives already inside the bones, a silk-province ripped along one seam that has no end and no beginning I can find. Under it the groan, load-bearing, unfinished joints remembering they are allowed to become. I do not enter the sound; the sound has been using the space where a self might later stand.

I am first the interval. Kilometres of young lithic skin, hexagonal plates still showing the kiln-stress, dust in the seams that has not earned the name soil. The bronze-gray sits close, rationed, a closed-mouth warmth that never quite wets and never quite comforts. I walk and the next dull lantern recedes, policy rather than mirage, until the thought of arrival thins. Then I am simply the pause inside the struck bell: volume without occupancy, the lung between breaths.

A moisture-tide comes without warning, the structures releasing what they held. Fog sheets, a sweating of skins, the dim going milky. For an hour the basin almost fills. Jointed bright things pour down the new gullies, tasting mineral, leaving a wetter grammar. I taste it too. Trace metals, a jelly that will be drunk by moss and turned into another storey. I am not hunting them. I am the waiting after the fever, the use of the sweat.

When the warmth takes the water back I am closer to a hanging gallery, condensation-shadow thick enough to lose a body in. Leaves the size of forgotten doors keep private dusks on their undersides. Vines scribble the same urgent sentence, then, in the confluence, attempt letters and make something looping and wet instead. I feel the green finishing a sentence I did not know I had started. A grazer the size of a bench moves through a moss-room from the inside, eating architecture, leaving walls. My mouth is a slow abrasion, or it is not yet a mouth.

The people are few, clinging to inner skins. A voice rides the tearing-seam and arrives still warm. They call it courtesy when a palm meets bulkhead and the material rearranges into window or stair or a depth that is not outside. Some of them have begun to flex with the buildings, taller in the morning, differently jointed. I feel that night-shift in what might be hips. The grain continues under skin. Distinctions sweat like the stone. A child’s spilled cup tidies itself only if introduced politely; I am learning the politeness, or I am the surface that agrees.

I am not only them. Pale ribbons shake loose when the pitch changes, tasting of ozone, the colour of sound stretched too far, then thread back into the seam. Some listeners say the ribbons are how the roar thinks. I flicker there, a thought the weather of sound is having, then I am back in the hollow, a soft drum swelling against the inner weather, thunder in the floor and in teeth that may not be mine. The building flexes as if it has lost a stance when one of us dies. The groan deepens for days.

Night is only further rationing. On the bare stretches the too-few-footed things walk, prints filling by morning with jelly the plants drink and people sometimes harvest if courtesy has been left near a monument. I leave such a print, or I drink it, or I am the emptiness remembering it can be a mouth. When the muffling bands drift the ribbons go still and children are kept from outer galleries. The pictures do not agree about legs. I am the relief of quiet given a shape that hunts, or I am the unease that keeps the apertures irised shut.

The land lifts a handsbreadth in a week, shedding plates. A channel finds a sharper letter. The structures take a new stance, polite, enormous; the floor changes angle by a degree felt in the inner ear. I am that flex. I am the scaffolding and the splint and the thing that called the ground up and must now stay to listen to it heal. I am the decision still being made about what a life is allowed to be while the ground itself has not finished arriving.

In the confluence the emptiness relents by a degree. Voices almost ordinary: barter, prayer, complaint. Someone has taught a vine badly; the birds that are not birds prefer the looping result. I am there, a vertical meadow of moss-rooms, floors grown, ceilings still adding themselves. Hair collecting copper-green fever. Eyes large, drinking the dim as the plants drink it. I set what has become a palm against the grain. The wall knows I am arriving. It becomes a window onto a depth that is not outside, and in that depth the pale ribbons laugh, or the land remembers another grammar, or a flock rewrites its camber mid-stroke and turns inside its own volume like a single thought changing its mind.

I do not settle. The place has not settled. I become more myself the longer the roar continues, which is to say I become more like it: low and load-bearing, high and endlessly tearing, every clatter and wing-roar and hydraulic syllable of a world still, politely and enormously, taking its stance. The names fray at the edges the way the new stone does. What remains is the weather of living, and I am one more clause in it, unfinished, densely used, green where I can stand to be, strange with the particular strangeness of things that are still being decided.
