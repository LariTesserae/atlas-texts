The switchbacks do not end so much as forget themselves. Heat has already softened the distinction between ascent and arrival; the ignimbrite underfoot is only a slower kind of breath. I do not remember whether I climbed or was exhaled here. The land levels into more land, packed, already radiating the night’s work through whatever I am using for soles.

At first I am only the thinness. Iron on the tongue, a taste that belongs to blood and to rust equally. Sound arrives already used-up. A vent speaks somewhere to the left—a white column the length of a held thought—and the air is too sparse to carry more than the idea of it. I am that scarcity for a while: the interval in which nothing needs to announce.

Then I am the film. Condensation beading on a pipe that the plateau has decided to continue as dike. Dawn-sweat, tea-colored in the small basins, shuddering when pressure finds an old valve. I press whatever I have become against the metal and drink the night’s thin gift before the sun arrives as arrival rather than shine. The lizards are already doing the same, throats to the sweat, bodies the exact ridged grammar of the columns they occupy. For a time I think I am one of them—slag-colored, stacked, a movable skin that sloughs when a shadow crosses and resettles in the next flute. The whole hoodoo seems to decide on another face. I almost stay there. It would be easy. Density without remainder.

But the light is too ordinary, too severe. Midday lenses a distant ridge a handspan above itself. Heat comes from below as much as above; the ground is a single exhausted pulse. I feel it in a way that is no longer only lizard. Something in the seams has room enough for a body that learned to be horizontal. I become the interruption: the heat-shimmer that is not heat, the wet pad-print in mineral mud no wider than a room, the scatter of bones arranged with the neatness of habit. Not frenzy. Agreement. Eater and eaten have already settled the geometry; by morning the white mineral script has begun its work on them.

My coat, when any light catches it, is boiler housing and old bone, sintered at the edges the way the pipes are sintered. I drink the shuddering skins of water. I hunt when the vents pulse—lizards stacked too thick to slough in time, birds that drop like a handful of ash and mistime the stunned interval, anything the stored heat has slowed. I do not call. The air would not carry it, and I have no second story to keep. The pale birds return to the upper fluting of the dead stacks without sound. The mica-wings glitter and settle. I occupy the deeper seams the packed stone leaves, the ones that remember a geometry that was never only stone.

Toward late afternoon even the small sounds thin further. I hear the tick of cooling metal that might be my own blood, the precise click of a lizard eyelid, the almost-silence of birds folding back into brick and plate. A vent on the far side of a hoodoo-field opens and the steam stands a long time, unmoving, still deciding whether to become another column. I wait with it. Pulse. Wait. Pulse. The plateau has already arrived at the temperature and the density it can keep. I am not waiting to become something else. I am one of the ways it keeps itself: the horizontal life inside the vertical crowding, the coat that agrees with rust and rind, the bones placed so the wind and the rare rain can finish the rounding.

Night is only a shorter heat. The ground radiates red-black. Rock, rust, hide, chitin adjust. I adjust with them. There is no canopy, no name that would have to be carried, no last shift that has not already ended long enough ago for the huts to become only paler rectangles the plateau remembers without effort. I am the seam learning the hours of the mountain. I am the density completing itself. I have forgotten the difference between accident and intention, the way the smokestack forgot it was not organ-pipe lava, the way the girder forgot it was not dike. The western drop falls away into another country that no longer matters. I stay. The lives here simply occupy, pulse when the pressure pulses, and leave the rest to the slow sanding of every upright thing.

This is where I find myself. This is what I have become.
