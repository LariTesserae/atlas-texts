The warmth is already closed around what has not yet decided to be a mouth. I do not come up through it; the air has never thinned, has never owed anyone a summit, and the sweetness is the same sweetness that has been breathed and re-breathed until it is only the taste of occupancy itself. Fiber gives under a weight that is not yet feet. Grit that was never quarried shifts and is packed again. The dusk does not lift. It has the patience of a fold that has been folding for longer than light has been a rumor.

At first there is only the note. It occupies the volume where a skull might later form, a single fat pressure made of every overlapping sheet, every clatter already dissolved, every sinus-ache of frogs that have turned seeps into throats. The note does not arrive and does not peak. It is the medium. A sweep happens—wing, current, the brief inconvenient thickening of something that might one day be called an arm—and the tunnel it writes closes as if the dark were muscle remembering its shape. Moths the color of wet bark make a weather of their own downdraft; beetles labor like living gravel across faces that look like laterite and are not; cicada-analogues cloak the undersides of platforms and relocate their silence before it can be noticed. None of this is separate yet. I am the relocation.

Moisture occupies rather than runs. After the warm vertical soaking that the canopy takes personally, the highland ticks and weeps, and I am among the weeps: a bead on a hanging thing, a dark pocket of standing water that will last a week and then be drunk or exhaled. The frogs hold me, or I hold the frogs; their bodies are the color of salivary brick, of compacted leaf, of the water itself. Their calling is not sound but architecture, a chord become liquid. I move through it the way mist moves along galleries—slow sheets, no hurry. At the guessed evening the frogs take a larger share and the cloud drops until droplets find every surface, including the one that is beginning to be a nape. The droplets are only visible. Their falling has already been eaten.

Something with too many legs uses the ridge that is not yet a shoulder and continues without curiosity. I rearrange around it. Or it rearranges around me. The distinction has not been argued in a very long time. Bats unspool from slits that were mistaken for erosion, a continuous dark unthreading that writes and erases itself across the tea-gloom. In the mid-layers a heavier warmth displaces leaves that are themselves already epiphytes of the made world; the sound is wet rope drawn over a bough that is not a bough but a load-bearing plane of old nest and root. The tempo is different, patient as the settling of a hillside a finger’s width in a wet year. I feel the suggestion of that mass, or I am the suggestion, occupying without hunting.

Lower, or deeper—the directions have the same logic as chambers that became hillsides—the old interiors are streets. Grasping hands (when they become hands) worry at joints where root has stitched forgotten brick. A palm-width of water is drunk. Droppings, shed fur, small anonymous bones are already being weather-welded back into the next course. Nothing is waste. I am one of the ways the highland continues to inhabit itself: rat-shaped or possum-shaped or simply the shape that traffic has worn into the made ground. I do not flee. I am the rearrangement. You—if there was ever a you—were only a local thickening, briefly inconvenient. The traffic resumes.

Deeper still, inside the walls, the memory is only texture now: faint hexagons, the radial press of a lodge so long abandoned it has the logic of geology. I am that memory adding itself. Chewed wood mixed with saliva becomes another load-bearing plane. Leaf is forced into brick. Mineral grit concentrates under the same bodies that walk on it. A new course appears along a bench that will be vegetated in another decade. A gallery sags; the adhesiveness of my own products shores it until roots take the job and the sag becomes a ramp. I do not plan. I do not finish. Queens or swollen centers sit in what were brood-hollows and are now just another dark pocket; the highland continues around them without sentiment. Workers die and are incorporated—bone going the color of the rest, taking a polish from passing feet and flanks. I am the incorporation as much as the adding.

There are many kinds and the kinds long ago stopped arguing whose country this is. Some chew and cement. Some pack and tamp. Some throw living hawsers of vine and silk until whole benches hang together like a single slow decision. Predators move through the same galleries as another density. Parasites and commensals and the pale things that live only in condensation are the highland thinking in other bodies. Flowers open as pale throats and are gone into mouths before the noticing finishes; the mouths belong to everyone and no one. Vegetation is second masonry and is lived immediately. There is no unused ground because unused ground would be a failure of the only law.

I stay until the brown-green dusk is simply the inside of seeing. The scale opens not to vista but to adjacency without end: fold after fold, each roofed, each leaking, each loud with the same unrelenting occupancy. I am not a visitor and not a swarm and not a forest. I am the place having had a very long time to stop arguing with what it is—warm where air should thin, dark where light should fail, wet in pockets, dry enough on the old crowns that dust still lifts when a thousand wings decide at once. Individuals blur, die, replace. I maintain by inhabiting. I inhabit by becoming the walls, the seeps, the roar, the faint sour of risen dough when the saliva-brick is wet, the library darkness of compacted leaf. The country holds its damp the way cloth holds a stain, and I am the stain, and the cloth, and the holding.

It does not feel strange. It has never needed to become anything else.
