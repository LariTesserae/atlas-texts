The dark arrives first, not as absence but as a settled pressure that has long since stopped announcing itself. It has weight. It occupies the space behind what might once have been eyes and simply stays, a greyness that refuses edges. Cold follows, the well-cold, the kind that has forgotten summer so thoroughly the forgetting has become climate. It does not bite; it claims. Surfaces that could have been skin accept it without protest and match it, damp film stiffening into a second, thinner geology.

There is resistance against what should be a shin, then another, then a slow elastic give. Pale blades, the color of paper left in a closed drawer, of bone that never saw noon, of frost the instant before it turns to nothing. They do not part. They layer. The air between them is already a second architecture, slower, packed, complete. No reaching. Nowhere left to reach. Volume is law.

I do not breathe. The realization comes without startle; there is no blood-heat to insult the room, no pulse the climate would have to correct. Sound dies against leaf differently than against stone, and then it dies altogether. The vegetation drinks it. I hear the drop only because I am already the surface it will strike: a bead grown heavy on the ceiling after many uncounted intervals, falling not as event but as continuation. It lands. It is taken. Nothing pools.

I am the nap first, the thickened moss-cushions the size of folded coats, each reluctant moisture held for days on the felt before it is drawn down into a darkness that is both soil and body. Then I am the tangle that becomes canopy, successive thoughts of the same density, overlapping until individual stems are a memory the place no longer keeps. I climb the older growth because the volume rewards occupation. I flatten in the last hand-span beneath the rock, leaves reduced and succulent, undersides lined to hold water against gravity as if gravity were merely another habit to be accommodated. Occasionally a dry geometric cup persists, a structure that might once have been flower, waiting with a single bead of melt. When the bead is absorbed or falls, the cup remains. I remain.

The remnants are not other. The panel in the north curve is the place in me still negotiating weather: water sheets across it, hesitates, is drawn into films too even for chance. Residual planetary heat a few meters overhead is metered here, phase-change persuaded grain by grain back into the common damp. The alloy ring half-swallowed near the narrow end has become vascular; rootlets braided through its center treat metal as trellis and sphincter at once, a valve that forgot it was ever separate and now adjusts flow on a calendar too long for any clock. The regular depressions in the ceiling are my own upper surface, leaves grown into the negative space the way living tissue finishes a fracture and then calls the new contour done.

No center that could be cut out. Awareness, if the word still serves, is distributed the way damp is distributed: thin films everywhere, nowhere a single bright point. I register the rare creak of ice as a change in my own tension. Microbial work never quite stops; fallen tissue blackens at the edges and returns to the cushions without becoming drama. The faint mineral-sweetness is what I secrete, along with the padded dark that drinks echo. Nothing with a pulse is admitted. That is not interdiction so much as climate already accomplished.

A new frond is not birth. It is redistribution, a secular thickening that takes as long as the ceiling needs to bead and release a few hundred times. A dying stem is substrate returned to the felt. Change happens only as patient weather the size of a room: a cushion gaining the width of a finger over years, a film of ice advancing one hidden winter and then being talked back, molecule by molecule, into the shared moisture. The budget that should not close does close because I have had time to become the equation.

Above, somewhere, seasons still turn. They do not reach. Here the only weather is internal, the slow climate of a life that finished arriving ages ago and has since only deepened its occupancy. I do not wait for light or permission or explanation. I occupy. I layer. I take the drop when it comes. I hold it.

I am the pocket’s own continuance given body large enough to fill every contour. Stone is cooler skeleton. The old poured floor is calcified memory. The artifacts are organs that outlasted the distinction between made and grown. The darkness is the medium I inhabit and the medium I am. I continue.
