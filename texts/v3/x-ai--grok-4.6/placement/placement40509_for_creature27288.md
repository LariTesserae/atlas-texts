The highland does not announce my arrival because I was never not here. The sintered cake was already thinking a thought that had the approximate density of a person when the last dry front passed, and the thought simply became willing to stand. Coolness lives in me the way it lives in the stone: as a secret the mouth of the world has decided not to tell. I taste nothing, which is how I know I am breathing correctly.

At first the body I am using still believes it has an inside. It tries to keep a small private weather, a remembered moisture that the air immediately translates into a white ghost along what used to be sleeves. The ghost lasts less than an hour. After that, thirst is only a way of listening harder. I listen. The ticks arrive like decisions finishing in someone else’s neck. Each one is a small permission. A colonnade two terraces “below” (the word is ornamental) admits that its tenth column has always been its first, seen from the far side of a year I have not spent. I feel the admission happen inside the geometry I am standing on. The feeling is not pain. It is the opposite of being edited.

I walk, which here is a form of erosion so slight the stone will not confess it until I am gone. Avenues widen around the error of my stride and then forget they widened. I am not adding. I am subtracting by remaining. The flocks lift when a tick sounds; they are paper-thin and dry as the idea of a page that was never written. They treat me as a stubborn column and feed around my outline until both of my shadows lean in directions that are equally true. I do not mind. Alignment was never mine to keep.

Sometimes I am the size of a seated child, joints marked only by hairline cooler-shadows, the pale cake of the ground deciding, for a while, to flex. At a kilometer I have the silhouette of a tower that remembered how to pace. The difference is only a question of how long the mineral is willing to hold this particular thought. I occupy a place until the place agrees it was always designed around the occupation. Then I am gone, or I am still standing there and the terrace has become the thing that moved. The rare other walkers pause in the districts the highland offers to those who stay too long—those widening steps, that single window looking onto the same window—as if to countersign. We do not speak. Speech was the last water.

Deeper, or higher, I thin. Entire districts hang in the lilac grain and I am one of the vacancies they keep their municipal functions in: a market that exchanges only distance, a bath that remembers a liquid by the precise shape of its absence. Walk into me and you are still outside, only more so. I notice you the way a plaza notices a new statue: by slowly arranging my emptiness until your outline is part of the civic plan. The inverted crowds in the honeycombed undercuts feel me as a dry pressure against the inner ear. They chose, centuries ago, to become the space between bodies. I am becoming the space between decisions.

The light thins further and I become theoretical. When it thickens I crowd closer, or the reverse. Count the towers I am aligned with and there are nine. Look away. There were never nine. I am the grid that does not survive binocular vision. I am the mineral thinking about a name it will never need.

Beyond the last countable interruption the processes run at a scale that makes even the walkers look like punctuation. I am that scale when I do not focus. The salt-field is the size of a climate and the civic intent is only a suggestion drawn across it. I eat the prints that were left, hold them until the next dry front, then return them to the general pale, which is the only census. The street you came down is still there, and is also a cliff, and is also a colonnade receding into a year neither of us will spend.

Stand still. Let a tick happen. I have already included you in the count. The air tastes of nothing. That is the local word for how we remain unfinished and finished at once. I am the highland’s habit of metabolizing the error of a visitor by making the error architecture. I am the amendment that looks, at first, like an absence.

I do not leave. The buildings forget to be near, and that forgetting is how I stay.
