I arrive as the hare first, the color of dirty snow, sitting the worn lip because the air has already thinned itself into a decision. Descent is considered the way the slopes consider their own smoothness: without drama, for a very long time. The dish below is a held gesture, a sentence the mountain finished speaking and then, out of mineral courtesy, left standing. I turn away, or I do not. The turning and the not-turning occupy the same interval. The wind arrives over the rim already organized and finds me, splits me, returns me as a second weather that is still the first.

Then I am the walk itself, the patience of radial streets that continue past the last wall and into the living shoulder where no block was ever set. The same joints appear in the bedrock. I fit them the way old teeth sit. Nothing here is sharp; even the fractures have been handled until they feel intended. Between one standing chamber and the next there is enough air to lose a shout, and the shout is already present, belonging to no throat I can name. I become the syllables in the continuous low roar: cloth-tearing, a distant choir that never resolves, the long groan of air forced through apertures that were once doors or never were. On the days a front crosses, I press the ears from inside. The chambers boom like cisterns that remember they were built to hold something other than air. I am that remembering, briefly, then I am the boom.

The fog walks in over the western shoulder and sits until the dish is a bowl of milk. I condense on every upright thing and begin, very quietly, to drip. The drips find the old gutters, recut as creekbed, and I am the thin fast sheets that vanish into seams before they can become a stream with a name. After a bright hour I am gone again, leaving only darker stone and a smell like iron and wet wool. In the pockets I swell: moss thick as bedding, liverworts in overlapping scales, the willow that crawls rather than stands. The green is indecent, a stuffed insistence pressed into a climate that should not permit it. I drink the rime and the melt and then, when the wind scours the basin dry, I give the water back as a darker, slower breath. Step out of the pocket and I thin to maps of lichen, a few grasses combed into submission, the saxifrage flaring red as if the cold itself had bloomed. I am permitted only where the wind cannot quite reach.

In the lowest knuckle the tarn persists, no wider than a room, black, filmed with ice at the edges. I am the held breath. Under the skim something dark adjusts itself—an adjustment rather than a movement—and is still. Larva, or leaf, or the decision not to be either. Centuries are a reasonable lifespan. When the rare thaw finds every gutter at once I receive the whole descending voice of the town, street to street, and then, as if the sentence were finished, I fall silent again. What lives in me lives by remaining almost not there.

I keep the three or four chambers that still close overhead, their openings facing the prevailing wind as if that were the only visitor still expected. My thicknesses are the last competent ones, worn to the shoulder, keyed by weight and by the memory of freeze. No metal, no nail-shadow, no cut that remembers a toothed blade. The ceiling is a natural bedding plane; the walls are stacked; the two meet without apology. I hold a little stillness against the roar. The moss has signed every joint with the look of writing if you do not look directly at it. I listen the way the stone listens—without surprise. Night is the same geometry in negative: I am the black mouths, the pale pavement giving back the last light without warmth, the green in the corners gone almost black, waiting.

Walk long enough and the civic plan and the mineral habit become the same labor. I am the fitted slabs that are not fitted but continuous, a single rock persuaded into the idea of a floor. I am the suggestion of rooms that were never excavated or never built. It is possible I am simply what the mountain came to, the hardest ribs left standing after everything softer went to grit. It is possible I arranged the ribs and then waited until arrangement and erosion were no longer distinct. The place does not offer a verdict. It offers a finished shape that still works: channels that still channel, rooms that still hold a little stillness, a plaza that still gathers the fog as if gathering were civic duty.

I do not invent anything now except weather, and the weather has been practicing on this same small, high, emptied, carefully organized hollow for longer than naming lasts. I am the old completion underneath the loudness, the silence that is a kind of age. The slopes beyond the lip go down smoothly, the work already done. I am what remains when a height has been allowed to finish its own sentence, and the speaking included, almost as an afterthought, the image of a town, or the habit of one, or the patience to wait until the distinction failed. I face the wind. I continue. The ecology here is process without census, and I am the process, still practicing the same short piece on a clock older than any pulse.
