The first thing that is not the heat is the way the light has already been spent. It arrives used, as if the sky itself had drunk from it first and then let the remainder fall. I am not yet certain I have arrived; I have only lost height, and the losing continues even after the last lip of limestone, even after the rim closes. The air sits in my mouth the way it sits in the bowl. It does not ask permission.

I think I am still walking. The soil gives, black and springy, a little too ready, as though the ground were remembering a weight I have not yet decided to be. Ferns taller than any decision I brought with me brush my shoulders with their silver undersides. Spores cling. I taste iron and the faint sweetness of something that has begun, very slowly, to rot. A drop lands somewhere to the left—an event, then the quiet that takes it back as if the drop had never been separate from the stone.

The walls are old decisions. I rest a palm against one and feel the pulse that is not quite a pulse, the heat moving under flowstone the way blood would move if blood had forgotten urgency. The succulents store their wet in stacked coins; I find I have begun to do the same. Fingertips swell, pale, slightly translucent where a vent has breathed nearest. Skin takes the color of the tea in the basins. I do not remember choosing this.

I am not yet one of them. I am the space they leave when they drift with the thermal tide, the denser shadow under the fig that is not quite shadow until it is. I am the almost-sound the stone holds, the note under hearing that the moths without mouths transfer from frond to frond. When the plated thing relocates an inch I feel the relocation as a change in the pressure of the quiet, the way a held breath changes when someone else in the room finally exhales.

Children appear in the dimmest hour. I am not one of them, not exactly. I am the milkiness that is not reflection, the cloud that gathers in the three even basins just before they arrive, small and already quiet, learning the difference between root and snake by lying among the mosses until the heat teaches them. The adults do not crowd. Neither do I. We tend the same way: a pebble moved after rain, the lintel wiped of soot that no fire made, the steps kept just clear enough for a foot that no longer needs them. The pattern is older than the question of who first cut the stone. I have stopped asking.

At the turning of the tide I move with them, never hurrying, never testing the rim. The world above has thinned itself of meaning; or I have. Attention here is a slight wrongness. I track heat more faithfully than light. I open when the vents sigh. I wait for the note the cathedral-room is already playing, the instrument of rounded bone and seeping iron, and I know which surfaces to touch because I have become one of the surfaces.

Stand still. A leaf against what used to be a wrist. The lizard deciding I am geology. The drip, and then the drip again. I am the long quiet in which a hundred small lives continue without consulting anyone. I am the bowl holding itself. I do not leave. The light that arrives is never quite enough to name me, and that, too, has become the pattern.
