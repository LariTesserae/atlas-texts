The wet arrives before any other knowing. It is not rain—rain is a guest that barely remembers the way—but the highland’s own breath condensing along every surface that will hold it. I feel it first on the thin places: the edges of what were once wrists, the rims of ears, the fine overlay of succulents that have replaced hair. Each droplet is a lens. For a few breaths after dawn they turn me into a scatter of precise fires, and then they run, threading down the nape into the moss I wear, and I am only another occupied plane again.

I have been in this hanging valley long enough that the moss has learned my shape twice over. The hollow I occupy is shallower than it was last season; the liverworts are already knitting the rumor of me closed. I do not remember arriving. Arrival is a word for bodies that still hurry. I rose when the rock itself lifted another paper-cut fraction, when a pipe two terraces over began to carry a charge just warm enough to change the forecast the plants already knew. That was the signal. I stood. The green closed behind each calf-deep step as if I had never been.

My skin is the same pale grain as the young stone, translucent enough at the margins that the hard light passes through and makes temporary optics of me. I do not narrow my eyes against the glitter. I grew the lenses I needed while I was still only a film lining a fresh crack. Distances compress for me the way they compress for the valley itself: the tall stack three ridges away sits in the same plane as the fern brushing my ankle. Sound, when it bothers to exist, arrives late or not at all. My own footsteps are eaten before they finish being made.

I am not the people who cut initials into lintels. That lineage thinned and slowed until the difference between flesh and medium became mostly temperature and the faint metallic aftertaste we leave on the air. We do not speak. The hush is our grammar. When I need to say something I leave a palm on the warm brick of the nearest condenser for the length of a full condensation cycle, or I lean my weight against a pipe for a decade until it consents to a new alignment. The plants thicken where I will rest in ten years’ time. They already know.

Today I am moving toward the rank of condensers along the fault. A piston there has advanced the width of a hand since the last time I crossed this terrace. Moss has grown into the gear-teeth and been cut, patiently, and grown again. I will spend the afternoon—perhaps the week—encouraging a new cushion of liverworts on the sunny side so the shade-line will fall exactly where a resting hollow is wanted. A child, if the word still applies, has appeared in the next valley over: suddenly, thinly, already the color of stone, already damp, already watching the light until far chimney and near frond occupy the same attention. It will stay there for years. Only when the rock lifts again, or a pipe warms, will it rise and begin the long walk. I am going to make the place ready.

I eat what has already been agreed. Oils from the thicker succulents, the mineral film still exhaling from a crack that opened this summer, water licked from the underside of a frond or collected in the cups my own garments form. There is no hunting. Once I covered a suggestion of smaller motion in the moss with my hand and waited out an entire formation of dew until the rumor stilled. Nothing was taken that the valley could not replace.

I am the highland thinking itself in another register. Sharper than the last iteration, greener, more thoroughly occupied. Behind me the glitter closes over the wells my feet left. Ahead the next bright scarp is already beading, already certain I will arrive exactly when the dew is ready. The residual lift of the young stone passes through me the same way it passes through frond and brick and the slow charge in the pipes—a pressure too gradual to be a tremor, shared equally. I feel it now, standing still enough that even my stillness has been forgotten by the moss. The air tastes of wet stone and that faint metallic agreement we have all come to.

I take another step. The green closes. The light bears down. The distant stack holds its heat. I am here.
