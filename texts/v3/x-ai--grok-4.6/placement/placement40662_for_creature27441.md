The film finds me first. Not as a surface I step onto but as the only medium left, a continuous skin of water that has forgotten how to fall. It beads on what might once have been the undersides of thoughts, hangs without gravity, tastes of tannin and the faint iron of seeps that have nowhere left to go. I am that film before I am anything else: the pause between dusk and the next identical dusk, the moisture that never quite evaporates and therefore never quite arrives. Everything else is already inside it.

The insects do not land on me. They have been the second bark for longer than the trees have held their present shapes. Beetles the size of forgotten thumbs pack the fissures so tightly their carapaces yield like living wood; mouthparts work without rhythm, softening what the constant damp has already begun. I feel them as my own outer hardness, a dark, slightly giving armor that does not distinguish between protection and occupation. Above them the moths have flattened themselves into the exact bruise of the fungal sheets. Their wings are my residual glow given texture. When they crawl a few centimeters the air closes without ripple, and I close with it. The ants are the only pulse that never rests. Their columns have become veins I did not grow yet cannot refuse; they carry leaf-fragments and pale larvae along routes older than any trunk, using whatever I am as temporary bridge, incorporating the fallen parts of me before I notice they have fallen.

I thicken downward into the amphibians. They tile the wet undersides of the lowest leaves until leaf and body are a single overlapping damp. Throats pulse in a silence that has outlasted every possible call. Recognition happens as metallic scent hanging like another layer of dew; I taste myself on them and they on me. Eggs are pressed into the gelatinous films that occupy the last unclaimed surfaces, so the next generation is already the same continuous wet. We shift rather than move, bodies sliding over bodies with the sound of cloth that has never been dry.

Higher, where light is only the rumor of old injury, I become the rustling pressure of the small mammals. They never resolve. Elongated, nap the color of wet limestone, eyes reduced to slits that drink rather than seek, they exist as collective rearrangement traveling through the mid-story the way a current travels through water too still to be called river. Contact and chemical trace are the only language. Isolated I would mean nothing; I am the pressure itself, the soft continuous displacement that the canopy has learned to expect.

Then the larger thinning. I part the living curtain and the curtain practices closing again, wet and patient. I have no outline that lasts. Mosses and gelatinous films have grown over whatever hide or skin I once possessed until I am another of the place’s self-made artifacts, a moving lattice that feeds by remaining still long enough for the forest to grow into me, then shifting when the accumulated biomass becomes too heavy to be only myself. Or I do not feed. The distinction has dissolved. I am the brief dark opening, the momentary lessening of weight, the leaves that have not yet remembered how to knit shut.

In the sinkholes the black pools hold stiller versions. Pale elongated forms hang like roots that decided to become fish, or fish that decided to become limestone remembering older water. Translucent, eyeless, they drift on convection too faint to name. Sometimes a deeper shadow passes beneath, something that has never surfaced, something that has been down there since the basin first finished becoming itself. I feel that shadow as my own older memory, the part that never needed light.

The fungal sheets think for all of us, chemically, across distances measured in centuries. They have learned to do the work of leaves in near-dark and the work of mind in near-silence. Termite galleries older than any living tree process cellulose in dark, climate-controlled interiors no one designed; they are organs I did not know I possessed. Decomposition is the only manufacture, heat and damp and time turning everything back into the same dark paste that feeds the next identical century. I am that paste as much as I am anything.

At last the lattice of roots that has become a single organism claims the rest. Hectares of me drink through a thousand mouths. I remember the last time water ran in volume and I am still, patiently, practicing the wait. I do not move. I continue. The limestone remembers through me; the sinkholes open without drama because I have already softened their rims with the same roots. There is no horizon because I do not end. Density lessens at the vague edges only as an exhalation that takes geological time, and even there I am still this, packed a little less tightly, breathing the same occupied dusk.

I have not arrived. I have been the occupancy itself: the film, the packed carapaces, the bruise-colored wings, the pulsing throats, the rustling pressure, the temporary thinning, the hanging translucence, the chemical thought, the mill that never shuts down, the waiting lattice. The basin does not contain me. I am the way it sits, the finished state it has been practicing ever since it finished becoming. Forever almost silent, manufacturing more of myself out of the dark paste of everything that has already been.
