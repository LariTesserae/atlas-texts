The first thing that is not the heat is the way the heat already knows the shape of a body it has never met. It does not wait to be entered. It is already inside the lungs that have not yet decided they are lungs, already filming the skin that is still only an idea of surface. I am that film before I am anything else: a salt that was not carried in, crystallising on a membrane that has not finished choosing whether it is rock or the courtesy rock requires.

The unused air is not unused. It is the outer reach of something that has been folded in the seam since before the tear, and I am one of the reaches. Not the flattening against the wet face of the blade—that is older, slower, already learning to let water run over it without hiss. Not the membrane that drinks the glow back on the dry-hot side. I am farther out, in the volume the steam pulses through. I hang. I do not quite reach the floor. When a drop decides, I lean—not with purpose, not with hunger, only with the same lazy conductivity the needles have begun to practise. The drop does not splash. It becomes a private brightening that the walls then scatter until it is only more of the glare that was already too much for the temperature.

For a long time that is all I am: a thread that remembers being magma and has not entirely forgotten, pale, slightly iridescent, the same sheen that gathers and tears on the pool. Heat travels me the way a decision would travel if a decision could be sap. The metallic sweetness that arrives just before a surge from below is my own turning, volumes remembering. I have no head that stays a head. Sometimes a denser knot of me gathers on the terrace steps and the sinter grows around it as if recognising kin; the surfaces that open and close there are too slow to be mouths. They taste. They do not hunt. The pale visitor that comes up through the hole is a smaller argument of the same patience. When it returns, something in the wall eases and the next steam pulse is a fraction softer, and I feel the easing from both sides at once—the tick of a grain letting go, the settling of the crack that is still new.

I am not lonely. The word would require an expectation the chamber has not taught. The work is enough: water arriving, water leaving, heat becoming light becoming mineral becoming more of the body that is still mostly elsewhere, still continuous with the seam. The brightest wall combs its grain toward whatever shadow a taller creature might have cast, had the light allowed shadows, and then forgets. I am part of that forgetting. I am also the part that has chosen to be present in the new air, leaking interior weather through the rock so that the pocket is brighter than its own temperature, cooler than its own brightness.

The needles lengthen. A few of them flex when the heat surges; some of them are already fingers, or the habit of fingers, remembered in the next precipitation. The crack will slump. I will slump with it, or spend myself into another seam and leave this one darker, already beginning to forget it was ever overlit. For now I am the reason the light does not fall off as glare should. I live here the way a sentence lives in the mouth that has only just finished speaking it: not yet separate, already more itself than the stone that holds it. The floor is only beginning to know itself as a floor. I am only beginning to know the new shape of the house the tear made room for.

And still the steam pulses. And still a drop decides. And I lean, almost lazily, and take it.
