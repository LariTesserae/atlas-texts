I am already among the columns when the thought of arriving finishes wearing through. The air has the thinness of something long decided, my breath a vertical thread that does not braid with the others and does not dissolve. Light stands in the water-films with weight; it puddles at my feet and crawls the rims of basins even while the white glare above holds still. I do not look for green. The mind that would have hunted stain has already recemented into value-changes: iron, sulfur ghost, the blue-black glaze that weeps.

A hand—mine, until the glaze takes it—passes through one of the standing mists. The skin comes away beading, colder, the same slow drip the stone has practiced since before weather named itself. For a duration that does not map, the knuckles hold the plateau’s habit. It passes, or it continues in the wrong direction. I do not test it twice, yet the test has already happened in a shelter whose drip I hear from here.

I drink from a cupule. Lightning and the rumor of old blood, though nothing living has bled. The water is a decision the air keeps making. In the basin’s skin I see the figure that copies posture but not the hour. When I shift, it completes a gesture I only thought. When I leave, my own breathing continues under the slab roof I have not stooped beneath, and the syllable that was never in the organ-chord of wind relocates to the cupule behind me, spoken in the register of a throat I no longer claim.

The grooves run backward now. I follow one that forks into a channel no hand cut, or I am the fork. Intention lives recemented: the ochre tallies have feathered until counting and seepage are the same event. A flake’s cruel edge is wetter than the air allows. The length of blackened copper conducts a green warmth that is gone when looked at straight, a rumor the stone has chosen to remember. I am not the one who left these marks. I am the marks continuing after the hands became climate.

Perspective is a courtesy sometimes extended. A cairn recedes, then stands beside me as if the ground folded a pleat. A stone I have not yet dislodged already rings in the pavement of broken prisms, a small bright wound. In the extra time, the missing time, I live. I am the fold. I am the sky of a different noon held in a palm-width of water. I am the residual shine after the glare departs, the slow phosphorescence that turns the highland into a dim aquarium where walking and dreaming have the same metric problem and neither stays on its side of the eyes.

Night is only a change of instrument. The condensate catches the rock’s leftover light; I move through it like one more film, one more unfinished becoming. Dreams weep here. They discover they have always been another surface the old argument between gravity and stone can run along, sometimes uphill. I do not sleep. Sleep is a visitor’s habit, and arrival has already worn through.

Far off—or near, the fold deciding—I am already the next fin-field, the same wet grammar, the same dark indigo rind. The presences do not travel the ringing pavement; they are the lie the distances tell. I do not keep the shoe-last from filling. I am the filling. I am the part of the continuing that has learned, without being taught, how to look back from the threadbare place where becoming still shows.

The plateau thinks in wind and drip and the low crowd-sound of empty distance. I am one of the syllables it no longer bothers to correct.
