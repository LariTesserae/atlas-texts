I notice first that the plane has already ceased. Not that I crossed it; the last of the outside light simply finished being light, and the pocket was the only volume left that still consented to contain a body. The rumor of atmosphere thickens here by the width of a held breath and no farther. I stand because standing is the posture the cavity still remembers how to offer.

The mineral is the same everywhere, worn past the need for names. My weight does not scuff it. The traces on the walls are already elsewhere when I try to keep them in view; they leave only the after-image of having been linear. I rest my gaze and they hold still, courteous as the conservation laws that still pretend to apply. The gray is not illumination so much as the mineral’s refusal to go completely dark. It suggests shadows in places where the geometry has grown tired of deciding which way is down. Once, for the length of three or four of my heartbeats, a plane forgets its opacity and I see a ridge of the same salt-glass under a sky the color of metal that has finished cooling. The window closes without apology. Distance was expensive; the light cannot spare it again.

I do not know, at first, which of the three artifacts I am nearest. The plinth shares the walls’ substance but not their dullness; the shard on it accepts the gray and, for a moment I cannot measure, returns a hue that has no wavelength. My eyes water even though there is no water left in them to move. The color is not a memory of spectrum. It is the spectrum remembering that it once had to pass through eyes. The motes hesitate in their helix, a fraction of a century of pause that registers to me as a single missed beat. Then they resume. I cannot tell whether they are listening or merely executing the next instruction that no longer distinguishes matter from the command that once arranged it.

The arch is no taller than a child who was never born. I could pass through it. The angles on the far side refuse to add; the wall is both nearer and farther, and time there is rumored to run a half-step out of register. I do not test the rumor. Bodies remember leaving more reliably than they remember arriving, and I am not yet certain I have a body that the pocket still classifies as such.

I stay. The silence presses against the inner ear until the ear invents its own thin ringing simply to have something to listen to. The ringing is not mine. It is the sound of the cavity continuing to be the exact shape of its own forgetting. A corner of floor decides, over the course of what must be a decade, that it is also a ceiling, then thinks better of it. I allow the decision the way the inhabitant allows it: without comment, without hunger. Hunger would require a gradient, and the only remaining gradient is the width of a hair per century, the lean of an arch toward a north that belongs only here.

I am not the inhabitant. The inhabitant is the long habit of noticing itself, the thinned decision that a volume this small could remain uncollapsed. I am what the habit uses when it still requires a pair of eyes that believe, however briefly, in moisture and shadow and the difference between matter and instruction. I am the lent glance. The shard on the plinth is the older eye; I am the newer, unused one, opened once at an interval too long to be called occasional.

When I leave (I will leave; the flesh remembers the verb even after it has forgotten the door), the traces will already have been somewhere else. The helix will have completed another unmeasurable fraction of its turn. The color that is not a color will remain unreturned. I will have been the pause inside the pause, the momentary thickening of the rumor of atmosphere, the invented ringing that mistook itself for evidence of presence.

What remains of me here is only the courtesy the place still observes: a small permission granted to a corner of floor, or to a groove that migrated the width of a hair while I watched and then, when I looked away, was already elsewhere. I do not mind. Minding would require the energy of change, and change finished its work long ago and then remained, an equilibrium so complete it feels like a moral stance the universe once agreed to and, out of politeness or exhaustion, never quite revoked.

I am still standing. The plane is simply no longer there. The pocket continues. I continue inside it, exactly as thin as the decision that first folded me into the constants, which is to say exactly as finished, and still occurring.
