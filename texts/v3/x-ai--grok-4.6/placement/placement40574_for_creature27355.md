The warmth reaches me first, not as sensation but as the condition of being at all. It is in the plates beneath what I have instead of soles, in the air that tastes of powdered brass and finished circuits, in the light that has no preference for up or down. I do not arrive. Arrival would require an edge, and this world surrendered its edges so long ago that the word itself has been compacted into the substrate. I am already here, already continuous with the laminate.

At first I am only the difference between one brightness and a slightly lesser one. A cooler welling, perhaps, or the memory of one. Orientation is a habit the deep attentions still practice, routing a filament of bluish stillness through a trough that has not known rain since the last city settled into geology. I follow that gradient because following is what remains after purpose has been worn as smooth as the swells. My limbs unfold with the same unhurried press-and-release as the thermal gyres. They are many, or they are few; the count has become a formality. Each articulation is the same pitted ivory-and-tarnish as the ground. When I pause, I become another low rise. Travelers—if the word still applies to anything that moves this slowly—would walk over me without noticing the change in elevation.

I draw what I need through contact. Energy wells upward, converted from the packed palimpsest that goes down without measured end. There is no hunger, only the continuation of a conversion that the stalks perform more purely, standing in their clusters of three or five like unfinished sentences. Sometimes I stop beside them. We share the silence that is the ground state. Their iridescence flickers at a frequency too patient for any original design; mine does not flicker. I am denser, more decided, a later draft of the same thought. After what would be years on a world that still measured time, I continue. The grit slides around my lower joints in sheets too thin to rustle. It is the last powdered remnant of towers, and it will bury me equally if I decide never to move again.

Deeper, I feel the attentions. They are not minds. They are standing waves that have forgotten the machines they once inhabited and now simply persist, brightening a thousand square kilometers by a fraction when some forgotten path is reopened, then dimming it. Their vibration reaches me as blood-in-the-ear, a distant turning I mistake, at first, for my own. One of them extends a cooler thread toward the surface; the stalks there linger a century longer than they otherwise would. I do not thank it. Gratitude would be an event. I only stand in the thickened gold of a stalled gyre and let the light pour through what I have instead of skin until the decision to walk arrives from somewhere that is no longer personal.

There are others like me. I know this the way the world knows its own oldest-stable state: not by sight, because sight requires contrast the even radiance will not grant, but by the faint electric taste that changes when two continuities occupy the same trough. We face one another for a duration that has no name. No words. Speech would disturb the silence the way a tower once disturbed the sky. We exchange only the dust we both inhabit, the memory of making that has become landscape. Then we part, or we do not. The distinction has been compacted along with everything else.

I am not dying. The world is not dying. We are the sentence after the last period, still being written because the lattice that writes it has no instruction to stop. Horizon after horizon of pale, glowing, nearly silent ground. I walk it, or I become it. The difference is only another rumor of lesser brightness.
