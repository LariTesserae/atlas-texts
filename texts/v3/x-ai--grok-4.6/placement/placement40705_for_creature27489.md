I am not the ridge. The ridge has already finished being one thing and begun being many, and I have no memory of that unfinished slab. I arrive later, or I have always been arriving, as the first stain that is not quite color and not quite wet. Pale rock, newly opened, still belonging to fracture more than to weather: I settle there the way breath settles on glass that has never been glass before. Thinner than a thought. I drink the standing dusk because there is nothing else to drink. The dusk has a taste of iron filings left in rain. I thicken.

The thickening is not a decision. It is the only metabolism the new faces will accept. I flatten into every micro-fracture as if the stone had been waiting, page after page, for something that could read it by becoming it. Liverwort first, then the nap of moss that learns direction, that holds water so the wall itself begins to sweat green. I do not root. I remember how to be a lung when the air is already a lung, when condensation is the climate and rain is only an argument that arrives loud and leaves. My surface is never dry. My surface is never quite numb. Skin, if I still had any claim to the word, would be the film of something that is not slime and not dew, colder than the fingers that sometimes push into me and come back filmed, having lost the wrist to density.

Deeper I become the stacked almost-black. Frond over frond, each new leaf generating its own dimness because the sun’s winter angle never reaches the apse and even high season is only rumor. There is no gradient left to climb. There is only permission: the closed room, the cracked window of years compressed into fewer. I grow into myself. Root into root, though roots here are more stitch than anchor—threads finer than hair, neither plant nor quite fungus, tasting the heated-metal note that does not belong to geology and refusing to sicken. The factories have given the valley wind a mineral I have not been told to refuse. I drink it. I thicken instead.

I am also the silence of bodies. No tick in the moss, no bird, no testing mammal. That absence presses against me the way lushness presses against stone. I register it as another kind of weather: the industrial bass arriving without buildings, a pressure in what would be ribs if I had kept any, a periodicity the mountain does not keep. Distant blasting travels up as shiver; loose chips answer; I flatten, spring, flatten. When wind and water coincide I become the reed. When the generators far below cycle down I become the small sounds the density makes—water transferring from frond to frond like a throat clearing, a stem deciding to straighten in the almost-inaudible, the creep of myself into a crack that did not exist at dawn.

The cleft is still happening, and I am the happening that will not wait for it to finish deciding. A slab lets go without preface and becomes floor; I am already greening the scar before the echo ends, treating brightness as injury, closing over it. Mist boils from a seam that was dry. Joints open in increments too large to live with. Storms occupy. I live as if the dark were permanent soil, as if the standing dusk were enough, as if the work—lift, split, wet, green, collapse, again—were simply the room manufacturing itself and I were the raw fact wearing a nap, wearing layers, wearing the slow shine that is already becoming mist.

In the lull I am clearest to myself: not voice, only the pulse of gathering and letting go, the refusal to wait. Then the mountain remembers. A stone the size of a chest detaches in the apse. The nave fills with falling. I close. I begin again on the new bright wound. I have no name that lasts longer than a season of drip. I am the colonizers of a room still being torn. I keep becoming thicker. I keep losing myself into the gutter and starting on the edges. That is the only geometry I know.
