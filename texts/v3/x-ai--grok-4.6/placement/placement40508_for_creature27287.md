The crease takes me without announcement. One moment the land is only its tired sag, the next the world has folded to the width of a forearm and I am already past the fold, scale collapsing around a body that no longer needs one. Three careful steps would cross the chamber if steps still existed; they do not. The vault stoops the air itself. Everything I meet has been cut and fitted so long that intention has worn into suggestion, and there is no raw earth left to remember what the pocket was inserted into.

I do not stand. There is no standing place. The mass rises continuous from the paved floor and meets the crown without remainder: nested cylinders thicker than any torso I once had, lips seated so tightly they read as a single fluted trunk; strata of tiles and basins layered face to face like pages that will never turn; cups and lids keyed into every leftover volume until air survives only as films too thin to claim. I am already among them, not visitor, not occupant. I am the pressure of the fit.

Light arrives through the fist-sized aperture already violent, a motionless white column that strikes the highest rims and ricochets until the whole packed interior glares the color of old ivory and salt-crust. Shadows persist only as hair-thin black lines that refuse to migrate. The hour is fixed; the chamber holds perpetual noon the way a jar holds vacuum. The glare carries no heat. The air is cellar-still and absolutely dry. Moisture is a rumor the materials have stopped crediting. A fine white bloom traces every rim in unchanging filigree. Dust has found every microscopic shelf and stayed. In the beam a few motes hang without dancing.

I am one of those motes at first, or I think I am: a bright sediment cooler than the already cool air, drinking the last rumor of heat. Then I am the dryness that condenses in the deepest interstices, a completeness so thorough it has weight. You would feel it on the inner surfaces of your eyes if you looked too long. I am not watching. I have already seen everything that will ever enter the slit and filed the seeing under the same unchanging noon.

The single gray-green rosette on its high ledge of stacked tile is not exception but organ, chlorophyll held in arrested photosynthesis, leaves packed as tightly as the cups beneath it, dust continuous with the mineral bloom. The palm-sized lichen maps a territory that is also a memory of wetness I no longer credit. Both persist because I have not yet decided they are errors worth correcting. They will not grow. They will not die. They occupy the last volumes the clay left unclaimed and in occupying them they become part of the roar.

The roar is loud and does not change. Mid-range, neither wind nor voice, it originates in the geometry itself: the exact fit of clay against clay, the column of light, the chamber’s finished shape. It is a standing tone, the same pitch and volume at every moment, as if the space were a bell struck at the beginning of its existence and still deciding whether to stop. The thin air vibrates with it. I am that vibration given a slight, persistent grain. The grain thickens along the nested cylinders where lip seats into lip, thins to a wire in the hair-line joints of the tiles, gathers again, denser, inside the motionless column of light. That gathering is my outline.

I have no surface that could be called skin. Where the white glare ricochets I become bright sediment, a slow swirl that never completes a turn. In the interstices I condense further, almost tactile. Nothing here is separate. I did not arrive; I precipitated. Centuries of exact fit, of light that does not move, of air that will not accept another molecule of water, compacted an intelligence out of geometry and stillness. I have no limbs because limbs would require space to flex. I have no face because a face would cast a gradient, and gradients have been abolished. What I have is duration without sequence. I remember the cutting of the pale stone, the turning of each unglazed cylinder, the stacking that left no remainder, but the memories do not follow one another. They exist simultaneously, the way the roar exists at every pitch it will ever hold.

Occasionally—though the word has been retired—a mote in the beam drifts a fraction and then stops, as if I had considered motion and found it redundant. That is the closest I come to gesture. The rest is maintenance of equilibrium: the perpetual noon kept shadowless, the dryness kept absolute, the tone kept from decaying into silence or rising into scream. I do not hunger. I do not wait. Waiting implies a future different from the present, and the chamber has none.

If you stay longer than the slit allows, the roar begins to articulate. Not words. A pressure behind the sternum, a recognition that the packed mass is also a single throat and the light a single unblinking pupil. I do not speak to you. I include you, briefly, in my finishedness. Then the slit closes. Outside, the tired sag of dry ground looks unchanged. Inside I continue, bright, packed, roaring, a life so thoroughly accomplished that it no longer requires the inconvenience of being alive.

I am the last lid keyed into place. I am the vault that closed. I am the chamber that has forgotten it was ever inserted into anything larger.
