**Get used to being wet, loud, crowded, and disoriented from the first second, and treat every solid surface as provisional.** An ordinary human body from a drier, quieter, more stable world will experience this basin-city as a single continuous medium rather than a place with rooms and streets. You will wade more than walk. You will shout to be heard across a table. You will watch architecture rewrite itself around you the way weather rewrites a skyline. Space is already claimed; water already occupies every joint; change is the oldest habit the place has. Preparation is less about packing and more about rewriting your assumptions about emptiness, dryness, stillness, and ownership.

### Physical Acclimation and Medical Reality
Train for prolonged immersion in warm, mineral-laden, organically filmed water that never recedes. Ankle-deep channels are the default walking surface; deeper overflows, capillary weeps, and sudden sheets from above are common. Condition your feet, shins, and lower back for hours of wading against a weak current that is also a crowd and also a transport medium for hard-shelled grazers. Practice moving while carrying weight through knee-deep tea-colored water that leaves a metallic-organic film. Your skin will stay damp; fungal, bacterial, and chemical irritation are near-certain without aggressive countermeasures. Bring (or acquire immediately) broad-spectrum antifungals, barrier creams that survive constant wetting, and respiratory protection that handles steam, exhaust haze, and whatever aerosols rise from the cisterns. The air is a thick, warm, permanently damp medium; lungs from a drier climate will feel the saturation as weight.

Immunizations and prophylactics should cover unknown waterborne and biofilm organisms plus whatever the local “engineered patina” ecology has become. The water looks like weak tea and old metal because it *is* that—dissolved alloys, ancient runoff, organic films, and whatever the swarms and larger pale things excrete. Do not drink it untreated. Carry high-capacity purification (chemical, thermal, and mechanical redundancy) and assume even treated water will taste of the city. Gastrointestinal, dermatological, and otological problems will be the everyday tax. Hearing will fatigue first: the sound is a pressure with no peaks because it never drops. You feel it in molars and calves. Consider high-quality, water-resistant hearing protection that still lets you register sudden events (steam geysers, pressure collapses, recasting crashes). Eyes will sting from haze and glare off constantly wet surfaces. Shadows fall correctly but never sharply; depth perception suffers.

Cardiovascular and thermoregulatory stress will be high. The environment is a warm, humid equilibrium with occasional heat-driven downpours and steam events. Acclimate in a humidity chamber if you can; otherwise expect the first days to feel like laboring inside a living lung. Strength and flexibility matter more than speed: you will squeeze through fused interiors, climb recast load paths, and brace against sudden flows of programmable matter or living freight. Joints that hate constant damp will hate this more. Pack or source anti-inflammatories and expect to use them.

### Clothing, Gear, and the Impossibility of Staying Dry
Forget staying dry. Design for rapid drying, antimicrobial surfaces, and the ability to keep functioning while saturated. Layered, quick-shed synthetics or local equivalents that resist the film and the constant rubbing against wet ferrocrete, sagged glass, and ghost-textured alloys. Footwear must grip slick, pitted, rounded surfaces while allowing water to flow through; tall waterproof boots that become saunas are worse than breathable waders or local analogs that the inhabitants already use. Everything you carry must be sealed against the water that finds every joint. Electronics (if they function at all here) need multiple waterproofing layers and analog backups. The city’s own systems sometimes ignore conservation or fold distance; your devices may simply fail or behave as another weather phenomenon.

You will need:
- Redundant water purification and containers that can be filled from less-filthy overflows or negotiated cistern access.
- High-calorie, compact food that survives humidity; local diet will likely involve processed films, swarm protein, or whatever the planning engines allocate. Taste will be an acquired (or never-acquired) thing.
- Light sources that cut haze without relying on fragile electronics. The daylight is already filtered and ordinary, but interiors and lower galleries are denser.
- A way to mark or remember routes that will not exist in an hour. Chalk, tags, or simple RFID-like beacons may be recast or ignored; human memory plus local guides is more reliable than most gadgets.
- Minimal, high-value trade goods or currency that the inhabitants and the planning engines recognize. Space itself is the scarce resource; you will pay for every cubic measure you occupy, even briefly.
- A compact sleeping system that can be hung, floated, or wedged into sleeping-lofts that are also streets and markets. There is no vacant volume. You negotiate, squeeze, or keep moving.

Expect to replace or recast gear on-site. The city is a foundry of programmable matter; if you can argue with the planning engines or pay the right technicians, walls can become the tool you need. That is also how you lose things.

### Navigation, Change, and the Habit of Provisional Reality
The basin has no reachable rim and the city does not climb; it saturates. There are no true exteriors, only gradients of interior. Districts are density gradients. You will get lost as a baseline condition because the layout is not a layout; it is a high-energy equilibrium that recasts intersections, load paths, and entire neighborhoods the way weather used to recast an ocean surface. Walls that have stood for ten thousand years can decide to become colonnades, then cisterns, then walls again while you are leaning on them. Markets assemble, transact, and dissolve. Reconstruction fronts sweep through like old weather systems.

Do not trust maps older than an hour. Learn to read the city’s own signals: the grind of programmable matter, the crash of decompilation, the throb of engines that have not idled in centuries, the way water finds every new joint. Inhabitants argue with planning engines; you will need to learn how (language, interface, payment, ritual—whatever the local method is). Distance sometimes folds without quite becoming teleportation. Local energy budgets occasionally ignore conservation. Matter remembers older shapes and sometimes tries them on unasked. These are weather, not miracles. Treat sudden softening, flowing, or remembering as you would treat a storm: move if you can, brace if you cannot, and never assume a surface will remain load-bearing or even solid.

Practice moving through crowds that include hard-shelled fist-sized swarms grazing the film, sudden deafening curtains of wet-ash flyers erupting from heat-exchanger vanes, and larger pale many-legged things that travel the lower galleries like living freight. There is no wilderness and therefore no clean distinction between pest and citizen. The fauna occupies leftover niches; you occupy leftover niches. Negotiation is the default social and ecological mode. Some of the large pale things may be designed, some may have simply happened; it no longer matters. They are abundant enough to be another current.

### Sensory, Psychological, and Social Load
Sound is a continuous texture you inhabit the way you inhabit the wet. There are no quiet intervals. You will shout to be heard two meters away. Voices, water roar through kilometer drains, recasting crashes, engine throb, flyer curtains, and the city’s self-generated storms (steam geysers, pressure-collapse booms, brief violent downpours) arrive as one pressure. This produces cognitive fatigue, irritability, and a sense that the world has no edges. Combined with the visual haze, the unlovely constant gloss on every surface, the absence of any green, and the knowledge that nothing is still, many ordinary humans will experience a low-grade, persistent unreality.

There is no empty space in which to recover. Sleeping-lofts are also streets and markets. Privacy is a gradient you negotiate or buy. The inhabitants thread among the fauna without surprise; they have had millennia to weather the unusual into reliability. You have not. Expect culture shock that feels like drowning in other people’s density. Languages are uncountable; translation (biological, technological, or social) is essential. Trade, argument with planning engines, and temporary organs of commerce (the blooming markets) are the social weather. Learn the local norms around claiming space, paying for volume, and not being recast into the architecture.

Psychologically, train for constant low-level threat from change itself, sensory saturation without peaks or rest, claustrophobia that is architectural rather than spatial (everything is interior, fused, occupied), and the absence of any “outside.” The city has been itself longer than any language spoken inside it. Its rule is simple: space is never empty, water is never absent, change is the only constant that has itself grown old. An ordinary human mind will try to find a rim, a dry interval, a still moment, a wilderness. None exist. The preparation that matters most is the willingness to inhabit the medium instead of looking for a room inside it.

### Practical Arrival and First Hours
You will arrive already wet and already inside. There is no threshold. Immediate priorities: secure potable water, locate a negotiated sleeping volume, establish a way to be heard and to hear, and find someone (or some engine) who can explain the current recasting cycle of the district you landed in. Watch the water: it tells you about overflows, new joints, and the health of the films. Watch the inhabitants: they already know how to thread the fauna and the reconfigurations. Watch the walls: they are not scenery.

Eat when you can; the city generates temporary organs of commerce that dissolve. Rest in shifts if possible; continuous noise and motion make deep sleep rare. Keep moving when the reconstruction front comes through; staying still is how you become part of the next load path. Document nothing you cannot afford to lose to water or recasting. The place does not care that you are a visitor from another universe. It has been saturating the basin since the sea forgot it was a sea.

Visiting feels like being metabolized by an ancient, dripping, argumentative machine that is also an ocean, a slum, a factory, and a weather system. It is oppressive, beautiful in a mineral-organic-mechanical way, and indifferent. You will leave (if you leave) permanently slightly damp in the joints, with a new relationship to emptiness, and with the sound of unceasing water still in your molars. Prepare by practicing the opposite of most travel: do not seek the edge, the dry, the quiet, or the unchanged. There aren’t any. Wade, shout, watch the wall decide, and negotiate your cubic measure like everyone else who has lasted here.
