**Physical realities first: this place will try to unmake the wet, warm, time-bound animal you are.**

The highland is a finished sentence spoken in salt and gypsum and something finer than ash. It has no water and treats the concept as a grammatical error. Any liquid you carry leaves you within the first hour as a white rime on your clothes and then as nothing; your own moisture will follow more slowly. Thirst arrives as a change in hearing rather than a dryness in the mouth. The air is cool, still, and tastes of purity (which is the local word for absence). Light is a perpetual bruise-an-hour-before-dawn that never quite arrives and never quite fails. Sound is almost theoretical except for the ticks—the stone contracting, decisions finishing. Those ticks are the only clock. After enough of them a doorway has shifted a finger-width, a stair has gained a tread, a plaza has always been a slightly larger circle. Perspectives do not close. Distances are extended and withdrawn as a courtesy. Elevation is a continuous fact rather than a number; streets can be walked as vertical faces until the inner ear files a complaint and then, finding no fluid to argue with, falls silent. You will leave prints. The ground will give them back over hours you will not notice passing.

An ordinary human body is an error of typesetting here. Scale is civic and geological at once. Avenues know they are avenues; plazas have civic emptiness; colonnades march until they forget why. Between façades a person can walk a day and meet only the faint herringbone of drainage that never carried liquid. The masonry is continuous with bedrock—a change of mind in the mineral rather than construction. No tool marks, no fittings, no metal that remembers a forge. Vegetation is a mineral crust that copies branching and then becomes wall. Fauna is rarer: walkers the size of a seated child at ten meters (a tower that learned to pace at a kilometer), made of the same pale cake, joints marked only by cooler hairlines. They do not hunt or flee. They stand until the place agrees it was designed around them, then they are gone or you have become the thing that moved. Smaller flocks of something like shed window-glare lift on ticks and resettle as rearranged absences; they eat alignment.

The city is finished and has always been unfinished. You may add nothing. You may subtract only by walking, which is a form of erosion so slight the stone will not admit it until you are gone. If you stay long enough it will offer you a district of your own—a widening, steps that fit your stride, a tower with one window looking onto the same window. This is not hospitality. It is metabolism of the visitor error. Walkers may pause there as if confirming the amendment.

**Preparation is therefore mostly subtraction, insulation of self, and acceptance that rescue, grief, and ordinary timekeeping are courtesies the place can withdraw.**

Do not bring water in any open or semi-open form. It will not stay and the white ghost it leaves is a census mark. Pre-hydrate to medical limits if your physiology allows; consider (if your travel method permits) a fully closed-cycle moisture recapture garment that treats sweat, breath, and urine as sacred and never vents. Even then expect thirst-as-hearing to begin. Bring no food that requires water to swallow or digest comfortably; calorie-dense, low-residue rations that can be taken dry. The place does not appear to offer calories or forbid them; it simply has no interest.

Clothing should be pale, loose-layered, and mineral-tolerant. The ground and dust are sintered salts and gypsum ghosts; they will cake, enter seams, and take the print of every fold. Avoid anything that rustles or reflects; the smaller flocks eat alignment and sudden glare may be taken as an invitation to rearrange you. No metal that “remembers a forge” if you can help it—the description is specific. Soft, dull fasteners, ceramic or stone-composite tools if you must have any. A wide-brimmed or hooded layer against the unchanging bruise-light; the cool lives in the stone and will leach through any thin fabric if you sit or lean. Footwear that leaves as little impression as possible and that you can empty of accumulating fines. The ground gives prints back; do not become attached to your own tracks as navigation.

A timepiece that does not rely on light, sound, or assumed Euclidean flow. A mechanical or inertial counter that you treat as advisory only. Count ticks yourself, but understand they are the sound of decisions finishing, not warnings. They will change the street behind you. Do not sleep in a plaza that has begun to believe it is a room; the light thinning is permission, not hour. When upper districts become theoretical the lower ones crowd closer (or the reverse). Have a pre-agreed internal signal for “leave now”—a phrase, a counted sequence, a physical token you will not lose to the pale.

Navigation cannot be Euclidean. Standard maps, compasses, and ranging will lie or loop. Train beforehand in holding contradictory spatial facts without resolving them: a colonnade that recedes correctly until the tenth column which is also the first seen from its far side; a portal the height of a person that opens onto the same plaza you left which is also a salt-pan; shadows that lean in two directions and neither is a lie. Practice walking a vertical face as floor until the inner ear quiets. Accept that “nine towers” will not remain nine when you look away. Distance is a courtesy; do not rely on it for rescue or for measuring how far you have come. The only reliable exit instruction is the one given: walk until the buildings forget to be near. You will not hear yourself go. The highland will keep your prints until the next dry front and then return them to the general pale, which is its only census.

Psychological preparation is the larger part. This place metabolizes attention and identity the way it metabolizes prints. Perspectives that refuse to close, interiors that are not contained, buildings that are only the idea of enclosure drawn in thinner mineral—these will erode the habit of a single consistent self. Isolation is absolute and civic at once; you are an error walking through administrative emptiness. The walkers are not hostile; they are the city adjusting itself. Do not try to communicate, follow, or flee them. Standing still long enough for a place to agree it was designed around you is how you begin to be metabolized. The mineral bloom in the lee of walls is the entire flora and has no juice or ambition; do not expect companion life.

Before departure, spend time in other non-orientable or slowly transforming spaces if your traveler’s experience includes them. Practice remaining oriented to your own body and recent decisions rather than to visual closure. Rehearse the rules as mantras: add nothing; subtract only by walking; water is ungrammatical; light is permission; ticks are decisions finishing; do not sleep where a plaza is becoming a room; if a district begins to fit your stride, that is the beginning of digestion. Carry one small, personal, non-mineral object that has no civic or geometric meaning—a scrap of something organic or a remembered scent—solely as an anchor you will not use for anything else. You may lose it. That is acceptable.

Expect the following sequence on arrival: the highland is already underfoot and already past the horizon. What you take for sky is more mineral, thinned. Coolness in the stone like a secret in a closed mouth. The first hours will feel like walking through a photograph of a city that has been left out in a dry century. Then the first tick. Then the realization that a doorway has moved or a stair has gained a tread and you did not see it happen. Then the first failure of perspective to close. Then thirst-as-hearing. Then, if you linger, the offer of a district that fits. Leave before the offer becomes a widening you recognize as yours.

You can see the further country from any high terrace if you do not focus: a grid so open it is only civic intent drawn across a salt-field the size of a climate. Processes there are faster or slower; geometry is less willing to pretend it ever tiled the plane. It looks like more city. It looks like the first city. Do not go there on a first visit.

The highland does not begin and does not end. An ordinary human can walk it for a while, leave faint prints, and depart by becoming forgettable. Preparation is the art of remaining an error the stone has not yet decided to correct. Stand still only long enough to let one tick happen, confirm the street you came down is still there (and also a cliff, and also a colonnade from a year you have not spent), then keep moving until the buildings lose interest in being near. The air will still taste of nothing. That is the local word for purity. You will not hear yourself go.
