**Arrive sealed, already inside your own small weather.** The basin does not negotiate with lungs, skin, or unarmored thought. An ordinary human body—warm, wet, full of empty intervals and the habit of light—lasts seconds here before the packed silt occupies the spaces between cells, the cold finishes the occupation, and the pressure makes the rest academic. You do not “visit” this place the way you visit a valley or a shore. You insert a temporary, self-contained exception into a medium that has no use for exceptions and then you try to feel what the exception feels like before the basin remembers itself around you.

The first preparation is therefore not courage or curiosity but a complete, portable refusal of the medium. You will need a sealed pressure hull or an equivalent full-body exosuit rated for the constant impartial weight that begins the moment you arrive. Depth is not given because the surface above no longer belongs to this place; treat it as hadal or worse. Ordinary human engineering from most timelines will not suffice. Source (or have already sourced) materials and life-support from a universe that solved extreme-pressure isolation without becoming theatrical about it: a rigid or semi-rigid vessel that maintains one atmosphere and 37 °C internally, recycles air indefinitely, and rejects heat so the 0.5 °C water cannot occupy you through conduction. The hull must be smooth enough that silt grains cannot key into it and begin the long process of wearing it into the same last sentence as everything else. Joints and seals are the first things the basin will try to rewrite. Redundancy on every penetration. No “just this once” openings. If you open your mouth the grit is already between your teeth; the same logic applies to every valve.

Movement through the packed dark is not swimming and not walking. It is a continuous negotiation with a fluid that has forgotten it is fluid. Grain touches grain. There is no empty interval. Your vehicle or suit needs low-speed, high-torque propulsion that can push against a medium whose density is closer to wet sand or cement slurry than to water—screws that will not bind, perhaps articulated limbs or a crawling undercarriage that can feel for the nearly-level floor and the one-degree rumor of slope. You will feel currents as sideways shoves without wind, as pressure walking across the hull, as sudden coarseness against sensors. Turbidity storms last days. Design for being shoved, rotated, and then left exactly where you were. Anchors or temporary pitons that can bite the glazed clay without becoming permanent artifacts. Inertial navigation plus whatever tactile and chemical mapping you can manage; light is a theory and sound is a minority event. A powerful lamp will illuminate the flour immediately in front of the port and little else. Learn to read the changing grain against the hull the way the description says: coarseness of a recent slide, silk of a quiet hour, the brief fist-sized emptiness where two currents have not yet closed the seam. That emptiness is the closest the basin comes to hospitality. It will close.

Life support must assume weeks, not hours. A storm can last a week and leave the floor looking unchanged. Recycling, caloric stores, waste handling, and medical capacity for the slow stupidness that even a perfect suit cannot entirely prevent: thought itself slows to the speed of the silt. Fingers go stupid; the mind follows. Bring stimulants or neural pacing if your originating timeline has them, but do not expect them to win. The cold is not theatrical. It occupies. The darkness has texture you can almost trust, and that almost-trust is itself a hazard.

Mental and temporal preparation is the larger part of the work, because the place finished dying and then kept existing out of habit. Ordinary human attention is built for weather, for edges, for things that are still becoming. Here the stone reached its final form long ago. The water never signed the treaty, so the turbulence continues as the last habit of a world that has already spent its shapes. You will experience this as a profound, impartial sameness interrupted by rare, almost insulting specifics: a bacterial film the color of old bruise in the lee of a cobble, a single leafless pale stalk leaning forever, a brief segmented signature in the silt, once a larger darkness that is not the water, and, if you are patient and lucky, three flakes of stone whose cluster is slightly too intentional. That is the entire city. That is the entire age of tools. Miss them and you will still be right. The ecology is an afterthought the basin has not had the energy to cancel.

Train in sensory deprivation that includes resistance and cold, but understand that tanks and mud pits are only cartoons. Practice moving by touch and current-feel until the absence of horizon stops being an emergency. Sit with geological time until the idea of a mountain here feels like the insult it is. The basin will rewrite you without changing you; the risk is that you will begin to prefer the rewriting. Thought slows. The packed dark begins to have a texture you can almost trust. That is the moment most travelers either leave or stay too long. Decide in advance what “too long” means and give someone (or some automatic process) the authority to extract you. Multiverse arrival drops you already below the surface; departure is your responsibility.

Do not bring anything you are unwilling to lose or to have the basin keep “by failing to destroy it quite completely.” The three flakes are the cautionary tale. If you must sample, take only what the currents have already peeled free and record everything else. The rare life—if the film, the stalk, the pale segmented thing, the larger darkness count as life—does not stay to be known. Do not chase. Do not assume purpose or its memory is directed at you. The rules have no authors: what is heavy sinks, what is finer stays up longer and then also sinks, the water will always find a way to move because the ice is always calving somewhere above a lid that does not belong to this place.

You will not see the surface. You will not feel weather. You will not find soil or mountains or a name that has survived. What you will find, if you remain sealed and patient, is the texture of a finished dying that did not stop. The contradiction is the place. Prepare to be inside that contradiction without trying to resolve it. When the hull begins to feel like the only remaining interval of emptiness, that is the signal. Leave while you can still tell the difference between the basin’s ancient working dark and the smaller dark you brought with you. The silt will close the seam behind you as if you had never made room. That, too, is the place being itself.

The ordinary human part of you will want light, edges, and the possibility of change. Pack those things inside the hull, use them sparingly, and do not be surprised when they begin to feel like the insult a mountain would be. Visit anyway if you must. Just arrive already below, already sealed, and already willing to be almost nothing for a while.
