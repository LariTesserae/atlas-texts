**Do not arrive finished.** That is the first and last rule. This pocket is embryonic geology and half-assembled life; it has not agreed on a schedule, a scale, or even a single consistent version of itself. An ordinary human who brings too much completion—metal, certainty, a watch, a name spoken aloud, a plan that assumes linear time or Euclidean space—will either be gently edited out or slowly mineralized into the dish-floor. Prepare as if you are visiting a dream that is still deciding whether it wants to be a cave.

### Locating and entering the seam
The crack is the only honest door and it is not reliable. It is forearm-length, rimmed in that pale mineral that cannot decide between salt and bone, and it migrates a few centimeters left or right of itself when unobserved. You will not find it by searching hard. Walk the surface ground with the unfocused attention you use when looking for a word on the tip of your tongue. Notice the places where your foot wants to hesitate for no topographic reason. The rind catches a slightly different quality of daylight—cooler, more overexposed. When you see it, do not crouch immediately. Stand a moment and let the earth finish choosing. If it is still sliding, wait; forcing it produces a seam that opens onto nothing or onto a version of the chamber that has already hardened and will not let you back.

The drop is short but not kind. The pocket is the size of a held breath; the ceiling will be against your shoulders or the back of your skull the moment you are through. Enter feet-first if you can, or accept that you will land in a crouch that feels like an argument with the rock. Do not bring a rope or piton. Nothing metal survives here as an object, and a line would become another unfinished vein, possibly growing in the wrong direction. Once inside, the seam above you may remain a crack, become a pale oval fringed with grass-roots, or vanish entirely. That is normal. Do not stare at it hoping to keep it open; the light here does not like to be pinned.

### Body and kit
Dress in layers that can be shed without drama. The temperature is cellar-cool and the air wants your breath to linger as a small ghost. Cotton or linen that can take a faint mineral film is better than synthetics that refuse to be claimed. Soft-soled shoes or none; the floor is still remembering how to be stone and will record every hard tread as a patch of premature hardness. Expect the damp to find you in films rather than drops. It will bead on your clothes, gather in the creases of your palms, and consider your shoes as possible new substrate for the pale lichen. This is not hostility. It is the pocket’s only way of noticing you.

Bring almost nothing. A small cloth for the films of water if your eyes or mouth become too dry. No flask; the isolated puddles look like holes punched through to a brighter, finished room and drinking from them is how visitors become part of the next draft. No knife, no notebook that expects to remain paper, no light (the sourceless glare is already too much and a second source creates late, misplaced shadows that confuse the animals). If you must record, use memory or a single unfinished thought you are willing to leave behind. Anything complete will either fail to exist or will be slowly unmade into the grain.

Eat lightly beforehand and do not plan to eat inside. Time eddies; hunger may not arrive on schedule, or it may arrive twice. The isopods and millipedes feed with unhurried courtesy on films and on each other; you are not on the menu unless you insist on becoming geology.

### Perception and mind
The light is the weather. It sits in the air like condensation on glass—cool, sourceless, slightly too bright, washing every surface into overexposure. Shadows are late and appear in the wrong places. Train yourself beforehand to accept visual lag: an object will exist, then its shadow will arrive as an afterthought, possibly belonging to a slightly different object. Do not correct it. The chamber is still inventing darkness.

Sound lives at the threshold. Your blood will seem loud and a little embarrassed. Drops decide rather than splash. A hundred thousand millipede legs produce silk instead of rustle. Practice silence until your own breathing does not feel like an interruption. The animals occupy space without displacing brightness; they pass through one another, or through slightly different versions of the same few inches. You will feel crowded and empty at once. That is the correct sensation.

Time does not pass; it recirculates. A millipede column, a thickening of glare that almost becomes a shape, the white vein advancing one grain, the cairn gaining or losing a stone—these happen, then eddy. Distances stretch when you stop measuring them and snap back when you start. Look too long at any palm-sized puddle and you will see a larger, older, or already-vanished chamber reflected there, complete with real shadows and the sound of distant water this room has not earned. Blink. It is only water again. Do not try to climb into the reflection. That is how the pocket practices being true, and it does not need your help finishing itself.

The low ceiling will press. Standing is an argument you will lose. Move in a crouch or on hands and knees, slowly, letting the unfinished stone yield a fraction under your thumb and then forget the dent. The geology is being assembled by processes that have not synchronized. Ribs push farther when you look away. Treat every surface as provisional.

### Conduct inside
Be a guest who does not take up much schedule. The pocket has room for a visitor who remains slightly unfinished. Do not stack another stone on the leaning cairn unless the count has already changed in your peripheral vision and the new arrangement feels like an invitation rather than a correction. The parallel scratches are bright because every cut here leaks light; do not add to them. They are the idea of writing, not writing yet.

The animals will mostly ignore you. Pale millipedes braid along the walls and drink condensation; moths the color of overexposed paper are handed from one still current to another; midges hang as a motionless blizzard that relocates without beating wings; the fist-sized watcher with too many shoulder-blades clings to a forming cornice, breathes silently, and drinks from a puddle without crossing the distance. Once they all faced the same way—toward a thickening in the air that almost had edges. Nothing came through. If it happens again, do not stare at the thickening and do not look away in fear. Simply occupy the same quality of attention the millipedes use: present, unhurried, willing to be in two slightly different places at once.

Predation is silent and courteous. A moth is simply no longer among the currents. New animals assemble first as concentrations of glare, then as legs, then as the habit of walking. Do not try to help or hinder. Where many feet have passed the floor chooses a texture; where nothing has walked it remains almost clay. Walk lightly enough that you do not force the choice.

If the entrance-seam vanishes, stay still. The pocket is still being born and has not yet decided it needs an outside. Panic hardens the stone. The damp will find you, the lichen will consider you, the stacked stones may adjust their number. This is not imprisonment; it is inclusion in a process that has not agreed on an ending.

### Risks that are actually the place itself
- The air thickening into a shape that almost has edges. Nothing has come through yet. Treat it as weather.
- Your own breath remaining behind you as a small cloud the room has not agreed to dismiss. Do not walk back into it expecting it to be yours.
- Becoming a stain that acquired chlorophyll, or a film, or a new lamination. The incorporation is slow and polite. You will notice first as a coolness that does not leave when you leave.
- Time folding so that you exit before you entered, or after the chamber has finished itself into an ordinary cave with ordinary shadows. Carry no expectation of sequence.
- The reflection in the puddles showing a version of the room that is larger, older, or gone. That version is practicing. You are not required to join the rehearsal.

### Leaving and after
When the seam reappears—or when the brightness develops an outside again—leave the same way you entered: unfinished, without souvenirs. The mineral rind may have grown a little. The animals will not watch you go; they are busy arriving into the emptiness.

Afterward you may find that ordinary shadows arrive late for a few days, that your breath sometimes lingers in still air, that puddles look briefly like holes. Distances may stretch when you stop measuring them. This fades, or it becomes a useful attunement for the next unfinished place. Do not try to wash the faint mineral film off too quickly; it is how the pocket remembers you without keeping you.

You could have stayed. The chamber would not have minded. It is still being constructed and has room in its schedule. The fact that you chose to leave is the only finished thing you are allowed to bring out.
