**A sealed, heated, chemically inert full-body environmental suit is non-negotiable, along with redundant power, sensory damping, spatial-awareness tools that do not rely on Euclidean assumptions, and a mental framework that treats personal continuity as provisional.** You are not visiting a landscape. You are inserting a fragile, wet, chemically naive mammal into a closed industrial metabolism that has been running so long the distinction between “terrain,” “weather,” “organism,” and “process” has worn away. An ordinary human lasts minutes unprotected and hours even well-equipped if they treat this like weather instead of like being inside a living machine that is also eating itself.

The first sensation is the cold that is already inside everything. The water is not falling; it is the default state. It weeps from mineral faces, condenses out of the dim air, and stands in black sheets. It is near-freezing and mineral-saturated. Your suit must be a true dry system with active heating (chemical or electrical, redundant) rated for prolonged immersion in brine and oxide slurry. Seams, valves, and gloves fail first. Anything that lets the film in will carry dissolved metals and “the memory of motion” straight to skin and lungs. The slurry itself is abrasive, packed with shed carapaces and frozen brines; it will grind and corrode ordinary fabrics or even many high-end polymers. Bring spare outer layers or a sacrificial abrasion skin you can shed and replace. The chemistry will not grow anything green and will not be kind to terrestrial organics. Assume the water and air are both slightly toxic and slightly conductive.

You will never have personal space. Density is total. Every square of surface, every volume of air or water, is occupied by something that breathes, ticks, or simply occupies. Huge many-legged hulls the size of collapsed foundries drag themselves on living treads; their undersides are forests of siphoning mouths. Smaller jointed crawlers unscrew one another for parts and immediately reconfigure. Ice-and-wire flocks pass in screaming sheets whose shadows weigh more than their bodies. When they collide they do not die; they become louder. You cannot step aside. There is no interval. Your suit therefore needs some form of low-profile displacement or temporary exclusion field if your tech allows it—even a simple rigid frame or inflatable buffer that creates a few tens of centimeters of claimed volume. Without it you will be walked through, unscrewed, or incorporated. The larger hulls will not notice you any more than a glacier notices a pebble, but the smaller converters will treat you as available feedstock or spare components. They are not malicious. They are converters.

Sound is the primary weather and it never stops. Millions of internal engines that are also metabolisms produce a continuous low roar. Excreted gears lock into temporary machines that run for an hour then dissolve. Ice-flocks keen at frequencies that hurt. Underneath everything is a subsonic grind—the planet still trying to become even flatter. Bring active noise cancellation plus passive isolation, but understand that some of the energy is mechanical vibration transmitted through the slurry and through your bones. Subsonics will shake your organs. Bone-conduction dampers or a full-body harness that isolates you from the ground help. You will still feel it. You will still hear things arrive from directions that do not exist. Sounds routinely precede or follow their sources. Accept that your auditory map of the world is a lie and do not trust it for threat assessment.

Light is a grey-blue dusk that never changes. There is no sun, or the sun has been worn as thin as everything else. Sparks jump between hulls (static or language). Some smaller fauna carry dull orange internal furnaces; the light is immediately swallowed. Pale chemical glows pulse in the deepest black channels. Bring your own lights, preferably narrow-spectrum or polarized so they are less likely to be eaten by the mist, and infrared or other non-visual sensors. Visual range is short. Things appear inside the space you occupy without having crossed the interval. Distances lengthen while you watch. A working assembly line can spontaneously organize out of the slurry—raw ore crawling up, being sorted and stamped by nothing, then eaten—then slump back into wet ticking debris when the local weirdness eddies elsewhere. Your eyes and brain will try to impose continuity. They will fail. Train yourself beforehand to treat visual data as provisional and to cross-check with non-optical sensors (vibration, conductivity, whatever your kit provides). Do not stare at any one patch of slurry too long. The remaining rules fray at the corners if you look too long.

Reality does not hold its shape. This is not metaphor. You need tools that do not assume stable metric or causal order: inertial dead-reckoning that you reset frequently, multiple independent clocks, perhaps a small ensemble of entangled or otherwise correlated particles if your tech allows, or simply the humble practice of marking your own body and suit with unique, hard-to-copy identifiers so you can tell whether a piece of you is still you. Things can occupy the same volume. A creature fifty meters away can be inside you. Occasional spontaneous machines will try to process whatever is nearby. Keep moving, but not in any pattern the local converters might interpret as an assembly step. Randomness is your friend; predictability is feedstock.

You cannot live off the land. Everything eats the output of everything else. There are no producers, only converters. Predator and prey are temporary job titles. The same organism occupies both roles depending on which way the local physics is leaning. The water tastes of copper, old electricity, and the memory of motion; do not drink it. Bring all consumables in sealed, inert containers. Assume anything you leave unattended for more than a few minutes will be disassembled or incorporated. Waste heat, discarded wrappers, even exhaled breath may be treated as raw material. Minimize your chemical and thermal signature.

Psychologically, prepare for continuous, high-amplitude sensory assault plus the slow erosion of the idea that you occupy a privileged, continuous location in space. The plain has no edges you can reach and no center. You can walk for days and the horizon will not tilt. The only remaining rule is that there is no unused space, and even that frays. Isolation, if you can achieve it at all, will feel like theft. Many travelers report a growing sense that they themselves are a temporary configuration of parts that the plain will eventually reuse. This is not mystical; it is an accurate description of the local economy. Counter it with strict personal rituals, recorded voice notes to yourself that you play back on a schedule, and a pre-agreed abort condition. Ordinary humans have limited tolerance for environments that treat individuality as a brief, inefficient assembly state.

Exit is not geographic. Because the place has no reachable edge, your only reliable way out is whatever method brought you in (portal, jump, fold, whatever your traveler’s kit uses). Test it immediately upon arrival and at regular intervals. Reality warps can make the return coordinates unreliable. Keep a dead-man’s switch or a timed automatic recall. If you stay long enough that the local physics begins to treat your recall device as just another converter, you will not leave.

In short: seal yourself against the wet and the cold and the chemistry, claim a tiny volume of space by force or by field, damp the sound that never stops, distrust every sensory channel, carry everything you need, move unpredictably, and never assume the interval between you and anything else is empty or stable. The plain is the factory and the factory is the plain. It has been grinding itself flatter for longer than there have been names for time. You are a brief, noisy, chemically interesting impurity. Be prepared to be processed.
