**Prepare as if you are about to briefly occupy a completed, finished object rather than a space.** This is not a cave, ruin, or room you move through. It is a sealed, self-contained artifact of packed artifacts—an envelope of pale fitted stone whose interior has been filled to a density that leaves no usable volume. An ordinary human body has no standing place, no path, and no margin. You will experience it as a momentary insertion into an equilibrium that has already decided everything: light, sound, dryness, arrangement. The visit is sensory, brief, and non-negotiable in its constraints. The slit will close; the surrounding dry land will treat the pocket as if it never opened.

### Mental and psychological preparation
Confront the core paradox first: you are entering a place that has no room for you and does not change. The chamber is “finished.” Edges have rounded to their terminal softness and stopped. Temperature, humidity, shadow, and tone are uniform and unvarying. There is no weather, no cycle, no quieter corner. The roar is a standing tone that has been sounding since the geometry locked. The light is a motionless white column that never migrates. Vegetation is a single arrested rosette and a palm-sized lichen film—an error the place has not bothered to correct. Nothing lives, ticks, or travels.

This produces a specific psychological pressure: the feeling of being an interruption in something that outlasted purpose. Ordinary human instincts (look for a way through, find a place to stand, wait for the light to shift, expect the sound to modulate) will fail immediately. Claustrophobia here is not merely spatial; it is ontological. The packed clay cylinders, nested tiles, basins, cups, and lids form a single continuous mass. Air exists only as films. You will feel the vault “stooped,” the scale collapsed, and your own body as the only remaining variable.

Train for this. Practice remaining still in extremely confined, brightly lit, noisy environments (a packed storage closet with a work light and a constant mid-range tone, or a sensory-deprivation adjacent setup with glare and a standing roar). Use timed exposures. The goal is not comfort but the ability to look, listen, and feel the cool dryness on your face without thrashing or attempting to rearrange anything. Accept that you cannot stay and that the place will not acknowledge your presence as meaningful. If you have a history of panic in tight spaces, strong glare, or unrelenting sound, do not go; the combination is simultaneous and inescapable once inside.

Meditate on completeness. The objects reached the one arrangement this light, dryness, and note require. You are not meant to add, subtract, or interpret. Treat the visit as witnessing a sealed jar that happens to contain a vacuum of change.

### Physical and logistical preparation
The entrance is a vertical crease no wider than a forearm in already low, tired dry ground. It looks like ordinary settling. An ordinary adult human cannot simply walk in; the slit is a squeeze even for a slender person turning sideways and exhaling. Inside, the chamber itself can be crossed in three careful steps only if there were steps—there are none. The mass meets the vault without remainder.

- **Body and clothing**: Wear the slimmest, most flexible, low-profile clothing possible—thin, smooth fabrics that will not snag on rims or catch mineral bloom. No belts, no pockets that bulge, no jewelry, no boots with tread that could disturb dust. Barefoot or the thinnest possible slippers if the floor must be touched at all (it probably cannot). Remove anything that increases your cross-section. If you are not already lean and flexible, this location is poorly suited; the packing leaves no forgiveness.
- **Senses and protection**: The light is violent, white, motionless, and ricochets until the entire ivory-and-salt-crust mass glares. Shadows survive only as hair-thin black lines. Ordinary sunglasses will be insufficient against the perpetual shadowless noon. Use high-quality wraparound glare protection or welding-style lenses rated for intense, non-solar white light; test them against comparable brightness beforehand. The roar is loud, mid-range, unchanging, and seems to originate in the exact fit of clay against clay and the chamber’s finished shape. It is a standing tone, the thin air vibrating with it. High-quality earplugs or custom-molded protection that still allow you to perceive the pitch (you need to hear it to understand the place) are essential; the vibration will be felt in the chest and skull regardless. The air is cool, absolutely dry, and cellar-still; the glare carries no heat. Skin and mucous membranes will register the dryness immediately. A thin layer of unscented balm on lips and any exposed skin can reduce the immediate desiccation feel, but do not expect moisture. The place no longer credits liquid.
- **Duration and supplies**: You cannot stay. There is nowhere to put a foot. Plan for seconds to a very few minutes at most—the time required to look, listen, and feel. Carry nothing extra. No water (you will not drink), no food, no tools, no sampling kit. A small, soft cloth to wipe your face of the dryness afterward is the maximum. Any object you bring becomes an error like the rosette.
- **Navigation and return**: As a multiverse traveler you presumably have a method for locating and re-entering the slit (or the equivalent coordinate). Mark nothing on the outside; the land is already a tired sag that looks like any other. The slit is easy to miss even when you know it is there. Have a reliable exit trigger or countdown; once inside, the geometry and the roar make ordinary time sense unreliable. The chamber holds its vacuum of change the way a sealed jar holds vacuum. Do not rely on the light or sound shifting as a cue—they will not.

### Entry, conduct, and exit
Approach the low ground without ceremony. The opening will appear as a crease. Exhale, turn, and insert yourself. The scale collapses at once. You will feel the vault low, the air stooped, the joints of the stone worn into suggestions of intention. No raw substrate, no “outside” rock. Everything is made and complete.

Do not try to create a path. Do not shift a cylinder, tile, or cup. The density *is* the architecture. Any movement that disturbs the packing is an act of destruction against a finished equilibrium. You will occupy the films of air, if at all. Your presence is the only unaccounted volume.

Look: the white column strikes the highest rims and fills the mass with glare. Note the unchanging filigree of mineral bloom, the dust that has found every microscopic shelf and stayed, the motes hanging without dancing. Listen: the roar is constant in pitch and volume. Feel: cool dryness on the face, the vibration in the air, the absence of heat or humidity gradient.

That is the visit. When the interval ends, withdraw the same way you entered. The slit closes behind you. The ordinary dry land forgets the pocket was ever open. Do not linger at the crease or attempt to reopen it immediately; the place has no increment or decay and does not reward persistence.

### After the visit
The contrast with ordinary land will be sharp: moving air, migrating shadows, variable sound, the possibility of moisture, insects, change. You may feel a residual vibration or a dryness in the sinuses and eyes. Hydrate and rest your senses. Do not attempt to describe the exact tone or the precise glare to others in a way that treats them as souvenirs; the chamber is not extractable. The single gray-green rosette and the lichen film are the only other living things that have been tolerated. You were not.

This location does not care that you came. It sat in the low ground holding its glare and its unvarying roar before you and will do so after. An ordinary human prepares by becoming as small, still, and temporary as possible, then leaving the completed thing completed.
