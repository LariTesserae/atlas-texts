**Pack for a finished world that is still proving it is finished.** An ordinary human body is built for gradients, weather that argues, horizons that eventually admit a crease. This peneplain offers none of that. You will walk on the last vocabulary of rock under a sun that does its ordinary work across distances that have outlasted meaning. Preparation is less about conquest or even exploration than about remaining a temporary, respectful exception to a stability that has to work continuously to stay still.

**Atmosphere, temperature, and the body.** The air is thin and cool in the long withholding, not theatrical cold. Breath is visible if you look; you usually will not, because looking costs attention and attention here has far to go. Acclimatize if your transit allows it, but assume you arrive unacclimated. Carry supplemental oxygen—concentrators with redundant filters and bottled backup—because the scarcity is structural, not just altitudinal. Monitor saturation continuously; the cool dryness will mask early hypoxia as simple fatigue. Clothing is layered insulation against the steady subtractive wind (pressure more than noise) plus full-face filtration. The dust is so fine it behaves like a gas; it will occupy lungs, eyes, joints, and every seal you own. Use P100 or better respirators with positive-pressure options, sealed goggles or a hood, and clothing that can be shaken or vacuumed without releasing clouds. Skin will desiccate. Carry occlusive moisturizers and accept that they will feel like a small heresy. Hydration is the non-negotiable. Bring more water than any reasonable calculation, plus high-efficiency recycling (urine, condensate, even the faint moisture you can coax from exhalation). Local water is a theft from mineral lattices—a long, unkind chemistry that tastes of the end of rivers. Do it only in extremity and know you are taking something the plateau has already finished using. Food is entirely imported, compact, high-calorie, requiring no fire (fire is easy and pointless; there is almost nothing to burn and the wind arrives as subtraction). Electrolytes matter more than usual because everything is dry.

**Movement and navigation.** You walk. Residual swells are so broad a day’s climb is imperceptible; polygonal pavements the size of cities have edges softened until they look poured. Boots must handle interlocking mineral, grit older than language, and the occasional centimetre-scale lift-and-settle of a plate. Distances bleach at midday into visual silence; dawn and dusk last because they have more surface to work across. Navigation cannot rely on magnetic compasses (they sulk along certain lines) or any single instrument class (they disagree with one another in polite, consistent ways near the works). Use stellar fixes at night (stars come down close), inertial systems with frequent resets, optical odometry, and a simple count of the scattered works. You will not get lost in the ordinary sense because lost requires difference; you can travel a month and the horizon still has not acquired a crease. Mark nothing permanently. Paths do not persist. The plateau’s last form includes a clause about them. Previous visitors left cairns already being persuaded back into pavement, huts whose corners have begun to forget they were corners, vehicles of the wrong generation still faintly warm. Your traces will receive the same courtesy.

**Shelter and the works.** A portable shelter is useful but secondary. Prefer the lee of a polygon edge or, better, the still disc that some artifacts maintain against the general unrest. The works themselves—half-sunk rings with faces too fine to hold dust, tripods driven through pavements that should have shattered, patches of locally increased resolution, lines the dust prefers not to cross—are not ruins and not maintained. They operate at a duty cycle you cannot audit. They finished the mountains over an age and then declined to clock out. Approach with instruments on analog backup and multiple independent clocks. You will learn almost nothing except that physics here has not ended; it has become weather. One class gives the air a brief thunderstorm taste. Another anchors a knot of the silent storm. A ridge of pavement may unzip along ten kilometres, turn over, and lie down again in a new and equally final arrangement, all in a hush you feel in your teeth before you understand you have seen it. Do not camp on obvious seams. The rare animals (low, many-legged or not, the colour of the pavement, moving with unhurried guilt) avoid the works. The crust-plants (grey-blue, brittle, lithic films, the occasional stem with no ambition) approach them. This is as close as the ecology comes to an opinion. Treat the works as weather that thinks. Observe, record the disagreements, do not attempt to switch anything off. They are deriving the same answer they have always derived.

**The unstill emptiness.** Fine dust organizes. It braids, collapses into transient walls, rotating sheets, hollow columns that walk, then forgets itself. These are not storms in any language you brought. They are the turbulence of a proof that is already done. They have almost no medium left to complain in. Stay out of the denser architectures; they can subtract heat, scatter light, or simply occupy the volume you meant to breathe. Ground-level restlessness is quieter and stranger: a polygon lifts a centimetre, a seam remembers metal, whole districts shimmer with heat that is not thermal. Carry simple mechanical sensors (tilt, pressure, a weight on a string) because electronics will be polite and wrong. Night is clean. Some works answer the stars with a change in how a surface holds darkness. Meteors sometimes fail to complete their fall and are received. Do not assume every streak is harmless.

**Mind and duration.** The emptiness is structural. Density is a rumour of presence. Sound is private: your blood, the tick of cooling gear, conversations dead at ten metres. Shouting is rudeness toward the scale. Sensory diet will be extreme: visual silence at noon, long coloured dawns (iron-rose, bone, desaturated copper-green), the occasional darker seam you can follow for a week, then nothing. The psychological load is the finished-ness itself, the patience of rock that has already heard every argument weather can make, and the knowledge that the turbulence is only the derivation continuing. Solitude here is not loneliness of the usual kind; it is the absence of anything that needs you to be present. Bring whatever anchors an ordinary human mind—journals that will themselves become grit, recorded voices, a simple repetitive practice—and accept that they will feel small. People have been here and left or stayed and become another rarity. Decide in advance how long “enough” is. A month is easy to imagine and hard to inhabit. Fitness for endless walking on hard, almost-level stone matters more than peak strength. Joints, feet, and the slow accumulation of fine dust in everything will decide more than heroics.

**What you will not find and what you must not do.** No free water worth the chemistry. No fuel. No forage you should trust. No maps that remain true. Do not try to “use” the works; their physics is not a resource. Do not build anything meant to last; the surface will sand it back into the last shape. Do not expect the rare fauna to care that you are the main event; they have already learned otherwise. Fire, if you make it, will be a small, pointless brightness against a scale that does not notice. Leave the garden (if you find the regular lattice of grey-blue crust angling toward a point the sun does not occupy) untouched. It is tended by nothing and thriving on ordinary light plus a remainder.

You arrive already on the tall thing. Everything that was going to be high has been high so long that height became a plane. Pack so that you can walk, breathe, drink, and remain an unhurried witness while the dust organises and disorganises like a continent thinking, while the ground occasionally completes a thought it started before there were witnesses, and while the sun does the ordinary work of a sun across a distance that has outlasted meaning. Then leave, or stay and accept that your path will not persist. The plateau has already finished. It is only still working to remain so.
