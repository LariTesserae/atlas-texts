**Environmental suit or layered protection is non-negotiable; treat this as a sealed, high-heat, high-dust industrial foundry crossed with an unweathered crystal geode that has been running at equilibrium since before your species existed.** An ordinary human cannot improvise here. The air is abrasive mineral haze (salt, gypsum, unnamed harder crystals) that never settles, the heat is dry kiln-level with no evaporative relief, every surface that is not a machine is a fresh fracture-face, and the volume is packed to the width of a turned shoulder with sessile or near-sessile life plus hot iron. You will be in constant light contact. Nothing yields, startles, or dies to make space.

### Core Physiological Threats and Countermeasures
The atmosphere does not support unaided human respiration for long. It is warm cloth: dry, dusty, oxygen-poor in feel even if the partial pressure is technically adequate. Fine particulates will coat mucosa, lungs, and any exposed moisture (eyes, mouth, sweat). Bring a full-face respirator or powered air-purifying respirator with P100/HEPA plus chemical cartridges rated for dust, metal fumes, and unknown organics; carry spare filters and a plan to change them without contamination. Goggles or a sealed visor are mandatory—crystal grit and hanger-shed mineral will find any gap. Skin coverage must be complete: cut-resistant, heat-tolerant base layer (the crystals remain knife-edged; nothing has been rounded), over which a loose, breathable but sealed outer layer that can shed dust. Avoid anything that traps sweat against skin for long; the dryness will desiccate you anyway.

Heat load is constant and unrelenting. Estimate well above human comfort (kiln-overnight territory). You will not cool by sweating efficiently because the air itself abrades and the humidity is effectively zero. Use a cooling vest or phase-change packs if your visit is longer than minutes, plus copious water carried internally (hydration bladder, electrolyte tabs). Dehydration and heat exhaustion arrive faster than you expect because there is no weather, no breeze that is not already accounted for, and no shade that is not already occupied. Monitor yourself: dizziness, cessation of sweating, confusion. The place will not help you.

Noise is architectural—foundry-level, layered, never-varying (engine basso, turbine whine, pawl clack, clicker stridulation as a single unending chord). Ear protection (plugs plus muffs) is wise for the first hours to prevent auditory fatigue and disorientation; after that some travelers report the sound becoming a usable silence in which thought is possible. Test your tolerance beforehand with prolonged industrial recordings plus heat. The fauna appear to live on the noise as well as heat and salt; blocking it entirely may feel like sensory amputation.

No local water, no drinkable fluid of any kind. The only liquid is recycled amber lubricant sipped by the paper-barnacles and returned to the sump. Bring all hydration, all calories, all waste containment. Do not add anything to the equilibrium. Urine, sweat, food scraps, even exhaled moisture are foreign chemistry. Pack it out or contain it. The chamber has been finished for a very long time; your presence is already an increment of volume.

### Movement, Contact, and Biology
You cannot walk freely. Eighty paces would cross it if the bodies allowed; they do not. Aisles exist as suggestions between pillars, housings, and the great horizontal engine. You will be touched continuously: brass-colored clicker carapaces (fist-sized, packed wing-case to wing-case, rasping the same grain), warm pulsing hanger-ribbons (flat, meat-strap or smoked-tongue, hooked to crystal points, shedding grit that joins the haze), pale sessile barnacles opening and shutting in time with the dynamo (hot copper, crushed pepper, impossible floral). The white chlorophyll-less stalks occupy rather than grow. Nothing flees. Nothing attacks. They hold position the way the flywheel holds rpm. Expect this as the baseline texture of existence here, not an event.

Unknown biochemistry. Do not assume Earth rules. The clickers may metabolize salt and current; the hangers heat and sound; the barnacles oil and vibration. The shed grit is breathed by everything, including you. Possible allergens, irritants, or slow toxins. Minimize open wounds (the crystals will try to make them). Cover every cut immediately. Do not ingest, do not rub eyes, do not assume the smell is harmless. The place has no decay cycle you can recognize; populations are a shape, not a process. You are the only thing that can introduce death or growth.

Hot surfaces and moving parts: the engine jacket, flywheel, belts disappearing into crystal slots. The three-step stairs end in air. The hatch wheel is warm. Watch footing in the packed congregation of surfaces. A stumble puts you against knife-edged mineral or iron that has forgotten cooling.

### Psychological and Temporal Preparation
This is the harder part. The chamber is a sealed sentence, spoken once and still being spoken. Nothing is an event. The fracture faces are as sharp as the hour they split. Bulbs do not dim. The belt does not fray. Fauna do not leave gaps or make crowds. Time, in the ordinary sense, has not been admitted. After the initial sensory siege (heat, abrasion, volume of bodies, unceasing sound, dim amber dying into rumor of vault), a kind of climate settles: the loudness becomes thinkable silence, the heat a condition you were always going to have. Some visitors report a flattening of urgency, a temptation to simply occupy, to become another packed presence.

Prepare for that flattening. Practice long stillness in heat and noise beforehand. Bring a personal timekeeper (mechanical if possible—electronics may not love the dust and heat) and a reason to look at it. The place will not remind you that hours have passed. Identity, memory of weather, the insult of water and seasons—these become theoretical. Existential vertigo is common: you are transient meat in a finished grove of iron and crystal and bodies that have no successor century.

The hatch is the only variable. Chest-height iron wheel, three stairs then a gap the hangers have left. It will turn. On the other side is either identical nave after identical nave or the unthinkable corridor toward a surface where edges dull and water still works. Decide in advance whether you are a tourist of stasis or whether you will test the lid. Opening it is the only action that could be an event. The room does not require you either way.

### Practical Kit (Minimal, Because Volume Is Occupied)
- Full respiratory and ocular protection + spares.
- Cut- and heat-resistant coverage, dust-shedding outer layer, cooling if duration >30–60 min.
- 4–8+ liters water plus electrolytes per person (scale to planned stay; there is no refill).
- High-calorie, low-residue food; complete waste bags.
- Headlamp / backup light (the amber is local and dies quickly).
- Basic trauma kit emphasizing puncture, abrasion, heat injury, respiratory distress.
- Compact mechanical watch or timer, notebook (paper survives), perhaps one small personal object that reminds you of change.
- No open flames, no unnecessary electronics, nothing that sheds fibers or moisture.

Condition yourself: heat walks in dry climates, dust exposure (controlled), prolonged loud environments, tight spaces with strangers who do not move. Medical clearance for lungs, thermoregulation, claustrophobia, psychiatric stability. Tell someone your intended duration and the hatch decision.

You will not be attacked. You will not be welcomed. You will be layered in, near-touching, another increment of the packed solid that only pretends to be atmosphere. The flywheel turns. The clickers rasp the same grain. The young walls keep their knives. If you stay long enough the weirdness stops being weird and becomes the rule. Leave before you forget that elsewhere things still dull, still drink, still end. Or do not. The chamber has its equilibrium. You are optional.
