The first impression is that nothing lives here at all. The basin has the finished look of a thing that used to contain life the way a dry river used to contain water: thoroughly, then not, then only in the grammar of absence. The boulders are only boulders. The black shrubs are only clots of forgotten wire. The roar occupies every interval so completely that any heartbeat would have to hide inside it or be erased by it. You walk a long time believing the place is vacant in the strictest sense, a high arithmetic of stone and dimness that has already subtracted every variable.

Then the rumors begin to resolve.

They stand on the ribs of rock the way the ridges themselves stand: not posed, not vigilant, simply arrived at a posture that no longer requires adjustment. At a distance they suggest goats or antelope, the same tired silhouette the mountains have worn down to, but the legs articulate one extra time, a quiet surplus of joint that lets them lock against a wind that never arrives. Their hides are the color of wet slate and old bruise. Rime feathers the northern faces of them and never thickens. They do not graze. They do not turn their heads. The interval between one and the next is the same empty distance that separates boulder from boulder, and they treat that interval as habitat. Their stillness is not patience. Patience still hopes. These have finished hoping in the same era the weather finished arguing. If you approach (and the distances will let you, then refuse to, then let you again), you see that their eyes, when they have them, are the same unspilled beads that swell in the lee of stones. They look at the basin the way the basin looks at itself: without hour, without intent to acquire one.

In the permitted clauses of the river, other lives hold. The pale shapes are not quite fish and not quite lung; they are the water’s decision to remain water. They occupy the dark slicks without rippling them, gill-slits that are also pores in stone, bodies that taste faintly sweet when the cold has soaked all the way through. They do not swim. Swimming would be a verb the place no longer conjugates. Sometimes a thread of current exists for twenty paces and they exist inside it, and when the channel goes dry basalt again they are simply elsewhere, or the same, or stacked a fraction off under the slow glass of the ground. You smell them only when you are already kneeling, and by then the kneeling has begun to feel like one of the permitted clauses too.

The birds are worse because they occupy the lid. Wings open, never traveling, they are pinned shadows against a sky that is already the inside of a mineral. They live at the exact frequency your ribs have learned. Their flight is prepaid, included in the roar the way the sound of an unfallen bead is already included. They do not land. Landing would complete something.

These are the obvious ones, the exceptions the land is still deciding whether to keep. The deeper inhabitants are harder to separate from the finished condition itself.

They occupy the doorways. Not standing in them so much as continuing the idea of passage the lintels remember. Two uprights and a capstone frame a rectangle of slightly different dimness, and inside that rectangle a density gathers that is almost a figure, almost the memory of a room, almost a person who has forgotten whether they were entering or leaving and then forgotten the difference. No face, or a face that is only the sky they frame. Lichen maps them in rust and ash-green. They have the long accepted profiles of things ground down until grinding and ground agreed. You can walk through; the density parts like cold air and then is there again behind you, unchanged. They do not watch you leave. Watching would be a change in the light, and the light here is equilibrium.

Along the cliff-spirals something follows the groove without progressing. The cut is as weathered as the uncut stone; you cannot tell incision from anatomy, and the inhabitant is the same ambiguity given just enough will to keep circling a center that refuses to sit where the curve requires. It lives in the refusal. Its movement, if it is movement, has the patience of geology and the sound of the roar. Sometimes, in the seams where two slopes meet, you glimpse the same spiral in the second basin, its inhabitant a fraction colder, its doorway standing in a slightly different place. They do not acknowledge one another. Acknowledgment would be an event.

The leaning cairns are sleepers, or the sleepers are cairns. Flat stones stacked in a tilt that does not quite obey down, and the tilt has always been that tilt. If you wait long enough for waiting to become decorative, you may see that the stones breathe the way rime breathes: a held note, a swelling that never breaks. They dream the stack. They are the residue of an arranging that is no longer occurring, the way salt is the residue of a tide. No tools among them, no cut younger than the mountain. Whatever they were before they became this, they weathered into permanence around the becoming and then stopped.

There are smaller ones still. In the viscous dark of the deepest cuts, where the loudness is neither muffled nor increased, lives collect like condensation that has decided never to run. They have the look of clenched moss, blackened and brittle, or of fists of iron wire that forgot they were shrubs. They cling to cracks that hold a teaspoon of damp. Their metabolism is the settled cold. A few of them are shadows that arrived first; the stones have not yet caught up, and the shadows live in that slight offset, feeding on the delay in depth perception, on the far ridge that is suddenly under your hand and then far again. They are the breakdown incorporated, thoroughly, a long time ago.

None of them eat in any way that would constitute an event. They take the metal taste of the thin air, the faint sweetness of the seeps, the prepaid roar. They take the emptiness itself, which is the finished condition and therefore inexhaustible. They do not reproduce. They do not die. Those are processes, and process is the one erosion this height no longer permits. Time is a surface here, frosted, and they live on it the way lichen lives on stone: mapped, thin, almost stain. A footprint they leave (if the dust allows it) looks as new a year later and also looks as if it had always been about to be there. Their own forms have that same quality: decided, held, a continuous fact.

If they have a collective name it is not spoken. The roar was already saying it. Your shout, if you make one, is taken in as confirmation: the place was already that sound, and they were already inside it. They do not learn you. They do not forget you. Need would be a change.

Walk the recursive openness long enough and you begin to understand they are not inhabitants so much as the basin’s way of having not been entirely alone when it seized. Reality broke just far enough, then weathered itself smooth around the break, and these are the smooth places that still remember having been something that could break. They are the extra joint, the unspilled bead, the doorway that frames a room that both was and was never. They persist the way the ridges persist: ridge behind ridge, each one the same age as the last, worn to the same tired angle.

You can leave. They will not notice the difference. Noticing would require the dimness to lift, the cold to trend, the water to choose to fall. The high places do not recede. They simply continue, and what lives in them continues with them, loudly, forever, already finished.
