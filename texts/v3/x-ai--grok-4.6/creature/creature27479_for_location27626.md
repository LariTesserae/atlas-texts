The heat has been keeping them longer than the stone has been keeping its shape. They do not come from elsewhere; they precipitate. What lives here is the basin remembering how to hold itself, the same way a bruise remembers the blow after the skin has already changed color.

At first they are only a reluctance in the water. A sheet of condensate that should have fallen from a high lip simply does not. It thickens, milkier than the rest, and the milkiness acquires a slow internal weather—currents that are not quite currents, densities that refuse to dilute. You would miss them if you were still using eyes the way eyes were meant to be used. They occupy the same register as the migrating puddles: present, then not, then present again a little to the left of where the ground insists they ought to be. Distance does not bind them any more firmly than it binds the façades.

When they choose to gather, they gather the way mineral crust gathers, flake by patient flake. The body (if the word can be stretched that far) is a packed, weeping agglomeration of dissolved masonry, pale flour of old walls, and the metallic tea of the channels. It stands the way the isolated masses stand: shouldered, pitted, fluted by the same weather that has been working the city since before corners were forgotten. Edges are suggestions. Faces, when they occur, are the faces of wet stone that has decided, for a century or two, to look back. They do not have mouths. Speech here is the sound the stone already makes—ticking, settling, the low unvoiced syllable of steam—and they add to it only a slight extra weight, a structural complaint that lasts a little longer than geology strictly requires.

They prefer the inward courts, the lowest and wettest, where the script of absence is densest and the rewriting never pauses. There the air is more body than air, and they are more air than body. A single inhabitant may occupy an entire plaza-shelf for a span that would empty calendars, standing so still that lichen-tired growths colonize the lee of its knees the way they colonize any other wall. Then, without announcement, it takes the city’s own slow step: a sloughing, a rearrangement of mass no wider than a finger, and the ground where it stood weeps a darker stain that itself begins to migrate. Sometimes the step carries it through one of the darker rectangles that are not quite doors. It does not enter an interior. It folds. You may later find the same density of weeping stone looking back at you from an angle the street did not possess a moment earlier, or reflected in a puddle that shows a complete and impossible sky.

They do not eat. They exchange. Heat rises through them the way it rises through the dish of the world; water enters and leaves them in the same unfinished cycle that never quite becomes rain or river. What they keep is the interval—the unused space between masses, the rationed presence. They are the city’s habit of arrangement continuing after intention has dissolved. Their thoughts (again the word must be stretched until it thins) move at the speed of erosion. A consideration may last the time it takes a colonnade to lean a degree closer to attending. They do not hunt or flee or gather in the way anything that ever learned animals would understand. The hush never learned them either; they are a different grammar, older, mineral, written in drips.

Sometimes one of them is indistinguishable from a ruin until the ruin inhales. The honeycombed surface draws a long, steaming breath and the pits briefly fill with a milkier light, as if the stone were remembering a sun it never had and then politely forgetting again. They have no use for the slightly-less-dark that sometimes loosens the vapor. In the tighter dark they are more themselves: the heat closer, the walls leaning not in threat but in recognition. The direction is always inward. They are walking the same long agreement the city walks, dissolving and reforming, thinning the way a thought thins when held too long without light, and yet never quite vanishing, because the basin has not finished giving its stored heat back.

If you stay until the ground is more water than you accounted for, you may notice that the air against your skin has begun to bead with a slightly heavier, more deliberate wet. That is as close as they come to greeting. They do not mind you. They simply fail to share your geometry, and the failure is not unkind. It is tenure. The city is still being written, drip by drip, and they are both the hand and the page, the absence and the mineral, the heat that has nowhere else to go. They will still be rearranging after the last isolated mass has been read back into flour and the last channel has forgotten it was ever a street. The place does not end. Neither do they. They only continue the sentence.
