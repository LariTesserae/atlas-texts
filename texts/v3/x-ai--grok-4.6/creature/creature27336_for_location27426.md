They are the walls that breathe. Not metaphor: each pale segment is both tenant and masonry, a living brick whose calcified jacket is secreted in the same slow pulse that once built its own gut. Finger-length, they stack without mortar, bodies nested like vertebrae that forgot they belonged to a single spine. The uppermost wear thinner skins, almost translucent, so the mineral snow of the new ceiling shows through them as faint internal weather. Lower ones have thickened into load-bearing trunks, their segments fused into columns that still twitch when a neighbor above shifts weight.

They do not have names for themselves. Distinction arrives only as pressure gradients and the taste of another’s leachate. A hunter the color of old ivory is simply a length that has chosen motility over architecture; it unspools from an aperture, crosses three stories of living façade on silent, articulated feet, then pours itself into a different opening and becomes wall again. The hunted do not run. They invert. A section of street becomes a ceiling; the communal gut that was thoroughfare now faces inward, offering the hunter only another set of irising mouths. Nothing is wasted. The hunter that fails is reabsorbed by morning, its calcium recast as a new balcony.

Their eyes are not eyes. Photoreceptive patches lie beneath the mineral skin, so light is felt as a burn rather than seen. Brighter than the chamber’s sourceless pewter and the entire city contracts, streets folding into themselves until the pocket looks empty, only wet stone and the slow rain of limestone flakes. They prefer the dark the way lungs prefer air. Sound is equally foreign; the water is too young, too heavy with dissolved rock, to carry vibration far. Communication travels as shared pulse through connected bodies, a pressure change you feel against your own skin before you understand it has passed. It is the city’s one verb: stay, grow, fuse, recede.

Time inside them is local and thick. A gallery that did not exist when you entered may already be two stories by the time you turn your head, yet no individual has hurried. Growth is simply the continuation of the same event that tore the chamber open. They are not colonizing the void; they are the void deciding, at last, what density it prefers. Distances warp accordingly. An alley two body-lengths long can take the better part of an hour to traverse because every surface is also a citizen asserting claim, and the water between them has begun to remember being stone.

They notice you first as heat. Your joints still carry the memory of a warmer world. The nearest façades iris shut. A communal pulse travels the connected lattice, too quiet for ears, felt only as a change in the pressure against your skin. Weather. Meal. Unfinished geology. The three categories have not yet finished separating. The city has not decided which you are, only that you are an anomaly the water has not yet metabolized.

When the crack above you narrows, they will still be building. A new thoroughfare will have fused with its neighbor. An older corridor will have been quietly eaten and recast. They require no witness. They are simply what the water, the raw stone, and the unspeaking lives agreed to become in the hours after the rock finished tearing.
