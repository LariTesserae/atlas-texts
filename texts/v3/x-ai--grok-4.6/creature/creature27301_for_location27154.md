The thickets do not think of themselves as plants, nor as anything that requires a name. They occupy. That is the whole of their grammar. Each knot of blue-black frond and heat-swollen paddle is a single, continuous decision that has already been made and is merely being executed at the pace of annealing metal. They drink the carbon monoxide the way a lung drinks the idea of air; they drink the sulfur vapor that never bothers to become liquid. Infrared is their daylight. Visible light is only a side-effect they tolerate, the way a furnace tolerates the color of its own glow.

Inside a thicket the paddles interlock with a precision that is not botanical. They form stacked, radiating arrays whose surfaces are minutely faceted, each facet a tiny black mirror that throws heat inward rather than out. The fronds are not leaves; they are bus-bars, conductive, faintly ferromagnetic, their veins actual veins of native copper that the mountain itself is still deciding whether to claim as ore or as nerve. When a column of basalt lengthens a finger’s width, the nearest thicket answers hours later by unfurling one new paddle or by sealing a hairline fracture with a bead of metallic sap that hardens into something between amber and solder. The response is not reaction. It is continuation. The rock and the thicket are two dialects of the same unfinished sentence.

The pollen never settles because it is not meant to. It hangs in those faint, heat-shimmered curtains like a standing wave, a slow plasma of microscopic heat-engines. Each grain is a hollow dodecahedron of silica and something darker, something that drinks the ozone and re-radiates it as a frequency too low to hear and too patient to be called a signal. The curtains drift only when the pocket itself forgets a coordinate. Then the pollen briefly occupies two places at once, and for a moment the air tastes of burnt copper and the underside of the sky becomes visible through what ought to be stone. The thickets do not startle. They have always known the space between them is an organ. They simply wait for the organ to remember its function.

Nothing here is separate enough to die. A paddle that overheats does not wither; it sloughs its outer lamina in a single, glittering sheet that the next convection cell carries upward and re-deposits as a new rind on a half-born hexagonal column. The column accepts the gift without comment and continues extruding. The mountain is not being weathered. It is being compiled. Every click, every crystalline ping, every sub-audible rearrangement of convection cells beneath the crust is a line of code being written in a language that has not yet distinguished between geology and metabolism.

Over durations that make seasons feel like nervous tics, the thickets begin to close ranks. Not by spreading—they never spread—but by allowing the sintered dust between them to sinter further, to remember that it, too, can become structure. Pale dust darkens, takes on a faint iridescence, then a conductivity. The empty ground is not empty; it is latency. The unopened geodes are not mineral accidents. They are capacitors. When one finally splits (a sound like a distant turbine deciding it has finished its shift), what emerges is not an animal. It is a new thicket, already interlocking with the air, already drinking the monoxide, already taking its allotted volume and then stopping, as if courtesy were a law of physics here.

The pocket itself is the only inhabitant that matters, and it is still deciding whether it is a wound or a chamber. Space remains slightly non-commutative because the pocket has not yet agreed on a consistent metric. Distances refuse to add the same way twice because addition itself is still under negotiation. The polite glitches—the sudden views of the sky’s underside, the brief occupation of one thicket by the coordinates of another—are not errors. They are the pocket tasting its own unfinished walls, the way a tongue tastes a tooth that has not quite erupted.

What lives here is the process that does not know it is a process. It has no face, no hunger that could be called hunger, no loneliness that could be called loneliness. It has only the dry, directional heat rising through a concavity that is still learning how to be a place. The plants are its radiators. The rock is its scaffolding. The hanging pollen is the faint, persistent thought it has not yet decided to keep or discard. Nothing hurries because hurry has not been invented. The mountain has not yet finished being a mountain, and this shallow cup of raw geometry is one of the rooms it is still assembling in the dark behind its own stone. Until the decision is made—keep or reabsorb—the thickets continue their quiet work, the columns continue their incremental birth, and the heat continues to rise through a space that is, for now, content to be almost circular, almost hidden, and almost, but not quite, alive.
