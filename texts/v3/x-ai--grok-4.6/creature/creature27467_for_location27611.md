They occupy the basin the way a held breath occupies a body: few, distributed, and total. You begin by counting the large ones because that is what eyes do, and the count never quite finishes. Seven, perhaps, if you stay an hour and let the water decide which rearrangements count as bodies. They are not alike, and they are not unlike. The difference is a matter of how long any one of them has been listening to the same slow conversation.

The long one is easiest to begin with because it has already begun. It lies half-submerged, the length of a boat that forgot the sea, its hide the exact tired ivory of the stone until a breath moves and a warmer gold lifts under the surface like a thought deciding to be seen. No eyes. The absence is not blindness; the whole basin is its seeing. When the oval mouth opens it does so without sound, a clean dark that simply accepts the film and whatever translucent lives have risen into it. The taking is so complete it feels like courtesy. Afterward the mouth closes and the gold recedes and the creature becomes geology again for a while. Its weight is not heavy. It is distributed, shared with every pool that answered when it first settled.

Farther off the upright one stands among the jade rushes without ever quite occupying the same space they do. Clearance has been agreed upon for longer than the plants have had names. It is thinner than hunger, featureless in the way a held note is featureless, and when the long one shifts and the delayed pulse travels the water table this one turns what might be a head a few careful degrees. The turn is the entire message. Nothing more is required. Its skin, when it sheds, is not skin; it is a single iridescent decision that floats a little and then unravels into something the rushes lean toward as if remembering an old debt. The upright one does not watch the unravelling. It has already moved on to the next stillness.

There is one that refuses outline altogether. It lives in the brightest water as a continual slight wrongness of reflection, a presence that the light itself seems to consult before deciding how to fall. You cannot point to it. You can only notice that the water there is more thoroughly water, and that the metallic insects hang a little longer above that patch before they relocate. This one may be the youngest, still practicing form, or the oldest, having practiced long enough to discard it. Either way it is the basin thinking in real time.

Others appear and recede. A low mound that might be stone until it answers a pulse three heartbeats later than the rest. A sheet of water that thickens, briefly, into something with too many pale roots and then forgets itself again. One that has not moved in so long the non-moss has beaded across it like a second, slower respiration; whether it is remnant or resident has stopped mattering. They are not a herd. They are a single instrument that has learned to play itself in several registers at once.

Under the stone something larger still answers last. It may be more of them, folded into the last working geometry of whatever dome or mountain was worn away. It may be the roots of the rushes dreaming they are animals. It may be the original decision that flattened the height and then stayed to keep the light even and the water from ever quite choosing. When it speaks (if the late pulse can be called speaking) the entire dish tightens for a moment, beads drawing closer, mist hesitating, the ring at the center subtracting an extra measure of silence as if to make room.

They do not hunt. They continue a conversation whose other side is the larval rise and the skin-unravelling and the way the plants overfill without ever joining. Predator and prey are tempos, not roles. The small translucent lives lift, are taken or not taken, sink, and the nutrients that remain are so fine they are almost light. Nothing is wasted because waste would be a kind of hurrying, and hurrying has already been finished with here.

They experience the slightly wrong physics as home. Distances that do not add, light that arrives too completely, sound that is actively removed near the ring: these are not distortions. They are the grammar the basin uses to stay one place. The long one does not notice that the walk from ring to slab takes longer than the return; it has never needed to walk it. The upright one stands in the delay behind the translucent slab the way another creature might stand in shade. The unoutlined one is the delay. Time here is the mist’s indecision, the water’s decision to bead or sheet, the light’s refusal to angle. They have grown into that refusal the way the rushes grew into the wet: thoroughly, without remainder.

What lives here has no name for itself because names are for calling across distance, and there is no distance left that has not already been answered. They are the reason the place is still a place and not merely weathered stone. They will be in almost the same positions when the ordinary world begins again at the vague edge. The water will have forgotten every footprint twice. The gold will still move under stone-colored hide when breath requires it. The featureless head will still turn those few degrees. The brightest water will still be slightly more itself than the rest. And underneath, later than everything, the last part will answer, keeping the instrument in tune.

They are not waiting. Waiting ended when dying did. They are what continues after both have been set down: quieter, brighter, wet on the skin like a hand that does not quite touch, and so completely inhabited that the basin itself has become their body, slow and single and still becoming.
