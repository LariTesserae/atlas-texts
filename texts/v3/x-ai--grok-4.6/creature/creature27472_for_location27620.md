They are few enough that the hollow seems, at first, almost uninhabited. You notice them only when a balcony that was not there a moment ago frames one of them, or when a walkway that has just finished rearranging itself deposits another, already walking as if the new alignment had always been intended. Their number never quite resolves. Sometimes you count seven; later, in the same span of light, you count eleven and cannot say where the others came from or whether they were always present in the rooms that exist only while regarded.

They wear the place. The garments are the same poured night as the walls, but thinner, almost membranous, matte and faintly refractive. They drink the mineral damp as it beads, so that the wearers remain dry while a faint white bloom appears on everything they have recently touched and then fades. The cloth has no visible seams or fastenings; it simply continues, adjusting its fall whenever a street forgets its previous geometry. Underneath, the bodies are spare, elongated in ways that do not quite match any single species’ proportion. Limbs too many-jointed or too few, depending on the hour. Skin the color of the lichen, parchment that has been written on and then sloughed. Faces that hold still until you look away; when you look back the arrangement of features has shifted by a degree, as though a later or earlier version has stepped forward.

They move with the same peristaltic patience as the conduits. No hurry, because hurry would require a consistent now. They step from a threshold that is still becoming a wall into a corridor that opens onto itself three minutes damper, and they do not comment. Their competence is the competence of those who long ago stopped treating discrepancy as error. When they must speak, the words arrive late, carrying a faint mineral taste, as if the air has been instructed to remember vibration only after a delay. The speech itself is spare, almost never more than a single phrase, and even that seems addressed as much to the stone as to one another. Sometimes the lichen answers, briefly displaying a fragment of the same delayed sentence before powdering it away.

Higher up, on ledges the buildings have not yet decided whether to absorb, the pale many-jointed thing continues its unhurried traverse. It is thinner than any metabolism would seem to require, a sequence of pale articulations that catch the filtered light and hold it a moment too long. It never descends. It may be feeding on the unfinished quartz in the air, or it may be the air’s way of growing a body. The inhabitants do not look at it often. When they do, their delayed words sometimes include a term that might be a name or might be a measurement of remaining incompletion. The thing does not acknowledge them. It has not yet been assigned a reason to.

Near the vents the wiry shrubs persist, their rime-edged leaves marking time by relocating. The inhabitants sometimes pause beside them, not to tend but to wait, as if the shrubs were slower versions of themselves. A hand (or what serves as a hand that hour) may rest on a branch; when the hand lifts, the branch has already decided to be half a meter leftward, and the garment has already drunk the new damp that decision produced.

At the cooling plaza the lattices click their soft, thought-changing clicks. The figures who pass through the only open space do not linger. They treat the unfinished fountain-or-statue-or-nothing with the same unhurried courtesy they show the rest of the hollow: an acknowledgment that something is still arriving, and that they themselves are part of what has not yet finished deciding. Their shadows, detaching and arriving early, sometimes remain in the plaza after the bodies have gone, thin exploratory shapes that the lattices appear to measure before the paving remembers to be solid again.

No one crosses the lip. The unfinished stone simply stops, and so do they. They deepen instead, folding more carefully into the space already occupied, their garments and their delayed voices and their slightly wrong joints becoming another layer of the city’s infrastructure. They are waiting, or they are the waiting. The rock continues to arrive, or continues not to. Either way they remain, few and quiet, drinking the damp, speaking late, letting the hollow transcribe them as it transcribes everything else that has not yet been required to finish.
