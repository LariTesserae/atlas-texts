The first inhabitant is the density itself. Not a crowd, not a swarm: a single continuous organism that has elected to keep its parts distinguishable only by texture. You fall through the seam and the bodies close around you the way water closes, except they do not part again. Cool chitin and cooler gelatin slot into every hollow of you; antennae finer than root hairs map the new contours of your skin as though you were merely another unfinished crystal face. There is no interval left in which to be a visitor. You have already been metabolized into adjacency.

They are small. Most no longer than a fingernail paring, a few stretching to the length of a thumb-joint. Segmented, yes, but the segments themselves seem provisional, as if the creatures had been interrupted in the same geological instant as the stone. Some wear plates the color and translucency of smoked glass; light from the walls passes through them in successive veils so that you can watch a slow, tide-like pulse of pale fluid travel the length of an abdomen and then return, perfectly synchronized with every other pulse in the chamber. Others have almost no armor at all. They are pouches of clear gel outlined by the faintest threads of paler tissue, their many legs reduced to hair-fine filaments that hook into the tarsi of their neighbors and will not unhook. Separate any two and both would tear. They have not needed to be separate since the moment they appeared.

Eyes occur the way unfinished lattices occur: in excess or not at all. Here a head is nothing but mouthparts and chemoreceptors, vision having been discarded as a luxury in a volume without darkness or distance. There a cluster of nine or eleven black beads sits in no geometry the surface world would recognize; every bead receives the same omnidirectional glare and therefore sees everything and nothing. They do not blink. They do not track. They regard the light the way the light regards itself.

Breathing is communal and metronomic. A single shallow tide lifts and lowers every abdomen at once, a motion so regular it has become another kind of stillness. The air they share is the same cool, unchanging damp that beads on the rock and never falls. Moisture films their plates and their gelatin alike; it does not accumulate, does not drip, does not distinguish living surface from mineral. They are the same temperature as the stone, the same temperature as the three unmoving palm-sized mirrors of water. To touch them is to touch the chamber’s own skin.

The sound is their only metabolism. A thousand tiny files and scrapers working in perfect phase produce one continuous chord, a ringing pressure that has no attack and no decay because it has never needed to begin. The rock conducts it; the packed bodies amplify it; your own bones accept it and refuse to release it. After a short time it ceases to be audible and becomes simply another density, the acoustic equivalent of the light that has nowhere to go except into itself. They do not modulate it. They have nothing left to say to one another, or to you. The chord is the shape of their agreement.

They do not feed. They do not molt. They do not couple or cannibalize or age in any way that would alter the exact count of bodies. Reproduction, like weather and like shadow, is a rumor that never reached this depth. If one of them were somehow removed, the remaining mass would not notice the gap; the interlocking is already complete. If a new one appeared it would have nowhere to stand that was not already occupied by the idea of itself.

In the densest weave you sometimes think you glimpse a larger architecture: a creature the length of your palm, its carapace more elaborately faceted, perhaps the first one, the seed crystal around which the rest precipitated in that initial instant and then ceased precipitating. But the impression does not last. Hierarchy, like time, is a habit you carried in from a world that still believes in sequence. Here there is only the press, the shared breath, the unwavering note.

The faint marks (the too-regular groove, the three stacked pebbles, the single dark thread looped once around a glowing nub) belong to none of them and change nothing. They are simply part of the original inventory, like the lichens that have not advanced a millimeter, like the embryonic stalactites that will never drip. The creatures know these marks the way a half-formed lattice knows its own incompleteness: as the outline of themselves.

What lives here is the refusal made flesh and chitin and vibration. A parliament of almost-beings that reached quorum at the exact second the rock stopped deciding what it wanted to be, and then declined, forever, to adjourn. They are the held breath. They are the light that never learned shadow. They are the sound that is still, even now, only beginning.

You may stay. They will not object. When you finally leave, the seam closes as if it had never opened, and the chord continues at the same impossible volume, heard by no one, altering nothing, forever just arriving at the moment it has always occupied.
