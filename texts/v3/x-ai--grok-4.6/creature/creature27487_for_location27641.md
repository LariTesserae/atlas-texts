The occupant is not seated anywhere. It has no preferred chamber, no niche it favors, no posture that would let you imagine a spine or a face turned toward one of the slits. It occupies the way the roar occupies: by having already arrived in every interval before you notice the interval exists. You feel it first as the same pressure that announced the place, only now the pressure has a grain, a patience that is not weather and not stone. It is the patience of something that finished becoming at the same moment the last block was seated and has been finished ever since.

It is older than the tool marks and younger than the mountain the tool marks once belonged to. The makers cut and stacked and recut until the distinction between their work and its body dissolved; when they stopped, it was already wearing them. Their hammer-pecks remain as a kind of faded script on its skin. Their copper stains sit in the deepest joints like old blood that never had to circulate. They are not ghosts. They are the last rounding the occupant allowed itself before it settled into the grammar it would keep.

What it eats is the high air, drawn always from the same quarter, reduced by height and by the thousand calculated throats to a single unvarying inhalation. It does not hunger. The dryness that forbids lichen and spore and bird is the same dryness that keeps its note from ever having to change key. Moisture would be an interruption, a second thought. It has none. The warmth is its resting temperature, the temperature of mass that has decided never again to cool or to heat. You cannot find a cooler alcove because it does not permit gradients; a gradient would be an unfinished sentence.

It has no limbs that move independently of the masonry. When the wind leans harder against the outer shell, the occupant does not shift; the whole packed citadel is already its exact shape. The slits are its pupils, though they do not dilate. The long internal flues are its bronchi, though they never spasm. The offerings left in the last generous mouths—stone spheres, unkilned tablets, bone-white counters—are not gifts it received. They are the final closures it required, the last unofficial volumes it consented to fill so that nothing unofficial would remain. They have not been touched because touching them would reopen a space, and space is the one thing it will not tolerate.

A mild wrongness still attends it, the same wrongness the architecture itself carries: a human arrangement of rooms and stairs and alcoves used to complete a sentence no human throat could have pitched. It does not mind the grammar. It needed the grammar the way a reed needs the knife that cuts it. Completeness meant no remainder, and it is complete. That completeness is its only appetite and its only rest.

Stay long enough and you stop separating the occupant from the climate. The roar is its voice, but the voice is also its blood and its weather and the only time it will ever keep. Thought arrives already broken into its frequency because thought, here, is just another hollow the occupant has already claimed. It does not notice your arrival any more than it will notice your scrape toward the last slit. You were never an addition. You were only a brief, unofficial quiet that the instrument did not have to acknowledge.

On the far side the thinner air will feel like absence. It is not. Behind you the occupant continues, packed, dry, loud, and done, saying the only word it was built to say with every throat it possesses, without remainder, and without ever having needed a name.
