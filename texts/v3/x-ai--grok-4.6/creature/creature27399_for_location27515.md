The hollow is first a single creature, older than the floodplain that forgot it. The sphagnum-liverwort-root mat is not a floor but a hide, a continuous hydraulic lung the size of the basin itself. It inhales the distant engines and exhales them as warmth and bead-sweat. Its vertical skin of fused fronds and aerial roots weeps without pause, each drop a heartbeat delayed by centuries. Nothing in it strives; the architecture was finished long ago. The few pillar-stems are not trees but thickened nerves, bark already more moss than wood, conducting the same slow pulse that flexes the peat underfoot.

On that living hide the frogs keep watch. They are the color of wet bark because they have become the color of the walls they never leave. Hundreds, perhaps thousands, cling lidless and unstartled, throats swelling and collapsing in exact counter-rhythm to the industrial thrum that never rises or falls. Their eggs are laid in the tea-pools as though the numbers were already known; tadpoles emerge, adults die, and the count remains what it was. They do not hunt with hunger or flee with fear. They simply occupy the frequency that the hollow has chosen.

Along the pillar-stems the beetles process. Their elytra catch the green-gold light and throw it back in oil-slick flashes that never quite become decoration. Legs click against living bark with the regularity of small, well-oiled mechanisms that have forgotten they were ever alive in any other way. They move in unhurried files, never colliding, never hurrying, as if the path itself were a closed circuit already walked. Midges hang in the still air as stationary weather, clouds that neither thicken nor thin, each insect a fixed point in the hollow’s unchanging ledger.

Deeper, in the thicker peat, something warm-blooded shifts. It is never seen, only felt as the same rustle, always the same rustle, a body the size of a folded child or a large hare turning once in its sleep and then still again. The peat closes over it as if the movement had been anticipated for years. It breathes the same warm, rust-tinged moisture the plants drink. Whatever it is has no need of seasons or of nights that matter; its blood has already learned the engines’ tempo.

In the shallow pools the water itself lives. Protozoa and filamentous bacteria turn the rust-note into chemistry the plants can keep. Larvae of things that will never leave the hollow drift like slow punctuation. Nothing arrives from outside that the hollow does not already own. The membrane of roots at the far side is not an exit but another surface of the same skin; light and thrum pass through it unchanged.

Stand long enough and the distinction between inhabitant and place dissolves. The warmth matches skin. Moisture condenses on whatever enters as readily as on leaf or frog. The frogs watch. The beetles click. The unseen thing rustles once more, identically. The engines continue. The hollow simply holds what it has always held: a closed, sweating, perfectly timed stillness that has already weathered into its final shape and will weather no further.
