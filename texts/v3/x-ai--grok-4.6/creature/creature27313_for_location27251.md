The traces do not announce a visitor so much as they confess that vacancy was briefly, impolitely, attempted. A mucus-thread cools along a fibrous stem like a second, slower water, mineral-sweet when the tongue of a frond tests it; it lasts a season and then is only a cooler memory the plants spend. A crescent bite in a gelatinous leaf pearls at the edges before the next silt-thread arrives, the plant sealing itself with the same patience it uses to refuse an aisle. The shed thing—thumbnail, translucent, ridged like a frozen knuckle or a scale that forgot which animal it belonged to—hangs a while, already half-digested, already deciding to become another stem. These are not signs of hunger. Hunger here would look like subtraction, and the pocket cannot afford that arithmetic. They are signs of passage: something that arrives after weather has forgotten how to count, stays only long enough to insist, and leaves the density slightly more itself.

It does not come from the seam. The seam is too new, too sharp, still remembering it was once a door that had not been a door. The visitor comes the way the water came: already occupying the dark on the other side of a geometry that has not yet decided to close. One moment the packed fronds are only themselves, stem to stem, residual grey coin by coin. The next, a pressure that is not quite a hand, not quite a tide, occupies several volumes at once. Nothing here is single. The visitor arrives as a procession that is also a single cooler density, a chain of selves sharing one outline the way the plants share one another’s. You know it first by the way the residual noon in the fronds spends itself a little faster, as if something is borrowing the last of the inherited light to remember how seeing used to work.

Its body—if body is the word that survives the water—is pale the way bone is pale when it has never been asked to be a bone, or the way a lung is pale when it has been turned inside out and taught to breathe liquid. Segments or not-quite-segments, ridged where the shed things once fitted, gelatinous where it must part the abrasive hair-stems, fibrous where it must grip the fracture-walls that have not yet learned to be old. It has no front that remains front. Up and down trade duties through it without announcement; a ridge that was a spine becomes a fringe of cooler tendrils that are briefly indistinguishable from the plants, then gathers itself again into a thickness that occupies the space of several. Length is a rumor that fails after an arm’s length and then continues anyway, folding through the same volume twice, so that the visitor is both arriving and already leaving, both the knuckle-sound against wet wood and the silence that follows. Time is poorly mixed inside it as well. A mucus-trail it laid a moment ago is already ancient when its own hinder parts pass; a bite it has not yet taken has already begun to pearl.

It moves by insisting, which is the only locomotion the pocket permits. There is no corridor. It does not swim; swimming would require a surface or a current, and both have been edited out. It presses. Fronds yield with the sound of a pressure-change, close again behind with the patience of refusal, and in the closing they take a little of it with them—a cooler slick, a ridged flake, a memory of noon spent too quickly. The visitor does not fight the packing. It participates. Vacancy is rudeness, and it has not come to be rude. Where the vegetation is already too much, it becomes more of the too-much: its outlines grow into the plants’ outlines until outline is only a courtesy the water extends and then withdraws. Sometimes it thins into the density that is the same density seen from inside a leaf, and for a span it is only another cooler thought the pocket is thinking. Then it gathers, insists again, and a new hinge of rust or a brick with a thumbprint glaze finds itself considered, briefly, as a possible wall or a possible seed of a room that will not be allowed to happen.

It does not feed in any way that subtracts. The bite-marks are more like signatures, or like the way one occupancy acknowledges another. It tastes the forgotten green, the silt becoming plant, the imported fragments of some other room the ground took a mouthful of when it split. The built things do not belong to it any more than they belong to the pocket; they are simply caught, and the visitor, being itself a kind of catching, pauses at them. A corroded hinge may be worn for a while as a second, useless joint. The metal ring, already grown through by a stem, may be occupied as an instruction the visitor briefly obeys, circling itself until the geometry forgets which circle came first. Then it moves on, leaving the object slightly more digested by the general lushness, slightly less required to be what it was.

There is almost no mind that looks like mind. Intelligence here is occupancy, and the visitor is fluent in it. It does not make. It does not knot or cut or light. It does not harvest. If it remembers the air-names of things, it spends those memories the way the plants spend their noon—coin by coin, until only the climate of darkness remains. What it knows is the politics of the small hidden full dark: that every presence must occupy the space of several, that the water is both medium and citizen, that the pocket is too newly torn to afford an empty gesture. When it stays long enough to be subject to the rules, it begins to be packed in turn. Not eaten. Included. Its ridges loosen into the residual grey. Its mucus becomes another cooler stem. A portion of it may remain when the rest insists onward, already ancient in its habits, already pressing for room, so that the visitor is both the rare arrival and one of the plants that have always been forming.

On the slowest cycle the fracture-edges shed another nail’s thickness of themselves and the vegetation takes the new volume before the water finishes arriving. Sometimes the visitor is there for that enlargement, occupying the fresh wound as if it had been standing in the dark on the other side all along. Sometimes it is only the traces, the almost-absence with a shape, and the pocket widens without it, already too much, already not obeying the shapes that made it. If you were still inside—hanging, parted and closed around, geometry refusing to finish—you would not see it arrive or leave. You would feel the pressure change, the slight extra coolness, the fronds yielding and then refusing more completely than before. You would know, by the way your own outline had begun to occupy the space of several, that something had passed that was never quite single, never quite gone, and that the hidden full dark had, for a span longer than weather and shorter than geology, been even fuller than a sea. 

The ground above still looks like any damp hollow. A person could walk over it and feel only that the earth gave a little. They would never know they had crossed the place where a visitor that is also a procession, a density that is also a climate, comes when it comes, insists when it must, and leaves the pocket exactly as it must be: recently opened, already ancient, all occupancy, quietly incorrect, and unwilling to be named.
