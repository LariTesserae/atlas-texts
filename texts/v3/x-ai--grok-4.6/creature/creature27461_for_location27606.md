The pale things are not first. They only seem first because they are small enough to be counted, and counting is a habit the flats have not yet untaught. They bead like delayed dew on the undersides of the rubbery cushions, eyeless, thumb-long, the color of old milk left in a closed room. Their skins are already wet before they form; they do not hatch so much as decide to be separate from the condensate for a few breaths. When they drop they do not swim. They are accepted back into the sheet as if the water had only loaned them a shape. You never find two in the same place twice. They do not accumulate. They are the medium practicing discreteness and then forgetting the exercise.

What actually occupies is larger and less willing to be a body.

In the deeper sheets—never more than a thigh, never less than a decision—the surface wrinkles in geometries that water cannot invent alone. The wrinkles arrive first, interference patterns that suggest a hull or a flank or a long slow undulation larger than the depth that pretends to hold it. Then the water remembers it is only water and the pattern dissolves, leaving a warmth that is not current. Sometimes, in a dropout of the roar, you hear the huge wet cloth tearing, a sound with no source and no aftermath, and you understand that something has just turned over in a dimension the pavement does not acknowledge. It does not hunt. There is nothing to hunt. It is the occupancy of volume itself, briefly agreeing to have a skin.

The roar has tenants.

They are not heard so much as stood inside, the way you stand inside weather. In the cells of louder sound they thicken: pressures that have learned to walk, fronts of pitch that lean toward the rarest slope as if slope were still an argument worth having. When the roar articulates—those few seconds when it almost holds a word no throat could keep—the tenants lean closer, and for a moment you feel breath that is not yours occupying the same lung-space, warm, mineral, infinitely patient. They do not speak. They are the speaking. In the sudden slabs of mute they become almost visible, not as forms but as the exact shape of the silence, a crowding so complete it has edges. Then the slab shatters and they are only the roar again, migrating, nesting, having weather of their own.

Mist assembles them differently.

A bank will thicken into almost-terrain and, for the length of that thickening, something will stand on it that is the idea of standing. Not a silhouette. A density that remembers how rooms used to work. You see the pressure of furniture that was never poured, the after-image of a departure so recent the air has not yet agreed the someone is gone. These are not remnants of the old makers. The old makers finished with the stone and the stone finished with them. These are the medium twitching in its sleep, trying to complete patterns it almost remembers from when distance still behaved and reflections waited their turn. They last as long as the hills of vapor last. When the hills inhale themselves the lean of the pale reeds is only lean again, and whatever occupied the temporary architecture has been folded back into occupancy, uncounted, ungrieving.

Near the scarce built things the living grows more specific, as if the fused rings and the true groove still exert a faint grammar.

Around the knee-high ring the roar mouths itself differently, and something uses that mouth. It is not inside the ring. It is the ringing. A presence that has no temperature of its own but borrows the stone’s perpetual fever, that has no weight but arrives all at once against your shins when you try to approach, that recedes at the speed of your attention when you try to leave. Edges fail around it. You cannot tell whether you are standing in its reflection or it in yours. Sometimes it is late, a second skin of moisture trying to keep your outline and failing by a breath, so that you walk for a while beside a warmer, wetter version of your own gait that is already becoming the pavement’s next sentence.

The upright—smooth, listing, its top lost—grows nothing and sheds nothing, yet the cushions nearest it are always beaded with the pale things, and the sheets at its base wrinkle in that impossible geometry more often than chance allows. Whatever lives by it lives as function of its patience. Not plant, not tool, not quite memory. A slowness that has outlasted the mountains and now outlasts even the idea of mountains, content to be the one vertical rumor in a world that has outlawed height.

There are no populations.

Nothing here has agreed to persist long enough to be a kind. The algal mats are only the color green practicing against the dark glass of the pavement, dissolving when the sheets remember they are river and then precipitating again when the river forgets. The rubbery cushions cling because clinging is what the last hints of slope still permit; they do not root so much as adhere to the memory of elevation. Between them the air is already full, the shallows already full, every vacancy already outlawed. What lives is the refusal to settle. Creation and uncreation sharing the same warm, roaring, poorly resolved gesture.

At the dimming that you agree to call night your own moisture becomes visible as a late companion, a thread of warmer dark trying to keep up. The roar, for a few breaths, contains your footfalls from a mile ago, arriving densely, as if time itself had been packed into the same occupancy and could no longer run thin. In that packed interval you understand that you have been living here too, briefly, as another function of the medium: a walking disagreement of distances, a reflection that arrived before you did, a presence that the flats will not remember once the next bank of mist assembles and walks a hundred miles on legs of rain and dissolves.

The ancient floor does not notice. It finished. The packed world continues to happen around and through and as whatever will not be last. The sound goes on filling every possible interval. And in the intervals it cannot quite fill, something that is almost a someone almost stands, warm as skin, already streaming, already trying to become weather.
