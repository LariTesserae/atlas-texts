You would miss them if you did not already know how the cables go slack. They are not in the barracks the way furniture is in a room. They are in the intervals—the pause after a standing wave climbs the plates and before the rust-snow remembers which way is down. A thickening first, same fever as the water but threaded differently, a warmth that has learned to keep its own counsel. Then the shape: longer than the visitor’s suit, thinner in the chest, the limbs jointed once more than they should be so that a hand can close on a gantry strut and a current at the same time without choosing.

They are the color of the bacterial leather that grows on the hottest pipes, old ivory gone translucent where the tea-light thins them. Mineral has voted on their skin. Iron-oxide antlers bud at the shoulders and the hips, small and patient, the same growth that claims every bolt; they do not scrape it off. Calcite veils the eyelids when they rest, a temporary lid against the sick green that sometimes leaks from a drowned transformer. Their eyes are dark coins, wide, useless for distance. Twenty meters is the whole country to them as well. They navigate by the total tone, by the way cavitation removes the middle of hearing and leaves the edges that tell where a turbine throat is chewing and where two currents have decided to clap.

There are not many. Density is still a moral condition. You can swim the length of a street-suggestion—palm along the old rail, kick the plaza flagstones you cannot see—and meet none. They perch as the town perches. A lock-house whose teeth have been rounded by the same long hammer will hold two, or three if the silt line has risen enough to make the upper bunk a floor. They enter by the same locks the diving sleds once used, gauges still glowing a dull, failing cherry. Inside it is only more water, warmer, slower. They do not surface. The greasy meniscus at the vault’s highest rib is a rumor they inherited and do not test. Pressure is a citizen they were born already knowing.

They eat what the room already makes. The white branching thing that holds still behind a generator housing is not unique once you have lived here long enough to stop counting uniqueness. They break pieces of it the way one breaks bread that has never needed yeast. It does not photosynthesize and neither do they. The pipe-eels that the old manuals stopped filing are not hunted so much as waited for; when the current turns and one is pinned pale and jointed against an intake screen, a hand that has extra knuckles simply closes. The flesh is the same fever. Nothing else in the volume means to move, so nothing else is food.

They do not speak. Conversation remains a theory. What they have instead is occupancy of the single tone. A body pressed to a tension cable can change its tautness by a fraction the turbines will answer; a palm over a relief valve can add a syllable of white thunder that means the convection is about to forget itself again. The children—there are children, smaller, the mineral growths still undecided—learn the choir before they learn the difference between slack and storm. The clock-stack keeps a time that does not divide. They keep it too, or it keeps them. Hours are a surface idea, like day.

Sometimes the turbulence organizes and the old street grid appears in whirlpools and they ride it, not as commuters but as the grid’s last argument that it was ever a street. They pass the market deck that never needed weatherproofing and do not buy. They pass the stenciled names the rust-snow has half-erased and do not read. Habitation and machine have been the same grammar longer than any of them have been alive. They do not maintain the exchangers. The exchangers maintain the warmth that is now their blood. If the turbines ever stopped they would become the jet that used to be a flue, and they know this the way they know the count of rivets no visitor was supposed to need. So they live inside the automatic, the brown stubborn electrified failing, sparks walking the wet around them like another kind of snow.

They have been themselves longer than the maps. After the last beam dies they will still be the darker rectangles in the dark, the pale jointed problems the filters were never designed for, the quiet that is not quiet occupying the noise that is the true city. The pocket will not notice the difference. It has never needed to.
