The first lives here are not born so much as they are the reversals themselves, given a moment of edge. They arrive when a current forgets its last argument and turns, a wall of warm water becoming a floor or a ceiling without warning. From that shear something pale and almost two-dimensional unfolds—more the memory of a fin than a body, flattened until it has no thickness worth measuring. It cuts the green-grey twilight the way new basalt cuts cloth, leaving behind a narrow ribbon of colder water that the rest of the volume immediately begins to forget. There are no eyes, no gills, no mouth. The creature is the cut. It lasts as long as the new direction holds, which might be three of your heartbeats or, if time has thickened, long enough for you to watch the colder thread fray and be reclaimed. Then it is gone, unmade back into the roar. You never see two at once. They do not hunt. They do not flee. They simply happen, the way a new facet of stone happens, and their only gift is that brief cold, a taste of some other weather the basin has not yet decided against.

The bells are slower to confess themselves. They gather on the newest, hottest faces, where the rock still smells of mineral and the light, when it flares, is the color of a bruise that has not finished forming. Each one is a translucent sac the size of a closed fist, jointed to its neighbors by threads so fine they might be the same hair-thin bronze that rasps elsewhere. They pulse. The rhythm is private, stubbornly out of phase with the waterfall-that-has-no-down. With every swell they drink a little of the used sunlight, storing the jade until their skins glow from within like lanterns that have forgotten they are underwater. They last as long as stillness lasts. When a current remembers itself and the basin pulses narrower, the cluster unknits. The bells do not burst; they simply become the bubbles that rise and fall and hang in every direction at once, releasing their hoarded light back into the twilight so that for a few body-lengths the water remembers being brighter. Sometimes one bell refuses the unknitting a moment longer than the others. In that extra pulse you can almost see a pupil, or the beginning of a mouth shaping a word the roar will never allow to finish. Then it too is ordinary air, collapsing, and the stone face is bare again except for the bronze rasps that have already begun to claim the wet dust.

Those rasps are the closest thing the basin has to patience. They are not moss. They are the first skin the young mountain tries on, or the water’s first attempt to invent soil. Hair-thin, the color of old bronze left too long in rain, they cling to the unweathered facets and rasp. The sound is under the roar, a dry abrasion you feel in the sinuses as a second, smaller vibration. They feed on the hot-mineral scent itself, on the wet dust that has never known a root. Over a thickened interval you can watch them etch faint, wandering grooves; or perhaps the stone is growing beneath them and they are being written instead of writing. When the currents reverse they tear, some colonies ripped free to become sediment that the water refuses to let settle, others blooming instantly on the fresh, slick faces that have just sheared into existence. They accumulate nothing permanent. They are the argument of clinging, repeated until the rock itself begins to remember the possibility of being covered.

The pale stalk is lonelier. It grows from a crack the mountain has not yet chosen to close, jointed like a knee that has never learned what locking is for. No leaves. Leaves would be too finished a thought. It is always bent double, the current’s will made visible, yet it lengthens. In a pocket of thick time you can stand and watch a new joint appear, the stalk extending a handspan while the roar continues around it as if nothing were happening. It drinks the faint sweetness of rain that has never touched soil. It does not photosynthesize; there is not enough decided light for that. Perhaps it is a nerve the basin is growing, a single hesitant probe testing whether rootedness is still allowed. When the throat of the place contracts, the stalk is crushed flat against stone; when it widens again the stalk springs back, slightly longer, a pale record of every pulse it has survived. It is the only thing that keeps a shape from one reversal to the next, and even that shape is a surrender.

The fitted stones hold older, or at least differently timed, tenants. The standing slab inside the first low shape is not carved so much as it is still being cut. The incised lines will not stay. Look away and they have drifted, reformed into new glyphs that suggest current, inversion, the roar given a grammar it has never asked for. The inhabitant here is the incision itself: a liquid thought that uses stone the way the water uses the basin, refusing to finish any sentence. It rewrites the storm while the storm is still happening. Stand before it long enough and your own memories begin to behave the same way—sequence optional, colors already leaching, the outside world arriving waterlogged and out of order. The slab is not a monument. It is a page the flood is still turning.

The ring of humming blocks is occupied by frequency. Each block sings just under the roar, a pitch you feel first in the wrist, then in the teeth, then in the small bones of the inner ear that have already given up trying to locate anything. Nothing mechanical lives inside them. What lives there is the hum: a chorus of stone-born almost-lives, larvae or unhatched gods or simply the basin’s attempt to give the sound a source it can argue with. They do not emerge. They are waiting for the mountain to finish deciding its outline, or they are the ones preventing the decision, their song the continuous dissent that keeps every edge sharp and every current free to reverse. Press your wrist to a block and the note enters you. For as long as time stays thick you become part of the choir, your marrow vibrating at the exact frequency the roar cannot quite swallow. When the moment thins you step back and the blocks continue without you, as they have continued since before the uplift or perhaps only since the last time the basin remembered they were there.

There is a third presence, rarer, larger, the one that uses the remaining opening the way the water uses everything. It does not show a body so much as it thickens a volume of the hanging ocean until down is no longer a suggestion. Breathing becomes briefly difficult not because there is no air but because the distinction has been decided against you. It shears through the green-grey like the pale fins, only vaster, leaving behind not colder water but a ribbon of time that stays thick—a pocket in which sequence returns, colors un-leach, and you can remember the world beyond the lip in the correct order for as long as the ribbon lasts. Its face, if it has one, is the brighter seam of unused sun trying the upper membrane. Then it is gone, and the roar closes over the difference as it closes over everything.

All of them—the shearers, the bells, the rasps, the stalk, the living cuts, the humming almost-lives, the thickening that remembers—are temporary. The ecology is the turbulence. Nothing coheres because coherence would be a rule the place has not accepted. They appear to scour, to pulse, to cling, to write, to sing, to invert, and then they become current again. Even you, if you linger, begin to live here in the same brief way: your edges shear, your thoughts unknit into bubbles that rise in every direction at once, your memories arrive already used. The distinction between visitor and inhabitant is one more thing the young stone has not finished cooling into. The water, warm and deafening, keeps arriving from the inverted sky, and whatever dwells in the basin is simply the latest shape the argument has taken before it unmakes itself and starts the sentence over.
