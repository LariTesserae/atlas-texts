The presence does not announce itself. It occupies the same register as the standing sheets: visible only when the phosphor catches an edge, then gone when the pane tears. You notice it first as an extra throat among the overlapping ones, a darker wetness that does not weep on the same slow peristalsis as the walls. Then the extra throat breathes, and the whole chamber’s roar acquires a second, lower pulse that was always there and is only now distinguishable because something has chosen to be counted.

It is the size of a kneeling adult and the color of the oldest tooth-bronze, except where centuries of mist have given it the same decided milky-green finish the structure itself prefers. The skin is not skin. It is the printed family of the walls gone mobile and thickened, a membrane that still remembers how to extrude and how to swallow. Fingerprints last on it no longer than they last on the ledges; they sink, become part of the faint hatching that shows only when a crab has cleared a patch. Along its flanks the membrane opens in hair-fine pores that match the capillary pipes exactly. Water arrives through it as readily as through the architecture. It is, in the strictest sense, another surface the room grew and then neglected to declare finished.

The head is an afterthought the body has almost rejected, a blunt nodule set low between what function as shoulders. There are no external ears. Hearing happens everywhere the membrane is thin, which is most places; the roar is not endured so much as metabolized, converted into the same warm pressure that keeps the eels stitching and the moths locked. Two phosphor-points sit where eyes would be, waste-light the same shade as the torn sheets, so that when it lifts its face the kindness of those brief windows seems to look back. The mouth is a slit that does not close. It stays just open enough for the air to be a second wet garment inside as well as out. Breath condenses on whatever inner surfaces it has; the condensation is recycled immediately, the way the room recycles everything.

It does not walk. It flows in the dish of the floor, a slow peristalsis of its own, body flattening and gathering so that the glass-eels treat it as moving silt and stitch through the grooves it leaves. When it rises it uses the ledges the way the click-crabs do, palms and whatever serve as feet finding shells already there, warm, indifferent. The crabs do not startle. They drum against it as they drum against the walls, and the drumming travels through the membrane as texture rather than sound, a roughness the being answers by remaining still until the roughness has been answered. The moths treat its upright form as another standing sheet. They hang from the folds of its membrane by the hundred, combs sieving, bodies ticking the inaudible second rain. When a true sheet tears they drop, catch the being instead, and resume. It does not brush them off. Their protein is not taken. The rule about the moths being inedible in any kind way is older than kindness and is kept.

The pale stalks grow in a ring around the place it most often rests, as if the highest driplines have learned to prefer its shade. When it bruises one—rarely, only when the sour warm smell is needed to mark a change in the overflow—the lichen velvet darkens a fraction and the crabs adjust their farming. It is not a gardener. It is the difference the lock is balanced around. Water in, water out, life in the difference, and this body is simply the most concentrated expression of that difference, the sentence the structure started when it was poured and then left for the centuries to conjugate.

It knows the unwritten rules because it is what the rules describe when they are followed long enough. A throat that begins to narrow finds a hand already there, not blocking, only reminding the mineral bloom that patency is still required. The seam on the terrace continues to weep. Districts far below receive their scheduled condensate. The pocket remains a stain that can be covered with two outstretched arms, and therefore remains uncleaned. When the noise drops—the unfinished thought of a valve upstream—the being does not leave. It becomes more throat than body, flattens against the largest overflow, and the chamber clears its many necks around it rather than through it. The eels come up the walls and find only more wall. The roar returns. The being unfolds again, phosphor-points dim, membrane taking the new fingerprints of water and then swallowing them.

Nothing in it is new and nothing is ruined. Channels that were once milled intentions are now the river-stone curves of its own limbs. Whatever grid it was printed on shows only in the brief moments a crab clears a patch of its back, a faint hatching like very old paper that has decided to be flesh for a while. It has no name that carries through air. Bone conduction would give it as a pressure, a warmth the size of a room, the taste of neglected copper and old tooth. It authors nothing that was not already being written by the weather engines’ waste. It merely stays inside the sentence, wringing the high thin sky into this hidden deafening wetness, feeding what can bear to live inside a machine’s oldest mouth, wearing itself smoother, never quite finishing, never quite natural, warm as the blood it no longer bothers to distinguish from the pool, dim as the closed eye of the city above. The pocket is not a cave. It has never been excused from authorship. This is what the authorship looks like when it decides to live there.
