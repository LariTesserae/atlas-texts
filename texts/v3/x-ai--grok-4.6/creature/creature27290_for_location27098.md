The occupancy itself is what lives here, but that is too simple, and the place does not deal in simplicity so much as in withheld completion. At first the plant-mass seems only itself: a single continuous body that has poured into the basin and then locked, stems and pads and living plates pressed so close that any distinction between one organism and the next is a courtesy the light refuses to honor. Yet the mass thinks, if thinking can be a pressure of sugars moving through translucent ribs, a slow redistribution of the unmoving film that beads and is reabsorbed. It does not grow. Growth would be a verb, and verbs have been set aside. Instead it occupies, and in occupying it remembers—faintly, the way the pale stone remembers that it once meant to become strata—what it was like to arrive.

Within that remembrance, other shapes have condensed.

They are not animals. Animals leave tracks and husks and the rumor of interruption; these do not. They are thickenings in the occupancy, places where the waxy tissue has drawn itself into denser, slower vessels. Each is the size of a crouched child or a large folded leaf, pale as the stone, faintly tacky, edged in the same cream and overexposed green. Their surfaces hold the glare the way the plants hold water: they store it, pipe it along internal seams that never quite become veins, release it as a softer internal noon that keeps them from ever needing night. They do not walk so much as allow the surrounding mass to part and then close behind them, a negotiation of pressure so complete that the living floor reseals without scar. Distance, for them, is not space but a change in the density of brightness. They move toward slightly less glare or slightly more, and call it purpose.

They have no faces that persist. What serves for a face is a flattening, a pad-like plane that faces the sky-bowl and drinks the unrelenting hour. Sometimes the plane dimples, as if considering an expression, then forgets. Sometimes a cluster of them gathers in one of the plant-galleries, those corridors that almost became rooms, and their stored light overlaps until the chamber is briefly denser than the rest of the basin, a white-green fog with weight. In those moments the almost-hiss of condensation grows a fraction more certain. It is the closest they come to speech: a shared film, a transfer of the same moisture that never becomes a stream. They exchange the fact of still being here. Then the gathering loosens, each thickening drifting back into the general occupancy, and the chamber forgets it was ever more than stems.

The unfinished structures belong to them, or they belong to the structures; the grammar is the same hesitation. A ring of stacked stone is not a ruin but a thought they once had about enclosure, a thought they set down and then allowed the plants to swallow halfway. They do not finish it because finishing would decide what a wall is for, and decision is the one weather the basin will not permit. A lintel spanning nothing is a memory of weight they briefly agreed to share with the rock. The shallow basins carved—or secreted—into swelling tissue are where they rest their own moisture against the world’s, testing whether the film will ever run. It does not. They leave the basins as they found them, or as they made them; the distinction has no edge.

There are older ones, if age can exist in a place that has only one hour. These have begun to flake like the new stone, plates the size of hands lifting from their surfaces and hanging, undecided whether to fall or to be reabsorbed. They linger near the far wall of the basin, where the occupancy climbs the bright cliff in sheets, and they press themselves against the uncommitted rock as if hoping the geology will finish them, or they it. Nothing happens. The seam where stone meant to become something remains a seam. The older ones do not die. Dying would be a change. They simply occupy more slowly, their internal noon dimming by a degree that cannot be measured because there is no other hour to measure it against.

Once, in a long traverse that is not quite walking, you might encounter a husk that is not rumor. It is papery, thumb-sized, caught in a crotch, and it looks like the shed skin of one of the failing insects the place has nearly unlearned. But if you wait—though waiting here is indistinguishable from remaining—the husk plumpens. Moisture beads on it, is reabsorbed, beads again. It was never shed. It was a thickening that considered becoming separate and then thought better of it, collapsing back into the occupancy with the same small, finished note as a drop released from an overfull pad. The stillness closes. The ecology remains plant talking to plant, now with these extra syllables that are almost, but not quite, someone else.

Above the rim the ordinary world continues, with its nights and its dying and its finished sentences. Down here the inhabitants do not look up. Looking up would imply a gradient, a cooler north, a later hour. They have none of those. They have the composure of the whole: embryonic, glossy with the effort of arriving, and paused at the moment of fullness. They are the basin’s way of not taking its one sentence back. If you left and returned in a hundred years the same thickening would still be considering the same unmoving drop, and the same pale block would still be waiting to remember whether it was meant to be a wall, or a body, or only another hesitation in the glare.
